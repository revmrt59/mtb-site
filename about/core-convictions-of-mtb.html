Core Convictions of Mastering the Bible

1. The Bible Is the Infallible and Inerrant Word of God
I believe that all Scripture is God-breathed (theopneustos), making the original manuscripts fully authoritative, entirely trustworthy, and without error in all that they affirm. In classic theological terms, this affirms the verbal, plenary inspiration and inerrancy of Scripture.

2. God Is Infinitely Good
God is infinitely and unchangeably good. Everything He does flows from who He is. His justice, His hatred of sin, and even His judgment flow from His boundless goodness and love. We therefore read all Scripture through this foundational reality: God is good, and God is love.

3. We Were Created for Trinitarian Life
Father, Son, and Holy Spirit have eternally existed in perfect love and fellowship. Creation flows from the abundance of that life, and God's purpose in salvation is to bring us into relationship with Himself through Jesus Christ, by the Holy Spirit. Salvation is much more than the legal forgiveness of our sins; it is an invitation into the very life of God through Jesus Christ.

4. God Wants to Dwell With His People
From the garden to the tabernacle, from Christ dwelling among us to the Spirit dwelling within us, and finally to God dwelling with humanity forever, the presence of God runs throughout Scripture. I am presence-driven and unashamed. Whether Scripture describes God's people as a house, a family, or a bride, His eternal purpose is clear: God desires to dwell with His people through His Son.

5. Scripture Tells the Story of a Bride
The Song of Solomon beautifully illustrates the journey of the Bride and, in picture form, reflects the larger story running throughout Scripture—God drawing a people to Himself, transforming them through love, and preparing a Bride who loves Him and belongs wholly to Him.

6. God Desires to Make Us Wholly His
The story of Scripture reveals the God who is unlike any other through His Son, Jesus Christ, in order to make us wholly His. This is at the heart of what it means to be holy. Scripture cannot be rightly understood apart from this larger story of a holy God drawing a people to Himself and making them His own.

7. Jesus Must Have the Preeminence
Scripture ultimately leads us to Jesus. We read Scripture to know and encounter Him—to find life in Him, be transformed into His image, walk with Him in obedience, and give Him the preeminence in all things. We do not merely want to know more about Jesus; we want to know Jesus.

8. Open the Tupperware Container
Accurate interpretation matters. We must first understand what the text actually says in its historical, grammatical, literary, and biblical context. But that understanding is the container, not the final destination. Inside the container are the Spirit and life God intends His Word to impart.

Mastering the Bible seeks to open the Tupperware container-to move from accurate explanation into the spiritual reality the text reveals. We ask not only, “What does this passage mean?” but also, “What is God revealing here, and how does this truth lead us into greater knowledge of Him, relationship with Him, and life in Him?”

9. Read Scripture Through the Right Lens
A lens determines how we see what is already there. Scripture provides the light, but the lens through which we approach it profoundly affects what we see.

Our governing lens is the character and eternal purpose of God: God is infinitely good and holy; Scripture reveals His desire to dwell with humanity; Jesus stands at the center of His purposes; and the Spirit uses the Word to draw us into relationship with God and transform us into the likeness of Christ.

The lens never replaces sound interpretation or changes what the text says. It helps us see each passage within the larger reality of who God has revealed Himself to be and what He is accomplishing throughout Scripture.

10. Eat From the Tree of Life
From the beginning, Scripture presents us with two trees—the tree of the knowledge of good and evil and the tree of life. They picture two fundamentally different ways of approaching God and His Word.

We can read Scripture merely to accumulate knowledge: facts to know, doctrines to argue, rules to follow, principles to apply, and lists of what is good and what is bad. Much of that information may be true, yet knowledge by itself is not the ultimate purpose of Scripture.

Or we can come to the Word to receive life.

Jesus said, “The words that I speak to you are spirit, and they are life.” Scripture is intended to reveal God, communicate His heart, draw us into relationship with Jesus through the Holy Spirit, transform us into His likeness, and teach us to live from Him.

Mastering the Bible therefore values knowledge deeply, but refuses to stop there. We study the Word so that through what we understand, we may know the One who gave it. We do not merely study the tree—we eat its fruit.
