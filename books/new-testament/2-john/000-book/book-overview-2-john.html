Book Overview

At a Glance

2 John
Author

The letter comes from “the Elder” (v. 1), who does not give his name. The church has long identified him as John the apostle. In the second century Irenaeus quotes the letter as the words of “John, the disciple of the Lord” (Against Heresies 1.16.3; 3.16.8), and its language closely matches John’s Gospel and 1 John. Some identify the writer instead as another John, often called John the Elder. Either way, the first readers knew who “the Elder” was.

Audience

The Elder writes “to the elect lady and her children” (v. 1) and closes with greetings from “the children of your elect sister” (v. 13). The lady is most likely a local church, her children its members, and her sister the church where the Elder was. Some read her as an individual Christian woman and her family, and a few take the Greek word for “lady” (Kyria) as her name. The Elder’s shift to the plural “you” (vv. 6, 8, 10, 12) and his concern about whom to receive “into your house” fit a congregation meeting in a home.

Date

The letter gives no date or place. It is commonly placed around AD 85–95, near the time of 1 John, with which it shares its language and concerns. Ephesus is the traditional place of writing. The message of the letter does not depend on a precise date.

Historical Setting

First-century churches met in homes. Traveling teachers and missionaries moved from church to church and depended on believers to receive them, provide for them, and send them on their way (3 John 5–8). This practice carried the gospel across the Roman world, but a church’s welcome could also help a false teacher’s ministry. 2 John does not describe a particular visit, but its warning (vv. 10–11) assumes such a welcome was possible.

Purpose

The Elder had found some of the lady’s children “walking in truth” and rejoiced greatly (v. 4). At the same time, “many deceivers have gone out into the world” (v. 7). He writes to call the church to keep walking in truth and love and to warn them not to receive or support those who bring a false teaching about Christ, while hoping to visit and “speak face to face” (v. 12).

Central Message

The Elder calls the elect lady and her children to keep walking in the truth and to love one another by walking according to God’s commandments, and to guard that fellowship by abiding in the doctrine of Christ and refusing to support deceivers who do not confess Jesus Christ as coming in the flesh. Truth and love belong together and lead to fellowship with the Father and the Son and to full joy.

Key Themes

  • Truth that abides. Truth is something God’s people live in, not only something they know; it “abides in us and will be with us forever” (vv. 1–4).

  • Love lived through obedience. “This is love, that we walk according to His commandments” (vv. 5–6).

  • Abiding in the doctrine of Christ. Fellowship with the Father and the Son is kept by remaining in the truth about Jesus (vv. 7, 9).

  • Watchful perseverance. Believers must look to themselves so that they do not lose what has been worked for (v. 8).

  • Discerning hospitality. A church must not lend its home and blessing to the ministry of those who deny Christ (vv. 10–11).

  • Joy in fellowship. The Elder longs to speak face to face, “that our joy may be full” (v. 12).

Book Flow

2 John 1:1–3: Greeting in Truth and Love

The Elder greets the elect lady and her children, “whom I love in truth,” together with all who have known the truth. That truth “abides in us and will be with us forever” (v. 2). His greeting is also a promise: grace, mercy, and peace “will be with you” from the Father and from “the Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of the Father, in truth and love” (v. 3).

2 John 1:4–6: Walking in Truth and Love

The Elder rejoices greatly at finding some of her children walking in truth. He then pleads for the commandment they have had from the beginning, “that we love one another” (v. 5). He defines love as walking according to God’s commandments, joining love and obedience into one way of life.

2 John 1:7–11: Abiding in the Doctrine of Christ

Verse 7 turns the letter. “Many deceivers have gone out into the world who do not confess Jesus Christ as coming in the flesh,” so the believers must look to themselves, “that we do not lose those things we worked for, but that we may receive a full reward” (v. 8). Whoever does not abide in the doctrine of Christ “does not have God,” but whoever abides in it “has both the Father and the Son” (v. 9). The church is not to receive or greet the one who brings another teaching, for “he who greets him shares in his evil deeds” (v. 11).

2 John 1:12–13: Hope of Fellowship Face to Face

The Elder has many things to write but would rather come and “speak face to face, that our joy may be full” (v. 12). The letter ends as it began, with family language, as the children of the elect sister send their greetings.

Understanding the Book

What This Book Is About

2 John is a short pastoral letter about living in the truth of Jesus Christ. After a greeting centered on truth and love, the Elder rejoices over believers who walk in truth and pleads with the church to love one another by walking according to God’s commandments. He then warns about deceivers who deny that Jesus Christ came in the flesh, calls the church to abide in the doctrine of Christ, and instructs them not to receive or support those who bring another teaching. The letter closes with his hope of seeing them face to face.

Why This Book Matters

2 John shows that truth and love are not rivals. The same letter that pleads “that we love one another” (v. 5) tells the church not to receive those who deny Christ (vv. 10–11). For the Elder, guarding the truth about Jesus is one of the ways a church loves its people, and love itself is lived out by walking in God’s commandments (v. 6).

The letter is also one of the few places in the New Testament that speaks directly to a church’s support of traveling teachers. Read with 3 John, it gives practical wisdom for deciding whom a church welcomes, supports, and sends into ministry.

Key Concepts to Understand Before Reading

  • Truth. In John’s writings, truth is God’s reality revealed in Jesus, who is “the truth” (John 14:6). To walk in truth (v. 4) is to live in line with that revelation, not only to agree with it.

  • The commandment from the beginning. The command to love one another is not a new rule (v. 5). It is Jesus’ own command (John 13:34), which these believers have heard since their life in Christ began.

  • Coming in the flesh. The confession that Jesus Christ truly became human, with a real body (v. 7; compare John 1:14; 1 John 4:2–3). The deceivers denied this.

  • Antichrist. Anyone who opposes Christ or puts a false Christ in His place. 1 John speaks of the Antichrist who is coming and of “many antichrists” already present (1 John 2:18). In 2 John the deceiver who denies Christ’s coming in the flesh is “an antichrist” (v. 7).

  • The doctrine of Christ. “Doctrine” means teaching (v. 9). The phrase can mean the teaching about Christ or the teaching Christ Himself gave. In 2 John the immediate emphasis is the apostolic truth about Christ’s Person.

  • Receiving and greeting. In this setting, receiving a traveling teacher “into your house” and greeting him meant welcoming him into the church’s life and supporting his ministry (vv. 10–11). The Elder forbids that support for those who deny Christ. He does not forbid conversation, ordinary hospitality, or loving relationships, including with family members, who believe differently.

Christ and Life

The Elder presents “the Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of the Father” (v. 3). The title is relational. Jesus is the Son who makes the Father known (John 1:18; 14:9), and grace, mercy, and peace come from the Father and the Son together. Throughout the letter, the truth about Jesus cannot be separated from knowing God.

The Elder insists that Jesus Christ came “in the flesh” (v. 7). The eternal Word truly became human (John 1:14). The wider New Testament shows that He rose bodily and remains the one Mediator, the Man Christ Jesus, who lives to intercede for His people (Luke 24:39; 1 Timothy 2:5; Hebrews 7:24–25). A Christ who only seemed human could not truly share our life or bear our sin.

That is why verse 9 carries such weight. Whoever abides in the doctrine of Christ “has both the Father and the Son.” Right teaching about Jesus is not an end in itself. Fellowship with God comes through Christ Himself, and sound teaching reveals and guards the truth about the One through whom we know the Father (John 14:6; 17:3; 1 John 2:23). The doctrine of Christ is precious because Christ Himself is precious.

The truth the Elder speaks of is also living. It “abides in us and will be with us forever” (v. 2), words that recall Jesus’ promise of the Spirit of truth, who would “abide with you forever” (John 14:16–17). Believers do not merely hold correct ideas about Jesus. The truth makes its home in them, and they walk in it (v. 4).

That life shows itself in love expressed through obedience (vv. 5–6). Jesus said, “If anyone loves Me, he will keep My word; and My Father will love him, and We will come to him and make Our home with him” (John 14:23). Obedience is how love for God is lived out, and it is the setting in which the Father and the Son make their home with those who love Him. The letter closes with the hope “that our joy may be full” (v. 12), the same joy that fellowship with the Father and the Son produces (John 15:11; 1 John 1:3–4).

Reading Guide

Read This Book Remembering…

Read 2 John as a letter of love. The warning about deceivers comes from a pastor who loves this church “in truth,” rejoices over their faithfulness, and longs to see them. It calls the church to remain in the truth of Christ, not to live in suspicion.

Remember that verses 10–11 concern support and endorsement of false teaching about Christ, not every relationship with people who believe differently. Read the letter alongside 1 John, which develops the same concerns more fully.

Helpful Notes

Helpful Notes

  • Verse 8. The NKJV reads “that we do not lose those things we worked for.” Other manuscripts read “you” here, and the NLT follows that sense: “that you do not lose what we have worked so hard to achieve.” The NKJV wording includes the labor of the Elder and his co-workers among these believers.

  • Verse 9. “Transgresses” follows manuscripts that read a word meaning to step beyond. Other manuscripts read a word meaning to go ahead, which most modern translations follow. Either reading describes leaving the teaching of Christ behind.

  • Cerinthus. Irenaeus records a tradition, received from Polycarp, that John once left a bathhouse in Ephesus on learning that Cerinthus, who separated the divine Christ from the man Jesus, was inside (Against Heresies 3.3.4). The story illustrates the kind of error John opposed, but 2 John does not name Cerinthus or identify the deceivers.

  • Companion letters. 2 John and 3 John share a writer called “the Elder,” a similar length, and nearly identical closings about writing with ink and hoping to speak face to face (v. 12; 3 John 13–14). 2 John warns against welcoming false teachers; 3 John commends welcoming true ones.

  • References. 2 John has one chapter, so verses may be cited as 2 John 1:7 or simply as verse 7.
