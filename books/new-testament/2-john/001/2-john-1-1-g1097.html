KNOWN - ( ginōskō , Strong's G1097 )

Summary:

In 2 John 1:1, the word translated "known" refers to an experiential, relational, and settled understanding of divine truth. Rather than referring to mere intellectual awareness or abstract information, this knowledge denotes a personal encounter with the reality of God's revealed truth in Jesus Christ. The grammatical form in this passage emphasizes a completed past experience that produces an ongoing, permanent state of dynamic understanding and spiritual fellowship. All genuine believers share this common bond because they have personally come to know, embrace, and abide in the Truth.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • In classical Greek literature, knowledge often emphasized intellectual observation, rational analysis, or philosophical contemplation.

  • Under the influence of the Old Testament concept of knowledge, biblical Greek transforms this word into a relational concept where knowing involves personal encounter, intimacy, and active covenant response.

  • The term moves beyond detached academic perception to describe comprehension gained through direct personal experience and living relationship.

  • In Johannine literature, knowing truth is not simply recognizing factual propositions; it is entering into a living, transformational relationship with Jesus Christ, who is the personal incarnation of God's truth.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The verb signifies coming to know, learning, perceiving, recognizing, or understanding experientially.

  • It denotes active, personal awareness rather than passive theoretical retention.

  • In 2 John 1:1, the word appears as a perfect active participle, describing a past action with abiding present results: those who have come to know the truth and permanently possess that knowledge.

  • It implies spiritual discernment, personal conviction, and an ongoing personal reception of divine revelation.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Relational Intimacy: Portrays deep personal acquaintance and covenantal unity between individuals, as well as between God and His people.

  • Experiential Acquisition: Indicates knowledge gained through personal involvement, practical experience, and spiritual illumination rather than secondary hearsay.

  • Spiritual Enlightenment: Represents the opening of the believer's spiritual understanding by the Holy Spirit to recognize the reality of the gospel.

  • Abiding Perception: Functions as a defining mark of genuine discipleship, where knowing God necessarily results in walking in light, truth, and love.

4. Theological Implications

  • Truth is Personal: The truth known by believers is not an abstract philosophy, but divine reality embodied in Jesus Christ and revealed through the Gospel.

  • Assurance of Salvation: Genuine faith produces an enduring, permanent state of spiritual knowledge. The perfect tense underscores the permanence and security of the believer's relationship with the truth.

  • Unity of Believers: Shared experiential knowledge of the truth serves as the fundamental basis for authentic Christian love and unity across the global church.

  • Defense Against Error: Personal, experiential knowledge of divine truth provides essential spiritual discernment to withstand false teaching and counterfeit doctrines.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:1, John addresses "the elect lady and her children," expressing his love "in truth," and extends this love to "all those who have known the truth."

  • The context highlights the universal brotherhood of believers. Every true Christian across the world possesses the exact same settled, personal experience of divine truth.

  • The perfect tense emphasizes that believers came to know the truth at conversion and continue to live in the permanent reality of that knowledge today.

  • This shared knowledge creates an indestructible spiritual bond of love that unites true believers across geographical and cultural divides.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 8:32 - "And you shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free."

  • John 17:3 - "And this is eternal life, that they may know You, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom You have sent."

  • 1 John 2:3 - "Now by this we know that we know Him, if we keep His commandments."

  • 1 John 4:8 - "He who does not love does not know God, for God is love."

  • 2 Timothy 2:19 - "Nevertheless the solid foundation of God stands, having this seal: 'The Lord knows those who are His,' and, 'Let everyone who names the name of Christ depart from iniquity.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Countering Early False Teaching: In the late first century, early Gnostic sects claimed secret, elitist knowledge available only to a privileged few. John uses this word to affirm that true spiritual knowledge is fully accessible, public, relational, and shared equally by all genuine believers.

  • Pastoral Application: True Christian maturity connects orthodoxy with orthopraxy. Knowing the truth is not merely agreeing with a creed; it is abiding in dynamic, personal fellowship with Jesus Christ that reveals itself in practical obedience and love.
