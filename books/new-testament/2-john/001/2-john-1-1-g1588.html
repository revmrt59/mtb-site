ELECT - ( eklektos , Strong's G1588 )

Summary:

The Greek adjective *eklektos* means "chosen," "select," or "excellent," referring to those hand-selected by God out of His sovereign grace for His special possession and purpose. In 2 John 1:1, John addresses the "elect lady," a designation that underscores either an esteemed Christian woman or, more likely, a local sister church and her members as objects of God's divine choice. By framing his greeting with this word, John establishes a foundation of secure identity, spiritual privilege, and covenant responsibility, reminding his readers that they are chosen by God to walk in truth and love in a world filled with spiritual deception.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word *eklektos* is derived from the compound verb *eklegomai*, which combines *ek* (out of, from) and *lego* (to gather, call, or choose). The literal word picture is that of picking out or gathering something valuable from a larger group. In secular Greek antiquity, this term was used for choice military troops, select items of superior quality in the marketplace, or citizens singled out for honorable civic duties. It denotes both the act of selection and the outstanding quality or favored status of the one chosen. In the biblical context, this word picture transitions from a physical selection of goods or soldiers to God's deliberate, loving choice of a people for Himself out of the mass of humanity.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

In the New Testament, *eklektos* functions primarily as an adjective or a substantival adjective ("the elect"). It carries three primary lexical nuances:

  • Chosen or select, referring to those whom God has chosen out of the world for salvation and covenant relationship.

  • Choice, excellent, or precious, describing something of the highest quality or value, such as a precious gemstone.

  • Preferred or singled out for a specific office, honor, or task.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The New Testament uses *eklektos* to paint a vivid picture of divine value and belonging. It is used to describe Jesus Christ as the "chosen" chief cornerstone, precious in the sight of God, yet rejected by men (1 Peter 2:4, 6). The word picture of a building stone selected by a master architect highlights Christ's perfect suitability for God's saving purposes.

When applied to believers, the term serves as a collective title of honor, replacing Israel's Old Testament designations with the corporate identity of the Church as the "chosen generation" (1 Peter 2:9). The usage implies that believers do not belong to the world; they have been hand-picked by the Father, set apart, and endowed with immense value and security because of the One who chose them.

4. Theological Implications

The theology of election wrapped up in *eklektos* is deeply rooted in God's sovereign grace, love, and initiative.

  • Initiated by God: Election does not depend on human merit, performance, or foresight, but entirely on God's sovereign pleasure and mercy.

  • Purpose-Driven: God's selection of the elect is not merely for privileged status, but for holiness, obedience, and the proclamation of His praises.

  • Security and Comfort: Because God is the initiator of this choice, the elect stand in an unbreakable state of justification. No accusation can stand against those whom God has chosen and justified.

  • Covenant Continuity: It links New Testament believers directly to the covenantal language of the Old Testament, where Israel was God's chosen inheritance, showing that the Church now shares in this rich, covenantal identity.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 2 John 1:1, John addresses his letter "To the elect lady and her children." Interpreters have long debated whether this refers to an individual woman (perhaps named *Eklekte* or *Kyria*) or is a personification of a local congregation.

The weight of internal evidence strongly favors personification. The "lady" represents a local church, and "her children" are the individual believers who make up its membership. This corporate interpretation is reinforced in verse 13, where John sends greetings from "the children of your elect sister."

By addressing the church as "the elect lady," John immediately elevates their identity. Amidst the threat of itinerant false teachers and deceivers who deny the incarnation (v. 7), John reminds this local congregation of their ultimate security and origin: they are chosen by God. Their election calls them to stand firm in the truth, resist false doctrine, and walk in mutual love, confident that their community is founded upon the sovereign decree of God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 24:22 - "And unless those days were shortened, no flesh would be saved; but for the elect's sake those days will be shortened."

  • Romans 8:33 - "Who shall bring a charge against God's elect? It is God who justifies."

  • Colossians 3:12 - "Therefore, as the elect of God, holy and beloved, put on tender mercies, kindness, humility, meekness, longsuffering;"

  • 1 Peter 2:9 - "But you are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, His own special people..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

John beautifully balances the sovereign doctrine of election with the practical realities of Christian life. In 2 John 1:1, the elect status of the lady and her children is immediately paired with John’s love "in truth." Sovereign election does not breed cold intellectualism or isolation; rather, it produces a community bound together by genuine, Christ-centered love and absolute adherence to apostolic truth. Election is the root, while truth, love, and obedience are the necessary, visible fruits.
