TRUTH - ( aletheia , Strong's G225 )

Summary:

In 2 John 1:1, *aletheia* refers not merely to abstract factual accuracy or verbal honesty, but to the objective reality of God's ultimate revelation embodied in Jesus Christ and preserved in the apostolic gospel. To "love in truth" and to "know the truth" means to exist within the sphere of divine reality. This shared commitment to divine truth creates authentic Christian fellowship, grounds holy affection, and protects believers against spiritual deception.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The Greek term *aletheia* is formed from the negative prefix *a-* ("not" or "without") and the root *lanthano* or *lethe* ("hidden," "concealed," or "forgotten"). Etymologically, it conveys the concept of "un-concealment"—that which is unveiled, exposed to view, and fully manifest. In classical Greek thought, *aletheia* described reality as opposed to mere appearance, shadow, or illusion.

When the Hebrew Scriptures were translated into Greek (the Septuagint), *aletheia* was selected to translate the Hebrew word *emet*. While classical Greek stressed the intellectual concept of objective reality, the Hebrew *emet* emphasized reliability, firmness, stability, and covenantal faithfulness. Consequently, in New Testament usage, *aletheia* brings together these rich traditions: it denotes that which is objectively real and ultimate, while simultaneously conveying the absolute trustworthiness and faithfulness of God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

*Aletheia* carries a rich range of interrelated meanings across the New Testament:

  • Objective Reality: That which corresponds to actual fact, as opposed to falsehood, fiction, or pretense.

  • Divine Revelation: The absolute truth of God revealed to humanity, specifically manifested in the Person, work, and gospel of Jesus Christ.

  • Moral and Spiritual Integrity: Sincerity of heart, integrity of character, and freedom from hypocrisy.

  • Christian Orthodoxy: The body of apostolic doctrine that defines true Christian faith.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The New Testament portrays *aletheia* in several distinct dimensions:

  • Truth as a Person: Jesus Christ does not merely teach truth; He is "the truth" (John 14:6). In Him, the invisible God is unveiled to humanity.

  • Truth as a Sacred Message: The gospel is defined as "the word of truth" (Ephesians 1:13; 2 Timothy 2:15). It is the definitive account of salvation.

  • Truth as a Sphere of Life: Believers are instructed to "walk in truth" (3 John 1:4) and to "do the truth" (John 3:21). Truth is an environment in which the Christian thinks, speaks, and acts.

  • Truth as an Armor: Paul describes truth as a belt buckled around the waist (Ephesians 6:14), providing stability, readiness, and structural support for the spiritual warrior.

4. Theological Implications

Truth is rooted in the unchangeable character of God. Because God cannot lie, His word is absolute truth (John 17:17). *Aletheia* is never relative, subject to human consensus, or dependent upon cultural trends.

Furthermore, the New Testament tightly weaves truth and love together. Love disconnected from truth degenerates into sentimentality or tolerance of sin, whereas truth presented without love becomes harsh and legalistic. In biblical theology, authentic love is generated by and lived within the boundaries of God's revealed truth. Knowledge of *aletheia* delivers believers from spiritual bondage, producing moral transformation and guarding the church from demonic error.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 2 John 1:1, the apostle John uses *aletheia* four times in the first three verses, setting the theme for his short epistle. Writing to "the elect lady and her children," John declares that he loves them "in truth" (*en aletheia*). This prepositional phrase indicates both the sincerity of his love (genuine, unfeigned love) and the sphere of his love (a brotherhood forged by shared devotion to the gospel).

John adds that this love is shared by "all those who have known the truth." Here, "the truth" refers specifically to the apostolic doctrine regarding Jesus Christ—particularly His incarnation, deity, and redemptive work. In the context of 2 John, itinerant false teachers were spreading Christological errors. John establishes that true Christian affection is not universal or indiscriminate; it is bounded by shared submission to the objective truth of Christ.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 1:14 — "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we beheld His glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth."

  • John 8:32 — "And you shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free."

  • John 14:6 — "Jesus said to him, 'I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through Me.'"

  • John 17:17 — "Sanctify them by Your truth. Your word is truth."

  • 1 John 3:18 — "My little children, let us not love in word or in tongue, but in deed and in truth."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The emphasis on *aletheia* in 2 John establishes the prerequisite for biblical hospitality. John later instructs the church not to receive or greet anyone who brings a doctrine contrary to "the truth" (2 John 1:9-10), showing that loyalty to divine truth supersedes social politeness.

  • In the Johannine writings, "knowing the truth" is not merely an intellectual exercise; it involves experiential relationship with God that manifests in obedience, righteousness, and mutual love among believers.
