LOVE - ( agapao , Strong's G25 )

Summary:

In 2 John 1:1, "love" translates the Greek verb agapao, which describes an intentional, sacrificial, and covenantal devotion rooted in God's nature rather than fluctuating human emotion. John emphasizes loving "in truth," demonstrating that biblical agapao is never an abstract sentiment or an uncritical tolerance; it operates in complete alignment with divine reality and sound doctrine. This love actively seeks the spiritual well-being of fellow believers, unites the body of Christ across all cultural barriers, and reflects the sacrificial devotion Christ demonstrated toward His church.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In secular Greek literature prior to the New Testament, agapao was a relatively rare and non-descript verb compared to phileo (affectionate warmth or friendship) or eros (passionate or romantic desire). Classical writers often preferred terms that expressed emotional attachment or reciprocal passion. However, New Testament writers adopted agapao to capture a uniquely divine concept: a deliberate, selfless love that gives without requiring return, seeking the highest eternal good of the person loved. Rather than being driven by the beauty or value of the object, agapao flows from the sovereign character of the giver.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Agapao means to cherish, show active care for, demonstrate commitment to, and set preferential value upon someone or something.

  • To love unconditionally and sacrificially, willing the ultimate spiritual good of another.

  • To exercise deliberate, intentional goodwill toward others regardless of personal cost or merit in the recipient.

  • To prioritize God and His righteous standards above worldly desires or self-interest.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the New Testament, particularly within the Johannine writings, agapao describes:

  • The eternal relationship of shared devotion between God the Father and God the Son.

  • God's decisive, redemptive action in sending Jesus Christ to save a lost world.

  • The explicit, mandatory virtue that defines Christian discipleship and distinguishes genuine faith from empty profession.

  • The bond of fellowship that unites believers into a mutually supportive spiritual family.

4. Theological Implications

Agapao is fundamentally a theological term because God Himself is love. Biblical love is not a human achievement generated by self-will; it is the fruit of the Holy Spirit living within the believer. Furthermore, agapao cannot be separated from truth. True Christian love operates within the boundaries of divine revelation. Love without truth devolves into sentimental compromise, while truth without love becomes cold legalism. In God's kingdom, agapao standardizes Christian conduct and authenticates a believer's transformation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 2 John 1:1, the apostle John greets "the elect lady and her children," stating that he loves them "in truth." In this context, "in truth" serves as the sphere and source of John's agapao. This love is not a mere personal affection or social politeness; it is rooted in their shared commitment to the gospel of Jesus Christ. John adds that "not only I, but also all those who have known the truth" share this same love. This highlights that Christian agapao creates a universal, truth-bound fellowship among all genuine believers, binding them together in mutual commitment against the influence of deceptive teachers.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 3:16 - "For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son, that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have everlasting life."

  • John 13:34 - "A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another; as I have loved you, that you also love one another."

  • 1 John 4:11 - "Beloved, if God so loved us, we also ought to love one another."

  • 1 John 4:19 - "We love Him because He first loved us."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Agapao is primarily an act of the will accompanied by continuous action, rather than an uncontrollable feeling.

  • While agapao involves duty and conviction, it is not devoid of warmth or deep affection; it engages the whole heart in sincere care for others.

  • In pastoral application, loving "in truth" demands both compassion toward God's people and uncompromising vigilance against false teaching that threatens their spiritual welfare.
