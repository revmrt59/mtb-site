CHILDREN - ( teknon , Strong's G5043 )

Summary:

In 2 John 1:1, the word translated "children" (*teknon*) refers either to physical offspring or, metaphorically, to the members of a local church under the spiritual care of an apostle or elder. Derived from a root meaning "to bear" or "to bring forth," *teknon* emphasizes origin, vital relationship, and shared character. In Johannine literature, it highlights the warm, pastoral bond between a spiritual father and those under his care, as well as the deep spiritual identity of believers who have been born of God and bound together in His truth.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • Derived from the primary verb *tikto*, meaning "to bring forth," "to bear," or "to give birth."

  • Etymologically focuses on the act of birth and the direct lineage established by that birth.

  • Distinct from *pais* (which often stresses youth, child status, or servant relationship) and *brephos* (a newly born infant), *teknon* points to the familial relationship itself, highlighting that the child derives life and nature from the parent.

  • In ancient Greek culture and scriptural usage, the term moves naturally from biological offspring to spiritual disciples who derive their spiritual life, instruction, and maturity through a spiritual father or mentor.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • A physical child or descendant, regardless of age, in relation to parents.

  • A spiritual child or disciple, indicating a person brought to faith or nurtured in spiritual growth by an apostle or Christian minister.

  • A child of God, describing a believer who has been spiritually born again through the Holy Spirit.

  • Metaphorically, a person who demonstrates the character or nature of that which produces or influences them (e.g., "children of light").

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Biological Offspring: Denotes standard familial relations, emphasizing the obligation of love, nurture, and inheritance between parents and their children.

  • Spiritual Progeny: Pictures the relational bond between an apostle or pastor and believers in a congregation, reflecting intimate care, guidance, and spiritual legacy.

  • Divine Lineage: Pictures believers as those who share the very nature and moral character of God through divine rebirth, standing in stark contrast to the spirit of the world.

  • Character Identification: Used as an idiom to describe individuals defined by a dominant quality, such as obedience, wisdom, or light.

4. Theological Implications

  • Regeneration: The term underlines the spiritual reality of the new birth. Believers are not merely adopted into God's family legally; they are begotten by God, possessing His spiritual life and reflecting His Holy character.

  • Pastoral Heart: Illustrates that biblical leadership is fundamentally relational and familial rather than bureaucratic or institutional. Ministry flow from sacrificial, parent-like affection.

  • Covenant Community: Establishes the local church as a true family bound together by the shared life of God and a common commitment to divine truth.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:1, "her children" refers either to the biological children of a notable Christian woman ("the elect lady") or to the members of a local church personified as a mother.

  • Given John's characteristic stylistic usage in his epistles, the phrase most likely describes believers within a local congregation who are loved and nurtured by the apostle.

  • John grounds his pastoral love for these "children" in "the truth." True spiritual childship produces a shared devotion to sound doctrine and authentic Christian love, protecting the local church family from false teachers.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 1:12: "But as many as received Him, to them He gave the right to become children of God, to those who believe in His name:"

  • 1 John 3:1: "Behold what manner of love the Father has bestowed on us, that we should be called children of God! Therefore the world does not know us, because it did not know Him."

  • 3 John 1:4: "I have no greater joy than to hear that my children walk in truth."

  • Ephesians 5:8: "For you were once darkness, but now you are light in the Lord. Walk as children of light"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Contrast with *huios*: While Paul frequently uses *huios* ("son") to stress legal adoption, position, and full inheritance rights, John prefers *teknon* ("child") to stress birth origin, continuous family relationship, and shared moral nature with God.

  • John reserves *huios* almost exclusively for Jesus Christ as the unique, eternal Son of God, while using *teknon* for human believers who become God's children through regeneration.
