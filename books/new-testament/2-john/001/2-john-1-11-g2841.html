SHARES - ( koinōneō , Strong's G2841 )

Summary:

In 2 John 1:11, the word "shares" translates the Greek verb *koinōneō*, which means to enter into partnership, participate jointly, or become a partaker with someone in an enterprise or condition. Rather than indicating casual contact or mere presence, *koinōneō* describes active alignment and shared moral responsibility. In the context of John's warning against itinerant deceivers, extending a formal greeting or official hospitality to a false teacher effectively endorses their ministry, making the host a joint participant in their evil works.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • *koinōneō* derives from the adjective *koinos*, meaning "common" or "shared."

  • In ancient Greco-Roman business and legal contexts, *koinōneō* and its related noun *koinōnia* described commercial partnerships, joint financial ventures, or shared business ownership.

  • The term conveys the image of business partners who share equally in both the operations and the liability of a business venture.

  • When applied to human actions and relationships, it implies active involvement, personal connection, and shared outcomes.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The verb *koinōneō* signifies holding something in common with another, participating in an activity alongside others, or sharing in a specific state or quality.

  • The semantic range spans financial giving, spiritual fellowship, physical participation, and moral alignment.

  • When connected to sinful actions or false teaching, *koinōneō* means to become complicit with, give aid to, or share moral culpability for another person's transgressions.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Financial and Material Distribution: Used of Christians contributing to the needs of fellow believers or sharing financial support with Gospel ministers.

  • Spiritual Participation and Suffering: Used of believers participating in spiritual blessings or sharing in the sufferings of Christ.

  • Moral and Doctrinal Complicity: Used negatively when a person supports, enables, or approves of wrongful practices, taking on a shared share of the moral consequences.

4. Theological Implications

  • Biblical fellowship is never morally neutral; alignment with a person involves alignment with their mission and truth claim.

  • Christian love must always be regulated by truth; hospitality extended to those who actively work to destroy the doctrine of Christ distorts love into compromise.

  • Facilitating, funding, or validating deceptive ministry renders a believer spiritually accountable for the harm produced by that ministry.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:10-11, the apostle addresses itinerant false teachers who deny that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh.

  • To "greet" (*legein auto chairein*) in this culture was not merely a polite recognition, but an official blessing, expression of solidarity, and open-hearted invitation into the house-church assembly.

  • By welcoming such false teachers into the home or giving them a public blessing, the host provides them with food, shelter, platform, and credibility.

  • Consequently, the host becomes a joint participant (*koinōnei*) in their "evil deeds," sharing in the moral liability of spreading destructive heresy.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Romans 12:13 - "...distributing to the needs of the saints, given to hospitality."

  • Galatians 6:6 - "Let him who is taught the word share in all good things with him who teaches."

  • 1 Timothy 5:22 - "Do not lay hands on anyone hastily, nor share in other people's sins; keep yourself pure."

  • 1 Peter 4:13 - "...but rejoice to the extent that you partake of Christ's sufferings, that when His glory is revealed, you may also be glad with exceeding joy."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The noun form *koinōnia* is translated as "fellowship" or "communion" throughout the New Testament, highlighting that *koinōneō* is fellowship expressed through action.

  • Scripture distinguishes between reaching out to lost sinners with the gospel and offering organizational, financial, or ecclesiastical partnership to false teachers who subvert the gospel.
