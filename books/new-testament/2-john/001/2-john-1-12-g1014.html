WISH - ( boulomai , Strong's G1014 )

Summary:

The Greek verb *boulomai* refers to a deliberate exercise of the will, carried out with conscious intent, thoughtful planning, or purposeful choice. Unlike a temporary impulse or passive emotional longing, *boulomai* denotes a decision rooted in reflection and mind-set. In 2 John 1:12, the apostle John states that he did not "wish" or intend to communicate his extensive pastoral instruction merely through paper and ink. Instead, he intentionally restrained his writing because he preferred face-to-face fellowship, where spiritual joy could be fully realized. This term underscores a deliberate prioritization of personal presence over convenient, distant communication.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • Rooted in the concept of mental inclination and deliberate purpose, *boulomai* conveys a settled choice formed after inner reflection.

  • In classical Greek literature, the word was often paired with political counsel, formal decisions of an assembly, or deliberate administrative plans.

  • In the New Testament, *boulomai* stands alongside *thelō* (to desire or will). While *thelō* can express desire arising from emotion or immediate impulse, *boulomai* highlights the element of deliberate counsel, conscious intent, and reasoned decision-making.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • To intend, purpose, plan, or be minded to do something.

  • To exercise a conscious, deliberate choice after weighing options.

  • To desire with an underlying purpose or plan, rather than expressing a vague or passive hope.

  • In passive forms, it carries the sense of a settled determination or preference regarding a course of action.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Divine Counsel: Used to describe God's sovereign, purposeful determination in redemption and grace.

  • Apostolical Intentions: Used by apostles to communicate planned ministry trips, deliberate choices, or administrative decisions in local churches.

  • Rational Choice: Expresses a human decision where the mind deliberately inclines toward a specific action over alternatives.

4. Theological Implications

  • Incarnational Ministry: The gospel values relational presence. Communication via media (such as letters) is valid, but the ultimate expression of Christian love reflects Christ's own incarnation—being present among God's people.

  • Intentionality in Shepherd Care: Ministry choices are not accidental. John's choice to refrain from writing more was a deliberate act of pastoral wisdom aimed at maximizing spiritual joy.

  • God's Patient Will: The deliberate will of God inclines toward redemption and salvation rather than destruction, providing assurance to believers.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:12, John uses the aorist form *eboulēthēn* ("I did not wish" or "I did not intend").

  • John possessed "many things to write," yet he deliberately chose not to commit them to papyrus ("paper") and "ink."

  • The choice was not caused by laziness or lack of material, but by a conscious determination that paper and ink were inadequate substitutes for personal presence.

  • John's purpose was relational: he intended to come in person so that their mutual "joy may be full."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 2 Peter 3:9 – "The Lord is not slack concerning His promise, as some count slackness, but is longsuffering toward us, not willing (*boulomai*) that any should perish but that all should come to repentance."

  • 1 Timothy 2:8 – "I desire (*boulomai*) therefore that the men pray everywhere, lifting up holy hands, without wrath and doubting;"

  • Philemon 1:13 – "whom I wished (*boulomai*) to keep with me, that on your behalf he might minister to me in my chains for the gospel."

  • James 1:18 – "Of His own will (*boulomai*) He brought us forth by the word of truth, that we might be a kind of firstfruits of His creatures."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Technology vs. Presence: Papyrus and ink were the standard distance-communication technologies of the first-century Roman world. While John utilized epistles under divine inspiration, he recognized the limits of written media for deep pastoral connection.

  • Fullness of Joy: Biblical fellowship (*koinonia*) reaches its highest expression in personal, face-to-face ("mouth to mouth") encounter, reflecting the heavenly reality where believers will one day see God face to face.
