FULL - ( plēroō , Strong's G4137 )

Summary:

The Greek verb *plēroō* signifies filling a vessel to its absolute capacity, completing a process, or bringing something to its intended measure and perfection. In 2 John 12, the Apostle John uses this word in the perfect passive tense to describe a Christian joy that is completely filled up and brought to abiding maturity through direct, face-to-face fellowship. Written communication serves an essential role in communicating divine truth, but personal presence brings spiritual joy to its fullest expression. True joy is not merely an isolated, internal feeling; it reaches its divine capacity when shared in person among believers who walk together in the truth.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • Ancient secular Greek used *plēroō* to describe filling a ship with cargo, filling water jars to the brim, or satisfying a financial obligation or quota completely.

  • The imagery depicts a container filled to maximum capacity, leaving no empty space remaining.

  • In biblical literature, the term moves beyond literal containers to describe inner spiritual reality: hearts filled with joy, peace, wisdom, or the Holy Spirit, as well as prophecies filled to their complete realization.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • *Plēroō* denotes making full, bringing to completion, filling up to the top, or fulfilling a obligation or promise.

  • The word carries three primary semantic dimensions:

  • Literal: To fill an object or space entirely (e.g., filling a net with fish or a room with fragrance).

  • Executive: To fulfill, accomplish, or perform fully (e.g., fulfilling the law or completing a divine task).

  • Experiential/Spiritual: To permeate or completely possess a person's inner life (e.g., being filled with joy, fruit, or the Holy Spirit).

  • In 2 John 12, the grammatical form is a perfect passive subjunctive (*peplērōmenē*), indicating a state of complete fullness that is acted upon the believers from an outside divine source, resulting in an ongoing, continuous condition of completed joy.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • *Plēroō* appears over 80 times in the New Testament, functioning as a key theological term for divine completion.

  • Filling Vessels and Space: Used literally for nets filled with fish or houses filled with an aroma.

  • Fulfilling Prophecy and Scripture: Frequently used in the Gospels to indicate that Old Testament promises have reached their complete, intended realization in Jesus Christ.

  • Experiential Filling: Used in the Epistles to describe believers being filled with all the fullness of God, filled with the fruit of righteousness, or filled with the Holy Spirit.

  • Complete Joy: In Johannine literature, the expression "that your joy may be full" describes the ultimate purpose of apostolic teaching, answered prayer, and face-to-face fellowship.

4. Theological Implications

  • Incarnational Ministry: God designed human beings for embodied, relational communion. While letters and written texts communicate necessary doctrine, face-to-face fellowship completes the experiential joy of local church life.

  • Derivative Divine Gift: Christian joy is passive in its reception (*peplērōmenē*); believers do not generate this fullness from self-effort, but receive it as a work of God through the Holy Spirit and Christian fellowship.

  • Eschatological Foretaste: A heart filled with complete joy provides a present foretaste of the final, unhindered presence of God in glory.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 12, John balances the necessity of written scripture ("paper and ink") with the spiritual superiority of personal presence ("speak face to face").

  • The phrase translated "face to face" literally reads "mouth to mouth" (*stoma pros stoma*), emphasizing intimate, unhindered, personal interaction.

  • Paper (papyrus) and ink provided a vital medium for guarding truth against deceivers, yet cold ink on parchment could not fully substitute for the warmth of direct communion.

  • The dynamic interaction of believers sharing truth together in person expands the capacity of the soul, allowing joy to be filled to the brim.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 John 1:4 - "And these things we write to you that your joy may be full."

  • John 15:11 - "These things I have spoken to you, that My joy may remain in you, and that your joy may be full."

  • John 16:24 - "Until now you have asked nothing in My name. Ask, and you will receive, that your joy may be full."

  • Romans 15:13 - "Now may the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in believing, that you may abound in hope by the power of the Holy Spirit."

  • Ephesians 5:18 - "And do not be drunk with wine, in which is dissipation; but be filled with the Spirit,"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The combination of *chara* (joy) and *plēroō* (fill) forms a signature Johannine phrase, occurring in the Gospel of John as well as 1 and 2 John.

  • True spiritual joy differs sharply from worldly happiness; worldly happiness depends on favorable external circumstances, whereas gospel joy is an abiding fullness grounded in truth and personal Christian communion.

  • Pastoral applications highlight the danger of isolated or purely remote Christianity; physical gathering and face-to-face fellowship are essential instruments God uses to complete the joy of His church.
