TRUTH - ( aletheia , Strong's G225 )

Summary:

The Greek word *aletheia* conveys that which is objective, unhidden, dependable, and real, standing in stark contrast to falsehood, illusion, and deception. In 2 John 2, *aletheia* refers not merely to an abstract philosophical concept or a set of propositional doctrines, but to divine reality embodied in Jesus Christ and communicated through the Gospel. This truth is an enduring, living reality that indwells believers through the Holy Spirit and binds the Christian community together eternally.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • Etymologically formed from the negative prefix *a-* ("not") and *lanthano* or *lethe* ("to hide, conceal, or forget"), *aletheia* fundamentally carries the sense of "unhiddenness," "disclosure," or "that which is brought to light."

  • In classical Greek thought, *aletheia* denoted objective reality as opposed to mere appearance, human opinion, or illusion.

  • In the Septuagint (LXX), *aletheia* was chosen to translate the Hebrew noun *emet*, which carries the profound weight of firmness, constancy, reliability, and covenantal faithfulness.

  • In Johannine literature, *aletheia* synthesizes objective historical fact, ultimate spiritual reality, and God's covenantal faithfulness revealed supremely in the person and work of Jesus Christ.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The state or quality of being true, genuine, real, or factual, as opposed to counterfeit or false.

  • Truth in opposition to hypocrisy, deception, or moral compromise.

  • The absolute divine reality revealed by God regarding Himself, salvation, and human redemption through Jesus Christ—the core content of the Gospel.

  • Personal integrity, sincerity, and alignment of thought, word, and deed with God's revealed reality.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • *Aletheia* as a Person: Jesus explicitly identifies Himself with truth ("I am the way, the truth, and the life"), establishing that ultimate reality is personal rather than merely abstract.

  • *Aletheia* as an Indwelling Resident: In 2 John 2, truth is portrayed as an active, abiding force within the believer using the verb *meno* ("to remain, abide, reside"). Truth does not merely visit; it takes up permanent residence in the redeemed soul.

  • *Aletheia* as a Liberating Force: Truth breaks spiritual deception and releases believers from the bondage of sin and error (John 8:32).

  • *Aletheia* as a Sphere of Existence: Believers are instructed to walk "in truth," picturing an entire environment and manner of life shaped by God's revealed Word.

4. Theological Implications

  • Divine Origin and Trinitarian Reality: God is the God of truth, Jesus Christ is the embodiment of truth, and the Holy Spirit is the Spirit of Truth who guides believers into all truth.

  • Objective and Immutability: Divine truth is absolute, non-negotiable, and unalterable by cultural shifts, subjective feelings, or human philosophy.

  • The Essential Foundation for Love: Genuine Christian love (*agape*) cannot exist apart from truth. Truth establishes the boundaries, character, and integrity of Christian love.

  • Sanctifying Power: Truth is the primary instrument by which God cleanses, sets apart, and transforms the mind and character of the believer (John 17:17).

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 2, John addresses "the elect lady and her children," whom he loves "in truth," establishing that gospel truth is the foundational ground of all Christian relationship and affection.

  • The immediate setting involves the threat of deceivers and antichrists who denied the incarnational reality of Jesus Christ (2 John 7). *Aletheia* functions as the spiritual defense against heresy.

  • The phrase "which abides in us" highlights the continuous internal presence of God's revealed truth, sealed by the Holy Spirit within the body of believers.

  • The phrase "and will be with us forever" provides eternal assurance that God's truth is indissoluble and will never fail, fade, or be overcome by falsehood.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 8:32 – "And you shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free."

  • John 14:6 – "Jesus said to him, 'I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through Me.'"

  • John 17:17 – "Sanctify them by Your truth. Your word is truth."

  • 1 Timothy 2:4 – "who desires all men to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth."

  • 3 John 1:4 – "I have no greater joy than to hear that my children walk in truth."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Johannine theology rejects any separation between belief and practice; possessing the truth inevitably leads to "doing the truth" and "walking in truth."

  • The vital equilibrium of 2 John balances truth and love; truth without love becomes cold orthodoxy, while love without truth degenerates into sentimental compromise.
