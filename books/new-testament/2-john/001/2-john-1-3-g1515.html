PEACE - ( eirēnē , Strong's G1515 )

Summary:

The Greek word *eirēnē* denotes a state of wholeness, harmony, tranquility, and spiritual order resulting from reconciliation with God. In classical Greek context, the term primarily described the absence of war or social conflict, but the New Testament infuses it with the rich Hebrew concept of *shalom*—completeness, soundness, safety, and total relational well-being. In apostolic greetings such as 2 John 1:3, peace is not a mere emotional feeling or circumstantial quietness; it is an objective spiritual reality gifted by God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. Flowing directly from divine grace and mercy, this peace provides believers with inner stability, assurance, and unshakable quietness of soul even amidst surrounding deception and spiritual conflict.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • In ancient Greek culture, *eirēnē* referred negatively to the intermission or absence of military warfare and civil strife.

  • When the Hebrew Scriptures were translated into the Septuagint, *eirēnē* was chosen to translate the Hebrew word *shalom*.

  • This translation expanded the word's meaning beyond the passive absence of conflict to a positive condition of wholeness, spiritual health, prosperity, and covenantal harmony established by God.

  • The root of *eirēnē* links conceptually to the idea of joining together that which was broken or separated, forming a complete and unified whole.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • A state of spiritual quietness and tranquility of soul rooted in divine favor.

  • Harmonious relationship between human beings and God, established through the sacrificial reconciliation of Jesus Christ.

  • Exemption from the rage and havoc of war, expanding spiritually to safety and security under divine protection.

  • Internal harmony and order within the Christian church, free from strife, division, and factionalism.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • A bound wholeness: The concept pictures separate or fractured pieces brought back together into a cohesive, restored condition. Sin fractures human existence, but God's peace binds the soul back into proper alignment with Him.

  • A guarding garrison: The word functions as a military sentinel stationed over the heart and mind to keep out destructive anxiety, fear, and panic.

  • An officiating umpire: The term carries the sense of a inner governing authority that decides issues in the heart, guiding believers into harmonious communal living.

4. Theological Implications

  • Objective foundation before subjective experience: Peace with God (*eirēnē*) secured through justification precedes the internal peace of God experienced within the human heart.

  • Divine source: Peace originates from God the Father as the ultimate Author and is mediated through Jesus Christ, the Prince of Peace, who made peace through the blood of His cross.

  • Supernatural fruit: True biblical peace is not a product of human willpower, Stoic detachment, or favorable external circumstances, but a fruit produced within the believer by the Holy Spirit.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:3, *eirēnē* appears as the final element in the apostolic greeting triad: grace (*charis*), mercy (*eleos*), and peace (*eirēnē*).

  • A logical progression governs this sequence: grace is God's unmerited favor, mercy is His compassionate relief for human misery, and peace is the resulting state of spiritual wholeness and quietness.

  • John declares that this peace "will be with you," framing it as an absolute guarantee rather than a mere wish or prayer.

  • Writing to a congregation facing false teachers and deceivers, John emphasizes that divine peace provides spiritual stability and unshakable assurance when believers abide strictly in truth and love.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 2:14 – "Glory to God in the highest, / And on earth peace, goodwill toward men!"

  • John 14:27 – "Peace I leave with you, My peace I give to you; not as the world gives do I give to you. Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid."

  • Romans 5:1 – "Therefore, having been justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ,"

  • Philippians 4:7 – "and the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus."

  • Colossians 3:15 – "And let the peace of God rule in your hearts, to which also you were called in one body; and be thankful."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Contrast with worldly peace: Secular peace (*Pax Romana*) relied on military subjugation and fragile political treaties. Christ's peace is independent of external circumstances and endures during severe persecution and tribulation.

  • Essential link to truth and love: In 2 John 1:3, peace operates exclusively "in truth and love." Biblical peace never demands the compromise of divine truth, nor can it exist where genuine Christian love is absent.
