TRUTH - ( aletheia , Strong's G225 )

Summary:

In the New Testament, *aletheia* refers to divine reality as disclosed by God—absolute objective reality as opposed to mere appearance, deception, or falsehood. In Johannine literature, truth is not merely an abstract philosophy or a set of dry propositions; it is personified in Jesus Christ and expressed through the reality of the Gospel. In 2 John 1:3, "truth" serves as the vital climate and sphere within which divine grace, mercy, and peace are experienced, existing in an inseparable, balanced harmony with Christian love.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • Root Meaning: The Greek noun *aletheia* derives from the privative prefix *a-* ("not") and *lanthano* or *lethe* ("hidden" or "concealed"). Rooted in this etymology, the word carries the underlying graphic picture of unhiddenness, unconcealed reality, or truth laid bare to view.

  • Old Testament Background: In the Septuagint (LXX), *aletheia* frequently translates the Hebrew word *emet*. While the classical Greek emphasis focused primarily on intellectual or philosophical reality versus appearance, the biblical usage absorbs the rich Old Testament concept of *emet*—faithfulness, reliability, stability, and enduring trustworthiness. Truth in Scripture is therefore both factually accurate and relationally dependable.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Objective Reality: Truth is that which corresponds to actual reality as defined by God, standing in absolute opposition to falsehood, deception, or illusion (*pseudos*).

  • Revealed Divine Reality: It denotes divine revelation—the ultimate reality of God, His nature, His will, and His redemptive plan made known to humanity.

  • Moral Sincerity and Integrity: It refers to truthfulness in speech, uprightness of mind, and sincerity of character, free from hypocrisy or deceit.

  • The Content of the Gospel: It frequently functions as a technical term for the body of Christian doctrine, the message of salvation through Jesus Christ.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Sphere of Existence: The New Testament often uses the prepositional phrase "in truth" (*en aletheia*) to describe the environment or atmosphere in which a Christian lives, loves, worships, and walks.

  • The Person of Christ: Jesus does not merely teach truth; He is the incarnation of truth. In Him, the ultimate reality of God is fully manifested.

  • The Armor of the Believer: Truth is pictured as a belt buckled around the waist (Ephesians 6:14), providing stability, strength, and readiness for spiritual warfare.

  • Light Uncovering Darkness: Truth operates like a light that penetrates darkness, exposing lies and setting captives free from moral and spiritual bondage.

4. Theological Implications

  • Triune Source of Truth: God is the God of truth, Jesus Christ is truth incarnate, and the Holy Spirit is the Spirit of truth who guides believers into all truth. Truth is grounded in the unchanging character of God.

  • Absolute and Unchanging: Truth is objective, universal, and non-negotiable. It is not created by human consensus, nor does it shift with cultural trends.

  • Indispensable Partner to Love: Truth and love are inseparable in biblical theology. Truth without love leads to cold, abrasive orthodoxy; love without truth degenerates into sentimental compromise. Truth gives love its integrity, while love gives truth its warmth and witness.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • Atmosphere of Divine Blessing: In 2 John 1:3, John extends an apostolic blessing: "Grace, mercy, and peace will be with you... in truth and love." The prepositional phrase "in truth and love" defines the essential environment within which God's grace, mercy, and peace are truly known and enjoyed.

  • Safeguard Against Deception: Second John was written to a local church threatened by itinerant false teachers who denied the true incarnation of Jesus Christ. Emphasizing "truth" at the very outset safeguards the church against heresy, establishing that genuine Christian fellowship cannot exist apart from apostolic doctrine.

  • Balanced Christian Life: John couples "truth" directly with "love." In the context of false teachers, believers were called to maintain absolute doctrinal fidelity without abandoning genuine Christian love for one another.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 1:14 — "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we beheld His glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth."

  • John 8:32 — "And you shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free."

  • John 14:6 — "Jesus said to him, 'I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through Me.'"

  • John 17:17 — "Sanctify them by Your truth. Your word is truth."

  • 1 Timothy 3:15 — "but if I am delayed, I write so that you may know how you ought to conduct yourself in the house of God, which is the church of the living God, the pillar and ground of the truth."

  • 3 John 1:4 — "I have no greater joy than to hear that my children walk in truth."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Truth as an Active Walk: Scripturally, truth is not merely something to be believed or analyzed intellectually; it must be "done" (John 3:21) and "walked in" (2 John 1:4). Orthodoxy (right belief) must naturally issue in orthopraxy (right living).

  • Pastoral Application: When ministering to a congregation, emphasize that divine grace, mercy, and peace do not operate in a vacuum of subjective feelings. They are anchored in the objective truth of who God is and what Christ accomplished. Pastoral wisdom requires confronting error with truth while shepherding souls in love.
