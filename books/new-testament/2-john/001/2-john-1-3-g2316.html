GOD - ( theos , Strong's G2316 )

Summary:

In the New Testament, *theos* refers primarily to the one true, supreme Creator and God of Israel, uniquely revealed as Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. In 2 John 1:3, *theos* is modified by "the Father" (*patros*), explicitly linking God's absolute deity and supreme authority with His relational, gracious Fatherhood toward believers. Coupled alongside "the Lord Jesus Christ," this designation highlights both the divine equality of origin for apostolic blessings—grace, mercy, and peace—and the distinct personal relationships within the Godhead.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • In classical Greek literature, *theos* was a generic term used for any deity, cosmic power, or divine being within the pagan pantheon.

  • The Septuagint (LXX) radically redefined *theos* by using it to translate the Hebrew divine names *Elohim* and *YHWH*, infusing the Greek word with strict, absolute monotheism.

  • In the New Testament, *theos* abandons pagan polytheism entirely, representing the self-revealing, holy, sovereign God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, who is fully and finally revealed in Jesus Christ.

  • When paired with "Father" (*pater*), *theos* transitions from an abstract cosmic concept to the personal, covenant-keeping God who adopts redeemed sinners into His family.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • *Theos* denotes the supreme, eternal Deity, the Creator and Sustainer of all reality, possessing absolute sovereign authority, holiness, and power.

  • Primary semantic dimensions of *theos* in the New Testament:

  • The Godhead in absolute contrast to idols, false gods, or created beings.

  • God the Father specifically, as distinct from the Son and the Spirit within Trinitarian contexts.

  • Jesus Christ Himself as fully divine in nature and essence (e.g., John 1:1, John 20:28, Titus 2:13).

  • In 2 John 1:3, *theos* functions in the genitive case of source or origin ("from God the Father"), designating God as the ultimate fountainhead from which divine blessings flow.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • *Theos* appears over 1,300 times in the New Testament, functioning as the foundational theological term of apostolic teaching.

  • The Fountainhead of Blessing: *Theos* is consistently portrayed as the ultimate giver of life, righteousness, salvation, peace, and grace.

  • The Covenant Father: Frequently expressed as *theos pater* ("God the Father"), combining supreme authority with intimate, protective, and eternal love for His children.

  • Co-Equal Source with Christ: In apostolic greetings, *theos* is regularly joined with *kyrios* (Lord Jesus Christ), placing the Father and the Son together as the single divine source of spiritual gifts.

4. Theological Implications

  • Trinitarian Equality and Distinction: The joint invocation of "God the Father" and "the Lord Jesus Christ" in 2 John 1:3 demonstrates equality of divine essence and source while maintaining distinct personal identities.

  • Relational Sovereign: God is not a distant, unfeeling monarch; He is "the Father" who bestows grace, mercy, and peace upon His church.

  • Sovereign Initiator: Spiritual blessings do not originate from human effort or religious ritual, but proceed directly from the person and will of God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:3, the apostle John extends a confident tri-fold greeting: "Grace, mercy, and peace will be with you."

  • The origin of this blessing is explicitly rooted "from God the Father and from the Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of the Father."

  • John emphasizes "God the Father" to combat early Docetic and proto-Gnostic errors that sought to separate the true God from the historical human Jesus or deny Christ's eternal Sonship.

  • The phrase "in truth and love" forms the sphere in which God's grace, mercy, and peace operate, ensuring that true knowledge of God always results in active Christian fellowship and obedience.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 1:1: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God."

  • Romans 1:7: "To all who are in Rome, beloved of God, called to be saints: Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ."

  • 1 Corinthians 8:6: "yet for us there is one God, the Father, of whom are all things, and we for Him; and one Lord Jesus Christ, through whom are all things, and through whom we live."

  • 1 John 4:8: "He who does not love does not know God, for God is love."

  • Titus 2:13: "looking for the blessed hope and glorious appearing of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ,"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Pastoral Application: Grace, mercy, and peace are not mere subjective feelings or human wishful thinking; they are real, objective spiritual realities coming directly from God the Father.

  • Trinitarian Safeguard: True Christian faith requires confessing God as Father specifically in relation to Jesus Christ as Son. Distorting the Sonship of Christ inevitably distorts the biblical identity of God the Father.
