LOVE - ( agape , Strong's G26 )

Summary:

In 2 John 1:3, *agape* refers to the self-sacrificing, unconditional, and deliberate goodwill that originates in God and flows through believers. Linked directly with divine truth, this love is not a fluid emotion or mere sentiment, but a conscious commitment to seek the highest spiritual good of others in alignment with God's revealed character and commandments.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • In ancient classical Greek, *agape* and its associated verb *agapao* were used infrequently compared to *eros* (romantic or passionate love), *philia* (friendship and affection), or *storge* (family devotion). Classical literature often treated *agape* as a generic preference or mild fondness.

  • The writers of the New Testament elevated *agape* to express a unique, divine concept of love unrecognized in pagan philosophy—a sacrificial, covenantal devotion that acts unconditionally for the good of the recipient regardless of their perceived worth or merit.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • *Agape* signifies benevolent affection, active goodwill, and self-sacrificing devotion.

  • It is a deliberate choice of the will driven by the character of the one who loves, rather than an impulsive emotional reaction triggered by the attractive qualities of the object being loved.

  • It describes the very essence of God's posture toward humanity and serves as the highest moral standard for Christian conduct.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Sacrificial devotion: Expressed supreme action when God gave His only begotten Son for sinners while they were still helpless and ungodly.

  • The binding virtue: Pictured as a garment or a belt that holds the spiritual life together, serving as the unifying bond of perfection across the body of Christ.

  • Active obedience: Demonstrated not through dynamic feelings, but through tangible service, keeping divine commandments, and seeking the spiritual maturity of fellow believers.

4. Theological Implications

  • *Agape* finds its ultimate source in the Triune God, for God does not merely possess love, but is Himself love in His fundamental character.

  • It is a supernatural fruit produced in believers by the Holy Spirit, making it impossible to generate through unregenerate human effort or moral willpower.

  • It provides the essential environment in which all other divine blessings—such as grace, mercy, and peace—are experienced, sustained, and expressed within the Christian community.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:3, *agape* is paired directly with *aletheia* (truth) to define the sphere in which grace, mercy, and peace operate among God's people.

  • John links truth and love to guard against two destructive spiritual extremes: truth without love degrades into cold legalism, while love without truth dissolves into sentimental compromise and heresy.

  • In the immediate context of false teachers invading the church, *agape* anchors the apostolic greeting, assuring believers that genuine Christian love protects the flock by holding firmly to the truth of Jesus Christ.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 15:13: "Greater love has no one than this, than to lay down one's life for his friends."

  • 1 Corinthians 13:4: "Love suffers long and is kind; love does not envy; love does not parade itself, is not puffed up;"

  • Colossians 3:14: "But above all these things put on love, which is the bond of perfection."

  • 1 John 4:8: "He who does not love does not know God, for God is love."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Pastoral Application: Preachers must remind congregations that biblical *agape* is an act of obedience and sacrificial service, not a passive emotional state. Believers are commanded to love even their enemies, which is only possible because divine love has been poured into their hearts.

  • The Guardrail of Truth: Loving others biblical requires warning them against false doctrine. True *agape* never setting aside sound theology for the sake of artificial unity.
