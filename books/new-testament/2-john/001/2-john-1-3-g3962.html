FATHER - ( pater , Strong's G3962 )

Summary:

In the New Testament, *pater* refers literally to a male parent or ancestor, but its richest theological weight describes God as the First Person of the Trinity. God is the sovereign Creator, loving Sustainer, and eternal Father of the Lord Jesus Christ, as well as the spiritual Father of all who are adopted into His family through faith. In 2 John 1:3, *pater* functions as the divine source of grace, mercy, and peace, anchoring these covenantal blessings in the eternal relationship between God the Father and His Son, Jesus Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near East and the Greco-Roman world, the father (*pater*) held absolute authority over the household as the head of the family unit. The concept of the father involved far more than biological generation; it encompassed supreme authority, protection, provision, and the preservation of the family lineage and covenantal heritage. In Hebrew thought, God's revelation of Himself as Father underscored His compassionate care, faithful covenant bond, and rightful demand for obedience from His children. The New Testament elevates this concept by revealing the eternal, internal relationship within the Godhead, where God is uniquely the Father of the eternal Son, and through Christ, becomes the tender, approachable Father of believers.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

*Pater* carries a range of interconnected meanings across its New Testament occurrences:

  • The immediate male progenitor or biological father of an individual.

  • Ancestors, forefathers, or patriarchs of a nation or group.

  • A leader, teacher, or spiritual mentor who exercises wise guidance and nurturing authority over others.

  • God as the Creator, Sovereign Ruler, and ultimate source of all created life.

  • God as the Father of believers, who bestows spiritual life, adoption, inheritance, and holy discipline.

  • God as the eternal Father of the Lord Jesus Christ, expressing the intimate, co-equal relationship within the Holy Trinity.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Household Head and Provider: *Pater* pictures the ultimate head of the divine family who knows the daily needs of His children, provides their daily bread, and protects them from spiritual danger.

  • The Fountainhead of Blessing: The term presents God as the uncaused cause and original source from which all heavenly blessings—such as light, life, grace, mercy, and peace—ceaselessly flow.

  • The Wise Disciplinarian: *Pater* depicts a loving parent who trains, corrects, and shapes His children in righteousness so that they may share in His holiness.

  • The Adoptive Redeemer: In gospel usage, the Father welcomes former enemies into His house, granting them full legal rights as heirs of His eternal kingdom.

4. Theological Implications

  • Trinitarian Order: The term *pater* establishes the distinct personal identity of the First Person of the Trinity. The Father is unbegotten, eternally loving the Son, and sending the Son and the Spirit into the world for redemption.

  • Essential Divinity of Christ: By identifying Jesus as the Son of the Father, Scripture affirms that Jesus possesses the identical divine nature as the Father. They share equal essence, majesty, and power.

  • Divine Adoption: Through union with Christ, believers cease to be spiritual orphans. God bestows upon them the Spirit of adoption, enabling them to call upon the Sovereign God with childlike trust and intimacy.

  • Covenantal Security: God’s fatherhood guarantees eternal security for His children. A faithful Father does not discard His redeemed sons and daughters; His loving care endures forever.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 2 John 1:3, the apostle John writes: "Grace, mercy, and peace will be with you from God the Father and from the Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of the Father, in truth and love."

  • Dual Source of Blessing: John explicitly joins "God the Father" with "the Lord Jesus Christ," placing both on the exact same divine level as the co-equal sources of grace, mercy, and peace.

  • Repeated Emphasis: John uses the title twice in a single verse ("God the Father" and "the Son of the Father"). This intentional repetition counters early gnostic errors that attempted to separate the historical Jesus from the divine Son or split the God of the Old Testament from the Father of Christ.

  • Relational Foundation: The blessings of grace, mercy, and peace do not exist in an abstract vacuum; they originate within the eternal, loving relationship between the Father and the Son, and are communicated to believers who walk in truth and love.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 6:9 — "In this manner, therefore, pray: Our Father in heaven, Hallowed be Your name."

  • John 14:6 — "Jesus said to him, 'I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through Me.'"

  • Romans 8:15 — "For you did not receive the spirit of bondage again to fear, but you received the Spirit of adoption by whom we cry out, 'Abba, Father.'"

  • Ephesians 4:6 — "one God and Father of all, who is above all, and through all, and in you all."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Earthly vs. Heavenly Fatherhood: Human fatherhood is a reflected shadow intended to point to the perfection of divine fatherhood. Where human fathers fall short, God the Father remains perfectly wise, holy, loving, and present.

  • The Intimacy of *Abba*: When the Greek *pater* is paired with the Aramaic *Abba* in the New Testament, it captures the highest degree of warm, respectful, childlike intimacy, showing that the transcendent God of the universe is tenderly accessible to His children.
