GRACE - ( charis , Strong's G5485 )

Summary:

In the New Testament, *charis* denotes God's unmerited favor, sovereign goodwill, and transformative divine influence upon the human heart. In 2 John 1:3, grace is presented not merely as an initial event at salvation, but as an ongoing, pledged blessing flowing continuously from God the Father and Jesus Christ. This grace sustains, equips, and strengthens believers to remain steadfast in truth and active in love amid spiritual opposition.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • In ancient classical Greek, *charis* referred to charm, beauty, or a favorable disposition that brings joy and delight. It described an act of generosity or a favor extended freely to bring pleasure to another.

  • In the Septuagint, *charis* standardly translates the Hebrew noun *chen*, which signifies favor, acceptance, or graciousness found in the eyes of a superior.

  • The New Testament elevates this concept from cultural favor or aesthetic pleasantness to a foundational gospel doctrine: God's completely unearned, sovereign favor bestowed upon undeserving sinners through Jesus Christ.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Primary Meaning: Free, unmerited favor and active goodwill extended by God to humanity, independent of human work, worth, or merit.

  • Functional Meaning: The dynamic power of God operating within a believer, granting spiritual ability, strength, and sanctifying transformation.

  • Reflective Response: The internal gratitude, joy, and praise generated in the heart of the recipient upon receiving divine favor.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Grace as an Unearned Gift: Divine favor stands in absolute opposition to debt, earnings, or merit. It is imparted purely through God's rich benevolence.

  • Grace as a Realm: Believers do not merely receive grace at conversion; they are introduced into a permanent sphere or state of grace in which they live and stand securely.

  • Grace as Empowering Strength: Beyond initial pardon, grace functions as divine energy that strengthens believers for trials, endurance, and Christian service.

  • Grace as an Apostolic Greeting: Used consistently in apostolic benedictions, grace is invoked as a dynamic, present reality that bestows divine favor and divine power upon the church.

4. Theological Implications

  • Monergistic Origin of Salvation: Salvation is entirely initiated and accomplished by God's grace, leaving no room for human boasting or self-righteousness.

  • Continuous Agent of Sanctification: Grace does not encourage spiritual negligence; rather, it actively trains and empowers believers to reject ungodliness and live righteously in the present age.

  • Trinitarian Distribution: Grace originates in the love of God the Father and is mediated exclusively to humanity through the person and finished work of Jesus Christ, the Son.

  • Harmony with Truth and Love: Authentic divine grace never operates in isolation; it functions within the boundaries of divine truth and produces genuine Christian love.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:3, John expands the standard ancient greeting into a confident, declarative promise: "Grace, mercy, and peace will be with you."

  • The use of the future indicative verb emphasizes an assured, permanent supply of divine favor for believers holding fast to sound doctrine against deceivers.

  • *Charis* (grace) precedes *eleos* (mercy) and *eirene* (peace) in logical sequence: grace addresses man's guilt, mercy addresses man's misery, and peace addresses man's separation from God.

  • John specifically locates the operation of grace "in truth and love," demonstrating that true divine favor preserves believers in doctrinal orthodoxy while cultivating sacrificial love for one another.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 1:14: "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we beheld His glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth."

  • Romans 3:24: "being justified freely by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus,"

  • 2 Corinthians 12:9: "And He said to me, 'My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness.'"

  • Ephesians 2:8: "For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God,"

  • Titus 2:11: "For the grace of God that brings salvation has appeared to all men,"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Sustaining Grace for Daily Living: Believers often view grace exclusively through the lens of justification. 2 John 1:3 highlights grace as a daily, pledged resource necessary for persevering in the faith.

  • Safeguard Against Extremes: The contextual pairing of grace with truth guards against both legalism (which abandons grace) and licentiousness (which abandons truth).

  • Encouragement for Ministry Leaders: Pastoral service rests entirely upon the continuous supply of Christ's grace rather than human eloquence, wisdom, or effort.
