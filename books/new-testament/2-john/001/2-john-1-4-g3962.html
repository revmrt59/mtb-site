FATHER - ( pater , Strong's G3962 )

Summary:

The Greek word *pater* refers fundamentally to a generator, biological ancestor, or male parent. Throughout the New Testament, however, its most profound usage describes God in His relation to Jesus Christ and to believers. In 2 John 1:4, *pater* designates God the Father as the supreme authority and source of divine truth. Walking in truth is not merely adherence to abstract legalism, but a joyful, filial response to the binding commandment issued directly by the loving and sovereign Father.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the Greco-Roman world, the *paterfamilias* exercised absolute legal authority over the household, serving as its protector, provider, and supreme ruler. Ancient Jewish culture similarly viewed the father as the head of the covenant family, tasked with preserving truth, imparting instructions, and passing down the knowledge of God to future generations. The New Testament elevates this concept to its highest spiritual expression: God is not an distant monarch or abstract force, but a personal, holy Father who lovingly governs His household, instructs His children, and demands allegiance rooted in relational love.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term *pater* encompasses several distinct levels of meaning depending on context:

  • Biological ancestor or immediate male parent.

  • Forefather or patriarch of a nation, lineage, or tribe.

  • Spiritual mentor, founder, or teacher who nurtures spiritual offspring.

  • Originator, creator, or author of a practice, trait, or entity.

  • God the Father, the First Person of the Trinity—the ultimate source of life, creation, revelation, and divine commandment.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout Scripture, *pater* functions in three primary spheres:

  • Earthly Relationships: It describes biological lineage, such as Abraham as the father of Israel, or human parents who discipline and provide for their households.

  • Spiritual Metaphor: It denotes spiritual mentorship, where elders or apostles care for converts as their spiritual children.

  • Divine Revelation: It reveals the eternal relationship within the Godhead—God as the Father of the Lord Jesus Christ—and God's covenantal adoption of believers who are born of the Spirit.

4. Theological Implications

The revelation of God as *pater* carries deep Trinitarian and soteriological significance:

  • Eternal Relationship: Before creation, God existed eternally as Father in intimate fellowship with the Son.

  • Adoption and Assurance: Redemption secures divine adoption. Believers no longer relate to God as terrified slaves under condemnation, but as beloved children who possess full inheritance rights.

  • Source of Authority: Because God is Father, His directives carry absolute, loving authority. Divine commandments are given not to restrict freedom arbitrarily, but to direct His children in the path of life and holiness.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 2 John 1:4, the apostle John writes, "I rejoiced greatly that I have found some of your children walking in truth, as we received commandment from the Father."

  • Divine Mandate: The phrase "commandment from the Father" grounds the Christian imperative to walk in truth directly in God's authority. Truth is not defined by culture or personal preference, but by the explicit order of the Father.

  • Relational Obedience: John highlights that walking in truth is the natural behavior of true children of God. Obedience is presented not as a burden, but as a joy that reflects the moral character of the Father who commanded it.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 6:9 - "In this manner, therefore, pray: Our Father in heaven, Hallowed be Your name."

  • John 14:6 - "Jesus said to him, 'I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through Me.'"

  • Romans 8:15 - "For you did not receive the spirit of bondage again to fear, but you received the Spirit of adoption by whom we cry out, 'Abba, Father.'"

  • 1 John 3:1 - "Behold what manner of love the Father has bestowed on us, that we should be called children of God!"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Unity of Truth and Love: In John's epistles, truth and love are inseparable realities issuing from the Father. To honor the Father is to embrace both His truth in doctrine and His love in practice.

  • Pastoral Comfort: Understanding God as *pater* assures believers that the commandments of God are given by One who cares perfectly for their ultimate good, protection, and spiritual maturity.
