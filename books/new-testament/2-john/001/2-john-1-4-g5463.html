REJOICED - ( chairo, Strong's G5463 )

Summary:

The verb *chairo* expresses a deep, abiding gladness and delight. In 2 John 1:4, the apostle John uses this word to describe the profound spiritual joy he experienced upon discovering that members of this Christian community were living in accordance with God's truth. This is not a superficial, circumstantial happiness, but a holy joy that arises when the truth of the gospel is actively lived out in the lives of believers.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The verb *chairo* is etymologically linked to the noun *charis* (grace) and *chara* (joy). The linguistic relationship suggests that joy is the natural, inward response to experiencing grace. In secular Greek culture, *chairo* was a standard greeting, meaning "hail" or "greetings," wishing well-being upon another. In the New Testament, however, the word is elevated from a polite social convention into a profound theological term, signifying a deep-seated spiritual gladness that persists regardless of external circumstances.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meaning of *chairo* is to rejoice, be glad, or experience a state of joy. Lexical entries define it as being in a state of happiness or well-being. In the passive voice form used in 2 John 1:4 (*echaren*), the word carries the nuance of being made glad or being filled with joy by an external cause—specifically, the good news of the spiritual fidelity of the church.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the New Testament, *chairo* paints a word picture of a heart illuminated and warmed by divine reality. It is the joy of the shepherds seeing the Christ child, the joy of the father welcoming home the prodigal son, and the joy of the disciples realizing Jesus had risen from the dead. Because it shares a root with *charis* (grace), the word pictures a joy that is not manufactured by human effort, but is a gift received. It represents the emotional fruit of recognized grace.

4. Theological Implications

Biblical joy is deeply theological, tied directly to truth, obedience, and fellowship with God. It serves as an indicator of spiritual health. John's joy is not triggered by material prosperity or social status, but by spiritual reality. This demonstrates that for the believer, the ultimate source of gladness must be the advancement of God's truth. True joy is corporate; the spiritual success of one member brings joy to the whole body of Christ.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 2 John 1:4, John writes, "I rejoiced greatly." The addition of the adverb "greatly" (*lian*) intensifies the verb, indicating an overwhelming, exuberant gladness. John is writing to a church threatened by deceivers and antichrists who deny the incarnation (2 John 1:7). Against this dark backdrop of theological drift, finding believers who are "walking in truth" brings immense relief and joy to the apostle's heart. It reassures him that the gospel is bearing fruit and that God's people are standing firm against error.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 2:10: "When they saw the star, they rejoiced with exceedingly great joy."

  • Luke 15:5: "And when he has found it, he lays it on his shoulders, rejoicing."

  • Philippians 4:4: "Rejoice in the Lord always. Again I will say, rejoice!"

  • 3 John 1:3: "For I rejoiced greatly when brethren came and testified of the truth *that is* in you, just as you walk in the truth."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The passive voice of the verb in this verse (*echaren*) indicates that John did not actively summon this joy from his own willpower; rather, the joy was produced in him as a reaction to the evidence of grace in others. This teaches pastors and church leaders that spiritual encouragement is a reciprocal gift. Watching believers walk in the truth is a primary means by which God refreshes and restores the souls of His ministers.
