LOVE - ( agapao , Strong's G154 )

Summary:

The Greek verb *agapao* describes an unselfish, purposeful, and unconditional commitment to seek the ultimate good of another person. Unlike affection based purely on natural attraction or mutual benefit, this love stems from a conscious decision of the will aligned with God's moral character. In 2 John 1:5, the apostle John reminds believers that loving one another is not a novel requirement, but the fundamental, permanent mandate of the Christian life, inseparable from walking in divine truth.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • While secular Greek prioritized feelings of personal affection, family warmth, or physical passion, the New Testament writers elevated *agapao* to describe the distinct, sacrificial love displayed by God toward humanity.

  • In classical literature, *agapao* was a generic verb often used to express preference or contentment. Under apostolic usage, the word came to convey a deliberate, sacrificial benevolence that acts regardless of whether the recipient merits such kindness.

  • The verb represents a deep, purposeful commitment that issues directly in visible, practical action rather than remaining a mere internal emotional state.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • *Agapao* signifies actively valuing, caring for, demonstrating benevolence toward, and seeking the total well-being of another.

  • The term emphasizes deliberate intention, moral choice, and faithful devotion rather than impulsive emotional attraction.

  • The semantic range encompasses God’s supreme love for humanity, the believer’s reciprocal devotion to God, and the selfless care Christians are commanded to show to one another and to their neighbors.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Volitional Commitment: The term focuses on an act of the mind and will. It is commanded throughout Scripture precisely because it is governed by intentional spiritual choice rather than unpredictable human feeling.

  • Bound to Truth: Biblically, this love does not operate in a moral or doctrinal vacuum. It flourishes exclusively within the boundary of divine truth and righteous living.

  • Sacrificial Action: The highest expression of *agapao* is God’s self-giving act in sending Jesus Christ to die for sinners, establishing sacrificial giving as the primary hallmark of genuine love.

4. Theological Implications

  • Love originates in the core nature and character of God, who is Himself the source and definition of love.

  • Genuine love among believers serves as the primary visible evidence of regenerate life and true Christian discipleship.

  • New Testament love fulfills the moral intent of God's law, directing all human interactions toward holy living, spiritual protection, and selfless service.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:5, the apostle John writes to "the elect lady and her children," pleading for the persistent practice of brotherly love.

  • John explicitly notes that this commandment is not "new" in terms of substance or origin; it is the foundational message heard "from the beginning" of the Gospel proclamation.

  • In the broader context of 2 John, this call to love is tightly integrated with guarding against false teachers. True Christian love does not compromise essential doctrine or welcome spiritual deception; rather, loving one another requires walking in truth and obeying God's commandments.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 3:16 - "For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son, that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have everlasting life."

  • John 13:34 - "A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another; as I have loved you, that you also love one another."

  • John 14:15 - "If you love Me, keep My commandments."

  • 1 John 4:11 - "Beloved, if God so loved us, we also ought to love one another."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Biblical love (*agapao*) must never be confused with sentimental compromise or passive tolerance. In 2 John, love and truth (*aletheia*) are inseparable twin pillars of Christian fellowship.

  • To love fellow believers practically involves active spiritual care, mutual encouragement, doctrinal fidelity, and protecting the flock from harmful error.
