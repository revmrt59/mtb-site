LADY - ( kyria , Strong's G2959 )

Summary:

The Greek word *kyria* is the feminine counterpart to *kyrios* ("lord" or "master"), denoting a female owner, mistress, noblewoman, or respected lady. In the New Testament, it occurs specifically in 2 John (verses 1 and 5), addressed to the "elect lady" (*eklektē kyria*). The term expresses honor, affection, and spiritual dignity. Whether addressing an esteemed Christian woman of influence who hosted a house church or functioning as a personification of a local church assembly, the word underscores a covenantal relationship governed by divine truth and Christian love.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word *kyria* is derived from *kyrios*, which signifies supreme authority, ownership, or mastership. In classical Greek and Graeco-Roman society, *kyria* was an honorific designation given to the female head of a household or a woman of nobility and social standing. It expressed polite deferential respect, equivalent to the English term "lady" or "ma'am." In private letters preserved among secular papyri, *kyria* frequently appears as a title of endearment and respect in familial correspondence. Additionally, *Kyria* served as a proper personal name (Cyria) in the ancient Hellenistic world. In the biblical context, the term carries a warm, pastoral weight, blending social respect with spiritual fellowship within the family of God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

*Kyria* refers to a mistress, lady, female head of a household, or a woman of rank and dignity. It functions as the grammatical feminine equivalent of *kyrios* ("lord"). The semantic range includes:

  • A respectful title of address for a woman of status or spiritual maturity ("lady").

  • The mistress or female head of a domestic household.

  • A proper feminine name ("Cyria").

  • A figurative personification of a local Christian congregation.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Feminine Authority and Stewardship: Just as *kyrios* implies mastership or ownership, *kyria* highlights stewardship, dignity, and oversight within a domestic or communal sphere.

  • Honor and Pastoral Affection: The apostle John employs *kyria* to address the recipient with profound respect and pastoral care, combining formal deference with deep spiritual unity.

  • Domestic Assembly Imagery: Early Christian churches primarily met in domestic residences. A godly woman of resources (*kyria*) providing hospitality for believers naturally exercised significant influence and care within the local body of Christ.

4. Theological Implications

  • Dignity of Women in the Early Church: The use of an honorific derived from *kyrios* reflects the high theological standing, spiritual equality, and essential role of women in the apostolic era.

  • Covenantal Community: Relationships within the body of Christ are marked by honor, courtliness, and covenantal obligation rather than harsh hierarchy or cold detachment.

  • Truth as the Basis of Love: The title is intimately connected to walking in divine truth. Honor within the Christian community is grounded in shared adherence to apostolic doctrine and mutual love.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 2 John 1:5, John writes: "And now I plead with you, lady, not as though I wrote a new commandment to you, but that which we have had from the beginning: that we love one another."

The contextual nuance centers on two main interpretive views:

  • The Literal View: John is addressing an individual Christian woman of high social standing, spiritual maturity, and hospitality. She possessed a household, had children walking in the truth, and hosted a congregation. John’s plea to her is personal, gentle, and collaborative, encouraging her to lead her household and sphere of influence in active Christian love.

  • The Figurative View: John employs personification, addressing a local church metaphorically as an "elect lady" (*eklektē kyria*), with her "children" representing the members of the congregation and the "elect sister" (2 John 1:13) representing a sister church. Under this view, calling the church a *kyria* honors her as a bride-like entity under Christ’s lordship.

In either interpretation, the word sets a tone of deep pastoral appeal ("I plead with you"). John does not issue a legalistic decree but entreats this honored recipient to uphold the foundational, eternal commandment of love, preserving the assembly against deceivers and false teachers.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 2 John 1:1 — "The Elder, to the elect lady and her children, whom I love in truth, and not only I, but also all those who have known the truth,"

  • 2 John 1:5 — "And now I plead with you, lady, not as though I wrote a new commandment to you, but that which we have had from the beginning: that we love one another."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Old Testament Equivalence (LXX): In the Septuagint (the Greek Old Testament), *kyria* translates the Hebrew noun *geberet*, meaning "mistress" or "queen mother." It appears in Genesis 16:4, 8, 9 (describing Sarah as Hagar's mistress), 2 Kings 5:3 (describing Naaman's wife as mistress to the captive Israeli maid), and Psalm 123:2 ("As the eyes of a maid to the hand of her mistress").

  • Pastoral Application: John’s approach demonstrates that pastoral leadership is most effective when exercised through respectful appeal, honor, and warm relational bond rather than dictatorial authority.
