NOW - ( nyn , Strong's G3568 )

Summary:

The adverb *nyn* refers primarily to the present temporal moment ("at this time"), but in logical and apostolic contexts, it serves as an imperative transition marker. In 2 John 1:5, John transitions from greeting and commending the recipient to placing an urgent, present-tense appeal before her and her household. Rather than signaling a simple chronological shift, *nyn* functions as a pastoral transition—calling believers out of passive agreement into active, present-tense obedience to Christ's timeless commandment of mutual love.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The primary Greek adverb for the immediate present is *nyn*. In classical and Hellenistic Greek, it points directly to the point of time at which something is stated, occurring, or realized. Unlike words that refer to a general era or broader season (*kairos* or *aion*), *nyn* centers attention on the immediate "here and now."

Beyond its temporal force, *nyn* developed a crucial inferential or logical function in ancient Greek discourse. When paired with conjunctions like "and" (*kai nyn*) or "therefore" (*nyn oun*), it draws the listener from foundational statements into immediate practical application. It bridges the gap between historical truth and present duty, insisting that what has been declared in principle must now be manifested in practice.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The semantic range of *nyn* includes the following primary meanings:

  • Strict Temporal Sense: At the present time, right now, at this moment.

  • Logical or Inferential Sense: "As the case now stands," "under these present circumstances," "so now," or "this being so."

  • Redemptive-Historical Sense: Indicating the present gospel age brought about by the incarnation, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ, as opposed to the past age of shadow and ignorance.

  • Adjectival / Substantival Use: With the article (*ho nyn*), it signifies "the present age" or "the present state of affairs."

In pastoral exhortation, *nyn* frequently marks the turning point where established doctrine converts into immediate behavioral appeal.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Greek New Testament uses *nyn* in distinct theological patterns:

  • Temporal Contrast: It frequently contrasts past spiritual deadness or ignorance with present spiritual life and light (e.g., "once you were darkness, but now you are light in the Lord").

  • Eschatological Realization: It signals that the promised realities of salvation, worship, and kingdom access are actively present in Christ.

  • Pastoral Transition: In apostolic letters, *nyn* marks the pivot from preamble, doctrine, or thanksgiving to practical exhortation. It carries the weight of a loving directive: "Based on all that has been stated, here is what must be done right now."

### 4. Theological Implications

  • The Urgency of Present Obedience: Biblical truth is never intended merely for intellectual reflection or future consideration. The use of *nyn* anchors God's demands in the immediate present.

  • The Intersect of Eternity and Time: In Christian theology, the eternal commandment to love is brought into the temporal "now." Believers do not wait for better circumstances to walk in love; love is the immediate requirement of the present moment.

  • Pastoral Authority and Affection: Paired with verbs of pleading or asking, *nyn* demonstrates how apostolic authority operates—not through detached decree, but through earnest, present-moment entreaty grounded in shared truth.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 2 John 1:5 ("And now I plead with you, lady..."), John uses the phrase *kai nyn* ("and now") to transition from commended joy to earnest appeal.

In verse 4, John expresses great joy that he found children of the elect lady walking in truth. Having established this baseline of shared truth and joy, verse 5 uses *kai nyn* to introduce the core exhortation of the letter. John is not introducing a novel doctrine; he explicitly notes that this commandment is one "which we have had from the beginning."

The nuance of *nyn* here is both logical and practical: because we stand in the truth, and because you are already walking in it, *now* is the time to reinforce and actively practice our foundational mandate—loving one another. The timeless commandment demands timely, immediate execution.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 4:23: "But the hour is coming, and now is, when the true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth; for the Father is seeking such to worship Him."

  • Acts 17:30: "Truly, these times of ignorance God overlooked, but now commands all men everywhere to repent,"

  • Romans 8:1: "There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus, who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit."

  • 2 Corinthians 6:2: "For He says: 'In an acceptable time I have heard you, and in the day of salvation I have helped you.' Behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation."

  • 1 John 2:28: "And now, little children, abide in Him, that when He appears, we may have confidence and not be ashamed before Him at His coming."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The combination *kai nyn* ("and now") is a distinct marker in John's writings (cf. 1 John 2:28; 2 John 1:5). It reflects a gentle, fatherly shepherd pressing the church to take immediate spiritual action.

  • In Greek grammar, using *nyn* alongside an imperative or an appealing indicative verb (like *erōtō*, "I plead/ask") heightens the relational urgency of the request without relying on harsh command.
