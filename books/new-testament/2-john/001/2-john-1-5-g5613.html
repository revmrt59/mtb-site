THOUGH - ( hos , Strong's G5613 )

Summary:

The word translated "though" in 2 John 1:5 is the Greek particle *hos*, used in combination with the negative particle *oukh* ("not as though") to clarify apostolic intent and dispel potential misunderstanding. Rather than introducing a hypothetical contrast or an innovative mandate, *hos* functions to set aside a false impression—namely, that John was imposing a brand-new, unprecedented obligation on the church. Instead of presenting novelty, John anchors his appeal in the timeless truth of Christ's original teaching on love, showing that divine truth is both historically rooted and perpetually active in the life of the believer.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • *hos* is a comparative particle derived from the relative stem in Greek. It serves as an essential grammatical connector linking reality with appearance, comparison, degree, or manner.

  • In classical and Koine Greek discourse, *hos* bridges an action with its perceived character or intended perspective. When paired with a participle and preceded by a negation (as in 2 John 1:5, *oukh hos*), it carries the precise force of "not as if" or "not under the pretense that."

  • The historical and pastoral background reflects apostolic care. Writing to a beloved local congregation or Christian leader ("the elect lady"), John sought to urge adherence to essential Christian ethics without sounding overbearing, innovative, or legalistic.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • At its primary lexical level, *hos* denotes manner, degree, comparison, or reported perception, translated variously as "as," "like," "as it were," or "as though."

  • In conjunction with participles, it indicates the manner or pretense in which an action is performed or interpreted.

  • When expressing subjective perception, it frames an assumption or comparison, rendered in English as "as if" or "as though."

  • In 2 John 1:5, *hos* qualifies the nature of John's written exhortation: he does not write in the capacity of an innovator producing a novel commandment, but as an apostle passing along the original message received from the Lord.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Comparison and Analogy: *hos* frequently draws vivid comparisons, describing how believers are to live as lights or walk as children of God.

  • Pretense versus Reality: When combined with a negative, *hos* dismantles erroneous assumptions, distinguishing actual motive from mistaken perception.

  • Manner of Exhortation: It establishes the tone and framework of pastoral communication, clarifying that divine commands are grounded in established revelation rather than arbitrary human demands.

4. Theological Implications

  • Continuity of Divine Revelation: Truth in the kingdom of God is anchored in the foundational revelation of Jesus Christ. True spiritual depth is found not in inventing novel doctrines, but in deeper fidelity to the original gospel.

  • Pastoral Humility and Apostolic Authority: John does not claim the right to invent new moral requirements. True authority rests in faithfully stewarding what was delivered "from the beginning."

  • The Timeless Commandment of Love: Love is not an added requirement or a temporary theological phase, but the core expression of gospel life established by Jesus Himself.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:5, John uses *oukh hos* ("not as though") to pre-emptively guard against false teachers or confused believers who might accuse him of adding unauthorized burdens to the church.

  • The phrase disarms potential resistance by assuring the readers that the call to "love one another" is neither an extra burden nor a theological departure from Jesus' foundational teaching in John 13:34.

  • It highlights the complete harmony between historical revelation ("that which we have had from the beginning") and present Christian duty ("that we love one another").

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Acts 3:12: "So when Peter saw it, he responded to the people: 'Men of Israel, why do you marvel at this? Or why look so intently at us, as though by our own power or godliness we had made this man walk?'"

  • 2 Corinthians 10:2: "But I beg you that when I am present I may not be bold with that confidence by which I intend to be bold against some, who think of us as if we walked according to the flesh."

  • Philemon 1:14: "But without your consent I wanted to do nothing, that your good deed might not be by compulsion, as it were, but voluntary."

  • 1 Peter 2:16: "as free, yet not using liberty as a cloak for vice, but as bondservants of God."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Pastoral Communication: John's precise use of *hos* offers a practical model for ministry leaders, demonstrating how to issue firm spiritual exhortations with disarming clarity and pastoral warmth.

  • Guarding Against Novelty: Second John focuses heavily on confronting deceivers who "do not abide in the doctrine of Christ" (2 John 1:9). The clarification provided by *hos* establishes that authentic Christian teaching never strays into unbiblical novelties.
