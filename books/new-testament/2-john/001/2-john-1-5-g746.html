BEGINNING - ( arche , Strong's G746 )

Summary:

In 2 John 1:5, the word "beginning" (*arche*) refers not to the cosmological origin of the universe, but to the historical inception of the gospel message and the personal conversion of the believers. John reminds his readers that the commandment to "love one another" is neither a novel requirement nor an artificial theological innovation. Instead, it is a foundational truth established at the very onset of their Christian experience. *Arche* carries the dual sense of temporal priority and primary authority, anchoring true Christian conduct in the unalterable, original instruction of Jesus Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The Greek noun *arche* derives from the verb *archo*, meaning "to be first" in time, rank, or power. In classical Greek thought, *arche* denoted the first principle, ultimate origin, or primary cause from which everything else derives its existence. Philosophers used it to describe the fundamental material of the universe. In civic and administrative contexts, *arche* denoted supreme authority, office, or political rule, because a ruler occupies the first rank and governs from the head position. Within biblical literature, *arche* encompasses a dynamic range of meaning: the absolute starting point of creation, the inauguration of redemptive history, the foundational principles of Christian truth, and the structural authority of rulers and angelic beings.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The foundational concept of *arche* is primacy, expressed chronologically, positionally, or instructionally. Its main semantic dimensions include:

  • Chronological Point of Origin: The initial moment when an event, period, or creation began (e.g., the creation of the world or the start of a ministry).

  • First Principle or Foundation: The primary instruction, core element, or foundational truth upon which an entire framework of belief rests.

  • Ruler or Sphere of Authority: A domain of governance, magistrate, power, or authority occupying the first rank.

In John's writings, *arche* functions as a pivotal term. It denotes either the absolute eternal pre-existence of Christ (*"In the beginning..."*) or the historical starting point of gospel revelation (*"from the beginning"*).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Across the New Testament, *arche* illustrates distinct conceptual pictures depending on its narrative environment:

  • The Foundation of Creation: It marks the starting line of cosmic history, where time and space were brought into existence through the Word.

  • The Fountainhead of Truth: It depicts apostolic doctrine as a pristine stream flowing from its original source, requiring believers to guard against downstream corruption.

  • The Realm of Governance: It portrays civil rulers and spiritual principalities exercising authority within designated ranks.

  • The Supreme Archetype: Applied directly to Jesus Christ, who is called "the Beginning and the End," it portrays Him as the ultimate source, ruler, and consummator of all created reality.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of *arche* in the epistles of John safeguards the unchangeable nature of divine truth. Truth is not subject to progressive redefinition that contradicts its original revelation. In the first century, emerging false teachers claimed advanced, deeper insights that surpassed simple apostolic teaching. By commanding believers to return to what was received "from the beginning," Scripture establishes that genuine Christian doctrine is historically fixed. Theological novelty that departs from original apostolic revelation is not spiritual progress, but departure from the faith. Furthermore, grounding love in the *arche* demonstrates that mutual Christian love is not a secondary addendum to faith, but an essential component of the gospel message itself.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 2 John 1:5, the phrase "from the beginning" (*ap' arches*) holds a specific pastoral purpose. John writes to "the elect lady and her children" during a time when deceptive teachers were spreading error regarding the incarnation of Christ. John pleads with the church to walk in love, emphasizing that this is "not as though I wrote a new commandment to you, but that which we have had from the beginning."

Here, *arche* points to the initial proclamation of the gospel that these believers heard and accepted at their conversion. The commandment to love is "old" because it has been present since their spiritual inception; it is not a fresh invention. Contextually, John demonstrates that true Christian love is tied to original, sound doctrine. Loving one another does not mean compromising with error; rather, true love and original truth are inextricably linked.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 1:1 - "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God."

  • John 8:44 - "You are of your father the devil, and the desires of your father you want to do. He was a murderer from the beginning, and does not stand in the truth, because there is no truth in him."

  • 1 John 1:1 - "That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled, concerning the Word of life—"

  • 1 John 2:7 - "Brethren, I write no new commandment to you, but an old commandment which you have had from the beginning. The old commandment is the word which you heard from the beginning."

  • Revelation 22:13 - "I am the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End, the First and the Last."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Septuagint (LXX), *arche* translates the Hebrew word *bereshit* in Genesis 1:1 (*"In the beginning God created..."*). John deliberate echoes this phrase in John 1:1 and 1 John 1:1. However, a key distinction exists: in John 1:1, *arche* refers to uncreated eternity before time began, whereas in 2 John 1:5, *arche* refers to the historical beginning of the gospel proclamation in the life of the believer. The surrounding context strictly determines the temporal horizon of the word.
