COMMANDMENTS - ( entole , Strong's G1785 )

Summary:

In the New Testament, *entole* refers to an authoritative instruction, charge, or divine mandate given by God or Jesus Christ. Far from representing an oppressive legalistic burden, John uses *entole* to define the exact boundaries and practical expression of authentic divine love. To walk according to God's commandments is not an attempt to earn His favor, but the tangible evidence that His love has transformed the believer's heart, translating biblical truth into daily life.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • *Etymology*: Derived from *entellomai* (to command, charge, or enjoin), which combines *en* (in, upon) with *telos* (end, goal, or completion).

  • *Background Concept*: The term conveys the idea of setting a specific directive in motion to achieve an intended goal or end. It denotes a purposeful rule established by a sovereign authority to guide subjects into right living and protection.

  • *Old Testament Connection*: In the Septuagint (LXX), *entole* serves as the standard translation for the Hebrew word *mitzvah*, designating the covenant statutes and divine precepts given by God to Israel at Mount Sinai and through the prophets.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • *Entole* signifies a mandate, order, divine charge, or official commandment given by God, Christ, or an established authority.

  • *Scope*: The term refers both to individual divine instructions (such as the specific command to love one another) and to the comprehensive body of moral instruction provided in the Scriptures.

  • *Distinctive Character*: Unlike arbitrary human regulations, a divine *entole* flows directly from God's righteous nature. For the believer under the New Covenant, it functions not as an external legal mandate aimed at condemnation, but as an internalized guide written on the heart by the Holy Spirit.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • *Entole* occurs frequently across the Gospels and Johannine writings, often contrasted with human traditions (*paradosis*) that obscure God's true will.

  • In the Farewell Discourse and the Johannine epistles, *entole* is structurally tied to authentic discipleship. Love for Jesus is consistently validated by keeping His commandments.

  • *Associated Imagery*:

  • *A Navigational Pathway*: A divine directive that dictates the course of daily conduct (*peripateo*, "to walk").

  • *A Mark of Covenant Identity*: The visible proof of genuine fellowship with God and membership in His family.

  • *A Light Yoke*: A commandment that liberates rather than oppresses, described explicitly in Scripture as not burdensome (*bareia*).

4. Theological Implications

  • *Love and Law Unified*: Biblical theology refuses to separate divine love (*agape*) from moral obedience. Love without commandments devolves into self-defined sentimentality, while commandments without love produce cold legalism.

  • *Continuity of the Gospel*: The commandment given to the church is simultaneously "old" (the foundational message taught from the start of their faith) and "new" (realized with fresh power and clarity in Christ).

  • *Guardrail Against False Teaching*: *Entole* establishes clear ethical boundaries, exposing the error of antinomianism, which falsely claims that spiritual grace eliminates the need for moral obedience.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:6, John addresses a local church exposed to itinerant deceivers who separated Christian doctrine from moral accountability.

  • John intentionally shifts between the plural ("His commandments") and the singular ("the commandment") within a single sentence:

  • *The Plural ("commandments")*: Encompasses the full range of God's moral directives that govern Christian conduct.

  • *The Singular ("commandment")*: Distills all divine directives into their core essence: walking in sacrificial, truth-directed love toward one another.

  • The phrase "walk according to His commandments" establishes that love is defined by obedience. Christian love is not an unregulated emotion; it is active conformity to God's revealed instructions.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • *John 13:34*: "A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another; as I have loved you, that you also love one another."

  • *John 14:15*: "If you love Me, keep My commandments."

  • *1 John 3:23*: "And this is His commandment: that we should believe on the name of His Son Jesus Christ and love one another, as He gave us commandment."

  • *1 John 5:3*: "For this is the love of God, that we keep His commandments. And His commandments are not burdensome."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The phrase "heard from the beginning" (*ap' arches*) highlights apostolic stability. Believers need no "advanced" or speculative spiritual revelations; true maturity consists of remaining grounded in the foundational commandments of Christ.

  • The singular commandment of love in John's writings is dynamic: genuine faith in Christ (*orthodoxy*) inevitably bears fruit in practical love for the brethren (*orthopraxy*).
