HEARD - ( akouō , Strong's G191 )

Summary:

In 2 John 1:6, "heard" translates the Greek verb *akouō*, which carries far more weight than simple physical perception of sound. In biblical thought, to hear the word of God means to pay heed, internalize the truth, and respond in active obedience. John reminds his readers of the foundational truth they received from the beginning of their spiritual lives—the commandment to love one another—calling them to let that initial proclamation continually govern their daily conduct.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the Greco-Roman world, hearing was recognized as the primary sensory channel for instruction, public proclamation, and receiving tradition. In Hebrew thought (reflected in the Septuagint translation of *shama*), hearing is inseparable from doing. Hearing without active compliance was considered non-hearing. The biblical word picture of hearing involves an open heart yielding to a speaker's authority. In Johannine literature, hearing the voice of Jesus or the apostolic proclamation serves as the distinguishing mark of true sheep and genuine disciples.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

*Akouō* primarily means to hear, listen, pay attention to, or comprehend. Its full semantic range includes:

  • Physical perception of sound or spoken words.

  • Comprehension and spiritual understanding of a message.

  • Attentive listening that gives audience to a speaker.

  • Obedient response, where hearing leads directly to action.

In 2 John 1:6, the verb appears in the aorist active indicative form (*ēkousate*, "you heard"). This tense points back to a decisive, historic moment in the readers' past—the occasion when they first heard and accepted the apostolic gospel message and its moral demands.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

*Akouō* occurs over 400 times in the New Testament and forms the linguistic root for key compound words:

  • *Hypakouō* (obedience), which literally means "to listen under" authority.

  • *Parakouō* (disobedience or refusal to hear), which literally means "hearing alongside" or brushing sound aside.

Jesus regularly employed the verb to distinguish genuine faith from mere physical presence, declaring, "He who has ears to hear, let him hear!" In the letters of John, hearing the message "from the beginning" serves as a spiritual anchor against deceptive, novel teachings.

4. Theological Implications

  • Divine Truth demands a Response: Biblical revelation is not given for mere intellectual contemplation. Hearing God's word creates a moral obligation to live out what was heard.

  • Gospel Stability: Truth is not continually reinvented. Faithful Christian living relies on the fixed apostolic message heard at conversion.

  • The Unity of Love and Commandment: John links love directly to obedience. Hearing God's commandment to love is validated only when believers actively walk in that love.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 2 John 1:6, John addresses a community facing dangerous itinerant teachers who were spreading false doctrines. These deceivers claimed advanced, "higher" knowledge beyond the original gospel. John counters this spiritual elitism by directing believers back to what they "heard from the beginning." The nuance here is one of spiritual preservation: true growth is not departing from original apostolic truth to find something new, but walking faithfully in the fundamental truths already received.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 5:24 – "Most assuredly, I say to you, he who hears My word and believes in Him who sent Me has everlasting life, and shall not come into judgment, but has passed from death into life."

  • John 10:27 – "My sheep hear My voice, and I know them, and they follow Me."

  • 1 John 1:1 – "That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled, concerning the Word of life—"

  • 1 John 2:24 – "Therefore let that abide in you which you heard from the beginning. If what you heard from the beginning abides in you, you also will abide in the Son and in the Father."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Pastoral Application: In a culture that prioritizes novelty, believers are prone to seeking "new" spiritual experiences or teachings. John reminds shepherds and teachers that spiritual maturity comes from returning to and abiding in foundational truth.

  • Internalizing the Word: Hearing in 2 John is cumulative. The past act of hearing (*ēkousate*) must yield a present, continuous lifestyle of walking (*peripatēte*) in the commandment.
