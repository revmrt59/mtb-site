LOVE - ( agape , Strong's G26 )

Summary:

In 2 John 1:6, love (*agape*) is revealed not as a temporary feeling or passive affection, but as an active, covenantal devotion that expresses itself through continuous obedience to God's commandments. It is the deliberate, self-sacrificing commitment to walk according to divine truth, seeking God's glory and the moral and spiritual good of others. True Christian love is inseparable from God's revealed truth and serves as the practical evidence of saving faith.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In classical Greek, *agape* and its corresponding verb *agapao* were used infrequently and lacked special moral significance, often referring to a general fondness or preferring one thing over another. The writers of the New Testament elevated this term, imbuing it with deep theological meaning to describe the distinctive nature of divine love. Unlike *eros* (romantic desire) or *philia* (warm affection or friendship), *agape* does not depend on emotional warmth or the perceived value of the recipient. Instead, it originates in the holy, self-giving character of God. It represents an unconditional, willful commitment that gives selflessly for the highest good of another, regardless of cost or emotional reciprocity.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

*Agape* signifies a benevolent, deliberate, and self-sacrificing love driven by choice rather than emotional impulse. It is an intentional act of the will that actively seeks the ultimate spiritual and moral well-being of the beloved. Key aspects of its definition include:

  • Unconditional initiative: It stems from the character of the one who loves, not the worthiness of the object loved.

  • Action-oriented nature: It is not merely an internal feeling or static state, but a dynamic commitment demonstrated through tangible obedience and service.

  • Spiritual alignment: It is defined and bounded by divine holiness and truth, seeking outcomes that align with God's holy law.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the New Testament, *agape* is consistently depicted through metaphors of action, covenant, and sacrifice. It is portrayed as a clothing that believers put on, a bond that unites the body of Christ, and a directional pathway upon which believers walk. In 2 John 1:6, *agape* is directly paired with the metaphor of "walking" (*peripateo*), depicting love as a continuous, deliberate lifestyle governed by God's commandments. Love is not a static emotion to be felt; it is a path to be walked.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical concept of *agape* directly refutes the cultural misconception that love and law are opposing ideas. Scripture establishes that true love is the fulfillment of God's moral law. Because God Himself is love (1 John 4:8), His commandments are not arbitrary restrictions, but the structural design for how divine love operates in human relationships. To reject God's commandments in the name of "love" is to redefine love according to human autonomy. Biblical *agape* submits to God's authority, acknowledging that true devotion to God and genuine care for others can only occur within the boundaries of God's revealed truth.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 2 John 1:6, the apostle John addresses a local church facing false teachers who separated Christian spirituality from ethical obedience and orthodox truth. John clarifies that genuine love is defined by its allegiance to God's voice: "This is love, that we walk according to His commandments." In this passage, *agape* functions as the defining marker of true fellowship. It is neither subjective sentimentality nor doctrinal compromise. Instead, John demonstrates a reciprocal relationship between love and obedience: love provides the motivation for keeping God's commandments, while God's commandments provide the objective guardrails for how love is practiced.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 John 4:8 – "He who does not love does not know God, for God is love."

  • John 15:13 – "Greater love has no one than this, than to lay down one’s life for his friends."

  • Romans 13:10 – "Love does no harm to a neighbor; therefore love is the fulfillment of the law."

  • 1 Corinthians 13:13 – "And now abide faith, hope, love, these three; but the greatest of these is love."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The distinction between emotional affection and *agape* is vital for pastoral instruction. While feelings fluctuate, *agape* remains constant because it is rooted in divine command and empowered by the Holy Spirit.

  • The presence of *agape* serves as the primary test of genuine discipleship and orthodox living, guarding the church against both legalism (obedience without love) and antinomianism (claims of love without obedience).
