WALK - ( peripateo , Strong's G4043 )

Summary:

The word *peripateo* literally means to walk around or move about. In Biblical Greek, especially within the writings of John and Paul, it serves as the primary metaphor for daily lifestyle, ethical conduct, and the continuous orientation of one's life. In 2 John 1:6, walking according to God's commandments is identified as the concrete realization of biblical love. Genuine Christian love is not an abstract emotion or a mere sentimental feeling, but an active, habitual pattern of obedience to divine truth.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • Transliterated as *peripateo*, the term is a compound derived from *peri* (meaning "around" or "about") and *pateo* (meaning "to tread" or "to walk").

  • Its original physical sense describes moving from place to place on foot, walking about, or occupying oneself within a specific area.

  • In the Septuagint, *peripateo* was chosen to translate the Hebrew verb *halak*, which frequently denoted a person's moral conduct and daily walk before God. To "walk with God" or "walk in His statutes" in Old Testament thought meant to live a life shaped by covenant faithfulness.

  • By the New Testament era, this Hebraic background thoroughly enriched the Greek word, transforming a simple physical action into a dynamic metaphor for spiritual conduct and ethical lifestyle.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The basic physical definition is to walk, move about, or travel on foot.

  • The ethical and metaphorical definition is to conduct oneself, live, order one's behavior, or regulate one's life.

  • Grammatically, the present tense forms of *peripateo* express ongoing, habitual action rather than a single event. It speaks to the ongoing trajectory, routine habits, and deliberate steps of daily existence.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • In the Gospels, *peripateo* frequently carries its literal meaning, such as Jesus walking on the Sea of Galilee or healing the lame so they could walk.

  • In the Epistles, the word functions almost entirely as an ethical picture of spiritual life:

  • Walk in light versus walk in darkness (denoting moral purity versus sinful obscurity).

  • Walk by the Spirit versus walk by the flesh (denoting reliance on divine power versus human fallenness).

  • Walk worthy of the gospel (denoting living in agreement with one's high calling).

  • Walk in love and truth (denoting daily behavior governed by divine commandments).

4. Theological Implications

  • Love is active and verifiable. In Johannine theology, biblical love (*agape*) is never a passive feeling; it is manifested in deliberate actions aligned with God's revealed will.

  • Faith requires steady, step-by-step progress. A walk consists of continuous individual steps taken in a consistent direction. Spiritual maturity is rarely achieved through instantaneous leaps, but through regular obedience.

  • Right doctrine and right living are indivisible. Truth that is known intellectually must be walked out practically in love.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:6, *peripateo* is used twice to establish the exact definition of love and the ongoing duty of the believer.

  • John counters false teachers who elevated speculative knowledge over moral obedience. He insists that authentic love is defined by walking according to God's commandments.

  • The emphasis in this verse is on habitual conformity. Living in love means constantly measuring daily behavior against the standard of Christ's teaching, ensuring that every step reflects God's revealed character.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Ephesians 5:2: "And walk in love, as Christ also has loved us and given Himself for us, an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweet-smelling aroma."

  • Galatians 5:16: "I say then: Walk in the Spirit, and you shall not fulfill the lust of the flesh."

  • 1 John 1:7: "But if we walk in the light as He is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus Christ His Son cleanses us from all sin."

  • 3 John 1:4: "I have no greater joy than to hear that my children walk in truth."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Because *peripateo* emphasizes direction over momentary stumbling, it provides deep pastoral encouragement. A believer's walk is judged by the general path and destination of their life, not by an isolated fall, provided there is repentance and restoration.

  • The combination of "love" and "walking in commandments" protects believers from two errors: legalism (commandments without love) and emotionalism (love without commandments).
