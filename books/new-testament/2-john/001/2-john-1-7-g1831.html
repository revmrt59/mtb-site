GONE OUT - ( exerchomai , Strong's G1831 )

Summary:

The Greek verb *exerchomai* combines the preposition *ek* (out of) with *erchomai* (to come or go), signifying a deliberate departure from an established point of origin into another sphere. In 2 John 1:7, the phrase "gone out" describes false teachers who actively departed from the authentic apostolic community and its orthodox teaching to spread dangerous heresy throughout the world. Their departure was not merely a physical relocation, but an intentional, decisive break from apostolic truth and covenantal fellowship, revealing their true character as deceivers who deny the incarnate reality of Jesus Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The compound verb *exerchomai* brings together *ek* (denoting source or separation from within) and *erchomai* (to go or come).

  • The literal word picture is that of crossing a threshold, leaving an enclosure, or stepping out from behind a protective boundary into the open field or public square.

  • In ancient civic and communal life, departing out from a city wall or household meant leaving the security, governance, and fellowship of that entity. In New Testament usage, when applied to spiritual movement, leaving the assembly visualizes stepping outside the protective sanctuary of Christ's truth and apostolic oversight.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • *Exerchomai* indicates movement from inside a place, person, or collective group to the outside.

  • Primary semantic dimensions include:

  • Physical departure from a specific location such as a house, city, or region.

  • Public emergence or deployment into the wider world for a specific mission or task.

  • Relational, spiritual, or institutional separation from a community of origin.

  • The issuance or radiation of news, reports, or influence going out into wide public knowledge.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The term occurs over 200 times in the New Testament across narrative, theological, and apocalyptic contexts.

  • Mission and Commission: It describes the Son coming forth from the Father into the world (John 16:28), as well as faithful ministers setting out to proclaim the gospel (3 John 1:7).

  • Apostasy and Rupture: It describes individuals or groups exiting the local church fellowship to propagate deceptive doctrines (1 John 2:19; 2 John 1:7). This usage visualizes an internal rift becoming an explicit, visible separation.

  • Proclamation and Rumor: It refers to the word of the Lord or news of faith spreading rapidly out into surrounding regions (1 Thessalonians 1:8).

4. Theological Implications

  • Separation Exposes Reality: Truth produces unity, whereas fundamental Christological error ultimately drives separation. Defection from apostolic doctrine reveals that the defectors do not belong to the authentic body of Christ.

  • Counterfeit Mission: Just as Christ was sent out into the world with divine truth, Satan dispatches counterfeit workers out into the world equipped with spiritual deception. The kingdom of darkness mimics true gospel mission through false teachers.

  • The Boundaries of Fellowship: True Christian fellowship is defined by shared faith in the historic, bodily incarnation of Jesus Christ. Departing from this foundational truth constitutes an exit from the faith itself.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:7, the aorist tense form *exēlthon* points to a historical reality: deceivers have already completed their departure from the orthodox assembly and are now actively operating in the broader world.

  • Destination (*eis ton kosmon*): They went "into the world"—the realm aligned against God—to find receptive ears among those unschooled in apostolic doctrine.

  • The Heretical Marker: Their exit is tied directly to their refusal to confess Jesus Christ as coming in the flesh. Their departure demonstrates that doctrinal defect leads directly to practical and communal fracture.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 John 2:19 – "They went out from us, but they were not of us; for if they had been of us, they would have continued with us; but they went out that they might be made manifest, that none of them were of us."

  • 1 John 4:1 – "Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God; because many false prophets have gone out into the world."

  • 3 John 1:7 – "Because they went forth for His name’s sake, taking nothing from the Gentiles."

  • John 16:28 – "I came forth from the Father and have come into the world. Again, I leave the world and go to the Father."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • A striking contrast exists between 2 John 1:7 and 3 John 1:7. In 2 John, false teachers "have gone out" into the world to deceive, requiring believers to refuse them hospitality. In 3 John, faithful missionaries "went forth" for the sake of Christ's name, requiring believers to support them.

  • Biblical discernment requires evaluating the origin, doctrine, and destination of those who go out as spiritual teachers before opening the home or church to their influence.
