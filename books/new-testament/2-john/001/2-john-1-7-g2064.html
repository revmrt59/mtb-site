COMING - ( erchomai , Strong's G2064 )

Summary:

The Greek verb *erchomai* fundamentally signifies the action of moving from one place to another—to come, arrive, or appear. In 2 John 1:7, the present participle form (*erchomenon*) describes Jesus Christ as "coming in the flesh." This precise grammar highlights not merely a past historical event, but the essential, abiding reality of the Son of God taking on genuine human nature. John uses this word to confront early false teachers who denied the physical Incarnation, establishing that a true confession of Christ affirms His permanent, embodied humanity as the Savior of the world.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • In classical and biblical Greek, *erchomai* is the standard verb for arrival, entrance, or motion toward a goal.

  • In the Old Testament Greek translation (the Septuagint), *erchomai* frequently translates Hebrew verbs of motion such as *bo* ("to enter, come"), carrying rich prophetic expectations regarding God visiting His people or sending His promised Deliverer.

  • Prophetic literature established the title "The Coming One" (*ho erchomenos*) for the expected Messiah who would bring divine redemption (Psalm 118:26; Isaiah 35:4).

  • In ancient culture, the arrival of a king or official was an event of major public significance; in the New Testament, Christ’s coming represents the sovereign invasion of God's kingdom into human history.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • *Erchomai* means to come or go, to move from one point toward another, to arrive, or to make an appearance.

  • The word encompasses a broad semantic range, extending from simple physical movement to grand cosmic and divine manifestations.

  • In the present participle form (*erchomenon*) used in 2 John 1:7, the verb stresses continuous action, ongoing reality, or a permanent state of characterization rather than a completed past act.

  • The primary focus in incarnational passages is the entry of the eternal Son into the physical world through genuine human identity.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Messianic Identification: The term defines the promised Messiah as "the Coming One" who fulfills God's redemptive covenants (Matthew 11:3; Luke 7:19).

  • Divine Mission: It depicts the Son leaving the heavenly realm to accomplish the Father's mission on earth (John 3:31; 1 Timothy 1:15).

  • Continuous Manifestation: The present tense highlights that Christ’s arrival into humanity is an enduring reality, defining His ongoing work as Mediator.

  • Eschatological Return: The word frequently describes the glorious second coming of Jesus Christ to judge the earth and establish His eternal reign (Revelation 1:7; Matthew 24:30).

4. Theological Implications

  • Reality of the Incarnation: Jesus Christ is fully God and fully man. He did not merely appear to be human or temporarily borrow human flesh; He truly came as a physical, bodily human being.

  • Permanence of Humanity: By using the present participle, Scripture affirms that the glorified Christ retained His human nature after the Resurrection and Ascension. He remains the God-Man eternally.

  • Essential Test of Orthodoxy: Confessing the real, physical incarnation of Jesus is a vital doctrine of the Christian faith. Denying His coming in the flesh dismantles the reality of His substitutionary atonement on the cross.

  • Refutation of Dualism: Physical creation and human flesh are intrinsically valued by God. The Incarnation proves that matter is not inherently evil, but capable of being sanctified for divine glory.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:7, John addresses "many deceivers" who had gone out into the world spreading a proto-Gnostic heresy. These teachers argued that matter was evil and God could never actually inhabit physical flesh.

  • Rather than using a past-tense verb to describe the historical birth of Jesus, John uses the present participle *erchomenon* ("coming").

  • This phrasing characterizes Jesus Christ as permanently defined by His incarnation. He is "the One coming in the flesh."

  • Anyone who rejects this truth rejects the core identity of Jesus, earning the sobering designation of "a deceiver and an antichrist."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 11:3 - "and said to Him, 'Are You the Coming One, or do we look for another?'"

  • John 1:9 - "That was the true Light which gives light to every man coming into the world."

  • John 3:31 - "He who comes from above is above all; he who is of the earth is earthly and speaks of the earth. He who comes from heaven is above all."

  • Revelation 1:7 - "Behold, He is coming with clouds, and every eye will see Him, even they who pierced Him. And all the tribes of the earth will mourn because of Him. Even so, Amen."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Contrast with 1 John 4:2: In 1 John 4:2, John uses the perfect tense participle *elēlythota* ("has come in the flesh"), emphasizing the historical certainty and remaining effects of Christ's past birth. In 2 John 1:7, John uses the present participle *erchomenon* ("coming in the flesh"), emphasizing His present identity and perpetual human incarnation.

  • Practical Application for Believers: Salvation depends on a real Savior who took on real blood and flesh to die a real physical death in our place. Believers must steadfastly hold to biblical Christology and discern false teachings that spiritualize away the physical reality of Christ's work.
