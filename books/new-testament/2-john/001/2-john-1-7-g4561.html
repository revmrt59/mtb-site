FLESH - ( sarx , Strong's G4561 )

Summary:

In 2 John 7, the Greek word *sarx* refers to physical human nature and material bodily existence. Against the backdrop of early false teachers who denied that Jesus possessed a real human body, John uses *sarx* to emphasize the genuine, fully human incarnation of Jesus Christ. The Son of God did not merely appear to be human; He permanently took on real, physical human flesh. Rejecting Christ's physical incarnation dismantles the gospel of redemption, as Jesus had to be fully human in physical flesh to live, suffer, die, and resurrect on behalf of humanity.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

In classical Greek, *sarx* originally denoted the soft muscular tissue of the body as distinguished from the bones. Over time, its usage broadened in Jewish and Christian thought to encompass the physical body, mortal human existence, physical lineage, and human frailty.

In the Johannine epistles, *sarx* carries critical apologetic and historical weight. Late in the first century, early forms of Gnostic and Docetic philosophies began infiltrating the church. These philosophies taught that physical matter was inherently corrupt and evil, while spiritual reality was inherently pure. Consequently, false teachers asserted that the divine Son of God could never pollute Himself by taking on actual physical human flesh. John writes using *sarx* to combat this dangerous error, asserting that true Christian faith affirms the physical, historical reality of Jesus Christ in real human flesh.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term *sarx* possesses several distinct nuances across the New Testament depending on context:

  • Physical Body / Tissue: The literal material frame of humans or animals.

  • Human Nature / Personhood: Humanity in its totality, including physical existence.

  • Earthly Lineage / Ancestry: Biological heritage and physical descent.

  • Human Frailty and Mortality: Creaturely existence subject to weakness, pain, and temporal limits.

  • Fallen / Sinful Disposition: The unredeemed human nature oriented toward self and rebellion against God (prominent in Pauline theology).

In 2 John 7, *sarx* specifically refers to authentic human physical nature. It denotes the real, material body assumed by the Son of God at the Incarnation.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

The New Testament employs *sarx* in several clear categories:

  • The Incarnate Savior: Expressing the reality that God entered human history in a physical body (John 1:14; 1 John 4:2; 2 John 7).

  • Human Weakness: Contrast between physical limitations and spiritual willingness ("the spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak," Matthew 26:41).

  • Ethical Oppositions: Contrast between the spiritual life led by the Holy Spirit and the self-centered life led by fallen human desire (Galatians 5:16-17; Romans 8:5-8).

  • Redemptive Necessity: The physical medium through which atonement was accomplished on the cross (Ephesians 2:15; Colossians 1:22; 1 Peter 3:18).

### 4. Theological Implications

  • The Absolute Reality of the Incarnation: Jesus did not temporarily inhabit a phantom body or wear humanity as a disposable mask. He permanently united divine nature with real human flesh.

  • The Ground of Substitutionary Atonement: Salvation requires a real human representative. To bear human sin and endure human death, the Redeemer had to possess genuine human flesh and blood.

  • The Sanctification of Physical Existence: God's assumption of human flesh affirms the goodness of God's physical creation, refuting any dualistic belief that physical life is inherently evil.

  • The Test of Orthodoxy: Confessing that Jesus came in the flesh serves as a foundational benchmark for spiritual discernment and sound biblical doctrine.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 2 John 7, false teachers ("deceivers" and "antichrist") were wandering among congregations, denying that Jesus Christ came "in the flesh" (*en sarxi*).

John uses the present participle (*erchomenon en sarxi*, "coming in the flesh") to emphasize an abiding, continuous reality. Jesus Christ did not merely visit in flesh in the past; He remains the incarnate Lord today and will return in His glorified physical body. Denying Christ's true physical humanity invalidates His earthly life, His sacrificial death on the cross, and His bodily resurrection. To deny *sarx* in the person of Christ is to abandon the gospel entirely.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 1:14 - "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we beheld His glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth."

  • 1 John 4:2 - "By this you know the Spirit of God: Every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is of God,"

  • Romans 1:3 - "concerning His Son Jesus Christ our Lord, who was born of the seed of David according to the flesh,"

  • 1 Timothy 3:16 - "And without controversy great is the mystery of godliness: God was manifested in the flesh, justified in the Spirit, seen by angels, preached among the Gentiles, believed on in the world, received up in glory."

  • 1 Peter 3:18 - "For Christ also suffered once for sins, the just for the unjust, that He might bring us to God, being put to death in the flesh but made alive by the Spirit,"

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Pastoral Warning Against Modern Docetism: Believers must avoid functional Gnosticism, which reduces Christianity to an abstract, internal feeling while ignoring physical obedience, physical church community, and bodily holiness.

  • Comfort in Human Suffering: Because Jesus possessed real *sarx*, He experienced genuine physical hunger, exhaustion, pain, and sorrow. He is fully qualified to sympathize with believers in their physical trials and frailty.
