WORKED - ( ergazomai , Strong's G2038 )

Summary:

The verb *ergazomai* refers to active exertion, deliberate labor, and sustained effort aimed at producing a tangible result. Far from passive existence or superficial commitment, it describes the dedicated expenditure of energy—whether in physical trade, moral action, or spiritual ministry. In the context of Christian disciple-making and spiritual maturity, it highlights the purposeful effort invested by ministers and believers to build up the church, cultivate righteousness, and foster faithfulness. Losing what was worked for warns against spiritual carelessness that forfeits the full eternal reward intended for faithful labor.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The root noun *ergon* denotes a deed, action, or accomplishment.

  • The verbal form *ergazomai* carries the imagery of an artisan working raw materials into a finished masterpiece, a farmer tilling the soil to bring forth a harvest, or a craftsman pursuing a trade with skill and persistent sweat.

  • In classical and biblical usage, the term stands in sharp contrast to idleness, mere talk, or passive speculation. It requires intention, energy, personal investment, and active execution to achieve an intended objective.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • To work, labor, bring about, perform, or execute an activity.

  • To produce or accomplish something through intentional effort.

  • To carry on a business or trade, earning a livelihood through active work.

  • To practice or commit acts, whether good works of righteousness or deeds of lawlessness.

  • The core idea centers on active, productive engagement rather than potential, theory, or rest.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Labor in Gospel Ministry: The work of preaching, discipling, establishing local churches, and nurturing believers in truth requires strenuous spiritual and personal exertion.

  • Moral and Ethical Practice: Describes the active practice of good deeds toward others or, inversely, the persistent practice of sin and lawlessness.

  • Spiritual Investment versus Earthly Pursuit: Contrasts temporal labor for perishable food with spiritual labor for enduring heavenly value.

  • Practical Trade: Describes physical labor undertaken for daily sustenance, emphasizing honest work and diligence.

4. Theological Implications

  • Faith is active and fruitful; genuine faith expresses itself through labor and love, producing tangible spiritual fruit.

  • Salvation is by grace, yet believers are created in Christ Jesus for good works, which they are called to actively carry out.

  • Spiritual ministry involves real labor, emotional energy, and persistent work by pastors, teachers, and church members.

  • Eternal rewards (*misthos*) are linked to faithful labor; spiritual gains can be undermined or compromised if believers relax their vigilance or embrace error.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:8, John addresses believers, warning them against false teachers who deny the incarnation of Jesus Christ.

  • The phrase "those things we worked for" points to the hard-won spiritual results achieved through gospel preaching, discipleship, and communal steadfastness in the truth.

  • The focus is on mutual spiritual investment: apostles, teachers, and church members labored together to establish truth, produce spiritual growth, and build a lasting Christian witness.

  • The solemn warning reveals that spiritual fruit and heavenly reward can be compromised or lost if believers compromise with false teaching, abandoning the truth they originally labored to receive and protect.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 6:27 — "Do not labor for the food which perishes, but for the food which endures to everlasting life, which the Son of Man will give you, because God the Father has set His seal on Him."

  • John 9:4 — "I must work the works of Him who sent Me while it is day; the night is coming when no one can work."

  • Galatians 6:10 — "Therefore, as we have opportunity, let us do good to all, especially to those who are of the household of faith."

  • Matthew 7:23 — "And then I will declare to them, 'I never knew you; depart from Me, you who practice lawlessness!'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The relationship between labor (*ergazomai*) and reward (*misthos*) is dynamic: God graciously rewards the labor He empowers His people to perform.

  • Watchfulness is required to safeguard spiritual investment; labor spent planting and watering can be damaged if deception enters the fold.

  • Ministry success is measured not merely by starting a work, but by preserving its doctrinal integrity and spiritual vitality unto final completion.
