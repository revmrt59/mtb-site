THINGS - ( ha , Strong's G3739 )

Summary:

In 2 John 1:8, the word translated "things" is the neuter plural relative pronoun *ha* (a form of *hos*), functioning substantively to refer to the cumulative spiritual fruit, labor, and progress accomplished through gospel ministry and obedience to the truth. Rather than denoting material possessions, the term encompasses the eternal results of Christian work—steadfast faith, sound doctrine, spiritual growth, and community integrity—that stand in danger of being eroded or lost if believers succumb to deceptive, false teaching.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The Greek relative pronoun *hos* (*he*, *ho*; neuter plural *ha*) is a fundamental grammatical building block that joins clauses and qualifies nouns.

  • When used substantively without an explicit preceding noun, the neuter plural form (*ha*) acts as an expansive category meaning "the things which," "whatever things," or "that which."

  • In the context of first-century Christian apostolic ministry, spiritual labor involved planting churches, teaching truth, encouraging love, and enduring persecution. The background of 2 John involves local house churches threatened by traveling deceivers who denied the true incarnation of Jesus Christ.

  • The "things" represent the gathered harvest of this labor—the spiritual standing and maturity cultivated by the apostle and the local church community.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Lexically, G3739 (*hos*) refers to the relative pronoun "who, which, what, that."

  • In 2 John 1:8, the neuter plural accusative form *ha* functions as a direct object substantive: "those things which [we worked for]."

  • The term defines the entire scope of spiritual dynamic and achievement produced by divine grace through human effort. It refers to the enduring moral, theological, and relational fruit of gospel ministry.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The neuter plural relative pronoun operates flexibly in the New Testament to group multiple intangible realities into a singular collective concept.

  • It creates a visual picture of a storehouse or a cultivated field. The individual acts of faith, teaching, discipleship, and standing for truth are collected together into a unified crop ("those things").

  • To "lose those things" paints the imagery of a farmer working tirelessly through planting and tending seasons, only to allow pests or fire to destroy the crop right before harvest, resulting in a diminished reward.

4. Theological Implications

  • Spiritual investments made in time carry eternal consequences. Ministry labor and personal obedience generate real spiritual value that God recognizes and rewards.

  • Spiritual progress is not irreversible on a practical level; believers and communities can suffer severe loss of spiritual fruit and heavenly reward through doctrinal compromise or spiritual negligence.

  • Gospel work is inherently relational and cooperative. The ongoing perseverance of the congregation safeguards the labor invested by spiritual leaders, demonstrating that ministry fruit is preserved through collective adherence to truth.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:8, *ha* is directly tied to the verb *eirgasametha* ("we worked for" or "you worked for" in textual variants).

  • The solemn exhortation "Look to yourselves" establishes that false teachers pose a direct threat to the spiritual investment represented by *ha*.

  • John distinguishes between losing these accumulated spiritual accomplishments and receiving a "full reward" (*misthon plere*). The contextual nuance of "things" emphasizes that holding fast to the incarnation and truth of Christ protects the full spiritual compensation God promises to faithful servants.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 John 1:1 - "That which [ὅ] was from the beginning, which [ὅ] we have heard, which [ὅ] we have seen with our eyes, which [ὅ] we have looked upon, and our hands have handled, concerning the Word of life..."

  • Galatians 2:18 - "For if I build again those things which [ἅ] I destroyed, I make myself a transgressor."

  • Romans 8:25 - "But if we hope for what [ὅ] we do not see, we eagerly wait for it with perseverance."

  • Ephesians 2:10 - "For we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which [οἷς] God prepared beforehand that we should walk in them."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Early manuscript traditions vary between first-person plural ("we worked / we lose") and second-person plural ("you worked / you lose"). In either reading, the pronoun *ha* maintains its core function, referring to the work accomplished in the truth.

  • The verse underscores the pastor's heart: ministry is not merely about activity, but about protecting the lasting outcome of that activity until the day of Christ.
