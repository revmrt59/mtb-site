FULL REWARD - ( misthos plērēs , Strong's G3408 / G4134 )

Summary:

The phrase "full reward" (*misthos plērēs*) combines the noun *misthos* (wages, payment, divine recompense) with the adjective *plērēs* (filled to capacity, complete, lacking nothing). In 2 John 1:8, John issues an urgent pastoral warning to believers and ministers to remain steadfast in the truth of Christ so that the spiritual labor invested in the gospel is not diminished or forfeited. A full reward is not earnable salvation, but the complete, unclouded eternal recompense and commendation that God bestows upon faithful servants who persevere in sound doctrine and godly love without compromising with deceivers.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • In the ancient Greco-Roman commercial world, *misthos* referred to the exact wage due to a day laborer or contract worker upon completion of their duties. It was a standard economic term for agreed-upon pay for services rendered.

  • The adjective *plērēs* pictured a vessel filled to the brim, a full harvest, or a grain measure packed down without any shortfall.

  • When combined, the phrase conveys the image of a laborer receiving complete compensation for a finished project—no wage garnishment, no partial payout, and no loss due to breach of covenant or abandonment of the work.

  • In the Septuagint (LXX), the Old Testament background appears prominently in Ruth 2:12, where Boaz speaks to Ruth about a "full reward" (*misthos plērēs*) given by the LORD God of Israel under whose wings she came to take refuge.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • *Misthos* (Strong's G3408) signifies the payment for work done, wage, compensation, or divine recognition and reward given by God for faithful service.

  • *Plērēs* (Strong's G4134) signifies full, complete, filled up, abounding, or fully realized.

  • Together, *misthos plērēs* denotes compensation that is complete in every respect, leaving no deficiency. It refers to the maximum, uncompromised heavenly reward intended by God for those who persevere faithfully in Christ's truth and love to the end.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • *Misthos* occurs 29 times in the New Testament, frequently on the lips of Jesus in the Gospels to describe heavenly compensation for suffering, secret prayer, loving enemies, and faithful stewardship.

  • *Plērēs* occurs 16 times in the New Testament, describing things that are totally filled or complete, such as grace and truth in Christ or faith and the Holy Spirit in Stephen.

  • The imagery behind *misthos plērēs* emphasizes:

  • *The Laborer's Paycheck:* Receiving the total wage calculated for an entire day's labor without losing hours due to leaving the field early.

  • *The Complete Grain Measure:* A full basket filled to the top without hollow spaces or partial capacity.

4. Theological Implications

  • *Distinction Between Salvation and Reward:* Eternal life is an unearned gift of grace through faith in Jesus Christ. Rewards (*misthos*), however, are divine acknowledgments of faithful service, obedience, and endurance in truth rendered by believers.

  • *Possibility of Loss:* Scripture teaches that while a true believer's eternal destiny is secure in Christ, personal rewards at the Judgment Seat of Christ can be diminished or lost through spiritual negligence, doctrinal compromise, or unfaithfulness.

  • *Corporate and Pastoral Responsibility:* John highlights that spiritual leaders and church members work together. Defection into false doctrine not only harms individual believers but threatens to rob ministers and disciples alike of the spiritual fruit and full commendation they labored to produce.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:7-8, the apostle warns against false teachers—deceivers who deny that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh.

  • The imperative "Look to yourselves" (*blepete heautous*) is an urgent call for spiritual alertness and self-examination.

  • The danger is "losing what we worked for" (*ha eirgasametha*), which means forfeiting spiritual gains, losing fruitfulness, and suffering diminished heavenly recognition.

  • Receiving a "full reward" requires unwavering adherence to the true doctrine of Christ and genuine Christian love, ensuring that false teaching does not dismantle the spiritual work already accomplished.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • *Misthos* (Strong's G3408) - Reward / Wages:

  • Matthew 5:12 - "Rejoice and be exceedingly glad, for great is your reward in heaven, for so they persecuted the prophets who were before you."

  • Matthew 10:41 - "He who receives a prophet in the name of a prophet shall receive a prophet's reward. And he who receives a righteous man in the name of a righteous man shall receive a righteous man's reward."

  • 1 Corinthians 3:8 - "Now he who plants and he who waters are one, and each one will receive his own reward according to his own labor."

  • 1 Corinthians 3:14 - "If anyone's work which he has built on it endures, he will receive a reward."

  • Revelation 22:12 - "And behold, I am coming quickly, and My reward is with Me, to give to every one according to his work."

  • *Plērēs* (Strong's G4134) - Full / Complete:

  • John 1:14 - "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we beheld His glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth."

  • Ruth 2:12 (LXX) - "The LORD repay your work, and a full reward be given you by the LORD God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to take refuge."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • *Perseverance as the Condition for Fullness:* A full reward is directly connected to enduring faithfulness. Spiritual starts do not guarantee spiritual finishes; the reward is promised to those who remain in the doctrine of Christ without shrinking back.

  • *Pastoral Heart of the Apostle:* John speaks not with cold legalism, but with the warm concern of an aging shepherd who desires his spiritual children to receive everything God has prepared for them.

  • *Practical Application:* Believers must guard their minds against subtle theological errors, remain active in loving ministry, and protect the gains of past labor so that their final assessment before Christ brings unimpaired joy and glory to God.
