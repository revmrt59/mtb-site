RECEIVE - ( apolambano , Strong's G618 )

Summary:

In 2 John 1:8, "receive" translates the Greek verb *apolambano*, which signifies taking back, obtaining in full, or accepting what is due as a proper reward or settlement. Formed by combining the prefix *apo* (off, back, completely) with *lambano* (to take or receive), the word highlights an undiminished, complete reception of something set aside for a recipient. In the context of John's second epistle, believers are urged to remain watchful against deceivers so that they do not forfeit the spiritual harvest of their labors, but instead receive the complete, uncompromised reward prepared by God for steadfast faithfulness.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

In classical and Hellenistic Greek, *apolambano* belonged to commercial, judicial, and everyday social contexts. It described the formal act of settling accounts, collecting wages earned at the end of a job, recovering lost property, or receiving guest hospitality.

The prefix *apo-* intensifies the root verb *lambano* by introducing the element of origin, return, or completeness. Where *lambano* simply means "to take" or "to receive," *apolambano* stresses receiving back what is owed, collecting payment in full, or taking into one's possession that which rightfully belongs to the receiver. It paints the picture of a laborer standing before an employer at the end of the day, extending an open hand to collect every cent earned without deduction or dispute.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

*Apolambano* means to receive back, to repossess, to obtain in full, or to receive what is due as a recompense or outcome.

The semantic range of the verb encompasses four primary nuances:

  • To receive back something previously held or given out (recovering property, receiving back loans).

  • To receive completely or in full, leaving no remaining debt or balance unpaid.

  • To receive or welcome someone into full personal fellowship or hospitality.

  • To receive a deserved return, whether positive (a reward for faithful service) or negative (due punishment for wrongdoing).

In 2 John 1:8, the specific nuance intended is receiving in full a promised reward as the proper outcome of Christian perseverance.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the New Testament, *apolambano* conveys the exactness of divine and human accounts:

  • *Legal Recompense:* The repentant thief on the cross uses the term when acknowledging that he and his fellow criminal are receiving the exact penalty deserved for their crimes (Luke 23:41).

  • *Full Payment Received:* In the parable of the rich man and Lazarus, Abraham reminds the rich man that he received his good things in full during his earthly life, leaving no future account balance in eternity (Luke 16:25).

  • *Financial Settlement:* Lenders extend loans with the expectation of receiving back the exact principal from borrowers (Luke 6:34).

  • *Eschatological Inheritance:* Paul assures Christian servants that their earthly labors will culminate in receiving the full reward of their inheritance from the Lord Himself (Colossians 3:24).

When John uses the word in 2 John 1:8 alongside "a full reward," the image is that of a spiritual worker securing the total, unblemished pay for a lifetime of work, rather than having the payout reduced through spiritual negligence or compromise.

### 4. Theological Implications

The concept of receiving a "full reward" (*misthon pleres*) rooted in *apolambano* addresses key theological truths regarding divine judgment, stewardship, and perseverance:

  • *Grace and Rewards:* Salvation is an unmerited gift of grace through faith, yet God graciously promises heavenly rewards to believers based on their faithfulness, service, and endurance in truth.

  • *The Sovereign Exactness of God:* God is a righteous judge who keeps perfect books. No act of love, endurance under trial, or labor for the gospel goes unnoticed or unpaid by Him.

  • *The Reality of Spiritual Loss:* While a true believer's eternal life is secure in Christ, personal reward and fruitfulness can be diminished through compromise, false doctrine, or spiritual carelessness.

  • *Perseverance as the Path to Plenitude:* The full payout of heavenly glory requires enduring to the end in sound doctrine and active love.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 2 John 1:7-8, the apostle warns the church against antichrist deceivers who deny that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh. John issues an urgent pastoral command: "Look to yourselves" (*blepete heautous*). He sets up a stark contrast between two potential outcomes:

1. Losing the spiritual fruit and labor already invested ("that we do not lose those things we worked for").

2. Receiving the entire wage without loss ("that we may receive a full reward").

The verb *apolambano* functions as the triumphant alternative to loss. If believers guard themselves against false doctrine and remain anchored in the truth of Christ, their spiritual account remains intact. The reward they receive will not be fractional or discounted; it will be complete (*pleres*), reflecting the thoroughness of God's grace and their faithful resistance against deception.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 16:25 - "But Abraham said, 'Son, remember that in your lifetime you received your good things, and likewise Lazarus evil things; but now he is comforted and you are tormented.'"

  • Luke 23:41 - "And we indeed justly, for we receive the due reward of our deeds; but this Man has done nothing wrong."

  • Colossians 3:24 - "...knowing that from the Lord you will receive the reward of the inheritance; for you serve the Lord Christ."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Contrast with *Lambano*: While *lambano* refers to the general act of taking or receiving a gift, *apolambano* emphasizes the *appropriateness* or *completeness* of what is received. It carries the weight of entitlement based on covenant promise, agreement, or divine order.

  • Pastoral Application: Pastors and teachers must remind believers that current fidelity to biblical truth directly impacts future heavenly reward. Believers should not settle for a compromised Christian walk that risks forfeiting spiritual wages. Vigilance against false teaching ensures that the full harvest of past labors is fully realized on the day of Christ.
