LOSE - ( apollymi , Strong's G622 )

Summary:

In 2 John 1:8, the word "lose" translates the verb *apollymi*, which signifies suffering the loss of something valuable, forfeiting a reward, or allowing fruitful work to be rendered useless. Rather than referring to a loss of eternal salvation, John uses this term to warn believers against compromising with false doctrine. Turning aside to deceivers causes believers to forfeit the full spiritual reward and heavenly commendation that stems from past labor and faithful endurance.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root of *apollymi* is formed by combining the intensifying prefix *apo-* ("away" or "from") with *ollymi* ("to destroy" or "ruin"). The underlying imagery depicts something being ruined, wasted, or rendered utterly unfit for its intended purpose. It does not imply complete annihilation into non-existence, but rather the destruction of value, function, or usefulness.

In ancient Greek literature and everyday commerce, the word described a range of practical losses: a lost coin, a sheep wandering away from safety, ruined wineskins that could no longer hold wine, or wages lost due to breach of contract. The essential word picture is one of tragic waste—something precious that was earned, built, or possessed is squandered or destroyed.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb *apollymi* carries a broad semantic range depending on its voice and context:

  • Active sense: to destroy, bring to ruin, kill, or lose completely.

  • Middle/Passive sense: to perish, be ruined, be lost, or suffer destruction.

  • Financial and vocational sense: to forfeit wages, suffer the loss of an investment, or render previous labor void.

In non-physical and ethical contexts, it refers to forfeiting spiritual benefits, blessings, or divine compensation. The term emphasizes the loss of what was once within reach or already acquired through effort and grace.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Across the New Testament, *apollymi* appears in several vivid contexts:

  • The Lost Items: It describes the lost sheep, the lost coin, and the lost son, highlighting their state of separation from the owner or father until restored.

  • Ruined Vessels: It describes old wineskins that burst under the pressure of new wine, rendering them permanently ruined and useless.

  • Perishing in Danger: It expresses the desperate cry of the disciples in the storm when they feared physical death, declaring that they were perishing.

  • Spiritual Loss of Reward: It describes the forfeiture of divine reward when acts of righteousness or discipleship are abandoned or corrupted.

4. Theological Implications

The use of *apollymi* in connection with heavenly rewards highlights key aspects of Christian stewardship:

  • Distinction Between Salvation and Reward: Eternal life is a free gift of grace, whereas rewards (*misthos*) are divine commendations given for faithful labor and endurance. *Apollymi* in reward contexts does not mean losing eternal life, but suffering loss of heavenly compensation.

  • The Danger of Spiritual Drift: Past faithfulness does not render a believer immune to present deception. Spiritual diligence must be maintained until the end to secure a full harvest.

  • The Value of Spiritual Labor: God registers and values every work done for His kingdom. Forfeiting the fruit of that work through compromise is portrayed as a tragic waste of kingdom energy and grace.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 2 John 1:8, John writes against the backdrop of deceivers and antichrists who deny the true incarnation of Jesus Christ. He urges the believers, "Look to yourselves, that we do not lose those things we worked for, but that we may receive a full reward."

  • The phrase "look to yourselves" commands active vigilance against false teaching.

  • The loss (*apollymi*) specifically targets "those things we worked for"—the fruit of apostolic labor, personal growth, and gospel ministry.

  • Falling prey to false teachers invalidates past spiritual investments and deprives the believer of receiving a "full reward" (*plere misthon*) at the judgment seat of Christ. The contrast is not between being saved or lost, but between receiving a partial or complete reward.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 10:42 - "And whoever gives one of these little ones only a cup of cold water in the name of a disciple, assuredly, I say to you, he shall by no means lose his reward."

  • Luke 15:4 - "What man of you, having a hundred sheep, if he loses one of them, does not leave the ninety-nine in the wilderness, and go after the one which is lost until he finds it?"

  • John 6:39 - "This is the will of the Father who sent Me, that of all He has given Me I should lose nothing, but should raise it up at the last day."

  • Matthew 8:25 - "Then His disciples came to Him and awoke Him, saying, 'Lord, save us! We are perishing!'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

Grammatically, *apollymi* in 2 John 1:8 appears in the subjunctive mood following the conjunction *hina* ("so that / lest"), introducing a negative purpose clause. This structure presents a real warning to be heeded rather than an inevitable fate.

Early manuscript tradition shows a minor textual variation between "we" (*apolesomen*) and "you" (*apolesete*). Whether translated "that we do not lose" (focusing on the apostles' work in the believers) or "that you do not lose" (focusing on the believers' own effort), the pastoral principle remains identical: false doctrine destroys spiritual progress and forfeits divine reward.
