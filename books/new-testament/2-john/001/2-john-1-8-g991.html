LOOK - ( blepō , Strong's G991 )

Summary:

The Greek verb *blepō* literally denotes the act of seeing, observing, or perceiving with the physical eyes. In 2 John 1:8, expressed in the present active imperative form (*blepete*), it carries the figurative meaning of taking heed, exercising spiritual vigilance, and giving careful, continuous attention to one's conduct and doctrine. John commands his readers to actively guard themselves against deceptive influences so that they do not forfeit the spiritual fruits and eternal rewards resulting from faithful labor in the gospel.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The root meaning of *blepō* centers on physical vision—the functional capacity of the eyes to turn toward an object and observe it.

  • In classical and biblical Greek, the word frequently shifts from physical sight to mental and spiritual perception. Physical vision becomes a natural metaphor for moral alertness, spiritual discernment, and intellectual reflection.

  • When used as an imperative command ("look!"), *blepō* functions much like a military sentry's call to stay alert. It picture a guard standing watch on a wall, actively scanning the horizon to identify potential threats before they can breach the city gates.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The basic lexical definition of *blepō* includes possessing the faculty of sight, beholding, or visually observing.

  • Metaphorically, the verb signifies taking heed, considering carefully, exercising caution, or guarding against danger.

  • When paired with the reflexive pronoun (*heautous*, "yourselves"), the phrase *blepete heautous* means to examine one's own spiritual condition, exercise self-vigilance, and guard against personal deception.

  • The present tense of the imperative indicates an ongoing, habitual responsibility. It demands continuous spiritual awareness rather than a single, isolated moment of self-examination.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Visual Observation: Physical sight in contrast to blindness or physical inability to see.

  • Spiritual Discernment: The ability to perceive divine truth, recognize spiritual realities, and understand God's will.

  • Warning and Caution: A urgent call to beware of insidious dangers, false teachers, and doctrinal distortion.

  • Self-Reflection: Turning the lens of scrutiny inward to assess one's spiritual health and alignment with Scripture.

4. Theological Implications

  • Deception is Subtile and Dangerous: False teaching rarely presents itself as overt hostility; it often arrives disguised as truth. Continuous vigilance (*blepō*) is necessary to detect error.

  • Christian Stewardship of Ministry and Rewards: While eternal salvation is secured by grace through faith, scripture clearly teaches that faithful Christian labor yields spiritual rewards. Carelessness can lead to a loss of reward and diminished effectiveness.

  • Human Responsibility in Divine Preservation: Believers are kept by God's power, yet God executes that protection through the active, Spirit-empowered vigilance of His people. Preservation and active alertness work in complete harmony.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:7–8, John warns the local church community about deceptive teachers ("deceivers") who deny the incarnation of Jesus Christ.

  • The command "Look to yourselves" serves as a spiritual defense mechanism. John cautions believers that failure to guard against false teaching will result in forfeiting the fruit of their previous gospel labors.

  • The use of the second-person plural imperative emphasizes that spiritual vigilance is both a personal and a collective duty within the local church body; believers must watch over themselves and look out for one another.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Mark 13:33 – "Take heed, watch and pray; for you do not know when the time is."

  • 1 Corinthians 10:12 – "Therefore let him who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall."

  • Galatians 5:15 – "But if you bite and devour one another, beware lest you be consumed by one another!"

  • Colossians 4:17 – "And say to Archippus, 'Take heed to the ministry which you have received in the Lord, that you may fulfill it.'"

  • Hebrews 3:12 – "Beware, brethren, lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief in departing from the living God;"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • *Blepō* contrasts with *horao* (which often stresses the mental or spiritual vision of an objective reality) and *theaomai* (which emphasizes contemplative, intentional beholding). *Blepō* highlights the active application of attention and caution toward a specific object or hazard.

  • Pastoral Application: Spiritual vigilance is not suspicion or paranoia, but healthy biblical wisdom. Pastors and believers must intentionally inspect what doctrines and influences they permit into their lives and churches to preserve the purity of the gospel and secure a full reward.
