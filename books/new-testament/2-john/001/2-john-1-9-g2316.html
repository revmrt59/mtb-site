GOD - ( theos , Strong's G2316 )

Summary:

The Greek noun *theos* designates the supreme Divine Being, the eternal Creator, Sovereign, and Redeemer of the universe. In the Johannine epistles, and specifically in 2 John 1:9, "having God" describes an vital, personal, and covenantal relationship with the one true God. This relationship is inseparable from steadfast allegiance to the apostolic truth regarding Jesus Christ. To abandon or distort the doctrine of Christ is to forfeit fellowship with God entirely.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • In classical Greek literature, *theos* was used broadly for pagan deities, divine forces, or the personification of nature and fate.

  • In the Septuagint (LXX), *theos* was adopted as the standard Greek translation for the Hebrew *Elohim*, identifying the transcendent Creator who made covenant with Israel.

  • In New Testament usage, *theos* strips away polytheistic pagan assumptions and firmly anchors the identity of God as the holy, absolute, personal monotheistic Creator who has fully revealed Himself in the person and work of Jesus Christ.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • *Theos* designates the unique, transcendent God who exists eternally, possesses ultimate authority, and creates and sustains all things.

  • It denotes the divine nature and essence shared equally by God the Father, God the Son, and God the Holy Spirit.

  • In personal designations within the New Testament narrative, *theos* frequently identifies God the Father, emphasizing His sovereign initiative in salvation and revelation.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Relational Possession: The construct "has God" (*echei theon*) represents real spiritual union, ongoing fellowship, and personal communion with God.

  • Covenantal Light and Life: Possessing *theos* signifies walking in divine light, sharing in eternal life, and dwelling under divine protection and love.

  • Trinitarian Union: *Theos* cannot be possessed in isolation from Christ; fellowship with God is intrinsically bound to honoring the Son.

4. Theological Implications

  • Monotheistic Fulfillment: The God of the Old Testament Scriptures is fully and definitively revealed through Jesus Christ.

  • Christological Necessity: True knowledge of God is impossible apart from the incarnation and ministry of Jesus Christ.

  • Preservation of Truth: Fellowship with God is preserved through faithfulness to sound doctrine; spiritual life cannot exist where apostolic truth is rejected.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 2 John 1:9, John refutes early false teachers who claimed superior enlightenment while departing from the apostolic doctrine of Christ's incarnation.

  • The phrase "transgresses and does not abide" describes running beyond the established boundary of divine truth into destructive error.

  • John's declaration is categorical: anyone claiming a relationship with God while denying the true doctrine of Christ does not actually have God.

  • Conversely, abiding in the doctrine of Christ secures full fellowship with both the Father and the Son, establishing that *theos* is known exclusively through the Son.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 1:1 - "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God."

  • John 3:16 - "For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son, that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have everlasting life."

  • 1 John 4:8 - "He who does not love does not know God, for God is love."

  • 1 John 5:20 - "And we know that the Son of God has come and has given us an understanding, that we may know Him who is true; and we are in Him who is true, in His Son Jesus Christ. This is the true God and eternal life."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Pastoral Vigilance: Modern spiritual movements often attempt to retain belief in "God" while discarding or redefining Jesus Christ. 2 John 1:9 exposes the impossibility of this separation.

  • Assurance and Doctrine: Christian assurance is anchored not in mystical feelings, but in abiding in the revealed truth of the gospel.
