EG Culture - 2 John 1

HONOR || Through His Eyes

Culture Statement

Value Statement

Relationships are protected when we ascribe heaven’s value to the people around us. Like Jesus, we choose to interact with people, not as they deserve, but according to their God given value.

Living It in 2 John 1

John addresses this church as “the elect lady” (v. 1). Before he says anything else about them, he names what God has done: God chose them. He tells them he loves them in truth, that believers who have never met them love them too, and that he rejoiced greatly over what he found among them (vv. 1, 4). Even his instruction comes as a request: “And now I plead with you, lady” (v. 5). John had the authority to give orders, yet he spoke to the church according to what they are in God’s sight. Honor sees people as God sees them and speaks to them that way, even when there is something serious to say.

The letter also shows what honor does and does not require. John forbids the church to receive or greet a teacher who denies Christ in a way that would help his message spread (vv. 10–11). Honor does not mean endorsing what someone teaches or giving their ministry a platform. We may sometimes need to refuse a teaching, decline an invitation, or withhold endorsement from a ministry. Honor still governs how we respond. We can speak plainly and establish necessary boundaries without mockery, contempt, or forgetting the God-given value of the person involved.

FELLOWSHIP || Assembly Required

Culture Statement

Value Statement

We are united by our pursuit of the Lord. We come together to discover His heart and partner with His will. Breaking bread is optional, but strongly encouraged.

Living It in 2 John 1

John’s love for this church did not depend on shared background, location, or personality. “All those who have known the truth” love them, “because of the truth which abides in us” (vv. 1–2). That is what unites the people of God. Believers come from different histories, places, tastes, and opinions. What makes them one body is that they pursue the same Lord and share the same truth about Him. A fellowship held together only by friendship or preference is fragile. A fellowship gathered around Jesus Christ has something deeper holding it together, and it reaches beyond one congregation to “the children of your elect sister” (v. 13) in churches we may never visit.

John could have tried to say everything in this letter. Instead he hoped to come and be with them, “that our joy may be full” (v. 12). Christian fellowship is more than exchanging information. When believers gather, they pray with one another, hear the Word side by side, worship as one body, and share life around a table. A letter or a screen can carry news, and online services are a gift when we cannot be present, but neither fully replaces that shared life. Scripture calls believers not to forsake “the assembling of ourselves together” (Hebrews 10:25). John’s longing to be with this church shows why gathering is worth protecting rather than casually neglecting.

GENEROSITY || It's Not Ours Anyway

Culture Statement

Value Statement

We recognize that every resource we’ve been given comes from our generous Father. We choose to be just like Dad, and give as He leads.

Living It in 2 John 1

In the first-century church, hospitality was one of the main ways believers gave. A home, a meal, and provisions for the road kept traveling teachers moving from church to church and carried the gospel with them (3 John 5–8). John’s instruction in verses 10–11 shows that this kind of support had consequences. Receiving a traveling teacher gave him resources, credibility, and help on his way, so the same support that advanced the truth could also advance a lie. Whoever welcomed and supported a teacher who denied Christ “shares in his evil deeds” (v. 11). This does not make every act of hospitality an endorsement. It means believers should consider what their support advances.

Because every resource we have comes from our Father, our generosity should be directed by His truth, wisdom, and leading. The Holy Spirit is the Spirit of truth (John 16:13), and He does not lead believers to support a message that denies the Son. This should not make us stingy or suspicious. The same principle works in the other direction. When we give to the house of God, support missionaries, or open our homes to those who serve the Lord faithfully, we “become fellow workers for the truth” (3 John 8). Generosity joins us to the work it supports, and that is a privilege.

We are People of the Word

Culture Statement

Value Statement

The Holy Bible is the only infallible and final authority for Christian faith and life. The Holy Scriptures are timeless, inerrant and are to be read personally, discerned corporately, and walked out in community. We interpret all Scripture through the life and teachings of Jesus, trusting Holy Spirit to reveal truth and transform us as we submit ourselves fully to God’s revealed Word.

Living It in 2 John 1

John measures everything by what the church received at the beginning. The commandment is the one “we have had from the beginning” (v. 5). The test for any teacher is whether he brings “the doctrine of Christ” (vv. 9–10), with the truth that Jesus Christ came in the flesh at its center (v. 7). Jesus is the center of all Scripture, and 2 John shows why the truth about Him must stay at the center of how we read it. Spirit-filled believers welcome Holy Spirit’s voice and expect Him to speak. He leads believers deeper into the truth God has revealed about Christ, not away from it. Every teaching, prophetic word, spiritual impression, or claimed revelation must be weighed in light of Scripture and the truth about Jesus Christ.

John’s instructions address believers together, reminding us that discerning truth is not merely an individual responsibility. We benefit from testing teaching in the light of Scripture alongside mature believers and trusted spiritual leaders, especially when something sounds new. Reading the Bible personally remains essential, and discernment is strengthened in community. The goal is never only to be correct. The believers who brought John joy were “walking in truth” (v. 4). Truth is meant to be walked out, not simply known or defended.

Communication

Culture Statement

Value Statement

We “real talk” because we desire real relationships built on trust. We have 100% of our conversations, choosing to be authentic, honest, and open with one another. Awkward today, stronger tomorrow.

Living It in 2 John 1

John speaks to this church about both joy and concern. He tells them plainly what brought him joy: some of their children are walking in truth (v. 4). He tells them just as plainly about the danger they face: deceivers are at work, and “This is a deceiver and an antichrist” (v. 7). Then he says, “Look to yourselves” (v. 8). Having 100% of our conversations includes both encouragement and necessary correction. It means telling someone what we see God doing in them and telling them honestly when we see something that could cost them what God has built. That kind of communication is truthful and honoring, and it comes from love for the person.

John also had “many things” he chose not to put in writing, and he hoped to “speak face to face” (v. 12). Sensitive or difficult conversations deserve that kind of personal attention rather than being avoided or handled entirely through text messages. Those conversations may be awkward today. They make relationships stronger tomorrow.
