Chapter Study

2 John 1

Verse-by-Verse
2 John 1:1

NKJV

2 John 1:1 The Elder, To the elect lady and her children, whom I love in truth, and not only I, but also all those who have known the truth,(NKJV)

NLT

2 John 1:1 This letter is from John, the elder. I am writing to the chosen lady and to her children, whom I love in the truth — as does everyone else who knows the truth — (NLT)

Main Idea

John writes as a spiritual leader to a Christian community he deeply loves, united with them by the truth of the gospel that they share.

Verse Explanation

“The Elder,”

John identifies himself simply as “the Elder.” The title carries both authority and relationship. Early Christian tradition strongly identifies the writer as the apostle John, now an older and respected leader among the churches. He does not need to introduce himself by name because the recipients evidently know who is writing.

“To the elect lady and her children,”

The “elect lady” most likely refers figuratively to a local church, with “her children” referring to its members. This fits the letter’s closing, where John says, “The children of your elect sister greet you” (v. 13), naturally suggesting one congregation sending greetings to another. “Elect” means chosen, pointing to their identity as people belonging to God.

“whom I love in truth,”

John’s love for them is not merely personal affection. It is love shaped by and grounded in the truth they share in Christ. Throughout this short letter, John refuses to separate truth and love. Christian love does not move away from truth, and Christian truth does not make love unnecessary.

“and not only I, but also all those who have known the truth,”

The bond extends beyond John. Other believers who “have known the truth” share this love because the same truth has brought them into the same spiritual family. The verb translated “have known” carries the sense of coming to know and continuing in that knowledge. Truth here is more than information they have learned. It is truth they have received and now live within.

Study Notes

The identity of “the elect lady” has been debated. Some understand it as an individual Christian woman and her literal children. That is grammatically possible, but the church interpretation fits especially well with verse 13 and with the way John moves between singular and plural forms of address in the letter.

John's repeated use of “truth” is important for reading the whole letter. Truth appears repeatedly in verses 1-4 and becomes crucial when John warns about deceivers in verses 7-11. The opening language is therefore not incidental. John is establishing one of the letter’s controlling themes: genuine Christian love exists together with faithfulness to the truth.

Spirit & Life

John begins with something essential for healthy spiritual life: love and truth belong together. We do not have to choose between being people who love deeply and people who hold firmly to truth. In Jesus, the two meet perfectly.

There is also something beautiful in John's description of believers loving one another because they “have known the truth.” The truth of Christ does more than correct what we believe. It creates a shared life among people who belong to Him.

2 John 1:2

NKJV

2 John 1:2 because of the truth which abides in us and will be with us forever:(NKJV)

NLT

2 John 1:2 because the truth lives in us and will be with us forever. (NLT)

Main Idea

The truth believers share is not temporary or changing. It lives within them and will remain with them forever.

Verse Explanation

“because of the truth which abides in us”

John continues explaining the love described in verse 1. Believers love one another “because of the truth” they share. But John goes further: this truth does not merely surround them or inform them; it “abides in us.” The word “abides” means remains, stays, or continues. The truth of Christ has taken up a lasting place within God's people and shapes the life they share together.

“and will be with us forever:”

The truth that abides in believers will never pass away. Teachers may come and go, circumstances may change, and deceivers may challenge the faith, but God's truth remains. This gives weight to John's later warning against those who deny the truth about Jesus Christ. What God has revealed in Christ is not something believers outgrow or leave behind.

Study Notes

Verse 2 completes the thought begun in verse 1. John loves these believers “in truth,” and others who know the truth love them as well, “because of the truth which abides in us.” Their shared love grows out of their shared possession of the truth.

“Abides” translates meno, one of John's characteristic words. It means to remain, stay, or continue and appears frequently in John's Gospel and letters. Here it stresses permanence: the truth is not simply something Christians once learned; it remains in them.

Spirit & Life

There is wonderful stability here. Our life with God is anchored in truth that does not expire. The same truth that brought us to Christ continues to live within us and will “be with us forever.”

John's language also points beyond simply knowing correct teaching. Truth “abides in us.” God's desire is not merely that His truth fill our minds, but that it become an abiding reality within us, shaping how we know Him and how we love one another.

2 John 1:3

NKJV

2 John 1:3 Grace, mercy, and peace will be with you from God the Father and from the Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of the Father, in truth and love.(NKJV)

NLT

2 John 1:3 Grace, mercy, and peace, which come from God the Father and from Jesus Christ — the Son of the Father — will continue to be with us who live in truth and love. (NLT)

Main Idea

John assures believers that grace, mercy, and peace are theirs from the Father and Jesus Christ, and that these blessings are experienced in a life shaped by truth and love.

Verse Explanation

“Grace, mercy, and peace will be with you”

John moves beyond the customary greeting “Grace, mercy, and peace” by confidently saying they “will be with you.” Grace speaks of God's undeserved favor, mercy of His compassion toward those in need, and peace of the wholeness and well-being that come from being right with God. John presents these not merely as wishes, but as realities believers can know.

“from God the Father and from the Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of the Father,”

Grace, mercy, and peace come “from God the Father” and “from the Lord Jesus Christ.” John's deliberate description of Jesus as “the Son of the Father” stresses His unique relationship with the Father. This becomes especially important later in the letter when John confronts deceivers who distort the truth about Jesus Christ.

“in truth and love.”

These two words capture much of the heart of 2 John. The Christian life is not truth without love or love detached from truth. God's people are called to live in both. Truth gives love its foundation and direction, while love expresses the life that truth is meant to produce.

Study Notes

The threefold greeting “Grace, mercy, and peace” is unusual. “Grace and peace” is common in New Testament letters, while the addition of “mercy” gives the greeting particular richness.

The phrase “the Son of the Father” occurs only here in the New Testament. John could simply have said “Jesus Christ,” but the fuller expression fits the letter's concern with guarding the true identity of Jesus. The Father and the Son are distinct, yet John places them together as the source of “Grace, mercy, and peace.”

Spirit & Life

John's confidence is striking: “Grace, mercy, and peace will be with you.” Our life with God rests not only on what we must give Him, but on what continually comes to us from the Father and the Son. We live by His grace, depend upon His mercy, and receive His peace.

And all of this is “in truth and love.” Spiritual maturity never requires choosing one at the expense of the other. The closer we walk with Jesus, the more deeply His truth should govern us and His love should flow through us.

2 John 1:4

NKJV

2 John 1:4 I rejoiced greatly that I have found some of your children walking in truth, as we received commandment from the Father.(NKJV)

NLT

2 John 1:4 How happy I was to meet some of your children and find them living according to the truth, just as the Father commanded. (NLT)

Main Idea

John rejoices that members of this church are genuinely living according to God's truth, just as the Father has commanded His people to do.

Verse Explanation

“I rejoiced greatly that I have found some of your children walking in truth,”

John's joy comes from discovering believers who are “walking in truth.” In Scripture, “walking” describes the course and conduct of a person's life. These believers were not merely affirming the truth intellectually; their lives were being shaped by it. Truth had moved from something they knew to something they lived.

The word “some” does not necessarily imply that others were failing or had departed from the truth. John is speaking about those he had encountered or knew about. The emphasis is on his joy over what he had personally observed.

“as we received commandment from the Father.”

Walking in truth is not an optional level of Christian maturity. It reflects what believers have “received” from the Father. God's truth calls for a corresponding way of life. What we believe and how we live are meant to remain together.

Study Notes

“Walking in truth” is characteristic Johannine language. John uses a similar expression in 3 John 3-4, where he again expresses great joy over believers “walking in truth.” For John, truth is never merely doctrinal information. It is something known, embraced, and lived.

The “commandment” here is probably not one isolated command. In context, it points broadly to living faithfully according to what God has commanded. Verse 5 then brings John's focus specifically to the command to love one another.

Spirit & Life

John gives us a beautiful picture of what spiritual growth looks like: truth becoming a walk. There can be a distance between knowing what God says and ordering our lives around it. Maturity closes that distance.

There is also something revealing about John's joy. As a spiritual father, what thrilled him was not simply that people listened to his teaching, but that he could see them living it. The fruit of ministry is not merely truth understood. It is truth becoming life.

2 John 1:5

NKJV

2 John 1:5 And now I plead with you, lady, not as though I wrote a new commandment to you, but that which we have had from the beginning: that we love one another.(NKJV)

NLT

2 John 1:5 I am writing to remind you, dear friends, that we should love one another. This is not a new commandment, but one we have had from the beginning. (NLT)

Main Idea

John reminds the church that loving one another is not a new teaching but a command that has belonged to the Christian life from the beginning.

Verse Explanation

“And now I plead with you, lady,”

John addresses the “lady,” most likely the church he has been addressing since verse 1. “I plead with you” carries a warm, personal tone. John has authority as “the Elder,” but he appeals to them rather than speaking harshly.

“not as though I wrote a new commandment to you, but that which we have had from the beginning:”

John is not introducing a new requirement. The command he is about to give has been part of the Christian message “from the beginning,” meaning from the beginning of their life and instruction in Christ. They do not need a new revelation; they need to continue faithfully in what they have already received.

“that we love one another.”

This is the command John has in view. Love for fellow believers is fundamental to following Jesus. John echoes Jesus' command to His disciples in John 13:34. In the context of 2 John, however, this love will never be separated from truth. John has already joined the two together repeatedly, and he will soon warn that love does not require welcoming or supporting those who deny the truth about Christ.

Study Notes

The wording “not as though I wrote a new commandment” closely resembles 1 John 2:7. John's phrase “from the beginning” regularly points his readers back to the apostolic message about Christ that they had received rather than toward some supposedly newer or more advanced teaching.

There is an important connection to John 13:34, where Jesus calls love for one another “a new commandment.” There is no contradiction. The command was “new” when Jesus gave it in the distinctive form and measure of His own love. By the time John writes 2 John, it is no longer new to these believers; it has belonged to their Christian faith “from the beginning.”

Spirit & Life

John's words guard us against the temptation to confuse spiritual maturity with constantly discovering something new. Sometimes maturity means going deeper into what Jesus has already made clear.

And at the center of that life is “that we love one another.” Truth is essential in this letter, but truth is never meant to produce coldness. The truth we abide in should increasingly produce the love Jesus commanded.

2 John 1:6

NKJV

2 John 1:6 This is love, that we walk according to His commandments. This is the commandment, that as you have heard from the beginning, you should walk in it.(NKJV)

NLT

2 John 1:6 Love means doing what God has commanded us, and he has commanded us to love one another, just as you heard from the beginning. (NLT)

Main Idea

Biblical love is expressed through obedience to God. John brings love and obedience together as the way believers have been called to live from the beginning.

Verse Explanation

“This is love, that we walk according to His commandments.”

John defines love in practical terms. Love is more than affection, emotion, or good intentions; it expresses itself in a life that “walk[s] according to His commandments.” To love God is to take seriously what He says and allow His will to shape how we live. John does not separate a loving heart from an obedient life.

“This is the commandment, that as you have heard from the beginning,”

John again stresses that he is not giving them some new or advanced teaching. “From the beginning” points back to the message and instruction they received when they first came to Christ. In a letter that will shortly warn about deceivers, this matters. They are to remain with the truth they originally received rather than being drawn away by newer claims about Christ.

“you should walk in it.”

The command to love is something believers are to “walk in.” John moves from love as something commanded to love as a way of life. The Christian life is not simply knowing God's commands but continually ordering our lives according to them.

Study Notes

Verse 6 deliberately circles together three ideas: love, commandments, and walking. Love is defined by walking according to God's commandments, and the commandment they have heard is something they are to walk in. John is making obedience and love inseparable rather than treating them as competing emphases.

There is a close connection with Jesus' words in John 14:15 and 15:10. John is carrying forward Jesus' teaching that love for Him and obedience to His commands belong together.

The final “it” most naturally refers to the commandment to love one another from verse 5. John is therefore saying that love is not merely something believers agree with; it is the path in which they are to live.

Spirit & Life

John gives love substance. We can easily define love by what we feel or intend, but Scripture brings love under the lordship of Jesus. Genuine love listens to Him and responds to what He says.

There is also a beautiful simplicity in “you should walk in it.” Jesus calls us not merely to occasional acts of love but to a life whose normal pathway is love expressed through obedience.

2 John 1:7

NKJV

2 John 1:7 For many deceivers have gone out into the world who do not confess Jesus Christ as coming in the flesh. This is a deceiver and an antichrist.(NKJV)

NLT

2 John 1:7 I say this because many deceivers have gone out into the world. They deny that Jesus Christ came in a real body. Such a person is a deceiver and an antichrist. (NLT)

Main Idea

John warns that many deceivers are spreading false teaching about Jesus Christ, especially denying the reality of His coming in the flesh. What someone believes and teaches about Jesus matters profoundly.

Verse Explanation

“For many deceivers have gone out into the world”

The word “For” connects John's warning to his call to walk in truth, love, and obedience. There is a reason believers must remain firmly grounded in what they received from the beginning: “many deceivers” are actively spreading teaching that leads people away from the truth about Jesus.

“who do not confess Jesus Christ as coming in the flesh.”

John identifies the central error. These teachers refuse to confess the true Jesus Christ “as coming in the flesh.” Jesus did not merely appear to be human. The eternal Son truly entered human existence and became flesh. John's wording guards the reality of the incarnation against teaching that separated the divine Christ from the genuinely human Jesus or otherwise denied His true humanity.

“This is a deceiver and an antichrist.”

John gives the error a serious name. A teacher who denies the truth about Jesus is not simply mistaken on a secondary issue; he is a “deceiver and an antichrist.” “Antichrist” here describes someone whose teaching stands against the true Christ. John uses similar language in 1 John 2:18, 22 and 4:2-3.

Study Notes

The expression “coming in the flesh” is especially important. The Greek verb is a present participle, erchomenon, literally carrying the idea of “coming.” John is confessing Jesus Christ as the One who has truly come in human flesh, with wording that presents the incarnation as part of His enduring identity rather than a truth that can be discarded.

The false teaching John confronts appears closely related to that addressed in 1 John 4:2-3. We cannot reconstruct every detail of these teachers' beliefs with certainty, so it is better not to force them into a fully developed later heretical system. What John makes unmistakably clear is their decisive error: they deny the truth of Jesus Christ's real coming in the flesh.

John's use of “antichrist” is broader than the future end-times figure many readers immediately associate with the term. In John's letters, there are already “many antichrists” (1 John 2:18). Anyone who opposes or substitutes a false understanding of Christ for the apostolic Christ displays the spirit of antichrist.

Spirit & Life

John reminds us that discernment begins with Jesus. Spiritual teaching is not trustworthy merely because it sounds deep, loving, enlightened, or spiritual. The decisive question is what it does with the true Jesus Christ.

There is also a strong connection with John's repeated emphasis on remaining in what believers heard “from the beginning.” We do not mature beyond Jesus into something deeper. Genuine spiritual maturity takes us deeper into the truth of who He is.

2 John 1:8

NKJV

2 John 1:8 Look to yourselves, that we do not lose those things we worked for, but that we may receive a full reward.(NKJV)

NLT

2 John 1:8 Watch out that you do not lose what we have worked so hard to achieve. Be diligent so that you receive your full reward. (NLT)

Main Idea

John urges believers to remain watchful so that false teaching does not undo the fruit of faithful ministry and rob them of the full reward God desires them to receive.

Verse Explanation

“Look to yourselves,”

John turns the warning directly toward his readers. “Look to yourselves” means they must watch themselves carefully. Because deceivers are active, believers cannot afford spiritual carelessness. John is calling for alertness and discernment so they remain faithful to the truth they have received.

“that we do not lose those things we worked for,”

John is concerned that the spiritual fruit produced through faithful ministry could be damaged if the church embraces false teaching. The language recognizes that genuine labor has already been invested in them. They have received the truth, begun walking in it, and become the fruit of apostolic ministry. John does not want that work undermined.

“but that we may receive a full reward.”

John looks beyond merely avoiding loss. He wants the work to reach its intended completion and receive its “full reward.” The emphasis is not on earning salvation. The language of reward concerns the outcome of faithful service and perseverance. Their choices matter, and continued faithfulness to Christ has eternal significance.

Study Notes

There is a textual variation in this verse. Some manuscripts read essentially “what we worked for” and “that we may receive,” as reflected in the NKJV, while others read “what you worked for” and “that you may receive.” The difference changes whether John emphasizes the labor of the apostles, the believers themselves, or both, but the central warning remains the same: do not allow deception to bring loss to work already accomplished.

“Full reward” fits the broader New Testament distinction between salvation as God's gift and reward associated with faithfulness and service (1 Corinthians 3:8, 12-15; 2 Corinthians 5:10). John is not suggesting that believers purchase eternal life through good works.

Spirit & Life

There is a healthy seriousness in “Look to yourselves.” Past faithfulness does not make present vigilance unnecessary. What God has built in us is worth guarding.

John also reminds us that how we finish matters. God has done real work in our lives, and others have invested in us along the way. The goal is not simply to begin well, but to remain faithful to Jesus and allow His work in us to reach its intended fullness.

2 John 1:9

NKJV

2 John 1:9 Whoever transgresses and does not abide in the doctrine of Christ does not have God. He who abides in the doctrine of Christ has both the Father and the Son.(NKJV)

NLT

2 John 1:9 Anyone who wanders away from this teaching has no relationship with God. But anyone who remains in the teaching of Christ has a relationship with both the Father and the Son. (NLT)

Main Idea

John warns that anyone who moves beyond the true teaching about Christ actually leaves God behind, while the person who remains in that teaching has fellowship with both the Father and the Son.

Verse Explanation

“Whoever transgresses and does not abide in the doctrine of Christ does not have God.”

John exposes the seriousness of the deception he has been warning about. “Transgresses” carries the idea of going beyond or moving ahead. These false teachers apparently presented themselves as advancing into something more enlightened or spiritually developed, but John says that moving beyond the truth about Christ is not progress at all.

The decisive issue is whether someone “abide[s] in the doctrine of Christ.” In context, this especially concerns the apostolic truth about who Jesus is, including His genuine coming in the flesh from verse 7. A person cannot reject the true identity of the Son while claiming to possess a deeper knowledge of God. John says plainly that such a person “does not have God.”

“He who abides in the doctrine of Christ has both the Father and the Son.”

True spiritual life is found not in moving beyond Christ but in remaining in the truth about Him. To “abide” means to remain, continue, or stay. John again emphasizes stability and perseverance in what believers received from the beginning.

John also makes the relationship between the Father and the Son unmistakable. We cannot claim the Father while rejecting the Son. The person who remains in the true teaching about Christ “has both the Father and the Son.”

Study Notes

The NKJV “transgresses” translates proagon, which literally has the sense of going ahead or going beyond. In this context, “goes too far” or “runs ahead” captures the thought well. John may be deliberately challenging teachers who regarded their ideas as spiritual advancement. Their supposed progress had actually carried them outside apostolic truth.

“The doctrine of Christ” could grammatically mean either teaching about Christ or teaching given by Christ. The immediate context strongly favors teaching about Christ, especially John's concern in verse 7 with confessing Jesus Christ “as coming in the flesh.” It likely includes the foundational apostolic teaching concerning His person and work rather than every Christian doctrine without distinction.

This verse is therefore not teaching that every doctrinal disagreement means someone “does not have God.” John is dealing with a fundamental denial concerning Jesus Himself.

Spirit & Life

There is a powerful irony here: it is possible to move forward intellectually while moving backward spiritually. Not everything presented as deeper revelation is deeper truth. If our “progress” takes us beyond the Jesus revealed in Scripture, we have not advanced at all.

John gives us a better picture of maturity: abide. There is endless depth in Jesus, but we discover it by going deeper into Him, not by moving beyond Him.

2 John 1:10

NKJV

2 John 1:10 If anyone comes to you and does not bring this doctrine, do not receive him into your house nor greet him;(NKJV)

NLT

2 John 1:10 If anyone comes to your meeting and does not teach the truth about Christ, don’t invite that person into your home or give any kind of encouragement. (NLT)

Main Idea

John tells believers not to welcome or endorse teachers who bring false teaching about Christ, because Christian hospitality must never become participation in deception.

Verse Explanation

“If anyone comes to you and does not bring this doctrine,”

John continues directly from verse 9. “This doctrine” refers to the true apostolic teaching about Jesus Christ, particularly the truth that the Son truly came in the flesh. John is not addressing ordinary disagreements among Christians. He is dealing with traveling teachers who deny foundational truth about Jesus and seek to spread that teaching among believers.

“do not receive him into your house”

In the first-century church, homes were important places for Christian ministry. Traveling teachers depended upon hospitality for lodging, support, and opportunities to teach, and churches themselves often gathered in homes. Receiving such a teacher could therefore mean more than offering ordinary kindness. It could provide the person with a base from which to spread false teaching and communicate acceptance of his ministry. John tells the church not to give that kind of support.

“nor greet him;”

John's command is stronger than simply refusing lodging. The “greeting” in view should be understood in the context of welcoming and affirming the traveling teacher and his ministry. John is not commanding Christians to be rude or forbidding every ordinary social acknowledgment of someone who believes wrongly. His concern is that believers must not extend a welcome that expresses fellowship with or endorsement of teaching that denies Christ.

Study Notes

Hospitality carried considerable importance in the early church because traveling Christian workers did not have today's network of churches, hotels, communications, and financial support. Compare 3 John 5-8, where John strongly praises believers for receiving and supporting faithful traveling workers. The two letters together provide the balance: support those who faithfully carry the truth, but do not support those who spread deception.

“House” may refer to an individual Christian home, a house where the church met, or both. Either way, receiving a traveling teacher could materially assist his ministry.

The command “nor greet him” should be read with verse 11, which gives John's reason: such a greeting could make the believer a participant in the teacher's “evil deeds.” The issue is therefore not basic courtesy but spiritual partnership and endorsement.

Spirit & Life

John adds an important boundary to Christian love. Love does not require us to support everything someone teaches or does. Sometimes faithfulness to Jesus requires saying no to participation.

This also brings truth and love together again. John has just commanded believers to “love one another,” yet that same love must protect God's people from teaching that distorts Jesus. Biblical love is not undiscerning acceptance. It loves people without helping deception spread.

2 John 1:11

NKJV

2 John 1:11 for he who greets him shares in his evil deeds.(NKJV)

NLT

2 John 1:11 Anyone who encourages such people becomes a partner in their evil work. (NLT)

Main Idea

Supporting or endorsing someone who spreads false teaching about Christ can make us participants in the harm their ministry produces.

Verse Explanation

“for he who greets him”

John explains why he gave the strong instruction in verse 10. In this setting, “greets him” means more than casually saying hello. It refers to extending the kind of welcome that recognizes, encourages, or supports the false teacher and his ministry.

“shares in his evil deeds.”

The issue is participation. By providing hospitality, encouragement, or endorsement to someone spreading serious error about Christ, a believer can “share” in what that person is doing even without personally teaching the error.

John calls these “evil deeds” because the false teaching is not harmless theological speculation. It misrepresents Jesus Christ and leads others away from the truth. Helping such teaching spread means becoming involved in its harmful work.

Study Notes

The word translated “shares” is koinoneo, related to koinonia, often translated “fellowship.” It means to participate in, have a share in, or become a partner with something. John's concern is therefore partnership, not mere social contact.

This verse should be read together with 3 John 8, where John says believers who support faithful servants of the truth become “fellow workers for the truth.” The principle works in both directions. What we support can make us participants in the work being done.

Spirit & Life

John reminds us that our support carries spiritual significance. We participate not only through what we personally say and do, but sometimes through what we endorse, encourage, or help make possible.

That makes discernment an expression of faithfulness to Jesus. We want our encouragement, resources, relationships, and influence to strengthen what is true rather than unintentionally help deception advance.

2 John 1:12

NKJV

2 John 1:12 Having many things to write to you, I did not wish to do so with paper and ink; but I hope to come to you and speak face to face, that our joy may be full.(NKJV)

NLT

2 John 1:12 I have much more to say to you, but I don’t want to do it with paper and ink. For I hope to visit you soon and talk with you face to face. Then our joy will be complete. (NLT)

Main Idea

John deliberately saves much of what he wants to communicate for a personal visit, because face-to-face fellowship can accomplish something written communication cannot and can bring fuller joy.

Verse Explanation

“Having many things to write to you,”

John has more he wants to communicate than what appears in this short letter. The brevity of 2 John is therefore intentional. He has addressed the matters that required immediate attention, especially truth, love, obedience, and the danger of false teachers, while leaving other matters for a future conversation.

“I did not wish to do so with paper and ink;”

John is not criticizing written communication. He is using it at that very moment, and his writings would become part of Scripture. His point is simply that some things are better communicated personally. “Paper and ink” were the ordinary materials of letter writing, but they could not replace personal presence.

“but I hope to come to you and speak face to face,”

John expects to visit them and talk “face to face.” The NKJV expression literally reflects the idiom “mouth to mouth,” emphasizing direct, personal conversation. John values relationship alongside instruction. He does not merely want to send truth to them; he wants to be with them.

“that our joy may be full.”

John anticipates that their personal fellowship will bring “full” or complete joy. Their shared life in Christ is relational, and being together will allow the joy they already possess to reach a greater fullness.

Study Notes

The “paper” John mentions was probably papyrus, the common writing material made from the papyrus plant. “Ink” was typically a dark mixture made largely from soot or carbon combined with a binding agent.

The expression “face to face” translates an idiom literally meaning “mouth to mouth.” It describes direct personal communication and appears similarly in 3 John 14.

There is a textual variation between “our joy” and “your joy.” The NKJV follows the reading “our joy,” emphasizing the mutual joy John and the believers would experience together. Either reading leaves the central thought unchanged: personal fellowship would bring greater fullness of joy.

Spirit & Life

John quietly reminds us that spiritual life is deeply relational. Written teaching is valuable, but God did not design His people to live through information alone. There are dimensions of encouragement, fellowship, discernment, and joy that happen best “face to face.”

There is also something worth noticing in John's priorities. He has “many things” he could say, yet he does not feel compelled to put everything into the letter. He communicates what is necessary now and trusts that the rest can wait for personal fellowship. Not everything important has to be said immediately.

2 John 1:13

NKJV

2 John 1:13 The children of your elect sister greet you. Amen.(NKJV)

NLT

2 John 1:13 Greetings from the children of your sister, chosen by God. (NLT)

Main Idea

John closes with greetings from another Christian community, reinforcing the family bond shared by believers who belong to God.

Verse Explanation

“The children of your elect sister greet you.”

If “the elect lady” in verse 1 represents the church receiving the letter, then “your elect sister” most naturally represents another local church, probably the congregation where John was writing. Her “children” would be the believers in that congregation.

Their greeting is more than a formal ending. Two geographically separate churches are pictured as members of the same spiritual family. Both are “elect,” belonging to God, and their members are brothers and sisters joined together by their shared life in Christ.

“Amen.”

“Amen” expresses affirmation, carrying the sense of “truly” or “so be it.” It provides a simple closing affirmation to the letter.

Study Notes

Verse 13 strengthens the view that “the elect lady and her children” in verse 1 refers figuratively to a church and its members. An individual woman and her literal sister remain grammatically possible, but the church-to-church interpretation gives the letter a natural symmetry: one “elect” sister and her children greet another “elect” sister and her children.

“Amen” is included in the NKJV because it appears in the manuscript tradition underlying its text, but it is absent from a number of early manuscripts and many modern translations. Nothing in the meaning of the letter depends upon its inclusion.

Spirit & Life

The letter ends with a small but beautiful picture of the church. Individual congregations may gather in different places, but they belong to one family because they belong to Christ.

John began with believers who love one another “in truth” and ends with one church greeting another. Truth has not isolated them; it has joined them together. The truth of Christ creates a people who belong to Him and, therefore, to one another.
