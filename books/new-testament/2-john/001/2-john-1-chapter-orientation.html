Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 1
Main Idea:

The Elder calls the church to walk in truth and love, to abide in the doctrine of Christ, and to refuse support to deceivers who deny that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh.

Setting:

The writer calls himself “the Elder,” most likely the apostle John, and writes to “the elect lady and her children” (1:1), most likely a local congregation and its members. Traveling teachers depended on believers’ hospitality, which made the question of whom to welcome urgent.

Chapter Outline:

  • 2 John 1:1–3 — The Elder Greets the Elect Lady in Truth and Love

  • 2 John 1:4–6 — Walking in Truth and in Love

  • 2 John 1:7–9 — Abide in the Doctrine of Christ

  • 2 John 1:10–11 — Do Not Receive the Teacher Who Denies Christ

  • 2 John 1:12–13 — Hope of Speaking Face to Face

Key Verse:

“Whoever transgresses and does not abide in the doctrine of Christ does not have God. He who abides in the doctrine of Christ has both the Father and the Son.” (2 John 1:9, NKJV)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Second John is one of the shortest books in the Bible, and its first four verses are full of one word. The Elder loves the church “in truth,” along with “all those who have known the truth,” because of “the truth which abides in us and will be with us forever” (1:1–2). Truth here is no set of ideas held at arm’s length. It is the truth revealed in and about Jesus, who called Himself “the truth” (John 14:6), and it lives in His people.

That truth has a shape, and its shape is love. John rejoices to find some of the church’s children “walking in truth” (1:4), then pleads for the old commandment “that we love one another” (1:5). He defines love plainly: “that we walk according to His commandments” (1:6). John never asks the church to balance truth against love. Truth is the life in which love is lived.

Then comes the danger. “Many deceivers have gone out into the world who do not confess Jesus Christ as coming in the flesh” (1:7). To deny the real incarnation is to lose God Himself, for whoever leaves the doctrine of Christ “does not have God,” while whoever abides in it “has both the Father and the Son” (1:9). So the church must not welcome such teachers or wish them well in their work (1:10–11). Even this warning comes from love, and the letter ends with John’s longing to see them “face to face, that our joy may be full” (1:12).

Chapter Sections

2 John 1:1–3 — The Elder Greets the Elect Lady in Truth and Love

The Elder greets the elect lady and her children, whom he and all who know the truth love because the truth abides in them. Grace, mercy and peace come from the Father and from Jesus Christ, the Son of the Father, “in truth and love” (1:3), the two words that govern the whole letter.

2 John 1:4–6 — Walking in Truth and in Love

John rejoices over believers walking in truth and urges the commandment they have had from the beginning, to love one another. Love is not left vague: it is walking according to God’s commandments.

2 John 1:7–9 — Abide in the Doctrine of Christ

Many deceivers deny that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh, so the church must guard itself and not lose what has been worked for. Leaving the doctrine of Christ means not having God, while abiding in it means having both the Father and the Son.

2 John 1:10–11 — Do Not Receive the Teacher Who Denies Christ

The church is not to receive into the house or greet a teacher who comes without the doctrine of Christ, because such a welcome shares in his evil work. The command concerns support and endorsement of Christ-denying teaching, not ordinary kindness toward people who believe differently.

2 John 1:12–13 — Hope of Speaking Face to Face

John has much more to say but would rather speak face to face, so that their joy may be full. The children of the elect sister send greetings, a likely picture of one congregation greeting another.

Why This Chapter Matters

Second John shows that truth and love cannot be pulled apart. A church that keeps the truth without love grows cold, and a church that keeps a love detached from truth loses Christ. John holds both together because both come from Him.

The letter also shows why the identity of Jesus matters. The line John draws is not over every disagreement but over whether Jesus Christ truly came in the flesh, and he says that this question decides whether a person has God at all (1:7, 9). Finally, it shows that welcome is a kind of partnership. Believers can be kind to everyone, but they must not lend their homes and blessing to work that leads people away from Christ.

Key Themes to Watch For

Truth that abides. Truth lives in believers and will be with them forever (1:1–4).

Love that obeys. Love is walking according to God’s commandments, the command heard from the beginning (1:5–6).

The incarnation. The decisive confession is Jesus Christ “coming in the flesh” (1:7).

Abiding. Remaining in the doctrine of Christ means having the Father and the Son (1:2, 9).

Joy in fellowship. The letter moves between John’s great joy and his hope of full joy face to face (1:4, 12).

Key Words & Concepts

The Elder. The writer’s title for himself (1:1). He is most likely the apostle John, known to the church as an older leader with pastoral authority.

The elect lady. Most likely a local congregation, with “her children” its members (1:1) and her “elect sister” another church (1:13). Some take her as an individual Christian woman and her family.

Antichrist. Here anyone who denies Jesus’ coming in the flesh (1:7). John teaches elsewhere that many antichrists are already present (1 John 2:18).

Transgresses. The NKJV reads a word meaning to step beyond, while many manuscripts read goes ahead (1:9). Either way it describes leaving the teaching of Christ.

The doctrine of Christ. The apostolic teaching about Christ, especially His Person and incarnation (1:9–10).

Receive and greet. Welcoming a traveling teacher and wishing him well meant supporting his ministry (1:10–11; compare 3 John 5–8). John forbids that support for those who deny Christ.
