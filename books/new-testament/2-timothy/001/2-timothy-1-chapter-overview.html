Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 1
Main Idea:

Writing in chains, Paul calls Timothy, his beloved son, to stir up God’s gift, to share in suffering for the gospel without shame, and, with the help of the Holy Spirit, to guard the gospel Paul entrusted to him.

Setting:

Paul is the Lord’s prisoner in Rome (1:8, 17), and he reports that all those in Asia have turned away from him (1:15). Timothy, probably still serving in or near Ephesus, faces the same pressure to be ashamed of the gospel and of the apostle in chains.

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Second Timothy 1 is a father’s charge to his son, and Paul gives his love before his commands. Chained in Rome, he calls Timothy “a beloved son,” prays for him night and day, remembers his tears, and longs to see him (1:2–4). He thanks God for the “genuine faith” that lived first in Timothy’s grandmother Lois and his mother Eunice and now lives in Timothy (1:5). Every command that follows grows out of that faith and that affection.

The word “Therefore” turns thanksgiving into a charge. Timothy is to stir up God’s gift, because God has given a spirit “of power and of love and of a sound mind” (1:6–7), and he is to share in suffering for the gospel “according to the power of God” (1:8). That gospel is worth the suffering. God saved us by His own purpose and grace, given in Christ “before time began,” and Christ “has abolished death and brought life and immortality to light” (1:9–10). Facing death himself, Paul can say “I am not ashamed” (1:12), because Christ has already defeated the death that awaits him.

Two acts of entrusting stand side by side. Paul has committed his life and future to Christ, who is able to keep it “until that Day” (1:12), and Timothy must guard the gospel Paul entrusted to him. He does so “by the Holy Spirit who dwells in us” (1:14). The chapter closes by setting a crowd beside one man. Paul reports that all those in Asia turned away from him, but Onesiphorus “was not ashamed of my chain” and searched Rome until he found Paul (1:15–17). In Onesiphorus, Timothy can see exactly what Paul is asking of him.

Chapter Sections

2 Timothy 1:1–2 — Paul Greets His Beloved Son

Paul writes as an apostle by God’s will and ties his calling to “the promise of life which is in Christ Jesus,” even as he faces death. He greets Timothy as “a beloved son,” the relationship that stands behind every command that follows.

2 Timothy 1:3–5 — Thanksgiving for Timothy’s Genuine Faith

From prison Paul thanks God, prays for Timothy night and day, and longs to see him. His thanksgiving centers on the genuine faith that lived first in Lois and Eunice and that Paul is persuaded now lives in Timothy.

2 Timothy 1:6–7 — Stir Up the Gift of God

Because his faith is real, Timothy is to rekindle the gift God gave him for ministry when Paul laid hands on him. He can do it because God gives power, love, and a sound mind, not fear.

2 Timothy 1:8–12 — Sharing in Gospel Suffering Without Shame

Timothy must not be ashamed of the Lord’s testimony or of Paul the prisoner, but must share in suffering by God’s power for the gospel of grace given in Christ before time began and revealed in the Savior who abolished death. Paul suffers for that gospel without shame, persuaded that Christ is able to keep the life and future Paul has entrusted to Him until that Day.

2 Timothy 1:13–14 — Holding Fast the Sound Words and Guarding the Deposit

Timothy must hold fast the pattern of sound words he heard from Paul, keeping it in faith and love that are in Christ Jesus. Relying on the Holy Spirit who dwells in believers, he must guard the gospel and sound teaching committed to him.

2 Timothy 1:15–18 — Those Who Turned Away and Onesiphorus Who Stood Firm

Paul says that all those in Asia, Phygellus and Hermogenes among them, have turned away from him, but Onesiphorus often refreshed him, was not ashamed of his chain, and searched for him in Rome until he found him. Paul asks the Lord to show mercy to his household and to grant Onesiphorus mercy in that Day.

Why This Chapter Matters

Second Timothy 1 shows where courage for ministry comes from. Paul points Timothy to a gift God gave, to suffering endured “according to the power of God,” and to the Holy Spirit who dwells in believers and enables Timothy to guard the gospel (1:6–8, 14). Faithfulness under pressure rests on what God supplies.

The chapter also keeps sound teaching and love together. Timothy must keep Paul’s teaching intact, yet hold it “in faith and love which are in Christ Jesus” (1:13). Onesiphorus shows what that looks like: his loyalty to the gospel led him to risk being linked with a chained prisoner. Paul’s prayer that he “may find mercy from the Lord in that Day” (1:18) sets that costly kindness in the light of Christ’s return.

Key Themes to Watch For

Shame and courage. Timothy must not be ashamed, Paul is not ashamed, and Onesiphorus was not ashamed of Paul’s chain (1:8, 12, 16).

Faith passed on. Genuine faith lived first in Lois and Eunice, the same genuine faith took root in Timothy, and Paul’s sound teaching now rests in his care (1:5, 13–14).

God’s power for suffering. God gives power, love, and a sound mind, and Timothy shares in suffering according to God’s power rather than his own (1:7–8).

Entrusting and guarding. Paul entrusts himself to Christ, while Timothy, with the help of the Holy Spirit, guards the gospel Paul entrusted to him (1:12–14).

That Day. Christ’s coming day gives weight to Paul’s confidence and to his prayer for Onesiphorus (1:12, 18).

Key Words & Concepts

The gift of God (1:6). God’s enabling for the ministry to which Timothy was set apart when Paul laid hands on him. Paul does not list what the gift includes. The Greek verb behind “stir up” means to rekindle, as a person fans a fire back into flame.

A sound mind (1:7). A clear, disciplined, and self-controlled mind. Together with power and love, it is what God gives in place of fear.

Appearing (1:10). Here the word refers to Christ’s first coming, when He abolished death. Later in the letter the same word looks ahead to His return (4:1, 8).

That good thing which was committed to you (1:14). Something handed over for safekeeping. For Timothy it is the gospel and sound teaching Paul passed on to him. In 1:12, Paul uses similar language for his own life and future, which he has entrusted to Christ.

Asia (1:15). The Roman province in the western part of modern Turkey, where Ephesus was the leading city. Paul says that those in Asia turned away from him, but he does not explain their reasons.
