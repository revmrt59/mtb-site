2 Timothy 2 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Second Timothy 2 turns the charge to guard the gospel into a charge to pass it on and endure for it. Strengthened by grace and anchored in the risen Christ, Timothy is to entrust Paul’s teaching to faithful people, accept hardship, handle the word of truth rightly, keep himself clean for the Master’s use, and correct those in opposition with gentleness in the hope that God will grant them repentance.

Chapter Sections

1. 2 Timothy 2:1–2 — Strong in Grace and Entrusting the Teaching

Paul tells Timothy, his son, to “be strong in the grace that is in Christ Jesus” (2 Timothy 2:1). The things Timothy heard from Paul among many witnesses he must commit to faithful men who will be able to teach others also.

2. 2 Timothy 2:3–7 — The Soldier, the Athlete, and the Farmer

Timothy must endure hardship as a good soldier of Jesus Christ, for a soldier keeps himself from becoming entangled in the affairs of this life so that he may please the one who enlisted him. The athlete who competes by the rules and the hardworking farmer who shares first in the crops complete the picture, and Paul tells Timothy to consider these things while praying that the Lord will give him understanding in all things.

3. 2 Timothy 2:8–10 — Remember the Risen Christ and the Unchained Word

Paul calls Timothy to remember that Jesus Christ, of the seed of David, was raised from the dead according to Paul’s gospel. For that gospel Paul suffers chains as if he were an evildoer, yet “the word of God is not chained” (2 Timothy 2:9), and he endures all things for the sake of the elect so that they also may obtain salvation in Christ Jesus with eternal glory.

4. 2 Timothy 2:11–13 — The Faithful Saying

This faithful saying offers two promises and a warning: those who died with Christ will live with Him, those who endure will reign with Him, and “If we deny Him, He also will deny us” (2 Timothy 2:12). Its fourth line, “If we are faithless, He remains faithful; He cannot deny Himself” (2 Timothy 2:13), rests the whole saying on Christ’s own character, so that He stays true both to His promises and to His warning.

5. 2 Timothy 2:14–18 — The Approved Worker and the Spreading Error

Timothy must remind those he teaches of these things and charge them before the Lord not to strive about words, to the ruin of the hearers, while he himself works to be approved by God, “rightly dividing the word of truth” (2 Timothy 2:15). He must shun profane and idle babblings, whose message spreads like cancer, as seen in Hymenaeus and Philetus, who say the resurrection is already past and are overthrowing the faith of some.

6. 2 Timothy 2:19–21 — God’s Firm Foundation and Vessels for Honor

Despite that spreading error, God’s solid foundation stands, sealed with the assurance that the Lord knows those who are His and with the call for everyone who names Christ to depart from iniquity. In a great house there are vessels for honor and for dishonor, and whoever cleanses himself “from the latter” (2 Timothy 2:21) will be a vessel for honor, sanctified and useful for the Master, “prepared for every good work” (2 Timothy 2:21).

7. 2 Timothy 2:22–26 — The Lord’s Servant Who Corrects with Gentleness

Timothy is to flee youthful lusts, pursue righteousness, faith, love, and peace with those who call on the Lord out of a pure heart, and avoid foolish disputes. The Lord’s servant must not quarrel but be gentle, able to teach, and patient, correcting opponents in humility in the hope that God will grant them repentance, so that they may know the truth and escape the snare of the devil.
