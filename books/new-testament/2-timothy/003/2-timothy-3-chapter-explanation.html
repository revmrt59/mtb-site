Chapter Study

2 Timothy 3

Verse-by-Verse
2 Timothy 3:1
2 Timothy 3:1
NKJV

2 Timothy 3:1 But know this, that in the last days perilous times will come:(NKJV)

NLT

2 Timothy 3:1 You should know this, Timothy, that in the last days there will be very difficult times. (NLT)

Main Idea

Paul tells Timothy to know that in the last days perilous times will come. Read in light of Acts 2:16–21, the last days span the whole period from Pentecost to Christ’s return, so Timothy already lives in the times Paul describes.

Verse Explanation

Paul tells Timothy to know that in the last days perilous times will come. Read in light of Acts 2:16–21, the last days span the whole period from Pentecost to Christ’s return, so Timothy already lives in the times Paul describes.

“But know this,”

Paul turns to a warning. “But know this” calls for Timothy’s full attention. What follows is not a guess or a passing remark. Timothy must understand it clearly, because it describes the setting in which he must minister.

“that in the last days perilous times will come:”

“The last days” describes the period that began when Christ came and the Spirit was poured out at Pentecost, and that will end with Christ’s return. Peter used the same expression at Pentecost when he quoted Joel: “And it shall come to pass in the last days, says God, That I will pour out of My Spirit on all flesh” (Acts 2:17). Timothy was already living in the last days. That is why Paul will tell him to turn away from such people now (3:5).

In these days “perilous times will come.” “Perilous” means hard, difficult, dangerous. These are times that are hard to live through and hard to minister in. Paul is not describing a single crisis at the end of history. He is describing the kind of times that mark the whole age between Christ’s first and second comings. They come and go, and they may grow more intense, but they are always present in some form. The passage gives no timetable for Christ’s return.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “perilous” means difficult, harsh, or dangerous. It describes the two demon-possessed men in Matthew 8:28 as “exceedingly fierce.”

Paul’s first letter gives a similar warning: “Now the Spirit expressly says that in latter times some will depart from the faith” (1 Timothy 4:1).

2 Timothy 3:2
2 Timothy 3:2
NKJV

2 Timothy 3:2 For men will be lovers of themselves, lovers of money, boasters, proud, blasphemers, disobedient to parents, unthankful, unholy,(NKJV)

NLT

2 Timothy 3:2 For people will love only themselves and their money. They will be boastful and proud, scoffing at God, disobedient to their parents, and ungrateful. They will consider nothing sacred. (NLT)

Main Idea

People will be lovers of themselves, lovers of money, boasters, proud, blasphemers, disobedient to parents, unthankful, and unholy. The list begins with love turned inward, the root from which the other sins grow.

Verse Explanation

People will be lovers of themselves, lovers of money, boasters, proud, blasphemers, disobedient to parents, unthankful, and unholy. The list begins with love turned inward, the root from which the other sins grow.

“For men will be lovers of themselves,”

“For” explains why the times are perilous. The danger comes from what people will be like. Paul begins a long list of descriptions, and the first is the root of the rest: “lovers of themselves.” When people make themselves the center of their love, everything else falls out of order. Love meant for God and for others turns inward.

“lovers of money,”

“Lovers of money” follows naturally. Those who love themselves want what money can buy: comfort, security, pleasure, and status. Paul warned in his first letter that “the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil” (1 Timothy 6:10).

“boasters, proud,”

“Boasters” talk themselves up, claiming more than is true. “Proud” describes people who think themselves above others and look down on them. The first is about words, the second about attitude, and both grow from self-love.

“blasphemers,”

“Blasphemers” speak abusively. The word can refer to speaking against God or slandering people. Self-centered people do not hesitate to tear down anyone, including God, who stands in their way.

“disobedient to parents,”

“Disobedient to parents” describes a breakdown in the family. Children who refuse to honor their parents reject the first human authority God gives. Paul includes this sin in a similar list in Romans 1:30.

“unthankful, unholy,”

“Unthankful” people take what they receive without gratitude. Those who love themselves think they deserve everything, so they thank no one, not even God. “Unholy” describes people who have no regard for what is sacred. Nothing is set apart for God in their lives. They treat holy things as common.

Study Notes

The Greek words translated “lovers of themselves” and “lovers of money” both begin with the Greek word for love. The list ends in 3:4 with two more words formed the same way: “lovers of pleasure” and “lovers of God.” The list opens and closes with what people love.

The Greek word translated “proud” describes someone who shows himself above others. It is the word used in James 4:6 and 1 Peter 5:5: “God resists the proud.”

2 Timothy 3:3
2 Timothy 3:3
NKJV

2 Timothy 3:3 unloving, unforgiving, slanderers, without self-control, brutal, despisers of good,(NKJV)

NLT

2 Timothy 3:3 They will be unloving and unforgiving; they will slander others and have no self-control. They will be cruel and hate what is good. (NLT)

Main Idea

Paul continues the description: unloving, unforgiving, slanderers, without self-control, brutal, and despisers of good. When love is turned inward, ordinary affection, restraint, and respect for what is good break down.

Verse Explanation

Paul continues the description: unloving, unforgiving, slanderers, without self-control, brutal, and despisers of good. When love is turned inward, ordinary affection, restraint, and respect for what is good break down.

“unloving, unforgiving,”

Paul continues the list. “Unloving” describes people without natural affection, cold even toward their own families. The love that should exist between parents and children, husbands and wives, has dried up. “Unforgiving” describes people who will not make peace. They refuse to be reconciled and hold onto their grudges.

“slanderers,”

“Slanderers” spread false and damaging stories about others. The Greek word is the same word used for the devil, the great slanderer. People who slander are doing the devil’s work.

“without self-control,”

People “without self-control” cannot master their desires. Whatever they want, they take. They have no inner discipline to restrain them.

“brutal,”

“Brutal” describes people who are savage and cruel. Without love and without self-control, people become fierce and harsh toward others.

“despisers of good,”

“Despisers of good” describes people who do not love what is good. They may even hate it. Goodness has no attraction for them, and good people may annoy or threaten them.

When love turns inward, ordinary affection, restraint, and respect for what is good break down. The list in this verse shows what happens to human relationships when self takes God’s place.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “unloving” means without natural family affection. It appears also in Romans 1:31.

The Greek word translated “unforgiving” originally described someone who would not agree to a truce. It describes a person who refuses reconciliation.

The Greek word translated “slanderers” is the word used throughout the New Testament for the devil, whose name means “slanderer.” In 1 Timothy 3:11 and Titus 2:3 it describes people who slander others.

2 Timothy 3:4
2 Timothy 3:4
NKJV

2 Timothy 3:4 traitors, headstrong, haughty, lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God,(NKJV)

NLT

2 Timothy 3:4 They will betray their friends, be reckless, be puffed up with pride, and love pleasure rather than God. (NLT)

Main Idea

The people Paul describes will be traitors, headstrong, haughty, “lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God.” The list ends as it began, with loves that are disordered because God is not loved first.

Verse Explanation

The people Paul describes will be traitors, headstrong, haughty, “lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God.” The list ends as it began, with loves that are disordered because God is not loved first.

“traitors,”

“Traitors” betray those who trust them. They hand others over for their own gain. Luke uses the same word for Judas Iscariot, “who also became a traitor” (Luke 6:16).

“headstrong,”

“Headstrong” describes people who are reckless and rash, rushing ahead without thinking. They act on impulse and do not consider the consequences.

“haughty,”

“Haughty” means puffed up, swollen with conceit. Such people think much more highly of themselves than they should.

“lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God,”

The list ends with a summary that matches its beginning: “lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God.” Paul began with “lovers of themselves” (3:2) and ends here. People love pleasure more than they love God. They would rather enjoy themselves than know, worship, or obey Him. The words “rather than” show that this is a matter of choice and priority. God is not loved first, and that is the root disorder behind the whole list.

The list ends as it began, with loves that are disordered because God is not loved first. All the other sins in the list flow from this misplaced love.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “haughty” means puffed up or conceited. Paul uses the same word in 1 Timothy 3:6, where a new believer may be “puffed up with pride.”

The two words “lovers of pleasure” and “lovers of God” are formed the same way in Greek and sound alike. The wordplay sets the two loves side by side.

2 Timothy 3:5
2 Timothy 3:5
NKJV

2 Timothy 3:5 having a form of godliness but denying its power. And from such people turn away!(NKJV)

NLT

2 Timothy 3:5 They will act religious, but they will reject the power that could make them godly. Stay away from people like that! (NLT)

Main Idea

Such people keep up the outward appearance of godliness while denying its power, and Paul commands Timothy, “from such people turn away!” His command shows that these dangers are already present in Timothy’s day.

Verse Explanation

Such people keep up the outward appearance of godliness while denying its power, and Paul commands Timothy, “from such people turn away!” His command shows that these dangers are already present in Timothy’s day.

“having a form of godliness”

The people Paul describes have “a form of godliness.” They look religious. They may attend gatherings, use spiritual language, and keep religious customs. Their outward appearance suggests devotion to God. This makes the danger worse. Open godlessness is easier to recognize than godlessness dressed in religious clothing.

“but denying its power.”

They have the form of godliness, “but denying its power.” Real godliness has power. It changes people from the inside, producing love for God and others, self-control, humility, and holiness. These people keep the outward shape but reject the inner reality. Their lives show no sign of God’s transforming power. Their conduct denies what their religion claims.

“And from such people turn away!”

Paul gives a direct command: “from such people turn away!” Timothy must avoid them, refuse to follow them, and keep himself and the church from their influence. The command shows that these dangers are already present in Timothy’s day. Paul is not describing a distant future. He is telling Timothy how to respond to people he is already facing.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “form” refers to an outward shape or appearance. Paul uses the same word in Romans 2:20, where the Jewish teacher has “the form of knowledge and truth in the law.”

The Greek verb translated “turn away” appears only here in the New Testament. Its present tense means that Timothy is to keep away from such people as a continuing practice.

2 Timothy 3:6–9 — Deceivers Who Resist the Truth

2 Timothy 3:6
2 Timothy 3:6
NKJV

2 Timothy 3:6 For of this sort are those who creep into households and make captives of gullible women loaded down with sins, led away by various lusts,(NKJV)

NLT

2 Timothy 3:6 They are the kind who work their way into people’s homes and win the confidence of vulnerable women who are burdened with the guilt of sin and controlled by various desires. (NLT)

Main Idea

Some of these people creep into households and make captives of gullible women loaded down with sins and led away by various lusts. The deceivers gain control by working on people already burdened and unsettled.

Verse Explanation

Some of these people creep into households and make captives of gullible women loaded down with sins and led away by various lusts. The deceivers gain control by working on people already burdened and unsettled.

“For of this sort are those who creep into households”

“For” introduces a closer description of some of these people. “Of this sort” points to a particular group: false teachers who “creep into households.” The word “creep” suggests slipping in quietly, worming their way in. They do not announce their aims openly. They gain entrance into homes and spread their teaching privately, away from the church’s oversight.

“and make captives of gullible women”

These teachers “make captives of gullible women.” “Captives” pictures prisoners of war taken by force. The false teachers capture people and bring them under their control.

Their targets are “gullible women.” The Greek word is a diminutive form of the word for women, used to describe them as weak or vulnerable. Paul is not saying that all women are gullible. He is describing particular women whose situation, as the rest of the verse explains, made them easy targets.

“loaded down with sins,”

These women were “loaded down with sins.” They carried a heavy burden of guilt. People weighed down by sin often look for relief, and false teachers can exploit that need. They offer answers that seem to ease the burden without bringing the person to genuine repentance and forgiveness in Christ.

“led away by various lusts,”

The women were also “led away by various lusts.” Their desires pulled them in many directions. Without stable faith and self-control, they were easily drawn after whatever new teaching appealed to them. The deceivers gained control by working on people already burdened and unsettled.

Study Notes

The Greek verb translated “make captives” is used in Luke 21:24 for people “led away captive into all nations.” It describes taking prisoners in war.

The Greek word translated “gullible women” appears only here in the New Testament.

Paul’s first letter mentions younger widows who went “from house to house” and spoke “things which they ought not” (1 Timothy 5:13). That passage does not say they spread the false teaching described here. It does show that visits from house to house were already a concern in the church Timothy served.

2 Timothy 3:7
2 Timothy 3:7
NKJV

2 Timothy 3:7 always learning and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth.(NKJV)

NLT

2 Timothy 3:7 (Such women are forever following new teachings, but they are never able to understand the truth.) (NLT)

Main Idea

The women taken captive by these false teachers are “always learning and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth.” Their constant learning never reaches the truth, and that leaves them open to the deceivers.

Verse Explanation

The women taken captive by these false teachers are “always learning and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth.” Their constant learning never reaches the truth, and that leaves them open to the deceivers.

“always learning and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth.”

These women are “always learning.” They keep listening, studying, and seeking new ideas. But they are “never able to come to the knowledge of the truth.” Their learning never reaches its goal. They gather information but never arrive at the truth that saves and transforms.

Their problem is not a lack of exposure to teaching. It is the kind of teaching they follow and the direction of their hearts. The false teachers kept them learning without leading them to truth. Their constant learning leaves them open to the deceivers, because people who never settle on the truth will keep following whoever offers something new.

The verse warns against learning that becomes an end in itself. Real knowledge of the truth leads to faith in Christ, repentance, and a changed life (2:25). Learning that never arrives there cannot help a person. By contrast, Timothy learned the truth from Paul and from the Scriptures (3:14–15), and his learning led him to salvation through faith in Christ.

Study Notes

The phrase “knowledge of the truth” appears also in 2:25, where Paul hopes that God may grant opponents repentance “so that they may know the truth.” The Greek uses the same word for full knowledge in both places.

2 Timothy 3:8
2 Timothy 3:8
NKJV

2 Timothy 3:8 Now as Jannes and Jambres resisted Moses, so do these also resist the truth: men of corrupt minds, disapproved concerning the faith;(NKJV)

NLT

2 Timothy 3:8 These teachers oppose the truth just as Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses. They have depraved minds and a counterfeit faith. (NLT)

Main Idea

Just as Jannes and Jambres resisted Moses, these men resist the truth, for they are men of corrupt minds and disapproved concerning the faith. Their opposition belongs to a long pattern of resisting God’s truth and God’s servants.

Verse Explanation

Just as Jannes and Jambres resisted Moses, these men resist the truth, for they are men of corrupt minds and disapproved concerning the faith. Their opposition belongs to a long pattern of resisting God’s truth and God’s servants.

“Now as Jannes and Jambres resisted Moses,”

Paul compares the false teachers to “Jannes and Jambres,” who “resisted Moses.” These names do not appear in the Old Testament. They are the names Jewish tradition gave to the magicians of Pharaoh’s court who opposed Moses in Egypt (Exodus 7:11, 22). When Moses performed signs from God, the magicians imitated some of them with their enchantments, trying to discredit Moses.

The comparison fits. Just as the magicians imitated God’s power while opposing God’s servant, the false teachers imitated godliness while opposing the truth (3:5).

“so do these also resist the truth:”

The false teachers “also resist the truth.” Their opposition to Paul and Timothy is opposition to the truth itself. Their opposition belongs to a long pattern of resisting God’s truth and God’s servants.

“men of corrupt minds,”

They are “men of corrupt minds.” Their thinking is ruined, twisted away from the truth. Their problem is not merely a lack of information. Their minds themselves have been corrupted.

“disapproved concerning the faith;”

They are also “disapproved concerning the faith.” “Disapproved” means tested and found worthless, failing the test. When their faith is examined, it proves false. This is the opposite of what Timothy is called to be: “approved to God” (2:15).

Study Notes

The names Jannes and Jambres appear in later Jewish writings. They are named, for example, in an Aramaic paraphrase of Exodus (Targum Pseudo-Jonathan on Exodus 7:11). Paul uses the names known to his readers without endorsing the stories attached to them.

The Greek word translated “disapproved” is the negative of the word translated “approved” in 2:15. Paul uses the same word in Titus 1:16, where false teachers are “disqualified for every good work.”

2 Timothy 3:9
2 Timothy 3:9
NKJV

2 Timothy 3:9 but they will progress no further, for their folly will be manifest to all, as theirs also was.(NKJV)

NLT

2 Timothy 3:9 But they won’t get away with this for long. Someday everyone will recognize what fools they are, just as with Jannes and Jambres. (NLT)

Main Idea

But they will progress no further, for their folly will become plain to all, as the folly of those who opposed Moses did. Their advance has limits, and in time their error is exposed.

Verse Explanation

But they will progress no further, for their folly will become plain to all, as the folly of those who opposed Moses did. Their advance has limits, and in time their error is exposed.

“but they will progress no further,”

“But” brings comfort. The false teachers “will progress no further.” Their advance has limits. They may succeed for a while, but they will not keep advancing forever. God sets boundaries on how far error can go.

“for their folly will be manifest to all,”

The reason is that “their folly will be manifest to all.” “Folly” describes senselessness, foolishness, a lack of understanding. In time, the foolishness of their teaching will become clear to everyone. Error cannot hide forever. Eventually its emptiness is exposed.

“as theirs also was.”

This happened to Jannes and Jambres: “as theirs also was.” Pharaoh’s magicians could imitate some of Moses’ signs, but they could not keep up. When the plague of lice came, they could not reproduce it, and they admitted, “This is the finger of God” (Exodus 8:18–19). Later they could not even stand before Moses because of the boils (Exodus 9:11). Their folly was exposed. The same will happen to the false teachers in Timothy’s day.

Study Notes

The Greek verb translated “progress” is the same verb used in 2:16 (“will increase to more ungodliness”) and 3:13 (“will grow worse and worse”). The false teachers do advance in ungodliness and deception, but their influence will not advance indefinitely.

The Greek word translated “folly” means senselessness or a lack of understanding. Luke uses it in Luke 6:11, where the scribes and Pharisees “were filled with rage” after Jesus healed on the Sabbath, a senseless fury.

2 Timothy 3:10–13 — Paul’s Example Amid Persecution

2 Timothy 3:10
2 Timothy 3:10
NKJV

2 Timothy 3:10 But you have carefully followed my doctrine, manner of life, purpose, faith, longsuffering, love, perseverance,(NKJV)

NLT

2 Timothy 3:10 But you, Timothy, certainly know what I teach, and how I live, and what my purpose in life is. You know my faith, my patience, my love, and my endurance. (NLT)

Main Idea

In contrast, Timothy has carefully followed Paul’s doctrine, manner of life, purpose, faith, longsuffering, love, and perseverance. Timothy has seen the truth lived out in a whole life, not merely heard it taught.

Verse Explanation

In contrast, Timothy has carefully followed Paul’s doctrine, manner of life, purpose, faith, longsuffering, love, and perseverance. Timothy has seen the truth lived out in a whole life, not merely heard it taught.

“But you have carefully followed”

“But you” marks a sharp turn. Paul has described false teachers. Now he turns to Timothy, who is completely different. Timothy “carefully followed” Paul. The word means to follow closely, to pay close attention, to observe carefully. Timothy had not only heard Paul teach. He had watched Paul’s life closely for years.

“my doctrine, manner of life, purpose,”

Timothy followed Paul’s “doctrine,” his teaching. He knew what Paul believed and taught. He followed Paul’s “manner of life,” the way Paul lived day by day. He followed Paul’s “purpose,” the aim that drove Paul’s life and ministry.

“faith, longsuffering, love, perseverance,”

Timothy also saw Paul’s “faith,” his trust in God and faithfulness to Him. He saw Paul’s “longsuffering,” patience with difficult people. He saw Paul’s “love,” for God and for people. He saw Paul’s “perseverance,” endurance through hardship without giving up.

The list contrasts sharply with the list of vices in 3:2–5. The false teachers love themselves, money, and pleasure. Paul’s life was marked by faith, patience, love, and endurance. Timothy has seen the truth lived out in a whole life, not merely heard it taught.

Study Notes

The Greek verb translated “carefully followed” is the same verb used in 1 Timothy 4:6 for “the good doctrine which you have carefully followed.” It can describe following someone as a disciple or following events closely as an observer.

2 Timothy 3:11
2 Timothy 3:11
NKJV

2 Timothy 3:11 persecutions, afflictions, which happened to me at Antioch, at Iconium, at Lystra"”what persecutions I endured. And out of them all the Lord delivered me.(NKJV)

NLT

2 Timothy 3:11 You know how much persecution and suffering I have endured. You know all about how I was persecuted in Antioch, Iconium, and Lystra — but the Lord rescued me from all of it. (NLT)

Main Idea

Timothy also knows the persecutions and afflictions that came to Paul at Antioch, at Iconium, and at Lystra, and that the Lord delivered him out of them all. Paul’s sufferings and the Lord’s deliverance belong together in the example Timothy has followed.

Verse Explanation

Timothy also knows the persecutions and afflictions that came to Paul at Antioch, at Iconium, and at Lystra, and that the Lord delivered him out of them all. Paul’s sufferings and the Lord’s deliverance belong together in the example Timothy has followed.

“persecutions, afflictions,”

Timothy also knew Paul’s “persecutions” and “afflictions.” “Persecutions” are attacks by people who oppose the gospel. “Afflictions” are sufferings of any kind. Paul’s faithfulness brought him trouble, and Timothy had seen it.

“which happened to me at Antioch, at Iconium, at Lystra—”

Paul names three places: Antioch, Iconium, and Lystra. These cities were in the region of Galatia, in what is now central Turkey. Paul visited them on his first missionary journey. In Antioch of Pisidia, opponents stirred up persecution and expelled Paul and Barnabas (Acts 13:50). In Iconium, a plot was made to abuse and stone them, and they fled (Acts 14:5–6). In Lystra, Paul was stoned and dragged out of the city, and his attackers supposed he was dead (Acts 14:19).

Lystra was Timothy’s hometown (Acts 16:1). Timothy may well have seen or heard firsthand what Paul suffered there. Paul’s choice of these three cities would have stirred Timothy’s memory.

“what persecutions I endured.”

Paul “endured” these persecutions. He did not quit or turn back. He kept preaching and kept going. His endurance is the example Timothy has followed.

“And out of them all the Lord delivered me.”

Most important, “out of them all the Lord delivered me.” Paul suffered, but the Lord rescued him every time. Paul did not escape suffering, but he was not destroyed by it. The Lord brought him through. This echoes Psalm 34:19: “Many are the afflictions of the righteous, But the LORD delivers him out of them all.” Paul’s sufferings and the Lord’s deliverance belong together in the example Timothy has followed.

Study Notes

Antioch here is Antioch in Pisidia (Acts 13:14), not the Syrian city of Antioch where believers were first called Christians.

The Greek verb translated “delivered” is the same verb Paul uses in 4:17–18: “I was delivered out of the mouth of the lion,” and “the Lord will deliver me from every evil work.”

2 Timothy 3:12
2 Timothy 3:12
NKJV

2 Timothy 3:12 Yes, and all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution.(NKJV)

NLT

2 Timothy 3:12 Yes, and everyone who wants to live a godly life in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution. (NLT)

Main Idea

Indeed, all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution. What happened to Paul is the expected experience of everyone who seeks a godly life in Christ.

Verse Explanation

Indeed, all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution. What happened to Paul is the expected experience of everyone who seeks a godly life in Christ.

“Yes, and all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus”

Paul widens the view from himself to all believers. “Yes, and all” makes the statement universal. What happened to Paul is not unusual. It is what happens to everyone who seeks a godly life in Christ.

The people Paul has in mind “desire to live godly in Christ Jesus.” They want to live in a way that honors God. Their godliness is “in Christ Jesus,” rooted in union with Him.

“will suffer persecution.”

Such people “will suffer persecution.” Paul does not say they might. He says they will. A world that rejects God does not welcome people who live for Him. Godly living exposes ungodliness, and the world often reacts with hostility. Persecution may take many forms: violence, imprisonment, ridicule, rejection, or loss. But some form of opposition is the expected experience of everyone who seeks a godly life in Christ.

Jesus taught the same thing: “If they persecuted Me, they will also persecute you” (John 15:20). Paul and Barnabas told the new believers in Galatia, “We must through many tribulations enter the kingdom of God” (Acts 14:22).

Study Notes

The Greek verb translated “desire” expresses will or intent, not a passing wish.

The Greek verb translated “will suffer persecution” is related to the noun “persecutions” in 3:11.

2 Timothy 3:13
2 Timothy 3:13
NKJV

2 Timothy 3:13 But evil men and impostors will grow worse and worse, deceiving and being deceived.(NKJV)

NLT

2 Timothy 3:13 But evil people and impostors will flourish. They will deceive others and will themselves be deceived. (NLT)

Main Idea

Meanwhile, evil men and impostors will grow worse and worse, deceiving others and being deceived themselves. Their deception deepens even though their advance has limits.

Verse Explanation

Meanwhile, evil men and impostors will grow worse and worse, deceiving others and being deceived themselves. Their deception deepens even though their advance has limits.

“But evil men and impostors”

“But” contrasts the godly with the ungodly. While the godly suffer persecution, “evil men and impostors” continue their work. “Evil men” are wicked people who do harm. “Impostors” are people who pretend to be what they are not. The word was used for sorcerers, charlatans, and swindlers.

“will grow worse and worse,”

These people “will grow worse and worse.” Their wickedness and deception will deepen. Paul has already said that the false teachers will progress no further (3:9). Their outward advance has limits, but their own corruption will grow deeper.

“deceiving and being deceived.”

Evil men and impostors are “deceiving and being deceived.” They deceive others, and they are themselves deceived. Those who spread lies end up believing them. Their deception deepens even though their advance has limits.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “impostors” was used for sorcerers and swindlers. It fits the comparison with Pharaoh’s magicians in 3:8.

The Greek verb translated “will grow” is the same verb translated “progress” in 3:9. Paul uses it with irony: they do progress, but only toward what is worse.

2 Timothy 3:14–17 — Continuing in the Scriptures Given by God

2 Timothy 3:14
2 Timothy 3:14
NKJV

2 Timothy 3:14 But you must continue in the things which you have learned and been assured of, knowing from whom you have learned them,(NKJV)

NLT

2 Timothy 3:14 But you must remain faithful to the things you have been taught. You know they are true, for you know you can trust those who taught you. (NLT)

Main Idea

But Timothy must continue in the things he has learned and been assured of, knowing from whom he learned them. The faithfulness of those who taught him confirms the truth he has received.

Verse Explanation

But Timothy must continue in the things he has learned and been assured of, knowing from whom he learned them. The faithfulness of those who taught him confirms the truth he has received.

“But you must continue in the things which you have learned and been assured of,”

“But you” again marks a turn to Timothy, as in 3:10. While deceivers grow worse, Timothy “must continue” in what he has learned. He must not move away from it, add to it, or look for something new. He must stay where he is.

He has “learned” these things and “been assured of” them. He has not only heard them but become convinced of their truth. His faith is not a guess. It rests on solid ground.

“knowing from whom you have learned them,”

Timothy knows “from whom you have learned them.” He knows the people who taught him: Paul, and before Paul, his grandmother Lois and mother Eunice (1:5). He knows their character and faithfulness. Their faithfulness confirms the truth he has received. Unlike the false teachers, whose lives contradicted their words, Timothy’s teachers lived what they taught.

Study Notes

The text behind the NKJV reads a singular here, pointing to one teacher. Many other manuscripts read a plural, pointing to more than one. Either way, Timothy’s teachers included Paul, and 1:5 and 3:15 point to his grandmother and mother as well.

The Greek verb translated “been assured of” means to be convinced or fully persuaded.

2 Timothy 3:15

2 Timothy 3:15 You have been taught the holy Scriptures from childhood, and they have given you the wisdom to receive the salvation that comes by trusting in Christ Jesus. (NLT)

Main Idea

From childhood Timothy has known the Holy Scriptures, which are able to make him wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus. The sacred writings of Israel point to Christ, and through faith in Him they lead to salvation.

Verse Explanation

From childhood Timothy has known the Holy Scriptures, which are able to make him wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus. The sacred writings of Israel point to Christ, and through faith in Him they lead to salvation.

“and that from childhood you have known the Holy Scriptures,”

Timothy has known “the Holy Scriptures” “from childhood.” The word for childhood can mean infancy. From his earliest years, Lois and Eunice taught him the sacred writings of Israel, what we call the Old Testament. Jewish families were expected to teach God’s word to their children (Deuteronomy 6:6–7), and Timothy’s family had done so faithfully.

“which are able to make you wise for salvation”

These Scriptures “are able to make you wise for salvation.” They have power to lead a person to salvation. They do not merely give information. They make a person wise, able to understand God’s way of saving sinners.

“through faith which is in Christ Jesus.”

The Scriptures lead to salvation “through faith which is in Christ Jesus.” The Old Testament points to Christ. Its promises, prophecies, and patterns all find their fulfillment in Him. Jesus said, “these are they which testify of Me” (John 5:39). When the Scriptures are read with faith in Christ, they lead to salvation. The sacred writings of Israel point to Christ, and through faith in Him they lead to salvation.

Study Notes

The Greek phrase translated “the Holy Scriptures” is literally the sacred writings. It is a different expression from the word for “Scripture” in 3:16, though both refer to God’s written word.

The Greek word translated “childhood” is the word for an infant or a very young child. It is used for the infant Jesus in Luke 2:12.

2 Timothy 3:16
2 Timothy 3:16
NKJV

2 Timothy 3:16 All Scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness,(NKJV)

NLT

2 Timothy 3:16 All Scripture is inspired by God and is useful to teach us what is true and to make us realize what is wrong in our lives. It corrects us when we are wrong and teaches us to do what is right. (NLT)

Main Idea

All Scripture is given by inspiration of God and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, and for instruction in righteousness. Because Scripture comes from God, it teaches the truth, exposes what is wrong, sets people right, and trains them to live rightly.

Verse Explanation

All Scripture is given by inspiration of God and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, and for instruction in righteousness. Because Scripture comes from God, it teaches the truth, exposes what is wrong, sets people right, and trains them to live rightly.

“All Scripture is given by inspiration of God,”

Paul makes a foundational statement: “All Scripture is given by inspiration of God.” “All” means every part of Scripture, without exception. Here Paul speaks directly of the Old Testament Scriptures Timothy knew from childhood. Every word of them comes from God.

“Given by inspiration of God” translates a single Greek word meaning “God-breathed.” Scripture is breathed out by God. It comes from His mouth. God worked through human authors, but the words they wrote are God’s own words. Peter describes the same process: “holy men of God spoke as they were moved by the Holy Spirit” (2 Peter 1:21). Because Scripture comes from God, it carries His authority and shares His truthfulness.

Paul is speaking directly of the Old Testament Scriptures Timothy knew from childhood (3:15). The apostles’ writings were soon recognized as Scripture as well, and Peter places Paul’s letters alongside “the rest of the Scriptures” (2 Peter 3:16). Receiving Paul’s words for the whole Bible, Old and New Testaments, means confessing that God guided the human authors so that every word in every part of Scripture comes from Him, and that Scripture is without error in all it affirms in the original manuscripts.

“and is profitable for doctrine,”

Because Scripture comes from God, it “is profitable,” useful and beneficial. Paul lists four ways. First, it is profitable “for doctrine,” for teaching. Scripture teaches us what is true about God, ourselves, sin, salvation, and how to live.

“for reproof,”

Second, it is profitable “for reproof.” Scripture exposes what is wrong, in belief and in conduct. It shows us where we have gone astray.

“for correction,”

Third, it is profitable “for correction.” Scripture does not only show what is wrong. It sets things right, restoring us to the right path.

“for instruction in righteousness,”

Fourth, it is profitable “for instruction in righteousness.” Scripture trains us in right living, the way a parent trains a child. It shapes our character and conduct over time.

The four work together. Doctrine teaches what is right. Reproof shows what is wrong. Correction sets us right. Instruction trains us to stay right. Because Scripture comes from God, it teaches the truth, exposes what is wrong, sets people right, and trains them to live rightly.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “given by inspiration of God” means “God-breathed” and occurs only here in the New Testament. It describes Scripture as the product of God’s breath, coming out from Him.

The Greek can also be read “Every Scripture inspired by God is also profitable,” a rendering found in some older translations. On that reading, “inspired by God” describes the Scripture in view, and the main statement is that such Scripture is profitable. That wording does not itself make the direct statement the NKJV makes, that all Scripture is inspired by God. The NKJV rendering is the more natural reading of the Greek, is followed by most translations, and is the reading this study follows.

The Greek word translated “instruction” is the word for training a child. Paul uses the related verb in 2:25 for “correcting” those in opposition.

2 Timothy 3:17
NKJV

NLT

2 Timothy 3:17 God uses it to prepare and equip his people to do every good work. (NLT)

Main Idea

Scripture’s purpose is that the man of God may be complete, “thoroughly equipped for every good work.” In the Word he has already received, Timothy has what he needs for the work God gives him.

Verse Explanation

Scripture’s purpose is that the man of God may be complete, “thoroughly equipped for every good work.” In the Word he has already received, Timothy has what he needs for the work God gives him.

“that the man of God may be complete,”

Paul states Scripture’s purpose: “that the man of God may be complete.” “The man of God” was an Old Testament title for God’s servants, such as Moses and the prophets. Paul called Timothy “man of God” in his first letter (1 Timothy 6:11). Here the phrase describes Timothy and anyone who serves God.

Scripture makes the man of God “complete,” fit and capable, fully able to do what he has been called to do. He lacks nothing he needs.

“thoroughly equipped for every good work.”

He is “thoroughly equipped for every good work.” The picture is of something fully outfitted, like a ship supplied for a voyage. Scripture provides everything God’s servant needs for every task God gives him.

This was crucial for Timothy. He faced false teachers, opposition, and the loss of his spiritual father. What did he need to stand firm and fulfill his ministry? He needed the Scriptures. In the Word he has already received, Timothy has what he needs for the work God gives him. He does not need a new teaching or a secret knowledge. He needs to continue in the Word he has been given and preach it (4:2).

The phrase “every good work” recalls 2:21, where the vessel cleansed for the Master is “prepared for every good work.” God prepares His servant by cleansing him and equips him through His word.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “complete” and the verb translated “thoroughly equipped” come from the same root. The wordplay stresses that Scripture makes God’s servant fully ready.

“Man of God” is used in the Old Testament for Moses (Deuteronomy 33:1) and for prophets such as Elijah and Elisha.
