2 Timothy 3 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Second Timothy 3 sets the corruption and deception of the last days against the teaching, life, and Scriptures Timothy has received. Paul describes disordered loves and empty godliness, exposes deceivers who resist the truth, and then twice turns to Timothy with “But you” (2 Timothy 3:10, 14) to point him to Paul’s example and to the Scriptures, given by inspiration of God, that make him wise for salvation and fully equip him for every good work.

Chapter Sections

1. 2 Timothy 3:1–5 — Perilous Times and a Form of Godliness

Paul warns that in the last days perilous times will come, when people will be lovers of themselves, lovers of money, and “lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God” (2 Timothy 3:4). They will keep up the appearance of godliness while denying its power, and Paul tells Timothy, “from such people turn away!” (2 Timothy 3:5).

2. 2 Timothy 3:6–9 — Deceivers Who Resist the Truth

Some of these people creep into households and make captives of gullible women loaded down with sins, who are “always learning and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth” (2 Timothy 3:7). As Jannes and Jambres resisted Moses, these men of corrupt minds resist the truth, but they will progress no further, for their folly will be plain to all.

3. 2 Timothy 3:10–13 — Paul’s Example Amid Persecution

In contrast, Timothy has carefully followed Paul’s doctrine, manner of life, purpose, faith, longsuffering, love, and perseverance, and he knows the persecutions Paul endured at Antioch, Iconium, and Lystra, out of which the Lord delivered him. All who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution, while evil men and impostors grow worse, deceiving and being deceived.

4. 2 Timothy 3:14–17 — Continuing in the Scriptures Given by God

Timothy must continue in what he has learned, knowing from whom he learned it and remembering that from childhood he has known the Holy Scriptures, which are able to make him wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus. All Scripture is given by inspiration of God and is profitable for doctrine, reproof, correction, and instruction in righteousness, so that the man of God may be complete, “thoroughly equipped for every good work” (2 Timothy 3:17).
