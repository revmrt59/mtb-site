Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 4
Main Idea:

Before Christ, the coming Judge, Paul charges Timothy to preach the word and fulfill his ministry, then faces his own death confident that the Lord who stood with him when all forsook him will bring him safely into His heavenly kingdom.

Setting:

Paul writes as a prisoner in Rome with his death near (4:6). No one stood with him at his first defense, only Luke remains with him, and he urges Timothy to come before winter (4:11, 16, 21).

Chapter Outline:

  • 2 Timothy 4:1–5 — The Charge to Preach the Word

  • 2 Timothy 4:6–8 — Paul’s Finished Course and the Crown of Righteousness

  • 2 Timothy 4:9–13 — Come Quickly: Coworkers and Requests

  • 2 Timothy 4:14–18 — Alexander’s Harm and the Lord Who Stood with Paul

  • 2 Timothy 4:19–22 — Final Greetings and Benediction

Key Verse:

“I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith. Finally, there is laid up for me the crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous Judge, will give to me on that Day, and not to me only but also to all who have loved His appearing.” (2 Timothy 4:7–8)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Second Timothy 4 opens with “I charge you therefore” (4:1), carrying chapter 3 straight into the charge: the Scriptures that equip God’s servant for every good work (3:16–17) are the Word Timothy must now preach. Paul gives the charge “before God and the Lord Jesus Christ, who will judge the living and the dead at His appearing and His kingdom” (4:1). In the presence of that Judge, Timothy must “Preach the word!” whether the moment seems right or not, convincing, rebuking, and exhorting “with all longsuffering and teaching” (4:2). When hearers gather teachers who tell them what they want to hear (4:3–4), Timothy must stay watchful, endure afflictions, do the work of an evangelist, and fulfill his ministry (4:5).

Paul’s own death gives the charge its urgency. He is “already being poured out as a drink offering” (4:6). Looking back, he has fought the good fight, finished the race, and kept the faith (4:7). Looking ahead, he sees the righteous Judge giving him the crown of righteousness on that Day, “and not to me only but also to all who have loved His appearing” (4:8). The appearing that makes Timothy’s charge solemn is the same appearing that fills Paul’s death with hope.

Then the letter turns personal. Demas has forsaken Paul, “having loved this present world,” and “Only Luke is with me” (4:10–11). Paul wants Timothy, Mark, his cloak, and his books (4:11–13). He answers wrong in two ways side by side. He leaves Alexander’s repayment to the Lord and warns Timothy about him, yet of those who deserted him at his first defense he prays, “May it not be charged against them” (4:14–16). Over all of it stands the Lord: “But the Lord stood with me and strengthened me” (4:17). Paul trusts that same Lord to preserve him for His heavenly kingdom (4:18).

Chapter Sections

2 Timothy 4:1–5 — The Charge to Preach the Word

Before God and the Lord Jesus Christ, who will judge the living and the dead at His appearing, Paul charges Timothy to preach the word at every opportunity with courage, patience, and careful teaching. Because a time is coming when people will turn from sound doctrine to teachers who suit their desires, Timothy must stay watchful, endure hardship, do the work of an evangelist, and complete his ministry.

2 Timothy 4:6–8 — Paul’s Finished Course and the Crown of Righteousness

Paul sees his approaching death as an offering poured out to God and looks back on a fight fought, a race finished, and a faith kept. The righteous Judge will give him the crown of righteousness on that Day, and the same crown awaits all who have loved Christ’s appearing.

2 Timothy 4:9–13 — Come Quickly: Coworkers and Requests

Paul urges Timothy to come quickly, because Demas has deserted him out of love for this present world, Crescens and Titus have gone to other regions, and only Luke remains. He asks for Mark, whom he finds useful for ministry, and for his cloak, books, and parchments, and he notes that he has sent Tychicus to Ephesus.

2 Timothy 4:14–18 — Alexander’s Harm and the Lord Who Stood with Paul

Paul warns Timothy about Alexander the coppersmith and leaves his repayment to the Lord, while he asks that those who deserted him at his first defense not be held accountable. The Lord stood with Paul so that the message could be fully proclaimed to the Gentiles, and Paul trusts Him to rescue him from every evil work and bring him safely into His heavenly kingdom.

2 Timothy 4:19–22 — Final Greetings and Benediction

Paul greets Prisca, Aquila, and the household of Onesiphorus, gives news of Erastus and Trophimus, and urges Timothy again to come before winter. He passes on greetings from the believers with him and closes with a blessing of the Lord’s presence and grace.

Why This Chapter Matters

Second Timothy 4 ties faithful preaching to Christ’s appearing. Timothy preaches before the One who will judge the living and the dead, so he cannot shape his message to suit itching ears (4:1–3). That same appearing is what believers love and long for, and the crown Paul expects is promised to all who share that love (4:8).

The chapter is also honest about the cost of ministry. Paul has been deserted and harmed, he needs his cloak before winter, and he still wants his friends and his books. Yet the Lord stood with him so that the message could be preached fully (4:17). He prays that those who deserted him will not be held accountable, which shows mercy toward people who wound us. He also warns Timothy about Alexander, which protects others from a man who opposes the message (4:14–16).

Key Themes to Watch For

Preaching before the coming Judge. Christ’s appearing, kingdom, and judgment give weight to the charge to preach (4:1–2).

Sound doctrine and itching ears. Hearers who want teachers to suit their own desires turn from the truth to fables (4:3–4).

Finishing well. Timothy must fulfill his ministry as Paul has finished his race and kept the faith (4:5–7).

The crown for all who love His appearing. Paul’s hope belongs to every believer who finishes in faith (4:8).

The Lord who stands with His servant. When everyone forsook Paul, the Lord stood with him, and Paul trusts Him to bring him safely into His kingdom (4:16–18).

Key Words & Concepts

His appearing and His kingdom (4:1). Christ’s future return, when He will judge the living and the dead. The same appearing is what believers love and long for (4:8).

Itching ears (4:3). A picture of hearers who want to hear what pleases them. They gather teachers who suit their own desires and turn away from the truth.

Poured out as a drink offering (4:6). In Israel’s worship, wine was poured out alongside certain sacrifices. Paul uses this picture for his own life, now being emptied out to God as death approaches.

The crown of righteousness (4:8). The victor’s wreath given at the end of a contest. Here it stands for Christ’s final vindication and the inheritance He gives to all who finish in faith.

Demas and Mark (4:10–11). Demas is the only coworker whose leaving Paul calls desertion, and its cause was love for this present world. Mark is probably the John Mark who once left Paul’s mission (Acts 13:13; 15:36–39), yet Paul now calls him “useful to me for ministry.” The letter does not say how the relationship was restored.

The mouth of the lion (4:17). A picture of a past rescue from grave danger. Paul mentions it right after recalling his first defense, but he does not say exactly what the danger was. His final confidence looks beyond that earlier rescue to the Lord preserving him for His heavenly kingdom (4:18).
