2 Timothy 4 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Second Timothy 4 completes Paul’s final charge and then closes the letter. Before Christ, the coming Judge, Paul charges Timothy to preach the word with patience and to fulfill his ministry, sets his own finished course and the crown of righteousness before him, and ends with personal requests, news of coworkers, a warning, the Lord’s faithfulness at his first defense, and final greetings.

Chapter Sections

1. 2 Timothy 4:1–5 — The Charge to Preach the Word

Paul charges Timothy before God and the Lord Jesus Christ, who will judge the living and the dead at His appearing and His kingdom, to “Preach the word!” (2 Timothy 4:2), ready in season and out of season, convincing, rebuking, and exhorting with all longsuffering and teaching. A time is coming when people will not endure sound doctrine but will heap up teachers to suit their own desires, so Timothy must be watchful, endure afflictions, do the work of an evangelist, and fulfill his ministry.

2. 2 Timothy 4:6–8 — Paul’s Finished Course and the Crown of Righteousness

Paul says he is already being poured out as a drink offering and that the time of his departure is at hand, words that describe his approaching death and give urgency to the charge he has just given Timothy. He has fought the good fight, finished the race, and kept the faith, and the Lord, the righteous Judge, will give the crown of righteousness on that Day to Paul and to “all who have loved His appearing” (2 Timothy 4:8).

3. 2 Timothy 4:9–13 — Come Quickly: Coworkers and Requests

Paul urges Timothy to come quickly, for Demas has forsaken him, “having loved this present world” (2 Timothy 4:10), Crescens and Titus have gone to other regions, and only Luke is with him. Timothy is to bring Mark, who is useful to Paul for ministry, along with the cloak left at Troas and the books, especially the parchments, and Paul notes that he has sent Tychicus to Ephesus.

4. 2 Timothy 4:14–18 — Alexander’s Harm and the Lord Who Stood with Paul

Paul warns Timothy about Alexander the coppersmith, who did Paul much harm and greatly resisted their words, and leaves his repayment to the Lord, while of those who forsook him at his first defense he prays, “May it not be charged against them” (2 Timothy 4:16). Yet the Lord stood with Paul and strengthened him so that the message might be preached fully and all the Gentiles might hear, and Paul, once delivered “out of the mouth of the lion” (2 Timothy 4:17), now trusts the Lord, as death approaches, to deliver him from every evil work and preserve him for His heavenly kingdom.

5. 2 Timothy 4:19–22 — Final Greetings and Benediction

Paul sends greetings to Prisca and Aquila and to the household of Onesiphorus, gives news of Erastus and Trophimus, and urges Timothy to come before winter, passing on greetings from Eubulus, Pudens, Linus, Claudia, and all the brethren. He closes with the blessing, “The Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit. Grace be with you” (2 Timothy 4:22).
