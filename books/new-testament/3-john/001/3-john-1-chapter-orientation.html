Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 1
Main Idea:

The Elder rejoices that Gaius walks in the truth and urges him to keep receiving and sending workers who went out for Jesus’ name, so that believers become “fellow workers for the truth,” unlike the self-seeking Diotrephes.

Setting:

The Elder, most likely the apostle John, writes to his friend Gaius at a time when traveling believers depended on other Christians for help, and a leader named Diotrephes was shutting them out.

Chapter Outline:

  • 3 John 1:1–4 — The Elder Prays for Gaius and Rejoices in His Walk

  • 3 John 1:5–8 — Welcoming and Sending Those Who Went Out for His Name

  • 3 John 1:9–10 — Diotrephes Refuses the Elder and Shuts Out the Brethren

  • 3 John 1:11–12 — Imitate What Is Good: Demetrius Commended

  • 3 John 1:13–14 — Hope of Speaking Face to Face and Final Greetings

Key Verse:

“We therefore ought to receive such, that we may become fellow workers for the truth.” (3 John 1:8)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Third John is a letter to one man, and it opens with love and joy. The Elder calls Gaius “beloved,” prays that his health and circumstances will go as well as his soul, and names his highest joy: to hear “that my children walk in truth” (1:4). Brethren could testify to the truth in Gaius because they saw it in how he walked (1:3).

That walk takes a practical form. Gaius has welcomed believers who were strangers to him, and the Elder asks him to keep sending them forward “in a manner worthy of God” (1:6). They went out for Jesus’ name and took nothing from the Gentiles they went to reach, so those who receive them “become fellow workers for the truth” (1:8). God counts the one who opens a door and supplies a traveler as a partner in the work.

Diotrephes shows the opposite. His love of being first (1:9) leads to malicious words, refusing the brethren, forbidding those who wish to receive them, and putting them out of the church (1:10). The Elder answers, “do not imitate what is evil, but what is good” (1:11), and points to Demetrius, whose life agrees with the truth (1:12).

Chapter Sections

3 John 1:1–4 — The Elder Prays for Gaius and Rejoices in His Walk

The Elder greets Gaius, whom he loves in truth, and prays that Gaius’s health and circumstances will prosper as his soul prospers. Reports that Gaius walks in the truth give the Elder his greatest joy as a spiritual father.

3 John 1:5–8 — Welcoming and Sending Those Who Went Out for His Name

The Elder commends Gaius’s faithful love for believers who were strangers to him and asks him to send them on their way in a manner worthy of God. Because these workers went out for His name and took nothing from the Gentiles, believers ought to receive them and so share in their work for the truth.

3 John 1:9–10 — Diotrephes Refuses the Elder and Shuts Out the Brethren

Diotrephes’s desire to be first leads him to reject the Elder, speak maliciously against him and his companions, and use his authority to turn away the brethren, forbid others to welcome them, and expel those who want to do so. The Elder says he will bring these deeds to light if he comes.

3 John 1:11–12 — Imitate What Is Good: Demetrius Commended

The Elder urges Gaius to imitate what is good, showing that the way a person lives reveals whether he knows God. Demetrius gives Gaius a good example, with a good testimony from others, from the truth itself, and from the Elder and those with him.

3 John 1:13–14 — Hope of Speaking Face to Face and Final Greetings

The Elder leaves some matters unwritten because they need to be dealt with face to face, and he hopes to see Gaius soon. He closes with peace, greetings from the friends with him, and a request to greet the friends by name.

Why This Chapter Matters

Third John shows that walking in the truth is seen in ordinary, practical love. The Elder says Gaius serves the travelers “faithfully” (1:5) and measures that care by what is “worthy of God” (1:6). God takes notice of quiet service done for His people and counts it as part of His work.

The chapter also widens the work of the gospel to include those who stay home. By receiving and sending those who went out for the Name, believers become partners in spreading the truth (1:8). Through Diotrephes, the chapter warns that the love of being first can do real harm inside a church, even where no false teaching is charged (1:9–10).

Key Themes to Watch For

Walking in the truth. Gaius’s life matches the truth that is in him, and that brings the Elder great joy (1:3–4).

Welcoming and sending. Faithful love provides for believers on the road in a manner worthy of God (1:5–6).

Fellow workers for the truth. Those who receive and support the workers share in their work (1:7–8).

The love of preeminence. The desire of Diotrephes to be first turns into words and actions that shut others out (1:9–10).

Imitating what is good. Doing good shows that a person is of God, and Demetrius’s good testimony gives Gaius an example (1:11–12).

Key Words & Concepts

The Elder. The writer’s title for himself (1:1). He is most likely the apostle John, known to Gaius as a spiritual father (1:4).

Walk in truth. To live day by day in line with the truth God has revealed in Jesus (1:3–4).

For His name’s sake. The workers went out for Jesus’ sake. His name identifies the One they served (1:7).

Send forward. To help travelers on to the next stage of their journey, which could include food, money, or companions for the road (1:6).

Gentiles. In 1:7, the unbelieving outsiders the workers went to reach. These workers accepted no support from them, most likely to keep money from becoming a stumbling block to the gospel (compare 1 Corinthians 9:12). Their choice shows why the Elder called on believers to help them on their way.

Preeminence. First place. Diotrephes “loves to have the preeminence” (1:9), the place that belongs to Christ alone (Colossians 1:18).
