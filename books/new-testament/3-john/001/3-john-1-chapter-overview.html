Morning Chapter Explanation

3 John Chapter 1

Chapter 1

Central Message

John commends Gaius for faithfully walking in the truth and showing generous hospitality to fellow believers, while confronting the pride and controlling behavior of Diotrephes. The letter shows that truth is not merely something Christians believe; it is something they walk in, and that walk becomes visible in love, hospitality, faithfulness, and the way believers treat one another.

What This Chapter Is About

John writes warmly to Gaius, rejoicing over reports that he is walking in the truth and faithfully caring for traveling believers. He encourages Gaius to continue supporting those who have gone out “for His name’s sake.” In sharp contrast, Diotrephes loves to have the preeminence, rejects John’s authority, speaks maliciously, refuses to receive the brethren, and interferes with those who want to receive them. John then points to Demetrius as a man with a good testimony and closes with the hope of speaking with Gaius face to face.

The Heart of the Chapter

The heart of 3 John is the connection between truth and conduct. Gaius walks in the truth, and his life demonstrates it through faithful love and generous support of others who are serving Christ. Diotrephes provides the opposite picture: his desire for prominence produces rejection, harmful words, and controlling behavior. John makes the distinction plain: “He who does good is of God, but he who does evil has not seen God.”

Chapter Sections

3 John 1:1-4 - Gaius Walking in the Truth

John expresses his love for Gaius and his joy over the testimony he has heard about him. His greatest joy is “to hear that my children walk in truth.” Gaius’s life agrees with the truth he professes.

3 John 1:5-8 - Faithfully Supporting Those Who Go Forth for His Name

Gaius has shown hospitality to traveling believers, including those personally unknown to him. John encourages him to continue sending such workers forward “in a manner worthy of God.” Because they have gone out for Christ’s name and do not depend upon unbelievers for support, Christians who help them become “fellow workers for the truth.”

3 John 1:9-12 - Diotrephes and Demetrius: Two Contrasting Examples

Diotrephes “loves to have the preeminence.” His desire for position is seen in his rejection of John, malicious words, refusal to receive fellow believers, and efforts to prevent others from doing so. John tells Gaius not to imitate evil but good, then presents Demetrius as a man whose good testimony is widely recognized.

3 John 1:13-14 - Face to Face Fellowship

John has more to communicate but prefers not to do it with pen and ink. He hopes to see Gaius personally so they can “speak face to face,” and he ends with peace and greetings among friends.

Why This Chapter Matters

3 John shows that spiritual health becomes visible in ordinary relationships and choices. Gaius demonstrates truth through faithful service and generosity, while Diotrephes exposes the danger of making position and personal importance more valuable than the good of God’s people. The letter also shows that supporting faithful servants of Christ is genuine participation in their work: those who help them go forward become “fellow workers for the truth.”

Key Themes to Watch For

  • Walking in the truth

  • Truth expressed through conduct

  • Faithful hospitality

  • Supporting those who serve Christ

  • Fellow workers for the truth

  • The danger of loving preeminence

  • Spiritual authority and accountability

  • Imitating good rather than evil

  • A good testimony

Key Words & Concepts

Walking in truth - Living in a way that agrees with the truth of God. John’s joy comes not merely from knowing that Gaius believes the truth but from hearing that he consistently walks in it.

Hospitality - Gaius receives and assists traveling believers, even though some are strangers to him. In a setting where Christian workers depended heavily upon the hospitality of other believers, this was an important expression of partnership and love.

“For His name’s sake” - These believers have gone out in service to Christ. His name-who He is and the cause that belongs to Him-is the reason for their work and sacrifice.

Fellow workers for the truth - Those who support faithful Christian workers share meaningfully in their service. Gaius may not travel with them, but his hospitality and provision make him a participant in their work.

Preeminence - Diotrephes loves being first or holding the place of prominence. John shows that this is more than an inward attitude; it produces controlling and destructive behavior toward others.

Good testimony - Demetrius is commended not simply by John but “by all, and by the truth itself.” His life has established a reputation consistent with the truth he professes.

Helpful Notes

Hospitality in 3 John involves more than inviting someone into a home for a meal. Traveling Christian workers needed places to stay, food, protection, and practical help as they continued their ministry. John’s instruction to send them forward “in a manner worthy of God” points to generous, thoughtful support for their ongoing work.

Diotrephes is especially instructive because John connects his actions with his desire “to have the preeminence.” His treatment of others reveals what that desire has produced. He rejects legitimate authority, attacks others with words, refuses hospitality, and uses his influence to control those who disagree with him. The issue is therefore not simply a difficult personality but a desire for prominence that is damaging the life of the church.

Gaius, Diotrephes, and Demetrius provide three different pictures within one brief letter: faithful service, self-promoting leadership, and a well-established good testimony. John’s instruction, “Do not imitate what is evil, but what is good,” encourages the reader not merely to identify the difference but to choose which kind of life is worth following.
