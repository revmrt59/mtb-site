Acts Book Outline

Major Movements

I. The Risen Lord Prepares His Witnesses (1:1–1:26)

Jesus instructs His apostles, promises the Holy Spirit, commissions their witness, and ascends. The disciples return to Jerusalem, devote themselves to prayer, and restore the apostolic number by selecting Matthias.

II. The Spirit Is Poured Out and the Witness Takes Root in Jerusalem (2:1–6:7)

The Holy Spirit comes at Pentecost, Peter proclaims the risen Jesus, and a growing community forms around the apostles’ teaching and shared life. Healing, generosity, opposition, internal judgment, and practical needs reveal both the power of the witness and the care required to preserve the church’s faithfulness.

III. Persecution Scatters the Witness Beyond Jerusalem (6:8–9:31)

Stephen’s witness and death lead into persecution that scatters believers, carrying the word into Samaria and beyond. Philip proclaims Christ in Samaria and to the Ethiopian official, Saul encounters the risen Lord and begins preaching, and the church throughout Judea, Galilee, and Samaria is strengthened.

IV. God Opens the Door of Faith to the Gentiles (9:32–12:25)

Peter’s ministry leads to the household of Cornelius, where God gives the Holy Spirit to Gentile believers. The church in Jerusalem recognizes this work, Antioch becomes a major center of ministry, and Herod’s opposition fails to stop the growth of God’s word.

V. The Gentile Mission Expands and the Jerusalem Council Affirms Grace (13:1–15:35)

The Spirit sends Barnabas and Saul from Antioch, and churches are established through gospel proclamation amid faith and resistance. The Jerusalem council resolves the dispute over circumcision, affirming salvation through the grace of the Lord Jesus and the welcome of Gentile believers without requiring them to become Jews.

VI. The Witness Advances Through Macedonia, Achaia, and Asia (15:36–19:20)

Paul travels with new companions, strengthens existing churches, and follows the Spirit’s direction into Macedonia. The mission reaches Philippi, Thessalonica, Berea, Athens, Corinth, and Ephesus, where the word grows mightily amid spiritual conflict.

VII. Paul Journeys to Jerusalem and Bears Witness in Custody (19:21–26:32)

Paul determines to travel through Macedonia and Achaia to Jerusalem and then to Rome. His return leads to arrest, repeated defenses, and an appeal to Caesar; through hostility and detention, he continues testifying to Jesus’ resurrection and the hope promised in Scripture.

VIII. God Brings Paul to Rome and the Proclamation Continues (27:1–28:31)

Paul’s journey as a prisoner passes through a storm, shipwreck, and ministry on Malta before he reaches Rome. The book closes with Paul proclaiming God’s kingdom and teaching about the Lord Jesus Christ openly and without hindrance.
