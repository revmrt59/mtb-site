Colossians 1 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Colossians 1 moves from Paul's greeting, thanksgiving, and prayer for the church in Colosse into a sweeping declaration of the Son's supremacy over creation and the church, and then applies the reconciliation He accomplished to the Colossians themselves. The chapter closes with Paul's account of the suffering and labor his calling involves, an account that continues into 2:1–5.

Chapter Sections

1. Colossians 1:1–2 — Paul and Timothy Greet the Saints in Colosse

Paul introduces himself as "an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God," names Timothy beside him, and writes to "the saints and faithful brethren in Christ who are in Colosse" with grace and peace from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. Before he raises any concern, he greets the church as God's holy people and His faithful family.

2. Colossians 1:3–8 — Thanksgiving for the Gospel's Fruit Among the Colossians

Paul and Timothy thank God for the Colossians' faith in Christ Jesus and their love for all the saints, which grow from "the hope which is laid up for you in heaven." The gospel they learned from Epaphras is "bringing forth fruit" among them as it is in all the world, and Epaphras has told Paul of their "love in the Spirit."

3. Colossians 1:9–14 — Prayer for a Worthy Walk and Thanks for the Father's Rescue

Since hearing Epaphras's report, Paul has prayed that the Colossians may be "filled with the knowledge of His will" so that they "walk worthy of the Lord," bearing fruit, strengthened by God's power, and giving thanks. The prayer ends with what the Father has already done: He has delivered them from the power of darkness into the kingdom of His Son, "in whom we have redemption through His blood, the forgiveness of sins."

4. Colossians 1:15–20 — The Son Supreme in Creation, the Church, and Reconciliation

Paul declares that the Son is "the image of the invisible God," the One by whom and for whom all things were created and in whom all things hold together, and the head of the church who rose as "the firstborn from the dead." The Father was pleased for all the fullness to dwell in Him and through Him to reconcile all things to Himself, "having made peace through the blood of His cross."

5. Colossians 1:21–23 — The Colossians Reconciled and Called to Continue in the Faith

Paul turns Christ's reconciling work toward the Colossians, who were once "alienated and enemies" but are now reconciled through His death so that He may present them holy and blameless before Him. Paul ties that hope to a real condition, "if indeed you continue in the faith," and names himself a minister of this gospel.

6. Colossians 1:24–29 — Paul's Suffering and Labor to Make the Mystery Known

Paul rejoices in suffering for the sake of Christ's body, the church, as a steward entrusted to make God's word fully known. He announces the mystery now revealed among the Gentiles, "Christ in you, the hope of glory," and describes how he labors, by God's power at work in him, to "present every man perfect in Christ Jesus."
