Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 1
Main Idea:

The Son into whose kingdom the Father has rescued His people is supreme in creation, the church, and reconciliation, so the reconciled Colossians are called to continue in the faith while Paul labors to present them mature in Christ.

Setting:

Paul writes in chains (4:18), probably from Rome in the early AD 60s, to a church that learned the gospel from Epaphras (1:7). Epaphras's report of their "love in the Spirit" (1:8) prompted this chapter's thanksgiving and prayer.

Chapter Outline:

  • Colossians 1:1–2 — Paul and Timothy Greet the Saints in Colosse

  • Colossians 1:3–8 — Thanksgiving for the Gospel's Fruit Among the Colossians

  • Colossians 1:9–14 — Prayer for a Worthy Walk and Thanks for the Father's Rescue

  • Colossians 1:15–20 — The Son Supreme in Creation, the Church, and Reconciliation

  • Colossians 1:21–23 — The Colossians Reconciled and Called to Continue in the Faith

  • Colossians 1:24–29 — Paul's Suffering and Labor to Make the Mystery Known

Key Verse:

"And He is the head of the body, the church, who is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead, that in all things He may have the preeminence." (Colossians 1:18)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Colossians 1 moves from prayer into worship without changing subjects. Paul's prayer ends with the Father, who "has delivered us from the power of darkness and conveyed us into the kingdom of the Son of His love" (1:13). The very next lines tell who that Son is: "He is the image of the invisible God" (1:15), the One in whom the unseen God is made known. Paul says "all things" again and again in 1:16–20. All things were created by the Son and for Him, all things hold together in Him, and all things are reconciled through Him. The One who made the universe is also the head of the church and "the firstborn from the dead," so that "in all things He may have the preeminence" (1:18).

Behind the Son's supremacy stands the Father's delight. Paul writes that "it pleased the Father that in Him all the fullness should dwell" (1:19), so everything God is lives in the Son. Through that Son the Father made "peace through the blood of His cross" (1:20). This peace reaches across all creation. Read alongside 2:15, it includes hostile powers being disarmed and brought under Christ's rule, an outcome distinct from the reconciliation believers receive.

Then Paul says, "And you" (1:21). The peace that spans heaven and earth reaches people who "once were alienated and enemies." The God who placed His fullness in the Son now makes the Son known among Gentiles: "Christ in you, the hope of glory" (1:27). Throughout the chapter the Father takes the first step, and Paul's own suffering and labor serve to bring believers to maturity in that Christ.

Chapter Sections

Colossians 1:1–2 — Paul and Timothy Greet the Saints in Colosse

Paul writes as "an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God," with Timothy beside him, and he greets the church as "saints and faithful brethren in Christ" before he mentions any concern. He names "God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ" together as the source of the grace and peace he wishes them.

Colossians 1:3–8 — Thanksgiving for the Gospel's Fruit Among the Colossians

Paul and Timothy thank God for the Colossians' faith in Christ Jesus and their love for all the saints, both growing from "the hope which is laid up for you in heaven" (1:5). The gospel Epaphras taught them is the same message "bringing forth fruit" throughout the world, and Paul's warm commendation of Epaphras confirms that what they first received was the true gospel.

Colossians 1:9–14 — Prayer for a Worthy Walk and Thanks for the Father's Rescue

Since hearing Epaphras's report, Paul has prayed that the church will be "filled with the knowledge of His will" so that they "walk worthy of the Lord," fruitful, strengthened by God's power, and thankful. Their thanks looks back to what the Father has already done: He rescued them from the power of darkness into His Son's kingdom, where they have "redemption through His blood, the forgiveness of sins" (1:14).

Colossians 1:15–20 — The Son Supreme in Creation, the Church, and Reconciliation

Paul declares that the Son is "the image of the invisible God," the Creator who also holds all things together, and the head of the church who has first place among all who are raised from the dead. Because all the fullness of God dwells in Him, the Father reconciles all things through Him, "having made peace through the blood of His cross" (1:20).

Colossians 1:21–23 — The Colossians Reconciled and Called to Continue in the Faith

With "And you," Paul brings that peace home to the Colossians, who were once God's enemies and are now reconciled through Christ's bodily death, so that He may present them holy and blameless in His sight. The promise comes with a condition Paul means seriously, "if indeed you continue in the faith, grounded and steadfast" (1:23), and he ends the sentence by identifying himself as a minister of this same gospel.

Colossians 1:24–29 — Paul's Suffering and Labor to Make the Mystery Known

Paul rejoices to suffer for Christ's body, the church, as a steward God has entrusted with making His word fully known. That word is a mystery once hidden and now revealed among the Gentiles, "Christ in you, the hope of glory" (1:27), and Paul labors by God's own power to present every believer mature in Christ, a labor his account carries on into 2:1–5.

Why This Chapter Matters

Colossians 1 settles who Christ is before Paul warns about the teaching that pressed on the church in chapter 2. Once a reader has seen the Son as Creator, sustainer, head of the church, and the dwelling place of all God's fullness, Paul's later claim makes sense: "you are complete in Him" (2:10). No rule or experience can add to the One in whom all things hold together.

The chapter also shows the order of the Christian life. The Father qualifies, delivers, and reconciles first, and His people respond with thanksgiving and growing fruitfulness.

Finally, Colossians 1 keeps the promise and the warning together. Christ reconciles His people in order to present them blameless, and Paul calls them to continue in the faith. God uses that real warning to keep His people steadfast, and their endurance rests on His grace.

Key Themes to Watch For

The Son's preeminence (1:15–19). Paul places the Son first in creation, in the church, and in resurrection, and he grounds that first place in the fullness of God dwelling in Him.

Peace through the cross (1:20–22). Christ's death makes peace on the scale of creation and reconciles former enemies to God.

The Father acts first (1:3, 12–14, 19, 27). Thanksgiving answers what the Father has already done: He qualified His people, delivered them, and chose to make His mystery known.

The gospel bearing fruit (1:5–6, 9–10). The gospel bears fruit from the day it is heard, and Paul prays that the Colossians will keep bearing fruit as they grow in the knowledge of God.

Christ in you (1:26–28). God's once-hidden purpose is Christ living among Gentile believers as one people and in each of them personally, as their hope of glory.

Key Words & Concepts

Firstborn (1:15, 18). The word speaks of first place in rank and authority. Paul calls the Son "the firstborn over all creation" and immediately explains, "For by Him all things were created" (1:16), which places the Son on the Creator's side. He is not the first thing God made. "The firstborn from the dead" names Him as the source of resurrection life and the highest among all who are raised.

Fullness (1:19). Here "fullness" means the complete divine nature. Paul says it outright in 2:9: "For in Him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily." Everything God is lives in the Son.

Reconcile and "all things" (1:20–21). To reconcile is to end hostility and make peace. Believers who were once enemies are brought back to God. Read with 2:15, the same peace includes hostile powers being disarmed and brought under Christ's rule. Their defeat is different from salvation, and the verse does not teach that every creature will be saved.

Thrones, dominions, principalities, powers (1:16). These are names for ruling powers, including unseen ones. However exalted they are, every one was created by the Son and for Him. This prepares for Paul's statement that Christ is "the head of all principality and power" (2:10).

Mystery (1:26–27). In Colossians a mystery is God's purpose, kept hidden in past ages and now openly revealed to all His saints. Its heart is "Christ in you, the hope of glory," made known among the Gentiles.

"What is lacking in the afflictions of Christ" (1:24). Paul is speaking of the sufferings that still fall to him as he serves the church. The precise sense of the phrase is debated. The safeguard, though, is clear: the same chapter says believers are reconciled "through death" (1:22), so Christ's atoning death lacks nothing.
