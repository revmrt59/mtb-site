Colossians 2 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Colossians 2 finishes Paul's account of his struggle for the church and then turns to the letter's central appeal: having received Christ Jesus the Lord, the Colossians are to keep walking in Him. Because all the fullness of deity dwells in Christ and believers are complete in Him (2:9–10), Paul answers each pressure of human tradition, religious judgment, and bodily regulation with what God has already done for them in Christ.

Chapter Sections

1. Colossians 2:1–5 — Paul's Struggle for the Church's Full Understanding of Christ

Paul wants the Colossians and the believers in Laodicea to know how great a conflict he has for them, so that their hearts may be encouraged, “knit together in love,” and brought to know the mystery of God in Christ, “in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.” He writes this “lest anyone should deceive you with persuasive words,” and he rejoices at their good order and the steadfastness of their faith.

2. Colossians 2:6–7 — Keep Walking in Christ as You Received Him

“As you therefore have received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in Him” turns everything Paul has said about Christ into a direct appeal. Believers who are “rooted and built up in Him” are to stay established in the faith they were taught, abounding in it with thanksgiving.

3. Colossians 2:8–10 — Beware of Empty Philosophy, for You Are Complete in Christ

Paul warns against anyone who would cheat the church through philosophy “according to the tradition of men, according to the basic principles of the world, and not according to Christ.” His answer, which stands at the center of the chapter, is that all the fullness of the Godhead dwells bodily in Christ and that believers are “complete in Him,” who is head over every principality and power.

4. Colossians 2:11–12 — Circumcised, Buried, and Raised with Christ

In Christ believers were circumcised “with the circumcision made without hands,” buried with Him in baptism, and raised with Him “through faith in the working of God, who raised Him from the dead.” The power of this new life lies in God's working, and believers receive it through faith.

5. Colossians 2:13–15 — Made Alive, Forgiven, and Delivered Through the Cross

Believers were dead in their trespasses, but God made them alive together with Christ, forgave all their trespasses, and wiped out the handwriting of requirements that stood against them, “having nailed it to the cross.” At that same cross the principalities and powers were disarmed and made “a public spectacle.”

6. Colossians 2:16–17 — Let No One Judge You: Shadow and Substance

Because of what God has done in Christ, no one is to judge believers over food or drink, festivals, new moons, or sabbaths. These observances are “a shadow of things to come, but the substance is of Christ.”

7. Colossians 2:18–19 — Let No One Cheat You: Holding Fast to the Head

Paul exposes the person who delights in false humility and worship of angels and is “vainly puffed up by his fleshly mind.” That person's basic failure is “not holding fast to the Head,” the One from whom the whole body, nourished and knit together, grows with the increase that comes from God.

8. Colossians 2:20–23 — Died with Christ and Freed from Human Regulations

Paul asks why those who “died with Christ from the basic principles of the world” would again submit to rules such as “Do not touch, do not taste, do not handle,” which deal with things that perish and come from “the commandments and doctrines of men.” Such rules have “an appearance of wisdom,” but they “are of no value against the indulgence of the flesh.”
