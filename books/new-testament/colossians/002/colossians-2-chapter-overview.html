Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 2
Main Idea:

Because all the fullness of deity dwells bodily in Christ and believers are complete in Him, the Colossians are to keep walking in Him and refuse any rule or judgment that offers what He has already given.

Setting:

Paul writes in chains, probably from Rome in the early AD 60s. He completes his account of struggling for churches that have never seen him, then answers pressures he describes by their features: human tradition, food and calendar judgments, angel worship, and bodily rules.

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Colossians 2 turns on one sentence: “For in Him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily; and you are complete in Him” (2:9–10). “Godhead” is an older English word for deity, and “bodily” means that everything God is lives in Christ, who has a real human body. Since nothing of God lies outside Christ, the Colossians lack nothing that a new teaching, a spiritual power, or a special experience could supply. Every warning in the chapter grows out of that truth.

Paul reaches that center by way of his own struggle for the church. He labors so that they may know Christ, “in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge” (2:3). Those treasures are stored up in Christ, and believers find them by coming to Him. That is why smooth “persuasive words” (2:4) are dangerous, and why Paul’s central appeal is simply to keep going as they began: “As you therefore have received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in Him” (2:6).

Paul then answers every pressure by pointing to what God has already done. In 2:13–15 God is the One acting at every step. He made the spiritually dead “alive together with Him,” forgave “all trespasses,” and canceled the record against them, “having nailed it to the cross.” At that same cross He disarmed the hostile powers. Forgiveness and victory meet there, yet they differ: believers are forgiven, while the powers are defeated and brought under Christ’s rule. This is the cross through which God made “peace through the blood of His cross” (1:20). God’s goodness moves first, and every command in the chapter rests on His finished work.

The chapter ends by drawing out what union with Christ means. Those who “died with Christ from the basic principles of the world” (2:20) have no reason to submit again to human rules. The next chapter carries the argument on with “If then you were raised with Christ” (3:1). Freedom from regulations rests on dying with Him, and the new life rests on rising with Him.

Chapter Sections

Colossians 2:1–5 — Paul's Struggle for the Church's Full Understanding of Christ

Paul’s “great conflict” for the Colossians, for Laodicea, and for all who “have not seen my face in the flesh” is his earnest struggle in prayer and labor, carrying on the striving he described in 1:29. He wants their hearts encouraged, “knit together in love,” and settled in the knowledge of Christ, and he writes to guard a church that is already standing firm.

Colossians 2:6–7 — Keep Walking in Christ as You Received Him

With “therefore,” Paul turns to the appeal that every later warning serves: the Colossians received Jesus as Lord, and they are to keep conducting their everyday lives, their “walk,” in the same way. Like a tree with deep roots and a building rising on its foundation, they are “rooted and built up in Him,” established in what they were taught and “abounding in it with thanksgiving.”

Colossians 2:8–10 — Beware of Empty Philosophy, for You Are Complete in Christ

Paul warns against philosophy that rests on “the tradition of men” and “the basic principles of the world” and is “not according to Christ.” His answer stands at the center of the chapter: all of God’s fullness dwells bodily in Christ, believers are complete in Him, and He is “the head of all principality and power.”

Colossians 2:11–12 — Circumcised, Buried, and Raised with Christ

In Christ, believers received “the circumcision made without hands,” God’s own work of cutting away the old life ruled by sin, so Gentile believers lack nothing that physical circumcision could add. They were buried with Him in baptism and raised with Him “through faith in the working of God, who raised Him from the dead,” so the power of their new life is God’s working, received through faith.

Colossians 2:13–15 — Made Alive, Forgiven, and Delivered Through the Cross

God made those who were “dead in your trespasses” alive with Christ and forgave every one of their sins, canceling the written record of debt against them by nailing it to the cross. At that same cross He stripped hostile spiritual rulers of their power and showed their defeat openly, the way a conquered enemy was displayed in a victory parade.

Colossians 2:16–17 — Let No One Judge You: Shadow and Substance

Because of what God has done in Christ, no one may condemn believers over food, drink, festivals, new moons, or sabbaths, as if their standing with God depended on keeping them. These observances are “a shadow of things to come, but the substance is of Christ,” so judging by the shadow loses sight of the One it pointed to.

Colossians 2:18–19 — Let No One Cheat You: Holding Fast to the Head

Paul exposes a person who delights in “false humility and worship of angels” and is “vainly puffed up by his fleshly mind,” so that his humility is only for show. His root failure is “not holding fast to the Head,” Christ, from whom the whole body grows “with the increase that is from God.”

Colossians 2:20–23 — Died with Christ and Freed from Human Regulations

Paul asks why those who died with Christ would submit again to rules like ‘Do not touch, do not taste, do not handle,’ which concern things that perish and carry only human authority. Such rules look wise and demanding, yet they “are of no value against the indulgence of the flesh,” and Paul’s argument moves straight on to the risen life of 3:1–5.

Why This Chapter Matters

Many believers feel that something more is needed beyond Christ: a deeper experience, stricter rules, a required observance. Colossians 2 answers that longing with the words “complete in Him” (2:10). Since all of God’s fullness lives in Christ, no teacher or practice can add to what believers already have in Him.

The chapter also shows why rules cannot make anyone holy. Self-imposed regulations look devout, but they “are of no value against the indulgence of the flesh” (2:23). Holiness grows from union with Christ, as chapter 3 goes on to show, and that same chapter commands believers to “put to death your members which are on the earth” (3:5). Paul guards against trusting rules and against treating sin lightly.

Finally, believers do not have to live under accusation or in fear of spiritual powers. The record against them is nailed to the cross (2:14), and the powers have been disarmed by the One who is head over them all (2:10, 15).

Key Themes to Watch For

Complete in Christ (2:3, 9–10, 17). Every treasure of wisdom, all the fullness of deity, and the substance behind the shadows belong to Christ. Nothing believers need for life with God lies outside Him.

In Him and with Him (2:6–7, 9–13, 20). Paul keeps returning to these words: rooted and built up in Him, complete in Him, circumcised in Him, buried and raised with Him, made alive with Him, and dead with Christ to the world’s basic principles. Believers’ whole standing comes from sharing Christ’s life, and thanksgiving is its natural overflow (2:7).

The cross: debt canceled and powers defeated (2:13–15; compare 1:20). The same cross removes the record that stood against believers and openly defeats the hostile powers. God forgives His people and subdues their enemies.

Human tradition set against Christ (2:4, 8, 18, 20–23). “Persuasive words,” “the tradition of men,” “the commandments and doctrines of men,” and “an appearance of wisdom” all sound impressive, yet each draws believers away from Christ.

Holding fast to the Head (2:2, 6–7, 19). The NKJV speaks of hearts “knit together in love” (2:2) and of a body “knit together by joints and ligaments” (2:19). Spiritual growth is shared growth that comes from staying joined to Christ, and God Himself gives the increase.

Key Words & Concepts

“The fullness of the Godhead bodily” (2:9). “Godhead” is an older English word for deity. “Fullness” means God in His completeness, and “bodily” means that this fullness is present in Christ, who has a real human body. God is not partly in Christ and partly elsewhere, waiting to be found through other teachers or powers.

“The basic principles of the world” (2:8, 20). Paul links this phrase with human tradition and sets it against Christ. Its exact meaning is debated: it may refer to elementary religious rules and teachings, or to spiritual forces connected with the present world order. Paul’s point holds either way, because believers have died with Christ to this whole order.

Circumcision without hands and baptism (2:11–12). Paul says believers were circumcised “with the circumcision made without hands,” were “buried with Him in baptism,” and were raised “through faith in the working of God.” Salvation is by grace through faith alone. Paul’s words are best understood this way: water baptism is obedient faith that confesses Jesus as Lord and outwardly acts out burial and resurrection with Him, and in that obedience God works inwardly by grace, confirming to believers the reality of their new life in Christ. Paul does not say exactly when or how God does this inward work, and the saving power belongs to Christ and to God’s working, received through faith. His comparison also does not require matching every detail of circumcision to baptism.

“The handwriting of requirements” (2:14). This is best understood as a written record of debt that condemned believers. Readers debate exactly how it relates to the commandments given through Moses, but Paul’s point is plain: God wiped out whatever stood against them and nailed it to the cross.

“Principalities and powers” (2:10, 15). These words name ruling spiritual powers. Paul has already said they were created through Christ (1:16). Here Christ is their head, and at the cross God disarmed the hostile ones and displayed their defeat. They are conquered and brought under Christ’s rule. They are not forgiven.

“Worship of angels” and “which he has not seen” (2:18). Readers debate whether Paul means worship offered to angels or a claim to join in the worship angels offer, and the passage does not explain what role the teaching gave such beings. The NKJV reads “intruding into those things which he has not seen.” Some translations leave out “not” and describe someone dwelling on visions he claims to have seen, because the ancient manuscripts differ. On either reading, the decisive failure is “not holding fast to the Head” (2:19).
