Chapter Study

Colossians 3

Verse-by-Verse
Colossians 3:1
Colossians 3:1
NKJV

Colossians 3:1 If then you were raised with Christ, seek those things which are above, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God.(NKJV)

NLT

Colossians 3:1 Since you have been raised to new life with Christ, set your sights on the realities of heaven, where Christ sits in the place of honor at God’s right hand. (NLT)

Main Idea

Because believers have already been raised with Christ, Paul calls them to seek “those things which are above,” where the risen Christ reigns “at the right hand of God.” This “if” answers the “if you died with Christ” of 2:20 and states what is already true of them, so the new life starts from union with the risen Christ, not from a new set of rules.

Verse Explanation

Because believers have already been raised with Christ, Paul calls them to seek “those things which are above,” where the risen Christ reigns “at the right hand of God.” This “if” answers the “if you died with Christ” of 2:20 and states what is already true of them, so the new life starts from union with the risen Christ, not from a new set of rules.

If then you were raised with Christ, Paul opens the chapter by recalling something that has already happened to the Colossians. The “if” raises no doubt. It states where they stand, just as the “if you died with Christ” of 2:20 did. The word “then” draws a conclusion from chapter 2, where Paul told them that God “made alive together with Him” those who were dead in their trespasses (2:13) and that they were “raised with Him through faith in the working of God, who raised Him from the dead” (2:12). Their new life began when Christ’s resurrection became theirs.

seek those things which are above, The command follows the fact. Because their life is joined to the risen Christ, Paul tells them to pursue what belongs to Him. He has just rejected rules such as “Do not touch, do not taste, do not handle” (2:21), and he does not replace them with a stricter list. He points the Colossians to a Person and to the place where that Person reigns. To “seek” means to want something, go after it, and give oneself to it, so Paul is calling for the active pursuit of a whole life.

where Christ is, Paul defines “above” by Christ’s presence. Heaven matters here because the risen Christ is there. The things above are everything that belongs to His reign, His character, and His purposes, and believers seek them because they belong to Him.

sitting at the right hand of God. The right hand of a king was the place of honor and authority beside him. Christ is seated there, reigning now. Paul has already called Him “the head of all principality and power” (2:10) and has said that at the cross God disarmed those powers, “triumphing over them in it” (2:15). The One the Colossians are joined to already rules over every power the false teaching made so much of, so they have no reason to look anywhere else.

Study Notes

The NLT renders the NKJV’s “If then” as “Since,” which brings out that Paul is stating a settled fact about his readers rather than questioning whether it is true.

This verse opens the part of the letter that runs through 4:6. Every command in that stretch, from putting sin to death in 3:5 to gracious speech toward outsiders in 4:6, follows from the statement here that believers were raised with Christ.

Colossians 3:2
Colossians 3:2
NKJV

Colossians 3:2 Set your mind on things above, not on things on the earth.(NKJV)

NLT

Colossians 3:2 Think about the things of heaven, not the things of earth. (NLT)

Main Idea

Paul now turns to the mind: believers are to fix their thinking and desires on the things above rather than “on things on the earth.” The contrast looks back to regulations about things that “perish with the using” (2:22) and ahead to the “members which are on the earth” that must be put to death (3:5), so a mind set on heaven leads directly into obedience here and now.

Verse Explanation

Paul now turns to the mind: believers are to fix their thinking and desires on the things above rather than “on things on the earth.” The contrast looks back to regulations about things that “perish with the using” (2:22) and ahead to the “members which are on the earth” that must be put to death (3:5), so a mind set on heaven leads directly into obedience here and now.

Set your mind on things above, Verse 1 told the Colossians what to pursue. Verse 2 tells them where to keep their thoughts. To set the mind is to give one’s thinking and desires a settled direction, not an occasional glance upward. The “things above” are the same realities Paul named in verse 1, the things that belong to the risen Christ who reigns at God’s right hand. What fills a believer’s mind will soon govern what that believer does.

not on things on the earth. This contrast has to be read in context. Paul is not telling believers to ignore ordinary life; a few verses later he addresses their speech, their marriages, their children, and their work (3:8–25). The “things on the earth” are whatever competes with Christ for the mind. In chapter 2 that meant regulations about food and handling, which “all concern things which perish with the using” (2:22). In the next verses it means the sinful desires Paul calls “your members which are on the earth” (3:5). A mind fixed on Christ above is what equips a believer to put those earthly things in their place.

Study Notes

None.

Colossians 3:3
Colossians 3:3
NKJV

Colossians 3:3 For you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God.(NKJV)

NLT

Colossians 3:3 For you died to this life, and your real life is hidden with Christ in God. (NLT)

Main Idea

Paul gives the reason for setting their minds above: they have died with Christ, and their life is now “hidden with Christ in God.” Their new life is real and secure because it is bound up with Christ Himself, even though its glory cannot yet be seen.

Verse Explanation

Paul gives the reason for setting their minds above: they have died with Christ, and their life is now “hidden with Christ in God.” Their new life is real and secure because it is bound up with Christ Himself, even though its glory cannot yet be seen.

For you died, The word “For” introduces Paul’s reason for the commands of verses 1 and 2. The Colossians died with Christ, as Paul already said in 2:20. Their old life came to an end when they were joined to Christ in His death. Paul states this as something already done, not as a goal they must reach. That settled fact gives the commands both their reason and their strength: believers set their minds above because they no longer belong to the life they left behind.

and your life is hidden with Christ in God. Death is not the end of what Paul has to say. The Colossians now have life, and Paul tells them where it is. That life is “hidden,” meaning it is not on display to the world and is not yet fully visible even to believers themselves. It is hidden “with Christ,” so it shares His place and His safety. It is hidden “in God,” so it is kept by God Himself. The security of the believer’s life rests on Christ, not on how strong or impressive their spiritual progress looks. Because the most real thing about them cannot yet be seen, they need to keep their minds set above, where that life is kept.

Study Notes

Verse 3 does not say what believers died to, but 2:20 does: “if you died with Christ from the basic principles of the world.” The earlier verse shows that Paul has in mind a death that released them from the world’s basic principles and the human regulations tied to them.

The NLT expands this verse to “For you died to this life, and your real life is hidden with Christ in God.” The NKJV keeps the shorter wording, “For you died, and your life is hidden,” and leaves the reader to draw that sense from the context of 2:20 and 3:1.

The NKJV uses the word “hidden” elsewhere in Colossians. In 1:26 the mystery “has been hidden from ages and from generations, but now has been revealed,” and in 2:3 all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are “hidden” in Christ. In 3:3–4 the pattern is similar: the life now hidden with Christ will “appear” with Him.

Colossians 3:4
Colossians 3:4
NKJV

Colossians 3:4 When Christ who is our life appears, then you also will appear with Him in glory.(NKJV)

NLT

Colossians 3:4 And when Christ, who is your life, is revealed to the whole world, you will share in all his glory. (NLT)

Main Idea

Paul calls Christ Himself “our life,” so the believer’s life cannot be separated from Him. What is hidden now will be openly displayed when Christ appears, because believers will appear “with Him in glory,” and that sure future gives the present call its hope.

Verse Explanation

Paul calls Christ Himself “our life,” so the believer’s life cannot be separated from Him. What is hidden now will be openly displayed when Christ appears, because believers will appear “with Him in glory,” and that sure future gives the present call its hope.

When Christ who is our life appears, Paul looks ahead to the day Christ appears. He says “When,” not “if”: the appearing is certain, though this verse does not say when it will happen. He calls Christ “our life,” and the description goes further than calling Christ the source or the model of that life. Christ is the believer’s life, and the bond between them is so close that the one cannot be described apart from the other. Including himself in “our,” Paul speaks as one who shares this life with the Colossians.

then you also will appear with Him in glory. What is hidden now (3:3) will be openly shown then. Believers will appear “with Him,” sharing in His appearing, and “in glory,” in the splendor that belongs to Him. Paul earlier spoke of “Christ in you, the hope of glory” (1:27), and here he states plainly where that hope leads. This future gives the present commands their hope. Those who seek the things above are not chasing something uncertain. They are heading toward the day when the life they now hold by faith will be seen. The “Therefore” of verse 5 builds on everything Paul has said in these four verses.

Study Notes

The NKJV reads “Christ who is our life,” while the NLT reads “Christ, who is your life.” Both name Christ as the life of believers. The NKJV wording places Paul alongside his readers, then returns to address them directly with “then you also will appear.” The NLT keeps the focus on the readers throughout the verse.

Where the NKJV says that Christ “appears,” the NLT says that He “is revealed to the whole world.” The NLT wording brings out the public character of that appearing, which stands in contrast to the hidden life described in verse 3.

Colossians 3:5–11 — Putting Off the Old Life and Putting On the New Man

Colossians 3:5
Colossians 3:5
NKJV

Colossians 3:5 Therefore put to death your members which are on the earth: fornication, uncleanness, passion, evil desire, and covetousness, which is idolatry.(NKJV)

NLT

Colossians 3:5 So put to death the sinful, earthly things lurking within you. Have nothing to do with sexual immorality, impurity, lust, and evil desires. Don’t be greedy, for a greedy person is an idolater, worshiping the things of this world. (NLT)

Main Idea

Paul’s “Therefore” draws this command straight out of verses 1–4: because believers died and rose with Christ, they are to put to death the sinful practices that belonged to their old earthly life. He names sexual immorality (“fornication”), impurity, lustful passion, evil desire, and covetousness, which he calls idolatry because greed puts something else in God’s place.

Verse Explanation

Paul’s “Therefore” draws this command straight out of verses 1–4: because believers died and rose with Christ, they are to put to death the sinful practices that belonged to their old earthly life. He names sexual immorality (“fornication”), impurity, lustful passion, evil desire, and covetousness, which he calls idolatry because greed puts something else in God’s place.

Therefore draws this command straight out of verses 1–4. Paul has just told the Colossians that they died, that their life is “hidden with Christ in God” (3:3), and that they will appear with Christ in glory (3:4). Verses 1–2 already called them to seek the things above and set their minds there. Now, with that settled reality in view, Paul gives the first command to deal directly with sin: put it to death. The sequence matters. The command rests on what God has already done in uniting them with Christ, and that union supplies the power to obey. In chapter 2 Paul said that rules such as “Do not touch, do not taste, do not handle” (2:21) are “of no value against the indulgence of the flesh” (2:23). The life raised with Christ can do what those rules never could.

put to death your members which are on the earth Paul’s word “members” means the parts of the body. He speaks vividly, as though sin had taken up residence in hands, eyes, and appetites, and the list that follows names what he means. His target is the sinful practices themselves, not the body, since he has just dismissed harsh “neglect of the body” as worthless (2:23). “Put to death” calls for a decisive end to these sins rather than careful management of them. The words “on the earth” pick up verse 2: a mind set on things above leads straight into dealing with what belonged to the old earthly life.

fornication, uncleanness, passion, evil desire The first four names concern sexual sin and the desires behind it. Fornication is sexual immorality, and uncleanness is moral impurity more broadly. Passion and evil desire move inward, from what a person does to the cravings that drive the doing. Paul reaches past outward behavior into the heart where these practices begin.

and covetousness, which is idolatry Covetousness is the greedy desire to have more. Paul calls it idolatry because greed puts something else in God’s place, giving a possession or pleasure the trust and devotion that belong to Him alone. For people who are already “complete in Him” (2:10), grasping after more denies the very fullness Christ has given them.

Study Notes

The NLT divides this verse into three sentences. It renders “your members which are on the earth” as “the sinful, earthly things lurking within you,” translates “passion” as “lust,” and spells out “which is idolatry” as “a greedy person is an idolater, worshiping the things of this world.” Both translations identify sinful practices, not the physical body, as what must be put to death.

Paul opens both of the chapter’s main commands with “Therefore.” Here believers are to put sin to death, and in verse 12, “Therefore, as the elect of God,” they are told what to put on. Both commands rest on the union with Christ described in verses 1–4.

Colossians 3:6
Colossians 3:6
NKJV

Colossians 3:6 Because of these things the wrath of God is coming upon the sons of disobedience,(NKJV)

NLT

Colossians 3:6 Because of these sins, the anger of God is coming. (NLT)

Main Idea

Paul gives a sober reason for this decisive break: because of these very sins, “the wrath of God is coming” upon “the sons of disobedience,” meaning people whose lives are marked by refusing God. God’s coming judgment shows that these practices are serious, and believers must not treat them lightly.

Verse Explanation

Paul gives a sober reason for this decisive break: because of these very sins, “the wrath of God is coming” upon “the sons of disobedience,” meaning people whose lives are marked by refusing God. God’s coming judgment shows that these practices are serious, and believers must not treat them lightly.

Because of these things points back to the sins just named in verse 5. Paul has shown that these practices belong to the old life, and now he adds a sober reason for breaking with them completely.

the wrath of God is coming God’s wrath is His holy and settled opposition to evil, expressed in judgment. His wrath flows from His goodness rather than competing with it, because a good God cannot be indifferent to sexual exploitation, greed, and lies that ruin the people He made. The words “is coming” present that judgment as certain and already on its way. Because God will judge these sins, believers cannot treat them as harmless.

upon the sons of disobedience This expression describes people whose lives are marked by disobedience and refusal of God. Paul does not place the Colossians in that group, and the next verse speaks of these practices as part of their past. The warning still stands, though: the sins that bring God’s wrath have no place in the lives of those raised with Christ.

Study Notes

The NLT reads, “Because of these sins, the anger of God is coming,” and does not include the closing phrase “upon the sons of disobedience.” In either wording, the sins themselves are the reason God’s judgment is coming.

Colossians 3:7
Colossians 3:7
NKJV

Colossians 3:7 in which you yourselves once walked when you lived in them.(NKJV)

NLT

Colossians 3:7 You used to do these things when your life was still part of this world. (NLT)

Main Idea

Paul reminds the Colossians that these sins once described their own way of life, when they “lived in them.” The word “once” marks a real change, because that former life now belongs to their past.

Verse Explanation

Paul reminds the Colossians that these sins once described their own way of life, when they “lived in them.” The word “once” marks a real change, because that former life now belongs to their past.

in which you yourselves once walked The words “in which” refer back to “these things,” the sins of verse 5. Paul uses “walked” for a whole way of life, the steady direction of a person’s daily conduct. The words “you yourselves” address the Colossians directly. Paul is not describing strangers, and he leaves them no room to feel superior to the people of verse 6. “Once” marks a real change, because that way of walking now belongs to their past.

when you lived in them Before Christ, these sins were the very setting of their life, the place where they lived and moved. The contrast with verse 3 is plain. Their life is now “hidden with Christ in God,” so the old practices no longer define where or how they live.

Study Notes

“Walk” is one of the letter’s recurring words for conduct. Paul prays that the Colossians may “walk worthy of the Lord” (1:10), urges them, “so walk in Him” (2:6), and later tells them to “Walk in wisdom toward those who are outside” (4:5). Verse 7 describes the old walk that the new one replaces.

The NLT renders the verse, “You used to do these things when your life was still part of this world,” which also takes the verse as referring back to the sins of verse 5.

Colossians 3:8
Colossians 3:8
NKJV

Colossians 3:8 But now you yourselves are to put off all these: anger, wrath, malice, blasphemy, filthy language out of your mouth.(NKJV)

NLT

Colossians 3:8 But now is the time to get rid of anger, rage, malicious behavior, slander, and dirty language. (NLT)

Main Idea

With “But now,” Paul turns from their past to their present and widens the list to sins of temper and speech: anger, rage, malice, “blasphemy” (here meaning abusive, slanderous talk against others), and filthy language. Like old clothing, these are to be taken off and set aside.

Verse Explanation

With “But now,” Paul turns from their past to their present and widens the list to sins of temper and speech: anger, rage, malice, “blasphemy” (here meaning abusive, slanderous talk against others), and filthy language. Like old clothing, these are to be taken off and set aside.

But now These two words turn from the Colossians’ past to their present. Verse 7 described how they once lived, and verse 8 describes what fits the people they are now.

you yourselves are to put off all these Paul takes up the language of clothing. These sins are like garments from an old life, to be taken off and set aside. The picture prepares for verses 9–10, where Paul says believers have already put off the old man and put on the new, and for verses 12–14, where he tells them what to wear instead. “All these” leaves nothing in reserve.

anger, wrath, malice The list now widens from the desires of verse 5 to sins of temper. Anger and wrath describe hostile temper, whether it simmers or boils over. Malice is ill will, the wish to see another person harmed. These attitudes may begin inwardly, but they quickly tear at a church’s shared life.

blasphemy, filthy language out of your mouth Here “blasphemy” means abusive, slanderous talk against other people. Filthy language is obscene or degrading speech. By ending with “out of your mouth,” Paul shows where hostile attitudes break out: in words that wound others. The command against lying in the next verse continues this concern for speech.

Study Notes

The words “you yourselves” appear in both verse 7 and verse 8. The same people who “once walked” in these sins are now “to put off all these,” which underlines that Paul is describing one group of people whose life has changed.

In ordinary English, “blasphemy” usually means irreverent speech about God, and the NLT’s “slander” keeps readers from taking the word that way in this list. The NLT also has no separate phrase matching the NKJV’s “out of your mouth.”

In the NKJV, “out of your mouth” stands at the end of the list. It most naturally attaches to “filthy language,” though it may also gather the two speech sins of the list together. Either way, the closing words fix attention on speech.

Colossians 3:9
Colossians 3:9
NKJV

Colossians 3:9 Do not lie to one another, since you have put off the old man with his deeds,(NKJV)

NLT

Colossians 3:9 Don’t lie to each other, for you have stripped off your old sinful nature and all its wicked deeds. (NLT)

Main Idea

Paul commands believers not to lie to one another and gives the reason: they “have put off the old man with his deeds.” The “old man” is the person they were apart from Christ, and because that old self has already been taken off, deceiving a fellow believer no longer fits who they are.

Verse Explanation

Paul commands believers not to lie to one another and gives the reason: they “have put off the old man with his deeds.” The “old man” is the person they were apart from Christ, and because that old self has already been taken off, deceiving a fellow believer no longer fits who they are.

Do not lie to one another This command concerns honesty among believers. Lying destroys trust inside the church, the very body in which God’s peace is to rule (3:15). Believers cannot live as one body while they deceive each other.

since you have put off the old man Paul gives the reason for the command, and the reason is something already done. The “old man” is the person they were apart from Christ, the former humanity they once belonged to, and they have already taken it off. Paul does not tell them to become new people so that they can stop lying. He tells them to stop lying because that change has already taken place in their union with Christ.

with his deeds The old man came with a set of practices, and those deeds were taken off with him. Deception belongs to that discarded life, so lying to a fellow believer no longer fits who they are. Paul holds two things together here. In verses 5 and 8 he commands believers to put sins to death and put them off, and in verses 9–10 he grounds those commands in a putting off that has already happened.

Study Notes

Paul used this putting-off language earlier in the letter. In 2:11 he says believers were circumcised “with the circumcision made without hands, by putting off the body of the sins of the flesh.” Verse 9 draws on that settled reality to give the reason for the command.

The NLT reads, “for you have stripped off your old sinful nature and all its wicked deeds.” The phrase “old sinful nature” presents the old man mainly as something within the individual. The NKJV’s “old man” can carry the wider sense of the former humanity, the person and the people believers were apart from Christ. That wider sense fits the community language of verse 11.

Colossians 3:10
Colossians 3:10
NKJV

Colossians 3:10 and have put on the new man who is renewed in knowledge according to the image of Him who created him,(NKJV)

NLT

Colossians 3:10 Put on your new nature, and be renewed as you learn to know your Creator and become like him. (NLT)

Main Idea

Believers have also already “put on the new man,” the new humanity they belong to in Christ. That new life is still being renewed, growing in true knowledge so that it reflects the image of the Creator who made it, and Paul holds the finished change and the ongoing renewal together.

Verse Explanation

Believers have also already “put on the new man,” the new humanity they belong to in Christ. That new life is still being renewed, growing in true knowledge so that it reflects the image of the Creator who made it, and Paul holds the finished change and the ongoing renewal together.

and have put on the new man Alongside the putting off comes a putting on that is just as complete. The “new man” is the new humanity believers belong to in Christ. It is personal, because each believer has put it on. It is also shared, because verse 11 describes it as a community in which the old divisions give no one a privileged standing.

who is renewed in knowledge Although believers have already put on the new man, it is still being renewed. That renewal goes on continually, and it is God’s work in them. Paul names its direction with the words “in knowledge,” meaning true knowledge of God, the knowledge he prayed they would keep growing in (1:9–10). The false teaching offered rules with only “an appearance of wisdom” (2:23). Real growth in knowledge comes through the new life in Christ, and this ongoing renewal supplies daily strength for the commands in verses 5 and 8.

according to the image of Him who created him The renewal has a pattern and a goal. God created the new man, and He is renewing it so that it reflects His own image. Earlier Paul called the Son “the image of the invisible God” (1:15), so the new life comes to reflect more and more of God’s character as it is revealed in Christ. Paul holds the finished change and the ongoing renewal together. Believers have put on the new man, and that new man is still growing into the likeness of its Creator.

Study Notes

The NLT reads verse 10 as a command: “Put on your new nature, and be renewed as you learn to know your Creator and become like him.” The NKJV continues the statement begun in verse 9: believers “have put off the old man” and “have put on the new man.” Read that way, verses 9–10 give the reason for the command not to lie, and the direct command to “put on” comes in verse 12.

“Knowledge” is a recurring word in Colossians. Paul prays that the Colossians may be “filled with the knowledge of His will” (1:9) and go on “increasing in the knowledge of God” (1:10). He also says that in Christ “are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge” (2:3). The renewal “in knowledge” in verse 10 belongs to the same thread.

Colossians 3:11
Colossians 3:11
NKJV

Colossians 3:11 where there is neither Greek nor Jew, circumcised nor uncircumcised, barbarian, Scythian, slave nor free, but Christ is all and in all.(NKJV)

NLT

Colossians 3:11 In this new life, it doesn’t matter if you are a Jew or a Gentile, circumcised or uncircumcised, barbaric, uncivilized, slave, or free. Christ is all that matters, and he lives in all of us. (NLT)

Main Idea

In this renewed community, the lines that divided Paul’s world give no one a privileged standing before God. Those lines were ethnic and religious (Greek and Jew, circumcised and uncircumcised), cultural (barbarian, meaning a non-Greek outsider, and Scythian, a people regarded as especially uncivilized), and social (slave and free). Instead, “Christ is all and in all”: He defines this new people and lives among every one of them.

Verse Explanation

In this renewed community, the lines that divided Paul’s world give no one a privileged standing before God. Those lines were ethnic and religious (Greek and Jew, circumcised and uncircumcised), cultural (barbarian, meaning a non-Greek outsider, and Scythian, a people regarded as especially uncivilized), and social (slave and free). Instead, “Christ is all and in all”: He defines this new people and lives among every one of them.

where there is neither Greek nor Jew The word “where” refers to the new man of verse 10, the renewed humanity believers belong to in Christ. In this community, the lines that divided Paul’s world give no one a privileged standing before God. The first line is ethnic: Greek and Jew. Most of the Colossian believers were probably Gentiles (1:21, 27), and Paul assures them that they belong fully among God’s people.

circumcised nor uncircumcised The second line is religious. Circumcision marked Jewish identity, and its absence marked a person as an outsider to that people. Paul has already told the Colossians that they were circumcised “with the circumcision made without hands” (2:11), so no outward mark decides who belongs.

barbarian, Scythian The third line is cultural. A barbarian was a non-Greek outsider, and the Scythians were a people regarded as especially uncivilized. Even those whom the cultured world looked down on stand on equal ground in Christ.

slave nor free The fourth line is social. Slavery was part of the world Paul’s readers lived in, and the Colossian church included both enslaved people and their masters (3:22–4:1). In Christ, neither condition gives a person higher or lower standing. Paul is speaking about standing within the renewed community. He does not pretend these social differences have disappeared, because he goes on to give distinct instructions to bondservants and to masters.

but Christ is all and in all In place of every old marker of status stands Christ Himself. He is “all,” the One who defines this new people and gives them their identity, and He is “in all,” living among every one of them. This matches the mystery Paul proclaimed, “Christ in you, the hope of glory” (1:27), and God’s purpose “that in all things He may have the preeminence” (1:18).

Study Notes

Three groups in the NKJV list are opposite pairs joined by “nor”: Greek and Jew, circumcised and uncircumcised, slave and free. “Barbarian, Scythian” stands without a “nor.” These two names do not form an opposite pair, because both describe people outside Greek culture, and “Scythian” names a people regarded as especially uncivilized.

The NLT reads “a Jew or a Gentile” and “barbaric, uncivilized,” and it renders the final clause, “Christ is all that matters, and he lives in all of us.” That rendering takes “in all” as Christ’s presence in each believer. The NKJV’s more compact “Christ is all and in all” leaves room for both His presence in each believer and His presence among the whole community.

Colossians 3:12–17 — Clothed with Love and Ruled by Christ's Peace in One Body

Colossians 3:12
Colossians 3:12
NKJV

Colossians 3:12 Therefore, as the elect of God, holy and beloved, put on tender mercies, kindness, humility, meekness, longsuffering;(NKJV)

NLT

Colossians 3:12 Since God chose you to be the holy people he loves, you must clothe yourselves with tenderhearted mercy, kindness, humility, gentleness, and patience. (NLT)

Main Idea

Paul now tells believers what to put on, and he begins with who they already are: “the elect of God, holy and beloved.” Because God has chosen them, set them apart, and loved them, they are to clothe themselves with compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness (“meekness”), and patience (“longsuffering”).

Verse Explanation

Paul now tells believers what to put on, and he begins with who they already are: “the elect of God, holy and beloved.” Because God has chosen them, set them apart, and loved them, they are to clothe themselves with compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness (“meekness”), and patience (“longsuffering”).

Therefore ties these commands to what Paul has just said. Believers “have put on the new man” (3:10), and in that renewed community “Christ is all and in all” (3:11). Having described the new life they already belong to, Paul now describes the character that fits it. The commands that follow grow out of a change God has already made.

as the elect of God, holy and beloved — Paul tells the Colossians who they are before he tells them what to do. “The elect of God” are people God has chosen for Himself. “Holy” describes people set apart to belong to Him, and “beloved” means they are loved by Him. Each of these titles describes something God has done, and none of them is a reward for good behavior. Paul has already said that Christ reconciled them “to present you holy, and blameless, and above reproach in His sight” (1:22). The qualities he is about to list are the clothing of people God has already chosen and loved.

put on — Paul returns to the clothing picture of 3:8–10. Believers were told to “put off” anger, malice, and lying, and they “have put on the new man.” Now he names specific qualities to put on. Paul holds the two together: the new self is already theirs, and they are still to wear its character deliberately, day by day, in the way they treat one another.

tender mercies, kindness, humility, meekness, longsuffering — “Tender mercies” means heartfelt compassion that is moved by another person’s need. Kindness is goodness shown in action. Humility is a lowly view of oneself that does not insist on one’s own importance. “Meekness” means gentleness, the opposite of harsh or overbearing treatment, and “longsuffering” means patience with people over a long time, especially when they are difficult. Every one of these qualities faces outward toward other people. They replace the anger, wrath, and malice of 3:8, and they prepare for the bearing and forgiving that verse 13 describes.

Study Notes

The NLT renders the opening “Since God chose you to be the holy people he loves.” That wording makes explicit what the NKJV’s “as” implies: God’s choosing and loving is the reason for the command, and not only a description placed beside it.

Paul names five qualities here. In the NKJV he also named five sins to put to death in 3:5 and five to put off in 3:8, so this list of new clothing matches the length of each list of old practices.

“Longsuffering” appeared earlier in Paul’s prayer that the Colossians would be “strengthened with all might, according to His glorious power, for all patience and longsuffering with joy” (1:11). The patience Paul now tells them to put on is the same quality he asked God to supply by His power.

even as Christ forgave you, so you also must do — Christ’s forgiveness supplies both the pattern and the reason. Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly — “The word of Christ” is the message about Christ, the gospel the Colossians first heard as “the word of the truth of the gospel” (1:5). To “dwell” is to live in a place as one’s home, so Paul wants this message to settle in and stay. “In you” includes each believer’s heart and the gathered church together, and “richly” asks for abundance. The church had felt pressure from “the commandments and doctrines of men” (2:22). Paul’s answer is to fill the church with the word of Christ.

in all wisdom — The word of Christ produces true wisdom. Paul has said that in Christ “are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge” (2:3), while the human regulations had only “an appearance of wisdom” (2:23). The wisdom Paul has in mind comes from Christ’s word and guides how believers speak to one another.

teaching and admonishing one another — Teaching gives instruction in the truth, and admonishing means warning or correcting someone who is going astray. Paul expects believers to do both for “one another,” so this work does not belong to leaders alone. Earlier Paul described his own ministry as “warning every man and teaching every man in all wisdom” (1:28). That same kind of care now passes among the members of the church.

in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs — Singing becomes one of the ways the word of Christ teaches and warns. Together the three terms cover the range of songs the church used in its worship.

singing with grace in your hearts to the Lord — The songs are sung to the Lord, so they are both instruction for one another and worship offered to Him. “With grace in your hearts” calls for singing that comes from within, from hearts that have received God’s grace and answer it with gratitude. The words on believers’ lips are meant to match what is happening in their hearts.

“The word of Christ” can mean the message about Christ or the word Christ Himself speaks. The NLT chooses “the message about Christ,” and the letter’s emphasis on the gospel the Colossians heard and learned (1:5–7) fits that reading well.

The phrase “in all wisdom” can be attached either to the word dwelling richly or to the teaching that follows. The NKJV places it directly after “richly,” while the NLT joins it to the teaching: “Teach and counsel each other with all the wisdom he gives.”

The NKJV connects “psalms and hymns and spiritual songs” to teaching and admonishing, so the songs themselves become a means of instruction. The NLT connects them to singing instead: “Sing psalms and hymns and spiritual songs to God with thankful hearts.” Either way, singing and mutual instruction stand close together in the same sentence.

“With grace” can be understood as gratitude, which is how the NLT renders it (“with thankful hearts”), or as the grace God gives. The surrounding commands to “be thankful” (3:15) and to give thanks to the Father (3:17) make the sense of gratitude a natural fit.

the reward of the inheritance; The inheritance is the reward. Paul has already thanked the Father “who has qualified us to be partakers of the inheritance of the saints in the light” (1:12). People whose labor belonged to someone else are promised a full share in what God gives all His people.

for you serve the Lord Christ. This short clause sums up the whole instruction. However their days were spent, the Master these enslaved believers truly served was Christ, and He is the One who rewards them.

Study Notes

The NLT renders “knowing that” as “Remember that.” The NKJV states what these believers already know, while the NLT turns the same truth into a call to keep it in mind.

The NLT renders the last clause “the Master you are serving is Christ.” In English, that wording sets Christ beside the earthly “masters according to the flesh” of 3:22, and 4:1 completes the contrast by reminding masters that they “also have a Master in heaven.”

Main Idea

Paul adds a warning that the one who does wrong will be repaid, because God shows no partiality. Standing between the promise to enslaved believers and the command in 4:1 that masters give their bondservants “what is just and fair,” the warning holds everyone accountable to the same Lord, whatever their place in society.

Verse Explanation

Paul adds a warning that the one who does wrong will be repaid, because God shows no partiality. Standing between the promise to enslaved believers and the command in 4:1 that masters give their bondservants “what is just and fair,” the warning holds everyone accountable to the same Lord, whatever their place in society.

But he who does wrong The word “But” turns from promise to warning. Paul’s wording is general. It applies to an enslaved believer who cheats or shirks, and it applies just as surely to a master who mistreats the people under his control, to whom Paul speaks in 4:1.

will be repaid for what he has done, The Lord who rewards faithful service also repays wrongdoing. No one in the household stands outside His justice, and no wrong done within its walls escapes His notice.

and there is no partiality. Partiality means favoring someone because of who they are or the position they hold. The Lord does not judge by social standing. A master’s rank will not shield him, and an enslaved person’s low position neither excuses wrongdoing nor counts against him. Everyone answers to the same Lord, which is why Paul goes on to tell masters to give their bondservants “what is just and fair, knowing that you also have a Master in heaven” (4:1).

Study Notes

The NKJV does not name the one who shows no partiality. The NLT supplies the subject, “God has no favorites,” which agrees with the surrounding verses, where the Lord gives the reward (3:24) and masters answer to “a Master in heaven” (4:1).

The NLT puts the warning in the second person, “if you do what is wrong,” which keeps it addressed to the enslaved believers of verses 22–24. The NKJV’s third-person “he who does wrong” is more general. Because the verse stands between the words to bondservants and the command to masters, it can be read as a warning to enslaved believers or as an assurance that masters who wrong them will be repaid. The general wording allows both readings.
