Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 3
Main Idea:

Because believers were raised with Christ and their life is hidden with Him, they put off the old life and put on the new, so that God’s peace and Christ’s word govern the one body and every household relationship is lived under the Lord.

Setting:

Chapter 3 follows Paul’s warnings against human regulations in chapter 2. Its opening words, “If then you were raised with Christ” (3:1), answer “if you died with Christ” (2:20) and begin the call to live the new life under Christ’s lordship that runs through 4:6.

Chapter Outline:

  • Colossians 3:1–4 — Raised with Christ, Seeking the Things Above

  • Colossians 3:5–11 — Putting Off the Old Life and Putting On the New Man

  • Colossians 3:12–17 — Clothed with Love and Ruled by Christ's Peace in One Body

  • Colossians 3:18–25 — Wives, Husbands, Children, Fathers, and Bondservants Under the Lord

Key Verse:

“If then you were raised with Christ, seek those things which are above, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God.” (Colossians 3:1)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Colossians 3 begins with what is already true. The “if” in “If then you were raised with Christ” (3:1) expresses no doubt. It answers the “if you died with Christ” of 2:20 and states where believers already stand. Paul has just set aside rules such as “Do not touch, do not taste, do not handle” (2:21), and now he gives commands of his own. These commands grow out of union with the risen Christ, who is seated “at the right hand of God.” That union gives believers both the reason and the power to obey. Their life is “hidden with Christ in God” (3:3). It is real and secure now, though its glory is still unseen, and Paul promises that “When Christ who is our life appears,” they “will appear with Him in glory” (3:4).

From that heavenly starting point the chapter moves straight into earthly life. A mind set on things above leads directly to putting to death the sins of the old life (3:2, 5). Paul describes the change in terms of clothing. Believers take off anger, malice, and lying, and they put on tender mercies, kindness, humility, gentleness (“meekness”), and patience (“longsuffering”), with love over everything as “the bond of perfection” (3:8–14). They “have put on the new man” already, yet that new man “is renewed in knowledge” day by day (3:10). Paul holds the finished change and the continuing growth together.

Before Paul tells them what to wear, he reminds them who God has made them: “the elect of God, holy and beloved” (3:12). God chose them and loved them first, and Christ’s forgiveness of them is both the reason and the pattern for their forgiveness of one another (3:13). The new life is also a shared life. In the renewed community “Christ is all and in all” (3:11), the peace of God rules those “called in one body” (3:15), and the word of Christ lives among them as they teach and sing. The chapter ends in the household, where every relationship is placed under the Lord and enslaved believers hear, “you serve the Lord Christ” (3:24).

Chapter Sections

Colossians 3:1–4 — Raised with Christ, Seeking the Things Above

Because believers were raised with Christ, Paul calls them to seek the things above and set their minds on them, where Christ sits at the right hand of God. Their life is hidden with Christ now, and when He appears they will appear with Him in glory.

Colossians 3:5–11 — Putting Off the Old Life and Putting On the New Man

Paul’s “Therefore” draws his commands out of that new life: believers are to put to death sexual immorality, impurity, passion, evil desire, and covetousness, and to put off anger, malice, abusive speech, and lying. They have already put off the old man and put on the new man, which is being renewed in the image of its Creator, in a community where “Christ is all and in all.”

Colossians 3:12–17 — Clothed with Love and Ruled by Christ's Peace in One Body

As “the elect of God, holy and beloved,” believers put on tender mercies, humility, and patience, forgive one another as Christ forgave them, and above all put on love. The peace of God rules the one body, the word of Christ dwells richly in their teaching and singing, and everything is done “in the name of the Lord Jesus” with thanksgiving.

Colossians 3:18–25 — Wives, Husbands, Children, Fathers, and Bondservants Under the Lord

Paul speaks in pairs to wives and husbands, children and fathers, and bondservants, meaning enslaved people, and he places each relationship under the Lord. Enslaved believers serve “the Lord Christ” and will receive the inheritance from Him, and the pair is completed in 4:1: “Masters, give your bondservants what is just and fair, knowing that you also have a Master in heaven.”

Why This Chapter Matters

Colossians 3 shows how grace produces holiness. The rules of chapter 2 had “an appearance of wisdom” but were “of no value against the indulgence of the flesh” (2:23). The life raised with Christ can “put to death” the very desires those rules could not restrain (3:5). This guards against two opposite errors: trusting regulations to make people holy, and treating sin lightly when God’s wrath “is coming” because of it (3:6).

The chapter also shows that the Christian life is lived together. Forgiveness, love, peace, teaching, and singing all happen among “one another” (3:13, 16), and none of the old ethnic, religious, or social lines gives anyone a privileged standing (3:11).

Christ’s lordship also reaches the home and the daily work of the household. Each side of every pair has a duty before the Lord. A wife’s submission is measured by what is “fitting in the Lord” (3:18) and never permits coercion or wrongdoing. Enslaved believers share in the inheritance of God’s people, wrongdoing is repaid with “no partiality” (3:25), and masters answer to “a Master in heaven” (4:1).

Key Themes to Watch For

Raised with Christ (3:1–4). Union with the risen Christ is the settled reality behind every command in the chapter. Believers’ life is secure with Him now and will appear with Him in glory.

Putting off and putting on (3:8–10, 12, 14). Paul uses the picture of clothing for leaving the old life and wearing the new. Believers have already put on the new man, which is still being renewed.

One body where Christ is all (3:11, 15). Within the renewed community no ethnic, religious, cultural, or social line gives anyone privileged standing, and the peace of God governs their shared life.

The word of Christ and thanksgiving (3:15–17). Christ’s word dwells richly among believers as they teach, warn, and sing. Paul joins it to the commands to “be thankful” and to give thanks “to God the Father through Him.”

Every relationship under the Lord (3:17–24; 4:1). The phrases “in the name of the Lord Jesus,” “fitting in the Lord,” “well pleasing to the Lord,” and “as to the Lord” place all of life, including the household, under one Lord.

Key Words & Concepts

“Things above” and “the right hand of God” (3:1–2). These phrases point to where the risen Christ now reigns. Setting the mind there does not pull believers away from life on earth; it leads directly into obedience in daily life (3:5).

“Your members which are on the earth” (3:5). With these words Paul calls believers to put to death the sinful practices that belonged to their old earthly life, and he names them: fornication, uncleanness, passion, evil desire, and covetousness. He calls covetousness idolatry because greed puts something else in God’s place.

The old man and the new man (3:9–10). The “old man” is the person believers were apart from Christ. The “new man” is the new humanity they now belong to in Christ. Believers have already put it on, and it keeps growing in true knowledge so that it reflects the image of its Creator.

Barbarian and Scythian (3:11). A barbarian was a non-Greek outsider, and Scythians were a people regarded as especially uncivilized. Alongside Greek and Jew, circumcised and uncircumcised, and slave and free, these names mark the lines that divided Paul’s world.

“Let the peace of God rule” (3:15). Paul pictures God’s peace acting like an umpire that settles what governs believers’ relationships. This peace belongs to their shared calling “in one body,” and each believer’s personal peace is held within that shared life. The verse does not make an inward feeling of calm a test of guidance on its own.

Bondservants (3:22; 4:1). In the household instructions, “bondservants” refers to enslaved people. Paul speaks to them directly as believers with dignity before the Lord, and he calls their owners “masters according to the flesh” (3:22), reminding them that these masters are not their highest Lord.
