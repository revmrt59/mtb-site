Colossians 4 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Colossians 4 completes Paul’s teaching on the new life under Christ’s lordship by finishing the household instructions with the command to masters and calling the church to watchful, thankful prayer and to wise, gracious speech toward outsiders. The chapter then brings the whole letter to a close with the messengers who carry it, greetings from Paul’s fellow workers, instructions for the neighboring churches, and Paul’s signature in his own hand.

Chapter Sections

1. Colossians 4:1 — Masters Who Answer to a Master in Heaven

Paul completes the last household pair, begun with bondservants in 3:22–25, by telling masters to give their bondservants (the enslaved people in their households) “what is just and fair.” The reason is that masters also stand under authority: “you also have a Master in heaven.”

2. Colossians 4:2–4 — Watchful, Thankful Prayer for an Open Door

Paul calls the church to “continue earnestly in prayer, being vigilant in it with thanksgiving,” and asks them to pray for him and his companions as well. Although he is in chains, he asks them to pray that God “would open to us a door for the word,” so that he can make the mystery of Christ plain.

3. Colossians 4:5–6 — Wise Conduct and Gracious Speech Toward Outsiders

Turning from prayer for his preaching to the church’s own witness, Paul urges believers to “walk in wisdom toward those who are outside, redeeming the time.” He wants their speech to “always be with grace, seasoned with salt,” so that they know how to answer each person.

4. Colossians 4:7–9 — Tychicus and Onesimus Sent to Colosse

Paul commends Tychicus as “a beloved brother, faithful minister, and fellow servant in the Lord” and sends him to tell the church Paul’s news and to comfort their hearts. Onesimus travels with Tychicus, and Paul introduces him as “a faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you.”

5. Colossians 4:10–14 — Greetings from Paul’s Fellow Workers

Greetings come from Aristarchus, Mark, and Justus, the only Jewish believers among Paul’s coworkers, and from Epaphras, who is “always laboring fervently for you in prayers” that the Colossians “may stand perfect and complete in all the will of God.” Luke and Demas send their greetings as well.

6. Colossians 4:15–17 — Greetings to Laodicea, an Exchange of Letters, and a Charge to Archippus

Paul sends his own greetings to the believers in Laodicea and to Nymphas and the church that meets in that home, and he tells the Colossians to have this letter read in Laodicea and to read “the epistle from Laodicea” themselves. He then gives Archippus a personal charge to fulfill the ministry he has received in the Lord.

7. Colossians 4:18 — Paul’s Signature and Final Request

Paul takes the pen to sign the greeting “by my own hand,” asks the church to “Remember my chains,” and closes the letter with grace.
