Galatians Book Outline

Revision 1 • October 29, 2026

Major Movements

I. The True Gospel and Paul’s God-Given Commission (1:1–2:21)

Paul confronts the Galatians’ movement toward a different gospel and explains that his message and calling came through Jesus Christ. His dealings with the Jerusalem apostles and his confrontation with Peter show that justification by faith governs both gospel proclamation and fellowship between Jews and Gentiles.

II. The Promise Fulfilled in Christ and the Freedom of God’s Children (3:1–4:31)

Paul appeals to the Galatians’ reception of the Spirit, Abraham’s faith, and the priority of God’s promise. He explains the law’s temporary role and their adoption through Christ, then urges them not to return to bondage, contrasting slavery and freedom through Hagar and Sarah.

III. Standing in Freedom and Walking by the Spirit (5:1–6:10)

Paul warns that receiving circumcision as a requirement for justification abandons reliance on Christ. Freedom works through love as believers walk by the Spirit, restore the fallen, bear burdens, and persist in doing good.

IV. The Cross and the New Creation as Paul’s Final Boast (6:11–6:18)

Paul exposes the motives of those pressing circumcision and contrasts their boasting with his own boast in Christ’s cross. He closes by emphasizing the new creation and invoking peace, mercy, and grace.
