Hebrews 1:1
Hebrews 1:1
NKJV

Hebrews 1:1 God, who at various times and in various ways spoke in time past to the fathers by the prophets,(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 1:1 Long ago God spoke many times and in many ways to our ancestors through the prophets. (NLT)

Main Idea

Hebrews opens with God as the One who speaks: in earlier times He spoke to the fathers of Israel through the prophets, “at various times and in various ways.” That earlier revelation was truly God’s word, and it prepared for what He has now said in His Son.

Verse Explanation

Hebrews opens with God as the One who speaks: in earlier times He spoke to the fathers of Israel through the prophets, “at various times and in various ways.” That earlier revelation was truly God’s word, and it prepared for what He has now said in His Son.

“at various times and in various ways” God is the subject of the opening sentence. He spoke to Israel’s ancestors through the prophets in different periods and in different forms. The journey from promise to fulfillment begins with God making Himself known through those messengers. The same God who gave their words authority now addresses the people who hear this letter.

In these last days God has spoken to us by His Son, the final and fullest word that the prophets’ message anticipated. The Son is the appointed heir of all things and the One through whom God made the worlds, so the One who will inherit creation is the One through whom it came to be.

“in these last days” The coming of the Son marks the time in which God brings His earlier promises to fulfillment. God has spoken to us by the Son Himself, whose person and work make Him the fullest revelation of the Father.

“heir of all things” All things belong to the Son by the Father’s appointment. He is heir of the very creation God brought into being through Him. The Son stands at the beginning of creation and at the end toward which it moves.

“by Himself purged our sins” The Son Himself accomplished the cleansing that sinners needed before God. He then took the place of honor at God’s right hand. Hebrews 10:11–14 draws out the difference between priests who stand to offer repeatedly and Jesus who sits after His offering. Hebrews 7:25 shows what Jesus continues to do for those who come through Him.

“a more excellent name” The inherited name declares His royal standing. The next verse shows why the name Son gives Him a place no angel holds.

Hebrews 1:5–6 — The Son and Firstborn Whom Angels Worship

Study Notes

None.

Hebrews 1:5
Hebrews 1:5
NKJV

Hebrews 1:5 For to which of the angels did He ever say: "You are My Son, Today I have begotten You"? And again: "I will be to Him a Father, And He shall be to Me a Son"?(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 1:5 For God never said to any angel what he said to Jesus: “You are my Son.Today I have become your Father. ” God also said, “I will be his Father,and he will be my Son.” (NLT)

Main Idea

No angel was ever addressed as God addresses the Son in the royal words of Psalm 2, “You are My Son, Today I have begotten You,” or in His promise to David’s heir in 2 Samuel 7, “I will be to Him a Father, And He shall be to Me a Son.” These words identify Jesus as the promised royal Son, a standing no angel ever held.

Verse Explanation

No angel was ever addressed as God addresses the Son in the royal words of Psalm 2, “You are My Son, Today I have begotten You,” or in His promise to David’s heir in 2 Samuel 7, “I will be to Him a Father, And He shall be to Me a Son.” These words identify Jesus as the promised royal Son, a standing no angel ever held.

“to which of the angels” The question expects the answer that none received this address. In Psalm 2:6–8, God installs His king and gives him the nations as an inheritance. Hebrews applies the royal decree to Jesus. The word Today belongs to the declaration of the king’s standing, with the Son Himself already present in the work of creation in Hebrews 1:2.

“I will be to Him a Father” In 2 Samuel 7:12–16 God promised David a successor and a lasting throne. Hebrews joins that father-to-son promise to Psalm 2 so that both passages point to Jesus as the promised royal Son.

Hebrews 1:6
NKJV

Hebrews 1:6 But when He again brings the firstborn into the world, He says: "Let all the angels of God worship Him."(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 1:6 And when he brought his supreme Son into the world, God said, “Let all of God’s angels worship him.” (NLT)

Main Idea

When God brings His firstborn into the world, He commands, “Let all the angels of God worship Him.” Firstborn marks the Son’s rank as heir, and the command places Him above the angels as the One they worship.

Verse Explanation

When God brings His firstborn into the world, He commands, “Let all the angels of God worship Him.” Firstborn marks the Son’s rank as heir, and the command places Him above the angels as the One they worship.

“the firstborn” The title identifies the Son as the supreme heir, in keeping with Hebrews 1:2. Psalm 89:27 uses firstborn to describe the elevated rank of the king. God commands all His angels to worship the Son. The command puts the difference between their positions beyond question even though the time of the entrance is debated.

Study Notes

The word again can introduce another quotation or describe a repeated entrance. Readers have connected the entrance to Christ’s first coming, His exaltation, or His future return. Hebrews’ comparison here depends on whom the angels worship.

Main Idea

God never said to any angel, “Sit at My right hand, Till I make Your enemies Your footstool,” as He says to the Son in Psalm 110. The Son alone is enthroned beside God while His enemies are being brought into subjection.

Verse Explanation

God never said to any angel, “Sit at My right hand, Till I make Your enemies Your footstool,” as He says to the Son in Psalm 110. The Son alone is enthroned beside God while His enemies are being brought into subjection.

“Sit at My right hand” Psalm 110:1 gives the royal Son the place of authority beside God. Hebrews again asks which angel ever received such an invitation.

“Till I make Your enemies Your footstool” The footstool pictures complete subjection. God promises to bring the Son’s enemies under His rule while the Son already occupies the place of honor.

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

The angels are “ministering spirits sent forth to minister for those who will inherit salvation.” Far from rivaling the Son, they serve God by helping the people He is saving, which prepares for the warning that this salvation must not be neglected.

“ministering spirits” All the angels belong to this description. God sends them to serve those who will inherit salvation. Their service is real and honored, and their ministry helps explain the appeal in Hebrews 2:1–4. A message entrusted to angels carried accountability, and the salvation announced by the Lord calls for even closer attention.
