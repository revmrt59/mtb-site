Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 1
Main Idea:

God, who spoke through the prophets, has now spoken His final word in His Son, the heir, Creator, and Sustainer of all things, who purged our sins and sat down at God’s right hand, far above the angels.

Setting:

Hebrews opens with no greeting. Its hearers were most likely believers of Jewish background who knew Israel’s Scriptures well. Because Scripture links angels with the giving of the law (2:2), the comparison with the angels shows why the Son’s message demands even closer attention (2:1–4).

Chapter Outline:

  • Hebrews 1:1–4 — God Has Spoken in His Son

  • Hebrews 1:5–6 — The Son and Firstborn Whom Angels Worship

  • Hebrews 1:7–12 — Serving Angels and the Eternal, Unchanging Son

  • Hebrews 1:13–14 — The Enthroned Son and the Ministering Angels

Key Verse:

“who being the brightness of His glory and the express image of His person, and upholding all things by the word of His power, when He had by Himself purged our sins, sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high” (Hebrews 1:3)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Hebrews begins with God speaking. He spoke “at various times and in various ways” through the prophets, and that word was truly His. Now He “has in these last days spoken to us by His Son” (1:1–2). The Son is the heir of all things and the One through whom God made the worlds. He is “the brightness of His glory and the express image of His person,” and He holds all creation together “by the word of His power” (1:3). This same Son “by Himself purged our sins” and then “sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high.” His seat shows that the work of cleansing is finished.

The rest of the chapter proves the Son’s greatness from Israel’s own Scriptures. Seven quotations, mostly from the Psalms, set Him above the angels. God calls Him “My Son” and promises to be His Father, words He never spoke to an angel (1:5). He commands all the angels to worship Him (1:6). Angels are servants, swift as wind and fire, but to the Son God says, “Your throne, O God, is forever and ever” (1:8). The Son laid the earth’s foundation, and when heaven and earth wear out like an old garment, “You are the same, And Your years will not fail” (1:12). Only to the Son does God say, “Sit at My right hand” (1:13).

None of this belittles the angels. They are “ministering spirits sent forth to minister for those who will inherit salvation” (1:14). Their service prepares for the warning that follows: if this is who has spoken, His word cannot be ignored.

Chapter Sections

Hebrews 1:1–4 — God Has Spoken in His Son

The God who spoke through the prophets in many ways has now spoken His final word through His Son. The Son is heir and Creator of all things, the radiance of God’s glory and exact representation of His being, who sustains all things, purged sins, and took His seat at God’s right hand, far above the angels.

Hebrews 1:5–6 — The Son and Firstborn Whom Angels Worship

Psalm 2 and God’s promise to David show that God speaks to Jesus as His royal Son, something He never said to any angel. When God brings His firstborn into the world, He commands all the angels to worship Him.

Hebrews 1:7–12 — Serving Angels and the Eternal, Unchanging Son

God’s angels are servants, like wind and flame, but the Son reigns on an eternal throne of righteousness and is anointed with gladness. Hebrews applies Psalm 102 to the Son as the LORD who created heaven and earth and who stays the same when creation wears out.

Hebrews 1:13–14 — The Enthroned Son and the Ministering Angels

God never invited an angel to sit at His right hand, as He invites the Son in Psalm 110 until His enemies are subdued. The angels serve God by helping those who will inherit salvation.

Why This Chapter Matters

Hebrews 1 settles who Jesus is before it asks anything of its hearers. He is far more than a great teacher or a high-ranking angel. He is the Son through whom God made the world, the One who reveals God perfectly, and the One whom Scripture addresses as God and Lord (1:2–3, 8, 10). Everything else in Hebrews rests on that foundation.

The chapter also shows that God’s word in the Son is final and sufficient. The prophets’ message was true and prepared the way, and the Son completes it. Because He has already purged sins and sat down, the hearers do not need to look anywhere else for cleansing or for a word from God.

Key Themes to Watch For

God has spoken. The God who spoke through the prophets has now spoken in His Son (1:1–2).

The Son’s divine identity. The Son is Creator, Sustainer, the radiance of God’s glory, and is addressed as God (1:2–3, 8–12).

Finished cleansing. The Son purged sins by Himself and sat down (1:3).

Above the angels. The Son receives a better name, the angels’ worship, and an eternal throne (1:4–9).

The enthroned King. The Son sits at God’s right hand until His enemies are subdued (1:3, 13).

Key Words & Concepts

In these last days (1:2). The time that began with the Son’s coming, when God gave His final and fullest word.

The express image of His person (1:3). An exact representation, like the impression a seal leaves in wax. The Son perfectly shows who God is.

Purged our sins (1:3). Made cleansing for sins. Hebrews will later explain that the Son did this by offering Himself (9:14; 10:12).

The right hand of the Majesty on high (1:3). The place of highest honor and authority beside God. The Son sat down there because His cleansing work was complete.

Firstborn (1:6). A title of rank as heir, used of Israel’s king in Psalm 89:27. Scripture does not say exactly when God “brings the firstborn into the world,” whether at His birth, His exaltation, or His return, and the question can be left open.

Ministering spirits (1:14). The angels’ role as servants sent by God to help those who will inherit salvation.
