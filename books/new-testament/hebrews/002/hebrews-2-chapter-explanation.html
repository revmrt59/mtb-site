Chapter Study

Hebrews 2

Verse-by-Verse
Hebrews 2:1
Hebrews 2:1
NKJV

Hebrews 2:1 Therefore we must give the more earnest heed to the things we have heard, lest we drift away.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 2:1 So we must listen very carefully to the truth we have heard, or we may drift away from it. (NLT)

Main Idea

Because the Son is so far above the angels, “we must give the more earnest heed to the things we have heard, lest we drift away.” The danger the writer names is not a sudden break but a slow drift that comes from failing to pay attention to God’s word in His Son.

Verse Explanation

Because the Son is so far above the angels, “we must give the more earnest heed to the things we have heard, lest we drift away.” The danger the writer names is not a sudden break but a slow drift that comes from failing to pay attention to God’s word in His Son.

“give the more earnest heed” Therefore leads back to the Son’s superiority in chapter 1. The writer includes himself in the appeal to hold closely to the message the church has heard. A person can begin to drift by letting the truth slip out of attention and practice. The verse calls the whole congregation to take the Son’s word seriously now.

Hebrews 2:2
Hebrews 2:2
NKJV

Hebrews 2:2 For if the word spoken through angels proved steadfast, and every transgression and disobedience received a just reward,(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 2:2 For the message God delivered through angels has always stood firm, and every violation of the law and every act of disobedience was punished. (NLT)

Main Idea

The word spoken through angels, the law given at Sinai, proved steadfast, and every transgression and act of disobedience against it received a just penalty. Hebrews begins a lesser-to-greater argument: if that word carried such weight, the word spoken by the Son carries more.

Verse Explanation

The word spoken through angels, the law given at Sinai, proved steadfast, and every transgression and act of disobedience against it received a just penalty. Hebrews begins a lesser-to-greater argument: if that word carried such weight, the word spoken by the Son carries more.

“the word spoken through angels proved steadfast” God’s law given at Sinai was binding, and Scripture associates angels with its delivery (Acts 7:53 and Galatians 3:19). Its penalties were just and showed that God held His people answerable for what He had spoken. That established accountability is the first half of the comparison.

Hebrews 2:3
Hebrews 2:3
NKJV

Hebrews 2:3 how shall we escape if we neglect so great a salvation, which at the first began to be spoken by the Lord, and was confirmed to us by those who heard Him,(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 2:3 So what makes us think we can escape if we ignore this great salvation that was first announced by the Lord Jesus himself and then delivered to us by those who heard him speak? (NLT)

Main Idea

The writer asks, “how shall we escape if we neglect so great a salvation,” a salvation first spoken by the Lord Himself and confirmed to the writer and his hearers by those who heard Him. The question carries its own answer: since even the word spoken through angels brought a just penalty, as Hebrews 2:2 has said, those who neglect the salvation announced by the Lord will not escape God’s judgment.

Verse Explanation

The writer asks, “how shall we escape if we neglect so great a salvation,” a salvation first spoken by the Lord Himself and confirmed to the writer and his hearers by those who heard Him. The question carries its own answer: since even the word spoken through angels brought a just penalty, as Hebrews 2:2 has said, those who neglect the salvation announced by the Lord will not escape God’s judgment.

“how shall we escape if we neglect so great a salvation” If the word at Sinai carried consequences, those who neglect the greater salvation announced by the Lord will not escape God’s judgment. The Lord Himself first announced it, and firsthand hearers confirmed it to the writer and congregation. The greatness of the salvation and the authority of its Speaker give the warning its weight.

God has not placed “the world to come, of which we speak” under the rule of angels. Hebrews turns from the warning back to its argument about the Son, preparing to show who will rule that coming world.

“the world to come” God has not placed the coming world under angelic rule. This statement turns the argument from what angels helped deliver to who will exercise the rule God intends. Psalm 8 supplies the language for that next step.

Hebrews 2:9
Hebrews 2:9
NKJV

Hebrews 2:9 But we see Jesus, who was made a little lower than the angels, for the suffering of death crowned with glory and honor, that He, by the grace of God, might taste death for everyone.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 2:9 What we do see is Jesus, who for a little while was given a position “a little lower than the angels”; and because he suffered death for us, he is now “crowned with glory and honor.” Yes, by God’s grace, Jesus tasted death for everyone. (NLT)

Main Idea

What we do see is Jesus, made for a little while lower than the angels and sharing our lowliness so that by the grace of God He might “taste death for everyone.” Because He suffered that death on behalf of each one, He is now crowned with glory and honor, and in Him humanity’s lost calling is being fulfilled.

Verse Explanation

What we do see is Jesus, made for a little while lower than the angels and sharing our lowliness so that by the grace of God He might “taste death for everyone.” Because He suffered that death on behalf of each one, He is now crowned with glory and honor, and in Him humanity’s lost calling is being fulfilled.

“But we see Jesus” Jesus entered our low position for a time. By God’s grace He truly tasted death for everyone, on behalf of each one. The Jesus who suffered that death is now crowned with glory and honor. He fulfills the human calling the psalm announces and opens the way for the many sons He brings to glory.

Study Notes

The wording “a little lower” can convey a smaller degree of rank or a brief period of lowliness. Hebrews’ movement from suffering to crowning gives the time sense particular force as the words are applied to Jesus.

Hebrews 2:10–13 — The Captain of Salvation and His Brethren

Hebrews 2:10
Hebrews 2:10
NKJV

Hebrews 2:10 For it was fitting for Him, for whom are all things and by whom are all things, in bringing many sons to glory, to make the captain of their salvation perfect through sufferings.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 2:10 God, for whom and through whom everything was made, chose to bring many children into glory. And it was only right that he should make Jesus, through his suffering, a perfect leader, fit to bring them into their salvation. (NLT)

Main Idea

In bringing many sons to glory, it was fitting for God, for whom and by whom all things exist, “to make the captain of their salvation perfect through sufferings.” Jesus’ suffering did not correct a flaw in Him but completed the path by which He became the leader who brings God’s people to glory.

Verse Explanation

In bringing many sons to glory, it was fitting for God, for whom and by whom all things exist, “to make the captain of their salvation perfect through sufferings.” Jesus’ suffering did not correct a flaw in Him but completed the path by which He became the leader who brings God’s people to glory.

“for whom are all things and by whom are all things” God is both the source and the goal of creation. The saving path He chose fits His purpose of bringing many sons to glory.

“perfect through sufferings” The captain is the leader who opens the way for those who follow. Jesus was already sinless. Through suffering He reached the intended end of the work God gave Him and became fully fitted to lead His people to glory as their Savior and High Priest.

Since the children share flesh and blood, Jesus Himself took on the same human nature so that through His own death He might destroy the devil, who had the power of death. By dying, Jesus broke the devil’s power over His people, the power that had held them captive through the fear of death.

“He Himself likewise shared in the same” The children share flesh and blood, and the Son truly took on their mortal human condition. This enabled Him to die. Through His own death He broke the devil’s power over the people the devil had held through death and its fear.

NLT

Hebrews 2:17 Therefore, it was necessary for him to be made in every respect like us, his brothers and sisters, so that he could be our merciful and faithful High Priest before God. Then he could offer a sacrifice that would take away the sins of the people. (NLT)

Main Idea

Therefore Jesus had to be made like His brethren in every way, so that He might be “a merciful and faithful High Priest” in matters relating to God and make propitiation for the sins of the people. Through His sacrifice He makes atonement for their sins, and this first use of the title High Priest in Hebrews joins His sharing of our humanity with that atoning work.

Verse Explanation

Therefore Jesus had to be made like His brethren in every way, so that He might be “a merciful and faithful High Priest” in matters relating to God and make propitiation for the sins of the people. Through His sacrifice He makes atonement for their sins, and this first use of the title High Priest in Hebrews joins His sharing of our humanity with that atoning work.

“in all things He had to be made like His brethren” Jesus shared the human condition of the people He represents. His mercy faces their need, and His faithfulness characterizes His service before God.

“to make propitiation for the sins of the people” He offers atonement that deals with their sins before God. This is the first explicit use of High Priest for Jesus in Hebrews, and it joins His true humanity to the saving work He performs for His people.
