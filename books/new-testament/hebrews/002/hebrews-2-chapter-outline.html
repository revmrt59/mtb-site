Hebrews 2 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Hebrews 2 turns the Son’s supremacy into a warning not to drift from so great a salvation, then explains why the Son was made a little lower than the angels. By sharing flesh and blood and tasting death for everyone, Jesus brings many sons to glory, breaks the devil’s power, and becomes a merciful and faithful High Priest who helps those who are tempted.

Chapter Sections

1. Hebrews 2:1–4 — Do Not Neglect So Great a Salvation

Because the Son is greater than the angels, “we must give the more earnest heed to the things we have heard, lest we drift away” (Hebrews 2:1). If the word spoken through angels brought a just reward for disobedience, there is no escape for those who neglect the salvation first spoken by the Lord, confirmed by those who heard Him, and attested by God with signs, wonders, miracles, and gifts of the Holy Spirit.

2. Hebrews 2:5–9 — We See Jesus Crowned with Glory and Honor

God has not subjected the world to come to angels, and Psalm 8 speaks of man made a little lower than the angels and crowned to rule over God’s works. We do not yet see all things put under him, but “we see Jesus” (Hebrews 2:9), crowned with glory and honor because He suffered death, so that by the grace of God He might taste death for everyone.

3. Hebrews 2:10–13 — The Captain of Salvation and His Brethren

In bringing many sons to glory, it was fitting for God “to make the captain of their salvation perfect through sufferings” (Hebrews 2:10). He who sanctifies and those who are being sanctified are all of one family, and words from Psalm 22 and Isaiah 8 show that He is not ashamed to call them brethren.

4. Hebrews 2:14–16 — Through Death He Destroys the Devil’s Power

Since the children share flesh and blood, Jesus shared the same so that through death He might destroy him who had the power of death and release those held in bondage all their lives by fear of death. He gives aid not to angels but to “the seed of Abraham” (Hebrews 2:16).

5. Hebrews 2:17–18 — A Merciful and Faithful High Priest

Jesus had to be made like His brethren in all things so that He might be “a merciful and faithful High Priest” (Hebrews 2:17) and make propitiation for the sins of the people, dealing with their sins before God. Because He Himself suffered when He was tempted, He is able to aid those who are tempted.
