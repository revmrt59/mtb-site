Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 2
Main Idea:

Because the Son is greater than the angels, His salvation must not be neglected; this Son shared our flesh and blood, tasted death for everyone, broke the devil’s power, and became a merciful and faithful High Priest who helps those who are tempted.

Setting:

Hebrews 2 completes the book’s first movement (1:1–2:18). Its opening “Therefore” draws the first warning straight out of chapter 1, and its closing verses introduce Jesus as High Priest, the theme Hebrews develops from 4:14 onward.

Chapter Outline:

  • Hebrews 2:1–4 — Do Not Neglect So Great a Salvation

  • Hebrews 2:5–9 — We See Jesus Crowned with Glory and Honor

  • Hebrews 2:10–13 — The Captain of Salvation and His Brethren

  • Hebrews 2:14–16 — Through Death He Destroys the Devil’s Power

  • Hebrews 2:17–18 — A Merciful and Faithful High Priest

Key Verse:

“But we see Jesus, who was made a little lower than the angels, for the suffering of death crowned with glory and honor, that He, by the grace of God, might taste death for everyone.” (Hebrews 2:9)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The chapter opens with a warning that grows out of everything chapter 1 said about the Son. “We must give the more earnest heed to the things we have heard, lest we drift away” (2:1). The danger Hebrews names is a slow drift through inattention. If the law spoken through angels carried real penalties, “how shall we escape if we neglect so great a salvation” (2:3)? The Lord Himself announced it, eyewitnesses confirmed it, and God bore witness with signs, wonders, miracles, and gifts of the Holy Spirit.

Then Hebrews explains why the Son, who is above the angels, was made lower than them for a while. Psalm 8 speaks of humanity crowned with glory and set over God’s works, yet “now we do not yet see all things put under him” (2:8). What we do see is Jesus. He shared our lowliness, suffered death, and by the grace of God tasted death for everyone, and He is now crowned with glory and honor (2:9). Through suffering God made Him “perfect,” bringing Him to the goal of His calling as the captain who leads “many sons to glory” (2:10). He is not ashamed to call them brethren.

His death accomplished what no one else could. By sharing flesh and blood, He destroyed the devil’s power over death and set free “those who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage” (2:15). Being made like His brethren in every way also made Him “a merciful and faithful High Priest,” who deals with sin before God and helps those who are tempted, because He Himself suffered when tempted (2:17–18).

Chapter Sections

Hebrews 2:1–4 — Do Not Neglect So Great a Salvation

Because the Son is greater than the angels, believers must pay close attention to what they have heard, or they will drift away. If disobedience to the law brought a just penalty, there is no escape for those who neglect the salvation the Lord announced and God confirmed with signs and gifts of the Spirit.

Hebrews 2:5–9 — We See Jesus Crowned with Glory and Honor

Psalm 8 describes humanity’s calling to honor and rule over God’s works, a calling we do not yet see fulfilled. We do see Jesus, made for a little while lower than the angels, who by God’s grace tasted death for everyone and is now crowned with glory and honor.

Hebrews 2:10–13 — The Captain of Salvation and His Brethren

God brought Jesus, the leader of our salvation, to the goal of His calling through suffering, as He brings many sons and daughters to glory. The One who makes people holy and those He makes holy belong to one family, and Jesus gladly calls them His brothers and sisters.

Hebrews 2:14–16 — Through Death He Destroys the Devil’s Power

Jesus took on flesh and blood so that through His death He could break the devil’s power and free those enslaved all their lives by the fear of death. His help is for the people of God’s promise to Abraham, not for angels.

Hebrews 2:17–18 — A Merciful and Faithful High Priest

Jesus was made like His brothers and sisters in every way so that He could be a merciful and faithful High Priest who makes atonement for their sins. Because He suffered when tempted, He is able to help those who are being tempted.

Why This Chapter Matters

Hebrews 2 shows that turning away from God’s salvation can begin quietly. People who have heard the gospel can slowly stop paying attention and drift (2:1). Hebrews warns that those who neglect “so great a salvation” will not escape (2:3). The greatness of the One who has spoken makes careful listening a matter of life and death.

The chapter also shows how close the exalted Son has come to His people. He did not save them from a distance. He took their flesh and blood, faced death, and suffered under temptation. Because of that, believers who fear death or struggle with temptation have a High Priest who understands and is able to help (2:14–18).

Key Themes to Watch For

Do not drift. The greatness of the salvation spoken by the Lord demands careful attention (2:1–4).

Humanity’s calling fulfilled in Jesus. Psalm 8 finds its fulfillment in Jesus, crowned after tasting death for everyone (2:5–9).

The Son shares our humanity. Jesus took flesh and blood and calls His people brethren (2:11–14, 17).

Freedom from the fear of death. Jesus’ death broke the devil’s power and released captives (2:14–15).

A merciful and faithful High Priest. Jesus atones for sin and helps the tempted (2:17–18).

Key Words & Concepts

The word spoken through angels (2:2). The law given at Sinai, which Scripture associates with angels (Acts 7:53; Galatians 3:19). Hebrews argues from the lesser to the greater: if that word carried real penalties, the salvation spoken by the Lord carries more.

Taste death (2:9). To experience death fully. Jesus truly died, and He did so on behalf of each person.

Captain of their salvation (2:10). The leader who goes ahead and brings His people to glory.

Perfect through sufferings (2:10). Jesus was always without sin (4:15). “Perfect” here means reaching the intended goal: through suffering He completed the path that made Him the Savior and High Priest of His people.

The seed of Abraham (2:16). The people of God’s promise to Abraham. Elsewhere Scripture shows that all who belong to Christ by faith share in that promise (Galatians 3:29).

Propitiation (2:17). Dealing with the people’s sins before God through an atoning sacrifice. Hebrews will show that Jesus made that sacrifice by offering Himself.
