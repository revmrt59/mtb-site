Hebrews 3:1
Hebrews 3:1
NKJV

Hebrews 3:1 Therefore, holy brethren, partakers of the heavenly calling, consider the Apostle and High Priest of our confession, Christ Jesus,(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 3:1 And so, dear brothers and sisters who belong to God and are partners with those called to heaven, think carefully about this Jesus whom we declare to be God’s messenger and High Priest. (NLT)

Main Idea

Addressing believers as “holy brethren, partakers of the heavenly calling,” the writer calls them to consider Christ Jesus, “the Apostle and High Priest of our confession.” Jesus is the One God sent to speak to us and the One who represents us before God, so He deserves their full attention.

Verse Explanation

Addressing believers as “holy brethren, partakers of the heavenly calling,” the writer calls them to consider Christ Jesus, “the Apostle and High Priest of our confession.” Jesus is the One God sent to speak to us and the One who represents us before God, so He deserves their full attention.

“consider the Apostle and High Priest of our confession” Hebrews calls those set apart for God, who share a calling from heaven, to give Jesus their close attention. As Apostle He is the One God sent to bring His word. As High Priest He represents us before God. The two titles gather what the preceding chapters say about God speaking to us and Jesus helping His people.

“faithful to Him who appointed Him” Jesus carried out what God entrusted to Him. Moses also served faithfully in God’s house, a description that comes from Numbers 12:7. The comparison begins with the faithfulness they share before showing the different places they hold among God’s people.

“exhort one another daily” All members of the congregation have a part in helping one another hear God while His Today remains open. Sin deceives by making distrust and disobedience appear reasonable, and the heart grows hard as it yields. Daily encouragement gives the truth an answering voice before that resistance becomes settled.

The charge falls on that generation as a whole, the very people who had experienced God’s deliverance, though Scripture names Joshua and Caleb as the faithful exceptions (Numbers 14:29–30).

“who, having heard, rebelled” The people who rebelled were the generation Moses had led out of Egypt. They had received God’s word and witnessed His deliverance. Hebrews speaks of the generation as a whole, while Numbers 14:29–30 names Joshua and Caleb as exceptions.
