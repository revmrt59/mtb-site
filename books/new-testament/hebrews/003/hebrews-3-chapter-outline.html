Hebrews 3 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Hebrews 3 calls the brethren to consider Jesus, who is faithful as a Son over God’s house and worthy of more glory than Moses. Psalm 95 then becomes the Holy Spirit’s present warning: the wilderness generation could not enter God’s rest because of unbelief, so believers must hear His voice “Today” (Hebrews 3:7) and exhort one another daily so that no one is hardened.

Chapter Sections

1. Hebrews 3:1–6 — Jesus, Faithful as a Son over God’s House

The holy brethren are to consider Christ Jesus, the Apostle and High Priest of their confession, who was faithful like Moses yet is worthy of more glory, as the builder of a house has more honor than the house. Moses was faithful in God’s house as a servant, but Christ is faithful “as a Son over His own house, whose house we are if we hold fast the confidence and the rejoicing of the hope firm to the end” (Hebrews 3:6).

2. Hebrews 3:7–11 — The Holy Spirit Says, “Today”

Hebrews introduces Psalm 95 as the voice of the Holy Spirit: “Today, if you will hear His voice, Do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion” (Hebrews 3:7–8). The psalm recalls the fathers who tested God and saw His works for forty years in the wilderness, until He swore in His wrath that they would not enter His rest.

3. Hebrews 3:12–15 — Exhort One Another Daily

The brethren must beware lest any of them have “an evil heart of unbelief in departing from the living God” (Hebrews 3:12), and they are to exhort one another daily so that no one is hardened through the deceitfulness of sin. They have become partakers of Christ if they hold the beginning of their confidence steadfast to the end, while the psalm’s call to hear and not harden their hearts is still sounding.

4. Hebrews 3:16–19 — They Could Not Enter Because of Unbelief

Three questions identify the wilderness generation that heard God and rebelled: the people who came out of Egypt under Moses, whose corpses fell in the wilderness, and to whom God swore they would not enter His rest because they did not obey. Hebrews speaks of that generation as a whole, though Numbers 14:29–30 names Caleb and Joshua as exceptions, and it concludes that their disobedience showed unbelief: “So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief” (Hebrews 3:19).
