Chapter Study

Hebrews 4

Verse-by-Verse
Hebrews 4:1
Hebrews 4:1
NKJV

Hebrews 4:1 Therefore, since a promise remains of entering His rest, let us fear lest any of you seem to have come short of it.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 4:1 God’s promise of entering his rest still stands, so we ought to tremble with fear that some of you might fail to experience it. (NLT)

Main Idea

Since God’s promise of entering His rest still stands, the writer urges, “let us fear lest any of you seem to have come short of it.” The open promise calls for a serious and watchful response, not careless confidence.

Verse Explanation

Since God’s promise of entering His rest still stands, the writer urges, “let us fear lest any of you seem to have come short of it.” The open promise calls for a serious and watchful response, not careless confidence.

“a promise remains of entering His rest” The warning of chapter 3 has not closed the promise. The writer includes himself in taking seriously the danger that any member of the congregation might fall short. Fear here is the sober concern that hears God’s promise and is unwilling to let unbelief keep anyone outside.

Hebrews 4:2
Hebrews 4:2
NKJV

Hebrews 4:2 For indeed the gospel was preached to us as well as to them; but the word which they heard did not profit them, not being mixed with faith in those who heard it.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 4:2 For this good news — that God has prepared this rest — has been announced to us just as it was to them. But it did them no good because they didn’t share the faith of those who listened to God. (NLT)

Main Idea

Israel heard good news in God’s promise of rest in the land, and these hearers have heard good news in Christ, but the word Israel heard “did not profit them, not being mixed with faith in those who heard it.” In both settings the good news God spoke called for faith, and hearing it without faith brings no benefit.

Verse Explanation

Israel heard good news in God’s promise of rest in the land, and these hearers have heard good news in Christ, but the word Israel heard “did not profit them, not being mixed with faith in those who heard it.” In both settings the good news God spoke called for faith, and hearing it without faith brings no benefit.

“not being mixed with faith in those who heard it” Israel heard the good news of God’s promise concerning the land, and this congregation heard the salvation announced by the Son. Each generation was responsible to trust what God said to it. The word brought the wilderness generation no benefit because they refused to believe the God who had spoken.

Main Idea

“For we who have believed do enter that rest,” Hebrews declares, recalling God’s oath that the unbelieving generation would not enter it. God’s own works were finished from the foundation of the world, so the rest He offers has stood ready since creation.

Verse Explanation

“For we who have believed do enter that rest,” Hebrews declares, recalling God’s oath that the unbelieving generation would not enter it. God’s own works were finished from the foundation of the world, so the rest He offers has stood ready since creation.

“we who have believed do enter that rest” Hebrews speaks of believers entering now. God’s oath excludes the generation that refused Him, while the rest itself was already there because His own works had been finished from creation. The promise therefore reaches further back than Israel’s journey and further forward than the failure at its border.

Scripture speaks of the seventh day this way: “And God rested on the seventh day from all His works.” The rest God offers His people is His own rest, the rest He entered when His creating work was complete.

“God rested on the seventh day from all His works” Genesis 2:2–3 explains the rest God calls His own. God ceased because His work of creating was complete. Hebrews brings that rest into the discussion to show that entry into Canaan could never exhaust what God was promising.

Verse Explanation

In Psalm 95 God says of the rebellious generation, “They shall not enter My rest.” Setting this beside Genesis shows that the rest Israel missed was God’s own rest, not only a place to live.

“They shall not enter My rest” Psalm 95 repeats God’s oath concerning the rebels. Placing My rest beside Genesis shows whose rest is at issue. The generation’s exclusion was real, but the oath gave no reason to think God’s rest had ceased to be available to those who believe.

Their failure explains why the issue remains urgent for another generation, and the later summons in David confirms that the invitation is still open.

NLT

Hebrews 4:8 Now if Joshua had succeeded in giving them this rest, God would not have spoken about another day of rest still to come. (NLT)

Main Idea

If Joshua had given Israel the rest God promised when he led them into Canaan, God would not afterward have spoken of another day. Israel’s settlement in the land did not exhaust the promise of rest.

Verse Explanation

If Joshua had given Israel the rest God promised when he led them into Canaan, God would not afterward have spoken of another day. Israel’s settlement in the land did not exhaust the promise of rest.

“if Joshua had given them rest” Joshua did lead Israel into the land. The later word of God about another day shows that the land was an actual gift while God’s rest was a larger promise. Hebrews builds the distinction on the sequence of events: the settlement came first, and David’s summons came afterward.

Hebrews 4:9–11 — A Rest Remains for the People of God

Study Notes

None.

Hebrews 4:9
Hebrews 4:9
NKJV

Hebrews 4:9 There remains therefore a rest for the people of God.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 4:9 So there is a special rest still waiting for the people of God. (NLT)

Main Idea

“There remains therefore a rest for the people of God.” The writer draws his conclusion from Scripture: God’s rest is still available to His people today.

Verse Explanation

“There remains therefore a rest for the people of God.” The writer draws his conclusion from Scripture: God’s rest is still available to His people today.

“a rest for the people of God” The conclusion holds together God’s completed creation, Israel’s missed opportunity, and the later invitation. The rest is still there for God’s people. Faith begins to share in it now and awaits the full inheritance God has promised.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated rest here is sabbatismos, which carries the sense of Sabbath rest. It fits the link Hebrews has just made to God resting on the seventh day.

Hebrews 4:10

NLT

Hebrews 4:10 For all who have entered into God’s rest have rested from their labors, just as God did after creating the world. (NLT)

Main Idea

The one who has entered God’s rest “has himself also ceased from his works as God did from His.” Believers enter this rest by relying on God’s finished work in Christ rather than on their own efforts, while the promise also looks ahead to the full inheritance.

Verse Explanation

The one who has entered God’s rest “has himself also ceased from his works as God did from His.” Believers enter this rest by relying on God’s finished work in Christ rather than on their own efforts, while the promise also looks ahead to the full inheritance.

“ceased from his works as God did from His” The person who enters is the believer described in verse 3. God’s completed work supplies the pattern: rest follows a work that has reached its end. For believers, that rest is experienced now by relying on Christ’s finished work. His sacrifice cleanses the conscience and grants access to God, so we can come to Him on the strength of what Jesus has done (Hebrews 9:14 and 10:19–22).

“Let us therefore be diligent to enter that rest,” the writer urges, so that no one falls by following the same example of disobedience Israel set. Diligence here means persevering faith that keeps heeding God, the opposite of the wilderness generation’s unbelief.

“be diligent to enter that rest” Diligence is the perseverance of faith that keeps heeding God. Israel’s example shows why that earnestness is necessary: hearing without trust led to disobedience and exclusion. The call includes the whole congregation and rests on God’s open promise.

Hebrews 4:12–13 — The Living Word and the All-Seeing God

Study Notes

None.

Hebrews 4:12
Hebrews 4:12
NKJV

Hebrews 4:12 For the word of God is living and powerful, and sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing even to the division of soul and spirit, and of joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 4:12 For the word of God is alive and powerful. It is sharper than the sharpest two-edged sword, cutting between soul and spirit, between joint and marrow. It exposes our innermost thoughts and desires. (NLT)

Main Idea

“For the word of God is living and powerful,” sharper than any two-edged sword, “piercing even to the division of soul and spirit, and of joints and marrow,” and judging “the thoughts and intents of the heart.” God’s word reaches even to where soul and spirit are divided, exposing whether His hearers are truly trusting Him or hardening their hearts as Israel did.

Verse Explanation

“For the word of God is living and powerful,” sharper than any two-edged sword, “piercing even to the division of soul and spirit, and of joints and marrow,” and judging “the thoughts and intents of the heart.” God’s word reaches even to where soul and spirit are divided, exposing whether His hearers are truly trusting Him or hardening their hearts as Israel did.

“living and powerful” God’s word is active as it brings the hearers’ response into the open. Sharper than a sword with two edges, it reaches past outward behavior to the thoughts and intentions underneath.

“the division of soul and spirit” Together with joints and marrow, this pair shows how deeply God’s word penetrates. Scripture speaks of body, soul, and spirit (1 Thessalonians 5:23). The soul includes mind, will, and emotions, and the spirit is the immaterial, enduring part of a person brought to spiritual life in the new birth. God’s word reaches even where soul and spirit are divided and exposes whether we are listening to Him in faith.

No creature is hidden from God’s sight, and “all things are naked and open to the eyes of Him to whom we must give account.” The searching word leads directly to the God before whom every person stands fully exposed and accountable.

He sees both our actions and the intentions His word exposes. The awareness that we answer to Him gives weight to the invitation to draw near through Jesus.
