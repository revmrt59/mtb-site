Hebrews 4 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Hebrews 4 completes the Psalm 95 argument by showing that God’s rest remains open to those who believe, since Joshua did not exhaust the promise and God still says “Today” (Hebrews 4:7). The searching word of God makes that call urgent, and the chapter turns at Hebrews 4:14 to the great High Priest through whom believers hold fast and come boldly to the throne of grace.

Chapter Sections

1. Hebrews 4:1–8 — The Promise of Rest Still Stands

Since a promise of entering God’s rest remains, the hearers must fear lest any of them come short of it, for the word Israel heard “did not profit them, not being mixed with faith in those who heard it” (Hebrews 4:2). Drawing together God’s seventh-day rest, the wilderness oath, and the later “Today” (Hebrews 4:7) spoken in David, Hebrews concludes that if Joshua had given them rest, God would not afterward have spoken of another day.

2. Hebrews 4:9–11 — A Rest Remains for the People of God

“There remains therefore a rest for the people of God” (Hebrews 4:9), and whoever enters God’s rest ceases from his works as God did from His. The hearers are to be diligent to enter that rest so that no one falls by following the same example of disobedience.

3. Hebrews 4:12–13 — The Living Word and the All-Seeing God

The word of God is living and powerful, sharper than any two-edged sword, and “a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart” (Hebrews 4:12). No creature is hidden from God’s sight, and all things lie naked and open before Him to whom we must give account.

4. Hebrews 4:14–16 — Come Boldly to the Throne of Grace

Since believers have a great High Priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, they are to hold fast their confession, for He sympathizes with their weaknesses, having been tempted in all points as they are, “yet without sin” (Hebrews 4:15). They may therefore come boldly to the throne of grace to obtain mercy and find grace to help in time of need.
