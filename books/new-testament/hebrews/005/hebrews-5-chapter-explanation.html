Chapter Study

Hebrews 5

Verse-by-Verse
Hebrews 5:1
Hebrews 5:1
NKJV

Hebrews 5:1 For every high priest taken from among men is appointed for men in things pertaining to God, that he may offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 5:1 Every high priest is a man chosen to represent other people in their dealings with God. He presents their gifts to God and offers sacrifices for their sins. (NLT)

Main Idea

Every high priest is taken from among men and appointed to act for them in matters relating to God, offering both gifts and sacrifices for sins. A high priest stands between God and the people and represents them before Him.

Verse Explanation

Every high priest is taken from among men and appointed to act for them in matters relating to God, offering both gifts and sacrifices for sins. A high priest stands between God and the people and represents them before Him.

“appointed for men in things pertaining to God” The high priest is chosen from the people and serves on their behalf before God. Gifts and sacrifices are part of that appointment because the people’s sins have to be dealt with. This describes the office before Hebrews explains how Jesus fills it.

Study Notes

None.

Hebrews 5:2
Hebrews 5:2
NKJV

Hebrews 5:2 He can have compassion on those who are ignorant and going astray, since he himself is also subject to weakness.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 5:2 And he is able to deal gently with ignorant and wayward people because he himself is subject to the same weaknesses. (NLT)

Main Idea

Because the high priest is himself subject to weakness, he “can have compassion on those who are ignorant and going astray.” His own frailty helps him deal gently with people who stumble.

Verse Explanation

Because the high priest is himself subject to weakness, he “can have compassion on those who are ignorant and going astray.” His own frailty helps him deal gently with people who stumble.

“He can have compassion” A priest’s own weakness gives him reason to deal gently with people who lack understanding or have wandered. Compassion here shapes the way he treats them as he represents them before God. Hebrews has already shown that Jesus understands temptation, and the next verse shows where the two priesthoods differ.

Hebrews 5:3
Hebrews 5:3
NKJV

Hebrews 5:3 Because of this he is required as for the people, so also for himself, to offer sacrifices for sins.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 5:3 That is why he must offer sacrifices for his own sins as well as theirs. (NLT)

Main Idea

Because of that same weakness, the high priest must offer sacrifices for his own sins as well as for the sins of the people. Aaron’s priesthood could not escape the priest’s own need for atonement.

Verse Explanation

Because of that same weakness, the high priest must offer sacrifices for his own sins as well as for the sins of the people. Aaron’s priesthood could not escape the priest’s own need for atonement.

“so also for himself” The earlier high priest had sins of his own for which he had to offer sacrifice. On the Day of Atonement he brought an offering for himself and his household as well as the offering for the people (Leviticus 16:6, 15). This requirement helps explain why Jesus’ sinlessness matters to His priesthood.

Hebrews 5:4
Hebrews 5:4
NKJV

Hebrews 5:4 And no man takes this honor to himself, but he who is called by God, just as Aaron was.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 5:4 And no one can become a high priest simply because he wants such an honor. He must be called by God for this work, just as Aaron was. (NLT)

Main Idea

No one takes the honor of the high priesthood for himself; he must be called by God, “just as Aaron was.” The office is given by God’s appointment, not seized by human ambition.

Verse Explanation

No one takes the honor of the high priesthood for himself; he must be called by God, “just as Aaron was.” The office is given by God’s appointment, not seized by human ambition.

“he who is called by God” Priesthood is an honor God gives. Aaron received the office from God’s command in Exodus 28:1. The calling establishes the principle Hebrews applies to Jesus in the next two verses.

Hebrews 5:5–6 — God Appointed Christ as High Priest

Study Notes

None.

Hebrews 5:5
Hebrews 5:5
NKJV

Hebrews 5:5 So also Christ did not glorify Himself to become High Priest, but it was He who said to Him: "You are My Son, Today I have begotten You."(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 5:5 That is why Christ did not honor himself by assuming he could become High Priest. No, he was chosen by God, who said to him, “You are my Son.Today I have become your Father. ” (NLT)

Main Idea

In the same way Christ did not glorify Himself to become High Priest; He was appointed by God, who said to Him, “You are My Son, Today I have begotten You.” The God who declared Jesus to be His royal Son is the One who gave Him the honor of the priesthood.

Verse Explanation

In the same way Christ did not glorify Himself to become High Priest; He was appointed by God, who said to Him, “You are My Son, Today I have begotten You.” The God who declared Jesus to be His royal Son is the One who gave Him the honor of the priesthood.

“Christ did not glorify Himself” Jesus received the honor of priesthood from the Father. The decree from Psalm 2:7 first identifies Him as God’s royal Son. The same God who speaks that decree also makes the priestly appointment in the next quotation.

Hebrews 5:6
Hebrews 5:6
NKJV

Hebrews 5:6 As He also says in another place: "You are a priest forever According to the order of Melchizedek";(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 5:6 And in another passage God said to him, “You are a priest forever in the order of Melchizedek.” (NLT)

Main Idea

In another psalm God declares, “You are a priest forever According to the order of Melchizedek.” Psalm 110 joins God’s appointment to a priesthood that will never end, and Hebrews will later explain its connection to Melchizedek.

Verse Explanation

In another psalm God declares, “You are a priest forever According to the order of Melchizedek.” Psalm 110 joins God’s appointment to a priesthood that will never end, and Hebrews will later explain its connection to Melchizedek.

“You are a priest forever” Psalm 110:4 adds the priestly declaration that Psalm 2 did not make. The Son has a permanent office according to the pattern of Melchizedek. The promise comes by God’s own word, and chapter 7 will explain why that pattern brings a priesthood greater than Aaron’s.

Hebrews 5:7–10 — Perfected Through Suffering

Study Notes

None.

Hebrews 5:7
Hebrews 5:7
NKJV

Hebrews 5:7 who, in the days of His flesh, when He had offered up prayers and supplications, with vehement cries and tears to Him who was able to save Him from death, and was heard because of His godly fear,(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 5:7 While Jesus was here on earth, he offered prayers and pleadings, with a loud cry and tears, to the one who could rescue him from death. And God heard his prayers because of his deep reverence for God. (NLT)

Main Idea

In the days of His flesh, Jesus offered up prayers and supplications “with vehement cries and tears to Him who was able to save Him from death,” and He was heard because of His godly fear. The Father answered His reverent prayer by bringing Him through death and raising Him from it.

Verse Explanation

In the days of His flesh, Jesus offered up prayers and supplications “with vehement cries and tears to Him who was able to save Him from death,” and He was heard because of His godly fear. The Father answered His reverent prayer by bringing Him through death and raising Him from it.

“with vehement cries and tears” During His earthly life Jesus prayed with real anguish to the Father, who could save Him from death. He was heard because of His reverent submission. The Father carried Him through suffering and answered by bringing Him out of death, as Hebrews 13:20 later says. His tears and His trust belonged to the same obedience.

Study Notes

The expression rendered “save Him from death” can describe rescue out of death. The verse does not name the occasion of Jesus’ prayers. The language suits the anguish reported before the cross, while the statement that His prayer was heard reaches to the Father’s answer.

Hebrews 5:8
Hebrews 5:8
NKJV

Hebrews 5:8 though He was a Son, yet He learned obedience by the things which He suffered.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 5:8 Even though Jesus was God’s Son, he learned obedience from the things he suffered. (NLT)

Main Idea

Though Jesus was the Son, “yet He learned obedience by the things which He suffered.” The sinless Son came to know obedience by living it out at real cost in the midst of suffering.

Verse Explanation

Though Jesus was the Son, “yet He learned obedience by the things which He suffered.” The sinless Son came to know obedience by living it out at real cost in the midst of suffering.

“He learned obedience by the things which He suffered” Sonship brings the cost of His obedience into relief. Jesus knew what obedience meant by living it through suffering. The sinless Son carried out His Father’s will when doing so involved real pain.

Hebrews 5:9
Hebrews 5:9
NKJV

Hebrews 5:9 And having been perfected, He became the author of eternal salvation to all who obey Him,(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 5:9 In this way, God qualified him as a perfect High Priest, and he became the source of eternal salvation for all those who obey him. (NLT)

Main Idea

Having been perfected, Jesus became “the author of eternal salvation to all who obey Him.” Through His obedient suffering He completed His saving calling, and He is now the source of lasting salvation for those who respond to Him in obedience.

Verse Explanation

Having been perfected, Jesus became “the author of eternal salvation to all who obey Him.” Through His obedient suffering He completed His saving calling, and He is now the source of lasting salvation for those who respond to Him in obedience.

“the author of eternal salvation” Through obedient suffering Jesus reached the intended end of His saving work. As the One who completed it, He is the source of salvation that lasts forever. The people who receive it are described as obeying Him, a response of trust to the Savior who has done the saving.

Hebrews 5:10

Hebrews 5:10 And God designated him to be a High Priest in the order of Melchizedek. (NLT)

Main Idea

God has designated Jesus as High Priest “according to the order of Melchizedek.” The verse closes the comparison with Aaron by repeating the priesthood God promised in Psalm 110.

Verse Explanation

God has designated Jesus as High Priest “according to the order of Melchizedek.” The verse closes the comparison with Aaron by repeating the priesthood God promised in Psalm 110.

“called by God as High Priest” God identifies the Son who was perfected through suffering as the High Priest promised in the order of Melchizedek. This brings the discussion back to Psalm 110:4 and sets up the explanation that resumes in chapter 7.

Hebrews 5:11–14 — Dull of Hearing and in Need of Solid Food

Study Notes

None.

Hebrews 5:11
Hebrews 5:11
NKJV

Hebrews 5:11 of whom we have much to say, and hard to explain, since you have become dull of hearing.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 5:11 There is much more we would like to say about this, but it is difficult to explain, especially since you are spiritually dull and don’t seem to listen. (NLT)

Main Idea

The writer has much to say about Melchizedek, but it is hard to explain “since you have become dull of hearing.” The difficulty lies less in the subject than in hearers who have grown slow to listen.

Verse Explanation

The writer has much to say about Melchizedek, but it is hard to explain “since you have become dull of hearing.” The difficulty lies less in the subject than in hearers who have grown slow to listen.

“you have become dull of hearing” The writer has more to say about the Melchizedek priesthood, but the congregation’s listening has grown sluggish. The problem developed over time. Before explaining the priesthood further, Hebrews addresses the kind of hearing that will let its teaching take hold.

Hebrews 5:12

NLT

Hebrews 5:12 You have been believers so long now that you ought to be teaching others. Instead, you need someone to teach you again the basic things about God’s word. You are like babies who need milk and cannot eat solid food. (NLT)

Main Idea

Though the hearers ought to be teachers by now, they need someone to teach them again “the first principles of the oracles of God,” and they have come to need milk rather than solid food. Their growth has stalled so badly that they need the basics retaught.

Verse Explanation

Though the hearers ought to be teachers by now, they need someone to teach them again “the first principles of the oracles of God,” and they have come to need milk rather than solid food. Their growth has stalled so badly that they need the basics retaught.

“by this time you ought to be teachers” The hearers have had enough time to pass God’s teaching on to others. Instead, they need someone to teach them the first lessons of God’s word again. Milk and solid food picture the difference between needing to relearn those basics and being ready to receive fuller instruction.

“unskilled in the word of righteousness” A person who lives on milk is still an infant in the comparison. They lack experience in understanding and using the teaching that leads them in what is right. The verse explains why they have trouble taking in what the writer wants to teach.

Hebrews 5:14
NKJV

Hebrews 5:14 But solid food belongs to those who are of full age, that is, those who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 5:14 Solid food is for those who are mature, who through training have the skill to recognize the difference between right and wrong. (NLT)

Main Idea

Solid food belongs to the mature, to “those who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil.” Maturity grows as believers put God’s word into practice and train their judgment through use.

Verse Explanation

Solid food belongs to the mature, to “those who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil.” Maturity grows as believers put God’s word into practice and train their judgment through use.

“by reason of use” Mature people become able to receive solid food because they have practiced what they learned. Practice trains their judgment to recognize both good and evil. This growth is the maturity the writer will urge his hearers to pursue in Hebrews 6:1.
