Hebrews 5 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Hebrews 5 shows that Jesus meets the qualifications of a high priest, since God Himself appointed Him and He learned obedience through suffering, becoming the author of eternal salvation. The writer then pauses before explaining Melchizedek and rebukes hearers who have become dull of hearing and need the first principles taught again.

Chapter Sections

1. Hebrews 5:1–4 — Every High Priest Is Taken from Among Men

Every high priest is taken from among men and appointed to offer gifts and sacrifices for sins, and because he himself is subject to weakness, he can have compassion on the ignorant and those going astray, while for the same reason he must offer sacrifices for his own sins as well as for the people. No one takes this honor for himself; the high priest must be called by God, just as Aaron was.

2. Hebrews 5:5–6 — God Appointed Christ as High Priest

Christ did not glorify Himself to become High Priest; He was appointed by the One who said to Him, “You are My Son, Today I have begotten You” (Hebrews 5:5). In another place God declares, “You are a priest forever According to the order of Melchizedek” (Hebrews 5:6).

3. Hebrews 5:7–10 — Perfected Through Suffering

In the days of His flesh, Jesus offered up prayers and supplications “with vehement cries and tears to Him who was able to save Him from death” (Hebrews 5:7), and He was heard because of His godly fear. Though He was a Son, He learned obedience by the things He suffered, and having been perfected He became the author of eternal salvation to all who obey Him, called by God as High Priest according to the order of Melchizedek.

4. Hebrews 5:11–14 — Dull of Hearing and in Need of Solid Food

The writer has much to say about Melchizedek that is hard to explain, because the hearers “have become dull of hearing” (Hebrews 5:11). Though they ought to be teachers by now, they need the first principles again, like infants who need milk, while solid food belongs to the mature, whose senses are trained by practice to discern both good and evil.
