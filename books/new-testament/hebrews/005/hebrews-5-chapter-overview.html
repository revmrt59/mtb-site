Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 5
Main Idea:

God Himself appointed Jesus as High Priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek, and through His prayers, suffering, and obedience He became the author of eternal salvation, yet the hearers have grown dull of hearing and need to grow up.

Setting:

Hebrews 5 continues the teaching on Jesus’ priesthood that began at 4:14, measuring Him against the qualifications of Israel’s high priests descended from Aaron. At 5:11 the writer pauses for an appeal that runs through chapter 6 before he returns to Melchizedek in chapter 7.

Chapter Outline:

  • Hebrews 5:1–4 — Every High Priest Is Taken from Among Men

  • Hebrews 5:5–6 — God Appointed Christ as High Priest

  • Hebrews 5:7–10 — Perfected Through Suffering

  • Hebrews 5:11–14 — Dull of Hearing and in Need of Solid Food

Key Verse:

“though He was a Son, yet He learned obedience by the things which He suffered. And having been perfected, He became the author of eternal salvation to all who obey Him” (Hebrews 5:8–9)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Hebrews begins by describing every high priest from Aaron’s line. He was taken from among the people to represent them before God, offering gifts and sacrifices for sins. His own weakness helped him deal gently with those who went astray, though it also meant he had to offer sacrifices for his own sins. And no one could take that honor for himself; God had to call him, “just as Aaron was” (5:4).

Jesus meets both qualifications, in a greater way. He did not appoint Himself. The God who said, “You are My Son,” also said, “You are a priest forever According to the order of Melchizedek” (5:5–6). Nor is His sympathy borrowed from sin of His own, for He had none. “In the days of His flesh” He prayed “with vehement cries and tears to Him who was able to save Him from death” (5:7). The Father heard Him and brought Him through death and out of it. “Though He was a Son, yet He learned obedience by the things which He suffered” (5:8). He lived out obedience at real cost, and through it He reached the goal of His calling, becoming “the author of eternal salvation to all who obey Him” (5:9).

Then the writer stops. He has much to say about Melchizedek, but his hearers “have become dull of hearing” (5:11). By now they should be teaching others, yet they need the first principles again, like infants who still need milk. Solid food belongs to the mature, whose senses are trained by practice to tell good from evil (5:14).

Chapter Sections

Hebrews 5:1–4 — Every High Priest Is Taken from Among Men

Every high priest descended from Aaron was taken from among the people to represent them before God and offer sacrifices for sins. His own weakness helped him deal gently with the wayward but also required sacrifices for his own sins, and no one could take the office unless God called him.

Hebrews 5:5–6 — God Appointed Christ as High Priest

Christ did not take the honor of the high priesthood for Himself. God, who declared Him to be His Son, also appointed Him a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek.

Hebrews 5:7–10 — Perfected Through Suffering

During His earthly life Jesus prayed with loud cries and tears to the God who could save Him from death, and God heard Him because of His reverence. Though He was the Son, He learned obedience through suffering, and having reached the goal of His calling, He became the source of eternal salvation to all who obey Him.

Hebrews 5:11–14 — Dull of Hearing and in Need of Solid Food

The writer has much more to say about Melchizedek, but his hearers have become slow to listen. Instead of teaching others, they need the basics again, like infants on milk, while solid food belongs to the mature, who train their judgment by practice.

Why This Chapter Matters

Hebrews 5 shows that Jesus’ priesthood rests on God’s appointment and on a real human life of costly obedience. He prayed with cries and tears, suffered, and obeyed to the end. That is why believers can trust Him: He did not escape the weight of human weakness, and God vindicated His obedience by raising Him (5:7–9).

The chapter also warns that believers can stop growing. The problem in 5:11–14 is not the difficulty of the subject but the hearers’ slowness to listen. Those who live only on milk remain unskilled in the word of righteousness, while the mature have their senses trained by practice to discern good from evil (5:13–14).

Key Themes to Watch For

Called by God. No high priest appoints himself. In Psalm 2 God declares Jesus His royal Son, and in Psalm 110 He appoints that Son a priest forever (5:4–6).

Compassion for the weak. A high priest deals gently with those who go astray, and Jesus knows human weakness without sin (5:2, 7–8).

Obedience through suffering. The Son learned obedience by what He suffered (5:7–8).

The author of eternal salvation. Jesus, perfected through suffering, is the source of lasting salvation (5:9).

Growing up. Dull hearing stalls growth, while practice trains discernment (5:11–14).

Key Words & Concepts

High priest (5:1). The priest from Aaron’s line who represented Israel before God and offered sacrifices for sins, especially on the yearly Day of Atonement.

The order of Melchizedek (5:6, 10). A priesthood like that of Melchizedek, the king and priest of Salem who blessed Abraham (Genesis 14:18–20). God promised it in Psalm 110:4, and Hebrews explains it in chapter 7.

Heard because of His godly fear (5:7). God answered Jesus’ reverent prayer. In this context the answer is best seen in God bringing Him through death and raising Him from it (compare 13:20).

Perfected (5:9). Brought to the intended goal. Jesus was always without sin; through obedient suffering He completed His calling as Savior and High Priest.

Milk and solid food (5:12–14). Pictures of needing the basics taught again versus being ready to receive and use fuller teaching.

Senses exercised (5:14). Spiritual judgment trained through practice, the way a body grows strong through regular use.
