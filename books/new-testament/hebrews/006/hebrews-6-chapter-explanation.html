Hebrews 6:1
Hebrews 6:1
NKJV

Hebrews 6:1 Therefore, leaving the discussion of the elementary principles of Christ, let us go on to perfection, not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God,(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 6:1 So let us stop going over the basic teachings about Christ again and again. Let us go on instead and become mature in our understanding. Surely we don’t need to start again with the fundamental importance of repenting from evil deeds and placing our faith in God. (NLT)

Main Idea

Leaving behind the elementary teaching about Christ, the writer urges, “let us go on to perfection,” meaning maturity, without laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God. The basics remain the foundation, but believers are not meant to stay at the starting point.

Verse Explanation

Leaving behind the elementary teaching about Christ, the writer urges, “let us go on to perfection,” meaning maturity, without laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God. The basics remain the foundation, but believers are not meant to stay at the starting point.

“let us go on to perfection” The call grows out of the rebuke in Hebrews 5:11–14. Perfection here is reaching the intended maturity of a disciple. The elementary teachings remain the foundation on which that growth depends. Repentance turns a person from dead works, deeds that yield no life before God, and faith turns that person toward the God who gives life.

Hebrews 6:2
Hebrews 6:2
NKJV

Hebrews 6:2 of the doctrine of baptisms, of laying on of hands, of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 6:2 You don’t need further instruction about baptisms, the laying on of hands, the resurrection of the dead, and eternal judgment. (NLT)

Main Idea

The foundation also includes the doctrine of baptisms, laying on of hands, resurrection of the dead, and eternal judgment. The plural baptisms point to baptism in water, in the Holy Spirit, and of suffering, and the laying on of hands serves to impart blessing, to commission people for service, and to express identification or confirmation. These first teachings support the Christian life, and the call is to build on them rather than keep relaying them.

Verse Explanation

The foundation also includes the doctrine of baptisms, laying on of hands, resurrection of the dead, and eternal judgment. The plural baptisms point to baptism in water, in the Holy Spirit, and of suffering, and the laying on of hands serves to impart blessing, to commission people for service, and to express identification or confirmation. These first teachings support the Christian life, and the call is to build on them rather than keep relaying them.

“the doctrine of baptisms” Scripture speaks of immersion in water, baptism in the Holy Spirit, and Jesus’ baptism of suffering (Acts 2:38, Matthew 3:11, and Luke 12:50). These are part of the range the plural brings to mind.

“of laying on of hands” Scripture uses the act in imparting blessing, the Holy Spirit, gifts, or healing, in commissioning people for service, and in identifying or confirming a person or action. Acts 8:17 and 1 Timothy 4:14 illustrate impartation, Acts 13:2–3 shows commissioning, and Leviticus 1:4 shows identification with the offering. Laying on of hands is a real spiritual ministry, not an empty ceremony. It is practiced with prayer, humility, and the Holy Spirit’s guidance, ordinarily within the local church under its elders, and never in haste, as Paul warned Timothy: “Do not lay hands on anyone hastily” (1 Timothy 5:22). Resurrection and eternal judgment complete the list by placing this life under the promises and accountability of the age to come.

Study Notes

Hebrews 9:10 uses the related Greek term for ritual washings. Some readers therefore include Jewish washings alongside Christian baptism in the plural here.

Hebrews 6:4–8 — The Warning Against Falling Away

Study Notes

None.

Hebrews 6:4
Hebrews 6:4
NKJV

Hebrews 6:4 For it is impossible for those who were once enlightened, and have tasted the heavenly gift, and have become partakers of the Holy Spirit,(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 6:4 For it is impossible to bring back to repentance those who were once enlightened — those who have experienced the good things of heaven and shared in the Holy Spirit, (NLT)

Main Idea

The writer begins a grave warning about those who were once enlightened, have tasted the heavenly gift, and have become partakers of the Holy Spirit. He describes people who have received real and great privileges from God.

Verse Explanation

The writer begins a grave warning about those who were once enlightened, have tasted the heavenly gift, and have become partakers of the Holy Spirit. He describes people who have received real and great privileges from God.

“those who were once enlightened” The warning describes professing believers in the congregation who had received real spiritual privileges. They had been brought into the light of the gospel, had experienced the heavenly gift, and had shared in the Holy Spirit’s work. The writer describes what they received without pronouncing on their prior inward standing. The sentence begins with impossibility and reaches its conclusion in verse 6.

Hebrews 6:6
Hebrews 6:6
NKJV

Hebrews 6:6 if they fall away, to renew them again to repentance, since they crucify again for themselves the Son of God, and put Him to an open shame.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 6:6 and who then turn away from God. It is impossible to bring such people back to repentance; by rejecting the Son of God, they themselves are nailing him to the cross once again and holding him up to public shame. (NLT)

Main Idea

If people who have been enlightened and have tasted God’s gifts in this way fall away, it is impossible to renew them again to repentance, “since they crucify again for themselves the Son of God, and put Him to an open shame.” Their falling away is a deliberate repudiation of Christ that sides with those who rejected and shamed Him and turns from the only sacrifice for sins.

Verse Explanation

If people who have been enlightened and have tasted God’s gifts in this way fall away, it is impossible to renew them again to repentance, “since they crucify again for themselves the Son of God, and put Him to an open shame.” Their falling away is a deliberate repudiation of Christ that sides with those who rejected and shamed Him and turns from the only sacrifice for sins.

“to renew them again to repentance” Those who deliberately and decisively turn from Christ after receiving these privileges cannot be renewed again to repentance. By repudiating the Son they side with those who crucified and publicly shamed Him and reject the only sacrifice through which they could return (Hebrews 10:26). The consequence concerns settled rejection of Christ. A believer who grieves over sin and wants to come back can go to the High Priest for mercy and help (Hebrews 4:14–16).

Study Notes

The NKJV puts the falling away in an if clause. The Greek lists falling away in the same grammatical sequence as the experiences in verses 4–5. Both ways of presenting the sentence leave the earlier inward standing of the people undescribed while naming the act that brings the warning to bear.

Verse Explanation

But if land that drinks in the same rain bears thorns and briers, it is rejected and near to being cursed, “whose end is to be burned.” The rain is God’s good gift to both fields, and the difference lies in what each field produces; the thorns picture the response the warning has just described, in which people receive God’s gifts and then turn against the One who gave them.

“if it bears thorns and briers” The thorns grow on ground that has received the same rain. The field is rejected, near to a curse, and ends by being burned. This is the warning of verses 4–6 in a picture: receiving great spiritual privileges offers no refuge from judgment when the response is settled rejection of the Son.

Hebrews 6:15
Hebrews 6:15
NKJV

Hebrews 6:15 And so, after he had patiently endured, he obtained the promise.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 6:15 Then Abraham waited patiently, and he received what God had promised. (NLT)

Main Idea

After Abraham had patiently endured, “he obtained the promise,” receiving the son God had promised and then hearing God confirm His blessing with an oath after he offered Isaac. That real reception was the first stage of a promise whose fuller fulfillment Abraham’s family still awaited, as Hebrews 11:13 later explains.

Verse Explanation

After Abraham had patiently endured, “he obtained the promise,” receiving the son God had promised and then hearing God confirm His blessing with an oath after he offered Isaac. That real reception was the first stage of a promise whose fuller fulfillment Abraham’s family still awaited, as Hebrews 11:13 later explains.

“after he had patiently endured” Abraham waited about twenty-five years for the son God promised. He was seventy-five when he left Haran (Genesis 12:4) and one hundred when Isaac was born (Genesis 21:5). In Isaac he received the son through whom the promised family would come. Years later, after Abraham offered Isaac, God confirmed His blessing with the oath quoted in Hebrews 6:14 (Genesis 22:15–18). The birth of Isaac and the sworn confirmation were real receiving of the promise, yet the family’s full inheritance still lay ahead (Hebrews 11:13). Abraham shows what Hebrews means by receiving a promise through patient faith while the whole fulfillment remains future.

The hope set before believers is “an anchor of the soul, both sure and steadfast,” and it reaches into the Presence behind the veil. The believer’s hope is fastened in God’s own presence, where nothing can tear it loose.

“an anchor of the soul” Hope holds believers steady because it reaches into God’s presence behind the veil. The ordinary anchor holds by taking hold of something firm beyond the vessel. Hebrews pictures our hope taking hold where Jesus already is, which explains why it is both sure and steadfast.
