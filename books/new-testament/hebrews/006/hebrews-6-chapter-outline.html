Hebrews 6 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Hebrews 6 presses the dull hearers of Hebrews 5:11–14 to go on to maturity and warns them gravely about falling away after receiving great privileges. The writer then voices confidence in their love and rests their hope on God’s promise and oath, an anchor that enters behind the veil, where Jesus has gone as forerunner and High Priest forever.

Chapter Sections

1. Hebrews 6:1–3 — Go On to Maturity

Leaving the discussion of the elementary principles of Christ, the writer urges, “let us go on to perfection” (Hebrews 6:1), without laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works, faith toward God, baptisms, laying on of hands, resurrection of the dead, and eternal judgment. He adds, “And this we will do if God permits” (Hebrews 6:3).

2. Hebrews 6:4–8 — The Warning Against Falling Away

It is impossible to renew to repentance those who were once enlightened, tasted the heavenly gift, became partakers of the Holy Spirit, and tasted the good word of God and the powers of the age to come, if they fall away, since they crucify again for themselves the Son of God and put Him to an open shame. Land that drinks in the rain and bears useful herbs receives blessing from God, but land that bears thorns and briers is rejected and near to being cursed.

3. Hebrews 6:9–12 — Confident of Better Things

“But, beloved, we are confident of better things concerning you” (Hebrews 6:9), for God will not forget their labor of love shown toward His name as they ministered to the saints and still do. The writer wants each of them to show the same diligence to the end and to imitate those who through faith and patience inherit the promises.

4. Hebrews 6:13–18 — God’s Promise Confirmed by an Oath

God promised to bless and multiply Abraham and, having no one greater to swear by, swore by Himself, and Abraham obtained the promise after he had patiently endured. In the same way God confirmed His counsel to the heirs of promise with an oath, so that by two unchangeable things, in which it is impossible for God to lie, those who have fled for refuge might have strong consolation.

5. Hebrews 6:19–20 — An Anchor Behind the Veil

This hope is “an anchor of the soul, both sure and steadfast” (Hebrews 6:19), and it enters the Presence behind the veil. There Jesus has entered for us as forerunner, having become High Priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek.
