Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 6
Main Idea:

The writer urges his hearers to build on their foundation and go on to maturity, warns gravely that turning away from Christ after sharing in God’s great gifts leaves no way back to repentance, and then assures them of better things, resting their hope on God’s promise and oath and on Jesus, who has entered God’s presence for them.

Setting:

Hebrews 6 continues the appeal that began with the rebuke of dull hearing in 5:11–14. It closes by returning to Melchizedek (6:20), the subject chapter 7 explains.

Chapter Outline:

  • Hebrews 6:1–3 — Go On to Maturity

  • Hebrews 6:4–8 — The Warning Against Falling Away

  • Hebrews 6:9–12 — Confident of Better Things

  • Hebrews 6:13–18 — God’s Promise Confirmed by an Oath

  • Hebrews 6:19–20 — An Anchor Behind the Veil

Key Verse:

“This hope we have as an anchor of the soul, both sure and steadfast, and which enters the Presence behind the veil, where the forerunner has entered for us, even Jesus, having become High Priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek.” (Hebrews 6:19–20)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The chapter opens with a call to grow: “let us go on to perfection” (6:1), that is, to maturity. The writer names six foundations: repentance from dead works, faith toward God, baptisms, laying on of hands, resurrection of the dead, and eternal judgment. They remain the base of the Christian life, but believers are meant to build on them instead of laying them again.

Then comes one of the sternest warnings in Scripture. The writer describes people in the congregation who “were once enlightened,” tasted the heavenly gift, became “partakers of the Holy Spirit,” and tasted the good word of God and the powers of the age to come (6:4–5). These were real privileges. If such people deliberately turn away from Christ, it is impossible to renew them to repentance, “since they crucify again for themselves the Son of God, and put Him to an open shame” (6:6). Hebrews pictures two fields that drink the same rain. One bears useful crops and receives blessing from God; the other bears thorns and briers, is rejected and near to being cursed, and its end is to be burned (6:7–8). The text does not settle what their inward standing had been, but it gives the consequence its full weight. This is settled rejection of the only Savior, not the stumbling of a believer who keeps looking to Christ.

The writer immediately turns to his hearers with warmth: “But, beloved, we are confident of better things concerning you” (6:9). God has not forgotten their love shown in serving the saints. Their hope rests on God Himself, who confirmed His promise to Abraham with an oath, so that by two unchangeable things, “in which it is impossible for God to lie” (6:18), His people have strong encouragement. That hope is an anchor reaching into God’s presence, where Jesus has already gone ahead of them (6:19–20).

Chapter Sections

Hebrews 6:1–3 — Go On to Maturity

The writer urges his hearers to move beyond the elementary teaching about Christ and go on to maturity. The foundation of repentance, faith, baptisms, laying on of hands, resurrection, and eternal judgment remains, and with God’s help they will build on it.

Hebrews 6:4–8 — The Warning Against Falling Away

Those who have been enlightened, tasted God’s heavenly gift, shared in the Holy Spirit, and experienced the goodness of God’s word and the powers of the coming age cannot be renewed to repentance if they fall away, because they side with those who crucified the Son of God and expose Him to public shame. Two fields drink the same rain: the one that bears a useful crop receives God’s blessing, while the one that bears only thorns is rejected and near to a curse, and its end is to be burned.

Hebrews 6:9–12 — Confident of Better Things

Despite the warning, the writer is confident of better things for his hearers, things that belong with salvation. God remembers their love shown in serving the saints, and the writer wants them to keep the same diligence to the end and imitate those who inherit God’s promises through faith and patience.

Hebrews 6:13–18 — God’s Promise Confirmed by an Oath

God promised to bless Abraham and swore by Himself, and Abraham received what was promised after waiting patiently. God confirmed His purpose to His people with an oath as well as a promise, so that those who have fled to Him for refuge have strong encouragement, because God cannot lie.

Hebrews 6:19–20 — An Anchor Behind the Veil

Believers’ hope is a sure and steady anchor that reaches into God’s presence behind the veil. Jesus has already gone there ahead of them as forerunner and High Priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek.

Why This Chapter Matters

Hebrews 6 holds warning and assurance together, and both must keep their full force. Turning deliberately from Christ after sharing in God’s gifts is a real danger with real consequences (6:4–8). Yet the same chapter calls its hearers “beloved,” remembers their love, and anchors their hope in God’s oath (6:9–18). A believer who is struggling with sin or fearful about his standing is not the person the warning describes; he is invited to cling to the hope set before him.

The chapter also shows where hope finds its hold. Hope does not rest on the strength of a person’s feelings. It is fastened to God’s unchanging promise and to Jesus, who has entered God’s presence on His people’s behalf (6:17–20).

Key Themes to Watch For

Go on to maturity. Believers build on the foundation instead of laying it again (6:1–3).

The danger of turning away. Deliberate rejection of Christ after receiving His gifts leaves no way back to repentance (6:4–8).

Fruit that accompanies salvation. Love shown in serving the saints is fruit God remembers (6:9–12).

God’s promise and oath. God bound Himself by oath, and He cannot lie (6:13–18).

Hope anchored in God’s presence. Jesus has entered behind the veil as forerunner and High Priest (6:19–20).

Key Words & Concepts

Perfection (6:1). Maturity, reaching the goal God intends. Believers are called to grow up instead of staying at the starting point.

Baptisms and laying on of hands (6:2). Scripture speaks of baptism in water, in the Holy Spirit, and of suffering (Matthew 3:11; Luke 12:50; Acts 2:38), and some take the plural to include Jewish ritual washings. Laying on of hands appears in Scripture for imparting blessing and gifts, commissioning for service, and identification and confirmation (Numbers 27:18–23; Acts 13:1–3; 1 Timothy 4:14).

Partakers of the Holy Spirit (6:4). One of the real privileges shared by those the warning describes. The passage describes their experience within the gospel community without settling their inward standing.

Crucify again (6:6). Turning deliberately from Christ sides with those who rejected and shamed Him, and it turns from the only sacrifice that brings forgiveness.

Two immutable things (6:18). God’s promise and His oath. Both are unchangeable because God cannot lie.

Forerunner (6:20). One who goes ahead to open the way for others to follow. Jesus has entered God’s presence for His people.
