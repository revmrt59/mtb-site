Hebrews 7:1
Hebrews 7:1
NKJV

Hebrews 7:1 For this Melchizedek, king of Salem, priest of the Most High God, who met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him,(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 7:1 This Melchizedek was king of the city of Salem and also a priest of God Most High. When Abraham was returning home after winning a great battle against the kings, Melchizedek met him and blessed him. (NLT)

Main Idea

Hebrews returns to Melchizedek, “king of Salem, priest of the Most High God,” who met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him. Drawing on Genesis 14, the writer presents a real, historical king and priest who ruled Salem, ancient Jerusalem.

Verse Explanation

Hebrews returns to Melchizedek, “king of Salem, priest of the Most High God,” who met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him. Drawing on Genesis 14, the writer presents a real, historical king and priest who ruled Salem, ancient Jerusalem.

“king of Salem, priest of the Most High God” Genesis 14:18–20 presents Melchizedek as a historical king and priest. He met Abraham after the defeat of the kings who had taken Lot and blessed him. Salem is associated with Jerusalem in Psalm 76:2. The two offices in one man prepare for Hebrews’ comparison to the royal priest promised in Psalm 110.

Hebrews 7:4
Hebrews 7:4
NKJV

NLT

Hebrews 7:4 Consider then how great this Melchizedek was. Even Abraham, the great patriarch of Israel, recognized this by giving him a tenth of what he had taken in battle. (NLT)

Main Idea

“Now consider how great this man was, to whom even the patriarch Abraham gave a tenth of the spoils.” By giving him the tenth, Abraham, the honored father of Israel, acknowledged Melchizedek’s greater standing, and he did so long before the law of Moses.

Verse Explanation

“Now consider how great this man was, to whom even the patriarch Abraham gave a tenth of the spoils.” By giving him the tenth, Abraham, the honored father of Israel, acknowledged Melchizedek’s greater standing, and he did so long before the law of Moses.

“even the patriarch Abraham gave a tenth of the spoils” The writer appeals to the greatness of Abraham in Israel’s story so the gift will carry its full weight. If the patriarch honored Melchizedek this way, Melchizedek’s priestly standing must be taken seriously. Abraham’s tenth also precedes the law. The practice appears again before Sinai, is confirmed under the law, and is affirmed by Jesus in His words to the scribes and Pharisees (Genesis 28:22, Leviticus 27:30, and Matthew 23:23). Christians differ on its continuing force, and the New Testament gives the church no direct command to tithe.

The priests and those who give are therefore relatives by descent. This legal right sets up the striking case of a priest who receives a tenth from Abraham himself.

Hebrews 7:11–19 — A New Priest and a Change of Law

Study Notes

None.

Hebrews 7:11

NLT

Hebrews 7:11 So if the priesthood of Levi, on which the law was based, could have achieved the perfection God intended, why did God need to establish a different priesthood, with a priest in the order of Melchizedek instead of the order of Levi and Aaron? (NLT)

Main Idea

If perfection had come through the Levitical priesthood, under which the people received the law, there would be no need for another priest to arise “according to the order of Melchizedek, and not be called according to the order of Aaron.” God’s promise of a different priest shows that the Levitical priesthood could not bring people fully to God.

Verse Explanation

If perfection had come through the Levitical priesthood, under which the people received the law, there would be no need for another priest to arise “according to the order of Melchizedek, and not be called according to the order of Aaron.” God’s promise of a different priest shows that the Levitical priesthood could not bring people fully to God.

“if perfection were through the Levitical priesthood” Perfection here means reaching the intended goal of bringing worshipers fully to God. Israel received the law in connection with the Levitical priesthood. Yet the later promise of another priest in Melchizedek’s order raises the decisive question: why would God give another priest if Aaron’s order had already brought worshipers to that goal?

Verse Explanation

The law appoints as high priests men who have weakness, “but the word of the oath, which came after the law, appoints the Son who has been perfected forever.” God’s later oath establishes the Son, who has completed His saving calling, as High Priest forever.

“the Son who has been perfected forever” The law appointed men subject to weakness. God’s later oath appoints the Son, who has reached the intended end of His saving calling and holds the office forever. His completed offering and continuing priesthood give the congregation its lasting confidence.
