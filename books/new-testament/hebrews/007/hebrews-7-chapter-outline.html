Hebrews 7 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Hebrews 7 explains why Jesus, a priest according to the order of Melchizedek, holds a priesthood greater than that of the Levitical priests, arguing from Melchizedek’s standing over Abraham, the change of law a new priest requires, God’s oath, and the Son’s endless life. Because He continues forever, holy and without sin, having offered Himself once for all, Jesus is able to save to the uttermost those who come to God through Him.

Chapter Sections

1. Hebrews 7:1–3 — Melchizedek, King and Priest

Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of the Most High God, met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings, blessed him, and received a tenth of all. His name and title mean king of righteousness and king of peace, and Scripture records no father, mother, genealogy, beginning, or end for him, so that he is “made like the Son of God” (Hebrews 7:3) and remains a priest continually.

2. Hebrews 7:4–10 — Greater Than Abraham and Levi

“Now consider how great this man was” (Hebrews 7:4): the patriarch Abraham gave him a tenth of the spoils, and beyond all contradiction the lesser is blessed by the better. The Levitical priests receive tithes as mortal men, yet Levi himself, so to speak, paid tithes through Abraham to one of whom it is witnessed that he lives.

3. Hebrews 7:11–19 — A New Priest and a Change of Law

If perfection had come through the Levitical priesthood, there would have been no need for another priest according to the order of Melchizedek, and a change of priesthood requires a change of law. Jesus arose from Judah, a tribe Moses never connected with priesthood, and He became priest “according to the power of an endless life” (Hebrews 7:16), so the weak former commandment is annulled and a better hope is brought in, through which we draw near to God.

4. Hebrews 7:20–22 — The Oath and the Better Covenant

The Levitical priests became priests without an oath, but Jesus was appointed by God’s sworn word: “The LORD has sworn And will not relent” (Hebrews 7:21). By so much more Jesus has become “a surety of a better covenant” (Hebrews 7:22), that is, its personal guarantor.

5. Hebrews 7:23–25 — He Always Lives to Intercede

The former priests were many, because death prevented them from continuing, but Jesus continues forever and holds an unchangeable priesthood. “Therefore He is also able to save to the uttermost those who come to God through Him, since He always lives to make intercession for them” (Hebrews 7:25).

6. Hebrews 7:26–28 — The Holy High Priest Who Offered Himself Once

Such a High Priest was fitting for us: holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners, and higher than the heavens. Unlike the high priests who offer daily for their own sins and then for the people’s, He offered up Himself once for all, and the word of the oath appoints “the Son who has been perfected forever” (Hebrews 7:28).
