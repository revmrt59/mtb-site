Chapter Study

Hebrews 8

Verse-by-Verse
Hebrews 8:1
Hebrews 8:1
NKJV

Hebrews 8:1 Now this is the main point of the things we are saying: We have such a High Priest, who is seated at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens,(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 8:1 Here is the main point: We have a High Priest who sat down in the place of honor beside the throne of the majestic God in heaven. (NLT)

Main Idea

“Now this is the main point of the things we are saying: We have such a High Priest,” one who is seated at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens. Everything said about Jesus’ priesthood comes to this: believers have Him now, enthroned beside God.

Verse Explanation

“Now this is the main point of the things we are saying: We have such a High Priest,” one who is seated at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens. Everything said about Jesus’ priesthood comes to this: believers have Him now, enthroned beside God.

“We have such a High Priest” The priest just described in Hebrews 7 is the priest the congregation has now. He is seated in the heavens at the right hand of God’s throne, where His royal authority and priestly service meet. Hebrews calls this the main point because the better priest gives His people a real relationship to God’s presence.

Hebrews 8:2 There he ministers in the heavenly Tabernacle, the true place of worship that was built by the Lord and not by human hands. (NLT)

Verse Explanation

Jesus serves as “a Minister of the sanctuary and of the true tabernacle which the Lord erected, and not man.” His ministry takes place in the real dwelling of God, which the Lord Himself established.

“a Minister of the sanctuary” Jesus actively serves in the true tabernacle. The Lord Himself established that sanctuary. Its reality explains why the earthly sanctuary, though given by God, could serve as a copy.

Verse Explanation

Every high priest is appointed to offer gifts and sacrifices, so this High Priest must also have something to offer. Hebrews prepares to explain what Jesus offered, which chapters 9 and 10 make plain.

“this One also have something to offer” Offering gifts and sacrifices belongs to the assignment of every high priest. That established pattern raises the question of Jesus’ offering. Hebrews 9:24–28 answers it by joining His once-for-all sacrifice to His present appearance before God for us.

Hebrews 8:4 If he were here on earth, he would not even be a priest, since there already are priests who offer the gifts required by the law. (NLT)

Main Idea

If Jesus were on earth, He would not be a priest at all, since priests already offer the gifts the law requires. His priesthood does not belong to the earthly order of the law but to heaven.

Verse Explanation

If Jesus were on earth, He would not be a priest at all, since priests already offer the gifts the law requires. His priesthood does not belong to the earthly order of the law but to heaven.

“if He were on earth” The existing earthly priests offer the prescribed gifts under the law. Jesus comes from Judah, so He would hold no priestly place under those provisions. His ministry takes place in God’s presence under a different appointment.

Hebrews 8:5
NKJV

Hebrews 8:5 who serve the copy and shadow of the heavenly things, as Moses was divinely instructed when he was about to make the tabernacle. For He said, "See that you make all things according to the pattern shown you on the mountain."(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 8:5 They serve in a system of worship that is only a copy, a shadow of the real one in heaven. For when Moses was getting ready to build the Tabernacle, God gave him this warning: “Be sure that you make everything according to the pattern I have shown you here on the mountain.” (NLT)

Main Idea

The earthly priests serve “the copy and shadow of the heavenly things,” as Moses was told when he was about to build the tabernacle: “See that you make all things according to the pattern shown you on the mountain.” The tabernacle was God-given and true, yet it was a copy of the heavenly reality where Jesus now ministers.

Verse Explanation

The earthly priests serve “the copy and shadow of the heavenly things,” as Moses was told when he was about to build the tabernacle: “See that you make all things according to the pattern shown you on the mountain.” The tabernacle was God-given and true, yet it was a copy of the heavenly reality where Jesus now ministers.

“the copy and shadow of the heavenly things” Moses was told in Exodus 25:40 to build according to what God showed him on the mountain. The instruction gives the earthly tabernacle its authority and shows its limits. Its carefully ordered service points beyond itself to the heavenly sanctuary in which Jesus ministers.

Hebrews 8:6–7 — Mediator of a Better Covenant

Study Notes

None.

Hebrews 8:6
Hebrews 8:6
NKJV

Hebrews 8:6 But now He has obtained a more excellent ministry, inasmuch as He is also Mediator of a better covenant, which was established on better promises.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 8:6 But now Jesus, our High Priest, has been given a ministry that is far superior to the old priesthood, for he is the one who mediates for us a far better covenant with God, based on better promises. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus has now obtained a more excellent ministry, because He is also Mediator of “a better covenant, which was established on better promises.” From the heavenly sanctuary He brings His people into the better covenant God promised, with the inward change, personal knowledge of God, and forgiveness that the chapter goes on to name.

Verse Explanation

Jesus has now obtained a more excellent ministry, because He is also Mediator of “a better covenant, which was established on better promises.” From the heavenly sanctuary He brings His people into the better covenant God promised, with the inward change, personal knowledge of God, and forgiveness that the chapter goes on to name.

“Mediator of a better covenant” Jesus has received the more excellent ministry because He brings His people into the better covenant. The better promises that underlie it are the ones Jeremiah will name: God works within His people, gives them knowledge of Himself, and remembers their sins no more. Through Jesus those promises are already at work.

Study Notes

None.

Hebrews 8:7
NKJV

Hebrews 8:7 For if that first covenant had been faultless, then no place would have been sought for a second.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 8:7 If the first covenant had been faultless, there would have been no need for a second covenant to replace it. (NLT)

Main Idea

“For if that first covenant had been faultless, then no place would have been sought for a second.” The need for a second covenant shows the limits of the first, which did not bring the better promises God names in Hebrews 8:8–12: His laws written on the heart, every member knowing Him, and sins remembered no more.

Verse Explanation

“For if that first covenant had been faultless, then no place would have been sought for a second.” The need for a second covenant shows the limits of the first, which did not bring the better promises God names in Hebrews 8:8–12: His laws written on the heart, every member knowing Him, and sins remembered no more.

“no place would have been sought for a second” God’s announcement of another covenant shows the limits of the first. What the first covenant required, Jeremiah’s promise goes on to describe God bringing about within His people. Hebrews will also show why a new priestly offering is necessary for complete forgiveness.

Verse Explanation

Finding fault with the people, God announced through Jeremiah, “Behold, the days are coming, says the LORD, when I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah.” The new covenant is God’s own initiative, promised to His people Israel and Judah.

“with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah” God finds fault with the people and announces through Jeremiah a new covenant with the two houses named in the promise. Their unfaithfulness explains the setting of the promise. Hebrews quotes it to explain what Jesus now mediates.

“they did not continue in My covenant” God recalls taking the ancestors by the hand to bring them out of Egypt. That care makes their failure to remain faithful especially plain. His statement that He disregarded them describes His response to their breach and shows why the new covenant has to differ.

“I will put My laws in their mind and write them on their hearts” God takes the initiative to place His instruction within His people. The mind comes to understand what pleases Him, and the heart is moved to obey. God’s pledge to be their God names the relationship this inward work serves.

Hebrews 8:13 — The First Covenant Made Obsolete

Study Notes

None.

Hebrews 8:13
Hebrews 8:13
NKJV

Hebrews 8:13 In that He says, "A new covenant," He has made the first obsolete. Now what is becoming obsolete and growing old is ready to vanish away.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 8:13 When God speaks of a “new” covenant, it means he has made the first one obsolete. It is now out of date and will soon disappear. (NLT)

Main Idea

By calling this covenant new, God “has made the first obsolete,” and what is becoming obsolete and growing old “is ready to vanish away.” The first covenant and its priestly order give way to the new covenant Jesus mediates.

Verse Explanation

By calling this covenant new, God “has made the first obsolete,” and what is becoming obsolete and growing old “is ready to vanish away.” The first covenant and its priestly order give way to the new covenant Jesus mediates.

“He has made the first obsolete” By calling the covenant new God marks the earlier Sinai covenant and its priestly order as old. Growing old and being ready to vanish picture an order giving way to the covenant Jesus already mediates. Chapter 9 explains the earlier sanctuary and rites with that change in view.
