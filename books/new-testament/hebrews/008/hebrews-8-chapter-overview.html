At a Glance

Chapter 8
Main Idea:

The main point is that believers have a High Priest seated in heaven who ministers in the true tabernacle and mediates the better covenant God promised through Jeremiah, in which He writes His laws on His people’s hearts, makes Himself known to all of them, and remembers their sins no more.

Setting:

Hebrews 8 gathers up the priesthood argument of chapter 7 and moves it into God’s presence. It quotes Jeremiah 31:31–34 in full and prepares for the explanation of Christ’s sacrifice in chapters 9 and 10.

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

After seven chapters of argument, Hebrews pauses to state its main point: “We have such a High Priest” (8:1). He is seated at God’s right hand, serving in “the true tabernacle which the Lord erected, and not man” (8:2). The tabernacle Moses built was God-given and true to its pattern, but it was “the copy and shadow of the heavenly things” (8:5). Jesus ministers in the reality those copies pictured.

His ministry is greater because the covenant He mediates is greater, “a better covenant, which was established on better promises” (8:6). God Himself announced it through Jeremiah, finding fault with a people who “did not continue in My covenant” (8:9). The new covenant answers what the old could not. God will put His laws in His people’s minds and write them on their hearts. He will be their God, and they will be His people. All of them, “from the least of them to the greatest of them,” will know Him personally, and He will remember their sins and lawless deeds no more (8:10–12).

By calling this covenant new, God has made the first one obsolete, and what is growing old “is ready to vanish away” (8:13). The chapter leaves the reader standing before a High Priest who is already serving in heaven and already bringing His people into the covenant God promised.

Chapter Sections

Hebrews 8:1–5 — The Main Point: A High Priest in the Heavens

The main point of everything said so far is that believers have a High Priest seated at God’s right hand, serving in the true tabernacle the Lord Himself set up. Earthly priests served a copy and shadow of heavenly realities, made by the pattern God showed Moses.

Hebrews 8:6–7 — Mediator of a Better Covenant

Jesus’ ministry is greater because He mediates a better covenant founded on better promises. If the first covenant had been without fault, there would have been no need for a second.

Hebrews 8:8–12 — God’s Promise of a New Covenant

Finding fault with His people, God promised through Jeremiah a new covenant with Israel and Judah, unlike the one their fathers broke after the exodus. In it He writes His laws on their hearts, makes Himself known to all of them, and forgives their sins completely.

Hebrews 8:13 — The First Covenant Made Obsolete

By calling the covenant new, God declared the first covenant obsolete. What is aging and growing old is ready to disappear.

Why This Chapter Matters

Hebrews 8 shows that the gospel brings an inward change God Himself performs. Under the new covenant God does not only command His people from outside; He writes His laws on their hearts and makes Himself personally known to every one of them (8:10–11). Obedience grows out of what God does within.

The chapter also grounds believers’ confidence in forgiveness. God promises to remember their sins no more (8:12), and Jesus, the Mediator of that promise, is already serving in heaven. Every believer, from the least to the greatest, can know God and draw near through Him.

Key Themes to Watch For

The main point. Believers have a High Priest seated in heaven (8:1).

Copy and reality. The earthly tabernacle was a God-given copy of the heavenly sanctuary where Jesus serves (8:2–5).

A better covenant. Jesus mediates a covenant established on better promises (8:6–7).

Hearts made new. God writes His laws within His people and makes Himself known to all of them (8:10–11).

Sins remembered no more. Full forgiveness lies at the heart of the new covenant (8:12).

Key Words & Concepts

The true tabernacle (8:2). The heavenly reality of God’s dwelling, which the Lord Himself set up. Jesus ministers there.

Copy and shadow (8:5). The earthly tabernacle was real and God-given, but it pictured something greater. God told Moses to build it according to the pattern shown him on the mountain (Exodus 25:40).

Mediator (8:6). One who stands between two parties to establish and maintain their relationship. Jesus brings His people into the new covenant and keeps them in it.

New covenant (8:8). The covenant God promised through Jeremiah to the house of Israel and the house of Judah (Jeremiah 31:31–34). Jesus mediates it, bringing its promises of inward change, knowledge of God, and forgiveness to His people.

“All shall know Me” (8:11). Every member of the new covenant knows God personally. Teaching still helps believers grow in that knowledge, which will reach its fullness when Christ returns (1 Corinthians 13:12).

Obsolete (8:13). No longer in force. Hebrews applies the word to the Sinai covenant and its priestly order, which give way to the new covenant.
