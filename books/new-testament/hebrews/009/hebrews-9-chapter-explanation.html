Hebrews 9

Verse-by-Verse
Hebrews 9:1
Hebrews 9:1
NKJV

Hebrews 9:1 Then indeed, even the first covenant had ordinances of divine service and the earthly sanctuary.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 9:1 That first covenant between God and Israel had regulations for worship and a place of worship here on earth. (NLT)

Main Idea

The first covenant had its own regulations for worship and an earthly sanctuary. Hebrews begins by describing that God-given arrangement so it can show what Christ has done.

Verse Explanation

The first covenant had its own regulations for worship and an earthly sanctuary. Hebrews begins by describing that God-given arrangement so it can show what Christ has done.

“ordinances of divine service and the earthly sanctuary” The first covenant had rules for worship and a place in which that worship happened. Both were given by God. The writer first explains how the sanctuary worked so the difference Christ makes in access to God can be seen.

None.

Verse Explanation

The tabernacle’s first room, called the sanctuary, held the lampstand, the table, and the showbread. This was the part where the priests carried out their regular service.

“the lampstand, the table, and the showbread” These furnishings belonged to the first room, where priests ministered regularly. The showbread was bread set before the LORD on the table. Calling this room the sanctuary prepares for the description of an inner room with stricter access.

“behind the second veil” The inner curtain marked the boundary of the Holiest of All. The relation between the two rooms mattered because priests could enter the outer room regularly, while entry behind this curtain was sharply restricted.

“the golden censer and the ark of the covenant” The censer belongs to the worship associated with the inner room. The gold-covered ark contained the pot of manna, Aaron’s rod that budded, and the covenant tablets. Those objects recalled God’s provision, His confirmation of Aaron, and His covenant words.

Study Notes

The Greek term translated golden censer can also refer to the incense altar. Leviticus 16:12–13 describes the censer being carried behind the veil, while Exodus 30:6 places the incense altar in front of the veil in close relation to the ark.

“through the eternal Spirit offered Himself without spot to God” Jesus willingly offered Himself to God as the spotless sacrifice, empowered by the Holy Spirit. That is why the comparison reaches beyond outward purity.

“cleanse your conscience from dead works to serve the living God” Christ’s blood clears the conscience of guilt from sinful deeds and frees people from lifeless religious efforts to secure God’s favor. The result is freedom to serve the living God.

The plural belongs to the comparison with the earlier sacrifices, and the following verses identify what fulfills it: the one self-offering by which Christ opens access to God. The point is what makes approach possible for His people.

Hebrews 9:27 And just as each person is destined to die once and after that comes judgment, (NLT)

Main Idea

People are appointed to die once, and after this comes the judgment. Human death happens once and is followed by God’s verdict, and Hebrews uses this pattern to describe Christ’s single offering.

Verse Explanation

People are appointed to die once, and after this comes the judgment. Human death happens once and is followed by God’s verdict, and Hebrews uses this pattern to describe Christ’s single offering.

“after this the judgment” The ordinary human course is death once and then accountability before God. The writer uses that familiar order to prepare the contrast between Christ’s one death and what follows it at His second appearance.

Hebrews 9:28 so Christ was offered once to bear the sins of many. To those who eagerly wait for Him He will appear a second time, apart from sin, for salvation.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 9:28 so also Christ was offered once for all time as a sacrifice to take away the sins of many people. He will come again, not to deal with our sins, but to bring salvation to all who are eagerly waiting for him. (NLT)

Main Idea

So Christ was offered once “to bear the sins of many,” and He will appear a second time, apart from sin, “for salvation” to those who eagerly wait for Him. His first coming dealt with sin, and His return will bring His waiting people into full salvation.

Verse Explanation

So Christ was offered once “to bear the sins of many,” and He will appear a second time, apart from sin, “for salvation” to those who eagerly wait for Him. His first coming dealt with sin, and His return will bring His waiting people into full salvation.

“He will appear a second time” Christ was offered once to carry the sins of many. At His return that dealing with sin is already accomplished. He comes to bring the fullness of salvation to those who eagerly await Him, so His return is the completion toward which His people look.
