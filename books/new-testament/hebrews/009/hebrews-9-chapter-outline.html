Hebrews 9 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Hebrews 9 sets the restricted access of the earthly tabernacle beside the greater work of Christ, who entered God’s presence once for all with His own blood and obtained eternal redemption. His death puts the new covenant into effect, cleanses the conscience to serve the living God, and accomplishes what the repeated sacrifices could only picture, and He will appear a second time for the salvation of those who wait for Him.

Chapter Sections

1. Hebrews 9:1–5 — The Earthly Tabernacle and Its Furnishings

The first covenant had ordinances of divine service and an earthly sanctuary, with the lampstand, the table, and the showbread in the first part. Behind the second veil lay the Holiest of All, with the golden censer, the ark of the covenant and its contents, and “the cherubim of glory overshadowing the mercy seat” (Hebrews 9:5).

2. Hebrews 9:6–10 — The Way into the Holiest Not Yet Open

The priests served continually in the first part, but only the high priest entered the second, once a year and “not without blood” (Hebrews 9:7). By this the Holy Spirit showed that the way into the Holiest of All was not yet made manifest, because those gifts and sacrifices could not perfect the worshiper’s conscience and were imposed only until the time of reformation.

3. Hebrews 9:11–14 — Christ Entered with His Own Blood

Christ came as High Priest of the good things to come and entered the Most Holy Place once for all with His own blood, having obtained eternal redemption. If the blood of animals purified the flesh, “how much more shall the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered Himself without spot to God, cleanse your conscience from dead works to serve the living God?” (Hebrews 9:14).

4. Hebrews 9:15–17 — The Mediator’s Death and the Promised Inheritance

Christ is Mediator of the new covenant by means of death, redeeming the transgressions committed under the first covenant so that those who are called may receive the promise of the eternal inheritance. As a will takes effect only when the person who made it has died, His death puts that inheritance into effect.

5. Hebrews 9:18–22 — The First Covenant Dedicated with Blood

Not even the first covenant was dedicated without blood: Moses sprinkled the book and all the people, saying, “This is the blood of the covenant which God has commanded you” (Hebrews 9:20), and then sprinkled the tabernacle and all the vessels of the ministry. Under the law almost all things are purified with blood, and “without shedding of blood there is no remission” (Hebrews 9:22).

6. Hebrews 9:23–28 — Once at the End of the Ages

The earthly copies were purified with animal sacrifices, but Christ entered heaven itself, “now to appear in the presence of God for us” (Hebrews 9:24), and appeared once at the end of the ages to put away sin by the sacrifice of Himself. As it is appointed for people to die once and then face judgment, Christ was offered once to bear the sins of many, and He will appear a second time, apart from sin, for salvation to those who eagerly wait for Him.
