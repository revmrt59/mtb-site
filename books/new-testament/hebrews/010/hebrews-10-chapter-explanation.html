Hebrews 10:1
Hebrews 10:1
NKJV

Hebrews 10:1 For the law, having a shadow of the good things to come, and not the very image of the things, can never with these same sacrifices, which they offer continually year by year, make those who approach perfect.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 10:1 The old system under the law of Moses was only a shadow, a dim preview of the good things to come, not the good things themselves. The sacrifices under that system were repeated again and again, year after year, but they were never able to provide perfect cleansing for those who came to worship. (NLT)

Main Idea

The law has only “a shadow of the good things to come, and not the very image of the things,” so its repeated yearly sacrifices can never make worshipers perfect. A shadow points to something real but cannot supply it.

Verse Explanation

The law has only “a shadow of the good things to come, and not the very image of the things,” so its repeated yearly sacrifices can never make worshipers perfect. A shadow points to something real but cannot supply it.

“a shadow of the good things to come” The law’s sacrifices outlined what God would provide in Christ. Their yearly repetition shows that the outline could never bring worshipers to the goal of complete cleansing and lasting access. What the shadow anticipates must be accomplished by the offering itself.

Hebrews 10:5–10 — A Body Prepared to Do God’s Will

Study Notes

None.

Hebrews 10:5
Hebrews 10:5
NKJV

Hebrews 10:5 Therefore, when He came into the world, He said: "Sacrifice and offering You did not desire, But a body You have prepared for Me.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 10:5 That is why, when Christ came into the world, he said to God, “You did not want animal sacrifices or sin offerings.But you have given me a body to offer. (NLT)

Main Idea

When the Son came into the world, He spoke the words of Psalm 40 in the Greek form Hebrews quotes: “Sacrifice and offering You did not desire, But a body You have prepared for Me.” God prepared a human body for His Son, the body in which He would do God’s will and offer Himself.

Verse Explanation

When the Son came into the world, He spoke the words of Psalm 40 in the Greek form Hebrews quotes: “Sacrifice and offering You did not desire, But a body You have prepared for Me.” God prepared a human body for His Son, the body in which He would do God’s will and offer Himself.

“a body You have prepared for Me” Hebrews puts Psalm 40 on the Son’s lips as He comes into the world. God had provided Jesus with His own human body, in which He came to obey and which He would give as the sacrifice. The opening words about offering prepare for the contrast between the earlier sacrifices and the Son who gives Himself.

Study Notes

The ancient Greek wording of Psalm 40 quoted by Hebrews speaks of a prepared body. In Psalm 40:6 the NKJV, translated from the Hebrew, speaks of opened ears. Both images accompany the speaker’s readiness to do God’s will.

Verse Explanation

“which are offered according to the law” Hebrews gathers the kinds of sacrifices just named and stresses that they were lawful offerings. Their limitation is part of the argument about the order God gave, not a charge that worshipers had invented the sacrifices. The decisive change comes with the Son’s obedient offering.

Much worse punishment is deserved by the one “who has trampled the Son of God underfoot, counted the blood of the covenant by which he was sanctified a common thing, and insulted the Spirit of grace.” To treat the covenant blood that sanctified him as ordinary and worthless is to reject the very sacrifice that brings forgiveness, which is far graver than rejecting Moses’ law.

Verse Explanation

Much worse punishment is deserved by the one “who has trampled the Son of God underfoot, counted the blood of the covenant by which he was sanctified a common thing, and insulted the Spirit of grace.” To treat the covenant blood that sanctified him as ordinary and worthless is to reject the very sacrifice that brings forgiveness, which is far graver than rejecting Moses’ law.

“trampled the Son of God underfoot” The greater punishment corresponds to contempt for the Son, treating His covenant blood as common, and insulting the Spirit of grace. The person had been set apart by that blood in the sense the verse describes, and now treats it as ordinary. Hebrews leaves open whether that prior setting apart involved inward renewal. Its direct concern is what the person received and has willfully repudiated.
