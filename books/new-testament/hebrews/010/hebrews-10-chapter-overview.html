Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 10
Main Idea:

By offering His own body once for all, Jesus accomplished what the law’s repeated sacrifices never could, perfecting forever those who are being sanctified; so believers are to draw near, hold fast, and encourage one another, take seriously the warning against rejecting the Son, and keep their confidence until He comes.

Setting:

Hebrews 10:1–18 completes the teaching on Christ’s priesthood and sacrifice that began at 4:14. At 10:19 the book’s fourth movement begins, turning that teaching into a call to draw near and endure.

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The law’s yearly sacrifices only reminded people of their sins, “For it is not possible that the blood of bulls and goats could take away sins” (10:4). So the Son came saying, “a body You have prepared for Me” (10:5), and He offered that body once for all to do God’s will. Israel’s priests stood day after day, but Jesus offered one sacrifice and sat down. “For by one offering He has perfected forever those who are being sanctified” (10:14). Through Jeremiah the Spirit adds God’s promise to remember their sins no more, and where sins are forgiven, no further offering is needed (10:17–18).

On that foundation Hebrews makes its great appeal. Believers have boldness to enter God’s presence “by the blood of Jesus,” through “a new and living way” opened through His flesh, and they have a High Priest over God’s house (10:19–21). So, “let us draw near,” “let us hold fast,” and “let us consider one another,” gathering to stir up love and good works as the Day approaches (10:22–25).

Then comes a solemn warning, addressed to the whole congregation with the writer included: “if we sin willfully after we have received the knowledge of the truth, there no longer remains a sacrifice for sins,” only “a certain fearful expectation of judgment” (10:26–27). Hebrews describes this willful sin as trampling the Son of God underfoot, insulting the Spirit of grace, and treating as common “the blood of the covenant by which he was sanctified” (10:29). “Sanctified” here means set apart for God; the writer does not say whether that setting apart had involved inward renewal. The only sacrifice that forgives is the one rejected. The writer at once reminds his hearers of their earlier endurance and urges them not to throw away their confidence: “we are not of those who draw back to perdition, but of those who believe to the saving of the soul” (10:39).

Chapter Sections

Hebrews 10:1–4 — A Shadow That Could Not Take Away Sins

The law was only a shadow of the good things to come, so its sacrifices, repeated year after year, could never make worshipers perfect. They brought a yearly reminder of sin, because the blood of bulls and goats cannot take sin away.

Hebrews 10:5–10 — A Body Prepared to Do God’s Will

Coming into the world, the Son speaks Psalm 40: God prepared a body for Him, and He came to do God’s will. He sets aside the sacrifices the law required, and through the offering of His own body once for all, believers are made holy.

Hebrews 10:11–14 — One Sacrifice, and He Sat Down

Israel’s priests stand every day offering sacrifices that can never remove sins, but Jesus offered one sacrifice for sins forever and sat down at God’s right hand. By that single offering He has perfected forever those who are being made holy.

Hebrews 10:15–18 — The Spirit Witnesses to Forgiveness

The Holy Spirit confirms this through Jeremiah’s promise that God will write His laws on His people’s hearts and remember their sins no more. Where sins are forgiven, no more offering for sin is needed.

Hebrews 10:19–25 — Let Us Draw Near, Hold Fast, and Consider One Another

Because Jesus’ blood has opened a new and living way into God’s presence and He serves as High Priest over God’s house, believers can draw near with full assurance, cleansed in heart and body. They must hold fast their hope, because God is faithful, and keep meeting together to spur one another on to love and good works as the Day approaches.

Hebrews 10:26–31 — No Sacrifice Left for Willful Rejection

Those who received the knowledge of the truth and then deliberately reject Christ have no sacrifice left for their sins, only a terrifying expectation of judgment. If rejecting Moses’ law brought death, far worse punishment awaits those who trample the Son of God, treat as common the covenant blood by which they were set apart, and insult the Spirit of grace.

Hebrews 10:32–39 — Do Not Cast Away Your Confidence

The hearers are to remember how they once endured public suffering, stood with others who suffered, and joyfully accepted the loss of their goods because they had a better, lasting possession. They must not throw away their confidence but endure, for Christ is coming, and they are among those who believe to the saving of the soul.

Why This Chapter Matters

Hebrews 10 shows that Christ’s sacrifice is finished. One offering has dealt with sin forever, and God remembers forgiven sins no more (10:14, 17). Believers do not need to keep offering anything to be accepted. They can draw near with full assurance and a cleansed conscience.

The chapter also shows that this confidence is meant to be lived together and held to the end. Believers draw near, hold fast, and encourage one another, especially as the Day approaches (10:22–25). The warning of 10:26–31 describes deliberate, settled rejection of Christ, not the struggle of a believer who keeps coming back to Him. For those who are fearful or failing, the invitation of 10:19–22 stands open.

Key Themes to Watch For

Shadow and reality. The law’s repeated sacrifices could not take away sins (10:1–4, 11).

A body prepared. The Son came to do God’s will and offered His own body once for all (10:5–10).

Perfected forever. One offering perfects those who are being sanctified, and God remembers their sins no more (10:12–18).

Draw near together. Believers draw near, hold fast, and encourage one another (10:19–25).

Warning and confidence. Willful rejection of Christ leaves no sacrifice, while persevering faith leads to salvation (10:26–39).

Key Words & Concepts

A body You have prepared (10:5). Hebrews quotes Psalm 40 in the Greek form many Jewish communities used. The body is Jesus’ own, in which He came to do God’s will and which He offered once for all.

Sanctified (10:10, 14). Set apart for God and made holy. Believers are sanctified through Christ’s offering, and God continues that work in them.

Sat down (10:12). Israel’s priests always stood because their work was never done. Jesus sat down because His sacrifice was complete, though He continues to intercede for His people (7:25).

The new and living way (10:20). The way into God’s presence that Jesus opened through His own flesh, given in death, replacing the closed veil of the tabernacle.

Sin willfully (10:26). Hebrews defines this in 10:29 as settled, defiant rejection of Christ: trampling the Son of God, treating as common the covenant blood by which the person was sanctified, or set apart, and insulting the Spirit of grace.

“The just shall live by faith” (10:38). Words from Habakkuk 2:4. God’s righteous people live by trusting Him while they wait for the One who is coming, and this verse leads into the examples of faith in chapter 11.
