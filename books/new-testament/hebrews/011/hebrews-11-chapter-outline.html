Hebrews 11 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Hebrews 11 answers the call to live by faith (Hebrews 10:38–39) by showing what faith is and how it has lived through the generations, from Abel to the prophets and the unnamed faithful who suffered. The witnesses act on what God has said before they can see the outcome, and the chapter closes by joining their hope to ours in the fulfillment God has provided for His people together.

Chapter Sections

1. Hebrews 11:1–3 — What Faith Is

“Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen” (Hebrews 11:1): faith is firm assurance grounded in what God has promised, and it gives His unseen future real weight in present obedience and endurance. By faith the elders obtained a good testimony, and by faith we understand that the worlds were framed by the word of God, so that what is seen was not made of things which are visible.

2. Hebrews 11:4–7 — Abel, Enoch, and Noah

By faith Abel offered a more excellent sacrifice than Cain, Enoch was taken away so that he did not see death, and Noah, divinely warned of things not yet seen, prepared an ark for the saving of his household. Between these examples stands the principle that “without faith it is impossible to please Him” (Hebrews 11:6), for whoever comes to God must believe that He is and that He rewards those who diligently seek Him.

3. Hebrews 11:8–12 — Abraham and Sarah Trust the One Who Promised

By faith Abraham obeyed and went out, not knowing where he was going, and lived in tents in the land of promise, waiting for “the city which has foundations, whose builder and maker is God” (Hebrews 11:10). By faith Sarah received strength to conceive when she was past the age, because she judged Him faithful who had promised, and from one man, as good as dead, came descendants as many as the stars of the sky.

4. Hebrews 11:13–16 — Strangers Seeking a Heavenly Country

These all died in faith without receiving the promises, having seen them afar off and confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth. They desired a better, heavenly country, and so “God is not ashamed to be called their God, for He has prepared a city for them” (Hebrews 11:16).

5. Hebrews 11:17–22 — Faith That Looks Beyond Death

By faith Abraham offered up Isaac, the son of promise, concluding that God was able to raise him up even from the dead, from which he also received him in a figurative sense. By faith Isaac and Jacob blessed their descendants concerning things to come, and Joseph, when he was dying, spoke of Israel’s departure and gave instructions concerning his bones.

6. Hebrews 11:23–28 — The Faith of Moses

By faith Moses’ parents hid him without fearing the king’s command, and Moses himself refused to be called the son of Pharaoh’s daughter, choosing affliction with God’s people and “esteeming the reproach of Christ greater riches than the treasures in Egypt” (Hebrews 11:26). By faith he forsook Egypt, enduring “as seeing Him who is invisible” (Hebrews 11:27), and kept the Passover and the sprinkling of blood.

7. Hebrews 11:29–31 — The Red Sea, Jericho, and Rahab

By faith Israel passed through the Red Sea as by dry land, while the Egyptians who attempted it were drowned. By faith the walls of Jericho fell after they were encircled for seven days, and Rahab did not perish with those who did not believe, because she had received the spies with peace.

8. Hebrews 11:32–38 — Faith in Deliverance and in Suffering

Time would fail to tell of Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David, Samuel, and the prophets, who through faith subdued kingdoms and escaped the edge of the sword, while women received their dead raised to life again. Others were tortured, not accepting deliverance, “that they might obtain a better resurrection” (Hebrews 11:35), and still others endured mockings, chains, stoning, the sword, and destitution, people “of whom the world was not worthy” (Hebrews 11:38).

9. Hebrews 11:39–40 — Made Perfect Together

All these obtained a good testimony through faith, yet did not receive the promise in its fullness, even though some had received particular promises along the way (Hebrews 11:17, Hebrews 11:33). God had provided something better for us, the fulfillment He brings to all His people together through Christ, “that they should not be made perfect apart from us” (Hebrews 11:40).
