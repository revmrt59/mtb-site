Hebrews 12:1
Hebrews 12:1
NKJV

Hebrews 12:1 Therefore we also, since we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which so easily ensnares us, and let us run with endurance the race that is set before us,(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 12:1 Therefore, since we are surrounded by such a huge crowd of witnesses to the life of faith, let us strip off every weight that slows us down, especially the sin that so easily trips us up. And let us run with endurance the race God has set before us. (NLT)

Main Idea

Since believers are surrounded by “so great a cloud of witnesses,” the faithful of chapter 11, they are to lay aside every weight and the sin that so easily ensnares and run with endurance the race set before them. The witnesses’ lives testify that God’s promises are worth enduring for, and believers must set aside both sin and anything else that slows them down.

Verse Explanation

Since believers are surrounded by “so great a cloud of witnesses,” the faithful of chapter 11, they are to lay aside every weight and the sin that so easily ensnares and run with endurance the race set before them. The witnesses’ lives testify that God’s promises are worth enduring for, and believers must set aside both sin and anything else that slows them down.

“so great a cloud of witnesses” The lives in chapter 11 testify that God’s promises are worth enduring for. Hebrews pictures believers running a race already set before them. They are to put aside every hindrance, including things that may be legitimate in themselves, and the sin that easily entangles them. Endurance is how the race is run.

NLT

Main Idea

Believers run “looking unto Jesus, the author and finisher of our faith,” who for the joy set before Him endured the cross, despised its shame, and sat down at the right hand of God’s throne. The joy that carried Jesus through the cross included the people He was saving and bringing to glory.

Verse Explanation

Believers run “looking unto Jesus, the author and finisher of our faith,” who for the joy set before Him endured the cross, despised its shame, and sat down at the right hand of God’s throne. The joy that carried Jesus through the cross included the people He was saving and bringing to glory.

“the author and finisher of our faith” Jesus opens the course of faith and brings it to its appointed end. The runner’s attention belongs on Him.

“for the joy that was set before Him” Jesus looked ahead to the people He would redeem and bring to glory (Hebrews 2:10–13 and Isaiah 53:11). With that joy before Him, He endured the cross and treated its shame as incapable of turning Him aside. His seat at God’s right hand shows where that obedience led.

Hebrews 12:17

NLT

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

Afterward, when Esau wanted to inherit the blessing, he was rejected, “for he found no place for repentance, though he sought it diligently with tears.” Esau sought the blessing he had forfeited with tears, but he could not reverse the outcome of his choice.

“he found no place for repentance” Hebrews connects two events in Esau’s life. First he sold his birthright to Jacob for a meal (Genesis 25:29–34). Later, after Isaac had blessed Jacob, Esau begged his father for a blessing and wept (Genesis 27:30–38). Esau had treated his birthright as cheap. Later he earnestly wanted Isaac’s blessing, but he could not undo his earlier choice or reverse the blessing already given. Hebrews lets the irreversible outcome warn hearers about what they can throw away. Those who turn to Christ for mercy can come to the throne of grace (Hebrews 4:16).

Hebrews 12:27 This means that all of creation will be shaken and removed, so that only unshakable things will remain. (NLT)

Main Idea

The words “Yet once more” indicate the removal of what can be shaken, the things that are made, “that the things which cannot be shaken may remain.” God’s final shaking will remove what is temporary and leave what is eternal.

Verse Explanation

The words “Yet once more” indicate the removal of what can be shaken, the things that are made, “that the things which cannot be shaken may remain.” God’s final shaking will remove what is temporary and leave what is eternal.

“the removal of those things that are being shaken” The shaking removes what belongs to the created order insofar as it can be shaken. Its purpose is that what cannot be shaken will remain. The following verse names the lasting kingdom the hearers are already receiving.

Hebrews 12:28 Therefore, since we are receiving a kingdom which cannot be shaken, let us have grace, by which we may serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 12:28 Since we are receiving a Kingdom that is unshakable, let us be thankful and please God by worshiping him with holy fear and awe. (NLT)

Main Idea

“Therefore, since we are receiving a kingdom which cannot be shaken, let us have grace, by which we may serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear.” The gift of an unshakable kingdom calls for grateful, reverent worship.

Verse Explanation

“Therefore, since we are receiving a kingdom which cannot be shaken, let us have grace, by which we may serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear.” The gift of an unshakable kingdom calls for grateful, reverent worship.

“receiving a kingdom which cannot be shaken” God gives His people a share in what will endure. Grace enables them to serve Him acceptably, and the gift calls for gratitude, reverence, and godly fear. The assurance of the kingdom is the positive ground for the response Hebrews asks of them.

NKJV

Hebrews 12:29 For our God is a consuming fire.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 12:29 For our God is a devouring fire. (NLT)

Main Idea

“For our God is a consuming fire.” God’s holiness is the reason His people must worship Him with reverence and godly fear.

Verse Explanation

“For our God is a consuming fire.” God’s holiness is the reason His people must worship Him with reverence and godly fear.

“our God is a consuming fire” The God who grants access at Zion remains utterly holy. The language recalls Deuteronomy 4:24 and connects the warning with the reason for reverent worship. Believers can stand securely in His unshakable kingdom and take the voice of the living God with full seriousness.
