Hebrews 12 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Hebrews 12 turns the witnesses of chapter 11 into a call to run with endurance, looking to Jesus, who endured the cross for the joy set before Him, and to receive hardship as the Father’s loving discipline. It urges the congregation to strengthen the weak and watch over one another, then sets the terror of Sinai beside heavenly Zion and warns them not to refuse Him who speaks, since they are receiving a kingdom that cannot be shaken.

Chapter Sections

1. Hebrews 12:1–3 — Run the Race Looking unto Jesus

Surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, believers are to lay aside every weight and the sin which so easily ensnares and run with endurance the race set before them, “looking unto Jesus, the author and finisher of our faith” (Hebrews 12:2). He endured the cross for the joy set before Him and sat down at the right hand of God’s throne, and considering the hostility He endured keeps believers from growing weary and discouraged.

2. Hebrews 12:4–13 — The Father’s Loving Discipline

The hearers have forgotten the exhortation of Proverbs 3:11–12 that “whom the LORD loves He chastens” (Hebrews 12:6), and their hardship shows that God is dealing with them as sons, training them for their good so that they may share His holiness. Though discipline is painful for the present, it yields the peaceable fruit of righteousness, so they are to strengthen the hands which hang down and the feeble knees and make straight paths so that what is lame may be healed.

3. Hebrews 12:14–17 — Watch That No One Falls Short of Grace

Believers are to pursue peace with all people, and holiness, “without which no one will see the Lord” (Hebrews 12:14), watching that no one falls short of the grace of God and that no root of bitterness springs up and defiles many. No one should be profane like Esau, who sold his birthright for one morsel of food and afterward was rejected when he wanted the blessing, though he sought it diligently with tears.

4. Hebrews 12:18–24 — Not to Sinai, but to Mount Zion

Believers have not come to the mountain of fire, darkness, tempest, trumpet, and fearful voice that made even Moses tremble. They have come “to Mount Zion and to the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem” (Hebrews 12:22), to the church of the firstborn, to God the Judge of all, and to Jesus the Mediator of the new covenant, whose sprinkled blood speaks better things than that of Abel.

5. Hebrews 12:25–29 — A Kingdom That Cannot Be Shaken

“See that you do not refuse Him who speaks” (Hebrews 12:25), for if those who refused Him who spoke on earth did not escape, much less will those who turn away from Him who speaks from heaven. God has promised to shake earth and heaven once more so that what cannot be shaken may remain, and since believers are receiving an unshakable kingdom, they are to serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear, “For our God is a consuming fire” (Hebrews 12:29).
