Hebrews 13

Verse-by-Verse
Hebrews 13:1
Hebrews 13:1
NKJV

Hebrews 13:1 Let brotherly love continue.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 13:1 Keep on loving each other as brothers and sisters. (NLT)

Main Idea

“Let brotherly love continue.” The love believers already have for one another must keep going, and the instructions that follow show what that love looks like.

Verse Explanation

“Let brotherly love continue.” The love believers already have for one another must keep going, and the instructions that follow show what that love looks like.

“Let brotherly love continue” Hebrews calls the congregation to keep practicing the family affection it already knows. The words that follow put that enduring love into concrete acts of care. They show what acceptable worship from Hebrews 12:28 looks like in community life.

None.

Hebrews 13:2
Hebrews 13:2
NKJV

Hebrews 13:2 Do not forget to entertain strangers, for by so doing some have unwittingly entertained angels.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 13:2 Don’t forget to show hospitality to strangers, for some who have done this have entertained angels without realizing it! (NLT)

Main Idea

Believers must not forget to show hospitality to strangers, “for by so doing some have unwittingly entertained angels.” Those who open their homes may not know whom they are welcoming, so the stranger at the door is to be received with love.

Verse Explanation

Believers must not forget to show hospitality to strangers, “for by so doing some have unwittingly entertained angels.” Those who open their homes may not know whom they are welcoming, so the stranger at the door is to be received with love.

“Do not forget to entertain strangers” The object of hospitality is the person the host does not know. Some have welcomed angels without realizing whom they received. That unexpected possibility reminds the hearers how easily a stranger’s significance can be overlooked.

Study Notes

In Genesis 18–19 Abraham and Lot welcome visitors into their households before the full significance of those visitors’ presence becomes clear.

Hebrews 13:3
NKJV

Hebrews 13:3 Remember the prisoners as if chained with them"”those who are mistreated"”since you yourselves are in the body also.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 13:3 Remember those in prison, as if you were there yourself. Remember also those being mistreated, as if you felt their pain in your own bodies. (NLT)

Main Idea

Believers are to remember prisoners “as if chained with them,” and those who are mistreated, since they themselves are also in the body. Being in the body means they share the same human vulnerability and can suffer too, so they are to feel the pain of the imprisoned and the mistreated as if it were their own.

Verse Explanation

Believers are to remember prisoners “as if chained with them,” and those who are mistreated, since they themselves are also in the body. Being in the body means they share the same human vulnerability and can suffer too, so they are to feel the pain of the imprisoned and the mistreated as if it were their own.

“as if chained with them” Remembering prisoners calls for the kind of concern one would have if sharing their chains. The mistreated are included because the hearers also live in bodies that can suffer. The instruction moves from remembering their names to identifying with their real vulnerability.

“The LORD is my helper” Because God has promised to stay, believers can answer in the words of Psalm 118:6. The question about what man can do expresses courage in the face of human opposition. The congregation can speak confidently because the Lord’s help remains theirs even during suffering.

Study Notes

None.

Hebrews 13:7
Hebrews 13:7
NKJV

Hebrews 13:7 Remember those who rule over you, who have spoken the word of God to you, whose faith follow, considering the outcome of their conduct.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 13:7 Remember your leaders who taught you the word of God. Think of all the good that has come from their lives, and follow the example of their faith. (NLT)

Main Idea

The hearers are to remember their leaders, “who have spoken the word of God to you,” most likely leaders from the past, and to consider how their lives turned out and follow their faith. The faithful lives of those leaders give an example to imitate.

Verse Explanation

The hearers are to remember their leaders, “who have spoken the word of God to you,” most likely leaders from the past, and to consider how their lives turned out and follow their faith. The faithful lives of those leaders give an example to imitate.

“considering the outcome of their conduct” The leaders in view likely include former leaders who had spoken God’s word to the congregation. The hearers are to look at how their lives turned out and imitate the faith evident in them. The living Lord these leaders trusted is the reason their faith remains worth following.

Hebrews 13:9
NKJV

Hebrews 13:9 Do not be carried about with various and strange doctrines. For it is good that the heart be established by grace, not with foods which have not profited those who have been occupied with them.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 13:9 So do not be attracted by strange, new ideas. Your strength comes from God’s grace, not from rules about food, which don’t help those who follow them. (NLT)

Main Idea

Believers must not be carried about with various and strange doctrines, for “it is good that the heart be established by grace, not with foods.” A heart is made firm by God’s grace in Christ, not by religious rules about food that never profited those devoted to them.

Verse Explanation

Believers must not be carried about with various and strange doctrines, for “it is good that the heart be established by grace, not with foods.” A heart is made firm by God’s grace in Christ, not by religious rules about food that never profited those devoted to them.

“the heart be established by grace” The church is told to resist strange teachings that could carry it away from Christ. The contrast with foods shows that dietary observances have not profited those occupied with them. The stability the heart needs comes from God’s grace. The next passage directs attention to the sacrifice through which the congregation shares that grace.

Hebrews 13:10–14 — Outside the Camp

Study Notes

None.

Hebrews 13:10
Hebrews 13:10
NKJV

Hebrews 13:10 We have an altar from which those who serve the tabernacle have no right to eat.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 13:10 We have an altar from which the priests in the Tabernacle have no right to eat. (NLT)

Main Idea

“We have an altar from which those who serve the tabernacle have no right to eat.” The altar is Christ’s sacrifice on the cross, and eating from it pictures sharing in the benefits of that sacrifice, which those who rely on the old tabernacle cannot share.

Verse Explanation

“We have an altar from which those who serve the tabernacle have no right to eat.” The altar is Christ’s sacrifice on the cross, and eating from it pictures sharing in the benefits of that sacrifice, which those who rely on the old tabernacle cannot share.

“We have an altar” The altar points to Christ’s once-for-all sacrifice on the cross. Eating is a picture of sharing in its benefits. The people who keep relying on the tabernacle as their way to God have no right to share in the very sacrifice they are passing by. The next verse explains why eating and being outside the camp matter to the comparison.

Hebrews 13:11
Hebrews 13:11
NKJV

Hebrews 13:11 For the bodies of those animals, whose blood is brought into the sanctuary by the high priest for sin, are burned outside the camp.(NKJV)

NLT

Hebrews 13:11 Under the old system, the high priest brought the blood of animals into the Holy Place as a sacrifice for sin, and the bodies of the animals were burned outside the camp. (NLT)

Main Idea

The bodies of the animals whose blood the high priest brought into the sanctuary for sin “are burned outside the camp.” Hebrews draws on the Day of Atonement, when the sin offerings were not eaten but burned outside Israel’s camp.

Verse Explanation

The bodies of the animals whose blood the high priest brought into the sanctuary for sin “are burned outside the camp.” Hebrews draws on the Day of Atonement, when the sin offerings were not eaten but burned outside Israel’s camp.

“are burned outside the camp” The bodies of the sin offerings whose blood the high priest carried into the sanctuary were taken outside and burned. The arrangement connects the blood brought near to God with the body taken out of the camp. Hebrews will use that contrast to explain why Jesus suffered outside the gate.

Study Notes

Leviticus 6:30 forbids eating the sin offering whose blood is brought into the tabernacle. Leviticus 16:27 specifies burning those offerings outside the camp on the Day of Atonement.

“as those who must give account” The leaders currently caring for the congregation watch over people’s souls and must answer to God for that care. The church is asked to obey and yield to their oversight so that they can serve with joy. Grief in carrying that responsibility would harm the congregation itself.
