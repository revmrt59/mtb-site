Hebrews 13 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Hebrews 13 gives the call to serve God acceptably its everyday forms in brotherly love, hospitality, care for prisoners, faithful marriage, contentment, and a heart established by grace in the unchanging Jesus Christ. It calls believers to go to Jesus outside the camp, to offer God praise and good works, and to respond to their leaders, then closes with a prayer to the God of peace, personal news, and greetings.

Chapter Sections

1. Hebrews 13:1–6 — Love, Hospitality, Purity, and Contentment

“Let brotherly love continue” (Hebrews 13:1), shown in hospitality to strangers, remembrance of prisoners and the mistreated, honor for marriage, and conduct without covetousness. Believers can be content because God has said, “I will never leave you nor forsake you” (Hebrews 13:5), so they may boldly say that the LORD is their helper.

2. Hebrews 13:7–9 — Remember Your Leaders; Jesus Christ Is the Same

The hearers are to remember the leaders who spoke the word of God to them, considering the outcome of their conduct and following their faith, for “Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today, and forever” (Hebrews 13:8). They must not be carried about by various and strange doctrines, since the heart is established by grace and not by foods.

3. Hebrews 13:10–14 — Outside the Camp

“We have an altar from which those who serve the tabernacle have no right to eat” (Hebrews 13:10); the altar is Christ’s sacrifice, and eating from it pictures sharing in the benefits of that sacrifice. As the bodies of the sin offerings were burned outside the camp, Jesus suffered outside the gate to sanctify the people with His own blood, so believers are to go forth to Him, bearing His reproach and seeking the city to come.

4. Hebrews 13:15–19 — Sacrifices That Please God

In response to Jesus’ sacrifice, believers are to offer through Him continually the sacrifice of praise, the fruit of their lips, and not forget to do good and to share, “for with such sacrifices God is well pleased” (Hebrews 13:16). The same response shows itself in obeying the leaders who watch out for their souls as those who must give account to God, and in praying for the writer, who longs to be restored to them soon.

5. Hebrews 13:20–21 — The Prayer to the God of Peace

The writer prays that the God of peace, who brought up our Lord Jesus from the dead, “that great Shepherd of the sheep” (Hebrews 13:20), through the blood of the everlasting covenant, will make them complete in every good work to do His will. He asks God to work in them what is well pleasing in His sight through Jesus Christ, to whom be glory forever and ever.

6. Hebrews 13:22–25 — The Word of Exhortation and Final Greetings

The writer appeals to the brethren to bear with “the word of exhortation” (Hebrews 13:22) and tells them that Timothy has been set free and may come with him to see them. He asks them to greet all their leaders and all the saints on his behalf, passes on the greetings of those from Italy, and closes, “Grace be with you all” (Hebrews 13:25).
