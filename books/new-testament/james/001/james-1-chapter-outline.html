James 1 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

James 1 calls the scattered believers to meet trials with joy, to ask God for wisdom with wholehearted trust, and to depend humbly on Him whether lowly or rich, and it sets the birth of sin and death against the Father who “brought us forth by the word of truth” (James 1:18). Those He has given life must receive that word with meekness and show that their hearing is genuine by doing it, through controlled speech, care for the vulnerable, and a life kept unspotted from the world.

Chapter Sections

1. James 1:1 — James Greets the Scattered Twelve Tribes

James, calling himself “a bondservant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ” (James 1:1), greets the twelve tribes scattered abroad. The greeting names the readers whose trials, assemblies, and conduct the rest of the letter addresses.

2. James 1:2–4 — Joy in Trials That Produce Patience

James tells his brethren to count it all joy when they fall into various trials, because the testing of their faith produces patience. He urges them to let patience finish its work so that they may be “perfect and complete, lacking nothing” (James 1:4).

3. James 1:5–8 — Asking God for Wisdom in Faith

Anyone who lacks wisdom should ask God, who gives to all liberally and without reproach. The request must be made in faith, for the one who doubts is like a wave driven and tossed by the wind, “a double-minded man, unstable in all his ways” (James 1:8).

4. James 1:9–11 — The Lowly Brother and the Rich Who Fade

The lowly brother is to glory in his exaltation, and the rich in his humiliation, because the rich person will pass away like a flower of the field. As the sun’s burning heat withers the grass, so the rich man fades away in his pursuits.

5. James 1:12–15 — Enduring Temptation and the Birth of Sin

The man who endures temptation is blessed and, once approved, will receive the crown of life that the Lord has promised to those who love Him. God tempts no one; each person is drawn away by his own desires, and desire “gives birth to sin; and sin, when it is full-grown, brings forth death” (James 1:15).

6. James 1:16–18 — The Father of Lights Gives New Birth

James warns his beloved brethren not to be deceived, for every good and perfect gift comes down from the Father of lights, “with whom there is no variation or shadow of turning” (James 1:17). By His own will the Father brought His people forth by the word of truth, so that they might be a kind of firstfruits of His creatures.

7. James 1:19–21 — Receiving the Implanted Word with Meekness

Every believer must be swift to hear, slow to speak, and slow to wrath, since human anger does not produce the righteousness of God. Laying aside all filthiness and overflow of wickedness, they must “receive with meekness the implanted word, which is able to save your souls” (James 1:21).

8. James 1:22–25 — Doers of the Word, Not Hearers Only

A hearer who does not act on the word deceives himself, like a man who looks at his face in a mirror, goes away, and immediately forgets what he saw. The one who looks into “the perfect law of liberty” (James 1:25) and continues in it as a doer will be blessed in what he does.

9. James 1:26–27 — Religion That Is Pure Before God

Anyone who thinks he is religious but does not bridle his tongue deceives his own heart, and his religion is useless. Religion that is pure and undefiled before God visits orphans and widows in their trouble and keeps oneself unspotted from the world.
