James 2 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

James 2 tests whether the faith of “our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory” (James 2:1) is held without partiality and shows itself in mercy and obedient action. James exposes favoritism toward the rich as a breach of the royal law, warns that judgment is without mercy to the merciless, and shows through the needy brother or sister, Abraham, and Rahab that faith without works is dead.

Chapter Sections

1. James 2:1–7 — Partiality in the Assembly Dishonors the Poor

James forbids his brethren to hold their faith in the Lord of glory with partiality, as when they give the man with gold rings a good seat and tell the poor man to stand. God has chosen the poor of this world to be rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom, yet the church has dishonored the poor man, while the rich oppress the believers and blaspheme the noble name by which they are called.

2. James 2:8–13 — The Royal Law and Mercy at Judgment

Fulfilling the royal law, “You shall love your neighbor as yourself” (James 2:8), rules out partiality, which convicts people as transgressors of the whole law, since the same God gave every command. Believers must speak and act as those who will be judged by the law of liberty, for “judgment is without mercy to the one who has shown no mercy” (James 2:13).

3. James 2:14–17 — A Claimed Faith That Leaves the Needy Unhelped

James asks what profit there is if someone says he has faith but does not have works, and whether that faith can save him. Words of blessing to a brother or sister without clothing or daily food meet no need, and “faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead” (James 2:17).

4. James 2:18–20 — Faith Shown by Works, Not Mere Belief

The exchange presses the demand that faith be shown by works, since even the demons believe that there is one God and tremble. James then asks the foolish man whether he wants to know “that faith without works is dead” (James 2:20).

5. James 2:21–24 — Abraham’s Faith Made Perfect by Works

Abraham was justified by works when he offered Isaac his son on the altar, for faith was working together with his works, and by works faith was made perfect. The Scripture that says “Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness” (James 2:23) was fulfilled, and James concludes that a man is justified by works, and not by faith only.

6. James 2:25–26 — Rahab and the Body Without the Spirit

Rahab the harlot was likewise justified by works when she received the messengers and sent them out another way. James closes the argument with a comparison: “as the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead also” (James 2:26).
