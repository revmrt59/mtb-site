Chapter Study

James 3

Verse-by-Verse
James 3:1
James 3:1
NKJV

James 3:1 My brethren, let not many of you become teachers, knowing that we shall receive a stricter judgment.(NKJV)

NLT

James 3:1 Dear brothers and sisters, not many of you should become teachers in the church, for we who teach will be judged more strictly. (NLT)

Main Idea

James warns, “My brethren, let not many of you become teachers,” because those who teach “shall receive a stricter judgment.” He counts himself among the teachers who will give a heavier account to God at the final judgment, and who even now live under closer accountability, including the Father’s discipline of His children.

Verse Explanation

James warns, “My brethren, let not many of you become teachers,” because those who teach “shall receive a stricter judgment.” He counts himself among the teachers who will give a heavier account to God at the final judgment, and who even now live under closer accountability, including the Father’s discipline of His children.

“My brethren, let not many of you become teachers,”

James begins a long section on speech with a warning about teaching. “Let not many of you become teachers.” In the early church, teaching was a respected role. Some may have wanted it for the honor it brought. James does not forbid teaching. He warns against rushing into it.

“knowing that we shall receive a stricter judgment.”

The reason is accountability. Teachers “shall receive a stricter judgment.” Those who teach God’s word will give a heavier account to God. The future wording points primarily to the final judgment, when the Judge who stands at the door (5:9) will examine every teacher’s work.

Teachers also live under closer accountability now. God disciplines His children as a Father (Hebrews 12:6), and teachers who mislead, divide, or wound others with their words can expect that discipline. Their words also carry greater consequences because they influence many people.

James says “we.” He counts himself among the teachers. He is not lecturing from a safe distance. He knows he will answer to God for what he teaches.

Study Notes

Jesus said, “for every idle word men may speak, they will give account of it in the day of judgment” (Matthew 12:36). Teachers, whose work is done with words, face that accounting with special weight.

The Greek word translated “stricter” means greater or heavier.

James 3:2
James 3:2
NKJV

James 3:2 For we all stumble in many things. If anyone does not stumble in word, he is a perfect man, able also to bridle the whole body.(NKJV)

NLT

James 3:2 Indeed, we all make many mistakes. For if we could control our tongues, we would be perfect and could also control ourselves in every other way. (NLT)

Main Idea

“For we all stumble in many things,” James admits, and anyone who never stumbles in word is “a perfect man, able also to bridle the whole body.” Control of speech is a mark of mature self-control over the whole person.

Verse Explanation

“For we all stumble in many things,” James admits, and anyone who never stumbles in word is “a perfect man, able also to bridle the whole body.” Control of speech is a mark of mature self-control over the whole person.

“For we all stumble in many things.”

James admits a universal truth. “Stumble” means to make a mistake, to fall into sin. No one is exempt, not even James. This admission keeps the warning to teachers humble.

“If anyone does not stumble in word,”

James narrows the focus to speech. Of all the ways people stumble, speech is one of the most common. Words come so quickly that they often go out before a person has thought.

“he is a perfect man, able also to bridle the whole body.”

If someone “does not stumble in word, he is a perfect man.” “Perfect” means mature, as in 1:4. The person who controls his speech has reached a high level of maturity.

Such a person is “able also to bridle the whole body.” If a person can control his tongue, which is the hardest part of himself to control, he can control every other part. Control of speech is a mark of mature self-control over the whole person. James is not claiming that anyone has achieved this completely. He has just said that all stumble.

Study Notes

James used the image of a bridle in 1:26 (“does not bridle his tongue”). He develops it in the next verse with the bits in horses’ mouths.

James 3:3–6 — The Tongue’s Small Size and Great Power

James 3:3
James 3:3
NKJV

James 3:3 Indeed, we put bits in horses' mouths that they may obey us, and we turn their whole body.(NKJV)

NLT

James 3:3 We can make a large horse go wherever we want by means of a small bit in its mouth. (NLT)

Main Idea

People put bits in horses’ mouths to make them obey, and with that small piece of metal they turn the whole animal. The first picture shows how something small can direct something much larger.

Verse Explanation

People put bits in horses’ mouths to make them obey, and with that small piece of metal they turn the whole animal. The first picture shows how something small can direct something much larger.

“Indeed, we put bits in horses’ mouths that they may obey us,”

James gives the first of two pictures. People “put bits in horses’ mouths.” A bit is a small piece of metal placed in a horse’s mouth and attached to the reins. With it a rider can direct a powerful animal. The purpose is “that they may obey us.”

“and we turn their whole body.”

A small thing controls something much larger. The first picture shows how something small can direct something much larger.

Study Notes

None.

James 3:4
James 3:4
NKJV

James 3:4 Look also at ships: although they are so large and are driven by fierce winds, they are turned by a very small rudder wherever the pilot desires.(NKJV)

NLT

James 3:4 And a small rudder makes a huge ship turn wherever the pilot chooses to go, even though the winds are strong. (NLT)

Main Idea

Large ships driven by fierce winds are still turned “by a very small rudder wherever the pilot desires.” The second picture repeats the lesson of the bit: a small instrument sets the course of a great vessel.

Verse Explanation

Large ships driven by fierce winds are still turned “by a very small rudder wherever the pilot desires.” The second picture repeats the lesson of the bit: a small instrument sets the course of a great vessel.

“Look also at ships:”

The second picture is a ship. James asks his readers to picture a sailing vessel.

“although they are so large and are driven by fierce winds,”

Ships “are so large,” and they “are driven by fierce winds.” Powerful forces act on them.

“they are turned by a very small rudder wherever the pilot desires.”

Yet they “are turned by a very small rudder.” The rudder is tiny compared to the ship, but it directs the whole vessel “wherever the pilot desires.” The one who controls the rudder controls the ship. The second picture repeats the lesson of the bit: a small instrument sets the course of a great vessel.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “pilot” refers to the helmsman who steers the ship. The phrase “wherever the pilot desires” uses a word for impulse or intention.

James 3:5
James 3:5
NKJV

James 3:5 Even so the tongue is a little member and boasts great things. See how great a forest a little fire kindles!(NKJV)

NLT

James 3:5 In the same way, the tongue is a small thing that makes grand speeches. But a tiny spark can set a great forest on fire. (NLT)

Main Idea

“Even so the tongue is a little member and boasts great things.” As a small fire can set a great forest ablaze, the tongue’s influence is far out of proportion to its size.

Verse Explanation

“Even so the tongue is a little member and boasts great things.” As a small fire can set a great forest ablaze, the tongue’s influence is far out of proportion to its size.

“Even so the tongue is a little member and boasts great things.”

James applies the pictures. “The tongue is a little member.” It is small, like the bit and the rudder. But it “boasts great things.” It makes big claims and has great effects. Its influence is far out of proportion to its size.

“See how great a forest a little fire kindles!”

James adds a third picture: fire. A single spark can set a whole forest ablaze. Anyone who has seen a wildfire knows how quickly a small flame can spread and how much destruction it can cause. The tongue works the same way. A few careless words can start a blaze that destroys reputations, relationships, and churches.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “forest” can also mean brushwood or thicket. Either way, the picture is of a small fire spreading into a great blaze.

James 3:6
James 3:6
NKJV

James 3:6 And the tongue is a fire, a world of iniquity. The tongue is so set among our members that it defiles the whole body, and sets on fire the course of nature; and it is set on fire by hell.(NKJV)

NLT

James 3:6 And among all the parts of the body, the tongue is a flame of fire. It is a whole world of wickedness, corrupting your entire body. It can set your whole life on fire, for it is set on fire by hell itself. (NLT)

Main Idea

“The tongue is a fire, a world of iniquity” that defiles the whole body and “sets on fire the course of nature,” the whole course of human life, and it is itself set on fire by hell. Speech can spread corruption through every part of a person’s life, and its destructive power draws on evil itself.

Verse Explanation

“The tongue is a fire, a world of iniquity” that defiles the whole body and “sets on fire the course of nature,” the whole course of human life, and it is itself set on fire by hell. Speech can spread corruption through every part of a person’s life, and its destructive power draws on evil itself.

“And the tongue is a fire, a world of iniquity.”

James makes the comparison direct. “The tongue is a fire.” It can spread destruction like flame. It is also “a world of iniquity,” a whole world of evil contained in a small space. Every kind of sin can find expression through speech: lies, slander, boasting, cursing, gossip, and flattery.

“The tongue is so set among our members that it defiles the whole body,”

The tongue “is so set among our members,” placed among the parts of the body, “that it defiles the whole body.” Sinful speech does not stay in the mouth. It stains the whole person.

“and sets on fire the course of nature;”

This difficult phrase most likely means the whole course of human life, from birth to death. Sinful speech can set a person’s entire life ablaze.

“and it is set on fire by hell.”

Finally, the tongue “is set on fire by hell.” The destructive power of evil speech draws on evil itself. Behind the tongue’s worst work stands the realm of evil. This does not mean that every careless word comes directly from hell. It gives the destructive power of speech its full moral seriousness.

Study Notes

The Greek phrase translated “course of nature” reads literally the wheel of birth, or the wheel of existence. It most likely refers to the whole course of human life.

The Greek word translated “hell” is “Gehenna,” the name of the Valley of Hinnom outside Jerusalem. Jesus used it for the place of final judgment (for example, Matthew 5:22). Outside the Gospels, this is its only occurrence in the New Testament.

James 3:7–8 — No One Can Tame the Tongue

James 3:7
James 3:7
NKJV

James 3:7 For every kind of beast and bird, of reptile and creature of the sea, is tamed and has been tamed by mankind.(NKJV)

NLT

James 3:7 People can tame all kinds of animals, birds, reptiles, and fish, (NLT)

Main Idea

Every kind of beast, bird, reptile, and sea creature “is tamed and has been tamed by mankind.” Human beings have shown remarkable power to master the animal world.

Verse Explanation

Every kind of beast, bird, reptile, and sea creature “is tamed and has been tamed by mankind.” Human beings have shown remarkable power to master the animal world.

“For every kind of beast and bird, of reptile and creature of the sea,”

James lists the animal world. The list covers land animals, birds, creeping things, and sea creatures. It echoes the categories of creation over which God gave humanity dominion (Genesis 1:26).

“is tamed and has been tamed by mankind.”

People have shown remarkable ability to master animals. They train horses, tame birds, and handle snakes. The repetition, “is tamed and has been tamed,” stresses that this has been true throughout history and is still true. Human beings have shown remarkable power to master the animal world.

Study Notes

None.

James 3:8
James 3:8
NKJV

James 3:8 But no man can tame the tongue. It is an unruly evil, full of deadly poison.(NKJV)

NLT

James 3:8 but no one can tame the tongue. It is restless and evil, full of deadly poison. (NLT)

Main Idea

Yet “no man can tame the tongue,” which is “an unruly evil, full of deadly poison.” No human being can bring speech fully under control, which leaves believers dependent on the help God supplies.

Verse Explanation

Yet “no man can tame the tongue,” which is “an unruly evil, full of deadly poison.” No human being can bring speech fully under control, which leaves believers dependent on the help God supplies.

“But no man can tame the tongue.”

“But” brings a sharp contrast. Mankind can tame wild animals, “but no man can tame the tongue.” “No man” means no human being. The tongue is beyond human ability to tame completely.

This is a sobering diagnosis, but it is not a reason to give up. James has already called believers to bridle their tongues (1:26). He will soon speak of “the wisdom that is from above” (3:17) and of the God who “gives more grace” (4:6). What human effort cannot do, God can help His people do. The fruit of the Holy Spirit includes self-control (Galatians 5:22–23), which believers exercise in how they speak. No human being can bring speech fully under control, which leaves believers dependent on the help God supplies.

“It is an unruly evil, full of deadly poison.”

The tongue “is an unruly evil.” “Unruly” describes something that will not submit to control. People can break and tame animals, but the tongue resists every human effort to master it. It is also “full of deadly poison.” Like a snake’s venom, the tongue’s words can kill. They can destroy reputations, wound hearts, and ruin lives.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “no man” means no human being. James speaks of human ability, not of what God can do.

The text behind the NKJV reads a word meaning uncontrollable here. Many other manuscripts read a word meaning restless, the same word translated “unstable” in 1:8. That reading would link the tongue with the restlessness of the double-minded person.

James 3:9–12 — Blessing and Cursing from the Same Mouth

James 3:9
James 3:9
NKJV

James 3:9 With it we bless our God and Father, and with it we curse men, who have been made in the similitude of God.(NKJV)

NLT

James 3:9 Sometimes it praises our Lord and Father, and sometimes it curses those who have been made in the image of God. (NLT)

Main Idea

With the tongue “we bless our God and Father,” and with it “we curse men, who have been made in the similitude of God.” The same mouth honors God and attacks the people who bear His likeness.

Verse Explanation

With the tongue “we bless our God and Father,” and with it “we curse men, who have been made in the similitude of God.” The same mouth honors God and attacks the people who bear His likeness.

“With it we bless our God and Father,”

James points to a contradiction. “With it,” with the tongue, “we bless our God and Father.” Believers use their tongues to praise God, to pray, and to sing. This is the tongue at its best.

“and with it we curse men, who have been made in the similitude of God.”

Yet “with it we curse men.” The same tongue that praises God speaks evil against people. To “curse” here means to speak evil against someone, to wish harm on them, to tear them down with words.

The people cursed are “men, who have been made in the similitude of God.” “Similitude” means likeness. Every person bears God’s likeness (Genesis 1:26–27). To curse a person is to attack someone who bears God’s image. The same mouth honors God and attacks the people who bear His likeness.

James says “we.” He includes himself and his readers. This contradiction is not a problem only for outsiders.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “similitude” is the word used in the Greek translation of Genesis 1:26 for God’s “likeness” in which humanity was made.

James 3:10
James 3:10
NKJV

James 3:10 Out of the same mouth proceed blessing and cursing. My brethren, these things ought not to be so.(NKJV)

NLT

James 3:10 And so blessing and cursing come pouring out of the same mouth. Surely, my brothers and sisters, this is not right! (NLT)

Main Idea

“Out of the same mouth proceed blessing and cursing,” and James declares, “My brethren, these things ought not to be so.” Speech that both praises God and curses His image-bearers contradicts itself.

Verse Explanation

“Out of the same mouth proceed blessing and cursing,” and James declares, “My brethren, these things ought not to be so.” Speech that both praises God and curses His image-bearers contradicts itself.

“Out of the same mouth proceed blessing and cursing.”

James restates the contradiction. One mouth produces opposite things.

“My brethren, these things ought not to be so.”

James declares, “these things ought not to be so.” This is not how God’s people should speak. Speech that both praises God and curses His image-bearers contradicts itself. James does not forbid truthful correction. He forbids speech that tears down those God made.

Study Notes

The Greek verb translated “ought” appears only here in the New Testament. It means it is fitting or necessary.

James 3:11
James 3:11
NKJV

James 3:11 Does a spring send forth fresh water and bitter from the same opening?(NKJV)

NLT

James 3:11 Does a spring of water bubble out with both fresh water and bitter water? (NLT)

Main Idea

A spring does not “send forth fresh water and bitter from the same opening.” James uses the consistency of nature to show how unnatural it is for one mouth to pour out both blessing and cursing.

Verse Explanation

A spring does not “send forth fresh water and bitter from the same opening.” James uses the consistency of nature to show how unnatural it is for one mouth to pour out both blessing and cursing.

“Does a spring send forth fresh water and bitter from the same opening?”

James uses an image from nature, and the expected answer is no. A spring produces one kind of water. If its source is fresh, the water is fresh. If its source is bitter, the water is bitter.

In the dry lands of Israel, springs were precious. People knew which ones gave good water and which gave bitter or salty water. A spring that gave both would be unnatural. James uses the consistency of nature to show how unnatural it is for one mouth to pour out both blessing and cursing.

Study Notes

None.

James 3:12
James 3:12
NKJV

James 3:12 Can a fig tree, my brethren, bear olives, or a grapevine bear figs? Thus no spring yields both salt water and fresh.(NKJV)

NLT

James 3:12 Does a fig tree produce olives, or a grapevine produce figs? No, and you can’t draw fresh water from a salty spring. (NLT)

Main Idea

A fig tree cannot bear olives or a grapevine figs, and “no spring yields both salt water and fresh.” What comes out reveals the nature of the source, so a person’s speech shows what fills the heart.

Verse Explanation

A fig tree cannot bear olives or a grapevine figs, and “no spring yields both salt water and fresh.” What comes out reveals the nature of the source, so a person’s speech shows what fills the heart.

“Can a fig tree, my brethren, bear olives, or a grapevine bear figs?”

James adds more images. “Can a fig tree, my brethren, bear olives”? No. “Or a grapevine bear figs?” No. Each plant produces fruit according to its nature. A fig tree produces figs, a grapevine produces grapes.

“Thus no spring yields both salt water and fresh.”

James returns to the spring. What comes out reveals the nature of the source, so a person’s speech shows what fills the heart. Jesus taught the same: “For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks” (Matthew 12:34).

Study Notes

Jesus used similar images: “Do men gather grapes from thornbushes or figs from thistles?” (Matthew 7:16).

James 3:13–18 — Wisdom from Above and Wisdom That Is Earthly

James 3:13
James 3:13
NKJV

James 3:13 Who is wise and understanding among you? Let him show by good conduct that his works are done in the meekness of wisdom.(NKJV)

NLT

James 3:13 If you are wise and understand God’s ways, prove it by living an honorable life, doing good works with the humility that comes from wisdom. (NLT)

Main Idea

Whoever is “wise and understanding” should show it “by good conduct” done “in the meekness of wisdom.” Wisdom is proved by the way a person lives, and true wisdom carries itself with humility.

Verse Explanation

Whoever is “wise and understanding” should show it “by good conduct” done “in the meekness of wisdom.” Wisdom is proved by the way a person lives, and true wisdom carries itself with humility.

“Who is wise and understanding among you?”

James asks a question. This may connect to the teachers of 3:1, who claimed wisdom, but it applies to anyone who considers himself wise.

“Let him show by good conduct that his works are done in the meekness of wisdom.”

The one who claims wisdom must prove it. “Let him show by good conduct.” Wisdom is not proved by clever arguments or impressive knowledge. It is proved by how a person lives.

His works must be “done in the meekness of wisdom.” “Meekness” is humble gentleness, the opposite of arrogance. True wisdom does not show off. It carries itself with humility. Wisdom is proved by the way a person lives, and true wisdom carries itself with humility.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “conduct” refers to a person’s way of life as a whole.

The same word “meekness” appears in 1:21: “receive with meekness the implanted word.”

James 3:14
James 3:14
NKJV

James 3:14 But if you have bitter envy and self-seeking in your hearts, do not boast and lie against the truth.(NKJV)

NLT

James 3:14 But if you are bitterly jealous and there is selfish ambition in your heart, don’t cover up the truth with boasting and lying. (NLT)

Main Idea

If those who claim to be wise harbor “bitter envy and self-seeking” in their hearts, they must not boast and “lie against the truth.” Claiming wisdom while nursing envy and selfish ambition turns the claim itself into a lie.

Verse Explanation

If those who claim to be wise harbor “bitter envy and self-seeking” in their hearts, they must not boast and “lie against the truth.” Claiming wisdom while nursing envy and selfish ambition turns the claim itself into a lie.

“But if you have bitter envy and self-seeking in your hearts,”

James describes false wisdom. Some people have “bitter envy and self-seeking in your hearts.” “Bitter envy” is harsh jealousy, resentment of others’ success or position. “Self-seeking” is selfish ambition, a desire to promote oneself at others’ expense.

These attitudes are “in your hearts.” They may be hidden from others, but they are present inside.

“do not boast and lie against the truth.”

Those with such hearts must “not boast.” They must not claim to be wise. If they do, they “lie against the truth.” Their claim to wisdom is false. Claiming wisdom while nursing envy and selfish ambition turns the claim itself into a lie.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “self-seeking” originally described someone working for hire, and came to mean selfish ambition or rivalry. Paul uses it in Philippians 2:3 (“selfish ambition”).

James 3:15
James 3:15
NKJV

James 3:15 This wisdom does not descend from above, but is earthly, sensual, demonic.(NKJV)

NLT

James 3:15 For jealousy and selfishness are not God’s kind of wisdom. Such things are earthly, unspiritual, and demonic. (NLT)

Main Idea

The so-called wisdom that springs from envy and selfish ambition “does not descend from above, but is earthly, sensual, demonic.” James describes its character and source as belonging to this world, to unspiritual human nature, and to the realm of evil spirits rather than to God, without saying that every case of it involves direct demonic activity.

Verse Explanation

The so-called wisdom that springs from envy and selfish ambition “does not descend from above, but is earthly, sensual, demonic.” James describes its character and source as belonging to this world, to unspiritual human nature, and to the realm of evil spirits rather than to God, without saying that every case of it involves direct demonic activity.

“This wisdom does not descend from above,”

James identifies the source of this false wisdom. It does not come from God. True wisdom comes from God (1:5, 17). This so-called wisdom has a different origin.

“but is earthly, sensual, demonic.”

It “is earthly,” belonging to this world, limited to earthly values. It is “sensual,” belonging to unspiritual human nature, without God’s Spirit. It is “demonic,” coming from or resembling the realm of evil spirits.

The three words move downward: from the world, to fallen human nature, to the demonic. James describes the character and source of this false wisdom. He does not say that every case of envy or selfish ambition involves direct demonic activity.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “sensual” means natural or unspiritual. Paul uses it in 1 Corinthians 2:14 (“the natural man”), and Jude uses it in Jude 1:19 for people “not having the Spirit.”

James 3:16
James 3:16
NKJV

James 3:16 For where envy and self-seeking exist, confusion and every evil thing are there.(NKJV)

NLT

James 3:16 For wherever there is jealousy and selfish ambition, there you will find disorder and evil of every kind. (NLT)

Main Idea

Where envy and self-seeking exist, “confusion and every evil thing are there.” The fruit of false wisdom is disorder and every kind of wrongdoing.

Verse Explanation

Where envy and self-seeking exist, “confusion and every evil thing are there.” The fruit of false wisdom is disorder and every kind of wrongdoing.

“For where envy and self-seeking exist, confusion and every evil thing are there.”

James explains the fruit of false wisdom. “Confusion” means disorder, instability, chaos. “Every evil thing” means every kind of wrongdoing. Envy and selfish ambition do not stay in the heart. They produce conflict, division, and all kinds of evil in a community.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “confusion” is related to the word translated “unstable” in 1:8. It is used in 1 Corinthians 14:33: “God is not the author of confusion but of peace.”

James 3:17
James 3:17
NKJV

James 3:17 But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, willing to yield, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality and without hypocrisy.(NKJV)

NLT

James 3:17 But the wisdom from above is first of all pure. It is also peace loving, gentle at all times, and willing to yield to others. It is full of mercy and the fruit of good deeds. It shows no favoritism and is always sincere. (NLT)

Main Idea

“The wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, willing to yield, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality and without hypocrisy.” Purity comes first, and the qualities that follow show God’s wisdom at work in how a person treats others.

Verse Explanation

“The wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, willing to yield, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality and without hypocrisy.” Purity comes first, and the qualities that follow show God’s wisdom at work in how a person treats others.

“But the wisdom that is from above is first pure,”

James describes true wisdom. “The wisdom that is from above,” wisdom that comes from God, “is first pure.” Purity comes first. True wisdom is morally clean, free from mixed motives and hidden agendas. It is not mixed with envy or self-seeking.

“then peaceable, gentle, willing to yield,”

After purity come qualities that shape how wisdom treats others. It is “peaceable,” loving and promoting peace. It is “gentle,” reasonable and considerate. It is “willing to yield,” open to reason and easy to persuade when persuasion is right.

“full of mercy and good fruits,”

True wisdom is “full of mercy,” compassionate toward those in need. It is full of “good fruits,” producing good actions.

“without partiality and without hypocrisy.”

Finally, true wisdom is “without partiality,” not playing favorites, not divided in its judgments. It is “without hypocrisy,” sincere, genuine, without pretense.

Purity comes first, and the qualities that follow show God’s wisdom at work in how a person treats others. Each quality stands against the envy and self-seeking of 3:14–16.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “without partiality” can also mean undivided or without wavering. It is related to the word translated “doubting” in 1:6.

The Greek word translated “without hypocrisy” is used for “sincere faith” in 1 Timothy 1:5.

James 3:18
James 3:18
NKJV

James 3:18 Now the fruit of righteousness is sown in peace by those who make peace.(NKJV)

NLT

James 3:18 And those who are peacemakers will plant seeds of peace and reap a harvest of righteousness. (NLT)

Main Idea

“Now the fruit of righteousness is sown in peace by those who make peace.” Peacemakers who sow in peace reap a harvest of righteous living.

Verse Explanation

“Now the fruit of righteousness is sown in peace by those who make peace.” Peacemakers who sow in peace reap a harvest of righteous living.

“Now the fruit of righteousness is sown in peace by those who make peace.”

James ends with a picture of harvest. “The fruit of righteousness,” the harvest of right living, “is sown in peace.” Peace is the soil and the climate in which righteousness grows. It is sown “by those who make peace.” Peacemakers sow the seeds, and righteousness is the harvest. Peacemakers who sow in peace reap a harvest of righteous living.

This contrasts with the confusion and evil of 3:16. Envy and selfish ambition produce chaos. Peace produces righteousness. Jesus said, “Blessed are the peacemakers, For they shall be called sons of God” (Matthew 5:9).

Study Notes

The Greek phrase behind “by those who make peace” can be understood in more than one way. The NET main text reads among those who make peace, placing the sowing within a community of peacemakers. The NET notes also allow for those who make peace, meaning peacemakers receive the harvest, and possibly by those who make peace, meaning peacemakers do the sowing. The NKJV uses “by,” presenting the peacemakers as the ones who sow. All three readings connect peacemaking with the harvest of righteousness.
