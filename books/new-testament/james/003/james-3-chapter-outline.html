James 3 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

James 3 turns to teachers and the tongue, showing that a small member can direct and defile the whole course of life and that no human being can tame it. James then tests every claim to wisdom by the conduct it produces, setting the envy and self-seeking of earthly wisdom against “the wisdom that is from above” (James 3:17) and its harvest of righteousness sown in peace.

Chapter Sections

1. James 3:1–2 — Teachers and the Stricter Judgment

James warns that not many should become teachers, since those who teach “shall receive a stricter judgment” (James 3:1). We all stumble in many things, and anyone who does not stumble in word is a perfect man, able also to bridle the whole body.

2. James 3:3–6 — The Tongue’s Small Size and Great Power

Like a bit that turns a horse or a small rudder that steers a large ship, the tongue is a little member that boasts great things. It is a fire that defiles the whole body, “sets on fire the course of nature” (James 3:6), and is itself set on fire by hell.

3. James 3:7–8 — No One Can Tame the Tongue

Mankind has tamed every kind of beast, bird, reptile, and sea creature. Yet “no man can tame the tongue” (James 3:8), for it is an unruly evil, full of deadly poison.

4. James 3:9–12 — Blessing and Cursing from the Same Mouth

With the same tongue people bless God and curse men “who have been made in the similitude of God” (James 3:9), and James declares that these things ought not to be so. A spring does not send forth both fresh and bitter water, and a fig tree cannot bear olives, so one mouth should not yield both blessing and cursing.

5. James 3:13–18 — Wisdom from Above and Wisdom That Is Earthly

The wise person shows good conduct in the meekness of wisdom, but bitter envy and self-seeking reveal a wisdom that is earthly, sensual, and demonic, producing confusion and every evil thing. The wisdom from above is pure, peaceable, gentle, merciful, and without hypocrisy, and “the fruit of righteousness is sown in peace by those who make peace” (James 3:18).
