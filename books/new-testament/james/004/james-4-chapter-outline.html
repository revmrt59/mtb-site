James 4 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

James 4 traces quarrels to desires at war within and calls divided, worldly hearts back to God, who “gives more grace” (James 4:6) to the humble. The chapter closes by forbidding believers to speak evil of one another and take the Lawgiver’s place in condemning a brother, and by confronting merchants who plan as though their lives and profits were in their own hands, instead of saying, “If the Lord wills” (James 4:15).

Chapter Sections

1. James 4:1–3 — Wars and Fights from Desires Within

James traces quarrels to desires at war within people and shows how unchecked craving can drive them even to murder. They lack because they do not ask, and when they do ask, they receive nothing because they want to spend what they request on their pleasures.

2. James 4:4–6 — Friendship with the World and God’s Greater Grace

James calls his readers adulterers and adulteresses, for friendship with the world is enmity with God, and Scripture does not speak in vain when it says, “The Spirit who dwells in us yearns jealously” (James 4:5). Yet God “gives more grace” (James 4:6), resisting the proud and giving grace to the humble.

3. James 4:7–10 — Submitting to God and Drawing Near

James calls believers to submit to God, resist the devil, and draw near to God, who will draw near to them. They must cleanse their hands, purify their hearts, mourn over their sin, and humble themselves in the sight of the Lord, who will lift them up.

4. James 4:11–12 — The One Lawgiver and Judge

Whoever speaks evil of a brother and judges him speaks evil of the law and judges the law, setting himself up as its judge rather than a doer of it. “There is one Lawgiver, who is able to save and to destroy” (James 4:12), so no believer may take His place by condemning another.

5. James 4:13–17 — Planning Under the Lord’s Will

James confronts those who plan a year of buying and selling for profit, though they do not know what tomorrow holds and their life is a vapor that vanishes away. Instead of boasting in their arrogance, they ought to say, “If the Lord wills, we shall live and do this or that” (James 4:15), for anyone who knows to do good and does not do it sins.
