Chapter Study

James 5

Verse-by-Verse
James 5:1
James 5:1
NKJV

James 5:1 Come now, you rich, weep and howl for your miseries that are coming upon you!(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:1 Look here, you rich people: Weep and groan with anguish because of all the terrible troubles ahead of you. (NLT)

Main Idea

“Come now, you rich, weep and howl for your miseries that are coming upon you!” James announces judgment on the rich oppressors whose wrongs he is about to name.

Verse Explanation

“Come now, you rich, weep and howl for your miseries that are coming upon you!” James announces judgment on the rich oppressors whose wrongs he is about to name.

“Come now, you rich,”

James uses the same opening as in 4:13: “Come now.” This time he addresses “you rich.” These are not the rich believers of 1:10. They are the rich oppressors whose wrongs James is about to name: those who cheat their workers, live in luxury, and condemn the innocent.

“weep and howl for your miseries that are coming upon you!”

James tells them to “weep and howl.” “Howl” describes loud, anguished wailing, the cry of people in terrible distress. They should weep “for your miseries that are coming upon you.” Judgment is on its way. James announces judgment on the rich oppressors whose wrongs he is about to name.

Study Notes

The Greek verb translated “howl” appears only here in the New Testament. It is used in the Greek translation of the prophets for wailing at the approach of God’s judgment (for example, Isaiah 13:6).

James 5:2
James 5:2
NKJV

James 5:2 Your riches are corrupted, and your garments are moth-eaten.(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:2 Your wealth is rotting away, and your fine clothes are moth-eaten rags. (NLT)

Main Idea

The riches of the rich oppressors “are corrupted,” and their garments “are moth-eaten.” The wealth they trusted is already decaying and cannot last.

Verse Explanation

The riches of the rich oppressors “are corrupted,” and their garments “are moth-eaten.” The wealth they trusted is already decaying and cannot last.

“Your riches are corrupted,”

James describes the fate of their wealth. Their stored goods, such as grain, oil, and other produce, have rotted. What they hoarded is decaying.

“and your garments are moth-eaten.”

In the ancient world, fine clothing was a form of wealth. The rich stored up garments, and moths ate them. The wealth they trusted is already decaying and cannot last.

Jesus warned, “Do not lay up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust destroy and where thieves break in and steal” (Matthew 6:19).

Study Notes

The Greek verb forms in 5:2–3 describe the decay as already accomplished. James speaks as if the judgment had already begun.

James 5:3
James 5:3
NKJV

James 5:3 Your gold and silver are corroded, and their corrosion will be a witness against you and will eat your flesh like fire. You have heaped up treasure in the last days.(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:3 Your gold and silver are corroded. The very wealth you were counting on will eat away your flesh like fire. This corroded treasure you have hoarded will testify against you on the day of judgment. (NLT)

Main Idea

The rich oppressors’ gold and silver are corroded, and the corrosion “will be a witness against you and will eat your flesh like fire.” They have hoarded treasure “in the last days,” storing up wealth while judgment draws near.

Verse Explanation

The rich oppressors’ gold and silver are corroded, and the corrosion “will be a witness against you and will eat your flesh like fire.” They have hoarded treasure “in the last days,” storing up wealth while judgment draws near.

“Your gold and silver are corroded,”

James continues. Gold and silver do not literally rust, but James uses the image to show that even the most durable wealth will not last.

“and their corrosion will be a witness against you”

The corrosion “will be a witness against you.” Like evidence in a court, the rotting wealth will testify against them. It will prove that they hoarded what they should have used to help others.

“and will eat your flesh like fire.”

The wealth that should have been their security will become the means of their destruction. What they stored up will consume them.

“You have heaped up treasure in the last days.”

James sums up their folly. “The last days” is the period before the Lord’s return. Judgment is near (5:8–9). The rich have spent this critical time piling up wealth for themselves while judgment draws near.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “corroded” describes rust or tarnish. It is related to the word translated “corrosion.”

James 5:4
James 5:4
NKJV

James 5:4 Indeed the wages of the laborers who mowed your fields, which you kept back by fraud, cry out; and the cries of the reapers have reached the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth.(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:4 For listen! Hear the cries of the field workers whom you have cheated of their pay. The cries of those who harvest your fields have reached the ears of the LORD of Heaven’s Armies. (NLT)

Main Idea

The wages the rich oppressors “kept back by fraud” from the laborers who mowed their fields cry out, and the reapers’ cries “have reached the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth.” The Lord of hosts, the commander of heaven’s armies, hears the wronged workers.

Verse Explanation

The wages the rich oppressors “kept back by fraud” from the laborers who mowed their fields cry out, and the reapers’ cries “have reached the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth.” The Lord of hosts, the commander of heaven’s armies, hears the wronged workers.

“Indeed the wages of the laborers who mowed your fields, which you kept back by fraud, cry out;”

James names a specific wrong. The rich have hired “laborers who mowed your fields,” workers who harvested their crops. But the rich “kept back by fraud” the wages these workers earned. They cheated them.

God’s law required employers to pay day laborers promptly, at the end of each day, because the poor depended on those wages to live (Leviticus 19:13; Deuteronomy 24:14–15). The rich broke this law.

The withheld “wages” themselves “cry out.” The money kept back is like a witness calling for justice. Like Abel’s blood crying from the ground (Genesis 4:10), the stolen wages cry out to God.

“and the cries of the reapers have reached the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth.”

The workers themselves cry out too. The rich may ignore the cries of the poor, but God does not. “The Lord of Sabaoth” means the Lord of hosts, the commander of heaven’s armies. The God who hears the cries of the wronged workers has all the power of heaven at His command.

Study Notes

Deuteronomy 24:15 says the employer must pay the worker each day, “lest he cry out against you to the LORD, and it be sin to you.”

“Sabaoth” is a Hebrew word meaning “armies” or “hosts.” The title “Lord of Sabaoth” appears in the New Testament only here and in Romans 9:29.

James 5:5
James 5:5
NKJV

James 5:5 You have lived on the earth in pleasure and luxury; you have fattened your hearts as in a day of slaughter.(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:5 You have spent your years on earth in luxury, satisfying your every desire. You have fattened yourselves for the day of slaughter. (NLT)

Main Idea

James tells the rich, “You have lived on the earth in pleasure and luxury; you have fattened your hearts as in a day of slaughter.” Their self-indulgence has only fattened them like animals for the day of judgment.

Verse Explanation

James tells the rich, “You have lived on the earth in pleasure and luxury; you have fattened your hearts as in a day of slaughter.” Their self-indulgence has only fattened them like animals for the day of judgment.

“You have lived on the earth in pleasure and luxury;”

James describes how the rich have lived. They indulged themselves while their workers went unpaid. They enjoyed comfort and extravagance at the expense of the poor.

“you have fattened your hearts as in a day of slaughter.”

The image comes from farming. Animals were fattened before being slaughtered. The rich have been fattening themselves with luxury, unaware that they are preparing themselves for judgment. Their self-indulgence has only fattened them like animals for the day of judgment.

Study Notes

“A day of slaughter” may refer to a feast day when animals were slaughtered, or to the day of God’s judgment. The prophets use similar language for judgment (Jeremiah 12:3).

James 5:6
James 5:6
NKJV

James 5:6 You have condemned, you have murdered the just; he does not resist you.(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:6 You have condemned and killed innocent people, who do not resist you. (NLT)

Main Idea

The rich have “condemned” and “murdered the just,” who “does not resist you.” The just one here can stand for all the innocent people the rich have harmed, and he does not fight back against their power.

Verse Explanation

The rich have “condemned” and “murdered the just,” who “does not resist you.” The just one here can stand for all the innocent people the rich have harmed, and he does not fight back against their power.

“You have condemned, you have murdered the just;”

James names the worst of their crimes. The rich used their power, including the courts, to condemn innocent people and even put them to death. “The just” is singular, but it most likely represents the innocent people harmed by the rich as a group.

“he does not resist you.”

He does not fight back against their power. He may be unable to resist, or he may choose not to. Either way, he is defenseless before them.

The pattern recalls the suffering of Christ, the Righteous One who was condemned and put to death without resisting (1 Peter 2:21–23). James leaves the identity of the innocent open and keeps the charge focused on the oppressors’ conduct.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “the just” is also used for Jesus as “the Just One” (Acts 7:52). Some interpreters see a reference to Jesus here, though the context points to the innocent poor in general.

James 5:7–11 — Patient Endurance Until the Lord Comes

James 5:7
James 5:7
NKJV

James 5:7 Therefore be patient, brethren, until the coming of the Lord. See how the farmer waits for the precious fruit of the earth, waiting patiently for it until it receives the early and latter rain.(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:7 Dear brothers and sisters, be patient as you wait for the Lord’s return. Consider the farmers who patiently wait for the rains in the fall and in the spring. They eagerly look for the valuable harvest to ripen. (NLT)

Main Idea

Turning to the brethren, James tells them to be patient “until the coming of the Lord.” As a farmer waits patiently for “the early and latter rain” to bring the precious harvest, believers wait for the Lord with patient hope.

Verse Explanation

Turning to the brethren, James tells them to be patient “until the coming of the Lord.” As a farmer waits patiently for “the early and latter rain” to bring the precious harvest, believers wait for the Lord with patient hope.

“Therefore be patient, brethren, until the coming of the Lord.”

James turns from the rich oppressors to the “brethren,” the believers who were suffering under them. “Therefore” draws a conclusion. Because judgment is coming on the oppressors, believers must “be patient.” They must wait “until the coming of the Lord.” The Lord will return, and He will set things right. Believers do not need to take revenge or give up hope.

“See how the farmer waits for the precious fruit of the earth,”

James gives an example. “See how the farmer waits.” A farmer plants his seed and then waits for “the precious fruit of the earth,” the harvest. He cannot rush it. He must wait.

“waiting patiently for it until it receives the early and latter rain.”

The farmer waits “patiently.” He waits “until it receives the early and latter rain.” In Israel, the “early” rain came in the fall, softening the ground for planting. The “latter” rain came in the spring, bringing the crops to maturity. The farmer depended on both, and he could not control either. He waited with hope. As a farmer waits patiently for the early and latter rain to bring the precious harvest, believers wait for the Lord with patient hope.

Study Notes

The early and latter rains are mentioned in God’s promises to Israel (Deuteronomy 11:14; Joel 2:23).

The Greek word translated “coming” is often used in the New Testament for the return of Christ (for example, 1 Thessalonians 4:15).

James 5:8
James 5:8
NKJV

James 5:8 You also be patient. Establish your hearts, for the coming of the Lord is at hand.(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:8 You, too, must be patient. Take courage, for the coming of the Lord is near. (NLT)

Main Idea

“You also be patient. Establish your hearts, for the coming of the Lord is at hand.” The nearness of the Lord’s coming gives believers reason to stand firm.

Verse Explanation

“You also be patient. Establish your hearts, for the coming of the Lord is at hand.” The nearness of the Lord’s coming gives believers reason to stand firm.

“You also be patient.”

James applies the farmer’s example. As the farmer waits for the harvest, believers must wait for the Lord.

“Establish your hearts,”

To establish the heart is to make it firm, steady, and strong. Believers must not let their hearts be shaken by suffering or by the wait.

“for the coming of the Lord is at hand.”

James gives the reason. The Lord’s return is near. This gives urgency and hope. The waiting will not last forever. The nearness of the Lord’s coming gives believers reason to stand firm. The passage gives no timetable, but it calls believers to live in readiness.

Study Notes

None.

James 5:9
James 5:9
NKJV

James 5:9 Do not grumble against one another, brethren, lest you be condemned. Behold, the Judge is standing at the door!(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:9 Don’t grumble about each other, brothers and sisters, or you will be judged. For look — the Judge is standing at the door! (NLT)

Main Idea

“Do not grumble against one another, brethren, lest you be condemned.” Pressure must not turn believers against each other, for “the Judge is standing at the door.”

Verse Explanation

“Do not grumble against one another, brethren, lest you be condemned.” Pressure must not turn believers against each other, for “the Judge is standing at the door.”

“Do not grumble against one another, brethren, lest you be condemned.”

Suffering can make people irritable. Under pressure, believers can turn on each other. James warns, “Do not grumble against one another.” “Grumble” means to groan or complain. Believers must not blame one another or vent their frustration on each other.

The warning carries a consequence: “lest you be condemned.” Grumbling against fellow believers is itself a sin that God will judge.

“Behold, the Judge is standing at the door!”

James gives the reason. The Lord is not far away. He is like a judge standing just outside the door, ready to enter. He will judge the oppressors, and He will also judge believers who grumble against one another. Pressure must not turn believers against each other.

Study Notes

Jesus used similar language of His return: “know that it is near—at the doors!” (Matthew 24:33).

James 5:10
James 5:10
NKJV

James 5:10 My brethren, take the prophets, who spoke in the name of the Lord, as an example of suffering and patience.(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:10 For examples of patience in suffering, dear brothers and sisters, look at the prophets who spoke in the name of the Lord. (NLT)

Main Idea

James points to “the prophets, who spoke in the name of the Lord,” as an example of suffering and patience. Faithful servants of God before them endured hardship while they spoke His word.

Verse Explanation

James points to “the prophets, who spoke in the name of the Lord,” as an example of suffering and patience. Faithful servants of God before them endured hardship while they spoke His word.

“My brethren, take the prophets, who spoke in the name of the Lord, as an example of suffering and patience.”

James points to an example. “Take the prophets” as a model. They “spoke in the name of the Lord.” They were God’s faithful messengers. Yet they suffered. Many were rejected, persecuted, imprisoned, or killed.

They are “an example of suffering and patience.” They suffered for doing God’s will, and they endured it patiently. Faithful servants of God before them endured hardship while they spoke His word. If God’s prophets suffered, believers should not be surprised when they suffer too.

Study Notes

Jesus said, “for so they persecuted the prophets who were before you” (Matthew 5:12).

James 5:11
James 5:11
NKJV

James 5:11 Indeed we count them blessed who endure. You have heard of the perseverance of Job and seen the end intended by the Lord"”that the Lord is very compassionate and merciful.(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:11 We give great honor to those who endure under suffering. For instance, you know about Job, a man of great endurance. You can see how the Lord was kind to him at the end, for the Lord is full of tenderness and mercy. (NLT)

Main Idea

“We count them blessed who endure,” James says, recalling “the perseverance of Job” and the outcome the Lord brought about. The end of Job’s story shows “that the Lord is very compassionate and merciful.”

Verse Explanation

“We count them blessed who endure,” James says, recalling “the perseverance of Job” and the outcome the Lord brought about. The end of Job’s story shows “that the Lord is very compassionate and merciful.”

“Indeed we count them blessed who endure.”

James makes an observation. People look back on those who endured suffering faithfully and consider them blessed. The prophets who suffered are honored now. James returns to the theme of 1:12: “Blessed is the man who endures.”

“You have heard of the perseverance of Job”

James names one example: “the perseverance of Job.” Job lost his wealth, his children, and his health (Job 1–2). He struggled, complained, and questioned God. But he did not curse God or abandon Him. He endured.

“and seen the end intended by the Lord—”

James’s readers know how Job’s story ended. God restored Job’s fortunes and blessed him more than before (Job 42:10–17). The suffering was not the end of the story.

“that the Lord is very compassionate and merciful.”

God did not abandon Job. Through it all, He was compassionate and merciful. The lesson is about God’s character, not a promise that every sufferer will receive the same earthly outcome. Believers can endure because the Lord is full of compassion.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “perseverance” is the same word translated “patience” in 1:3–4.

The Greek word translated “very compassionate” appears only here in the New Testament. It joins a word for “much” with a word for compassion felt deep within.

James 5:12 — Let Your Yes Be Yes

James 5:12
James 5:12
NKJV

James 5:12 But above all, my brethren, do not swear, either by heaven or by earth or with any other oath. But let your "Yes" be "Yes," and your "No," "No," lest you fall into judgment.(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:12 But most of all, my brothers and sisters, never take an oath, by heaven or earth or anything else. Just say a simple yes or no, so that you will not sin and be condemned. (NLT)

Main Idea

“But above all, my brethren, do not swear,” whether by heaven, by earth, or with any other oath. A believer’s everyday “Yes” should mean “Yes” and “No” should mean “No,” so that the plain word is trustworthy and the speaker does not fall into judgment.

Verse Explanation

“But above all, my brethren, do not swear,” whether by heaven, by earth, or with any other oath. A believer’s everyday “Yes” should mean “Yes” and “No” should mean “No,” so that the plain word is trustworthy and the speaker does not fall into judgment.

“But above all, my brethren, do not swear,”

James gives a command he considers especially important: “above all.” “Do not swear.” He is not speaking of foul language. He is speaking of oaths, calling on God or something sacred to guarantee that one’s words are true.

“either by heaven or by earth or with any other oath.”

People used these oaths to make their words sound more convincing. Sometimes they used them to evade responsibility, thinking an oath by heaven or earth was less binding than an oath by God (compare Matthew 23:16–22).

“But let your ‘Yes’ be ‘Yes,’ and your ‘No,’ ‘No,”

Instead, believers must let their “Yes” be “Yes” and their “No” be “No.” Their plain word should be enough. They should be so consistently truthful that oaths are unnecessary. James echoes Jesus’ teaching in Matthew 5:34–37.

“lest you fall into judgment.”

A warning follows. Careless or evasive speech leaves people accountable to God. A believer’s everyday word should be trustworthy.

Study Notes

This verse concerns everyday speech, not solemn legal testimony. Jesus answered when the high priest put Him under oath (Matthew 26:63–64). Paul called God as witness in serious matters (2 Corinthians 1:23; Galatians 1:20). God Himself confirmed His promise with an oath (Hebrews 6:13–17). The same standard of complete truthfulness applies in and out of court.

James 5:13–15 — Prayer for the Suffering and the Sick

James 5:13
James 5:13
NKJV

James 5:13 Is anyone among you suffering? Let him pray. Is anyone cheerful? Let him sing psalms.(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:13 Are any of you suffering hardships? You should pray. Are any of you happy? You should sing praises. (NLT)

Main Idea

Anyone who is suffering should pray, and anyone who is cheerful should “sing psalms.” Every condition of life, hard or joyful, is to be brought to God.

Verse Explanation

Anyone who is suffering should pray, and anyone who is cheerful should “sing psalms.” Every condition of life, hard or joyful, is to be brought to God.

“Is anyone among you suffering? Let him pray.”

James addresses different conditions of life. The word “suffering” covers hardship of any kind. The response is prayer. Bring the hardship to God.

“Is anyone cheerful? Let him sing psalms.”

When life is good, believers should praise God. Joy should turn to worship. Every condition of life, hard or joyful, is to be brought to God.

Study Notes

The Greek verb translated “suffering” is related to the noun translated “suffering” in 5:10. The verb translated “sing psalms” means to sing praise, often with musical accompaniment.

James 5:14
James 5:14
NKJV

James 5:14 Is anyone among you sick? Let him call for the elders of the church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord.(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:14 Are any of you sick? You should call for the elders of the church to come and pray over you, anointing you with oil in the name of the Lord. (NLT)

Main Idea

The sick person should “call for the elders of the church,” who pray over him and anoint him “with oil in the name of the Lord.” The oil is a tangible sign of the Holy Spirit’s power coming upon the sick person to heal through the prayer of faith.

Verse Explanation

The sick person should “call for the elders of the church,” who pray over him and anoint him “with oil in the name of the Lord.” The oil is a tangible sign of the Holy Spirit’s power coming upon the sick person to heal through the prayer of faith.

“Is anyone among you sick?”

James addresses a third condition. The word describes physical illness, weakness of the body.

“Let him call for the elders of the church,”

The “elders” are the spiritual leaders of the local congregation. The sick person takes the initiative and calls them. This is a continuing practice for the church.

“and let them pray over him,”

The elders come and “pray over him.” Prayer is the main thing they do.

“anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord.”

They also anoint him “with oil in the name of the Lord.” Oil had practical uses in the ancient world, including treating wounds (Luke 10:34). In this passage, however, the anointing is symbolic. The oil is a tangible sign of the Holy Spirit’s power coming upon the sick person to heal through the prayer of faith. Jesus’ disciples “anointed with oil many who were sick, and healed them” (Mark 6:13).

The anointing is done “in the name of the Lord.” The power does not lie in the oil or in the elders. It lies in the Lord. Medical care is not set aside, but it is not the point of the oil in this passage.

Study Notes

The Greek verb translated “anointing” describes applying or rubbing on oil. The verb by itself does not decide the purpose of the act. The setting, with prayer in the Lord’s name, gives it its meaning.

The Greek word translated “church” is the usual New Testament word for a congregation of believers. In 2:2 James used the word for “synagogue” for their assembly.

James 5:15
James 5:15
NKJV

James 5:15 And the prayer of faith will save the sick, and the Lord will raise him up. And if he has committed sins, he will be forgiven.(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:15 Such a prayer offered in faith will heal the sick, and the Lord will make you well. And if you have committed any sins, you will be forgiven. (NLT)

Main Idea

“The prayer of faith will save the sick, and the Lord will raise him up.” This is prayer offered in the faith the Holy Spirit can give for a particular healing under the Lord’s will, and the Lord Himself raises the sick person up and, “if he has committed sins, he will be forgiven,” a condition that leaves room for sickness unrelated to the person’s own sin.

Verse Explanation

“The prayer of faith will save the sick, and the Lord will raise him up.” This is prayer offered in the faith the Holy Spirit can give for a particular healing under the Lord’s will, and the Lord Himself raises the sick person up and, “if he has committed sins, he will be forgiven,” a condition that leaves room for sickness unrelated to the person’s own sin.

“And the prayer of faith will save the sick,”

James makes a promise. “The prayer of faith” is prayer offered in trust that God will act. It is faith the Holy Spirit can give for a particular healing (compare 1 Corinthians 12:9). Such prayer “will save the sick.” In this setting, “save” means to heal, to restore to health.

“and the Lord will raise him up.”

The Lord heals. The elders pray, but the Lord acts. “Raise him up” means to raise him from his sickbed, to restore him to health.

This promise must be held together with the Lord’s will (4:15). Believers pray expectantly, trusting the Lord to heal, while submitting to His will as Jesus did (Luke 22:42). When healing does not come, the outcome gives no grounds to blame the sick person or their family. The Spirit may withhold the specific faith for an immediate healing while the church continues to pray and care for the person. The believer’s wider hope remains the resurrection, when the body will be fully and finally healed (Romans 8:23).

“And if he has committed sins, he will be forgiven.”

James adds a second promise. The word “if” makes it conditional: where sins have been committed, the sick person will be forgiven.

Separately, some illness can be connected with sin (compare 1 Corinthians 11:30). Recognizing such a connection calls for discernment, and it never gives grounds for blaming the sick. Sickness is not always caused by sin (John 9:1–3). Scripture shows sickness arising from different sources. God can use sickness to discipline (2 Chronicles 26:16–21), and Satan can be a source of affliction (Job 2:7; Luke 13:16). Whatever the cause, it is the Lord who raises the sick.

Study Notes

The Greek verbs translated “save” and “raise up” can describe healing and getting up from illness. The same verbs are used elsewhere for salvation and resurrection (for example, Ephesians 2:8; 1 Corinthians 15:15).

James 5:16–18 — Confession, Prayer, and Elijah’s Example

James 5:16
James 5:16
NKJV

James 5:16 Confess your trespasses to one another, and pray for one another, that you may be healed. The effective, fervent prayer of a righteous man avails much.(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:16 Confess your sins to each other and pray for each other so that you may be healed. The earnest prayer of a righteous person has great power and produces wonderful results. (NLT)

Main Idea

Believers are to confess their trespasses to one another and “pray for one another, that you may be healed.” “The effective, fervent prayer of a righteous man avails much,” because the Lord answers such prayer.

Verse Explanation

Believers are to confess their trespasses to one another and “pray for one another, that you may be healed.” “The effective, fervent prayer of a righteous man avails much,” because the Lord answers such prayer.

“Confess your trespasses to one another,”

James broadens the instruction. Believers should admit their sins to each other. This confession is mutual, among fellow believers, not only to elders. Where the Spirit exposes sin connected with suffering, honest confession deals with what stands in the way.

“and pray for one another, that you may be healed.”

Confession and prayer go together. Healing comes through the Lord’s answer to the prayers of His people.

Such confession calls for humility, appropriate confidentiality, and ongoing care.

“The effective, fervent prayer of a righteous man avails much.”

James gives encouragement. “Righteous” describes a person who lives rightly before God, not a sinless person. The prayer of such a person is “effective” and “fervent,” earnest and powerful. It “avails much.” It accomplishes a great deal, because the Lord answers such prayer.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “effective, fervent” is a single word describing prayer at work, energetically active. Interpreters debate whether it describes the prayer itself or the power it has when God answers it.

James 5:17
James 5:17
NKJV

James 5:17 Elijah was a man with a nature like ours, and he prayed earnestly that it would not rain; and it did not rain on the land for three years and six months.(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:17 Elijah was as human as we are, and yet when he prayed earnestly that no rain would fall, none fell for three and a half years! (NLT)

Main Idea

“Elijah was a man with a nature like ours,” yet when he prayed earnestly that it would not rain, no rain fell on the land for three years and six months. His example shows that God answers the earnest prayer of an ordinary person.

Verse Explanation

“Elijah was a man with a nature like ours,” yet when he prayed earnestly that it would not rain, no rain fell on the land for three years and six months. His example shows that God answers the earnest prayer of an ordinary person.

“Elijah was a man with a nature like ours,”

James gives an example of powerful prayer: “Elijah.” He was one of the greatest prophets in Israel’s history. Yet James stresses that he “was a man with a nature like ours.” He was not superhuman. He had the same weaknesses as everyone else. After his great victory on Mount Carmel, he ran in fear and asked to die (1 Kings 19:3–4).

“and he prayed earnestly that it would not rain;”

The account in 1 Kings 17:1 records Elijah announcing the drought. James tells us that prayer lay behind that announcement.

“and it did not rain on the land for three years and six months.”

God answered. Jesus gave the same length of time (Luke 4:25). An ordinary man prayed, and God shut the heavens. His example shows that God answers the earnest prayer of an ordinary person.

Study Notes

The phrase “prayed earnestly” reads literally prayed with prayer, a Hebrew way of expressing intensity.

1 Kings 18:1 says the rain came “in the third year.” One common explanation is that James and Luke count from the beginning of the dry season before Elijah’s announcement.

James 5:18
James 5:18
NKJV

James 5:18 And he prayed again, and the heaven gave rain, and the earth produced its fruit.(NKJV)

“And he prayed again, and the heaven gave rain, and the earth produced its fruit.”

Elijah “prayed again.” On Mount Carmel, he bowed down with his face between his knees and prayed until a cloud appeared (1 Kings 18:42–45). God answered again: “the heaven gave rain, and the earth produced its fruit.” The drought ended, and life returned to the land. God’s answer to his second prayer ended the drought and brought life back to the land.

Study Notes

The phrase “produced its fruit” echoes the farmer waiting for “the precious fruit of the earth” in 5:7.

James 5:19–20 — Turning Back the Wanderer

James 5:19
James 5:19
NKJV

James 5:19 Brethren, if anyone among you wanders from the truth, and someone turns him back,(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:19 My dear brothers and sisters, if someone among you wanders away from the truth and is brought back, (NLT)

Main Idea

James considers the case in which “anyone among you wanders from the truth, and someone turns him back.” A believer who strays from God’s truth can be brought back through the care of another believer.

Verse Explanation

James considers the case in which “anyone among you wanders from the truth, and someone turns him back.” A believer who strays from God’s truth can be brought back through the care of another believer.

“Brethren, if anyone among you wanders from the truth,”

James ends the letter with a final concern. He describes “anyone among you” who “wanders from the truth.” “Among you” points to someone within the community of believers. The person is best understood as a genuine believer who has strayed. To wander means to go astray, to be led off course. The truth includes both right belief and right living. A person can wander in either.

“and someone turns him back,”

Another believer goes after the one who has wandered and helps him return. James does not say this is the job of elders only. Any believer can do it. A believer who strays from God’s truth can be brought back through the care of another believer.

Study Notes

The Greek verb translated “wanders” means to be led astray or deceived. It is the same verb used in 1:16: “Do not be deceived.”

James 5:20
James 5:20
NKJV

James 5:20 let him know that he who turns a sinner from the error of his way will save a soul from death and cover a multitude of sins.(NKJV)

NLT

James 5:20 you can be sure that whoever brings the sinner back from wandering will save that person from death and bring about the forgiveness of many sins. (NLT)

Main Idea

The one who turns a sinner “from the error of his way will save a soul from death and cover a multitude of sins.” Turning a wandering believer back interrupts sin’s destructive course, which can end even in physical death, and brings the returning wanderer to God’s forgiveness.

Verse Explanation

The one who turns a sinner “from the error of his way will save a soul from death and cover a multitude of sins.” Turning a wandering believer back interrupts sin’s destructive course, which can end even in physical death, and brings the returning wanderer to God’s forgiveness.

“let him know that he who turns a sinner from the error of his way”

The one who turns a wanderer back should “know” what he has done. He “turns a sinner from the error of his way.” He has helped someone off a path of sin.

“will save a soul from death”

“Soul” here means a person. The death in view is the destructive course of sin that James described in 1:15. Sin begins its damaging work as soon as a person gives it room: joy fades, relationships suffer, spiritual vitality drains, integrity erodes. Left unchecked, that course can end even in premature physical death (compare 1 Corinthians 11:30). Turning the wanderer back interrupts that course.

“and cover a multitude of sins.”

The sins covered are chiefly the wanderer’s. When he returns, God forgives him through Christ (1 John 1:9). Turning back also stops the sins that would otherwise follow.

The church has a part in this covering. Love addresses wrongdoing with truth, but it also protects a repentant person from gossip and needless exposure. Proverbs says, “love covers all sins” (Proverbs 10:12), and Peter writes, “love will cover a multitude of sins” (1 Peter 4:8). Love also supports accountability and protects those who have been harmed.

James ends his letter abruptly, without a closing greeting. His last words call believers to care for those who stray.

Study Notes

Interpreters understand the death in this verse in several ways. Some take it as premature physical death specifically as God’s discipline. Some take it as eternal death for a person whose profession of faith was never genuine. Others take it as eternal death that a genuine believer could incur by turning away. James’s own description of sin’s course from desire to death in 1:14–15 supports reading this verse as the cumulative damage sin itself brings, which can include physical death.

The phrase “a multitude of sins” matches the wording of 1 Peter 4:8. Peter echoes Proverbs 10:12 rather than quoting it; the NKJV of Proverbs reads “love covers all sins.”
