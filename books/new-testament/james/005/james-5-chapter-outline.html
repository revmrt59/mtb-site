James 5 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

James 5 announces judgment on rich oppressors whose withheld wages cry out to “the Lord of Sabaoth” (James 5:4), that is, the Lord of hosts, who hears the workers, then turns to the brethren with a call to patient endurance until the coming of the Lord. The letter closes with instruction on truthful speech, prayer in suffering and sickness, mutual confession, and turning back anyone who wanders from the truth.

Chapter Sections

1. James 5:1–6 — Judgment on Rich Oppressors

James tells the rich to weep and howl for the miseries coming upon them, for their hoarded riches are corrupted and the corrosion of their gold and silver will witness against them. They kept back the laborers’ wages by fraud, lived in pleasure and luxury, and condemned and murdered the just, who does not resist them.

2. James 5:7–11 — Patient Endurance Until the Lord Comes

Like a farmer waiting for the early and latter rain, the brethren must be patient and establish their hearts, “for the coming of the Lord is at hand” (James 5:8), and they must not grumble against one another. The prophets and Job show that those who endure are blessed, and the end of Job’s story reveals “that the Lord is very compassionate and merciful” (James 5:11).

3. James 5:12 — Let Your Yes Be Yes

James calls believers to make their everyday word trustworthy without using oaths to bolster a doubtful claim or leave themselves a way out. Let your “Yes” be “Yes,” and your “No,” “No,” lest you fall into judgment (James 5:12).

4. James 5:13–15 — Prayer for the Suffering and the Sick

The suffering should pray and the cheerful should sing psalms, while the sick should call for the elders of the church, who pray over them and anoint them with oil in the name of the Lord. “The prayer of faith will save the sick, and the Lord will raise him up” (James 5:15), and any sins the sick person has committed will be forgiven.

5. James 5:16–18 — Confession, Prayer, and Elijah’s Example

Believers are to confess their trespasses to one another and pray for one another so that they may be healed. “The effective, fervent prayer of a righteous man avails much” (James 5:16), as Elijah shows: he was a man with a nature like ours, yet when he prayed earnestly it did not rain for three years and six months, and when he prayed again the heaven gave rain.

6. James 5:19–20 — Turning Back the Wanderer

If anyone among the believers wanders from the truth and someone turns him back, the one who helps him should know what that rescue accomplishes. Turning a sinner from the error of his way “will save a soul from death and cover a multitude of sins” (James 5:20).
