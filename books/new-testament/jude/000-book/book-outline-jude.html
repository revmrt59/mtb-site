Jude Book Outline

Revision 2 • October 23, 2026

Major Movements

I. Called, Kept, and Urged to Contend (Jude 1:1–4)

Jude, a bondservant of Jesus Christ, greets those called, sanctified by God the Father, and preserved in Jesus Christ. He sets aside his intended letter about their common salvation to urge them to contend earnestly for the entrusted faith, because certain men have crept in unnoticed who turn grace into lewdness and deny Christ’s lordship. This movement states the letter’s occasion and purpose; everything that follows develops it.

II. The Certain Judgment of the Ungodly Intruders (Jude 1:5–16)

Jude reminds his readers of what they already know: God judged unbelieving Israel after the exodus, the angels who did not keep their proper domain, and Sodom and Gomorrah. He then exposes the intruders as self-willed, irreverent, greedy, and empty, walking in the way of Cain, Balaam, and Korah, and confirms their end with Enoch’s prophecy of the Lord coming to judge the ungodly. Their presence in the fellowship does not shield them from the reckoning God has long announced.

III. But You, Beloved: Remain in God’s Love and Rescue Others (Jude 1:17–23)

Jude turns from the intruders to the believers. They are to remember the apostles’ warning about divisive mockers who lack the Spirit, and to build themselves up on their most holy faith, pray in the Holy Spirit, keep themselves in the love of God, and look for the mercy of the Lord Jesus Christ unto eternal life. Contending for the faith takes the shape of compassion and urgent rescue toward those endangered by error, while hating even the garment defiled by the flesh.

IV. Glory to the God Who Is Able to Keep (Jude 1:24–25)

Jude ends in worship. God is able to keep His people from stumbling and to present them faultless before the presence of His glory with exceeding joy; to God our Savior belong glory, majesty, dominion, and power, both now and forever.
