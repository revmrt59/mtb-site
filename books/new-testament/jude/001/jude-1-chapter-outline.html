Jude 1 Chapter Outline

Revision 1 • APPROVED • October 23, 2026

Chapter Focus

Jude’s single chapter is the whole letter. After greeting God’s called and kept people, Jude urges them to contend for the entrusted faith, exposes the certain judgment of the ungodly intruders through biblical examples and prophecy, turns to the beloved with the shape of faithful response and merciful rescue, and closes in praise of the God who is able to keep them.

Chapter Sections

1. Jude 1:1–2 — Jude Greets the Called and Kept

Jude, a bondservant of Jesus Christ and brother of James, writes to those who are called, sanctified by God the Father, and preserved in Jesus Christ. He prays that mercy, peace, and love be multiplied to them.

2. Jude 1:3–4 — The Urgent Call to Contend for the Faith

Jude sets aside the letter he intended about their common salvation and exhorts his readers “to contend earnestly for the faith which was once for all delivered to the saints.” The reason is that ungodly men have crept in unnoticed, turning God’s grace into lewdness and denying the Lord Jesus Christ.

3. Jude 1:5–7 — Three Reminders of God’s Judgment

Jude reminds his readers of the Lord’s destruction of unbelievers after the exodus, of the angels who left their proper domain and are kept for judgment, and of Sodom and Gomorrah, set forth as an example. Together these judgments show that past privilege or position gives no shelter to persistent rebellion.

4. Jude 1:8–10 — Dreamers Who Revile What They Do Not Know

The intruders defile the flesh, reject authority, and speak evil of dignitaries. Michael the archangel would not bring a reviling accusation even against the devil but appealed to the Lord, while these men speak evil of what they do not know and corrupt themselves like brute beasts.

5. Jude 1:11–13 — Woe to Those Who Follow Cain, Balaam, and Korah

Jude pronounces woe on the intruders as followers of Cain, Balaam, and Korah. He then exposes their emptiness with images of spots in the love feasts, clouds without water, fruitless trees, raging waves, and wandering stars for whom darkness is reserved.

6. Jude 1:14–16 — Enoch’s Prophecy of the Lord’s Coming Judgment

Jude applies Enoch’s prophecy that the Lord comes with ten thousands of His saints to convict the ungodly of their deeds and their harsh words against Him. He then describes the intruders as grumblers and flatterers who walk according to their own lusts.

7. Jude 1:17–19 — Remember the Apostles’ Warning

With “But you, beloved” Jude turns to his readers and calls them to remember the apostles’ warning that mockers would come in the last time. Such people cause divisions and do not have the Spirit.

8. Jude 1:20–23 — Keep Yourselves in the Love of God and Rescue Others

Believers are to build themselves up on their most holy faith, pray in the Holy Spirit, keep themselves in the love of God, and look for the mercy of the Lord Jesus Christ. That faithfulness reaches outward in discerning compassion and urgent rescue, while hating even the garment defiled by the flesh.

9. Jude 1:24–25 — Glory to the God Who Is Able to Keep

Jude closes by praising the God who is able to keep His people from stumbling and present them faultless before the presence of His glory with exceeding joy. To God our Savior belong glory and majesty, dominion and power, now and forever.
