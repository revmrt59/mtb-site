Matthew Book Outline

Revision 3 November 17, 2026

Major Movements

I. The Promised King Comes (Matthew 1:1–4:16)

Matthew opens with “the Son of David, the Son of Abraham” (1:1), presenting Jesus as the heir of David’s royal line and as the Seed through whom God promised Abraham that all the nations would be blessed (Genesis 22:18). The angel announces His saving mission, “He will save His people from their sins” (1:21), and Matthew identifies His virgin birth as the fulfillment of Isaiah’s word about the Son called Immanuel, “God with us” (1:22–23). The Father declares at the Jordan, “This is My beloved Son” (3:17); in the wilderness Jesus answers each of the devil’s tests from Deuteronomy as the faithful Son; and His move to Capernaum fulfills Isaiah’s promise of “a great light” for a people in darkness (4:12–16).

II. The King Announces the Kingdom in Word and Deed (Matthew 4:17–11:1)

“From that time Jesus began to preach” (4:17) that the kingdom of heaven is at hand, and He calls fishermen to follow Him. In the Sermon on the Mount (5:1–7:29) Jesus declares that He came to fulfill the Law and the Prophets (5:17) and calls for a righteousness that reaches the heart, and the crowds are astonished because “He taught them as one having authority” (7:29); chapters 8–9 show the same authority healing the sick, stilling the sea, forgiving sins, and calling sinners. Moved with compassion for crowds “like sheep having no shepherd” (9:36), He sends the Twelve to “the lost sheep of the house of Israel” (10:6) with His message and power and prepares them for the cost of witness (10:16–42).

III. The King Meets Rejection and Recognition (Matthew 11:2–16:20)

John’s question from prison, “Are You the Coming One, or do we look for another?” (11:3), opens a movement in which Jesus’ words and works divide those who hear Him. Unrepentant towns and the Pharisees’ plot to destroy Him (12:14) stand against the “babes” to whom the Father reveals the truth (11:25), and the parables of chapter 13 explain these different responses to the kingdom word. Rejection at Nazareth, the death of John, two miraculous feedings, and repeated clashes with Israel’s leaders lead to Peter’s confession, “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God” (16:16); Jesus’ blessing, His promise to build His church, and His charge to tell no one close the scene (16:17–20).

IV. The King Teaches the Way of the Cross (Matthew 16:21–20:34)

“From that time Jesus began to show to His disciples that He must go to Jerusalem” (16:21) to suffer, be killed, and be raised, and He calls every follower to “deny himself, and take up his cross” (16:24). At the transfiguration the Father identifies Jesus as “My beloved Son” and commands, “Hear Him!” (17:5), and the teaching of chapter 18 directs the community that lives under the King toward humility, care for the little ones, restorative correction, and forgiveness from the heart. On the road to Jerusalem His teaching on marriage, children, wealth, and reward overturns ordinary measures of greatness, for “the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give His life a ransom for many” (20:28).

V. The King Confronts Jerusalem (Matthew 21:1–23:39)

Jesus enters Jerusalem as the King who comes “Lowly, and sitting on a donkey” (21:5), cleanses the temple, and curses the fruitless fig tree. When the chief priests and elders challenge His authority, He answers with parables of two sons, wicked vinedressers, and a wedding feast, warning the leaders who reject Him that “the kingdom of God will be taken from you and given to a nation bearing the fruits of it” (21:43), and He answers every test until no one dares to question Him further (22:46). His woes against the scribes and Pharisees end in lament over the city that refused His care: “See! Your house is left to you desolate” (23:38).

VI. The King Promises His Return and Calls for Readiness (Matthew 24:1–25:46)

As He leaves the temple, Jesus foretells that “not one stone shall be left here upon another” (24:2), a warning fulfilled in Jerusalem’s fall that also foreshadows the final judgment, and on the Mount of Olives He answers His disciples’ questions about the temple, His coming, and the end of the age (24:3). He warns of deception and persecution, promises that the gospel will be preached to all the nations (24:14), and describes the coming of the Son of Man, read primarily as future, while leaving its precise timing in relation to Jerusalem’s fall open (24:29–31); since no one knows the day or the hour, His disciples must watch (24:36–44). The parables of the faithful servant, the ten virgins, and the talents show readiness as faithful service and inward preparation, and when the King comes in His glory, the nations’ treatment of “the least of these My brethren” (25:40), the vulnerable disciples sent in His name, reveals their response to Him and ends in “everlasting punishment” or “eternal life” (25:46).

VII. The King Gives His Life and Rises with All Authority (Matthew 26:1–28:20)

Two days before the Passover, Jesus announces that “the Son of Man will be delivered up to be crucified” (26:2); He gives His disciples the covenant meal in which His blood is “shed for many for the remission of sins” (26:28) and yields to the Father’s will in Gethsemane. Betrayed, denied, condemned, and mocked as “THE KING OF THE JEWS” (27:37), He cries out in real anguish and forsakenness as He bears judgment, in the opening words of Psalm 22, a psalm that moves on to trust and vindication (27:46); the centurion confesses, “Truly this was the Son of God!” (27:54). Raised on the third day, the King who holds all authority in heaven and on earth (28:18) sends His disciples to make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them and teaching them to observe all He commanded, with the promise, “I am with you always” (28:18–20).
