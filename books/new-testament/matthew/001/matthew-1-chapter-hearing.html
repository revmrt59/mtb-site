Matthew 1:1
NKJV

Matthew 1:1 The book of the genealogy of Jesus Christ, the Son of David, the Son of Abraham:(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 1:1 This is a record of the ancestors of Jesus the Messiah, a descendant of David and of Abraham : (NLT)

Understand: What the verse says

Matthew opens his Gospel by naming Jesus as the Christ, the Son of David, and the Son of Abraham. Jesus is the promised King who will sit on David's throne and the promised descendant through whom God will bless all the nations of the earth.

Observe: Something to notice

Matthew names this genealogy for the One at its end. Old Testament genealogies took the name of the ancestor who began the line, such as “the genealogy of Adam” (Genesis 5:1), and Matthew calls this one “the genealogy of Jesus Christ.” He gives Him two titles that belong together. “Son of David” says He has the right to rule (2 Samuel 7:12–16). “Son of Abraham” says the blessing promised to all the families of the earth comes through Him (Genesis 12:3), and the Gospel ends with Jesus sending His disciples to “all the nations” (28:19).

Hear: What God may be speaking to me

The One who has authority over me is the same One through whom God's blessing comes. When Jesus asks for my whole life, He asks as the King who came to bless.

And as someone who was once far off from God's promises, I was already in view when God told Abraham that all the families of the earth would be blessed through him. From the opening verse of Matthew, God's heart is reaching beyond Israel to the nations and ultimately to people like me.

Matthew 1:2
NKJV

Matthew 1:2 Abraham begot Isaac, Isaac begot Jacob, and Jacob begot Judah and his brothers.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 1:2 Abraham was the father of Isaac.Isaac was the father of Jacob.Jacob was the father of Judah and his brothers. (NLT)

Understand: What the verse says

The promise God gave Abraham passes through Isaac and Jacob to Judah, the son from whom Israel's kings would come (Genesis 49:10). By adding “and his brothers,” Matthew keeps the whole family of Israel in view as the line moves toward the Messiah.

Observation 1

Observe: Something to notice

From the beginning, the line leading to Christ did not follow what people would have expected. Isaac's birth was humanly impossible. Jacob was not the firstborn. Judah was not Jacob's oldest son. The line was in accordance to God's promise and by His choice. Paul points to Isaac and Jacob when he makes this same point (Romans 9:6–13).

Hear: What God may be speaking to me

Others may not expect me to be God’s choice. I may not even expect myself to be the one God would choose. My calling, gifts, and usefulness are root in His calling, not my natural ability, position, or background.

Observation 2

Observe: Something to notice

Neither man was the same at the end of his life as he had been earlier. God worked on both of them over many years. Jacob was humbled through his encounters with God. Judah, who had once been willing to sacrifice his brother for his own interests, became willing to sacrifice himself for his brother. The genealogy lists men God used while He was also dealing with their character.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me

God does not require me to have everything right before He begins to use me. Jacob and Judah both had serious flaws, and God changed them over many years. He is still doing that work in me.
