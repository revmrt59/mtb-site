Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 1
Main Idea:

Matthew introduces Jesus Christ as the Son of David and the Son of Abraham through a carefully arranged genealogy, then tells of His conception by the Holy Spirit and His naming as the Savior who is Immanuel, “God with us.”

Setting:

Matthew 1 opens the New Testament and the first movement of Matthew’s Gospel, The Promised King Comes (1:1–4:16), which sets out who Jesus is before He begins to preach.

Chapter Outline:

  • Matthew 1:1–17 — The Genealogy of Jesus Christ

  • Matthew 1:18–25 — The Birth of Jesus Christ

Key Verse:

“And she will bring forth a Son, and you shall call His name JESUS, for He will save His people from their sins.” (Matthew 1:21, NKJV)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Matthew’s first sentence tells the reader what the whole Gospel will prove: Jesus Christ is “the Son of David, the Son of Abraham” (1:1). David points to the throne God promised would last forever; Abraham points to the promise that all nations would be blessed through his family. Before Jesus says a word, Matthew places Him at the meeting point of both promises.

The genealogy that follows is history arranged with purpose. Matthew sets it in three groups of fourteen generations, from Abraham to David, from David to the deportation to Babylon, and from Babylon to the Christ (1:17). The line rises to a king, falls into exile and then waits, and the waiting ends in Jesus. Along the way Matthew names women an ancient record could easily have left out: Tamar, Rahab, Ruth and “her who had been the wife of Uriah” (1:6). Their stories hold scandal, grief, outsiders brought in, and the surprising providence of God. Then the pattern breaks. After the long chain of “begot,” Matthew writes that Joseph was “the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus” (1:16). Joseph gives Jesus his royal line; he does not give Him life.

The birth account explains why. Mary is “found with child of the Holy Spirit” (1:18). Joseph, “a just man” (1:19), plans to end the betrothal quietly, until God speaks to him through an angel in a dream. He obeys, takes Mary home and names the child Jesus, “for He will save His people from their sins” (1:21). Matthew sees in all this the fulfillment of Isaiah’s word that the virgin would bear a Son called Immanuel, “God with us” (1:23). The Savior of sinners is God Himself come to be with His people, and Matthew’s Gospel will end with the same promise, “I am with you always” (Matthew 28:20).

Chapter Sections

Matthew 1:1–17 — The Genealogy of Jesus Christ

Matthew traces Jesus’ ancestry from Abraham through David and the exile to Joseph, arranging it in three groups of fourteen so that Israel’s whole history leads to the Christ. The names include kings and ordinary men, faithful and unfaithful, and four women before Mary whose stories show God’s grace working through unlikely people.

Matthew 1:18–25 — The Birth of Jesus Christ

Mary is found with child by the Holy Spirit before she and Joseph come together, and Joseph, a just man, is redirected by an angel in a dream to take her as his wife. He obeys and names the child Jesus, the Savior from sins, in fulfillment of the prophecy of Immanuel, “God with us.”

Why This Chapter Matters

Matthew 1 shows that Jesus did not appear out of nowhere. God had made promises to Abraham and David and carried them through centuries of faithfulness and failure, exile and silence, until they met in one Person. The Christian faith rests on what God did in history, and this chapter puts names and generations to it.

The chapter also shows the kind of salvation Jesus brings. The angel does not say He will free Israel from Rome, but that He will save His people from their sins, and Matthew adds that in Him God is with us. The family line, with its outsiders and broken stories, already shows a God who works His purposes through people who could not earn a place in them.

Key Themes to Watch For

Promise kept. Jesus is the heir of the promises to David and Abraham, and history moves toward Him (1:1, 17).

Grace in the family line. Tamar, Rahab, Ruth and Bathsheba show God working through unexpected people and histories (1:3, 5–6).

The work of the Holy Spirit. Jesus’ conception is “of the Holy Spirit,” a true miracle (1:18, 20).

Obedient faith. Joseph receives God’s word in a dream and does exactly what he is told (1:19–20, 24–25).

God with us. The names Jesus and Immanuel reveal who He is and what He came to do (1:21–23).

Key Words & Concepts

Son of David, Son of Abraham. Titles of descent, not immediate parentage. David points to Jesus as the promised King, Abraham to the promise of blessing for all nations (1:1).

Fourteen. Matthew’s structuring number (1:17). The Hebrew consonants of David’s name add up to fourteen (D-W-D, 4 + 6 + 4), which may reinforce the Davidic emphasis, though Matthew does not explain the number. Like other biblical genealogies, the list may pass over some generations.

The women. Tamar (Genesis 38), Rahab of Jericho (Joshua 2), Ruth the Moabite and Bathsheba, “her who had been the wife of Uriah” (1:6). Matthew identifies Bathsheba as Uriah’s wife without giving her personal name. He gives no single reason for naming these women, but two came from outside Israel and each story shows God’s providence. Mary is the fifth woman mentioned in the genealogy (1:16), and the change in wording at her name sets her apart from the others.

Betrothed. A binding commitment before the couple lived together, so that Joseph is already called Mary’s husband and could end it only by divorce (1:18–19).

Jesus. The Greek form of Joshua, a name meaning the LORD saves, explained by the angel: “He will save His people from their sins” (1:21).

Immanuel. A Hebrew name from Isaiah 7:14 meaning “God with us” (1:23). Matthew presents it as fulfilled in Jesus’ birth and as a statement of who He is.
