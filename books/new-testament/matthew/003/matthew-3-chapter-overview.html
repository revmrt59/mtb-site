Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 3
Main Idea:

John the Baptist calls Israel to repent because the kingdom of heaven is at hand, points to the mightier One who will baptize with the Holy Spirit and fire, and at Jesus’ baptism the Father declares Him His beloved Son as the Spirit descends upon Him.

Setting:

Some years after chapter 2, John preaches in the wilderness of Judea near the Jordan. Jesus comes from Galilee to be baptized before beginning His public ministry.

Chapter Outline:

  • Matthew 3:1–6 — John Prepares the Way of the Lord

  • Matthew 3:7–12 — John Warns of Judgment and Points to the Coming One

  • Matthew 3:13–17 — The Baptism of Jesus and the Father’s Declaration

Key Verse:

“And suddenly a voice came from heaven, saying, ‘This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.’” (Matthew 3:17)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

John’s message is short and urgent: “Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand!” (3:2). He is the voice Isaiah promised, preparing the way of the LORD, and the One whose way he prepares is about to arrive in Jesus. Crowds come confessing their sins. When Pharisees and Sadducees come too, John refuses to let them hide behind being Abraham’s children. Repentance is a real turning to God, and it shows in fruit. The ax is already at the root.

John knows he is only the forerunner. The One coming after him is mightier, and He will baptize with the Holy Spirit and fire, giving His Spirit to His people and separating wheat from chaff. Then Jesus Himself steps into the water. He has no sin to confess, yet He insists on being baptized “to fulfill all righteousness” (3:15), willingly doing everything the Father’s saving plan requires and standing with the people He came to save. As He comes up, the heavens open, the Spirit descends like a dove, and the Father speaks His delight in His beloved Son.

Chapter Sections

Matthew 3:1–6 — John Prepares the Way of the Lord

John preaches repentance in the wilderness of Judea, announcing that the kingdom of heaven is at hand. Matthew identifies him as Isaiah’s voice preparing the way of the LORD, and crowds come to be baptized, confessing their sins.

Matthew 3:7–12 — John Warns of Judgment and Points to the Coming One

John confronts the Pharisees and Sadducees, demanding fruit worthy of repentance and warning that descent from Abraham will not save them. He points to the mightier One who will baptize with the Holy Spirit and fire, gathering the wheat and burning the chaff.

Matthew 3:13–17 — The Baptism of Jesus and the Father’s Declaration

Jesus comes to be baptized, and when John protests, Jesus answers that this is fitting to fulfill all righteousness. As He comes up from the water, the Spirit descends upon Him and the Father declares Him His beloved Son, the One in whom He is well pleased.

Why This Chapter Matters

Matthew 3 shows that the King’s arrival calls for a response. Family heritage and religious standing cannot replace turning to God, and real repentance becomes visible in changed conduct. John’s warning is severe, yet the same chapter holds out the gift of the Spirit from the coming One.

The chapter also reveals who Jesus is before He preaches a word. The Father speaks, the Son stands in the water, and the Spirit descends, so the triune God is present together at the start of Jesus’ public mission. The Father’s words recall the royal Son of Psalm 2 and the Spirit-anointed Servant of Isaiah 42:1. The sinless Son identifies with sinners, and the Father’s pleasure in Him gives repentant people confidence to come to Him.

Key Themes to Watch For

Repentance. Turning to God in view of His approaching reign, shown in confession and fruit (3:2, 6, 8).

Warning of judgment. The ax at the root and the burning chaff press the urgency of John’s call (3:10, 12).

The coming One. John points beyond himself to the mightier One who gives the Spirit (3:11).

The obedient Son. Jesus is baptized to fulfill all righteousness (3:15).

Father, Son, and Spirit. The Father speaks and the Spirit descends upon the Son (3:16–17).

Key Words & Concepts

Kingdom of heaven (3:2). God’s reign arriving in the King. Matthew uses “heaven” as a reverent Jewish way of referring to God; it is the same reign as the kingdom of God (19:23–24).

Repent (3:2). To turn from sin to God with a changed mind and life. John expects the change to bear visible fruit (3:8).

Baptism of John (3:6). A washing in the Jordan that expressed repentance and prepared people for the coming One. It differs from the Christian baptism Jesus later commands (28:19).

Holy Spirit and fire (3:11). The coming One gives His Spirit to His people. In the immediate setting, fire speaks of judgment, as the burning chaff shows (3:12).

Fulfill all righteousness (3:15). To complete everything God’s saving plan requires. Jesus does this through obedient identification with the people He came to save.
