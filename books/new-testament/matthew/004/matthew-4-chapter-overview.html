Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 4
Main Idea:

Jesus, the Father’s beloved Son, overcomes the devil’s temptations by trusting and obeying God’s word, then moves to Galilee, where He begins to preach the kingdom, calls His first disciples, and heals every kind of sickness among the people.

Setting:

Immediately after His baptism, the Spirit leads Jesus into the wilderness. Matthew 4:16 closes the Gospel’s opening movement, and 4:17 begins Jesus’ public proclamation.

Chapter Outline:

  • Matthew 4:1–11 — The Faithful Son Overcomes the Tempter

  • Matthew 4:12–17 — The Great Light Dawns in Galilee

  • Matthew 4:18–22 — Jesus Calls Four Fishermen to Follow Him

  • Matthew 4:23–25 — Jesus Teaches, Preaches, and Heals throughout Galilee

Key Verse:

“But He answered and said, ‘It is written, “Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God.” ’” (Matthew 4:4)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The Son whom the Father has just declared is now tested. After forty days of fasting, Jesus is truly hungry, and the devil comes with three offers: bread on demand, a spectacular leap from the temple, and the kingdoms of the world without the cross. Each answer comes from Deuteronomy, where Moses reminded Israel of its testing in the wilderness. Israel grumbled, tested God, and turned to idols. Jesus trusts the Father’s provision, refuses to put God to the test, and gives worship to God alone. Where Israel failed, the Son stands faithful.

Victory in the wilderness leads to light in Galilee. Jesus settles in Capernaum, and Matthew hears Isaiah’s promise that people sitting in darkness have seen a great light. “From that time” (4:17) Jesus preaches repentance because the kingdom of heaven is at hand. He calls four fishermen, who leave nets, boat, and father to follow Him. Then He goes throughout Galilee teaching, preaching, and healing. The King has come, and His reign is already reaching real people in real need.

Chapter Sections

Matthew 4:1–11 — The Faithful Son Overcomes the Tempter

Led by the Spirit into the wilderness, Jesus is tempted by the devil after forty days of fasting. He answers each temptation with Scripture, refusing to misuse His power, to test the Father, or to worship anyone but God, and the devil leaves Him.

Matthew 4:12–17 — The Great Light Dawns in Galilee

After John’s arrest, Jesus makes His home in Capernaum, fulfilling Isaiah’s promise of light for Galilee of the Gentiles. From that time He begins to preach, “Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand” (4:17).

Matthew 4:18–22 — Jesus Calls Four Fishermen to Follow Him

Walking by the Sea of Galilee, Jesus calls Peter and Andrew, then James and John. Peter and Andrew, whom He promises to make fishers of men, immediately leave their nets, and James and John immediately leave the boat and their father to follow Him.

Matthew 4:23–25 — Jesus Teaches, Preaches, and Heals throughout Galilee

Jesus travels through Galilee teaching, preaching the gospel of the kingdom, and healing every kind of sickness and disease. Matthew distinguishes the sick from the demon-possessed, and great crowds from many regions follow Him.

Why This Chapter Matters

Matthew 4 shows the Son winning where God’s people had lost. His victory is real, and it was won by the same means God gives His people: trust in the Father’s word, refusal to test God, and undivided worship. Because Jesus was tempted and did not sin, He is able to help those who are tempted (Hebrews 2:18; 4:15).

The chapter also marks the turning point at 4:17. The kingdom Jesus announces is not only an idea. It calls people to leave what they hold and follow Him, and it reaches the sick and the oppressed with healing. His teaching, His call, and His compassion belong together from the start.

Key Themes to Watch For

The faithful Son. Jesus obeys the Father where Israel failed in the wilderness (4:1–11).

“It is written.” Scripture rightly understood guides Jesus’ trust and obedience (4:4, 7, 10).

Light in the darkness. The King’s arrival in Galilee fulfills Isaiah 9:1–2 (4:15–16).

The call to follow. Jesus’ call takes priority over work and family (4:19–22).

Kingdom in word and deed. Teaching, preaching, and healing display the King’s authority and mercy (4:23–24).

Key Words & Concepts

Tempted by the devil (4:1). The Spirit leads Jesus into the wilderness, and the devil, a real personal enemy, tests Him. The aim is to turn the Son from trusting obedience.

Forty days and forty nights (4:2). The length of Jesus’ fast. It recalls Israel’s forty years in the wilderness, the setting of the Deuteronomy passages Jesus quotes.

Pinnacle of the temple (4:5). A high point of the temple in Jerusalem. The devil quotes Psalm 91 to urge Jesus to force the Father to rescue Him.

Galilee of the Gentiles (4:15). Isaiah’s name for the northern region surrounded by other nations. The light dawns first among people who seemed far from God’s blessing.

Fishers of men (4:19). Jesus’ promise to make His followers able to bring people into His kingdom. The fruit comes from His work in them.
