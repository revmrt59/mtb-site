Matthew 5 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Matthew 5 opens the Sermon on the Mount as Jesus, seated on a mountain, teaches His disciples while the multitudes listen. He pronounces the blessedness of those who belong to the kingdom, calls them the salt of the earth and the light of the world, declares that He came not to destroy the Law or the Prophets but to fulfill them, and then, in six contrasts introduced by “But I say to you,” brings God’s commands to bear on anger, lust, divorce, oaths, retaliation, and love for enemies.

Chapter Sections

1. Matthew 5:1–12 — The Beatitudes

Seeing the multitudes, Jesus goes up on a mountain and teaches His disciples, pronouncing blessed the poor in spirit, those who mourn, the meek, those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, the merciful, the pure in heart, the peacemakers, and those persecuted for righteousness’ sake. He tells His disciples to rejoice when they are reviled and persecuted for His sake, for great is their reward in heaven.

2. Matthew 5:13–16 — Salt of the Earth and Light of the World

Jesus tells His disciples, “You are the salt of the earth” (Matthew 5:13), warning that salt which loses its flavor is good for nothing. He also calls them the light of the world and tells them to let their light shine before men, so that people may see their good works and glorify their Father in heaven.

3. Matthew 5:17–20 — Jesus Fulfills the Law and the Prophets

Jesus declares, “I did not come to destroy but to fulfill” (Matthew 5:17), and affirms that not one jot or tittle will pass from the law till all is fulfilled. Whoever breaks one of the least of these commandments and teaches others to do so will be called least in the kingdom of heaven, and whoever does and teaches them will be called great, but unless His hearers’ righteousness exceeds that of the scribes and Pharisees, they will by no means enter it.

4. Matthew 5:21–26 — Anger, Contempt, and Reconciliation

Jesus brings the command against murder to bear on anger and contemptuous words, warning that these deserve judgment. He tells His hearers to be reconciled with a brother before offering a gift at the altar and to agree quickly with an adversary on the way to court.

5. Matthew 5:27–30 — Lust and Decisive Removal of Sin

Jesus declares that whoever looks at a woman to lust for her has already committed adultery with her in his heart. With the vivid picture of plucking out a right eye or cutting off a right hand, Jesus calls His hearers to remove decisively whatever leads them into sin, for it is better to lose one member than for the whole body to be cast into hell.

6. Matthew 5:31–32 — Divorce

Jesus sets the law’s provision for a certificate of divorce beside His own word. Whoever divorces his wife for any reason except sexual immorality causes her to commit adultery, and whoever marries a woman who is divorced commits adultery.

7. Matthew 5:33–37 — Oaths and Truthful Speech

Jesus confronts the evasive use of oaths in ordinary speech, forbidding swearing by heaven, earth, Jerusalem, or one’s own head, for each belongs to God or lies beyond human control. Instead His disciples are to let their “Yes” be “Yes” and their “No” be “No,” since whatever is more than these is from the evil one.

8. Matthew 5:38–42 — Yielding the Right to Retaliate

Against the principle of “An eye for an eye and a tooth for a tooth” (Matthew 5:38), Jesus tells His hearers not to resist an evil person but to turn the other cheek, give up their cloak, and go the second mile. They are to give to the one who asks and not turn away the one who wants to borrow.

9. Matthew 5:43–48 — Love for Enemies

Jesus commands His hearers to love their enemies and pray for those who persecute them, so that they may be sons of their Father in heaven, who sends sun and rain on the evil and the good alike. Loving only those who love them is no more than tax collectors do, and Jesus concludes, “Therefore you shall be perfect, just as your Father in heaven is perfect” (Matthew 5:48).
