Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 5
Main Idea:

On the mountain Jesus describes the blessed people of His kingdom, calls them salt and light, declares that He fulfills the Law and the Prophets, and shows that the righteousness He requires reaches the heart behind anger, desire, marriage, speech, retaliation, and love.

Setting:

Matthew 5 opens the Sermon on the Mount (5–7), the first of Matthew’s five great discourses. Jesus teaches His disciples while the crowds gathered from Galilee and beyond listen.

Chapter Outline:

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The King begins with blessing. The poor in spirit, the mourners, the meek, and the hungry for righteousness are not the people the world usually calls fortunate, yet Jesus declares that the kingdom belongs to them. These qualities describe one character growing together by God’s grace: acknowledged need of God, grief over sin, strength yielded to Him, mercy in action, an undivided heart, and peacemaking, even under persecution. People like this are salt and light, and their good works lead others to glorify their Father in heaven.

Then Jesus speaks about the Scriptures: “I did not come to destroy but to fulfill” (5:17). He fulfills the Law and the Prophets in His person, His perfect obedience, His saving work, and His authoritative teaching. The righteousness He requires exceeds that of the scribes and Pharisees because it reaches the heart. Six times He says, “But I say to you,” and each time He goes beneath the act to its source: anger and contempt, cultivated lust, faithless divorce, evasive oaths, the urge to repay insult, and love limited to friends. The standard is the Father Himself, who sends rain on the just and the unjust.

Chapter Sections

Matthew 5:1–12 — The Beatitudes

Jesus pronounces blessed the poor in spirit, the mourners, the meek, the hungry for righteousness, the merciful, the pure in heart, the peacemakers, and the persecuted. These belong to His kingdom now and will receive its full inheritance.

Matthew 5:13–16 — Salt of the Earth and Light of the World

Jesus calls His disciples the salt of the earth and the light of the world. Their visible good works are meant to bring glory to their Father in heaven.

Matthew 5:17–20 — Jesus Fulfills the Law and the Prophets

Jesus declares that He came to fulfill the Law and the Prophets, not to destroy them. Careless teaching about God’s commands makes a person least in the kingdom, while outward-only righteousness will not enter it at all.

Matthew 5:21–26 — Anger, Contempt, and Reconciliation

Jesus brings the command against murder to bear on anger and contempt. He urges His hearers to seek reconciliation quickly, even before offering a gift at the altar.

Matthew 5:27–30 — Lust and Decisive Removal of Sin

Jesus shows that lustful looking is already adultery in the heart. His images of the eye and hand call for decisive removal of whatever leads to sin, not bodily harm.

Matthew 5:31–32 — Divorce

Jesus confronts the easy use of a certificate of divorce. Except for sexual immorality, divorcing a wife wrongs her and leads to adultery, and Jesus returns to the subject in 19:3–12.

Matthew 5:33–37 — Oaths and Truthful Speech

Jesus forbids evasive oaths in everyday speech, since heaven, earth, and Jerusalem all belong to God. A disciple’s plain “Yes” and “No” should be trustworthy.

Matthew 5:38–42 — Yielding the Right to Retaliate

Jesus calls His disciples to yield the right to retaliate when insulted and wronged. They turn the other cheek, give more than is demanded, and help those who ask.

Matthew 5:43–48 — Love for Enemies

Jesus commands love and prayer for enemies, after the pattern of the Father who gives sun and rain to the evil and the good. His children are to be perfect, mature in love, as their Father is.

Why This Chapter Matters

Matthew 5 shows what life under the King looks like from the inside out. The Beatitudes do not describe a ladder for earning entrance. They describe people who depend on God and whose lives are being changed by His grace.

The chapter also settles how Christians relate to the Old Testament. Jesus does not discard it. He brings it to its intended purpose, and obedience now flows through Him. His commands reach farther than outward conduct, which exposes every heart and points to the promised new heart and Spirit (Ezekiel 36:26–27). The call to love enemies shows the Father’s own generosity, seen most fully when Christ died for His enemies (Romans 5:8–10).

Key Themes to Watch For

The character of the kingdom. The Beatitudes portray the people who receive God’s reign (5:3–12).

Visible witness. Salt and light make the Father’s goodness seen in actual conduct (5:13–16).

Fulfillment. Jesus brings the Law and the Prophets to their purpose (5:17–18).

Righteousness of the heart. Each “But I say to you” reaches beneath the act to its source (5:21–48).

Like the Father. Love for enemies reflects the Father’s indiscriminate kindness (5:44–48).

Key Words & Concepts

Blessed (5:3). Favored by God and secure in His kingdom, even amid hardship. It describes God’s gift, not a passing feeling.

Poor in spirit (5:3). Those who know they have nothing to offer God and depend on Him completely.

Meek (5:5). Strength yielded to God’s control. The meek can serve, receive correction, and trust God instead of fighting for themselves.

Fulfill (5:17). To bring something to its intended completion. Jesus fulfills Scripture by who He is, what He does, and what He teaches.

Raca (5:22). An Aramaic word of contempt meaning roughly “empty-headed.” Jesus treats contemptuous words as serious sin.

Perfect (5:48). Complete or mature, especially in love that reaches beyond friends to enemies, as the Father’s does.
