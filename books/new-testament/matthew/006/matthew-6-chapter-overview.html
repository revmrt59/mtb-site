Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 6
Main Idea:

Jesus calls His disciples to give, pray, and fast before their Father who sees in secret, to store up treasure in heaven and serve God alone, and to trust the Father’s care instead of worrying, seeking His kingdom first.

Setting:

Matthew 6 continues the Sermon on the Mount. Having shown in chapter 5 how righteousness reaches the heart, Jesus now turns to devotion, possessions, and daily needs.

Chapter Outline:

  • Matthew 6:1–4 — Giving in Secret

  • Matthew 6:5–15 — Prayer and the Lord’s Prayer

  • Matthew 6:16–18 — Fasting in Secret

  • Matthew 6:19–24 — Treasure in Heaven and Undivided Service

  • Matthew 6:25–34 — Do Not Worry; Seek First the Kingdom

Key Verse:

“But seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things shall be added to you.” (Matthew 6:33)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The Father fills this chapter. He sees in secret, He knows what His children need before they ask, He feeds the birds and clothes the lilies, and He knows they need food and clothing. Giving, prayer, and fasting are meant to be done before Him, not before an audience. When the goal is to be seen, the applause of people is the whole reward. When devotion is directed to the Father, He sees and He rewards.

At the center Jesus gives a pattern for prayer. It begins with the Father’s name, His kingdom, and His will, asking that His rule be gladly received on earth as it is in heaven. Only then does it bring daily bread, forgiveness, and the request “do not lead us into temptation, but deliver us from the evil one” (6:13), asking the Father to spare His children from trials in which they might stumble. God may test His children, but He never entices them to sin. The same priority runs through the rest of the chapter. A person’s treasure directs the heart, and no one can serve God and money. Children who seek their Father’s kingdom first do not need to be consumed by worry, because His care for them is greater than His care for the birds and the grass.

Chapter Sections

Matthew 6:1–4 — Giving in Secret

Jesus warns against giving to be seen by others. Gifts given in secret are seen by the Father, who rewards them.

Matthew 6:5–15 — Prayer and the Lord’s Prayer

Jesus teaches His disciples to pray privately and simply to a Father who already knows their needs, and He gives them the Lord’s Prayer. He adds that those who forgive others will be forgiven, and those who refuse to forgive will not be.

Matthew 6:16–18 — Fasting in Secret

Jesus tells His disciples to fast without drawing attention to themselves. Their fasting is for the Father who sees in secret.

Matthew 6:19–24 — Treasure in Heaven and Undivided Service

Jesus urges His hearers to store up treasure in heaven, where it cannot be lost. A good eye fills the whole body with light, and no one can serve two masters, God and mammon.

Matthew 6:25–34 — Do Not Worry; Seek First the Kingdom

Jesus tells His disciples not to worry about food, drink, or clothing, pointing to the birds and the lilies that the Father cares for. They are to seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness and to live today’s life in trust.

Why This Chapter Matters

Matthew 6 exposes how easily religious practice can become a performance. Jesus does not discourage giving, prayer, or fasting. He calls people to do them for the One who matters, and He promises that the Father notices what no one else sees.

The chapter also shows that faith reaches into money and daily needs. Seeking the kingdom first means letting the King’s interests order every area of life, from home and work to possessions and plans. The Lord’s Prayer teaches that pattern. It asks for God’s will to be done on earth among people as it is in heaven, which includes the surrender of the one who prays, and then it trusts Him for bread, forgiveness, and protection. The promise does not guarantee wealth, but it does free God’s children from anxious preoccupation with tomorrow.

Key Themes to Watch For

The Father who sees. Secret devotion receives the Father’s reward (6:4, 6, 18).

The kingdom first. The prayer and the closing command put God’s reign and will ahead of personal needs (6:10, 33).

Forgiving others. Forgiveness received from God and given to others belong together (6:12, 14–15).

Undivided allegiance. Treasure, the eye, and the two masters reveal where the heart is fixed (6:19–24).

Trust instead of worry. The Father’s care for birds and lilies shows how much more He cares for His children (6:25–32).

Key Words & Concepts

Hypocrites (6:2, 5, 16). Literally actors. Jesus uses the word for people who perform religious acts to win admiration.

Vain repetitions (6:7). Empty words piled up to secure an answer. Jesus does not forbid sincere repetition.

Hallowed (6:9). Treated as holy. The prayer asks that the Father’s name be honored, beginning in the life of the one praying.

The evil one (6:13). The NKJV reads “deliver us from the evil one,” Satan himself. The prayer asks for protection from his traps and rescue from his influence.

Mammon (6:24). An Aramaic word for wealth and possessions, pictured as a master that competes with God for a person’s service.
