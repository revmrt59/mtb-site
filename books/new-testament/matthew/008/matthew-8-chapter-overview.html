Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 8
Main Idea:

Coming down from the mountain, Jesus shows the authority of His word in action: He cleanses a leper, heals a Gentile’s servant from a distance, heals and delivers many, calls followers to costly allegiance, stills a storm, and frees two demon-possessed men.

Setting:

Matthew 8 follows the Sermon on the Mount and begins the account of Jesus’ mighty works that continues through chapter 9, around Capernaum and the Sea of Galilee.

Chapter Outline:

  • Matthew 8:1–4 — Jesus Cleanses a Leper

  • Matthew 8:5–13 — The Centurion’s Faith and the Healing of His Servant

  • Matthew 8:14–17 — Healings at Peter’s House

  • Matthew 8:18–22 — The Cost of Following Jesus

  • Matthew 8:23–27 — Jesus Calms the Storm

  • Matthew 8:28–34 — Jesus Delivers Two Demon-Possessed Men

Key Verse:

“So the men marveled, saying, ‘Who can this be, that even the winds and the sea obey Him?’” (Matthew 8:27)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The crowds had marveled that Jesus taught “as one having authority” (7:29). Now they see that authority at work. He reaches out and touches a leper, and the man is clean. A Roman centurion asks Him only to speak a word, and Jesus marvels at a Gentile’s faith, telling of many who will come from east and west to feast with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. At Peter’s house He heals the sick and casts out spirits, and Matthew hears Isaiah’s words: “He Himself took our infirmities and bore our sicknesses” (8:17). The Servant is already lifting the burdens of the redemption He came to bring.

In the middle of the healings Jesus makes clear what following Him costs. Then He shows who He is. A single rebuke calms the sea, and the disciples ask, “Who can this be?” On the other shore, demons recognize Him as the Son of God and beg to be sent into the swine. The men are freed, yet the townspeople plead with Jesus to leave. His presence brings rescue, and it also demands a response.

Chapter Sections

Matthew 8:1–4 — Jesus Cleanses a Leper

Jesus touches a leper and makes him clean with a word. He sends the man to show himself to the priest as Moses commanded, as a testimony.

Matthew 8:5–13 — The Centurion’s Faith and the Healing of His Servant

A centurion trusts Jesus to heal his servant simply by speaking, and Jesus praises his faith above any He has found in Israel. He warns that many from east and west will share the feast of the kingdom while sons of the kingdom are cast out.

Matthew 8:14–17 — Healings at Peter’s House

Jesus heals Peter’s mother-in-law, who rises and serves Him, and that evening He heals the sick and casts out spirits with a word. Matthew sees Isaiah 53:4 fulfilled in these healings.

Matthew 8:18–22 — The Cost of Following Jesus

A scribe offers to follow Jesus anywhere, and Jesus warns that the Son of Man has nowhere to lay His head. To a disciple who wants to bury his father first, He says, “Follow Me, and let the dead bury their own dead” (8:22).

Matthew 8:23–27 — Jesus Calms the Storm

Jesus sleeps through a great storm on the sea until His frightened disciples wake Him. He rebukes their little faith, then rebukes the winds and sea, and there is a great calm.

Matthew 8:28–34 — Jesus Delivers Two Demon-Possessed Men

In the country of the Gergesenes, two fierce demon-possessed men meet Jesus, and the demons recognize Him as the Son of God. He sends them into a herd of swine that rushes into the sea, and the whole city begs Him to leave.

Why This Chapter Matters

Matthew 8 shows that the King’s authority is not limited to words on a mountain. It reaches disease, distance, nature, and the powers of darkness, and it is joined to compassion. He touches the untouchable and welcomes a Gentile’s faith. These healings belong to the salvation Christ brings, so His people may pray with bold expectation, while the complete renewal of the body awaits the resurrection (Romans 8:23).

The chapter also shows that seeing Jesus’ power does not automatically produce faith. A Roman soldier trusts Him, the disciples fear in the storm, and a whole town asks Him to go away. Following Him takes priority over comfort and every other claim.

Key Themes to Watch For

Authority of His word. Jesus heals, calms, and delivers by speaking (8:3, 8, 16, 26, 32).

Faith from unexpected places. A Gentile centurion trusts Jesus’ authority (8:10–12).

The Servant who heals. Jesus fulfills Isaiah 53:4 by healing the sick (8:17).

The cost of following. Discipleship comes before security and every competing claim (8:19–22).

Who is this? The calming of the sea and the demons’ cry reveal Jesus’ identity (8:27, 29).

Key Words & Concepts

Leprosy (8:2). One of the serious skin disorders that made a person ceremonially unclean and separated from the community. Jesus’ touch shows His compassion, and the cleansing and the visit to the priest open the way back to ordinary community life (8:4).

Centurion (8:5). A Roman officer over about a hundred soldiers. As a Gentile, he understood authority and trusted Jesus to command healing from a distance.

Son of Man (8:20). Jesus’ favorite name for Himself, drawn from Daniel 7:13–14. Here it joins His future dominion with His present homelessness.

Little faith (8:26). Faith present but overwhelmed by fear. Jesus rebukes the disciples, then rescues them.

Gergesenes (8:28). The people of a largely Gentile region on the eastern shore of the Sea of Galilee, where the herd of swine was kept.
