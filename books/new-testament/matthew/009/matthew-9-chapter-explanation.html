Chapter Study

Matthew 9

Verse-by-Verse
Matthew 9:1
Matthew 9:1
NKJV

Matthew 9:1 So He got into a boat, crossed over, and came to His own city.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 9:1 Jesus climbed into a boat and went back across the lake to his own town. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus gets into a boat, crosses over, and comes to His own city, Capernaum. Having been sent away from the Gergesene region, He returns to the town where He has made His home.

Verse Explanation

Jesus gets into a boat, crosses over, and comes to His own city, Capernaum. Having been sent away from the Gergesene region, He returns to the town where He has made His home.

“So He got into a boat, crossed over, and came to His own city.”

The people of the Gergesene region had begged Jesus to leave (8:34). He does not force Himself on them. He crosses the Sea of Galilee and returns to “His own city.” This is Capernaum, the town where He made His home after leaving Nazareth (4:13).

Study Notes

Mark 2:1 places the healing that follows in Capernaum, in a house where Jesus was staying.

Matthew 9:2
Matthew 9:2
NKJV

Matthew 9:2 Then behold, they brought to Him a paralytic lying on a bed. When Jesus saw their faith, He said to the paralytic, "Son, be of good cheer; your sins are forgiven you."(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 9:2 Some people brought to him a paralyzed man on a mat. Seeing their faith, Jesus said to the paralyzed man, “Be encouraged, my child! Your sins are forgiven.” (NLT)

Main Idea

People bring Him a paralyzed man lying on a bed, and seeing their faith, Jesus tells him, “Son, be of good cheer; your sins are forgiven you.” He sees the persistent trust of the man and those who carried him, and He meets the man’s deepest need first.

Verse Explanation

People bring Him a paralyzed man lying on a bed, and seeing their faith, Jesus tells him, “Son, be of good cheer; your sins are forgiven you.” He sees the persistent trust of the man and those who carried him, and He meets the man’s deepest need first.

“Then behold, they brought to Him a paralytic lying on a bed.”

A man who cannot walk is carried to Jesus on a “bed,” probably a mat or stretcher. He cannot come by himself, so others bring him.

“When Jesus saw their faith,”

Jesus sees “their faith.” It includes the man and the friends who carried him. Mark and Luke add that they opened the roof to lower him down when the crowd blocked the door (Mark 2:3–4; Luke 5:18–19). Their trust in Jesus refused to give up when obstacles stood in the way.

The friends’ faith brought the man to Jesus. It did not take the place of his own relationship with God. Jesus speaks to the man himself.

“He said to the paralytic, ‘Son, be of good cheer; your sins are forgiven you.”

Jesus speaks tenderly. He calls the man “Son” and tells him to take courage. Then He says something no one expected: “your sins are forgiven you.” The man came for healing. Jesus meets his deepest need first.

Jesus does not say that this man’s sin caused his paralysis. Scripture does not treat every illness as the result of a particular sin (John 9:1–3). Here, forgiveness comes first because every person’s greatest need is to be made right with God.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “be of good cheer” means take courage. Jesus uses the same word in 9:22 and 14:27.

Matthew 9:3
Matthew 9:3
NKJV

Matthew 9:3 And at once some of the scribes said within themselves, "This Man blasphemes!"(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 9:3 But some of the teachers of religious law said to themselves, “That’s blasphemy! Does he think he’s God?” (NLT)

Main Idea

Some of the scribes say to themselves, “This Man blasphemes!” They recognize that Jesus is claiming to do what only God can do, and they judge Him guilty of dishonoring God.

Verse Explanation

Some of the scribes say to themselves, “This Man blasphemes!” They recognize that Jesus is claiming to do what only God can do, and they judge Him guilty of dishonoring God.

“And at once some of the scribes said within themselves,”

The “scribes” were experts in the law of Moses. They do not speak out loud. They react “within themselves.”

“This Man blasphemes!”

Their thought is an accusation. To blaspheme is to dishonor God. The scribes understand that only God can forgive sins against Himself (Mark 2:7). They see that Jesus is claiming to do what belongs to God alone. Since they do not believe He is God, they conclude He is guilty of dishonoring God.

Study Notes

Under the law, blasphemy was punishable by death (Leviticus 24:16). This is the first time in Matthew that this charge is raised against Jesus. It will be raised again at His trial (26:65).

Matthew 9:4
NKJV

NLT

Matthew 9:4 Jesus knew what they were thinking, so he asked them, “Why do you have such evil thoughts in your hearts? (NLT)

Main Idea

Knowing their thoughts, Jesus asks why they think evil in their hearts. He reveals that He knows what is hidden in the heart.

Verse Explanation

Knowing their thoughts, Jesus asks why they think evil in their hearts. He reveals that He knows what is hidden in the heart.

“But Jesus, knowing their thoughts,”

The scribes spoke only in their hearts, but Jesus knows what they are thinking. Scripture teaches that God alone knows the hearts of all people (1 Kings 8:39). Jesus’ knowledge of their thoughts supports the very claim they are rejecting.

“said, ‘Why do you think evil in your hearts?”

Jesus brings their hidden thoughts into the open. He calls them “evil.” They are condemning the One who has come to forgive.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “knowing” can also be translated seeing. Some manuscripts read seeing their thoughts.

Matthew 9:5
Matthew 9:5
NKJV

Matthew 9:5 For which is easier, to say, "˜Your sins are forgiven you,' or to say, "˜Arise and walk'?(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 9:5 Is it easier to say ‘Your sins are forgiven,’ or ‘Stand up and walk’? (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus asks which is easier to say, “Your sins are forgiven you,” or “Arise and walk.” Anyone can claim to forgive unseen sins, but a command to walk can be tested immediately.

Verse Explanation

Jesus asks which is easier to say, “Your sins are forgiven you,” or “Arise and walk.” Anyone can claim to forgive unseen sins, but a command to walk can be tested immediately.

“For which is easier, to say, ‘Your sins are forgiven you,’ or to say, ‘Arise and walk’?”

Jesus asks a question that exposes their thinking. Saying either sentence is equally easy. But one can be checked and the other cannot. Anyone could claim to forgive sins, because no one can see whether sins have been forgiven. A command to “Arise and walk” would be proved true or false in front of everyone.

Study Notes

Both forgiving sins and healing require God’s power. The question concerns which claim can be seen and tested.

Matthew 9:6
Matthew 9:6
NKJV

Matthew 9:6 But that you may know that the Son of Man has power on earth to forgive sins""”then He said to the paralytic, "Arise, take up your bed, and go to your house."(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 9:6 So I will prove to you that the Son of Man has the authority on earth to forgive sins.” Then Jesus turned to the paralyzed man and said, “Stand up, pick up your mat, and go home!” (NLT)

Main Idea

So that they may know “the Son of Man has power on earth to forgive sins,” Jesus tells the paralyzed man to get up, take his bed, and go home. The visible healing confirms His invisible authority to forgive.

Verse Explanation

So that they may know “the Son of Man has power on earth to forgive sins,” Jesus tells the paralyzed man to get up, take his bed, and go home. The visible healing confirms His invisible authority to forgive.

“But that you may know that the Son of Man has power on earth to forgive sins’—”

Jesus states the purpose of what He is about to do. He wants them to know that “the Son of Man” has authority to forgive sins “on earth.” God forgives from heaven. Jesus claims to exercise that same authority here, in person.

“Son of Man” is Jesus’ favorite title for Himself. It draws on Daniel 7:13–14, where one like the Son of Man receives authority and an everlasting kingdom from God.

“then He said to the paralytic, ‘Arise, take up your bed, and go to your house.”

Jesus turns from the scribes to the paralyzed man and gives three commands. Get up. Pick up the mat that carried you. Go home. The man who was carried in will walk out carrying his own bed.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “power” means authority, the right and power to act.

When the crowds see it, they marvel and glorify God, “who had given such power to men.” They recognize God’s work in Jesus, though they do not yet grasp who He is.

The crowds are amazed, and they give glory to God. They recognize that God has done this.

“who had given such power to men.”

They praise God for giving such authority “to men.” They see God at work in Jesus, but they do not yet grasp who He is. Jesus is not merely a man to whom God has lent power. He is the Son of Man who has authority to forgive sins.

Matthew 9:9–13 — Jesus Calls Matthew and Eats with Sinners

Matthew 9:9
Matthew 9:9
NKJV

Matthew 9:9 As Jesus passed on from there, He saw a man named Matthew sitting at the tax office. And He said to him, "Follow Me." So he arose and followed Him.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 9:9 As Jesus was walking along, he saw a man named Matthew sitting at his tax collector’s booth. “Follow me and be my disciple,” Jesus said to him. So Matthew got up and followed him. (NLT)

Main Idea

Passing on, Jesus sees a man named Matthew sitting at the tax office and says, “Follow Me,” and Matthew gets up and follows Him. Jesus calls a tax collector, a man despised by his people, and Matthew leaves his post at once.

Verse Explanation

Passing on, Jesus sees a man named Matthew sitting at the tax office and says, “Follow Me,” and Matthew gets up and follows Him. Jesus calls a tax collector, a man despised by his people, and Matthew leaves his post at once.

“As Jesus passed on from there, He saw a man named Matthew sitting at the tax office.”

The man Jesus notices is the writer of this Gospel. He was sitting “at the tax office,” a booth where taxes or tolls were collected, probably on goods moving through Capernaum.

Tax collectors worked for the ruling powers and often took more than was owed. Many Jews regarded them as traitors and sinners. Matthew records his own calling without trying to hide what he had been.

“And He said to him, ‘Follow Me.’ So he arose and followed Him.”

Jesus gives a simple call: “Follow Me.” Matthew responds at once. He leaves his booth and his income and goes with Jesus. Luke 5:28 adds that he left all.

Study Notes

Mark 2:14 and Luke 5:27 call him Levi. Many people in that time had two names. The list of apostles in 10:3 identifies him as Matthew the tax collector.

Matthew 9:10
Matthew 9:10
NKJV

Matthew 9:10 Now it happened, as Jesus sat at the table in the house, that behold, many tax collectors and sinners came and sat down with Him and His disciples.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 9:10 Later, Matthew invited Jesus and his disciples to his home as dinner guests, along with many tax collectors and other disreputable sinners. (NLT)

Main Idea

As Jesus reclines at table in the house, many tax collectors and sinners come and eat with Him and His disciples. Jesus welcomes into His fellowship people others shunned.

Verse Explanation

As Jesus reclines at table in the house, many tax collectors and sinners come and eat with Him and His disciples. Jesus welcomes into His fellowship people others shunned.

“Now it happened, as Jesus sat at the table in the house,”

Jesus is at a meal “in the house.” Luke 5:29 says that Matthew gave a great feast for Jesus in his own house.

“that behold, many tax collectors and sinners came and sat down with Him and His disciples.”

Matthew’s guests include people others avoid. “Sinners” describes people known for breaking God’s law or living outside the religious customs of the community. Matthew’s friends are people like him. Jesus welcomes them to His table.

In that culture, eating together expressed acceptance and friendship. By sharing the meal, Jesus shows them that He has come for them.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “sat” means to recline. Guests at a formal meal reclined on couches around a low table.

Matthew 9:11
Matthew 9:11
NKJV

Matthew 9:11 And when the Pharisees saw it, they said to His disciples, "Why does your Teacher eat with tax collectors and sinners?"(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 9:11 But when the Pharisees saw this, they asked his disciples, “Why does your teacher eat with such scum? ” (NLT)

Main Idea

Seeing this, the Pharisees ask His disciples, “Why does your Teacher eat with tax collectors and sinners?” They see Jesus’ table fellowship with sinners as a scandal.

Verse Explanation

Seeing this, the Pharisees ask His disciples, “Why does your Teacher eat with tax collectors and sinners?” They see Jesus’ table fellowship with sinners as a scandal.

“And when the Pharisees saw it,”

The “Pharisees” were a group that was devoted to careful observance of the law and of traditions about purity. They kept apart from people they considered unclean.

“they said to His disciples, ‘Why does your Teacher eat with tax collectors and sinners?”

They do not ask Jesus directly. They ask His disciples. Their question is an objection. To them, a true teacher of God would not share a table with such people. They see His fellowship with sinners as a scandal.

Study Notes

Luke 15:2 records the same complaint: “This Man receives sinners and eats with them.” Jesus answers it with the parables of the lost sheep, the lost coin, and the lost son.

Matthew 9:12
Matthew 9:12
NKJV

Matthew 9:12 When Jesus heard that, He said to them, "Those who are well have no need of a physician, but those who are sick.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 9:12 When Jesus heard this, he said, “Healthy people don’t need a doctor — sick people do.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus answers that the healthy do not need a physician, but the sick do. He has come as a physician to people who know they need healing.

Verse Explanation

Jesus answers that the healthy do not need a physician, but the sick do. He has come as a physician to people who know they need healing.

“When Jesus heard that,”

Jesus answers the question Himself.

“He said to them, ‘Those who are well have no need of a physician, but those who are sick.”

Jesus uses a simple picture. A doctor goes to the sick, not to the healthy. Jesus compares Himself to a “physician.” He has come to people who need healing. The tax collectors and sinners know they are sick. The Pharisees think they are well.

Jesus is not saying the Pharisees have no need of Him. Everyone needs the Physician. But only those who recognize their need will come to Him for healing.

Study Notes

The image of God as healer appears in Exodus 15:26: “For I am the LORD who heals you.”

Matthew 9:13
Matthew 9:13
NKJV

Matthew 9:13 But go and learn what this means: "˜I desire mercy and not sacrifice.' For I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners, to repentance."(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 9:13 Then he added, “Now go and learn the meaning of this Scripture: ‘I want you to show mercy, not offer sacrifices.’ For I have come to call not those who think they are righteous, but those who know they are sinners.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus tells them to go and learn what Hosea 6:6 means, “I desire mercy and not sacrifice,” for He came to call sinners, not the righteous, to repentance. God values mercy above religious observance, and Jesus extends that mercy by calling sinners to turn to Him.

Verse Explanation

Jesus tells them to go and learn what Hosea 6:6 means, “I desire mercy and not sacrifice,” for He came to call sinners, not the righteous, to repentance. God values mercy above religious observance, and Jesus extends that mercy by calling sinners to turn to Him.

“But go and learn what this means: ‘I desire mercy and not sacrifice.”

Jesus sends the Pharisees back to Scripture. “Go and learn” was a phrase teachers used to send students to study a text. The Pharisees knew the law well, but they had not grasped this verse. It comes from Hosea 6:6.

God commanded sacrifices, but He never wanted religious ritual without a merciful heart. Mercy is God’s own stated priority. The Pharisees kept the rules of separation but showed no mercy to those who needed God most.

“For I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners, to repentance.”

Jesus states His mission. He came “to call” sinners “to repentance,” to turn from sin to God. His mercy welcomes sinners. His call also changes them.

Those who think they are already “righteous” do not see their need for the call. Jesus’ words are an invitation to everyone who knows he is a sinner.

Study Notes

Jesus quotes Hosea 6:6 again in 12:7.

Some manuscripts do not include the words to repentance in this verse. Luke 5:32 includes them.

Matthew 9:14–17 — The Bridegroom, New Cloth, and New Wine

Matthew 9:14
Matthew 9:14
NKJV

Matthew 9:14 Then the disciples of John came to Him, saying, "Why do we and the Pharisees fast often, but Your disciples do not fast?"(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 9:14 One day the disciples of John the Baptist came to Jesus and asked him, “Why don’t your disciples fast like we do and the Pharisees do?” (NLT)

Main Idea

John’s disciples ask Jesus why they and the Pharisees fast often but His disciples do not. They wonder why Jesus’ followers do not share their practice of devotion.

Verse Explanation

John’s disciples ask Jesus why they and the Pharisees fast often but His disciples do not. They wonder why Jesus’ followers do not share their practice of devotion.

“Then the disciples of John came to Him,”

The followers of John the Baptist come with a question. John had called Israel to repentance and lived a life of self-denial (3:4).

“saying, ‘Why do we and the Pharisees fast often, but Your disciples do not fast?”

They notice a difference. They and the Pharisees “fast often,” going without food as an act of devotion. Jesus’ disciples do not. They want to know why.

Study Notes

The law required fasting only on the Day of Atonement (Leviticus 16:29–31). The Pharisees fasted twice a week (Luke 18:12).

Matthew 9:15
Matthew 9:15
NKJV

Matthew 9:15 And Jesus said to them, "Can the friends of the bridegroom mourn as long as the bridegroom is with them? But the days will come when the bridegroom will be taken away from them, and then they will fast.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 9:15 Jesus replied, “Do wedding guests mourn while celebrating with the groom? Of course not. But someday the groom will be taken away from them, and then they will fast. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus answers that the bridegroom’s friends cannot mourn while the bridegroom is with them, but when he is taken away, then they will fast. His presence brings the joy of a wedding, and His coming departure points toward His death.

Verse Explanation

Jesus answers that the bridegroom’s friends cannot mourn while the bridegroom is with them, but when he is taken away, then they will fast. His presence brings the joy of a wedding, and His coming departure points toward His death.

“And Jesus said to them, ‘Can the friends of the bridegroom mourn as long as the bridegroom is with them?”

Jesus answers with a wedding picture. The bridegroom’s friends were the guests closest to him. A wedding was a time of joy and feasting that could last a week. No one would fast or mourn during it.

Jesus is the bridegroom. In the Old Testament, God pictured Himself as the bridegroom of His people (Isaiah 62:5). Jesus’ presence brings that joy. This is not a time for mourning.

“But the days will come when the bridegroom will be taken away from them,”

The joy will be interrupted. This is the first hint in Matthew of Jesus’ death. He will be taken from His disciples by force.

“and then they will fast.”

When He is taken away, the disciples will grieve and fast. Fasting has a place in the life of His followers (6:16–18). It fits a time of longing for Him.

Study Notes

John the Baptist also called Jesus the bridegroom and himself the friend of the bridegroom (John 3:29). Revelation 19:7 pictures the marriage of the Lamb at the end of the age.

The Greek phrase translated “friends of the bridegroom” is literally the sons of the bridechamber.

Matthew 9:16
Matthew 9:16
NKJV

Matthew 9:16 No one puts a piece of unshrunk cloth on an old garment; for the patch pulls away from the garment, and the tear is made worse.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 9:16 “Besides, who would patch old clothing with new cloth? For the new patch would shrink and rip away from the old cloth, leaving an even bigger tear than before. (NLT)

Main Idea

No one sews a patch of unshrunk cloth on an old garment, because it pulls away and makes the tear worse. Jesus has not come to patch new life onto old religious forms.

Verse Explanation

No one sews a patch of unshrunk cloth on an old garment, because it pulls away and makes the tear worse. Jesus has not come to patch new life onto old religious forms.

“No one puts a piece of unshrunk cloth on an old garment;”

Jesus uses a picture from everyday life. “Unshrunk cloth” is new fabric that has not been washed. Sewing it onto “an old garment” would be a mistake.

“for the patch pulls away from the garment, and the tear is made worse.”

When the garment is washed, the new patch shrinks. It pulls on the old cloth around it and tears it further.

Jesus has not come to patch something new onto an old way of life. He does not add a little grace to a system of works. What He brings requires something new from the inside out.

Study Notes

Luke 5:36 records a related form of the saying, in which the new garment is also torn to make the patch.

Matthew 9:17
Matthew 9:17
NKJV

Matthew 9:17 Nor do they put new wine into old wineskins, or else the wineskins break, the wine is spilled, and the wineskins are ruined. But they put new wine into new wineskins, and both are preserved."(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 9:17 “And no one puts new wine into old wineskins. For the old skins would burst from the pressure, spilling the wine and ruining the skins. New wine is stored in new wineskins so that both are preserved.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Nor does anyone put new wine into old wineskins, or they burst and both are lost; new wine goes into new wineskins, and both are preserved. The new life Jesus brings by His Spirit requires people made new, soft and yielded to God, rather than rigid religious routine that cannot contain it.

Verse Explanation

Nor does anyone put new wine into old wineskins, or they burst and both are lost; new wine goes into new wineskins, and both are preserved. The new life Jesus brings by His Spirit requires people made new, soft and yielded to God, rather than rigid religious routine that cannot contain it.

“Nor do they put new wine into old wineskins, or else the wineskins break, the wine is spilled, and the wineskins are ruined.”

“Wineskins” were containers made of animal skin. As new wine fermented, it expanded. A new skin could stretch. An old skin had become dry and stiff. It would burst, and both the wine and the skin would be lost.

“But they put new wine into new wineskins, and both are preserved.”

New wine needs “new wineskins.” The new wine pictures the fresh and expanding work Jesus brings by His Spirit and the gospel of grace. The wineskins picture the people who receive it, individual believers and the communities they form.

A new wineskin is soft and flexible. It has been made new through death to self. It stays open to God and stretches as He works. A hardened religious routine cannot hold what Jesus brings. It bursts under the pressure of the Spirit’s work.

Jesus did not come to throw away the Scriptures (5:17). He came to fulfill God’s purpose and to make His people new (2 Corinthians 5:17). The warning is against letting good spiritual practices become religious pride or resisting the Spirit’s work because we prefer what is familiar.

Study Notes

Mark 2:22 and Luke 5:37–39 record the same saying. Luke adds that no one who has drunk old wine immediately desires new, because he says the old is better.

Matthew 9:18–26 — A Ruler’s Daughter Raised and a Woman Healed

Matthew 9:18
Matthew 9:18
NKJV

Matthew 9:18 While He spoke these things to them, behold, a ruler came and worshiped Him, saying, "My daughter has just died, but come and lay Your hand on her and she will live."(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 9:18 As Jesus was saying this, the leader of a synagogue came and knelt before him. “My daughter has just died,” he said, “but you can bring her back to life again if you just come and lay your hand on her.” (NLT)

Main Idea

As Jesus is speaking, a ruler comes, worships Him, and says, “My daughter has just died, but come and lay Your hand on her and she will live.” His request goes beyond healing; he trusts Jesus to restore life itself.

Verse Explanation

As Jesus is speaking, a ruler comes, worships Him, and says, “My daughter has just died, but come and lay Your hand on her and she will live.” His request goes beyond healing; he trusts Jesus to restore life itself.

“While He spoke these things to them, behold, a ruler came and worshiped Him,”

Jesus is still speaking when “a ruler” comes to Him. Mark and Luke name him Jairus and say he was a ruler of the synagogue (Mark 5:22; Luke 8:41). He was a respected leader in the town. He kneels before Jesus.

“saying, ‘My daughter has just died, but come and lay Your hand on her and she will live.”

In Matthew’s shorter account, the father says his daughter “has just died.” Mark and Luke tell the story in more detail, with the news of her death arriving while Jesus was on the way (Mark 5:23, 35). Matthew summarizes the whole account.

The father asks Jesus to “lay Your hand on her.” He believes that if Jesus touches her, “she will live.” He trusts Jesus to restore life itself.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “worshiped” describes bowing down or kneeling before someone in reverence.

Matthew 9:20
Matthew 9:20
NKJV

Matthew 9:20 And suddenly, a woman who had a flow of blood for twelve years came from behind and touched the hem of His garment.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 9:20 Just then a woman who had suffered for twelve years with constant bleeding came up behind him. She touched the fringe of his robe, (NLT)

Main Idea

On the way, a woman who has suffered from bleeding for twelve years comes up behind Jesus and touches the hem of His garment. Her long illness had made her ceremonially unclean, so she approaches quietly from behind.

Verse Explanation

On the way, a woman who has suffered from bleeding for twelve years comes up behind Jesus and touches the hem of His garment. Her long illness had made her ceremonially unclean, so she approaches quietly from behind.

“And suddenly, a woman who had a flow of blood for twelve years”

On the way, a woman approaches. She has suffered from bleeding “for twelve years.” Mark 5:26 adds that she had spent everything on doctors and had only grown worse.

Under the law, this condition made her ceremonially unclean (Leviticus 15:25–27). Anyone she touched would also become unclean. For twelve years she had lived on the edge of community and worship.

“came from behind and touched the hem of His garment.”

She comes quietly “from behind.” She does not want to be noticed. She touches “the hem of His garment.” This was probably one of the tassels that Jewish men wore on the corners of their outer garment (Numbers 15:38–39).

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “hem” can refer to the edge of a garment or to the tassels commanded in the law.

Matthew 9:21
Matthew 9:21
NKJV

Matthew 9:21 For she said to herself, "If only I may touch His garment, I shall be made well."(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 9:21 for she thought, “If I can just touch his robe, I will be healed.” (NLT)

Main Idea

The suffering woman tells herself, “If only I may touch His garment, I shall be made well.” Her reaching out comes from trust that Jesus can heal her.

Verse Explanation

The suffering woman tells herself, “If only I may touch His garment, I shall be made well.” Her reaching out comes from trust that Jesus can heal her.

“For she said to herself, ‘If only I may touch His garment, I shall be made well.”

Matthew reveals what she was thinking. She is convinced that Jesus can heal her. Her reaching out expresses her trust in Him.

Matthew 9:22

Matthew 9:22 But Jesus turned around, and when He saw her He said, "Be of good cheer, daughter; your faith has made you well." And the woman was made well from that hour.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 9:22 Jesus turned around, and when he saw her he said, “Daughter, be encouraged! Your faith has made you well.” And the woman was healed at that moment. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus turns, sees her, and says, “Be of good cheer, daughter; your faith has made you well,” and she is healed from that hour. He does not let her slip away unnoticed but personally welcomes and restores her, calling her “daughter.”

Verse Explanation

Jesus turns, sees her, and says, “Be of good cheer, daughter; your faith has made you well,” and she is healed from that hour. He does not let her slip away unnoticed but personally welcomes and restores her, calling her “daughter.”

“But Jesus turned around, and when He saw her He said,”

Jesus does not let her slip away unnoticed. He turns and looks at her.

“Be of good cheer, daughter; your faith has made you well.”

He speaks to her personally. He calls her “daughter,” a word of tenderness and belonging. Her illness had lasted twelve years, and under the law it made her ceremonially unclean (Leviticus 15:25–27). Jesus does not let her go unnoticed. He addresses her personally and welcomes her.

He tells her that her “faith” has made her well. It was not the cloth that healed her. It was Jesus, whom she trusted.

“And the woman was made well from that hour.”

The healing is immediate and lasting.

Study Notes

Jesus says be of good cheer both to the paralytic in 9:2 and to the woman here.

Jesus goes through all the cities and villages, teaching in their synagogues, preaching the gospel of the kingdom, and healing every sickness and disease. Matthew repeats the summary of Matthew 4:23, framing the words and works of chapters 5–9.
