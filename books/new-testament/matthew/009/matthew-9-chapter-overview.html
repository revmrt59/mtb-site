Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 9
Main Idea:

Jesus shows His authority to forgive sins, welcomes sinners to His table, brings the joy and newness of the Bridegroom, restores life, health, and sight, and, moved with compassion for the shepherdless crowds, tells His disciples to pray for laborers for the harvest.

Setting:

Matthew 9 completes the account of Jesus’ mighty works begun in chapter 8. He returns to Capernaum, “His own city” (9:1), where opposition from religious leaders begins to grow.

Chapter Outline:

  • Matthew 9:1–8 — Jesus Forgives and Heals a Paralytic

  • Matthew 9:9–13 — Jesus Calls Matthew and Eats with Sinners

  • Matthew 9:14–17 — The Bridegroom, New Cloth, and New Wine

  • Matthew 9:18–26 — A Ruler’s Daughter Raised and a Woman Healed

  • Matthew 9:27–31 — Two Blind Men Receive Their Sight

  • Matthew 9:32–34 — A Mute Man Delivered and Two Responses

  • Matthew 9:35–38 — Compassion for the Multitudes and Prayer for Laborers

Key Verse:

“But go and learn what this means: ‘I desire mercy and not sacrifice.’ For I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners, to repentance.’” (Matthew 9:13)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The chapter opens with a paralyzed man carried by friends. Jesus sees their faith, the paralytic’s together with his friends’, and first says, “Son, be of good cheer; your sins are forgiven you” (9:2). When scribes call this blasphemy, Jesus heals the man so they may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins. Then He calls Matthew from the tax office and eats with tax collectors and sinners. To the Pharisees He quotes Hosea: “I desire mercy and not sacrifice” (9:13). The Physician has come for the sick, calling sinners to repentance.

Jesus’ presence changes everything. Wedding guests do not fast while the Bridegroom is with them, and His new work cannot be patched onto old religious habits. It needs new wineskins. The restorations that follow show the same life: a ruler’s daughter raised, a woman healed after twelve years, two blind men given sight, and a mute man set free. Some marvel, while the Pharisees accuse Him of working by the ruler of demons. The chapter ends with Jesus Himself, seeing weary crowds like sheep without a shepherd, moved with compassion, and asking His disciples to pray for workers.

Chapter Sections

Matthew 9:1–8 — Jesus Forgives and Heals a Paralytic

Jesus tells a paralyzed man that his sins are forgiven, and when scribes accuse Him of blasphemy, He heals the man to show His authority to forgive. The crowds glorify God for giving such authority.

Matthew 9:9–13 — Jesus Calls Matthew and Eats with Sinners

Jesus calls Matthew the tax collector and eats with tax collectors and sinners. Answering the Pharisees, He says that the sick need a physician and that God desires mercy, not sacrifice.

Matthew 9:14–17 — The Bridegroom, New Cloth, and New Wine

Asked why His disciples do not fast, Jesus says the friends of the Bridegroom cannot mourn while He is with them, though days of mourning will come. New cloth and new wine show that His new work requires new wineskins.

Matthew 9:18–26 — A Ruler’s Daughter Raised and a Woman Healed

A ruler asks Jesus to raise his daughter, and on the way a woman with a twelve-year flow of blood touches His garment in faith. Jesus tells her that her faith has made her well, then takes the girl by the hand and she rises.

Matthew 9:27–31 — Two Blind Men Receive Their Sight

Two blind men cry out to the Son of David for mercy. Jesus asks whether they believe He can do this, touches their eyes, and they see.

Matthew 9:32–34 — A Mute Man Delivered and Two Responses

Jesus casts a demon out of a mute man, and he speaks. The crowds marvel, but the Pharisees say Jesus casts out demons by the ruler of the demons.

Matthew 9:35–38 — Compassion for the Multitudes and Prayer for Laborers

Jesus teaches, preaches, and heals in all the cities and villages. Seeing the weary crowds like sheep without a shepherd, He is moved with compassion and tells His disciples to pray the Lord of the harvest to send out laborers.

Why This Chapter Matters

Matthew 9 shows that Jesus’ deepest work is forgiveness. The healing of the paralytic makes visible an authority that belongs to God alone, and the table with sinners shows that His mercy reaches people others had written off. Religious knowledge can exist without mercy, and Jesus sends the Pharisees back to Hosea to learn it.

The chapter also shows where mission begins. Before Jesus sends the Twelve in chapter 10, He looks at the crowds with compassion and tells His disciples to pray. The work of the harvest grows out of His care for people and depends on His sending, not on human plans.

Key Themes to Watch For

Authority to forgive. The Son of Man forgives sins and proves it by healing (9:2–8).

Mercy toward sinners. Jesus calls Matthew and eats with sinners, quoting Hosea 6:6 (9:9–13).

The Bridegroom’s newness. His presence brings joy and calls for new wineskins (9:15–17).

Faith that reaches Him. The paralytic and his friends, the ruler, the woman, and the blind men bring their need to Jesus (9:2, 18, 21, 28).

Compassion and harvest. Jesus’ compassion leads to prayer for laborers (9:36–38).

Key Words & Concepts

Blasphemy (9:3). Speaking against God or claiming what belongs to Him. The scribes understand that forgiving sins is God’s right.

Tax collectors (9:9–10). Matthew sat at a tax office collecting taxes. People in this occupation were often dishonest and were widely despised as sinners.

Bridegroom (9:15). Jesus’ picture of Himself, recalling God as Israel’s husband (Isaiah 62:5). His presence is a time of joy.

New wine and new wineskins (9:17). Fresh wine expanded as it fermented and needed flexible skins. Jesus’ new work calls for hearts and fellowships that are soft and open, not hardened by religious routine.

Son of David (9:27). A title for the promised Messiah from David’s line. The blind men call Jesus by it as they ask for mercy.

Moved with compassion (9:36). A deep inward stirring of pity that leads to action. It is what moves Jesus toward the crowds.
