Chapter Study

Matthew 10

Verse-by-Verse
Matthew 10:1
Matthew 10:1
NKJV

Matthew 10:1 And when He had called His twelve disciples to Him, He gave them power over unclean spirits, to cast them out, and to heal all kinds of sickness and all kinds of disease.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 10:1 Jesus called his twelve disciples together and gave them authority to cast out evil spirits and to heal every kind of disease and illness. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus calls His twelve disciples and gives them power over unclean spirits to cast them out and to heal every kind of sickness and disease. He answers the harvest prayer of chapter 9 by sharing His own authority with those He sends.

Verse Explanation

Jesus calls His twelve disciples and gives them power over unclean spirits to cast them out and to heal every kind of sickness and disease. He answers the harvest prayer of chapter 9 by sharing His own authority with those He sends.

“And when He had called His twelve disciples to Him,”

Chapter 9 ended with Jesus telling His disciples to pray for laborers (9:38). Now He answers that prayer by sending some of the ones who prayed. He calls “His twelve disciples” to Himself. The number twelve recalls the twelve tribes of Israel. Jesus is gathering a renewed people of God around Himself.

“He gave them power over unclean spirits, to cast them out, and to heal all kinds of sickness and all kinds of disease.”

Jesus shares His own authority with them. They will do what He has been doing: cast out “unclean spirits” and heal every kind of sickness. The words echo the summary of His own ministry in 4:23 and 9:35. The disciples go out as His representatives, carrying His authority.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “power” means authority. It is the same word used of Jesus’ authority to forgive sins in 9:6.

Matthew 10:2
Matthew 10:2
NKJV

Matthew 10:2 Now the names of the twelve apostles are these: first, Simon, who is called Peter, and Andrew his brother; James the son of Zebedee, and John his brother;(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 10:2 Here are the names of the twelve apostles: first, Simon (also called Peter),then Andrew (Peter’s brother),James (son of Zebedee),John (James’s brother), (NLT)

Main Idea

Matthew lists the twelve apostles, beginning with Simon called Peter and his brother Andrew, and James and John, the sons of Zebedee. These are the first fishermen Jesus called, now named as “apostles,” men sent with His authority.

Verse Explanation

Matthew lists the twelve apostles, beginning with Simon called Peter and his brother Andrew, and James and John, the sons of Zebedee. These are the first fishermen Jesus called, now named as “apostles,” men sent with His authority.

“Now the names of the twelve apostles are these:”

This is the only place where Matthew calls them “apostles.” The word means those who are sent. They are disciples, learners who follow Jesus, and now apostles, messengers whom He sends.

“first, Simon, who is called Peter, and Andrew his brother;”

“Simon” is named “first.” He is called “Peter,” meaning rock (16:18). He often speaks for the group. His brother “Andrew” was called with him (4:18).

“James the son of Zebedee, and John his brother;”

The second pair of brothers are “James” and “John,” the sons of Zebedee. They were fishermen who left their father’s boat to follow Jesus (4:21–22).

Study Notes

The lists of the Twelve appear in Matthew 10:2–4, Mark 3:16–19, Luke 6:14–16, and Acts 1:13. Peter is always first and Judas Iscariot always last.

Matthew 10:3
Matthew 10:3
NKJV

Matthew 10:3 Philip and Bartholomew; Thomas and Matthew the tax collector; James the son of Alphaeus, and Lebbaeus, whose surname was Thaddaeus;(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 10:3 Philip,Bartholomew,Thomas,Matthew (the tax collector),James (son of Alphaeus),Thaddaeus, (NLT)

Main Idea

The list continues with Philip, Bartholomew, Thomas, “Matthew the tax collector,” James the son of Alphaeus, and Thaddaeus. Matthew names himself by his former, despised trade, a reminder of the grace that called him.

Verse Explanation

The list continues with Philip, Bartholomew, Thomas, “Matthew the tax collector,” James the son of Alphaeus, and Thaddaeus. Matthew names himself by his former, despised trade, a reminder of the grace that called him.

“Philip and Bartholomew;”

“Philip” was from Bethsaida, the hometown of Peter and Andrew (John 1:44). “Bartholomew” is often identified with Nathanael, whom Philip brought to Jesus (John 1:45).

“Thomas and Matthew the tax collector;”

“Thomas” is remembered for his doubts after the resurrection and for his confession, “My Lord and my God!” (John 20:28). Matthew calls himself “the tax collector.” He does not hide his past. He is a living example of the mercy Jesus showed to sinners (9:9–13).

“James the son of Alphaeus, and Lebbaeus, whose surname was Thaddaeus;”

This second “James” is identified by his father. “Lebbaeus” was also called “Thaddaeus.” Luke and Acts call him Judas the son of James (Luke 6:16; Acts 1:13).

Study Notes

Only Matthew’s list adds the tax collector to Matthew’s name.

Some manuscripts read only Thaddaeus in this verse.

Matthew 10:4
Matthew 10:4
NKJV

Matthew 10:4 Simon the Cananite, and Judas Iscariot, who also betrayed Him.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 10:4 Simon (the zealot ),Judas Iscariot (who later betrayed him). (NLT)

Main Idea

The list ends with Simon the Cananite and “Judas Iscariot, who also betrayed Him.” From the beginning Matthew marks the betrayer within the chosen circle.

Verse Explanation

The list ends with Simon the Cananite and “Judas Iscariot, who also betrayed Him.” From the beginning Matthew marks the betrayer within the chosen circle.

“Simon the Cananite,”

This “Simon” is distinguished from Simon Peter. “Cananite” comes from an Aramaic word meaning zealous. Luke calls him Simon the Zealot (Luke 6:15). He may once have belonged to a movement that zealously opposed Roman rule.

Jesus’ chosen twelve included a tax collector who had worked with Rome and a zealot who may have opposed it. Jesus brings together people who would otherwise have been enemies.

“and Judas Iscariot, who also betrayed Him.”

The list ends with “Judas Iscariot.” Matthew identifies him by what he will do: he “betrayed Him.” Even Judas received authority and was sent out with the others.

Study Notes

Iscariot may mean man of Kerioth, a town in southern Judea (Joshua 15:25). If so, Judas may have been the only one of the Twelve from Judea.

Matthew 10:5–15 — The Twelve Sent to the Lost Sheep of Israel

Matthew 10:5
Matthew 10:5
NKJV

Matthew 10:5 These twelve Jesus sent out and commanded them, saying: "Do not go into the way of the Gentiles, and do not enter a city of the Samaritans.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 10:5 Jesus sent out the twelve apostles with these instructions: “Don’t go to the Gentiles or the Samaritans, (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus sends out the Twelve with instructions not to go among the Gentiles or enter a Samaritan town. This first mission has a deliberately limited scope.

Verse Explanation

Jesus sends out the Twelve with instructions not to go among the Gentiles or enter a Samaritan town. This first mission has a deliberately limited scope.

“These twelve Jesus sent out and commanded them, saying:”

Jesus sends out the Twelve with instructions. The rest of the chapter records what He told them.

“Do not go into the way of the Gentiles, and do not enter a city of the Samaritans.”

For this mission, Jesus limits where they may go. They must not go to “the Gentiles,” non-Jewish peoples. They must not enter “a city of the Samaritans,” the mixed people living between Galilee and Judea.

This restriction belongs to this particular sending. It does not mean Jesus cares nothing for the nations. After His resurrection, He will send His disciples to all nations (28:19).

Study Notes

The Samaritans descended from Israelites who remained in the northern kingdom and from peoples the Assyrians settled there (2 Kings 17:24–41). Jews and Samaritans had long been divided.

Matthew 10:6
Matthew 10:6
NKJV

Matthew 10:6 But go rather to the lost sheep of the house of Israel.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 10:6 but only to the people of Israel — God’s lost sheep. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus sends the Twelve instead “to the lost sheep of the house of Israel.” As Israel’s promised King, Jesus seeks His own people first, a priority later widened to all nations without abandoning Israel.

Verse Explanation

Jesus sends the Twelve instead “to the lost sheep of the house of Israel.” As Israel’s promised King, Jesus seeks His own people first, a priority later widened to all nations without abandoning Israel.

“But go rather to the lost sheep of the house of Israel.”

The disciples are to go to Israel first. Jesus describes the people as “lost sheep.” He had seen them as sheep without a shepherd (9:36).

The Messiah came first to the people God had promised to save. God made His promises to Abraham and his descendants. Jesus keeps those promises by gathering Israel first. Later the mission will reach the nations, and the blessing promised to Abraham will reach all the families of the earth (Genesis 22:18). Israel is not replaced. The King’s people grow around Him to include the nations.

Study Notes

Jesus says the same of His own mission in 15:24: “I was not sent except to the lost sheep of the house of Israel.”

Paul writes that Christ became a servant to the circumcision to confirm the promises made to the fathers, and that the Gentiles might glorify God for His mercy (Romans 15:8–9).

Verse Explanation

As the Twelve go, they are to preach, “The kingdom of heaven is at hand.” They carry the same message Jesus Himself proclaims.

The disciples carry the same message that John and Jesus preached (3:2; 4:17). God’s rule has come near in the person of Jesus.

Matthew 10:8

Main Idea

The Twelve are to heal the sick, cleanse lepers, raise the dead, and cast out demons, and “Freely you have received, freely give.” Their works display the kingdom’s restoring power, and since they received this authority as a gift, they must not sell it.

Verse Explanation

The Twelve are to heal the sick, cleanse lepers, raise the dead, and cast out demons, and “Freely you have received, freely give.” Their works display the kingdom’s restoring power, and since they received this authority as a gift, they must not sell it.

“Heal the sick, cleanse the lepers, raise the dead, cast out demons.”

The disciples are to do the works Jesus has been doing in chapters 8–9. He has healed the sick, cleansed a leper, raised the dead, and cast out demons. Their deeds will show that God’s kingdom has come.

“Freely you have received, freely give.”

The disciples received their authority as a free gift. They must not sell it. They are to give freely what grace has given them.

Study Notes

Some manuscripts do not include raise the dead.

Peter refused money from Simon the sorcerer, who wanted to buy the power to give the Holy Spirit (Acts 8:18–20).

Verse Explanation

Jesus tells the Twelve to take no gold, silver, or copper in their money belts. Their mission depends on God’s provision, not their own resources.

Matthew 10:10
Matthew 10:10
NKJV

Matthew 10:10 nor bag for your journey, nor two tunics, nor sandals, nor staffs; for a worker is worthy of his food.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 10:10 Don’t carry a traveler’s bag with a change of clothes and sandals or even a walking stick. Don’t hesitate to accept hospitality, because those who work deserve to be fed. (NLT)

Main Idea

Nor are Jesus’ messengers to take a travel bag, an extra tunic, sandals, or staffs, because “a worker is worthy of his food.” They are to travel light and trust God to supply their needs through those who receive them.

Verse Explanation

Nor are Jesus’ messengers to take a travel bag, an extra tunic, sandals, or staffs, because “a worker is worthy of his food.” They are to travel light and trust God to supply their needs through those who receive them.

“nor bag for your journey, nor two tunics, nor sandals, nor staffs;”

They are not to take a traveler’s “bag” for supplies, an extra tunic, spare sandals, or a staff. They travel light. They depend on God and on the people who welcome them.

These instructions belong to this particular sending. Later Jesus will tell His disciples to take a money bag and a sack (Luke 22:35–36). What remains for every disciple is the principle of dependence on God and generosity in ministry.

“for a worker is worthy of his food.”

God will provide for them through the hospitality of others. A “worker” deserves to be fed.

Study Notes

Mark 6:8–9 allows a staff and sandals. Matthew’s wording may forbid taking an additional staff and sandals for the journey.

Paul applies this principle to the support of ministers (1 Corinthians 9:14; 1 Timothy 5:18).

Matthew 10:16–23 — Sheep among Wolves: Persecution and Endurance

Matthew 10:16
Matthew 10:16
NKJV

Matthew 10:16 "Behold, I send you out as sheep in the midst of wolves. Therefore be wise as serpents and harmless as doves.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 10:16 “Look, I am sending you out as sheep among wolves. So be as shrewd as snakes and harmless as doves. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus sends them out “as sheep in the midst of wolves” and tells them to be “wise as serpents and harmless as doves.” Vulnerable among hostile people, they need shrewd discernment joined with innocent integrity.

Verse Explanation

Jesus sends them out “as sheep in the midst of wolves” and tells them to be “wise as serpents and harmless as doves.” Vulnerable among hostile people, they need shrewd discernment joined with innocent integrity.

“Behold, I send you out as sheep in the midst of wolves.”

Jesus now looks beyond the immediate journey. His messengers will be vulnerable, like “sheep” surrounded by “wolves.” The world will be hostile.

“Therefore be wise as serpents and harmless as doves.”

They need two qualities. They must be “wise as serpents,” shrewd and careful, aware of danger. They must also be “harmless as doves,” innocent and pure, without deceit. Wisdom without innocence becomes cunning. Innocence without wisdom becomes naivety. The disciple needs both.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “harmless” means unmixed or pure. Paul uses it in Romans 16:19, where he wants believers to be wise in what is good and simple concerning evil.

Verse Explanation

Jesus warns them to beware of people who will hand them over to councils and flog them in their synagogues. His messengers will face official opposition, even from religious authorities.

“But beware of men,”

“for they will deliver you up to councils and scourge you in their synagogues.”

They will be handed over to “councils,” local Jewish courts. They will be whipped in “synagogues,” where such punishments were carried out. The opposition will come first from their own people.

Study Notes

Paul received the synagogue punishment of thirty-nine lashes five times (2 Corinthians 11:24). The apostles were beaten by order of the Sanhedrin (Acts 5:40).

Matthew 10:23
Matthew 10:23
NKJV

Matthew 10:23 When they persecute you in this city, flee to another. For assuredly, I say to you, you will not have gone through the cities of Israel before the Son of Man comes.(NKJV)

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

When the Twelve are persecuted in one town, they are to flee to another, for they will not finish going through the cities of Israel before the Son of Man comes. Jesus’ resurrection in royal authority would come first and open the mission to all nations, while His return in glory remains the final horizon for a mission to Israel that is not yet finished.

“When they persecute you in this city, flee to another.”

Jesus gives permission to flee. When persecution comes in one place, they may go to another. Fleeing danger is not cowardice. It allows the message to spread to new places. The church in Acts was scattered by persecution and preached wherever it went (Acts 8:1, 4).

“For assuredly, I say to you, you will not have gone through the cities of Israel before the Son of Man comes.”

Jesus promises that He will come before they have finished going through Israel’s towns. Their mission is urgent, and it will not be completed before He comes.

This coming has a decisive first stage in Jesus’ resurrection. When He rose, He received all authority in heaven and on earth (28:18). That authority opened the mission beyond Israel to all nations (28:19), before the disciples had finished Israel’s towns. The final horizon is Jesus’ return in glory. The mission to Israel continues until then.

Study Notes

This verse has been understood in many ways. Jesus’ resurrection authority (28:18–20) and His final return in glory (25:31) belong together in its horizon.
