Matthew 10 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Matthew 10 records the second of Jesus’ major discourses as He gives His twelve disciples authority over unclean spirits and sickness and sends them to the lost sheep of the house of Israel to preach that the kingdom of heaven is at hand. His instructions then reach beyond that first journey to the persecution His messengers will face, the Father’s care that frees them from fear, the cost of loving Him above family and life, and the reward of those who receive them.

Chapter Sections

1. Matthew 10:1–4 — Jesus Gives Authority to the Twelve

Jesus calls His twelve disciples and gives them power over unclean spirits, to cast them out, and to heal all kinds of sickness and disease. Matthew then lists the names of the twelve apostles, beginning with Simon called Peter and ending with Judas Iscariot, “who also betrayed Him” (Matthew 10:4).

2. Matthew 10:5–15 — The Twelve Sent to the Lost Sheep of Israel

Jesus sends the Twelve not to the Gentiles or Samaritans but to “the lost sheep of the house of Israel” (Matthew 10:6), to preach that the kingdom of heaven is at hand and to heal, cleanse, raise the dead, and cast out demons, giving freely as they have received. They are to take no provisions but stay with worthy households, and from any house or city that will not receive them they are to shake off the dust from their feet.

3. Matthew 10:16–23 — Sheep among Wolves: Persecution and Endurance

Jesus sends His disciples as sheep in the midst of wolves and tells them to be wise as serpents and harmless as doves, warning that they will be delivered to councils, scourged, and brought before governors and kings for His sake. When that happens the Spirit of their Father will speak in them, and though they will be hated by all for His name’s sake, “he who endures to the end will be saved” (Matthew 10:22), and when they are persecuted in one city, they are to flee to another.

4. Matthew 10:24–33 — Do Not Fear Those Who Kill the Body

Since a disciple is not above his teacher, Jesus’ followers should expect the treatment He receives, yet they are not to fear people but to speak openly what He tells them. They are to fear God, who can destroy both soul and body in hell, and to trust the Father who cares for every sparrow, for whoever confesses Jesus before men He will confess before His Father in heaven, and whoever denies Him before men He will deny before His Father.

5. Matthew 10:34–39 — Not Peace but a Sword: Loving Jesus Above All

Jesus declares, “I did not come to bring peace but a sword” (Matthew 10:34), for allegiance to Him will set members of a household against one another. Whoever loves father, mother, son, or daughter more than Him, or does not take up his cross and follow Him, is not worthy of Him, and whoever loses his life for His sake will find it.

6. Matthew 10:40–42 — Receiving Jesus’ Messengers

Jesus declares that whoever receives His messengers receives Him and the One who sent Him. Whoever receives a prophet or a righteous man will receive that one’s reward, and whoever gives one of these little ones a cup of cold water in the name of a disciple will by no means lose his reward.
