Matthew 11:1
Matthew 11:1
NKJV

Matthew 11:1 Now it came to pass, when Jesus finished commanding His twelve disciples, that He departed from there to teach and to preach in their cities.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 11:1 When Jesus had finished giving these instructions to his twelve disciples, he went out to teach and preach in towns throughout the region. (NLT)

Main Idea

When Jesus finishes instructing His twelve disciples, He leaves to teach and preach in their cities. The mission discourse closes, and Jesus Himself continues the work He has just sent His disciples to share.

Verse Explanation

When Jesus finishes instructing His twelve disciples, He leaves to teach and preach in their cities. The mission discourse closes, and Jesus Himself continues the work He has just sent His disciples to share.

“Now it came to pass, when Jesus finished commanding His twelve disciples,”

Jesus has finished instructing the Twelve. Matthew marks the end of each of Jesus’ five major teaching sections with similar words (7:28; 13:53; 19:1; 26:1).

“that He departed from there to teach and to preach in their cities.”

Jesus does not stop working while the disciples go out. He continues His own ministry of teaching and preaching in the towns of Galilee.

Study Notes

The phrase in their cities likely refers to the towns of Galilee.

Matthew 11:2–6 — John’s Question from Prison

Matthew 11:2
Matthew 11:2
NKJV

Matthew 11:2 And when John had heard in prison about the works of Christ, he sent two of his disciples(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 11:2 John the Baptist, who was in prison, heard about all the things the Messiah was doing. So he sent his disciples to ask Jesus, (NLT)

Main Idea

While in prison, John hears about the works of Christ and sends two of his disciples to Him. The faithful forerunner, now suffering for his ministry, brings his question to Jesus Himself.

Verse Explanation

While in prison, John hears about the works of Christ and sends two of his disciples to Him. The faithful forerunner, now suffering for his ministry, brings his question to Jesus Himself.

“And when John had heard in prison about the works of Christ,”

John the Baptist is “in prison.” Herod Antipas had arrested him for condemning Herod’s marriage to his brother’s wife (14:3–4). From prison, John hears about “the works of Christ,” the deeds Matthew has recorded in chapters 8–9.

“he sent two of his disciples”

John cannot come himself, so he sends two of his followers to Jesus.

Study Notes

The Jewish historian Josephus says that John was imprisoned at Machaerus, a fortress east of the Dead Sea.

Matthew calls Jesus Christ here, the Greek form of Messiah. He identifies Jesus as the One whose works John has heard about.

Main Idea

Through his disciples John asks, “Are You the Coming One, or do we look for another?” Jesus answers by pointing to His works; John’s imprisonment may not have matched what he expected of the Messiah, though Matthew does not state his exact reasons for asking.

Verse Explanation

Through his disciples John asks, “Are You the Coming One, or do we look for another?” Jesus answers by pointing to His works; John’s imprisonment may not have matched what he expected of the Messiah, though Matthew does not state his exact reasons for asking.

“and said to Him, ‘Are You the Coming One, or do we look for another?”

John’s question is direct. “The Coming One” was a title for the Messiah. John had announced One coming after him who would baptize with the Holy Spirit and fire and bring judgment (3:11–12).

Now John is in prison. Jesus is healing and preaching, but He has not overthrown the wicked or set John free. John may have wondered why Jesus did not look like the Messiah he expected. He brings his question honestly to Jesus.

Study Notes

Psalm 118:26 says, “Blessed is he who comes in the name of the LORD!” The crowds will shout these words at Jesus’ entry into Jerusalem (21:9).

Matthew 11:5
Matthew 11:5
NKJV

Matthew 11:5 The blind see and the lame walk; the lepers are cleansed and the deaf hear; the dead are raised up and the poor have the gospel preached to them.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 11:5 the blind see, the lame walk, those with leprosy are cured, the deaf hear, the dead are raised to life, and the Good News is being preached to the poor.” (NLT)

Main Idea

The blind see, the lame walk, lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the poor hear the gospel. These works echo Isaiah’s promises of the Messiah’s day, showing John that God’s promised salvation has arrived in Jesus.

Verse Explanation

The blind see, the lame walk, lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the poor hear the gospel. These works echo Isaiah’s promises of the Messiah’s day, showing John that God’s promised salvation has arrived in Jesus.

“The blind see and the lame walk; the lepers are cleansed and the deaf hear;”

Jesus describes His works in words that echo Isaiah’s prophecies. Isaiah promised that in the day of God’s salvation the eyes of the blind would be opened, the ears of the deaf unstopped, and the lame would leap like a deer (Isaiah 35:5–6).

“the dead are raised up and the poor have the gospel preached to them.”

Jesus adds two more signs: life for the dead and good news for the poor. The second echoes Isaiah 61:1, where the Anointed One is sent to preach good news to the poor.

John knew these prophecies. Jesus is telling him that the promises are being fulfilled. The Messiah has come.

Study Notes

Isaiah 35 also speaks of God coming with vengeance (Isaiah 35:4), and Isaiah 61:2 speaks of the day of vengeance. Jesus’ answer emphasizes salvation. The judgment John announced will come, but not yet.

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

As the messengers leave, Jesus asks the crowds what they went into the wilderness to see, “A reed shaken by the wind?” John was no wavering man swayed by every pressure.

After John’s messengers leave, Jesus speaks to the crowd about John. He does not want them to think less of John because of his question.

“What did you go out into the wilderness to see? A reed shaken by the wind?”

Many had gone out to the wilderness to hear John. Jesus asks what they expected. “A reed shaken by the wind” bends whichever way the wind blows. John was not that kind of man. He spoke the truth even to a king, and he went to prison for it.

Study Notes

Reeds grew along the Jordan River, where John baptized.

John was a prophet, but he was more than that. He was the one whom the prophets themselves foretold.

Main Idea

John is the one of whom Scripture says, “Behold, I send My messenger before Your face, Who will prepare Your way before You.” As Malachi foretold, John is the forerunner who prepares the way for the Lord’s coming in Jesus.

Verse Explanation

John is the one of whom Scripture says, “Behold, I send My messenger before Your face, Who will prepare Your way before You.” As Malachi foretold, John is the forerunner who prepares the way for the Lord’s coming in Jesus.

“For this is he of whom it is written:”

Jesus identifies John from Scripture.

“Behold, I send My messenger before Your face, Who will prepare Your way before You.”

The quotation draws on Malachi 3:1. God promised to send His “messenger” ahead of Him to prepare the way. John is that messenger. He prepared the way for Jesus.

In Malachi, the messenger prepares the way before the LORD Himself. By applying the words to John and Himself, Jesus identifies His own coming with the coming of the LORD.

Study Notes

The wording also echoes Exodus 23:20, where God promises to send an Angel before Israel. The Greek word for messenger is also the word for angel.

Matthew 11:11
Matthew 11:11
NKJV

Matthew 11:11 "Assuredly, I say to you, among those born of women there has not risen one greater than John the Baptist; but he who is least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 11:11 “I tell you the truth, of all who have ever lived, none is greater than John the Baptist. Yet even the least person in the Kingdom of Heaven is greater than he is! (NLT)

Main Idea

No one born of women has risen greater than John the Baptist, yet the least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he. John stands at the threshold of the kingdom, and the privilege of sharing in its arrival surpasses even his great role.

Verse Explanation

No one born of women has risen greater than John the Baptist, yet the least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he. John stands at the threshold of the kingdom, and the privilege of sharing in its arrival surpasses even his great role.

“Assuredly, I say to you, among those born of women there has not risen one greater than John the Baptist;”

Jesus gives John the highest praise. No one born of a woman, that is, no human being, has been greater than John. His role in God’s plan was unique.

“but he who is least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he.”

Yet the smallest person in God’s kingdom is greater than John. This is not a judgment on John’s character or salvation. John stood at the turning point between the age of promise and the arrival of the kingdom. The least person in the kingdom has a greater privilege. He lives in the time of fulfillment that John only announced.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “least” is the comparative form, literally lesser. It may mean the lesser one in the kingdom.

Matthew 11:12
Matthew 11:12
NKJV

Matthew 11:12 And from the days of John the Baptist until now the kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and the violent take it by force.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 11:12 And from the time John the Baptist began preaching until now, the Kingdom of Heaven has been forcefully advancing, and violent people are attacking it. (NLT)

Main Idea

From the days of John until now “the kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and the violent take it by force.” As John’s imprisonment shows, the kingdom and its messengers are meeting violent opposition from those who resist it.

Verse Explanation

From the days of John until now “the kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and the violent take it by force.” As John’s imprisonment shows, the kingdom and its messengers are meeting violent opposition from those who resist it.

“And from the days of John the Baptist until now the kingdom of heaven suffers violence,”

Since John began preaching, the kingdom has faced “violence.” John himself sits in prison. Jesus faces growing hostility. The kingdom and its messengers are under attack.

“and the violent take it by force.”

“The violent” are those who oppose the kingdom. They try to seize it and destroy it. Herod’s imprisonment of John is a clear example.

Study Notes

The Greek verb in the first half has the same form whether it means the kingdom is being acted on or the kingdom is acting. Read the first way, as the NKJV does, the kingdom suffers violence. That fits John’s imprisonment and the hostility Jesus faces. Read the second way, the verb describes the kingdom advancing forcefully. Luke 16:16 uses the same verb form in a different saying, where people are pressing into the kingdom. That saying supports reading the response to the kingdom as eager entry rather than opposition. In Matthew’s setting, the NKJV reading of violent opposition remains primary.

The people rejected Jesus too. They called Him “a glutton and a winebibber,” someone who eats and drinks too much. “But wisdom is justified by her children.”

God’s “wisdom” is shown to be right by its results. The lives changed through John’s and Jesus’ ministries prove that God was at work in both.

Matthew 11:21
NKJV

NLT

Matthew 11:21 “What sorrow awaits you, Korazin and Bethsaida! For if the miracles I did in you had been done in wicked Tyre and Sidon, their people would have repented of their sins long ago, clothing themselves in burlap and throwing ashes on their heads to show their remorse. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus pronounces woe on Chorazin and Bethsaida, saying that if Tyre and Sidon had seen such works, they would have repented long ago in sackcloth and ashes. Gentile cities known for pride would have responded better than these privileged Jewish towns.

Verse Explanation

Jesus pronounces woe on Chorazin and Bethsaida, saying that if Tyre and Sidon had seen such works, they would have repented long ago in sackcloth and ashes. Gentile cities known for pride would have responded better than these privileged Jewish towns.

“Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida!”

“Woe” is a cry of grief and a warning of judgment. “Chorazin” was a town about two miles north of Capernaum. “Bethsaida” was on the northern shore of the Sea of Galilee. It was the hometown of Peter, Andrew, and Philip (John 1:44).

“For if the mighty works which were done in you had been done in Tyre and Sidon,”

“Tyre and Sidon” were Gentile cities on the Mediterranean coast. The prophets condemned them for their pride and wickedness (Isaiah 23; Ezekiel 26–28).

“they would have repented long ago in sackcloth and ashes.”

Jesus says these pagan cities would have repented if they had seen what Chorazin and Bethsaida saw. “Sackcloth and ashes” were signs of grief and repentance.

Study Notes

The Gospels record no miracles in Chorazin, and only one in Bethsaida (Mark 8:22–26). Jesus’ words show that He did many works there that are not recorded (John 21:25).

His words echo Isaiah 14:13–15, where the king of Babylon says he will ascend into heaven but is brought down to Sheol.

Even “Sodom,” the city destroyed for its wickedness (Genesis 19:24–25), would have repented if it had seen what Capernaum saw.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “Hades” refers to the realm of the dead. Here it pictures complete humiliation and judgment.

Matthew 11:25–30 — The Son Reveals the Father and Gives Rest

NLT

Matthew 11:25 At that time Jesus prayed this prayer: “O Father, Lord of heaven and earth, thank you for hiding these things from those who think themselves wise and clever, and for revealing them to the childlike. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus thanks the Father, Lord of heaven and earth, for hiding these things from the wise and prudent and revealing them to babes. The Father reveals the truth about His Son not to the self-sufficient but to the humble and dependent.

Verse Explanation

Jesus thanks the Father, Lord of heaven and earth, for hiding these things from the wise and prudent and revealing them to babes. The Father reveals the truth about His Son not to the self-sufficient but to the humble and dependent.

“At that time Jesus answered and said,”

The tone changes from warning to prayer. In the middle of rejection, Jesus turns to His Father.

“I thank You, Father, Lord of heaven and earth,”

Jesus calls God “Father” and “Lord of heaven and earth.” He speaks with closeness and with reverence for God’s rule over all.

“that You have hidden these things from the wise and prudent and have revealed them to babes.”

Jesus thanks the Father for hiding the truth about the kingdom from “the wise and prudent” and revealing it to “babes.” The wise are people who trust their own learning, like the scribes and Pharisees. The babes are humble people, like the disciples, who receive God’s truth with simple trust.

God does not reject learning. He resists pride. The humble receive what the proud refuse.

Study Notes

Paul makes a similar point in 1 Corinthians 1:26–29: God chose the foolish things of the world to put to shame the wise.

“All things have been delivered to Me by My Father,”

Jesus makes a great claim. The Father has given “all things” to Him. This includes all authority and all knowledge of God. Jesus will say after His resurrection that all authority in heaven and on earth has been given to Him (28:18).

Main Idea

Jesus invites, “Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.” He calls everyone weighed down by sin and the exhausting effort of self-reliance to come to Him and receive rest as a gift.

Verse Explanation

Jesus invites, “Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.” He calls everyone weighed down by sin and the exhausting effort of self-reliance to come to Him and receive rest as a gift.

“Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden,”

The One who alone reveals the Father now invites everyone to come to Him. The invitation is for “all.” It is for those who “labor,” who are worn out from effort, and for those who are “heavy laden,” weighed down by burdens.

People carry many burdens: sin, guilt, self-reliance, pride, comparison with others, and the need to prove themselves. Many in Jesus’ day were also burdened by the heavy demands the religious leaders placed on them (23:4).

“and I will give you rest.”

Jesus promises rest. This rest is His gift. It is given to everyone who comes to Him. It is the rest of forgiveness and peace with God, received by coming to Jesus.

Study Notes

The Greek verb translated “give rest” means to refresh or relieve.

Main Idea

Jesus says to take His yoke, learn from Him, because He is gentle and lowly in heart, and they will find rest for their souls. Coming to Jesus leads into a life under His rule and instruction, where disciples learn His meekness and humility and find a deeper rest within.

Verse Explanation

Jesus says to take His yoke, learn from Him, because He is gentle and lowly in heart, and they will find rest for their souls. Coming to Jesus leads into a life under His rule and instruction, where disciples learn His meekness and humility and find a deeper rest within.

“Take My yoke upon you and learn from Me,”

A “yoke” was a wooden frame that joined animals together for work. Teachers spoke of taking on the yoke of the law. Jesus invites people to take His yoke, to come under His teaching and authority and to learn from Him.

“for I am gentle and lowly in heart,”

Jesus is not a harsh master. He is meek and humble. He does not crush those who come to Him.

“and you will find rest for your souls.”

There is a second rest here. In 11:28 Jesus gives rest to those who come to Him. Here, those who take His yoke and learn from Him “will find rest.” This rest is discovered as disciples live under His instruction and become gentle and humble like Him. Pride, self-reliance, and the need to prove ourselves give way to dependence on Him.

Study Notes

The phrase rest for your souls comes from Jeremiah 6:16, where God tells His people to walk in the old paths: “Then you will find rest for your souls.”

Jesus’ “yoke” still carries a “burden.” Following Him still involves obedience and work. But His yoke fits well. It is “easy” in the sense of kind and well-suited. His burden is “light.” He carries it with His disciples.
