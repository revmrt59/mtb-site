Matthew 12:1
Matthew 12:1
NKJV

Matthew 12:1 At that time Jesus went through the grainfields on the Sabbath. And His disciples were hungry, and began to pluck heads of grain and to eat.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 12:1 At about that time Jesus was walking through some grainfields on the Sabbath. His disciples were hungry, so they began breaking off some heads of grain and eating them. (NLT)

Main Idea

On a Sabbath Jesus goes through the grainfields, and His hungry disciples begin to pick heads of grain and eat. The scene sets up a conflict over what faithful Sabbath observance requires.

Verse Explanation

On a Sabbath Jesus goes through the grainfields, and His hungry disciples begin to pick heads of grain and eat. The scene sets up a conflict over what faithful Sabbath observance requires.

“At that time Jesus went through the grainfields on the Sabbath.”

Jesus had just offered rest to the weary (11:28–30). Now a conflict arises over the “Sabbath,” the day of rest God gave Israel. The Sabbath was the seventh day of the week, set apart for rest and worship (Exodus 20:8–11).

“And His disciples were hungry, and began to pluck heads of grain and to eat.”

The disciples are “hungry.” As they walk along the paths through the fields, they pick some heads of grain, rub them in their hands, and eat. The law allowed travelers to pick grain by hand from a neighbor’s field (Deuteronomy 23:25).

Study Notes

Luke 6:1 adds that they rubbed the grain in their hands.

Matthew 12:2
Matthew 12:2
NKJV

Matthew 12:2 And when the Pharisees saw it, they said to Him, "Look, Your disciples are doing what is not lawful to do on the Sabbath!"(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 12:2 But some Pharisees saw them do it and protested, “Look, your disciples are breaking the law by harvesting grain on the Sabbath.” (NLT)

Main Idea

The Pharisees object, “Look, Your disciples are doing what is not lawful to do on the Sabbath!” They treat picking grain as forbidden work and hold Jesus responsible for His disciples.

Verse Explanation

The Pharisees object, “Look, Your disciples are doing what is not lawful to do on the Sabbath!” They treat picking grain as forbidden work and hold Jesus responsible for His disciples.

“And when the Pharisees saw it,”

The Pharisees are watching closely.

“they said to Him, ‘Look, Your disciples are doing what is not lawful to do on the Sabbath!”

The Pharisees do not object to picking grain. They object to doing it on the Sabbath. Their traditions counted plucking grain as reaping and rubbing it as threshing. Both were forbidden work on the Sabbath. Jesus is responsible for His disciples, so they bring the charge to Him.

Study Notes

The law forbade work on the Sabbath (Exodus 20:10) but did not define every kind of work. Jewish tradition later listed thirty-nine categories of forbidden work, including reaping and threshing.

Matthew 12:4
Matthew 12:4
NKJV

Matthew 12:4 how he entered the house of God and ate the showbread which was not lawful for him to eat, nor for those who were with him, but only for the priests?(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 12:4 He went into the house of God, and he and his companions broke the law by eating the sacred loaves of bread that only the priests are allowed to eat. (NLT)

Main Idea

David entered God’s house and ate the showbread, which only the priests were permitted to eat. Scripture itself records that human need took precedence over a ceremonial rule in David’s case.

Verse Explanation

David entered God’s house and ate the showbread, which only the priests were permitted to eat. Scripture itself records that human need took precedence over a ceremonial rule in David’s case.

“how he entered the house of God and ate the showbread”

David was fleeing from Saul. He came to the tabernacle at Nob and asked the priest for food. The only bread available was the “showbread,” the twelve loaves placed before the LORD each Sabbath (Leviticus 24:5–9).

“which was not lawful for him to eat, nor for those who were with him, but only for the priests?”

The law reserved the showbread for the priests. Yet the priest gave it to David and his men because they were hungry. Scripture does not condemn him. Human need took priority over a ceremonial rule.

If David, the anointed king, could do this, how much more can the disciples of the Son of David?

Study Notes

The house of God here refers to the tabernacle, which was at Nob at that time.

Main Idea

Jesus adds that the priests in the temple work on the Sabbath and yet are blameless. The law itself requires temple service that would otherwise count as Sabbath work.

Verse Explanation

Jesus adds that the priests in the temple work on the Sabbath and yet are blameless. The law itself requires temple service that would otherwise count as Sabbath work.

“Or have you not read in the law that on the Sabbath the priests in the temple profane the Sabbath, and are blameless?”

Jesus gives a second example, this time from the law itself. The priests work in the temple on the Sabbath. They offer sacrifices and replace the showbread (Numbers 28:9–10). Technically they break the Sabbath rest, yet they are “blameless.” The service of God in the temple takes priority.

Study Notes

Rabbis taught that temple service overrode the Sabbath.

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

Jesus declares that “in this place there is One greater than the temple.” If temple service justifies Sabbath work, the presence of One greater than the temple carries even greater authority.

“Yet I say to you that in this place there is One greater than the temple.”

Jesus makes a bold claim. If temple service justified the priests’ work, then service to Jesus justifies His disciples. He is greater than “the temple.”

The temple was the place of God’s presence. Jesus is claiming something greater. In Him, God Himself is present (1:23).

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “One greater” is neuter, something greater. It may refer to Jesus Himself or to the kingdom present in Him.

Main Idea

A man with a withered hand is there, and they ask Jesus whether it is lawful to heal on the Sabbath, hoping to accuse Him. They use a suffering man as bait for a legal trap.

Verse Explanation

A man with a withered hand is there, and they ask Jesus whether it is lawful to heal on the Sabbath, hoping to accuse Him. They use a suffering man as bait for a legal trap.

“And behold, there was a man who had a withered hand.”

In the synagogue is a man with a “withered hand.” His hand was shriveled or paralyzed. Luke 6:6 says it was his right hand, the hand he would need for work.

“And they asked Him, saying, ‘Is it lawful to heal on the Sabbath?’—that they might accuse Him.”

The Pharisees ask a question to set a trap. Their goal is “that they might accuse Him.” Their tradition allowed medical help on the Sabbath only if a life was in danger. This man’s condition was not life-threatening. If Jesus healed him, they could accuse Him of breaking the Sabbath.

Study Notes

Mark 3:2 and Luke 6:7 say they watched Him closely to see whether He would heal on the Sabbath.

Matthew 12:18
Matthew 12:18
NKJV

Matthew 12:18 "Behold! My Servant whom I have chosen, My Beloved in whom My soul is well pleased! I will put My Spirit upon Him, And He will declare justice to the Gentiles.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 12:18 “Look at my Servant, whom I have chosen.He is my Beloved, who pleases me.I will put my Spirit upon him,and he will proclaim justice to the nations. (NLT)

Main Idea

Isaiah writes, “Behold! My Servant whom I have chosen, My Beloved in whom My soul is well pleased!” God puts His Spirit on Him, and He will proclaim justice to the Gentiles. The Father’s words at Jesus’ baptism echo here, identifying Jesus as the Spirit-anointed Servant whose mission reaches the nations.

Verse Explanation

Isaiah writes, “Behold! My Servant whom I have chosen, My Beloved in whom My soul is well pleased!” God puts His Spirit on Him, and He will proclaim justice to the Gentiles. The Father’s words at Jesus’ baptism echo here, identifying Jesus as the Spirit-anointed Servant whose mission reaches the nations.

“Behold! My Servant whom I have chosen,”

God speaks of “My Servant.” God has chosen Him for a special task. Matthew identifies Jesus as this Servant.

“My Beloved in whom My soul is well pleased!”

God calls the Servant “My Beloved” and says He is “well pleased” with Him. These words echo the Father’s voice at Jesus’ baptism (3:17).

“I will put My Spirit upon Him,”

God’s “Spirit” rests on the Servant. At Jesus’ baptism the Spirit descended on Him like a dove (3:16).

“And He will declare justice to the Gentiles.”

The Servant will bring “justice” to the nations. His mission reaches beyond Israel. Justice here means God’s right order, His saving rule.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “justice” can also be translated judgment.

Matthew 12:20
Matthew 12:20
NKJV

Matthew 12:20 A bruised reed He will not break, And smoking flax He will not quench, Till He sends forth justice to victory;(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 12:20 He will not crush the weakest reedor put out a flickering candle.Finally he will cause justice to be victorious. (NLT)

Main Idea

“A bruised reed He will not break, And smoking flax He will not quench,” until He brings justice to victory. Jesus is gentle with the weak and faltering, restoring rather than discarding them, while His saving purpose moves on to certain triumph.

Verse Explanation

“A bruised reed He will not break, And smoking flax He will not quench,” until He brings justice to victory. Jesus is gentle with the weak and faltering, restoring rather than discarding them, while His saving purpose moves on to certain triumph.

“A bruised reed He will not break,”

A “bruised reed” is a damaged stalk, bent and almost broken. Most people would snap it off and throw it away. The Servant does not.

“And smoking flax He will not quench,”

“Smoking flax” is a lamp wick that is almost out. It gives more smoke than light. Most people would put it out. The Servant does not.

These images picture people who are weak, wounded, and failing. Jesus does not discard them. He restores them. Believers who are damaged or whose faith has grown faint can come to Him. He draws them back to His Word, to prayer, to repentance, and to life in the Spirit. He renews what is almost gone.

“Till He sends forth justice to victory;”

His gentleness is not weakness. He will bring “justice to victory.” He will complete His mission and establish God’s rule. His gentleness serves His victorious purpose.

Study Notes

The images are pictures, not a description of how every weak believer came to be weak. Isaiah 42:3 reads, “A bruised reed He will not break, And smoking flax He will not quench; He will bring forth justice for truth.”

Jesus explains the sign. Jonah spent three days and nights inside a great fish (Jonah 1:17). Then he came out alive.

Jesus will be in the grave for the same length of time, and then He will rise. His death and resurrection will be the great sign.

Counting these days most naturally places Jesus’ crucifixion on Thursday, before a special Passover Sabbath that fell on Friday, followed by the weekly Sabbath on Saturday. This gives three nights and three days in the tomb, Thursday, Friday, and Saturday, before the women found the tomb empty at dawn on Sunday (28:1–6). Other faithful interpreters explain the phrase by the Jewish custom of counting any part of a day as a whole day.

Verse Explanation

Whoever does the will of His Father in heaven is His brother and sister and mother. Doing the Father’s will marks those who belong to Jesus’ family, a bond deeper than any natural tie.

“For whoever does the will of My Father in heaven is My brother and sister and mother.”

Jesus defines His true family. It is made up of those who do “the will of My Father.” Anyone, man or woman, can belong. Doing the Father’s will marks a person as part of Jesus’ family.

Doing God’s will does not earn a place in the family. It shows that a person belongs. It begins with believing in the One God sent (John 6:29).

These family members are the brethren of Jesus whom He will mention in 25:40.

Study Notes

Jesus adds sister, though only His brothers are mentioned as standing outside. Women are fully included in His family.
