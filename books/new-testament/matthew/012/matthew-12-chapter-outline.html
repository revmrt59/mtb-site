Matthew 12 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Matthew 12 shows opposition to Jesus hardening as the Pharisees challenge Him over the Sabbath, plot how they might destroy Him, and attribute His casting out of demons to Beelzebub. Matthew presents Jesus as the Lord of the Sabbath and the gentle Servant of Isaiah’s prophecy, and Jesus answers His opponents with warnings about blasphemy against the Spirit, the words that come from the heart, the sign of the prophet Jonah, and the danger of an empty house, before naming those who do His Father’s will as His family.

Chapter Sections

1. Matthew 12:1–8 — Lord of the Sabbath

When the Pharisees accuse Jesus’ disciples of doing what is unlawful on the Sabbath by plucking heads of grain, Jesus answers from what David did and from the priests’ service in the temple. He declares that One greater than the temple is here, that God desires mercy and not sacrifice, and that “the Son of Man is Lord even of the Sabbath” (Matthew 12:8).

2. Matthew 12:9–14 — Healing a Withered Hand on the Sabbath

In the synagogue Jesus is asked whether it is lawful to heal on the Sabbath, and He answers that if they would lift a sheep out of a pit, it is lawful to do good on the Sabbath. He restores the man’s withered hand, and the Pharisees go out and plot against Him, “how they might destroy Him” (Matthew 12:14).

3. Matthew 12:15–21 — The Chosen Servant of Isaiah’s Prophecy

Knowing their plot, Jesus withdraws, heals all who follow Him, and warns them not to make Him known. Matthew says this fulfills Isaiah’s prophecy of God’s chosen Servant, upon whom He puts His Spirit, who will not quarrel or cry out, will not break a bruised reed or quench smoking flax, and in whose name Gentiles will trust.

4. Matthew 12:22–32 — The Beelzebub Accusation and Blasphemy against the Spirit

When Jesus heals a demon-possessed man who is blind and mute, the multitudes ask whether this could be the Son of David, but the Pharisees say He casts out demons by Beelzebub. Jesus answers that a kingdom divided against itself cannot stand, that if He casts out demons by the Spirit of God the kingdom of God has come upon them, that no one can plunder a strong man’s goods unless he first binds the strong man, and that blasphemy against the Spirit will not be forgiven.

5. Matthew 12:33–37 — Good and Bad Trees and the Words of the Heart

Jesus declares that a tree is known by its fruit and that “out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks” (Matthew 12:34). He warns that people will give account for every idle word in the day of judgment, for by their words they will be justified or condemned.

6. Matthew 12:38–42 — The Sign of Jonah

When some of the scribes and Pharisees ask for a sign, Jesus answers that no sign will be given to an evil and adulterous generation except the sign of the prophet Jonah: as Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of the great fish, so the Son of Man will be in the heart of the earth. The men of Nineveh and the queen of the South will rise up in the judgment and condemn this generation, for One greater than Jonah and Solomon is here.

7. Matthew 12:43–45 — The Return of the Unclean Spirit

Jesus describes an unclean spirit that leaves a man, finds the house empty, swept, and put in order, and returns with seven other spirits more wicked than itself, so that the last state of that man is worse than the first. He concludes, “So shall it also be with this wicked generation” (Matthew 12:45).

8. Matthew 12:46–50 — Jesus’ True Family

When Jesus is told that His mother and brothers are outside seeking to speak with Him, He stretches out His hand toward His disciples and says, “Here are My mother and My brothers!” (Matthew 12:49). He declares that “whoever does the will of My Father in heaven is My brother and sister and mother” (Matthew 12:50).
