Matthew 13:1
Matthew 13:1
NKJV

Matthew 13:1 On the same day Jesus went out of the house and sat by the sea.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 13:1 Later that same day Jesus left the house and sat beside the lake. (NLT)

Main Idea

On the same day Jesus leaves the house and sits by the sea. The teaching that follows grows out of the conflict and division of chapter 12.

Verse Explanation

On the same day Jesus leaves the house and sits by the sea. The teaching that follows grows out of the conflict and division of chapter 12.

“On the same day Jesus went out of the house and sat by the sea.”

This is the same day as the conflict and the conversation about Jesus’ family in chapter 12. Jesus leaves the house and sits beside the Sea of Galilee. Teachers usually sat to teach.

Study Notes

Chapter 13 contains the third of Jesus’ five major teaching sections in Matthew.

Such great crowds gather that Jesus gets into a boat and sits, while the whole crowd stands on the shore. The boat becomes His platform for teaching a vast audience.

“so that He got into a boat and sat; and the whole multitude stood on the shore.”

Matthew 13:3
Matthew 13:3
NKJV

Matthew 13:3 Then He spoke many things to them in parables, saying: "Behold, a sower went out to sow.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 13:3 He told many stories in the form of parables, such as this one: “Listen! A farmer went out to plant some seeds. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus speaks many things to them in parables, beginning, “Behold, a sower went out to sow.” He turns to stories drawn from ordinary life to teach about the kingdom.

Verse Explanation

Jesus speaks many things to them in parables, beginning, “Behold, a sower went out to sow.” He turns to stories drawn from ordinary life to teach about the kingdom.

“Then He spoke many things to them in parables,”

Jesus teaches in “parables.” A parable is a story or picture drawn from ordinary life that teaches a spiritual truth. This is the first time Matthew uses the word.

“saying: ‘Behold, a sower went out to sow.”

The first parable describes a farmer sowing seed. Farmers scattered seed by hand as they walked across a field. Some seed inevitably landed in places where it could not grow well.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “parables” means a placing beside, a comparison.

Matthew 13:4

Main Idea

As the sower scatters seed, some falls by the wayside, and birds come and eat it. Seed that lands on the hard path never gets into the ground.

Verse Explanation

As the sower scatters seed, some falls by the wayside, and birds come and eat it. Seed that lands on the hard path never gets into the ground.

“And as he sowed, some seed fell by the wayside;”

Some seed falls on the path beside or through the field. The ground there is packed hard by people walking on it.

“and the birds came and devoured them.”

The seed cannot sink into the hard ground. It lies on the surface, and birds eat it.

Study Notes

Jesus explains this part of the parable in 13:19.

Verse Explanation

Some falls on stony places with little soil, and it springs up quickly because the earth is shallow. Quick growth in thin soil looks promising at first.

“Some fell on stony places, where they did not have much earth;”

Some seed falls where a thin layer of soil covers a shelf of rock. Such ground is common in Galilee.

“and they immediately sprang up because they had no depth of earth.”

The seed sprouts quickly. The shallow soil warms fast, so the plants come up early.

Study Notes

The stony places are not ground full of loose stones but ground with rock just beneath the surface.

Study Notes

Isaac’s harvest in Genesis 26:12 was a hundredfold, a sign of the LORD’s blessing.

NKJV

Main Idea

Jesus answers that it has been given to them to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven, but not to the others. Understanding the kingdom is a gift God gives to those who draw near to Jesus.

Verse Explanation

Jesus answers that it has been given to them to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven, but not to the others. Understanding the kingdom is a gift God gives to those who draw near to Jesus.

“He answered and said to them, ‘Because it has been given to you to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven,”

The disciples have been “given” understanding. Knowing “the mysteries of the kingdom” is a gift from God. A mystery in Scripture is a truth once hidden and now revealed by God.

“but to them it has not been given.”

Others have not received this understanding. In chapters 11–12 many have rejected Jesus’ plain words and works. Parables now reveal truth to those who draw near and conceal it from those who have refused Him.

Study Notes

Their hearts have grown dull, their ears hard of hearing, and their eyes closed, lest they see, hear, understand, turn, and be healed. Their blindness is the fruit of persistent refusal, and it keeps them from the healing God offers.

“For the hearts of this people have grown dull.”

The problem lies in their “hearts.” They have grown dull, insensitive to God.

“Their ears are hard of hearing, And their eyes they have closed,”

They have closed their own eyes. Their blindness is willful.

“Lest they should see with their eyes and hear with their ears, Lest they should understand with their hearts and turn, So that I should heal them.”

If they would see, hear, and understand, they would “turn,” repent, and God would “heal” them. Their persistent refusal brings God’s judgment of continued blindness. Isaiah’s words give that refusal its full seriousness.

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

The hearer on rocky ground has no root in himself and lasts only a while, and when trouble or persecution comes because of the word, he immediately stumbles. Shallow reception collapses under the cost of following.

“yet he has no root in himself, but endures only for a while.”

His response has no depth. He lasts only for a short time.

“For when tribulation or persecution arises because of the word, immediately he stumbles.”

The wider teaching of Scripture shows where deep roots come from. Believers are rooted in Christ and built up in Him (Colossians 2:6–7). They are rooted and grounded in His love (Ephesians 3:17–19). Roots go deep when believers draw their life, identity, and security from Christ, not from their own performance. Growth comes from being loved and accepted in Christ.

Matthew 13:23
NKJV

NLT

Matthew 13:23 The seed that fell on good soil represents those who truly hear and understand God’s word and produce a harvest of thirty, sixty, or even a hundred times as much as had been planted!” (NLT)

Main Idea

The seed on good ground is the one who hears the word, understands it, and bears fruit, some a hundredfold, some sixty, some thirty. A heart that receives and understands God’s word produces a harvest of righteous character and conduct.

Verse Explanation

The seed on good ground is the one who hears the word, understands it, and bears fruit, some a hundredfold, some sixty, some thirty. A heart that receives and understands God’s word produces a harvest of righteous character and conduct.

“But he who received seed on the good ground is he who hears the word and understands it,”

The fourth hearer takes the word in. Understanding here means more than knowing facts. It means taking the word into the heart and living by it.

“who indeed bears fruit and produces: some a hundredfold, some sixty, some thirty.”

This person “bears fruit.” The fruit includes righteousness, Christlike character, love in relationships, faithful stewardship, and service (Galatians 5:22–23). The amount varies, but every good hearer produces fruit.

The four soils describe different responses to God’s word. They can also describe different areas of one disciple’s heart. A believer may receive God’s word gladly in one area while another is hardened by hurt, another is shallow, and another is crowded by worries. Jesus’ words call each hearer to examine his own heart, not to label others as bad soil.

Hosea 10:12 says, “Break up your fallow ground, For it is time to seek the LORD.”

Matthew 13:54 He returned to Nazareth, his hometown. When he taught there in the synagogue, everyone was amazed and said, “Where does he get this wisdom and the power to do miracles?” (NLT)

Main Idea

Coming to His hometown, Jesus teaches in the synagogue, and the people are astonished, asking where He got this wisdom and these mighty works. They cannot deny His wisdom and power, but they question their source.

Verse Explanation

Coming to His hometown, Jesus teaches in the synagogue, and the people are astonished, asking where He got this wisdom and these mighty works. They cannot deny His wisdom and power, but they question their source.

“When He had come to His own country, He taught them in their synagogue,”

Jesus returns to “His own country,” His hometown of Nazareth. He teaches in the synagogue.

“so that they were astonished and said, ‘Where did this Man get this wisdom and these mighty works?”

The people are amazed. They cannot deny His wisdom or His miracles. But they cannot understand where they come from.

Study Notes

Luke 4:16–30 records a visit to Nazareth in which the people tried to throw Jesus off a cliff.

Matthew 13:55 Is this not the carpenter's son? Is not His mother called Mary? And His brothers James, Joses, Simon, and Judas?(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 13:55 Then they scoffed, “He’s just the carpenter’s son, and we know Mary, his mother, and his brothers — James, Joseph, Simon, and Judas. (NLT)

Main Idea

The people of Jesus’ hometown ask, “Is this not the carpenter’s son?” and name His mother Mary and His brothers James, Joses, Simon, and Judas. They reason from His familiar family to conclude that He is nothing more than one of their own.

Verse Explanation

The people of Jesus’ hometown ask, “Is this not the carpenter’s son?” and name His mother Mary and His brothers James, Joses, Simon, and Judas. They reason from His familiar family to conclude that He is nothing more than one of their own.

“Is this not the carpenter’s son?”

They know Jesus as “the carpenter’s son.” Joseph had been a builder or craftsman. Mark 6:3 calls Jesus Himself the carpenter.

“Is not His mother called Mary? And His brothers James, Joses, Simon, and Judas?”

They know His mother and His brothers by name. They think they know everything about Him.

Study Notes

James later led the church in Jerusalem and wrote the letter of James. Judas, or Jude, wrote the letter of Jude.

Matthew 13:57 And they were deeply offended and refused to believe in him. Then Jesus told them, “A prophet is honored everywhere except in his own hometown and among his own family.” (NLT)

Main Idea

The people of Nazareth take offense at Jesus, and He says that a prophet is not without honor except in his own country and his own house. Those closest to Jesus’ upbringing stumble over Him.

Verse Explanation

The people of Nazareth take offense at Jesus, and He says that a prophet is not without honor except in his own country and his own house. Those closest to Jesus’ upbringing stumble over Him.

“So they were offended at Him.”

Instead of believing, they take offense. Their familiarity with Jesus becomes a stumbling block.

“But Jesus said to them, ‘A prophet is not without honor except in his own country and in his own house.”

Jesus answers with a proverb. A prophet is honored everywhere except among those who know him best.

Study Notes

John 4:44 records the same saying. John 1:11 says, “He came to His own, and His own did not receive Him.”

Matthew 13:58
NKJV

Matthew 13:58 Now He did not do many mighty works there because of their unbelief.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 13:58 And so he did only a few miracles there because of their unbelief. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus does not do many mighty works in His hometown because of the people’s unbelief. Their refusal to trust Him shuts them off from what He would have done among them.

Verse Explanation

Jesus does not do many mighty works in His hometown because of the people’s unbelief. Their refusal to trust Him shuts them off from what He would have done among them.

“Now He did not do many mighty works there because of their unbelief.”

Jesus did only a few miracles in Nazareth. Matthew gives the reason: “because of their unbelief.” They refused to trust Him.

Their unbelief was a refusal to receive Jesus. Matthew’s explanation applies to this scene. It does not mean that every person who remains sick lacks faith.

Study Notes

Mark 6:5–6 says that He laid His hands on a few sick people and healed them, and He marveled because of their unbelief.
