Matthew 13 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Matthew 13 records the third of Jesus’ major discourses, a series of kingdom parables spoken to the multitudes by the sea, together with Jesus’ answer to His disciples about why He speaks in parables, followed by further explanation and parables given to the disciples in the house. Through the sower, the wheat and tares, the mustard seed, the leaven, the hidden treasure, the pearl, and the dragnet, Jesus explains why people respond to the kingdom word so differently, how the kingdom grows until the final separation at the end of the age, and why it is worth everything, and the chapter closes as His hometown of Nazareth takes offense at Him.

Chapter Sections

1. Matthew 13:1–9 — The Parable of the Sower

Sitting in a boat while great multitudes stand on the shore, Jesus tells of a sower whose seed falls by the wayside, on stony places, among thorns, and on good ground. Only the seed on good ground yields a crop, some a hundredfold, some sixty, some thirty, and Jesus says, “He who has ears to hear, let him hear!” (Matthew 13:9).

2. Matthew 13:10–17 — Why Jesus Speaks in Parables

When the disciples ask why He speaks in parables, Jesus answers that it has been given to them to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven, but not to those who see without seeing and hear without hearing, in whom Isaiah’s prophecy is fulfilled. He tells the disciples that their eyes and ears are blessed, for many prophets and righteous men desired to see and hear what they see and hear.

3. Matthew 13:18–23 — The Sower Explained

Jesus explains that the seed is the word of the kingdom: the wicked one snatches away what was sown by the wayside, the one on stony places receives it with joy but has no root in himself and stumbles when tribulation or persecution arises because of the word, and the cares of this world and the deceitfulness of riches choke the word among the thorns. The good ground is the one who hears the word and understands it, and he bears fruit.

4. Matthew 13:24–30 — The Parable of the Wheat and the Tares

Jesus likens the kingdom of heaven to a man who sows good seed in his field, but an enemy sows tares among the wheat while men sleep. The owner tells his servants to let both grow together until the harvest, when the reapers will gather the tares to be burned and the wheat into his barn.

5. Matthew 13:31–35 — The Mustard Seed, the Leaven, and Speaking in Parables

Jesus likens the kingdom of heaven to a mustard seed, the least of all seeds, which grows into a tree where the birds of the air nest, and to leaven that a woman hides in three measures of meal until it is all leavened. Matthew adds that Jesus spoke all these things to the multitude in parables, fulfilling the prophet’s word, “I will open My mouth in parables” (Matthew 13:35).

6. Matthew 13:36–43 — The Wheat and the Tares Explained

In the house Jesus explains that He, the Son of Man, sows the good seed, the field is the world, the good seeds are the sons of the kingdom, the tares are the sons of the wicked one, the enemy is the devil, and the harvest is the end of the age. At that harvest the Son of Man will send out His angels to gather all who practice lawlessness into the furnace of fire, and the righteous will shine forth as the sun in their Father’s kingdom.

7. Matthew 13:44–46 — The Hidden Treasure and the Pearl of Great Price

Jesus likens the kingdom of heaven to treasure hidden in a field, which a man finds and, for joy over it, sells all that he has to buy that field. He likens it also to a merchant who finds one pearl of great price and sells all that he has to buy it.

8. Matthew 13:47–52 — The Dragnet and the Householder’s Treasure

Jesus likens the kingdom of heaven to a dragnet that gathers fish of every kind, from which the good are kept and the bad thrown away, and He says the angels will likewise separate the wicked from among the just at the end of the age. When the disciples say they have understood, He tells them that every scribe instructed concerning the kingdom of heaven is like a householder who brings out of his treasure things new and old.

9. Matthew 13:53–58 — Rejected in His Hometown

When Jesus has finished these parables, He goes to His own country and teaches in their synagogue, and the people are astonished at His wisdom and mighty works yet take offense at Him as the carpenter’s son. Jesus says, “A prophet is not without honor except in his own country and in his own house” (Matthew 13:57), and He does not do many mighty works there because of their unbelief.
