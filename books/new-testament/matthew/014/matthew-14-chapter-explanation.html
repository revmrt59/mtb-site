Chapter Study

Matthew 14

Verse-by-Verse
Matthew 14:1
Matthew 14:1
NKJV

Matthew 14:1 At that time Herod the tetrarch heard the report about Jesus(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 14:1 When Herod Antipas, the ruler of Galilee, heard about Jesus, (NLT)

Main Idea

At that time Herod the tetrarch hears the report about Jesus. This is Herod Antipas, son of the Herod of chapter 2, and news of Jesus now reaches the ruler of Galilee.

Verse Explanation

At that time Herod the tetrarch hears the report about Jesus. This is Herod Antipas, son of the Herod of chapter 2, and news of Jesus now reaches the ruler of Galilee.

“At that time Herod the tetrarch heard the report about Jesus”

This “Herod” is Herod Antipas, a son of Herod the Great, who tried to kill the infant Jesus (2:16). Herod Antipas ruled Galilee and Perea. A “tetrarch” was a ruler of part of a kingdom under Roman authority. News of Jesus’ miracles reaches him.

Study Notes

Herod the Great died soon after Jesus’ birth (2:19). His kingdom was divided among his sons. Herod Antipas ruled from 4 BC to AD 39.

Matthew 14:2
Matthew 14:2
NKJV

Matthew 14:2 and said to his servants, "This is John the Baptist; he is risen from the dead, and therefore these powers are at work in him."(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 14:2 he said to his advisers, “This must be John the Baptist raised from the dead! That is why he can do such miracles.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Herod tells his servants, “This is John the Baptist; he is risen from the dead,” and that is why such powers are at work in him. Hearing of Jesus, Herod identifies Him with the prophet he himself had put to death.

Verse Explanation

Herod tells his servants, “This is John the Baptist; he is risen from the dead,” and that is why such powers are at work in him. Hearing of Jesus, Herod identifies Him with the prophet he himself had put to death.

“and said to his servants, ‘This is John the Baptist; he is risen from the dead,”

Herod concludes that Jesus is John the Baptist come back to life. His guilty conscience may lie behind this fear, since Herod had John killed.

“and therefore these powers are at work in him.”

Herod thinks that a man raised from the dead would have miraculous powers. John had done no miracles in his lifetime (John 10:41).

Study Notes

Others thought Jesus was Elijah or one of the prophets (16:14).

Matthew 14:3
NKJV

Main Idea

Herod had arrested John, bound him, and put him in prison because of Herodias, his brother Philip’s wife. Matthew now looks back to explain how John came to be imprisoned and killed.

Verse Explanation

Herod had arrested John, bound him, and put him in prison because of Herodias, his brother Philip’s wife. Matthew now looks back to explain how John came to be imprisoned and killed.

“For Herod had laid hold of John and bound him, and put him in prison”

Matthew now explains what happened to John earlier. Herod had arrested him and put him in chains.

“for the sake of Herodias, his brother Philip’s wife.”

The reason was “Herodias.” She was “his brother Philip’s wife,” married to Herod’s half-brother. Herod divorced his own wife and married Herodias while her husband was still alive.

Study Notes

This Philip is not Philip the tetrarch mentioned in Luke 3:1. Herodias was also Herod’s niece.

The king is sorry, yet because of his oaths and his dinner guests he orders John’s head to be given to her. Matthew names the oath and the guests as the reasons, and an oath cannot justify putting an innocent man to death.

“nevertheless, because of the oaths and because of those who sat with him, he commanded it to be given to her.”

Herod gave the order anyway. He did not want to break his oath or lose face before his guests. Pride and fear of people led him to commit murder.

An oath to do wrong should never be kept. Herod’s promise gave him no right to kill God’s messenger.

Study Notes

Matthew calls Herod king, though his official title was tetrarch. People often used the title king for such rulers.

Peter climbs out of the boat and walks on the water toward Jesus. He takes a real step of faith in response to Jesus’ real command.

Stepping out in faith means obeying what Jesus actually says. Risk is not a virtue by itself. Peter’s courage was tied to Jesus’ word.
