Matthew 14 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Matthew 14 sets the death of John the Baptist at Herod’s birthday feast against the compassion of Jesus, who heals the sick and feeds about five thousand men, besides women and children, with five loaves and two fish. Jesus then comes to His disciples walking on the sea, rescues Peter when he begins to sink, and is worshiped as the Son of God, and the chapter closes with the sick of Gennesaret made perfectly well by touching the hem of His garment.

Chapter Sections

1. Matthew 14:1–12 — The Death of John the Baptist

Hearing reports about Jesus, Herod the tetrarch says, “This is John the Baptist; he is risen from the dead” (Matthew 14:2), and Matthew then tells how Herod had imprisoned John for saying it was not lawful for him to have his brother Philip’s wife, Herodias. At Herod’s birthday the daughter of Herodias, prompted by her mother, asks for John’s head on a platter, and although the king is sorry, he has John beheaded because of his oath and his guests, and John’s disciples bury the body and tell Jesus.

2. Matthew 14:13–21 — Jesus Feeds the Five Thousand

When Jesus withdraws by boat to a deserted place, a great multitude follows, and He is moved with compassion and heals their sick. When evening comes, He tells His disciples, “You give them something to eat” (Matthew 14:16), and with five loaves and two fish He feeds about five thousand men, besides women and children, and twelve baskets of fragments are left over.

3. Matthew 14:22–33 — Jesus Walks on the Sea and Rescues Peter

After praying alone on the mountain, Jesus comes to His disciples in the fourth watch of the night, walking on the sea, and tells them, “Be of good cheer! It is I; do not be afraid” (Matthew 14:27). Peter walks on the water toward Him but begins to sink when he sees the boisterous wind, and Jesus catches him, and when they get into the boat and the wind ceases, those in the boat worship Him, saying, “Truly You are the Son of God” (Matthew 14:33).

4. Matthew 14:34–36 — Healings at Gennesaret

When they cross over to the land of Gennesaret, the men of that place recognize Jesus and bring Him all who are sick. They beg that the sick might only touch the hem of His garment, and as many as touch it are made perfectly well.
