Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 14
Main Idea:

Herod Antipas’s murder of John the Baptist stands beside the compassion of Jesus, who feeds five thousand with five loaves and two fish, walks on the sea, rescues sinking Peter, receives His disciples’ worship as the Son of God, and heals all who touch Him.

Setting:

Matthew 14 continues the third movement. Herod Antipas, son of the Herod of chapter 2, rules Galilee and hears reports about Jesus.

Chapter Outline:

  • Matthew 14:1–12 — The Death of John the Baptist

  • Matthew 14:13–21 — Jesus Feeds the Five Thousand

  • Matthew 14:22–33 — Jesus Walks on the Sea and Rescues Peter

  • Matthew 14:34–36 — Healings at Gennesaret

Key Verse:

“Then those who were in the boat came and worshiped Him, saying, ‘Truly You are the Son of God.’” (Matthew 14:33)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Two kings appear in this chapter. Herod Antipas hears about Jesus and fears that John the Baptist has risen from the dead. Matthew explains why. John had told Herod that his marriage to his brother’s wife was unlawful, and Herod imprisoned him. At a birthday feast, bound by a rash oath and anxious about his guests, Herod has John beheaded. Faithful witness costs John his life, and his disciples bury him and tell Jesus.

The other King withdraws, but when the crowds follow, He is moved with compassion and heals their sick. Then He tells His disciples, “You give them something to eat” (14:16). They have five loaves and two fish, and in His hands it feeds about five thousand men besides women and children, with twelve baskets left over. That night Jesus prays alone, then comes to His disciples walking on the sea. “Be of good cheer! It is I; do not be afraid” (14:27). Peter steps out at His word, walks toward Him, and begins to sink when he looks at the wind. Jesus catches him immediately, the wind stops, and the disciples worship: “Truly You are the Son of God.”

Chapter Sections

Matthew 14:1–12 — The Death of John the Baptist

Herod fears that Jesus is John the Baptist risen from the dead. Matthew recounts how John’s rebuke of Herod’s marriage led to his imprisonment and to his beheading at Herod’s birthday feast.

Matthew 14:13–21 — Jesus Feeds the Five Thousand

Moved with compassion, Jesus heals the sick and then feeds about five thousand men, besides women and children, from five loaves and two fish. The disciples distribute the food, and twelve baskets of fragments remain.

Matthew 14:22–33 — Jesus Walks on the Sea and Rescues Peter

After praying alone, Jesus comes walking on the sea in the night. Peter walks toward Him at His command, begins to sink when he sees the wind, and is caught by Jesus, and the disciples worship Him as the Son of God.

Matthew 14:34–36 — Healings at Gennesaret

At Gennesaret people bring all their sick to Jesus, begging to touch the hem of His garment. As many as touch it are made perfectly well.

Why This Chapter Matters

Matthew 14 shows what kind of King Jesus is by setting Him beside Herod. One king uses power to protect his pride and silence a prophet. The other uses His power to heal, feed, and rescue. The cost of faithful witness is real, but the King’s compassion is the larger reality in the chapter.

The chapter also trains disciples in dependence. Jesus gives them a task beyond their resources and then supplies what they lack. He invites Peter out onto the water, and when Peter falters, His hand is immediate. Courage that steps out at the King’s word is commended, fear that looks at the storm is exposed, and the rescue leads to worship.

Key Themes to Watch For

The cost of witness. John dies for speaking God’s truth to a ruler (14:3–12).

Compassion that provides. Jesus heals and feeds the crowd (14:14–21).

Disciples in dependence. The disciples bring what little they have and distribute what He supplies (14:16–19).

Faith and fear. Peter steps out at Jesus’ word and sinks when he looks at the wind (14:28–31).

The Son of God. Jesus’ “It is I” leads to the disciples’ worship (14:27, 33).

Key Words & Concepts

Herod the tetrarch (14:1). Herod Antipas, ruler of Galilee and Perea. He is a son of Herod the Great, who sought to kill the infant Jesus.

Herodias (14:3). The wife of Herod’s brother Philip, whom Herod married. John declared the marriage unlawful.

Twelve baskets (14:20). The leftover fragments show the abundance of Jesus’ provision. Matthew distinguishes this feeding from the later feeding of four thousand (16:9–10).

Fourth watch (14:25). The last part of the night, roughly three to six in the morning.

It is I (14:27). Jesus’ reassurance identifies Him to His frightened disciples. Spoken by One walking on the sea, it echoes God’s own way of naming Himself.
