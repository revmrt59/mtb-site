Matthew 15

Verse-by-Verse
Matthew 15:1
Matthew 15:1
NKJV

Matthew 15:1 Then the scribes and Pharisees who were from Jerusalem came to Jesus, saying,(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 15:1 Some Pharisees and teachers of religious law now arrived from Jerusalem to see Jesus. They asked him, (NLT)

Main Idea

Scribes and Pharisees come from Jerusalem to question Jesus. Religious authorities from the capital now come to examine Him.

Verse Explanation

Scribes and Pharisees come from Jerusalem to question Jesus. Religious authorities from the capital now come to examine Him.

“Then the scribes and Pharisees who were from Jerusalem came to Jesus, saying,”

A delegation of religious leaders comes from Jerusalem, the center of Jewish religious authority. They have traveled to Galilee to investigate Jesus. Their opposition is growing more organized.

Study Notes

Mark 7:1 says that they gathered to Jesus after coming from Jerusalem.

Matthew 15:2
Matthew 15:2
NKJV

Matthew 15:2 "Why do Your disciples transgress the tradition of the elders? For they do not wash their hands when they eat bread."(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 15:2 “Why do your disciples disobey our age-old tradition? For they ignore our tradition of ceremonial hand washing before they eat.” (NLT)

Main Idea

They ask why His disciples break “the tradition of the elders” by not washing their hands before eating. They judge the disciples by human tradition rather than God’s command.

Verse Explanation

They ask why His disciples break “the tradition of the elders” by not washing their hands before eating. They judge the disciples by human tradition rather than God’s command.

“Why do Your disciples transgress the tradition of the elders?”

The charge concerns rules passed down from earlier teachers. These were teachings handed down by respected rabbis. They were meant to explain and protect God’s law, but the Pharisees treated them as equally binding.

“For they do not wash their hands when they eat bread.”

The specific charge is that the disciples do not wash their hands before eating. This was not about hygiene. It was a ritual washing meant to remove ceremonial uncleanness.

Study Notes

Mark 7:3–4 explains that the Pharisees and all the Jews did not eat unless they washed their hands in a special way, holding the tradition of the elders.

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

Jesus answers with a question: why do they break the commandment of God for the sake of their tradition? He turns the charge back on them, showing that their tradition actually violates God’s law.

“He answered and said to them,”

Jesus answers their question with a question of His own.

“Why do you also transgress the commandment of God because of your tradition?”

They accuse the disciples of breaking tradition. Jesus accuses them of breaking “the commandment of God.” Their traditions had become more important to them than God’s own word.

Study Notes

The contrast between God’s commandment and human tradition runs through 15:3–9.

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

God commanded, “Honor your father and your mother,” and decreed death for anyone who curses father or mother. Jesus sets God’s clear command at the center of the dispute.

“For God commanded, saying, ‘Honor your father and your mother’;”

Jesus quotes the fifth commandment (Exodus 20:12). God commanded His people to honor their parents. This included caring for them in their old age.

“and, ‘He who curses father or mother, let him be put to death.”

Jesus also quotes Exodus 21:17. God treated dishonoring parents as a serious sin.

Study Notes

Paul calls the fifth commandment the first commandment with promise (Ephesians 6:2).

Main Idea

But they teach that whoever tells his parents that whatever help he might have given them is “a gift to God” is released from the command. Their tradition allowed a religious vow to excuse a son from supporting his parents.

Verse Explanation

But they teach that whoever tells his parents that whatever help he might have given them is “a gift to God” is released from the command. Their tradition allowed a religious vow to excuse a son from supporting his parents.

“But you say, ‘Whoever says to his father or mother,”

Jesus contrasts God’s command with what the Pharisees teach.

“Whatever profit you might have received from me is a gift to God’—”

A person could declare that money or property was dedicated to God. Then he could refuse to use it to support his parents. Mark 7:11 calls this Corban, meaning a gift to God.

Study Notes

The dedication did not necessarily mean the property went to the temple. It could simply be withheld from the parents.

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

What goes into the mouth does not defile a person, but what comes out of the mouth defiles him. Jesus answers the handwashing charge by locating true defilement in the heart rather than in food eaten with unwashed hands.

Food does not make a person spiritually unclean. Eating with unwashed hands does not defile anyone.

“but what comes out of the mouth, this defiles a man.”

What makes a person unclean is what comes out of the mouth, the words that come from the heart.

Study Notes

Jesus explains this saying more fully in 15:17–20.
