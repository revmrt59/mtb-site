Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 15
Main Idea:

Jesus exposes a tradition that sets aside God’s command and teaches that defilement comes from the heart, then grants a Canaanite woman’s plea for mercy because of her great faith, heals great multitudes, and feeds four thousand with seven loaves.

Setting:

Matthew 15 continues the third movement. Pharisees and scribes come from Jerusalem with an accusation, and Jesus then travels to the region of Tyre and Sidon before returning to the Sea of Galilee.

Chapter Outline:

  • Matthew 15:1–9 — Tradition and the Commandment of God

  • Matthew 15:10–20 — What Comes from the Heart Defiles

  • Matthew 15:21–28 — The Canaanite Woman’s Great Faith

  • Matthew 15:29–31 — Jesus Heals Great Multitudes by the Sea of Galilee

  • Matthew 15:32–39 — Jesus Feeds the Four Thousand

Key Verse:

“Then Jesus answered and said to her, ‘O woman, great is your faith! Let it be to you as you desire.’ And her daughter was healed from that very hour.” (Matthew 15:28)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The leaders from Jerusalem complain that Jesus’ disciples eat without washing their hands as the elders’ tradition required. Jesus asks why they break God’s command for their tradition. God said to honor father and mother, yet their rule allowed a man to dedicate his support as a gift to God and leave his parents in need. Isaiah described people who honor God with their lips while their hearts are far from Him. Then Jesus tells the crowd that defilement does not come from food entering the mouth. Evil thoughts, murders, adulteries, and lies come from the heart, and that is what defiles a person.

The next scene shows a very different heart. A Canaanite woman, a Gentile, cries to Jesus as Lord and Son of David for her demon-possessed daughter. Jesus first names His mission to Israel’s lost sheep. She accepts His picture of the household and asks only for the crumbs that fall from the master’s table, and Jesus says, “O woman, great is your faith!” (15:28). Back by the Sea of Galilee, Jesus heals the multitudes, who glorify the God of Israel, and feeds four thousand men besides women and children.

Chapter Sections

Matthew 15:1–9 — Tradition and the Commandment of God

Pharisees and scribes accuse Jesus’ disciples of breaking the elders’ tradition by not washing before eating. Jesus shows that their own tradition breaks God’s command to honor parents and applies Isaiah’s words about lip worship to them.

Matthew 15:10–20 — What Comes from the Heart Defiles

Jesus teaches that what defiles a person comes out of the heart, not from food going into the mouth. He tells His disciples that the Pharisees are blind leaders and lists the evils that proceed from the heart.

Matthew 15:21–28 — The Canaanite Woman’s Great Faith

A Canaanite woman pleads for her demon-possessed daughter, and Jesus first speaks of His mission to Israel. Her humble, persistent reply draws His praise for her great faith, and her daughter is healed that hour.

Matthew 15:29–31 — Jesus Heals Great Multitudes by the Sea of Galilee

Great multitudes bring the lame, blind, mute, and maimed to Jesus, and He heals them. The people marvel and glorify the God of Israel.

Matthew 15:32–39 — Jesus Feeds the Four Thousand

Jesus has compassion on the crowd that has been with Him three days and now has nothing to eat, lest they faint on the way. With seven loaves and a few fish He feeds four thousand men besides women and children, and seven large baskets are left over.

Why This Chapter Matters

Matthew 15 shows that God looks past religious practice to the heart. Traditions can be useful, but when they excuse disobedience to God, they become a way of honoring Him with the lips while the heart is far away. The real need is a clean heart, which no washing of hands can supply.

The chapter also shows mercy reaching beyond Israel. Jesus honors His mission to Israel, yet a Gentile mother receives what she asks because she trusts His mercy. Her faith is not a claim on Jesus but humble dependence on His abundance. The healings and the feeding in a largely Gentile setting, where the crowd glorifies the God of Israel, point toward the blessing of Abraham reaching the nations.

Key Themes to Watch For

God’s command over tradition. Human tradition must not set aside God’s word (15:3–6).

The heart’s condition. Evil comes from within and defiles a person (15:18–20).

Great faith. The Canaanite woman trusts the King’s mercy (15:22–28).

Israel and the nations. Jesus’ mission to Israel and mercy to a Gentile stand together (15:24–28, 31).

Compassionate provision. Jesus heals and feeds the multitudes (15:30–38).

Key Words & Concepts

Tradition of the elders (15:2). Rules handed down by Jewish teachers beyond the written Law, including ritual handwashing before meals.

A gift to God (15:5). A pledge dedicating money or property to God. The tradition allowed it to excuse a person from supporting his parents.

Defile (15:11). To make unclean before God. Jesus locates the true source of defilement in the heart.

Canaanite (15:22). A name recalling Israel’s ancient enemies. The woman is a Gentile from the region of Tyre and Sidon.

Little dogs (15:26). Small household dogs under the family table. Jesus uses the picture to draw out the woman’s faith, not to insult her.
