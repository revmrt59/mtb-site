Matthew 16

Verse-by-Verse
Matthew 16:1
Matthew 16:1
NKJV

Matthew 16:1 Then the Pharisees and Sadducees came, and testing Him asked that He would show them a sign from heaven.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 16:1 One day the Pharisees and Sadducees came to test Jesus, demanding that he show them a miraculous sign from heaven to prove his authority. (NLT)

Main Idea

The Pharisees and Sadducees come together to test Jesus, asking Him to show them a sign from heaven. Groups usually opposed to each other unite in demanding proof on their own terms.

Verse Explanation

The Pharisees and Sadducees come together to test Jesus, asking Him to show them a sign from heaven. Groups usually opposed to each other unite in demanding proof on their own terms.

“Then the Pharisees and Sadducees came,”

Two rival groups come together against Jesus. The “Pharisees” were devoted to the law and its traditions. The “Sadducees” were a priestly group who accepted only the five books of Moses and denied the resurrection (22:23). They usually opposed each other. Now they unite against Jesus.

“and testing Him asked that He would show them a sign from heaven.”

They want a miracle in the sky that would prove His authority. Their purpose is to test Him, not to believe.

Study Notes

This is the first time Matthew mentions the Pharisees and Sadducees together since 3:7.

In the region of Caesarea Philippi, Jesus asks His disciples, “Who do men say that I, the Son of Man, am?” He brings the question of His identity to the center.

Matthew 16:18
Matthew 16:18
NKJV

Matthew 16:18 And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 16:18 Now I say to you that you are Peter (which means ‘rock’), and upon this rock I will build my church, and all the powers of hell will not conquer it. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus declares that Peter is “Peter,” and “on this rock I will build My church,” and the gates of Hades will not prevail against it. Christ builds His church on the Father-revealed truth Peter has confessed, that Jesus is the Christ, and not even the power of death can overcome it.

Verse Explanation

Jesus declares that Peter is “Peter,” and “on this rock I will build My church,” and the gates of Hades will not prevail against it. Christ builds His church on the Father-revealed truth Peter has confessed, that Jesus is the Christ, and not even the power of death can overcome it.

“And I also say to you that you are Peter,”

Jesus gives Simon the name “Peter,” which means rock in Greek.

“and on this rock I will build My church,”

Jesus will build His “church” on “this rock.” The rock is the revealed truth Peter has just confessed: Jesus is the Christ, the Son of the living God. Peter has a real role as the one who first confessed this and as a leading apostle. But the foundation is Christ Himself (1 Corinthians 3:11).

The “church” is the community of people gathered around Jesus. It belongs to Him: “My church.” He builds it.

“and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.”

“Hades” is the realm of the dead. Its gates picture the power of death. Death cannot defeat Christ’s work. His church will endure.

Study Notes

The Greek word for Peter is petros, and the word for rock is petra. The two words are related.

The Greek word translated “church” is ekklesia, meaning an assembly or congregation. Matthew uses it here and in 18:17.

Matthew 16:19
Matthew 16:19
NKJV

Matthew 16:19 And I will give you the keys of the kingdom of heaven, and whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven."(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 16:19 And I will give you the keys of the Kingdom of Heaven. Whatever you forbid on earth will be forbidden in heaven, and whatever you permit on earth will be permitted in heaven.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus promises to give Peter the keys of the kingdom of heaven, so that what he binds or looses on earth will be bound or loosed in heaven. The keys are authority entrusted by the King and exercised under His word and on His terms, announcing His forgiveness to those who repent and believe and warning those who reject Him.

Verse Explanation

Jesus promises to give Peter the keys of the kingdom of heaven, so that what he binds or looses on earth will be bound or loosed in heaven. The keys are authority entrusted by the King and exercised under His word and on His terms, announcing His forgiveness to those who repent and believe and warning those who reject Him.

“And I will give you the keys of the kingdom of heaven,”

Keys give access and authority. Jesus entrusts Peter, and through him the church, with authority to act on His behalf in matters of access to the kingdom.

Peter used these keys when he opened the door of the kingdom to Jews at Pentecost (Acts 2:14–41) and to Gentiles at the house of Cornelius (Acts 10:34–48).

“and whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven.”

To “bind” and “loose” were terms for forbidding and permitting. Through proclaiming the gospel, the church declares the King’s forgiveness to those who repent and believe. It warns those who reject Him that they remain in their sins. Through correction within the church, it acts concerning fellowship under His word (18:15–20; John 20:21–23).

The church does not invent its own terms. It announces and acts on the King’s terms. Final judgment belongs to Him.

Study Notes

The Greek verbs can be translated will have been bound and will have been loosed. The church declares on earth what heaven has already determined.

Jesus gives the same authority to bind and loose to the church in 18:18.

From that time Jesus begins to show His disciples that He must go to Jerusalem, suffer many things from the elders, chief priests, and scribes, be killed, and be raised on the third day. Now that His identity is confessed, Jesus reveals the path of the cross and resurrection that His mission requires.

“From that time Jesus began to show to His disciples that He must go to Jerusalem,”

This is a turning point. “From that time” Jesus begins to teach openly about His coming death. He “must” go to Jerusalem. It is God’s plan.

“and suffer many things from the elders and chief priests and scribes,”

He will suffer at the hands of Israel’s leaders. The “elders,” “chief priests,” and “scribes” made up the Sanhedrin, the Jewish ruling council.

“and be killed, and be raised the third day.”

His death will not be the end. He will rise again. Jesus never speaks of His death without His resurrection.

Study Notes

The phrase from that time also marks the beginning of Jesus’ preaching in 4:17.

Jesus foretells His death and resurrection again in 17:22–23 and 20:17–19.

Matthew 16:24–28 — Taking Up the Cross to Follow Jesus

Matthew 16:24
Matthew 16:24
NKJV

Matthew 16:24 Then Jesus said to His disciples, "If anyone desires to come after Me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow Me.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 16:24 Then Jesus said to his disciples, “If any of you wants to be my follower, you must give up your own way, take up your cross, and follow me. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus tells His disciples that whoever wants to come after Him must deny himself, take up his cross, and follow Him. Following Jesus means yielding self-rule and walking in costly obedience behind Him.

Verse Explanation

Jesus tells His disciples that whoever wants to come after Him must deny himself, take up his cross, and follow Him. Following Jesus means yielding self-rule and walking in costly obedience behind Him.

“Then Jesus said to His disciples,”

Jesus now teaches all His disciples what it means to follow Him.

“If anyone desires to come after Me, let him deny himself,”

To “deny himself” means to give up control of one’s own life. It does not mean denying oneself a few pleasures. It means yielding self-rule to Jesus.

“and take up his cross, and follow Me.”

A person carrying a cross was on the way to execution. To “take up his cross” means to accept whatever following Jesus costs. Luke 9:23 adds daily.

The cross can mean public loss or even death. It also includes ordinary acts of obedience: apologizing, forgiving, serving, speaking the truth, and letting go of control.

Jesus’ own death is unique. Only His cross saves. A disciple’s cross is costly obedience in following Him.

Study Notes

Jesus first gave this call in 10:38.

Matthew 16:25

Matthew 16:25 For whoever desires to save his life will lose it, but whoever loses his life for My sake will find it.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 16:25 If you try to hang on to your life, you will lose it. But if you give up your life for my sake, you will save it. (NLT)

Main Idea

Whoever wants to save his life will lose it, but whoever loses his life for Jesus’ sake will find it. Holding on to self-preservation leads to loss, while surrendering life to Jesus leads to true life.

Verse Explanation

Whoever wants to save his life will lose it, but whoever loses his life for Jesus’ sake will find it. Holding on to self-preservation leads to loss, while surrendering life to Jesus leads to true life.

“For whoever desires to save his life will lose it,”

The person who holds on to his life and refuses to give it to Jesus will lose it in the end.

“but whoever loses his life for My sake will find it.”

The person who gives up his life “for My sake” will find true life.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “life” is psyche, which can also mean soul.

Matthew 16:26 For what profit is it to a man if he gains the whole world, and loses his own soul? Or what will a man give in exchange for his soul?(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 16:26 And what do you benefit if you gain the whole world but lose your own soul? Is anything worth more than your soul? (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus asks what profit it is to gain the whole world and lose one’s soul, or what a person could give in exchange for his soul. Nothing in the world is worth the loss of one’s own soul.

Verse Explanation

Jesus asks what profit it is to gain the whole world and lose one’s soul, or what a person could give in exchange for his soul. Nothing in the world is worth the loss of one’s own soul.

“For what profit is it to a man if he gains the whole world, and loses his own soul?”

Even gaining everything in the world would be worthless if a person loses his soul.

“Or what will a man give in exchange for his soul?”

Nothing is worth as much as a soul. Nothing can buy it back once it is lost.

Study Notes

Psalm 49:7–8 says that no one can redeem his brother, for the redemption of their souls is costly.

Matthew 16:27 For the Son of Man will come in the glory of His Father with His angels, and then He will reward each according to his works.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 16:27 For the Son of Man will come with his angels in the glory of his Father and will judge all people according to their deeds. (NLT)

Main Idea

The Son of Man will come in His Father’s glory with His angels and then reward each person according to his works. The cross is not the end, because Jesus will return in glory as Judge.

Verse Explanation

The Son of Man will come in His Father’s glory with His angels and then reward each person according to his works. The cross is not the end, because Jesus will return in glory as Judge.

“For the Son of Man will come in the glory of His Father with His angels,”

Jesus looks ahead to His return. He will come in the “glory of His Father” with His angels.

“and then He will reward each according to his works.”

At His return, He will judge every person. Works do not earn salvation. They show the reality of faith, and God will reward faithful service.

Study Notes

Psalm 62:12 says that God renders to each one according to his work.

Matthew 16:28
Matthew 16:28
NKJV

Matthew 16:28 "Assuredly, I say to you, there are some standing here who shall not taste death till they see the Son of Man coming in His kingdom."(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 16:28 And I tell you the truth, some standing here right now will not die before they see the Son of Man coming in his Kingdom.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus solemnly says that some standing there will not taste death until they see the Son of Man coming in His kingdom. The transfiguration that follows gives three disciples a preview of His royal glory, and His resurrection and exaltation fulfill the promise in history.

Verse Explanation

Jesus solemnly says that some standing there will not taste death until they see the Son of Man coming in His kingdom. The transfiguration that follows gives three disciples a preview of His royal glory, and His resurrection and exaltation fulfill the promise in history.

“Assuredly, I say to you, there are some standing here who shall not taste death till they see the Son of Man coming in His kingdom.”

Jesus promises that some of those standing with Him will see “the Son of Man coming in His kingdom” before they die.

Matthew immediately records the transfiguration after six days (17:1). Peter, James, and John see Jesus’ glory on the mountain. Peter later wrote that they were eyewitnesses of His majesty (2 Peter 1:16–18). The promise is then fulfilled in history in Jesus’ resurrection and exaltation, when all authority is given to Him (28:18).

Study Notes

To taste death means to die.
