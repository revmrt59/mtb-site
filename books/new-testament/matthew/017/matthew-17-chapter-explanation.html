Matthew 17

Verse-by-Verse
Matthew 17:1
Matthew 17:1
NKJV

Matthew 17:1 Now after six days Jesus took Peter, James, and John his brother, led them up on a high mountain by themselves;(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 17:1 Six days later Jesus took Peter and the two brothers, James and John, and led them up a high mountain to be alone. (NLT)

Main Idea

Six days later Jesus takes Peter, James, and John and leads them up a high mountain by themselves. Matthew’s precise time note ties what follows to Jesus’ promise that some would see the Son of Man coming in His kingdom.

Verse Explanation

Six days later Jesus takes Peter, James, and John and leads them up a high mountain by themselves. Matthew’s precise time note ties what follows to Jesus’ promise that some would see the Son of Man coming in His kingdom.

“Now after six days Jesus took Peter, James, and John his brother,”

Six days after Jesus’ promise that some would see the Son of Man coming in His kingdom (16:28), He takes three disciples with Him. Peter, James, and John formed an inner circle. They were also with Jesus when He raised Jairus’s daughter (Mark 5:37) and in Gethsemane (26:37).

“led them up on a high mountain by themselves;”

Jesus leads them up “a high mountain,” away from the crowds. In Scripture, God often revealed Himself on mountains, as at Sinai (Exodus 24:15–18).

Study Notes

The mountain is not named. Tradition points to Mount Tabor. Many scholars suggest Mount Hermon, near Caesarea Philippi.

Luke 9:28 says about eight days, counting inclusively.

Matthew 17:2
Matthew 17:2
NKJV

Matthew 17:2 and He was transfigured before them. His face shone like the sun, and His clothes became as white as the light.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 17:2 As the men watched, Jesus’ appearance was transformed so that his face shone like the sun, and his clothes became as white as light. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus is transfigured before them; His face shines like the sun, and His clothes become as white as light. For a moment the three disciples see the divine glory of the King who has just spoken of His suffering.

Verse Explanation

Jesus is transfigured before them; His face shines like the sun, and His clothes become as white as light. For a moment the three disciples see the divine glory of the King who has just spoken of His suffering.

“and He was transfigured before them.”

Jesus is “transfigured.” His appearance changes before their eyes. The glory that was hidden in His humanity shines out.

“His face shone like the sun, and His clothes became as white as the light.”

His face shines like the sun. His clothing becomes dazzling white. The disciples see a glimpse of His divine majesty.

When Moses came down from Sinai, his face shone with reflected glory (Exodus 34:29–35). Jesus’ glory is His own.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “transfigured” is metamorphoo, from which the English word metamorphosis comes. Paul uses it for believers being transformed into the image of Christ (2 Corinthians 3:18) and for being transformed by the renewing of the mind (Romans 12:2).

NLT

Main Idea

Moses and Elijah appear and talk with Jesus. The great representatives of the Law and the Prophets stand with the One who fulfills them.

Verse Explanation

Moses and Elijah appear and talk with Jesus. The great representatives of the Law and the Prophets stand with the One who fulfills them.

“And behold, Moses and Elijah appeared to them, talking with Him.”

“Moses and Elijah” appear and speak with Jesus. Moses gave the law. Elijah was the great prophet. Together they represent the Law and the Prophets, which pointed to Jesus.

Luke 9:31 says they spoke about His departure, which He was about to accomplish at Jerusalem.

Study Notes

Both Moses and Elijah met God on a mountain (Exodus 33:18–23; 1 Kings 19:8–18). Both had unusual departures from this life (Deuteronomy 34:5–6; 2 Kings 2:11).

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

Peter says, “Lord, it is good for us to be here,” and proposes to build three tabernacles, for Jesus, Moses, and Elijah. While he is still speaking, the Father’s voice singles out Jesus as His beloved Son.

“Then Peter answered and said to Jesus, ‘Lord, it is good for us to be here;”

Peter speaks up. He is overwhelmed and wants the moment to last.

“if You wish, let us make here three tabernacles: one for You, one for Moses, and one for Elijah.”

Peter offers to build three shelters. His offer is sincere, but it puts Jesus on the same level as Moses and Elijah. Mark 9:6 says he did not know what to say, because they were greatly afraid.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “tabernacles” means tents or booths, like those used at the Feast of Tabernacles.

Matthew 17:5
NKJV

Matthew 17:5 While he was still speaking, behold, a bright cloud overshadowed them; and suddenly a voice came out of the cloud, saying, "This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. Hear Him!"(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 17:5 But even as he spoke, a bright cloud overshadowed them, and a voice from the cloud said, “This is my dearly loved Son, who brings me great joy. Listen to him.” (NLT)

Main Idea

While Peter is still speaking, a bright cloud overshadows them, and a voice from the cloud says, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. Hear Him!” The Father repeats His baptismal declaration and commands the disciples to listen to His Son, including His words about the cross.

Verse Explanation

While Peter is still speaking, a bright cloud overshadows them, and a voice from the cloud says, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. Hear Him!” The Father repeats His baptismal declaration and commands the disciples to listen to His Son, including His words about the cross.

“While he was still speaking, behold, a bright cloud overshadowed them;”

God interrupts Peter. A bright cloud covers them. In the Old Testament, a cloud often signaled God’s presence (Exodus 40:34–35).

“and suddenly a voice came out of the cloud, saying, ‘This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.”

The Father speaks. He repeats the words spoken at Jesus’ baptism (3:17). Jesus is His “beloved Son.”

“Hear Him!”

The Father adds a command. The disciples must listen to Jesus. Moses had promised that God would raise up a prophet like him, and the people must hear Him (Deuteronomy 18:15). Jesus is that Prophet. He is more than Moses and Elijah. He is God’s Son.

Study Notes

Peter later recalled hearing this voice from heaven when they were with Him on the holy mountain (2 Peter 1:17–18).

The promised forerunner is not still to be awaited. He has already appeared.

“and they did not know him but did to him whatever they wished.”

“Likewise the Son of Man is also about to suffer at their hands.”

What happened to John will happen to Jesus. The forerunner’s rejection points ahead to the suffering of the Son of Man.

Matthew 17:27 However, we don’t want to offend them, so go down to the lake and throw in a line. Open the mouth of the first fish you catch, and you will find a large silver coin. Take it and pay the tax for both of us.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Yet to avoid giving offense, Jesus tells Peter to go to the sea and catch the first fish, promising that he will find in its mouth a coin to pay for both of them. Jesus willingly sets aside His right for the sake of others and Himself provides what is needed.

Verse Explanation

Yet to avoid giving offense, Jesus tells Peter to go to the sea and catch the first fish, promising that he will find in its mouth a coin to pay for both of them. Jesus willingly sets aside His right for the sake of others and Himself provides what is needed.

“Nevertheless, lest we offend them,”

Even though He is free, Jesus chooses to pay. He does not want to cause unnecessary offense.

“go to the sea, cast in a hook, and take the fish that comes up first.”

Jesus sends Peter fishing.

“And when you have opened its mouth, you will find a piece of money;”

Inside the first fish, Peter will find a coin.

“take that and give it to them for Me and you.”

The coin will pay for both Jesus and Peter. Jesus willingly gives up His right and provides what is needed.

Study Notes

The coin was a stater, worth four drachmas. It was exactly enough to pay the tax for two people.
