Matthew 17 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Matthew 17 shows three disciples seeing Jesus transfigured in glory, with Moses and Elijah talking with Him, as the Father declares, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. Hear Him!” (Matthew 17:5). Coming down from the mountain, Jesus explains that Elijah has already come, delivers a boy His disciples could not heal, foretells again that He will be killed and raised the third day, and provides the temple tax for Himself and Peter so as not to cause offense.

Chapter Sections

1. Matthew 17:1–8 — The Transfiguration

Jesus takes Peter, James, and John up on a high mountain and is transfigured before them, His face shining like the sun, and Moses and Elijah appear talking with Him. A bright cloud overshadows them, the Father’s voice commands, “Hear Him!” (Matthew 17:5), and when the terrified disciples lift up their eyes, they see no one but Jesus only.

2. Matthew 17:9–13 — Elijah Has Already Come

Coming down from the mountain, Jesus commands the three to tell the vision to no one until the Son of Man is risen from the dead. When they ask why the scribes say Elijah must come first, He answers that Elijah has come already and was not recognized, and that the Son of Man will likewise suffer, and the disciples understand that He spoke of John the Baptist.

3. Matthew 17:14–21 — Jesus Heals a Boy the Disciples Could Not Heal

A man kneels before Jesus asking mercy for his epileptic son, whom the disciples could not cure, and Jesus rebukes the demon and the child is cured from that very hour. When the disciples ask privately why they could not cast it out, Jesus answers, “Because of your unbelief” (Matthew 17:20), and says that faith as a mustard seed could move a mountain, adding that this kind goes out only by prayer and fasting.

4. Matthew 17:22–23 — Jesus Again Foretells His Death and Resurrection

While they are staying in Galilee, Jesus tells His disciples that the Son of Man is about to be betrayed into the hands of men, who will kill Him, and that the third day He will be raised up. The disciples are exceedingly sorrowful.

5. Matthew 17:24–27 — The Temple Tax and the Coin in the Fish

At Capernaum the collectors of the temple tax ask Peter whether his Teacher pays it, and Jesus asks Peter whether kings take taxes from their sons or from strangers, concluding, “Then the sons are free” (Matthew 17:26). Nevertheless, so as not to offend them, He sends Peter to catch a fish whose mouth holds a coin to pay for both of them.
