Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 17
Main Idea:

On a high mountain Jesus is transfigured before three disciples and the Father commands them to hear His beloved Son, then Jesus delivers a boy His disciples could not help, foretells again His death and resurrection, and freely pays the temple tax to avoid giving offense.

Setting:

Matthew 17 follows six days after Peter’s confession and Jesus’ first announcement of the cross. The chapter moves from the mountain back to Galilee and Capernaum.

Chapter Outline:

  • Matthew 17:1–8 — The Transfiguration

  • Matthew 17:9–13 — Elijah Has Already Come

  • Matthew 17:14–21 — Jesus Heals a Boy the Disciples Could Not Heal

  • Matthew 17:22–23 — Jesus Again Foretells His Death and Resurrection

  • Matthew 17:24–27 — The Temple Tax and the Coin in the Fish

Key Verse:

“While he was still speaking, behold, a bright cloud overshadowed them; and suddenly a voice came out of the cloud, saying, ‘This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. Hear Him!’” (Matthew 17:5)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Six days after promising that some would see the Son of Man coming in His kingdom, Jesus takes Peter, James, and John up a high mountain. His face shines like the sun, and Moses and Elijah talk with Him. A bright cloud overshadows them and the Father speaks: “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. Hear Him!” (17:5). When the disciples look up, they see Jesus only. The glory they glimpse belongs to the One who has just told them He must suffer, and on the way down He links John the Baptist’s rejection with His own.

Coming down, they meet a desperate father whose son suffers terribly, and the disciples have failed to help. Jesus delivers the boy and tells them the reason was their unbelief. Even faith as small as a mustard seed can move mountains because it rests on God. Jesus again foretells His betrayal, death, and resurrection. The chapter closes in Capernaum. As the Son, Jesus is free from the tax for His Father’s house, yet so as not to offend He tells Peter to go and find in a fish’s mouth the coin to pay for them both.

Chapter Sections

Matthew 17:1–8 — The Transfiguration

Jesus is transfigured before Peter, James, and John, and Moses and Elijah appear with Him. The Father’s voice from the cloud declares Jesus His beloved Son and commands them to hear Him.

Matthew 17:9–13 — Elijah Has Already Come

Coming down, Jesus tells the disciples to say nothing until He has risen. He explains that Elijah has already come and was rejected, and they understand that He means John the Baptist, whose suffering foreshadows His own.

Matthew 17:14–21 — Jesus Heals a Boy the Disciples Could Not Heal

Jesus delivers a boy whom His disciples could not heal. He tells them their failure came from unbelief, that faith like a mustard seed can move mountains, and that this kind goes out only by prayer and fasting.

Matthew 17:22–23 — Jesus Again Foretells His Death and Resurrection

In Galilee Jesus tells the disciples again that He will be betrayed, killed, and raised on the third day. They are exceedingly sorrowful.

Matthew 17:24–27 — The Temple Tax and the Coin in the Fish

Asked whether He pays the temple tax, Jesus explains that sons of a king are free. Yet so as not to offend, He sends Peter to find a coin in a fish’s mouth to pay for them both.

Why This Chapter Matters

Matthew 17 shows the glory of the One who is going to the cross. The transfiguration gives three disciples a preview of the King’s majesty, and the Father’s command, “Hear Him,” includes listening when Jesus speaks of suffering. Moses and Elijah withdraw, and Jesus alone remains.

The chapter also teaches dependence. Past success did not keep the disciples from failing, and Jesus traces the failure to their unbelief, not to a weak technique. Faith, prayer, and fasting all look away from self to God. The temple tax shows another side of the King’s character. He has every right to refuse, yet He yields His right for the sake of others.

Key Themes to Watch For

The Son’s glory. Jesus’ face shines, and the Father declares Him His beloved Son (17:2, 5).

Hear Him. The Father commands the disciples to listen to Jesus (17:5).

Suffering before glory. John’s rejection and Jesus’ second prediction point to the cross (17:12, 22–23).

Dependent faith. The disciples’ failure is answered by faith in God, prayer, and fasting (17:19–21).

Freedom and restraint. The free Son pays the tax to avoid offense (17:25–27).

Key Words & Concepts

Transfigured (17:2). Changed in appearance so that His glory shone through. The disciples see who Jesus truly is.

Moses and Elijah (17:3). Great servants of God associated with the Law and the Prophets. They speak with Jesus but are not His equals.

Elijah must come first (17:10). Malachi 4:5 promised Elijah before the day of the LORD. Jesus says John the Baptist fulfilled that role.

Prayer and fasting (17:21). Dependent seeking of God. Many ancient manuscripts lack verse 21, but the same call to prayer appears in Mark 9:29.

Temple tax (17:24). An annual half-shekel payment for the upkeep of the temple. The collectors ask whether Jesus pays it.
