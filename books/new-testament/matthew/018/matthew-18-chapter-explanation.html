Verse-by-Verse
Matthew 18:1
Matthew 18:1
NKJV

Matthew 18:1 At that time the disciples came to Jesus, saying, "Who then is greatest in the kingdom of heaven?"(NKJV)

NLT

Main Idea

The disciples come to Jesus asking, “Who then is greatest in the kingdom of heaven?” Their concern with rank opens Jesus’ teaching on life in His community.

Verse Explanation

The disciples come to Jesus asking, “Who then is greatest in the kingdom of heaven?” Their concern with rank opens Jesus’ teaching on life in His community.

“At that time the disciples came to Jesus, saying,”

The disciples come to Jesus with a question. Mark 9:33–34 says they had been arguing on the way about who was the greatest.

“Who then is greatest in the kingdom of heaven?”

They want to know who will have the highest rank in the kingdom.

Study Notes

Chapter 18 contains the fourth of Jesus’ five major teaching sections in Matthew. It concerns life together among His disciples.

Jesus solemnly says that unless they turn and become like little children, they will by no means enter the kingdom of heaven. Entering the kingdom requires a humble, dependent turning, not ambition for status.

Jesus says they must be “converted,” turned around. They must change direction and become like little children. A child has no status and depends on others.

“you will by no means enter the kingdom of heaven.”

The disciples asked who would be greatest. Jesus says that without this change they will not even enter.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “converted” means to turn. It is the same word used in 13:15.

Matthew 18:6–9 — Warnings against Causing Others to Sin

Matthew 18:6
Matthew 18:6
NKJV

Matthew 18:6 "Whoever causes one of these little ones who believe in Me to sin, it would be better for him if a millstone were hung around his neck, and he were drowned in the depth of the sea.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 18:6 But if you cause one of these little ones who trusts in me to fall into sin, it would be better for you to have a large millstone tied around your neck and be drowned in the depths of the sea. (NLT)

Main Idea

Whoever causes one of these little ones who believe in Jesus to sin would be better off drowned in the sea with a millstone around his neck. Leading a vulnerable believer into sin is so serious that a terrible death would be preferable.

Verse Explanation

Whoever causes one of these little ones who believe in Jesus to sin would be better off drowned in the sea with a millstone around his neck. Leading a vulnerable believer into sin is so serious that a terrible death would be preferable.

“Whoever causes one of these little ones who believe in Me to sin,”

Jesus widens His focus to every humble believer. This includes children and also humble, vulnerable believers. To cause one of them to sin is a serious offense.

“it would be better for him if a millstone were hung around his neck, and he were drowned in the depth of the sea.”

A “millstone” was a heavy stone used to grind grain. Being drowned with one around the neck would be a terrible death. Yet that would be better than the judgment awaiting someone who leads a believer into sin.

Study Notes

The Greek verb behind causes to sin is skandalizo, to make someone stumble.

The millstone here is the large kind turned by a donkey.

Verse Explanation

Jesus warns not to despise one of these little ones, because their angels in heaven always see His Father’s face. God’s little ones are under His watchful care, served by angels who stand in His presence.

Matthew 18:15–20 — Restoring a Brother Who Sins

Matthew 18:15
Matthew 18:15
NKJV

Matthew 18:15 "Moreover if your brother sins against you, go and tell him his fault between you and him alone. If he hears you, you have gained your brother.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 18:15 “If another believer sins against you, go privately and point out the offense. If the other person listens and confesses it, you have won that person back. (NLT)

Main Idea

If a brother sins against you, go and tell him his fault privately, and if he listens, you have gained your brother. The first step aims at restoring the brother, not exposing or punishing him.

Verse Explanation

If a brother sins against you, go and tell him his fault privately, and if he listens, you have gained your brother. The first step aims at restoring the brother, not exposing or punishing him.

“Moreover if your brother sins against you,”

Jesus turns to what disciples should do when another believer sins against them.

“go and tell him his fault between you and him alone.”

The first step is private. Go to the person directly and speak with him alone. Do not spread the matter to others.

“If he hears you, you have gained your brother.”

The goal is restoration. If he listens and turns, the relationship is won back.

This instruction applies to ordinary wrongs between believers. Where someone faces abuse or danger, the person at risk must be protected and the wrongdoing handled responsibly. Jesus’ words must never be used to silence a report or to require someone to face an abuser alone.

Study Notes

Some manuscripts do not include the words against you.

Verse Explanation

Verse Explanation

Jesus concludes that His heavenly Father will do the same to anyone who does not forgive his brother from the heart. The warning is serious and direct; forgiveness releases from the heart the claim to personal revenge, while restored trust still requires repentance and changed conduct.

“So My heavenly Father also will do to you”

Jesus applies the parable directly to His disciples.

“if each of you, from his heart, does not forgive his brother his trespasses.”

God will deal the same way with anyone who refuses to forgive “from his heart.” A person who has truly received God’s mercy will extend mercy to others.

Forgiveness means giving up personal revenge and releasing the debt to God, even if the offender has not repented. Reconciliation is different. Restored trust requires repentance and changed behavior. Forgiveness is one-sided. Reconciliation takes two. A person can forgive and still keep wise boundaries where danger continues.

Forgiveness begins as a decision. The heart may still carry pain and resentment. Through prayer, remembering God’s mercy, honest confession, and the healing work of the Spirit, the heart can come to match the decision. Recurring grief or painful memories do not by themselves mean a person has refused to forgive.

Study Notes

Jesus taught the same principle in 6:14–15.
