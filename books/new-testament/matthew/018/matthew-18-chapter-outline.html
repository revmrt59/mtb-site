Matthew 18 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Matthew 18 records the fourth of Jesus’ major discourses, His teaching on life in the community of His disciples, given in answer to the question, “Who then is greatest in the kingdom of heaven?” (Matthew 18:1). Jesus calls His disciples to humble themselves like a little child, to guard the little ones from offense and seek those who stray, to pursue the restoration of a brother who sins, and to forgive from the heart as those who have been forgiven a debt they could never repay.

Chapter Sections

1. Matthew 18:1–5 — Greatness and the Humility of a Child

When the disciples ask who is greatest in the kingdom of heaven, Jesus sets a little child in their midst and says that unless they are converted and become as little children, they will by no means enter the kingdom. Whoever humbles himself as this little child is greatest in the kingdom, and whoever receives one such child in His name receives Him.

2. Matthew 18:6–9 — Warnings against Causing Others to Sin

Jesus warns that it would be better for whoever causes one of these little ones who believe in Him to sin to be drowned in the depth of the sea with a millstone around his neck. He declares woe because of offenses and, with the vivid picture of cutting off a hand or foot or plucking out an eye, calls His disciples to act decisively against whatever causes them to sin rather than be cast into everlasting fire.

3. Matthew 18:10–14 — The Father’s Care for the Little Ones and the Straying Sheep

Jesus warns His disciples not to despise one of these little ones, for their angels always see the face of His Father in heaven, and He tells of a man who leaves the ninety-nine to seek the one sheep that has gone astray and rejoices when he finds it. So it is not the will of the Father in heaven “that one of these little ones should perish” (Matthew 18:14).

4. Matthew 18:15–20 — Restoring a Brother Who Sins

If a brother sins against you, Jesus says, go and tell him his fault in private, for if he hears you, you have gained your brother; only if he will not hear are one or two more to be taken as witnesses, then if he refuses to hear them the matter is told to the church, and if he refuses to hear the church he is to be to you like a heathen and a tax collector. Jesus promises that what they bind and loose on earth will be bound and loosed in heaven, and that where two or three are gathered together in His name, He is there in the midst of them.

5. Matthew 18:21–35 — Forgiveness and the Parable of the Unforgiving Servant

When Peter asks whether he should forgive his brother up to seven times, Jesus answers, “up to seventy times seven” (Matthew 18:22), and tells of a king who forgives a servant an enormous debt, only to have that servant throw a fellow servant into prison over a small one. The king delivers the unforgiving servant to the torturers, and Jesus warns that His heavenly Father will do the same to anyone who does not forgive his brother from the heart.
