Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 18
Main Idea:

Answering the disciples’ question about greatness, Jesus teaches the life of His community: humble like a child, careful never to cause a little one to stumble, seeking the straying, restoring a sinning brother, and forgiving from the heart as those forgiven an unpayable debt.

Setting:

Matthew 18 is the fourth of Matthew’s great discourses, given in Capernaum after the temple-tax scene. Jesus speaks to His disciples about life together under His reign.

Chapter Outline:

  • Matthew 18:1–5 — Greatness and the Humility of a Child

  • Matthew 18:6–9 — Warnings against Causing Others to Sin

  • Matthew 18:10–14 — The Father’s Care for the Little Ones and the Straying Sheep

  • Matthew 18:15–20 — Restoring a Brother Who Sins

  • Matthew 18:21–35 — Forgiveness and the Parable of the Unforgiving Servant

Key Verse:

“‘So My heavenly Father also will do to you if each of you, from his heart, does not forgive his brother his trespasses.’” (Matthew 18:35)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The disciples want to know who is greatest. Jesus answers by placing a little child among them. Unless they turn and become humble like this child, they will not even enter the kingdom. From there His teaching moves to the “little ones who believe in Me” (18:6). Anyone who causes one of them to stumble faces terrible judgment. The Father’s angels always see His face, and like a shepherd leaving ninety-nine sheep to find one, the Father does not want a single little one to perish.

When a brother sins, the goal is to gain him. Jesus gives a patient path: go privately, then with witnesses, then to the church. The church acts under His authority, and He promises His presence where two or three gather in His name. Asked by Peter how often to forgive, Jesus tells of a servant forgiven a debt of ten thousand talents, a sum no one could repay, who then throttles a fellow servant over a hundred denarii. The king hands him over to the torturers. Mercy received must become mercy given, and Jesus warns that the Father will do the same to those who do not forgive from the heart.

Chapter Sections

Matthew 18:1–5 — Greatness and the Humility of a Child

Jesus places a child among His disciples and teaches that the greatest in the kingdom is the one who humbles himself like this child. Whoever receives such a child in His name receives Him.

Matthew 18:6–9 — Warnings against Causing Others to Sin

Jesus warns of severe judgment for anyone who causes one of His little ones to sin. He uses the images of cutting off a hand or foot and plucking out an eye to call for decisive action against sin.

Matthew 18:10–14 — The Father’s Care for the Little Ones and the Straying Sheep

Jesus warns against despising the little ones, whose angels always see the Father’s face. Like a shepherd seeking one lost sheep, the Father is not willing that one of them should perish.

Matthew 18:15–20 — Restoring a Brother Who Sins

Jesus teaches how to seek a brother who sins, moving from a private appeal to witnesses and then to the church. He gives authority to bind and loose and promises His presence among two or three gathered in His name.

Matthew 18:21–35 — Forgiveness and the Parable of the Unforgiving Servant

When Peter asks how often to forgive, Jesus answers seventy times seven. In the parable of the unforgiving servant, a man forgiven an enormous debt refuses mercy to a fellow servant, and the king hands him over to judgment.

Why This Chapter Matters

Matthew 18 shows that the King’s community is built on humility and care for the vulnerable, not on status. Greatness means taking the lowly place, protecting weak believers, and going after those who wander. The Father values each little one, so His people must too.

The chapter also gives a clear pattern for dealing with sin and offense. Correction aims at restoration, not humiliation, and it never requires someone at risk to face an abuser alone. Forgiveness is not the same as reconciliation. Forgiving from the heart releases personal revenge to God even before the offender repents, while restored trust requires repentance and changed conduct. The warning of the parable gives that call its full weight.

Key Themes to Watch For

Childlike humility. True greatness takes the lowly place (18:3–4).

Care for little ones. Disciples must not cause the vulnerable to stumble (18:6–10).

The seeking Father. He wills that none of His little ones perish (18:12–14).

Restoring a brother. Correction aims to gain the one who sins (18:15–17).

Forgiveness from the heart. Mercy received must be extended to others (18:21–35).

Key Words & Concepts

Little ones (18:6). Children, and also humble believers in Jesus who are easily hurt or led astray.

Their angels (18:10). Angels serve God’s little ones and stand in the Father’s presence. Jesus uses this to show how much the Father values them.

Bind and loose (18:18). Authority given to the church to act under the King’s word in matters of sin, repentance, and fellowship. Its decisions are accountable to heaven.

Seventy times seven (18:22). A way of saying that forgiveness has no set limit.

Ten thousand talents (18:24). An enormous sum, far beyond what any servant could repay. It pictures the debt of sin God forgives.

A hundred denarii (18:28). About a hundred days’ wages. A real debt, but tiny compared to what the first servant was forgiven.
