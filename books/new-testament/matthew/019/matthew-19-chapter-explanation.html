Matthew 19:9
Matthew 19:9
NKJV

Matthew 19:9 And I say to you, whoever divorces his wife, except for sexual immorality, and marries another, commits adultery; and whoever marries her who is divorced commits adultery."(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 19:9 And I tell you this, whoever divorces his wife and marries someone else commits adultery — unless his wife has been unfaithful. ” (NLT)

Main Idea

Whoever divorces his wife, except for sexual immorality, and marries another commits adultery, and whoever marries her who is divorced commits adultery. Jesus names sexual immorality as the exception and treats faithless divorce and remarriage as adultery against the marriage; the wider teaching of Scripture, including Paul in 1 Corinthians 7:15, gives further guidance.

Verse Explanation

Whoever divorces his wife, except for sexual immorality, and marries another commits adultery, and whoever marries her who is divorced commits adultery. Jesus names sexual immorality as the exception and treats faithless divorce and remarriage as adultery against the marriage; the wider teaching of Scripture, including Paul in 1 Corinthians 7:15, gives further guidance.

“And I say to you, whoever divorces his wife, except for sexual immorality,”

Jesus states His own teaching. He names one exception: “sexual immorality.” When a spouse commits sexual sin, the faithful spouse is not bound in the same way.

“and marries another, commits adultery;”

A man who divorces his wife without this ground and marries another commits adultery. In God’s eyes, the first covenant was wrongly broken.

“and whoever marries her who is divorced commits adultery.”

The same applies to the one who marries a wrongly divorced person.

Paul later addresses another situation. When an unbelieving spouse departs, the believer is not under bondage (1 Corinthians 7:12–15). This principle of abandonment can also apply to a spouse who repudiates the marriage covenant even while living in the same home, including persistent unrepentant physical abuse. Matthew does not name abuse as an exception. That conclusion is pastoral application from Paul’s teaching. Where there is danger, protection of a spouse and children must not be delayed.

Wrongful divorce is serious but forgivable. A remarriage entered wrongly may be an act of adultery against the former covenant. It is not a state of continuing adultery. The right response is repentance and faithfulness to the present marriage, not another divorce to undo the past (Deuteronomy 24:1–4).

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “sexual immorality” is porneia, a broad term for sexual sin.

Some manuscripts do not include the last clause of this verse.

Peter says, “See, we have left all and followed You. Therefore what shall we have?” Peter contrasts the disciples with the rich young man and asks about their reward.

Verse Explanation

Peter says, “See, we have left all and followed You. Therefore what shall we have?” Peter contrasts the disciples with the rich young man and asks about their reward.

“Then Peter answered and said to Him, ‘See, we have left all and followed You.”

Peter speaks up. The disciples have done what the rich man would not do.

“Therefore what shall we have?”

Peter asks what they will receive.

Study Notes

Peter, Andrew, James, and John left their nets and boats (4:20, 22).

NLT

Matthew 19:28 Jesus replied, “I assure you that when the world is made new and the Son of Man sits upon his glorious throne, you who have been my followers will also sit on twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus tells the Twelve that in the regeneration, the renewal of all things, when the Son of Man sits on His glorious throne, they who have followed Him will sit on twelve thrones judging the twelve tribes of Israel. This specific promise belongs to the apostles, who will share in His reign over a renewed Israel.

Verse Explanation

Jesus tells the Twelve that in the regeneration, the renewal of all things, when the Son of Man sits on His glorious throne, they who have followed Him will sit on twelve thrones judging the twelve tribes of Israel. This specific promise belongs to the apostles, who will share in His reign over a renewed Israel.

“So Jesus said to them, ‘Assuredly I say to you, that in the regeneration,”

“The regeneration” is the renewal of all things at the end of the age. God will make all things new (Revelation 21:5).

“when the Son of Man sits on the throne of His glory,”

Jesus will reign in glory as King.

“you who have followed Me will also sit on twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel.”

The apostles will share in His rule. They will sit on “twelve thrones” over “the twelve tribes of Israel.”

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “regeneration” is palingenesia, meaning new birth or renewal. Paul uses it for personal new birth in Titus 3:5.

NLT

Matthew 19:29 And everyone who has given up houses or brothers or sisters or father or mother or children or property, for my sake, will receive a hundred times as much in return and will inherit eternal life. (NLT)

Main Idea

Everyone who has left houses, family, or lands for Jesus’ name will receive a hundredfold and inherit eternal life. No sacrifice made for Jesus goes unrewarded, and the reward far exceeds what was given up.

Verse Explanation

Everyone who has left houses, family, or lands for Jesus’ name will receive a hundredfold and inherit eternal life. No sacrifice made for Jesus goes unrewarded, and the reward far exceeds what was given up.

“And everyone who has left houses or brothers or sisters or father or mother or wife or children or lands, for My name’s sake,”

The promise reaches beyond the Twelve. Anyone who gives up home, family, or property “for My name’s sake” will be rewarded.

“shall receive a hundredfold, and inherit eternal life.”

They will receive far more than they gave up. Mark 10:30 says they receive it now in this time, with persecutions, and in the age to come eternal life. The new family of God replaces what was lost.

Study Notes

The reward includes the fellowship of God’s people as brothers, sisters, and mothers (12:49–50).

NLT

Matthew 19:30 But many who are the greatest now will be least important then, and those who seem least important now will be the greatest then. (NLT)

Main Idea

But many who are first will be last, and the last first. God’s kingdom reverses human measures of status and privilege, a point the following parable develops.

Verse Explanation

But many who are first will be last, and the last first. God’s kingdom reverses human measures of status and privilege, a point the following parable develops.

“But many who are first will be last, and the last first.”

God’s kingdom reverses the world’s order. Those who seem important now may be last. Those who seem unimportant may be first.

Study Notes

Jesus illustrates this saying with the parable in 20:1–16 and repeats it in 20:16.
