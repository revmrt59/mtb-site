Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 19
Main Idea:

On the way toward Jerusalem, Jesus points the Pharisees back to God’s creation design for marriage, honors those called to singleness, welcomes little children, and exposes a rich young man’s attachment to wealth, declaring that salvation is possible only with God.

Setting:

Matthew 19 opens as Jesus finishes His fourth discourse and leaves Galilee for Judea beyond the Jordan. Great crowds follow, and He heals them there.

Chapter Outline:

  • Matthew 19:1–2 — Jesus Departs from Galilee for Judea

  • Matthew 19:3–12 — Marriage, Divorce, and Those Who Remain Unmarried

  • Matthew 19:13–15 — Jesus Blesses the Little Children

  • Matthew 19:16–22 — The Rich Young Man

  • Matthew 19:23–30 — Possible with God and the Reward of Those Who Follow

Key Verse:

“But Jesus looked at them and said to them, ‘With men this is impossible, but with God all things are possible.’” (Matthew 19:26)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The Pharisees try to trap Jesus with a question about divorce “for just any reason” (19:3). He takes them back to the beginning. God made them male and female, and in marriage the two become one flesh. What God has joined together, no one should separate. Moses permitted divorce because of hard hearts, but that was never the design. Jesus names one exception, sexual immorality. When the disciples react, He speaks of some who receive the calling to remain unmarried for the kingdom’s sake.

Then people bring little children, and the disciples try to send them away. Jesus welcomes them, saying the kingdom of heaven belongs to such as these. A rich young man comes asking what good thing he must do for eternal life. Jesus points him to God’s commands and then to the one thing holding him: sell your possessions, give to the poor, and follow Me. He goes away sorrowful. Jesus says it is easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle than for a rich man to enter the kingdom. With men it is impossible, “but with God all things are possible” (19:26).

Chapter Sections

Matthew 19:1–2 — Jesus Departs from Galilee for Judea

Having finished His teaching in Galilee, Jesus crosses into Judea beyond the Jordan. Great multitudes follow, and He heals them there.

Matthew 19:3–12 — Marriage, Divorce, and Those Who Remain Unmarried

Pharisees test Jesus about divorce, and He answers from creation that God joins husband and wife as one flesh. Moses’ permission came from hard hearts, divorce except for sexual immorality leads to adultery, and some receive the gift of singleness for the kingdom’s sake.

Matthew 19:13–15 — Jesus Blesses the Little Children

Little children are brought to Jesus for prayer, and when the disciples rebuke the people, Jesus welcomes the children. He lays His hands on them, saying that the kingdom belongs to such as these.

Matthew 19:16–22 — The Rich Young Man

A rich young man asks what good thing will gain him eternal life. Jesus points him to the commandments, then calls him to sell his possessions, give to the poor, and follow Him, and the young man leaves sorrowful.

Matthew 19:23–30 — Possible with God and the Reward of Those Who Follow

Jesus says it is hard for a rich man to enter the kingdom, but with God all things are possible. He promises the Twelve thrones in the regeneration and a hundredfold reward with eternal life to all who leave everything for His sake.

Why This Chapter Matters

Matthew 19 shows that God’s design governs life under the King. Marriage is a lifelong union that God Himself joins, and divorce is a serious departure from His purpose, though wrongful divorce is not beyond His forgiveness. Singleness received as a calling is honored too. Jesus names one exception, sexual immorality (19:9). Paul later addresses a different case, an unbelieving spouse who departs (1 Corinthians 7:12–15). Where there is abuse, pastoral care must first seek the protection of those in danger.

The chapter also exposes what competes with the King. The rich young man said he had kept the commandments (19:20), but Jesus’ call to sell his possessions exposed how tightly he held his wealth. Jesus does not lower the cost. He locates the hope of salvation in God, who does what people cannot do, and He promises that nothing given up for Him will be lost.

Key Themes to Watch For

God’s design for marriage. Creation governs Jesus’ answer about divorce (19:4–6).

Hard hearts. Moses’ permission responded to sin, not God’s original purpose (19:8).

Singleness for the kingdom. Some receive the call to remain unmarried (19:11–12).

Welcome for children. The kingdom belongs to such as these (19:14).

Wealth and salvation. Riches compete for the heart, but God makes salvation possible (19:21–26).

Key Words & Concepts

One flesh (19:5). The full union of husband and wife that God creates in marriage, quoted from Genesis 2:24.

Certificate of divorce (19:7). A written document Moses required in Deuteronomy 24:1. It regulated divorce without approving it.

Sexual immorality (19:9). The exception Jesus names in Matthew. Other biblical passages address related situations, such as a departing unbelieving spouse (1 Corinthians 7:12–15).

Eunuchs for the kingdom (19:12). Those who forgo marriage to serve God’s kingdom. Jesus presents this as a calling that some are given to receive.

Eye of a needle (19:24). A real sewing needle and a real camel. The exaggeration shows something impossible for people, which only God can do.

The regeneration (19:28). The renewal of all things when the Son of Man sits on His glorious throne.
