Matthew 20:1
Matthew 20:1
NKJV

Matthew 20:1 "For the kingdom of heaven is like a landowner who went out early in the morning to hire laborers for his vineyard.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 20:1 “For the Kingdom of Heaven is like the landowner who went out early one morning to hire workers for his vineyard. (NLT)

Main Idea

The kingdom of heaven is like a landowner who goes out early in the morning to hire laborers for his vineyard. Jesus answers Peter’s question about reward with a parable about the owner’s generosity.

Verse Explanation

The kingdom of heaven is like a landowner who goes out early in the morning to hire laborers for his vineyard. Jesus answers Peter’s question about reward with a parable about the owner’s generosity.

“For the kingdom of heaven is like a landowner who went out early in the morning to hire laborers for his vineyard.”

Jesus tells a parable that explains His words in 19:30: the first will be last and the last first. Peter had just asked what the disciples would receive for leaving everything (19:27). This parable answers his question.

A landowner goes out at dawn to hire workers for his vineyard. Day laborers gathered in the marketplace each morning, hoping to be hired.

Study Notes

The workday ran from about 6 a.m. to 6 p.m.

The law required that a hired worker be paid at the end of each day (Deuteronomy 24:14–15).

Matthew 20:15
Matthew 20:15
NKJV

Matthew 20:15 Is it not lawful for me to do what I wish with my own things? Or is your eye evil because I am good?'(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 20:15 Is it against the law for me to do what I want with my money? Should you be jealous because I am kind to others?’ (NLT)

Main Idea

The landowner asks whether he may not do what he wishes with his own things, or whether the worker’s eye is evil because the owner is good. The complaint exposes envy at another’s blessing, a heart that bargains for status instead of rejoicing in grace.

Verse Explanation

The landowner asks whether he may not do what he wishes with his own things, or whether the worker’s eye is evil because the owner is good. The complaint exposes envy at another’s blessing, a heart that bargains for status instead of rejoicing in grace.

“Is it not lawful for me to do what I wish with my own things?”

The money belongs to the owner. He may be generous with it.

“Or is your eye evil because I am good?”

An evil eye was a way of describing envy. The owner asks whether they are jealous because he is generous.

The problem is not unfairness. It is envy and entitlement. The first workers think their seniority and hard work give them a claim on the owner’s generosity. The opportunity to serve was already a gift. Service should come from love and gratitude, not from bargaining for status.

Study Notes

Jesus spoke of the evil eye in 6:23.

Matthew 20:16
Matthew 20:16
NKJV

Matthew 20:16 So the last will be first, and the first last. For many are called, but few chosen."(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 20:16 “So those who are last now will be first then, and those who are first will be last.” (NLT)

Main Idea

So the last will be first and the first last, for many are called but few chosen. In God’s kingdom service is a response to grace, not a claim on Him, and the workday also pictures God calling laborers throughout the ages, even at the eleventh hour before Christ returns.

Verse Explanation

So the last will be first and the first last, for many are called but few chosen. In God’s kingdom service is a response to grace, not a claim on Him, and the workday also pictures God calling laborers throughout the ages, even at the eleventh hour before Christ returns.

“So the last will be first, and the first last.”

Jesus repeats the saying of 19:30. God’s grace reverses human expectations.

“For many are called, but few chosen.”

Many hear God’s call. Not all respond with the right heart.

This parable can also be read as a picture of gospel work through the ages. God keeps calling workers into His harvest, even late in the day before Christ returns. Earlier workers should rejoice in His generosity to those called late. The hours are not a timetable, and they do not predict how long remains.

Study Notes

Many manuscripts do not include the last sentence of this verse. It appears in 22:14.

“just as the Son of Man did not come to be served,”

Jesus points to Himself. He did not come to be served, though He is the King.

“but to serve,”

He came to serve.

“and to give His life a ransom for many.”

This is the purpose of His coming. A ransom was a price paid to set someone free. Jesus gives His life in the place of many. His death pays the price that satisfies God’s just judgment against sin. It sets His people free. The ransom is not paid to Satan.

Jesus’ death is unique. Only His death saves. His disciples follow His example of service, but they cannot pay for anyone’s sin.

Study Notes

The Greek preposition translated “for” means in place of. It expresses substitution.

Isaiah 53:10–12 speaks of the Servant who bore the sin of many.
