Matthew 20 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Matthew 20 continues Jesus’ journey toward Jerusalem with the parable of the laborers in the vineyard, in which the landowner’s generosity overturns the first workers’ expectations, and with Jesus’ third announcement that He will be condemned, crucified, and raised the third day. When the mother of Zebedee’s sons asks for places of honor for them, Jesus teaches that greatness is found in service, for the Son of Man came to give His life a ransom for many, and the chapter ends as He gives sight to two blind men who call Him Son of David.

Chapter Sections

1. Matthew 20:1–16 — The Laborers in the Vineyard

Jesus likens the kingdom of heaven to a landowner who hires laborers at different hours of the day and at evening pays the last as much as the first, a denarius each. When the first complain, the landowner answers that he has done them no wrong and asks, “Or is your eye evil because I am good?” (Matthew 20:15), and Jesus concludes, “So the last will be first, and the first last” (Matthew 20:16).

2. Matthew 20:17–19 — Jesus Foretells His Death a Third Time

Going up to Jerusalem, Jesus takes the twelve disciples aside and tells them that the Son of Man will be betrayed to the chief priests and scribes, who will condemn Him to death. They will deliver Him to the Gentiles to mock, scourge, and crucify, and the third day He will rise again.

3. Matthew 20:20–28 — Greatness in Serving and the Son of Man’s Ransom

The mother of Zebedee’s sons asks that her two sons sit at Jesus’ right hand and left in His kingdom, and Jesus asks whether they are able to drink the cup He is about to drink. When the ten are displeased, He teaches that whoever desires to be great must be a servant, “just as the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give His life a ransom for many” (Matthew 20:28).

4. Matthew 20:29–34 — Two Blind Men Receive Their Sight

As Jesus leaves Jericho with a great multitude, two blind men by the road cry out, “Have mercy on us, O Lord, Son of David!” (Matthew 20:30), and they cry out all the more when the multitude tells them to be quiet. Jesus has compassion and touches their eyes, and immediately they receive their sight and follow Him.
