Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 20
Main Idea:

Jesus tells of a generous landowner who pays late workers the same as early ones, foretells His death a third time, redefines greatness as serving after the pattern of the Son of Man who gives His life as a ransom, and opens the eyes of two blind men.

Setting:

Matthew 20 continues Jesus’ teaching on the road to Jerusalem. The parable answers Peter’s question in 19:27 about what the disciples will receive for leaving everything.

Chapter Outline:

  • Matthew 20:1–16 — The Laborers in the Vineyard

  • Matthew 20:17–19 — Jesus Foretells His Death a Third Time

  • Matthew 20:20–28 — Greatness in Serving and the Son of Man’s Ransom

  • Matthew 20:29–34 — Two Blind Men Receive Their Sight

Key Verse:

“just as the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give His life a ransom for many.’” (Matthew 20:28)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Peter has asked what the disciples will have for leaving everything. Jesus answers with a landowner who hires workers throughout the day, some at dawn and some at the eleventh hour, and pays every one of them a denarius. Those hired first complain. The landowner has kept his agreement, and he is free to be generous with what is his. The story exposes envy, comparison, and entitlement. It also pictures gospel labor through the ages, with laborers still being called late in the day before Christ returns.

On the road, Jesus tells the Twelve for the third time that He will be condemned, mocked, scourged, and crucified, and will rise on the third day. Almost immediately the mother of James and John asks for the best seats in His kingdom, and the other ten are indignant. Jesus redefines greatness. Among His people, the great serve and the first become slaves, because “the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give His life a ransom for many” (20:28). Leaving Jericho, two blind men cry out to the Son of David. Jesus stops, has compassion, touches their eyes, and they follow Him.

Chapter Sections

Matthew 20:1–16 — The Laborers in the Vineyard

A landowner hires laborers at different hours and pays them all a denarius, beginning with the last. When the first complain, he answers that he has done them no wrong and is free to be generous, for the last will be first and the first last.

Matthew 20:17–19 — Jesus Foretells His Death a Third Time

Going up to Jerusalem, Jesus takes the Twelve aside and foretells that He will be betrayed, condemned, handed to the Gentiles, mocked, scourged, and crucified. He promises that He will rise on the third day.

Matthew 20:20–28 — Greatness in Serving and the Son of Man’s Ransom

The mother of James and John asks for her sons to sit beside Jesus in His kingdom, and the other disciples are indignant. Jesus teaches that greatness among His people is serving, after the pattern of the Son of Man who gives His life a ransom for many.

Matthew 20:29–34 — Two Blind Men Receive Their Sight

Leaving Jericho, two blind men cry out to Jesus as Lord and Son of David, though the crowd tells them to be quiet. Moved with compassion, Jesus touches their eyes, and they immediately see and follow Him.

Why This Chapter Matters

Matthew 20 searches the motives behind service. A worker can be busy and zealous while quietly bargaining for recognition or advantage. The landowner’s generosity shows that service is a response to grace, not a way to put God in debt. It also calls earlier workers to rejoice when God welcomes those who come late.

The chapter also stands at the center of Jesus’ saving mission. He gives His life in the place of many as their ransom, a unique act that sets His people free. The disciples are called to imitate His service, not to repeat His sacrifice. Greatness in His kingdom looks like the Son of Man stooping to serve.

Key Themes to Watch For

Grace in reward. The landowner is fair to all and generous to the last (20:13–15).

Envy and entitlement. The first workers resent the landowner’s kindness (20:11–12).

The coming cross. Jesus foretells His suffering and resurrection a third time (20:18–19).

Greatness in serving. The great among Jesus’ people are servants (20:26–27).

Mercy to those who cry out. Jesus hears the blind men and opens their eyes (20:30–34).

Key Words & Concepts

Denarius (20:2). A silver coin that was a common day’s wage for a laborer.

Eleventh hour (20:6). About five in the afternoon, with one hour of work left in the day.

Is your eye evil? (20:15). A way of asking whether someone is envious or grudging toward another’s good.

Cup (20:22). The suffering Jesus is about to drink. James and John will share suffering, though not His saving death.

Ransom (20:28). A price paid to set captives free. Jesus gives His life in the place of many, bearing what their sins deserve.
