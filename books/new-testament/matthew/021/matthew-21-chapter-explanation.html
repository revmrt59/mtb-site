Verse Explanation

As they approach Jerusalem and reach Bethphage at the Mount of Olives, Jesus sends two disciples ahead. The long journey to Jerusalem now reaches the city, and Jesus Himself directs what happens next.

Study Notes

Zechariah 14:4 pictures the LORD standing on the Mount of Olives in the day of His coming.

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

The prophecy says, “Behold, your King is coming to you, Lowly, and sitting on a donkey,” on a colt, the foal of a donkey. Zechariah’s promise shows Jesus entering as Israel’s King, humble rather than a conqueror on a war horse.

“Tell the daughter of Zion,”

This poetic name refers to Jerusalem and its people.

“Behold, your King is coming to you,”

Their “King” is coming. Jesus enters Jerusalem as the promised King of Israel.

“Lowly, and sitting on a donkey, A colt, the foal of a donkey.”

He comes “lowly,” humble and gentle. Conquering kings rode war horses. This King rides a donkey, the animal of peace. He comes to save, not to conquer by force.

Study Notes

Zechariah 9:9 adds that the King is just and having salvation. Zechariah 9:10 says He will speak peace to the nations.

Matthew 21:12–17 — Jesus Cleanses the Temple

Matthew 21:12
Matthew 21:12
NKJV

Matthew 21:12 Then Jesus went into the temple of God and drove out all those who bought and sold in the temple, and overturned the tables of the money changers and the seats of those who sold doves.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 21:12 Jesus entered the Temple and began to drive out all the people buying and selling animals for sacrifice. He knocked over the tables of the money changers and the chairs of those selling doves. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus enters the temple, drives out all who are buying and selling, and overturns the tables of the money changers and the seats of those selling doves. The King acts with authority to confront trading that has corrupted the purpose of God’s house.

Verse Explanation

Jesus enters the temple, drives out all who are buying and selling, and overturns the tables of the money changers and the seats of those selling doves. The King acts with authority to confront trading that has corrupted the purpose of God’s house.

“Then Jesus went into the temple of God”

Jesus enters the temple, the center of Israel’s worship.

“and drove out all those who bought and sold in the temple,”

He drives out the merchants. They sold animals for sacrifice in the outer court, the Court of the Gentiles.

“and overturned the tables of the money changers and the seats of those who sold doves.”

He overturns the tables of “the money changers,” who exchanged foreign coins for the coins required for the temple tax. He overturns the seats of those selling “doves,” the offering of the poor (Leviticus 12:8).

Study Notes

John 2:13–17 records a cleansing of the temple at the start of Jesus’ ministry.

Verse Explanation

He says, “It is written, ‘My house shall be called a house of prayer,’ but you have made it a ‘den of thieves.’” Joining Isaiah and Jeremiah, Jesus declares that the temple’s purpose of prayer has been replaced by exploitation.

“And He said to them, ‘It is written, ‘My house shall be called a house of prayer,”

Jesus quotes Isaiah 56:7. God’s house was meant to be “a house of prayer.” Isaiah adds that it was to be a house of prayer for all nations. The merchants filled the very court where Gentiles could pray.

“but you have made it a ‘den of thieves.”

He quotes Jeremiah 7:11. A “den of thieves” is a hideout where robbers feel safe. The leaders used the temple to profit and to shelter their sin.

Verse Explanation

Seeing a fig tree by the road, He finds nothing on it but leaves and says, “Let no fruit grow on you ever again,” and immediately the tree withers. Set between His temple action and His parables, the leafy but fruitless tree pictures a show of religion without the fruit God seeks.

“And seeing a fig tree by the road, He came to it and found nothing on it but leaves,”

Jesus sees a fig tree with leaves. A tree in leaf usually had early fruit. This tree has only leaves.

“and said to it, ‘Let no fruit grow on you ever again.’ Immediately the fig tree withered away.”

Jesus speaks to the tree, and it withers at once.

The tree pictures outward show without the fruit God seeks. Jesus has just found the temple full of activity but without true worship. The leafy, fruitless tree is a living picture of that condition.

Study Notes

The prophets compared Israel to a fig tree (Jeremiah 8:13; Hosea 9:10).
