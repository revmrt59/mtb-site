Matthew 21 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Matthew 21 brings Jesus into Jerusalem as the King who comes “Lowly, and sitting on a donkey” (Matthew 21:5), acclaimed by the multitudes as the Son of David. He cleanses the temple, curses a fruitless fig tree, and answers the chief priests and elders who challenge His authority with a question about John’s baptism and with two parables, the two sons and the wicked vinedressers, which warn the leaders that the kingdom of God will be taken from them and given to a nation bearing its fruits.

Chapter Sections

1. Matthew 21:1–11 — The King Enters Jerusalem

Jesus sends two disciples to bring a donkey and her colt, fulfilling the prophet’s word that Zion’s King would come to her lowly and sitting on a donkey. A very great multitude spreads clothes and branches on the road and cries, “Hosanna to the Son of David!” (Matthew 21:9), and when all the city asks who He is, the multitudes answer, “This is Jesus, the prophet from Nazareth of Galilee” (Matthew 21:11).

2. Matthew 21:12–17 — Jesus Cleanses the Temple

Jesus drives out those who buy and sell in the temple, declaring that it is written, “My house shall be called a house of prayer,” but they have made it a den of thieves (Matthew 21:13), and He heals the blind and the lame who come to Him there. When the chief priests and scribes are indignant at the children crying “Hosanna to the Son of David!” in the temple, Jesus answers them from the psalm about praise perfected from the mouths of babes, and He goes out to Bethany.

3. Matthew 21:18–22 — The Withered Fig Tree and the Prayer of Faith

Returning to the city in the morning, Jesus finds a fig tree with nothing on it but leaves and says, “Let no fruit grow on you ever again” (Matthew 21:19), and immediately it withers away. When the disciples marvel, He tells them that with faith and without doubt they could do this and even more, for whatever they ask in prayer, believing, they will receive.

4. Matthew 21:23–27 — The Leaders Question Jesus’ Authority

As Jesus teaches in the temple, the chief priests and elders ask by what authority He does these things. He answers with His own question, whether John’s baptism was from heaven or from men, and when they answer, “We do not know” (Matthew 21:27), He declines to tell them by what authority He acts.

5. Matthew 21:28–32 — The Parable of the Two Sons

Jesus tells of a man whose first son refuses to work in the vineyard but afterward regrets it and goes, while the second son says he will go but does not. The leaders acknowledge that the first did his father’s will, and Jesus tells them that tax collectors and harlots are entering the kingdom of God before them, because these believed John while the leaders did not.

6. Matthew 21:33–46 — The Parable of the Wicked Vinedressers

Jesus tells of a landowner whose vinedressers beat and kill the servants he sends for his fruit and finally kill his son, and He applies the psalm, “The stone which the builders rejected Has become the chief cornerstone” (Matthew 21:42). He declares that “the kingdom of God will be taken from you and given to a nation bearing the fruits of it” (Matthew 21:43), and the chief priests and Pharisees, perceiving that He speaks of them, seek to lay hands on Him but fear the multitudes.
