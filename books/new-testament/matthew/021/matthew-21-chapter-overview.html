Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 21
Main Idea:

Jesus enters Jerusalem as the humble King of Zechariah’s prophecy, cleanses the temple, withers a fruitless fig tree, refuses to answer leaders who will not be honest about John, and through two parables exposes their refusal to obey God and to receive His Son.

Setting:

Matthew 21 opens the fifth movement, The King Confronts Jerusalem (21:1–23:39). Jesus arrives from Jericho at the Mount of Olives days before the Passover.

Chapter Outline:

  • Matthew 21:1–11 — The King Enters Jerusalem

  • Matthew 21:12–17 — Jesus Cleanses the Temple

  • Matthew 21:18–22 — The Withered Fig Tree and the Prayer of Faith

  • Matthew 21:23–27 — The Leaders Question Jesus’ Authority

  • Matthew 21:28–32 — The Parable of the Two Sons

  • Matthew 21:33–46 — The Parable of the Wicked Vinedressers

Key Verse:

“Then the multitudes who went before and those who followed cried out, saying: ‘Hosanna to the Son of David! “Blessed is He who comes in the name of the LORD!” Hosanna in the highest!’” (Matthew 21:9)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Jesus plans His arrival. He sends for a donkey and her colt, and Matthew sees Zechariah’s promise fulfilled: “your King is coming to you, lowly, and sitting on a donkey” (21:5). The crowds spread garments and branches and cry, “Hosanna to the Son of David!” (21:9). Their “Hosanna” and the blessing on “He who comes in the name of the LORD” draw from Psalm 118:25–26, and the crowd applies the title “Son of David” to Jesus. In the temple He drives out the traders, quoting Isaiah and Jeremiah: God’s house of prayer has been made a den of thieves. Then the blind and lame come to Him there, and He heals them. The King arrives with both authority and mercy.

The next morning a fig tree full of leaves but without fruit withers at His word. Set between the temple scene and the parables, it pictures religion with an impressive appearance and no fruit. The chief priests and elders demand to know His authority, but they will not answer honestly about John. Jesus then tells of two sons, one who says no and later obeys, one who says yes and never goes. He tells of tenants who beat the owner’s servants and kill his son. The stone the builders rejected becomes the cornerstone, and the kingdom will be given to a people bearing its fruits.

Chapter Sections

Matthew 21:1–11 — The King Enters Jerusalem

Jesus rides into Jerusalem on a donkey’s colt, fulfilling Zechariah 9:9, as crowds spread garments and branches and cry, “Hosanna to the Son of David!” The whole city asks who He is, and the crowds call Him the prophet from Nazareth.

Matthew 21:12–17 — Jesus Cleanses the Temple

Jesus drives the buyers and sellers out of the temple, calling it a house of prayer made a den of thieves. He heals the blind and lame there and defends the children’s praise from Psalm 8.

Matthew 21:18–22 — The Withered Fig Tree and the Prayer of Faith

Finding a fig tree with leaves but no fruit, Jesus speaks, and it withers immediately. He tells His amazed disciples that whatever they ask in prayer, believing, they will receive, a promise to faith that trusts the Father and yields to His will, as Jesus Himself prays in Gethsemane (26:39).

Matthew 21:23–27 — The Leaders Question Jesus’ Authority

The chief priests and elders demand to know Jesus’ authority. He asks whether John’s baptism was from heaven or from men, and when they refuse to answer, He declines to tell them.

Matthew 21:28–32 — The Parable of the Two Sons

One son refuses his father’s command but later repents and works, while the other promises to go and never does. Jesus tells the leaders that tax collectors and harlots who believed John are entering the kingdom before them.

Matthew 21:33–46 — The Parable of the Wicked Vinedressers

Vinedressers beat and kill the owner’s servants and finally his son. Jesus applies Psalm 118:22 to Himself as the rejected stone and warns that the kingdom will be taken from these leaders and given to a fruit-bearing people.

Why This Chapter Matters

Matthew 21 shows the kind of King Jesus is. He does not arrive on a warhorse, yet He acts with full authority in God’s house. The same visit that overturns tables also brings healing to the blind and lame, and He receives praise that belongs to God.

The chapter also shows that God looks for fruit. Leaves without figs, words without obedience, and stewardship without submission all fall under His judgment. The people who bear the kingdom’s fruit are those gathered around Jesus, beginning in Israel and welcoming the nations. The warning falls on the chief priests and Pharisees who reject the Son, not on Jewish people as such.

Key Themes to Watch For

The humble King. Jesus fulfills Zechariah’s promise of a lowly King (21:5).

God’s house. Jesus restores the temple’s purpose as a house of prayer and healing (21:12–14).

Fruit, not leaves. The fig tree pictures religion without the fruit God seeks (21:19).

Saying versus doing. The two sons show that real response becomes obedience (21:28–31).

The rejected Son. The cornerstone the builders rejected is central to God’s purpose (21:37–42).

Key Words & Concepts

Hosanna (21:9). A Hebrew cry meaning “save now,” used in praise. The crowds draw it from Psalm 118:25–26.

Den of thieves (21:13). Jeremiah’s phrase for a temple used as a hiding place by people who did wrong. Jesus applies it to the trade in the temple courts.

Fig tree (21:19). A tree whose leaves promised fruit. Its withering pictures the judgment on outward show without fruit.

Vinedressers (21:33). Tenants entrusted with the owner’s vineyard, recalling Isaiah 5. They represent the leaders responsible for God’s people.

Chief cornerstone (21:42). The foundational stone of a building. The stone the builders rejected becomes the most important one.
