Verse Explanation

The kingdom is like a royal wedding feast. The king represents God. The son represents Jesus. A wedding feast was a picture of the joy of God’s kingdom (Isaiah 25:6).

The invited guests represent Israel, who had long been invited through the prophets.

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

The questioners answer, “Caesar’s,” and Jesus says, “Render therefore to Caesar the things that are Caesar’s, and to God the things that are God’s.” Civic obligations are real, but they stand beneath God’s greater claim on people, who bear His image.

“They said to Him, ‘Caesar’s.”

They answer that it is Caesar’s.

“And He said to them, ‘Render therefore to Caesar the things that are Caesar’s,”

“and to God the things that are God’s.”

But God has a greater claim. The coin bears Caesar’s image. Human beings bear God’s image (Genesis 1:27). Caesar may receive his coin. God must receive the whole person.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “render” means to give back, to pay what is owed.
