Matthew 22 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Matthew 22 continues Jesus’ confrontation with Jerusalem’s leaders as He tells the parable of the wedding feast and then answers three testing questions: about paying taxes to Caesar, about marriage in the resurrection, and about the great commandment in the law. Jesus then asks the Pharisees how the Christ can be both David’s Son and David’s Lord, and after that no one dares to question Him any more.

Chapter Sections

1. Matthew 22:1–14 — The Parable of the Wedding Feast

Jesus likens the kingdom of heaven to a king whose invited guests refuse to come to his son’s wedding, some making light of it and others killing his servants, so that the king sends his armies and then has his servants gather everyone they can find from the highways. When the king finds a guest without a wedding garment, he has him cast into outer darkness, “For many are called, but few are chosen” (Matthew 22:14).

2. Matthew 22:15–22 — Paying Taxes to Caesar

The Pharisees send their disciples with the Herodians to ask whether it is lawful to pay taxes to Caesar, hoping to entangle Jesus in His talk. Jesus asks whose image and inscription is on the denarius and answers, “Render therefore to Caesar the things that are Caesar’s, and to God the things that are God’s” (Matthew 22:21), and they marvel and go away.

3. Matthew 22:23–33 — The Sadducees and the Resurrection

The Sadducees, who say there is no resurrection, ask whose wife a woman married to seven brothers will be in the resurrection. Jesus answers that they are mistaken, not knowing the Scriptures nor the power of God, for in the resurrection people neither marry nor are given in marriage, and God, who said, “I am the God of Abraham,” is “not the God of the dead, but of the living” (Matthew 22:32).

4. Matthew 22:34–40 — The Great Commandment

A lawyer among the Pharisees asks Jesus which is the great commandment in the law, and Jesus answers that the first and great commandment is to love the LORD your God with all your heart, soul, and mind, and the second is to love your neighbor as yourself. He declares, “On these two commandments hang all the Law and the Prophets” (Matthew 22:40).

5. Matthew 22:41–46 — David’s Son and David’s Lord

Jesus asks the gathered Pharisees whose Son the Christ is, and they answer, “The Son of David” (Matthew 22:42). He then asks how David, speaking in the Spirit, can call Him Lord in the psalm, and no one is able to answer Him a word, nor does anyone dare to question Him from that day on.
