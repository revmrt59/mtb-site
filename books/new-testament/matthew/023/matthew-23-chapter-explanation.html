Chapter Study

Matthew 23

Verse-by-Verse
Matthew 23:1
Matthew 23:1
NKJV

Matthew 23:1 Then Jesus spoke to the multitudes and to His disciples,(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 23:1 Then Jesus said to the crowds and to his disciples, (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus speaks to the crowds and to His disciples. Having silenced His opponents, He now warns His hearers about the religious leaders.

Verse Explanation

Jesus speaks to the crowds and to His disciples. Having silenced His opponents, He now warns His hearers about the religious leaders.

“Then Jesus spoke to the multitudes and to His disciples,”

After silencing His opponents (22:46), Jesus speaks to the crowds and His disciples about the scribes and Pharisees. This is His final public teaching in Matthew.

Study Notes

Chapter 23 forms a bridge between the controversies of chapters 21–22 and the teaching about the end of the age in chapters 24–25.

Matthew 23:2
Matthew 23:2
NKJV

Matthew 23:2 saying: "The scribes and the Pharisees sit in Moses' seat.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 23:2 “The teachers of religious law and the Pharisees are the official interpreters of the law of Moses. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus says that the scribes and Pharisees sit in Moses’ seat. They hold the recognized role of teaching God’s law to the people.

Verse Explanation

Jesus says that the scribes and Pharisees sit in Moses’ seat. They hold the recognized role of teaching God’s law to the people.

“saying: ‘The scribes and the Pharisees sit in Moses’ seat.”

This image describes holding the position of teaching the law Moses gave. The scribes and Pharisees were recognized teachers of God’s law.

Study Notes

Some ancient synagogues had a stone seat for the teacher, called the seat of Moses.

Matthew 23:3
Matthew 23:3
NKJV

Matthew 23:3 Therefore whatever they tell you to observe, that observe and do, but do not do according to their works; for they say, and do not do.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 23:3 So practice and obey whatever they tell you, but don’t follow their example. For they don’t practice what they teach. (NLT)

Main Idea

The people are to observe and do whatever the scribes and Pharisees teach from Moses’ seat, but not to follow their example, because they do not practice what they say. Jesus distinguishes the law they teach from the lives they live.

Verse Explanation

The people are to observe and do whatever the scribes and Pharisees teach from Moses’ seat, but not to follow their example, because they do not practice what they say. Jesus distinguishes the law they teach from the lives they live.

“Therefore whatever they tell you to observe, that observe and do,”

When they teach what God’s law says, the people should obey it. Jesus does not mean every tradition they teach, since He has rejected some (15:3–9).

“but do not do according to their works; for they say, and do not do.”

But the people must not follow their example. They teach one thing and do another.

Study Notes

Paul makes the same charge in Romans 2:21–23.

Matthew 23:4
NKJV

NLT

Matthew 23:4 They crush people with unbearable religious demands and never lift a finger to ease the burden. (NLT)

Main Idea

The scribes and Pharisees tie up heavy burdens that are hard to carry and put them on people’s shoulders, but they will not lift a finger to help. They impose demands on others without compassion or help.

Verse Explanation

The scribes and Pharisees tie up heavy burdens that are hard to carry and put them on people’s shoulders, but they will not lift a finger to help. They impose demands on others without compassion or help.

“For they bind heavy burdens, hard to bear, and lay them on men’s shoulders;”

They load people with heavy rules. Their traditions added burdens to God’s law.

“but they themselves will not move them with one of their fingers.”

They do nothing to help people carry the load. Jesus offers a different yoke, one that is easy and light (11:28–30).

Study Notes

Peter called such burdens a yoke which neither our fathers nor we were able to bear (Acts 15:10).

Matthew 23:5
Matthew 23:5
NKJV

Matthew 23:5 But all their works they do to be seen by men. They make their phylacteries broad and enlarge the borders of their garments.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 23:5 “Everything they do is for show. On their arms they wear extra wide prayer boxes with Scripture verses inside, and they wear robes with extra long tassels. (NLT)

Main Idea

The scribes and Pharisees do all their works to be seen by people, making their phylacteries broad and the borders of their garments long. Phylacteries were small cases holding passages of Scripture worn on the forehead and arm, and these leaders enlarge them to make their devotion conspicuous.

Verse Explanation

The scribes and Pharisees do all their works to be seen by people, making their phylacteries broad and the borders of their garments long. Phylacteries were small cases holding passages of Scripture worn on the forehead and arm, and these leaders enlarge them to make their devotion conspicuous.

“But all their works they do to be seen by men.”

Their religious acts are done for show. They want people to notice them.

“They make their phylacteries broad and enlarge the borders of their garments.”

“Phylacteries” were small boxes containing Scripture verses, worn on the forehead and arm during prayer (Deuteronomy 6:8). The “borders” were tassels on their garments (Numbers 15:38–39). The Pharisees made theirs extra large to look especially devout.

Study Notes

Jesus warned against practicing righteousness to be seen by people in 6:1–18.

Matthew 23:8

Main Idea

Jesus’ disciples are not to be called “Rabbi,” for they have one Teacher, the Christ, and they are all brothers. In His community no one stands above others as a master teacher; all are brothers under Christ.

Verse Explanation

Jesus’ disciples are not to be called “Rabbi,” for they have one Teacher, the Christ, and they are all brothers. In His community no one stands above others as a master teacher; all are brothers under Christ.

“But you, do not be called ‘Rabbi’;”

Jesus tells His disciples not to seek such titles.

“for One is your Teacher, the Christ, and you are all brethren.”

They have one Teacher, the Christ. Everyone else is a brother. No one stands above the others.

Jesus is not forbidding every title. He is forbidding the use of titles to claim status and spiritual authority that belongs to Him.

Study Notes

Some manuscripts do not include the words the Christ in this verse.

“for One is your Father, He who is in heaven.”

God alone is their Father in this sense.

Paul calls himself a father to the Corinthians in the gospel (1 Corinthians 4:15). Jesus’ concern is status that rivals God, not every use of the word.

Matthew 23:13–22 — Woes for Shutting Out Others and Twisting Oaths

Matthew 23:13
Matthew 23:13
NKJV

Matthew 23:13 "But woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you shut up the kingdom of heaven against men; for you neither go in yourselves, nor do you allow those who are entering to go in.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 23:13 “What sorrow awaits you teachers of religious law and you Pharisees. Hypocrites! For you shut the door of the Kingdom of Heaven in people’s faces. You won’t go in yourselves, and you don’t let others enter either. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus pronounces woe on the scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, because they shut the kingdom of heaven against people, neither entering themselves nor letting others enter. Their teaching and example keep people from the kingdom they claim to guard.

Verse Explanation

Jesus pronounces woe on the scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, because they shut the kingdom of heaven against people, neither entering themselves nor letting others enter. Their teaching and example keep people from the kingdom they claim to guard.

“But woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites!”

Jesus pronounces the first of a series of woes. A “woe” is a cry of grief and a warning of judgment. He calls them “hypocrites,” actors wearing masks.

“For you shut up the kingdom of heaven against men;”

They lock the door of the kingdom. Their teaching keeps people away from Jesus.

“for you neither go in yourselves, nor do you allow those who are entering to go in.”

They will not enter, and they block others who want to.

Study Notes

The woes contrast with the blessings that open the Sermon on the Mount (5:3–12).

Matthew 23:14
Matthew 23:14
NKJV

Matthew 23:14 Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you devour widows' houses, and for a pretense make long prayers. Therefore you will receive greater condemnation.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 23:14

Main Idea

Jesus pronounces woe on the scribes and Pharisees because they devour widows’ houses and make long prayers for show, and they will receive greater condemnation. They exploit the most vulnerable while putting on a display of devotion, and their hypocrisy deepens their guilt.

Verse Explanation

Jesus pronounces woe on the scribes and Pharisees because they devour widows’ houses and make long prayers for show, and they will receive greater condemnation. They exploit the most vulnerable while putting on a display of devotion, and their hypocrisy deepens their guilt.

“Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites!”

Jesus pronounces another woe.

“For you devour widows’ houses, and for a pretense make long prayers.”

They take advantage of widows, the most vulnerable people. They make long prayers to look holy.

“Therefore you will receive greater condemnation.”

Their judgment will be heavier because they used religion to cover their greed.

Study Notes

Many manuscripts do not include this verse. Mark 12:40 and Luke 20:47 contain the same saying.

Woe to the scribes and Pharisees, blind guides who teach that swearing by the temple means nothing, but swearing by the temple’s gold is binding. They invent distinctions to escape the obligations of their oaths.

Jesus taught His disciples not to swear at all, but to let their yes mean yes (5:33–37).

Woe to the scribes and Pharisees, because they tithe mint, anise, and cummin but neglect the weightier matters of the law: justice, mercy, and faith, which they should have practiced without neglecting the tithe. They are careful about small duties while ignoring what matters most to God, and Jesus requires both in their proper order.

“Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites!”

Another woe.

“For you pay tithe of mint and anise and cummin,”

They carefully give a tenth of even the smallest garden herbs.

“and have neglected the weightier matters of the law: justice and mercy and faith.”

But they neglect what matters most. God cares more about fairness, compassion, and faithfulness than about precise accounting of herbs.

“These you ought to have done, without leaving the others undone.”

Jesus does not condemn their tithing. They should practice justice, mercy, and faithfulness while continuing to tithe. Both have their place, but the weightier matters come first.

Study Notes

Micah 6:8 asks what the LORD requires: to do justly, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God.

Main Idea

So on these leaders will come all the righteous blood shed on earth, from the blood of righteous Abel to the blood of Zechariah son of Berechiah, murdered between the temple and the altar. The sweep from the first murder in Scripture to Zechariah gathers the long history of murdered righteous ones to its climax in their guilt.

Verse Explanation

So on these leaders will come all the righteous blood shed on earth, from the blood of righteous Abel to the blood of Zechariah son of Berechiah, murdered between the temple and the altar. The sweep from the first murder in Scripture to Zechariah gathers the long history of murdered righteous ones to its climax in their guilt.

“that on you may come all the righteous blood shed on the earth,”

The guilt for all the righteous blood ever shed will come on them.

“from the blood of righteous Abel to the blood of Zechariah, son of Berechiah, whom you murdered between the temple and the altar.”

Abel was the first martyr in Scripture (Genesis 4:8). “Zechariah” was killed in the temple court (2 Chronicles 24:20–22). Chronicles is the last book in the Hebrew arrangement of the Old Testament. From Abel to Zechariah covers all the martyrs from the first book to the last.

Study Notes

2 Chronicles 24:20 calls Zechariah the son of Jehoiada. Jehoiada may have been his grandfather, or Zechariah may have had two names associated with his family. The prophet Zechariah was also called the son of Berechiah (Zechariah 1:1).

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

Jesus laments, “O Jerusalem, Jerusalem,” the city that kills the prophets and stones those sent to it, saying how often He wanted to gather her children as a hen gathers her chicks under her wings, but they were not willing. His judgment is spoken with deep compassion and longing to protect, yet the city refused Him.

“O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, the one who kills the prophets and stones those who are sent to her!”

Jesus cries out over Jerusalem. The city has a long history of rejecting God’s messengers.

“How often I wanted to gather your children together, as a hen gathers her chicks under her wings,”

Jesus longed to protect them. A hen spreads her wings over her chicks to shelter them from danger. That is what Jesus wanted to do for His people.

“but you were not willing!”

They refused. Jesus’ judgment comes with deep grief.

Psalm 91:4 says, “He shall cover you with His feathers, And under His wings you shall take refuge.”
