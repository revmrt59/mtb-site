Matthew 23 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Matthew 23 records Jesus’ public warning to the multitudes and His disciples about the scribes and Pharisees, who sit in Moses’ seat but do not practice what they teach, and His call for His disciples to live as brothers under one Teacher, where the greatest is the servant. Jesus then pronounces a series of woes on the scribes and Pharisees for hypocrisy and ends with a lament over Jerusalem, whose house is left to it desolate.

Chapter Sections

1. Matthew 23:1–12 — Teachers Who Do Not Practice What They Teach

Jesus tells the multitudes and His disciples to observe what the scribes and Pharisees teach from Moses’ seat but not to follow their works, for they say and do not do, lay heavy burdens on others, and do their works to be seen by men. His disciples are not to seek titles such as Rabbi, father, or teacher, for they have one Teacher and one Father, and “he who is greatest among you shall be your servant” (Matthew 23:11).

2. Matthew 23:13–22 — Woes for Shutting Out Others and Twisting Oaths

Jesus pronounces woes on the scribes and Pharisees, calling them hypocrites, for shutting up the kingdom of heaven against those who are entering, devouring widows’ houses while making long prayers, and making a proselyte twice as much a son of hell as themselves. He calls them blind guides for teaching that oaths by the temple or the altar mean nothing while oaths by its gold or gift are binding, and He declares that whoever swears by these swears by God who dwells in the temple and sits on the throne of heaven.

3. Matthew 23:23–28 — Woes for Neglecting the Weightier Matters and Inward Corruption

Jesus condemns the scribes and Pharisees for tithing mint, anise, and cummin while neglecting “the weightier matters of the law: justice and mercy and faith” (Matthew 23:23), straining out a gnat and swallowing a camel. He compares them to a cup cleansed only on the outside and to whitewashed tombs, beautiful outwardly but full of dead men’s bones, for outwardly they appear righteous but inside are full of hypocrisy and lawlessness.

4. Matthew 23:29–36 — Hypocritical Honor of the Prophets and Judgment on This Generation

Jesus condemns the scribes and Pharisees for building the tombs of the prophets while claiming they would not have shared in their fathers’ guilt, telling them they are witnesses that they are sons of those who murdered the prophets. He declares that He will send them prophets, wise men, and scribes whom they will kill and persecute, so that all the righteous blood shed on the earth, from Abel to Zechariah, will come upon this generation.

5. Matthew 23:37–39 — Jesus Laments over Jerusalem

Jesus cries, “O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, the one who kills the prophets and stones those who are sent to her!” (Matthew 23:37), for He longed to gather her children as a hen gathers her chicks, but they were not willing. He declares, “See! Your house is left to you desolate” (Matthew 23:38), and says they will not see Him again until they say, “Blessed is He who comes in the name of the LORD!” (Matthew 23:39).
