Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 23
Main Idea:

Jesus warns the crowds and His disciples against scribes and Pharisees who teach but do not practice, pronounces a series of woes on their hypocrisy, and then laments over Jerusalem, which would not let Him gather her children.

Setting:

Matthew 23 closes the fifth movement, The King Confronts Jerusalem. After silencing His opponents in chapter 22, Jesus speaks publicly in the temple during His final week.

Chapter Outline:

  • Matthew 23:1–12 — Teachers Who Do Not Practice What They Teach

  • Matthew 23:13–22 — Woes for Shutting Out Others and Twisting Oaths

  • Matthew 23:23–28 — Woes for Neglecting the Weightier Matters and Inward Corruption

  • Matthew 23:29–36 — Hypocritical Honor of the Prophets and Judgment on This Generation

  • Matthew 23:37–39 — Jesus Laments over Jerusalem

Key Verse:

“O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, the one who kills the prophets and stones those who are sent to her! How often I wanted to gather your children together, as a hen gathers her chicks under her wings, but you were not willing!” (Matthew 23:37)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Jesus acknowledges that the scribes and Pharisees sit in Moses’ seat, teaching the Law, but warns that they do not practice what they say. They tie heavy burdens on others and will not lift a finger to help. Among Jesus’ disciples it must be different. They have one Teacher, one Father, and one Master, and the greatest among them will be a servant.

Again and again He says, “Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites!” (23:13), and once He calls them “blind guides” (23:16). They shut people out of the kingdom, twist oaths to escape their word, tithe tiny herbs while neglecting “justice and mercy and faith” (23:23), and clean the outside of the cup while the inside is full of greed. They build tombs for the prophets while resisting God’s messengers in their own day, and this generation will bear the accountability for that long history of bloodshed. Yet the chapter does not end with anger. Jesus cries out over the city He loves: “How often I wanted to gather your children together, as a hen gathers her chicks under her wings, but you were not willing!” (23:37). Their house is left desolate until they welcome Him in the name of the Lord.

Chapter Sections

Matthew 23:1–12 — Teachers Who Do Not Practice What They Teach

Jesus tells the people to observe what the scribes and Pharisees teach from Moses’ seat but not to imitate their deeds. They seek honor and titles, but Jesus’ disciples are brothers under one Teacher, and the greatest must serve.

Matthew 23:13–22 — Woes for Shutting Out Others and Twisting Oaths

Jesus pronounces woes on leaders who shut people out of the kingdom and make converts worse than themselves. He exposes their evasive oath rules, which treat swearing by the temple or altar as less binding than swearing by gold or gifts.

Matthew 23:23–28 — Woes for Neglecting the Weightier Matters and Inward Corruption

Jesus rebukes the leaders for tithing herbs while neglecting justice, mercy, and faith, and He tells them to do these weightier things without leaving the tithe undone. His picture of straining out a gnat while swallowing a camel shows how distorted their priorities have become. Like whitewashed tombs, they look clean outside while full of hypocrisy and lawlessness within.

Matthew 23:29–36 — Hypocritical Honor of the Prophets and Judgment on This Generation

The leaders honor the tombs of the prophets while sharing their fathers’ spirit. Jesus foretells that they will persecute the messengers He sends and that the guilt for righteous blood from Abel to Zechariah will come upon this generation.

Matthew 23:37–39 — Jesus Laments over Jerusalem

Jesus laments over Jerusalem, which kills the prophets and would not let Him gather her children. Her house is left desolate, and she will not see Him until she says, “Blessed is He who comes in the name of the LORD!” (23:39).

Why This Chapter Matters

Matthew 23 searches every religious person, including the church. Jesus does not attack teaching or devotion. He exposes the gap between what is taught and what is lived, the hunger for honor, and the habit of majoring on small things while ignoring justice, mercy, and faith. His words are a specific confrontation of these leaders, not a verdict on all Jewish people.

The chapter also reveals the heart behind the King’s judgment. The One who pronounces woes is the One who longed to gather Jerusalem’s children under His wings. His severity and His compassion belong together, and the refusal of that care is what leaves the house desolate.

Key Themes to Watch For

Teaching without practice. The leaders say and do not do (23:3).

Servant greatness. Jesus’ disciples have one Teacher and serve one another (23:8–12).

Weightier matters. Justice, mercy, and faith must not be neglected (23:23).

Inside and outside. Outward religion can hide inward corruption (23:25–28).

Lament and judgment. Jesus grieves over the city He must judge (23:37–39).

Key Words & Concepts

Moses’ seat (23:2). The place of authority for teaching the Law in the synagogue.

Phylacteries (23:5). Small boxes containing Scripture verses worn on the arm and forehead. The leaders enlarged them to look devout.

Hypocrites (23:13). People whose outward religion hides a different heart. Jesus repeats the word throughout the woes.

Whitewashed tombs (23:27). Graves painted white so people would not touch them by accident. They looked clean outside and held death within.

Abel to Zechariah (23:35). The first and last martyrs recorded in the Hebrew Bible, which ends with 2 Chronicles. Together they span the whole history of God’s rejected messengers.

Desolate (23:38). Abandoned and left empty, preparing for Jesus’ prediction of the temple’s destruction in 24:2.
