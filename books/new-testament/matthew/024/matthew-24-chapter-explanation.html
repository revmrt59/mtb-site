Matthew 24:3
Matthew 24:3
NKJV

Matthew 24:3 Now as He sat on the Mount of Olives, the disciples came to Him privately, saying, "Tell us, when will these things be? And what will be the sign of Your coming, and of the end of the age?"(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 24:3 Later, Jesus sat on the Mount of Olives. His disciples came to him privately and said, “Tell us, when will all this happen? What sign will signal your return and the end of the world? ” (NLT)

Main Idea

As Jesus sits on the Mount of Olives, the disciples ask privately when these things will happen and what will be the sign of His coming and of the end of the age. They connect the temple’s fall with His coming and the end, and Jesus’ answer addresses both horizons.

Verse Explanation

As Jesus sits on the Mount of Olives, the disciples ask privately when these things will happen and what will be the sign of His coming and of the end of the age. They connect the temple’s fall with His coming and the end, and Jesus’ answer addresses both horizons.

“Now as He sat on the Mount of Olives,”

Jesus sits on the “Mount of Olives,” which overlooks the temple from the east.

“the disciples came to Him privately, saying, ‘Tell us, when will these things be?”

The disciples ask when the temple will be destroyed.

“And what will be the sign of Your coming, and of the end of the age?”

They also ask about His “coming” and “the end of the age.” They seem to connect the temple’s fall with the end of the world.

Jesus’ answer moves on two horizons. The near horizon is the destruction of Jerusalem in AD 70. The far horizon is His return in glory at the end of the age. The first crisis becomes a pattern that repeats through history and intensifies before His return.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “coming” is parousia, meaning arrival or presence. It was used of a king’s official visit.

Matthew 24:15
NKJV

NLT

Matthew 24:15 “The day is coming when you will see what Daniel the prophet spoke about — the sacrilegious object that causes desecration standing in the Holy Place.” (Reader, pay attention!) (NLT)

Main Idea

When the disciples see “the abomination of desolation” spoken of by Daniel standing in the holy place, readers must understand. Verse Explanation

When the disciples see “the abomination of desolation” spoken of by Daniel standing in the holy place, readers must understand. The prophecy was fulfilled first in the desecration and Roman siege of Jerusalem in AD 70, and that crisis also forms a pattern pointing to a final desecration before Christ returns.

“Therefore when you see the ‘abomination of desolation,’ spoken of by Daniel the prophet,”

Jesus refers to “the abomination of desolation” from Daniel (Daniel 9:27; 11:31; 12:11). It describes something detestable that desecrates God’s holy place and leaves it desolate.

“standing in the holy place”

The desecration happens in the temple area.

“(whoever reads, let him understand),”

Matthew adds a note to his readers to pay careful attention.

This prophecy was fulfilled in the first century when Roman armies surrounded and desecrated Jerusalem in AD 70. Luke 21:20 says, “when you see Jerusalem surrounded by armies, then know that its desolation is near.” It also forms a pattern that points to a final desecration before Christ’s return (2 Thessalonians 2:3–4).

Study Notes

In 167 BC the Syrian king Antiochus Epiphanes set up a pagan altar in the temple and sacrificed a pig. Many Jews saw this as a fulfillment of Daniel’s words.
