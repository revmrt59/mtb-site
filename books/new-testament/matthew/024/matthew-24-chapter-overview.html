Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 24
Main Idea:

Jesus foretells the temple’s destruction and, answering His disciples’ questions, warns of deception, persecution, and great tribulation, promises His glorious return as the Son of Man, and calls His servants to watchful faithfulness because no one knows the day or the hour.

Setting:

Matthew 24 opens the sixth movement and the fifth great discourse (24–25). Jesus leaves the temple and teaches His disciples privately on the Mount of Olives.

Chapter Outline:

  • Matthew 24:1–2 — Jesus Foretells the Temple’s Destruction

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The disciples admire the temple buildings, and Jesus tells them not one stone will be left on another. They ask when this will happen and what will be the sign of His coming and the end of the age. Jesus answers on two horizons. Near at hand lies a real Judean crisis: false christs, wars, persecution, love growing cold, the abomination of desolation, and an urgent command for those in Judea to flee. Jerusalem’s siege and the temple’s fall in AD 70 fulfill that warning, and “this generation” (24:34) saw these things. That first-century crisis also sets a pattern that recurs through the age and intensifies into a final tribulation before Christ returns.

Beyond it stands the coming of the Son of Man on the clouds with power and great glory, as unmistakable as lightning, gathering His elect from the four winds. Heaven and earth will pass away, but His words will not. Yet the day and hour belong to the Father alone. So the chapter ends with a household servant. The faithful one feeds those in his care, and the evil one uses the master’s delay to abuse others. Readiness shows in how we live while He seems away.

Chapter Sections

Matthew 24:1–2 — Jesus Foretells the Temple’s Destruction

As the disciples point out the temple buildings, Jesus declares that not one stone will be left upon another.

Matthew 24:3–14 — The Beginning of Sorrows and the Gospel to All Nations

On the Mount of Olives Jesus warns of false christs, wars, famines, and earthquakes, which are only the beginning of sorrows. His followers will be hated and persecuted, but whoever endures to the end will be saved, and the gospel of the kingdom will be preached to all nations before the end comes.

Matthew 24:15–28 — The Abomination of Desolation and the Great Tribulation

When the abomination of desolation stands in the holy place, those in Judea must flee at once, for the tribulation will be great. Jesus warns against false christs and false prophets, because His coming will be as visible as lightning.

Matthew 24:29–31 — The Coming of the Son of Man

After the tribulation, the heavens will be shaken, the sign of the Son of Man will appear, and all the tribes of the earth will see Him coming on the clouds with power and great glory. He will send His angels to gather His elect.

Matthew 24:32–35 — The Lesson of the Fig Tree

As a fig tree’s leaves show that summer is near, the signs Jesus has described will show that His coming is near. This generation will not pass away until all these things take place, and His words will never pass away.

Matthew 24:36–44 — No One Knows the Day or the Hour

No one but the Father knows the day and hour. As in the days of Noah, people will be unprepared, one will be taken and another left, and so disciples must watch and be ready.

Matthew 24:45–51 — The Faithful Servant and the Evil Servant

The faithful and wise servant feeds his master’s household and will be set over all his goods. The evil servant who abuses others during the delay will be cut in two and given his portion with the hypocrites.

Why This Chapter Matters

Matthew 24 gives Jesus’ people both a sober warning and a sure hope. They should not be surprised by deception, persecution, and distress, and they should not be shaken by every claim that the Christ has appeared. The gospel will go to all nations, and the Son of Man will return in glory.

The chapter also turns curiosity about timing into faithfulness. Jesus forbids calculating the day and hour and calls His servants to watch. Watching means faithful care for the people entrusted to us, carried on with endurance through delay and difficulty.

Key Themes to Watch For

The temple’s fall. Jesus’ prediction is fulfilled in Jerusalem’s destruction (24:2, 15–22).

Endurance and witness. Disciples endure persecution while the gospel reaches all nations (24:9–14).

Discernment. False christs and prophets will deceive many (24:4–5, 23–26).

The Son of Man’s return. He will come visibly in power and great glory (24:27, 30–31).

Watchful faithfulness. The unknown hour calls for ready, faithful service (24:42–51).

Key Words & Concepts

Beginning of sorrows (24:8). Literally birth pains. Wars and disasters are early pangs, not the end itself.

Abomination of desolation (24:15). Daniel’s phrase for a desecration of God’s holy place. It was fulfilled in the Roman siege and destruction of Jerusalem and points ahead to a final desecration before Christ’s return (Daniel 9:27; 2 Thessalonians 2:3–4).

Great tribulation (24:21). A time of unequaled distress. Jerusalem’s fall is its first fulfillment, and the pattern intensifies before the Son of Man comes.

This generation (24:34). Jesus’ contemporaries, who saw the events of verses 4–28 culminate in AD 70. The generation that sees the signs converge at the end will see His return.

One taken, the other left (24:40–41). A sudden separation. In verse 39 the flood takes people away in judgment, but in verses 40–41 Matthew uses a different verb, one that can describe receiving someone, as Jesus does in John 14:3. For those two reasons the identity of the one taken is left open. The point is to watch.
