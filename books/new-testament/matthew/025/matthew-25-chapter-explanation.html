Matthew 25:1
Matthew 25:1
NKJV

Matthew 25:1 "Then the kingdom of heaven shall be likened to ten virgins who took their lamps and went out to meet the bridegroom.(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 25:1 “Then the Kingdom of Heaven will be like ten bridesmaids who took their lamps and went to meet the bridegroom. (NLT)

Main Idea

The kingdom of heaven will then be like ten virgins who took their lamps and went out to meet the bridegroom. Continuing His call to readiness, Jesus pictures people professing to await Him, the bridegroom, at His coming.

Verse Explanation

The kingdom of heaven will then be like ten virgins who took their lamps and went out to meet the bridegroom. Continuing His call to readiness, Jesus pictures people professing to await Him, the bridegroom, at His coming.

“Then the kingdom of heaven shall be likened to ten virgins”

Jesus continues teaching about His return. “Then” connects this parable to the coming of the Son of Man (24:44–51). The kingdom at that time will be like ten young women at a wedding.

“who took their lamps and went out to meet the bridegroom.”

The young women are bridesmaids. They take their lamps and go out to meet “the bridegroom.” In Jewish weddings, the groom came at night to bring his bride to his home, and the wedding party went out with lamps to meet him and escort him to the feast.

The bridegroom is Christ. The virgins represent people who say they are waiting for Him. The lamps picture their visible life and witness.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “lamps” may refer to torches made of cloth soaked in oil and wrapped around a pole.

Verse Explanation

The master gives one servant five talents, another two, and another one, each according to his ability, and then leaves. A talent was a very large sum of money, and the master entrusts differing amounts matched to each servant’s ability.

Verse Explanation

The King answers that whatever they did for one of the least of these His brethren, they did for Him. Jesus so identifies with His vulnerable disciples and messengers that serving them is serving Him, and this mercy reaches outward to all who are in need.

“And the King will answer and say to them,”

The King explains.

“Assuredly, I say to you, inasmuch as you did it to one of the least of these My brethren, you did it to Me.”

Their kindness to His people was kindness to Him. Jesus calls His disciples His brethren (12:49–50; 28:10). Especially in view are His vulnerable disciples, sent in His name, who depend on the kindness of others (10:40–42). Receiving or neglecting them reveals how people respond to the King Himself.

Deeds of mercy do not earn the kingdom. They show allegiance to the King who identifies with His people. The wider teaching of Scripture also calls believers to show mercy to all people (Galatians 6:10).

Study Notes

Jesus said that whoever receives His disciples receives Him (10:40).
