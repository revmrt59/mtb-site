Matthew 25 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Matthew 25 completes the Olivet Discourse with three pictures of readiness for the King’s return: the wise and foolish virgins waiting for a delayed bridegroom, the servants entrusted with their master’s talents, and the Son of Man coming in His glory to separate all the nations. In the final scene the King counts what was done or not done to the least of His brethren as done or not done to Him, and the discourse ends with everlasting punishment and eternal life.

Chapter Sections

1. Matthew 25:1–13 — The Wise and Foolish Virgins

Jesus likens the kingdom of heaven to ten virgins who take their lamps to meet the bridegroom; the five wise take extra oil in their vessels along with their lamps, but the five foolish bring their lamps without any reserve of oil, so when the bridegroom comes at midnight after a delay, their lamps are going out. While the foolish go to buy oil, the wise go in with him to the wedding and the door is shut, and when the foolish come and ask to be let in, he answers, “I do not know you” (Matthew 25:12), and Jesus concludes, “Watch therefore, for you know neither the day nor the hour in which the Son of Man is coming” (Matthew 25:13).

2. Matthew 25:14–30 — The Parable of the Talents

A man traveling to a far country entrusts his goods to his servants, five talents to one, two to another, and one to another, each according to his ability. When he returns after a long time, the two who gained more are welcomed with “Well done, good and faithful servant” (Matthew 25:21), but the servant who hid his talent in the ground is called wicked and lazy, and the unprofitable servant is cast into outer darkness.

3. Matthew 25:31–46 — The Son of Man Judges the Nations

When the Son of Man comes in His glory, all the nations will be gathered before Him, and He will separate them as a shepherd divides his sheep from the goats, inviting those on His right hand to inherit the kingdom prepared for them. The King declares, “inasmuch as you did it to one of the least of these My brethren, you did it to Me” (Matthew 25:40), and those who did not do so “will go away into everlasting punishment, but the righteous into eternal life” (Matthew 25:46).
