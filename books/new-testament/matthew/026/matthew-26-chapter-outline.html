Matthew 26 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Matthew 26 opens the account of Jesus’ passion as He announces that the Son of Man will be delivered up to be crucified at the Passover. The chapter moves from the leaders’ plot, the anointing at Bethany, and Judas’ bargain through the Passover meal, where Jesus gives the bread and cup as His body and His blood of the new covenant, to His prayer in Gethsemane, His betrayal and arrest, His condemnation before the council, and Peter’s threefold denial.

Chapter Sections

1. Matthew 26:1–5 — The Plot to Kill Jesus

When Jesus has finished all these sayings, He tells His disciples, “You know that after two days is the Passover, and the Son of Man will be delivered up to be crucified” (Matthew 26:2). Meanwhile the chief priests, scribes, and elders assemble at the palace of the high priest, Caiaphas, and plot to take Jesus by trickery and kill Him, but not during the feast.

2. Matthew 26:6–13 — A Woman Anoints Jesus at Bethany

At the house of Simon the leper in Bethany, a woman pours very costly fragrant oil on Jesus’ head, and His disciples are indignant at the waste. Jesus defends her, saying that she has done a good work for Him and has done it for His burial, and that wherever this gospel is preached, what she has done will be told as a memorial to her.

3. Matthew 26:14–16 — Judas Agrees to Betray Jesus

Judas Iscariot, one of the twelve, goes to the chief priests and asks what they will give him to deliver Jesus to them. They count out thirty pieces of silver, and from that time he seeks opportunity to betray Him.

4. Matthew 26:17–25 — Jesus Foretells His Betrayal at the Passover

On the first day of the Feast of Unleavened Bread the disciples prepare the Passover as Jesus directs, and as they eat with Him that evening, He says that one of them will betray Him. He declares that the Son of Man goes as it is written of Him, but woe to the man by whom He is betrayed, and when Judas asks, “Rabbi, is it I?” Jesus answers, “You have said it” (Matthew 26:25).

5. Matthew 26:26–29 — The Bread and the Cup of the New Covenant

As they eat, Jesus takes bread, blesses and breaks it, and says, “Take, eat; this is My body” (Matthew 26:26), and He gives them the cup, saying, “this is My blood of the new covenant, which is shed for many for the remission of sins” (Matthew 26:28). He tells them He will not drink of the fruit of the vine again until He drinks it new with them in His Father’s kingdom.

6. Matthew 26:30–35 — Jesus Foretells the Disciples’ Scattering and Peter’s Denial

On the way to the Mount of Olives, Jesus tells His disciples that they will all stumble because of Him that night, as it is written, “I will strike the Shepherd” (Matthew 26:31), but that after He has been raised He will go before them to Galilee. When Peter insists that he will never stumble, Jesus tells him he will deny Him three times before the rooster crows, and all the disciples say they will not deny Him.

7. Matthew 26:36–46 — Jesus Prays in Gethsemane

In Gethsemane Jesus, exceedingly sorrowful, prays first, “O My Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from Me; nevertheless, not as I will, but as You will” (Matthew 26:39), and then prays twice more in submission to the Father’s will. After the first prayer He finds Peter, James, and John sleeping and tells them to watch and pray, and after the second He finds them asleep again; after the third He comes and asks whether they are still sleeping and resting, and tells them, “See, My betrayer is at hand” (Matthew 26:46).

8. Matthew 26:47–56 — Betrayal and Arrest

Judas arrives with a great multitude armed with swords and clubs and betrays Jesus with a kiss, and when one of Jesus’ companions cuts off the ear of the high priest’s servant, Jesus tells him to put his sword in its place. Jesus says that He could ask His Father for more than twelve legions of angels, but that the Scriptures must be fulfilled, and “Then all the disciples forsook Him and fled” (Matthew 26:56).

9. Matthew 26:57–68 — Jesus before Caiaphas and the Council

Jesus is led to Caiaphas, where the council seeks false testimony against Him, and He remains silent until the high priest puts Him under oath to say whether He is the Christ, the Son of God. Jesus answers, “It is as you said” (Matthew 26:64), and declares that they will see the Son of Man sitting at the right hand of the Power and coming on the clouds of heaven, and the council condemns Him as deserving death, spits in His face, and beats Him.

10. Matthew 26:69–75 — Peter Denies Jesus Three Times

In the courtyard of the high priest, Peter denies three times that he knows Jesus, the last time with cursing and swearing. Immediately a rooster crows, Peter remembers Jesus’ word, “Before the rooster crows, you will deny Me three times” (Matthew 26:75), and he goes out and weeps bitterly.
