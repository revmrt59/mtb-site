Matthew 27 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Matthew 27 follows Jesus from the council’s morning decision to Pilate’s judgment seat, where the chief priests and elders persuade the multitude to ask for Barabbas and to demand that Jesus be crucified. The chapter records Judas’ remorse and death, the soldiers’ mockery of the King of the Jews, the crucifixion and Jesus’ cry, “My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?” (Matthew 27:46), the signs that accompany His death, His burial in Joseph’s tomb, and the guard set to secure that tomb.

Chapter Sections

1. Matthew 27:1–2 — Jesus Delivered to Pilate

When morning comes, all the chief priests and elders of the people plot against Jesus to put Him to death. They bind Him, lead Him away, and deliver Him to Pontius Pilate the governor.

2. Matthew 27:3–10 — Judas’ Remorse and the Potter’s Field

Seeing that Jesus has been condemned, Judas is remorseful and brings back the thirty pieces of silver, saying, “I have sinned by betraying innocent blood” (Matthew 27:4), and when the chief priests and elders refuse responsibility, he throws down the silver in the temple and hangs himself. The chief priests use the money to buy the potter’s field as a burial place for strangers, and Matthew says this fulfilled what was spoken by Jeremiah the prophet.

3. Matthew 27:11–14 — Jesus Questioned by Pilate

When the governor asks Jesus, “Are You the King of the Jews?” (Matthew 27:11), Jesus answers, “It is as you say.” But to the accusations of the chief priests and elders He answers nothing, not one word, so that the governor marvels greatly.

4. Matthew 27:15–26 — Barabbas Released and Jesus Condemned

While Pilate sits on the judgment seat, his wife warns him to have nothing to do with “that just Man” (Matthew 27:19), but the multitude, persuaded by the chief priests and elders, asks for Barabbas to be released and cries out that Jesus be crucified. Pilate washes his hands before the multitude, saying, “I am innocent of the blood of this just Person” (Matthew 27:24), and the crowd gathered before him answers, “His blood be on us and on our children” (Matthew 27:25), and Pilate releases Barabbas and delivers Jesus, after scourging Him, to be crucified.

5. Matthew 27:27–31 — The Soldiers Mock the King

The governor’s soldiers gather the whole garrison around Jesus, strip Him, put a scarlet robe on Him and a crown of thorns on His head, and kneel before Him mocking, “Hail, King of the Jews!” (Matthew 27:29). They spit on Him, strike Him on the head with the reed, and lead Him away to be crucified.

6. Matthew 27:32–44 — The Crucifixion

Simon of Cyrene is compelled to carry Jesus’ cross to Golgotha, where the soldiers crucify Him, divide His garments by casting lots, and put over His head the accusation, “THIS IS JESUS THE KING OF THE JEWS” (Matthew 27:37). Those passing by blaspheme Him, the chief priests with the scribes and elders mock Him, saying, “He saved others; Himself He cannot save” (Matthew 27:42), and even the robbers crucified with Him revile Him.

7. Matthew 27:45–50 — The Death of Jesus

From the sixth hour until the ninth hour there is darkness over all the land, and about the ninth hour Jesus cries out with a loud voice, “Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani?” that is, “My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?” (Matthew 27:46). Some think He is calling for Elijah, and Jesus cries out again with a loud voice and yields up His spirit.

8. Matthew 27:51–56 — Signs at His Death and the Centurion’s Confession

The veil of the temple is torn in two from top to bottom, the earth quakes, the rocks are split, and the graves are opened, and many bodies of the saints are raised and, coming out after His resurrection, appear to many in the holy city. The centurion and those with him fear greatly, saying, “Truly this was the Son of God!” (Matthew 27:54), and many women who followed Jesus from Galilee look on from afar.

9. Matthew 27:57–61 — The Burial of Jesus

In the evening Joseph of Arimathea, a rich man who had also become a disciple of Jesus, asks Pilate for the body. He wraps it in a clean linen cloth, lays it in his own new tomb hewn out of the rock, and rolls a large stone against the door, while Mary Magdalene and the other Mary sit opposite the tomb.

10. Matthew 27:62–66 — The Tomb Sealed and Guarded

On the next day the chief priests and Pharisees remind Pilate that Jesus said He would rise after three days, and they ask that the tomb be made secure lest His disciples steal Him away and say He has risen from the dead. Pilate tells them they have a guard, and they make the tomb secure, sealing the stone and setting the guard.
