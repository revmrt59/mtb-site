NLT

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

After the Sabbath, as the first day of the week begins to dawn, Mary Magdalene and the other Mary come to see the tomb. The women who watched Jesus’ burial return at first light on Sunday, after the Sabbath days that followed His crucifixion.

“Now after the Sabbath, as the first day of the week began to dawn,”

The Sabbaths are over. Sunday morning is breaking. Matthew uses the plural Greek word for Sabbaths, reflecting the two Sabbaths that followed Jesus’ Thursday crucifixion: the High Sabbath on Friday and the weekly Sabbath on Saturday.

Jesus rose on Sunday, the day of the Feast of Firstfruits, which began the fifty-day count to Pentecost (Leviticus 23:10–16). Paul calls the risen Christ the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep (1 Corinthians 15:20).

“Mary Magdalene and the other Mary came to see the tomb.”

The two women who watched the burial (27:61) come to see the tomb.

Study Notes

The first day of the week became the day Christians gathered for worship (Acts 20:7; 1 Corinthians 16:2).

Matthew 28:19
Matthew 28:19
NKJV

Matthew 28:19 Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,(NKJV)

NLT

Matthew 28:19 Therefore, go and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jesus commands them to go and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them “in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.” The King sends His people to every nation, fulfilling the promise to Abraham, and those who believe are baptized into the one divine name of the Father, Son, and Spirit, revealed and entered through Jesus Christ, to whom all authority has been given.

Verse Explanation

Jesus commands them to go and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them “in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.” The King sends His people to every nation, fulfilling the promise to Abraham, and those who believe are baptized into the one divine name of the Father, Son, and Spirit, revealed and entered through Jesus Christ, to whom all authority has been given.

“Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations,”

Because Jesus has all authority, He sends His disciples. The main command is to “make disciples,” followers who learn from Jesus and obey Him.

The mission goes to “all the nations.” It is no longer limited to Israel (10:5–6). Israel is included, and the blessing promised to Abraham now reaches all peoples (Genesis 12:3).

“baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,”

New disciples are to be baptized. Baptism expresses faith and surrender to Christ. It identifies the believer with His death, burial, and resurrection (Romans 6:3–7). God works inwardly as believers respond in faith (Colossians 2:11–12). New birth is the work of the Spirit, not of water alone.

The word “name” is singular. Father, Son, and Holy Spirit share one divine name and authority. They are three distinct persons in one God. This triune name is revealed through the Lord Jesus Christ, in whom all the fullness of the Godhead dwells bodily (Colossians 2:9). The apostles baptized in the name of Jesus Christ (Acts 2:38; 19:5).

In application, MTB baptizes believers by immersion with the words: I baptize you into the name of the Lord Jesus Christ for the remission of sins and the circumcision of the heart. This practice honors the triune God revealed in Christ. MTB also recognizes Christian baptisms performed in the triune name. Matthew’s own wording is the triune name given in this verse.

Study Notes

The Greek word translated “make disciples” is a single verb, matheteuo. Go, baptizing, and teaching describe how disciples are made.

The Greek word translated “name” is singular.

“and lo, I am with you always, even to the end of the age.”

Jesus promises His presence “always,” every day, until the end of the age. Matthew began with Jesus as Immanuel, God with us (1:23). It ends with the same promise. The risen King goes with His people as they carry out His mission.

“Amen.”

The Gospel closes with a word of firm affirmation, meaning so be it.
