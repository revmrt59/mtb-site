Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 28
Main Idea:

Jesus rises bodily from the dead, meets the women as they leave the tomb to tell the disciples, and on a mountain in Galilee sends His disciples to make disciples of all nations under His authority, promising to be with them always, while the leaders try to explain the resurrection away.

Setting:

Matthew 28 completes the final movement and the Gospel. It begins at the tomb at dawn on the first day of the week and ends in Galilee.

Chapter Outline:

  • Matthew 28:1–10 — The Empty Tomb and the Risen Jesus

  • Matthew 28:11–15 — The Guards’ Report and the False Story

  • Matthew 28:16–20 — The Great Commission

Key Verse:

“teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and lo, I am with you always, even to the end of the age.’ Amen.” (Matthew 28:20)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The women come at dawn to see the tomb, and God answers the sealed stone and the guard. An angel rolls back the stone and sits on it. “He is not here; for He is risen, as He said” (28:6). As the women run with fear and great joy to tell the disciples, Jesus Himself meets them. They take hold of His feet and worship Him. The Jesus who was crucified is bodily alive, and He calls His frightened, scattered disciples “My brethren” (28:10).

Meanwhile the chief priests pay the soldiers to say that the disciples stole the body while they slept. The Gospel ends on a mountain in Galilee. The eleven worship, though some doubt, and Jesus comes near to them and declares that all authority in heaven and on earth has been given to Him. He sends them to make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the one name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, and teaching them to obey everything He commanded. The Gospel that began with Immanuel, God with us, ends with His promise: “I am with you always, even to the end of the age” (28:20).

Chapter Sections

Matthew 28:1–10 — The Empty Tomb and the Risen Jesus

After the Sabbath, Mary Magdalene and the other Mary come to the tomb, where an angel rolls back the stone and announces that Jesus is risen as He said. As they hurry to tell the disciples, Jesus meets them, they worship Him, and He sends them to tell His brethren to go to Galilee.

Matthew 28:11–15 — The Guards’ Report and the False Story

Some of the guards report to the chief priests what has happened. The leaders pay them to say that the disciples stole the body while they slept, and the story spreads among the Jews.

Matthew 28:16–20 — The Great Commission

The eleven meet Jesus on a mountain in Galilee and worship Him, though some doubt. Jesus declares His authority over heaven and earth, commands them to make disciples of all nations, baptizing and teaching, and promises to be with them always.

Why This Chapter Matters

Matthew 28 shows that the crucified King is alive in His body. The empty tomb, the women’s touch, and their worship stand together, and no bribe or sealed stone can undo what God has done. The resurrection vindicates Jesus, defeats death, and gives His people hope of their own bodily resurrection.

The chapter also defines the church’s mission. The King who has all authority sends His people to all nations, carrying the blessing promised to Abraham from Israel outward to the world. The mission is making disciples who are baptized and who obey Jesus, not only gathering professions or crowds. His presence is the promise that sustains the whole work until the end of the age.

Key Themes to Watch For

The bodily resurrection. The tomb is empty, and the women hold the risen Jesus’ feet (28:6, 9).

Fear and great joy. The news of the resurrection brings both awe and joy (28:8).

Resistance to the truth. The leaders bribe the guards to spread a false story (28:12–15).

All authority. The risen King rules over heaven and earth (28:18).

Mission and presence. Disciples are sent to all nations, and Jesus is with them always (28:19–20).

Key Words & Concepts

After the Sabbath (28:1). Matthew uses a plural word for Sabbath, fitting the High Sabbath of Unleavened Bread and the weekly Sabbath that came between Jesus’ burial and His Sunday resurrection.

My brethren (28:10). Jesus’ name for the disciples who had fled. His welcome restores them.

All authority (28:18). Complete rule over heaven and earth, given to the risen Son and fulfilling Daniel 7:13–14.

Make disciples (28:19). To bring people into following Jesus as learners who obey Him, through going, baptizing, and teaching.

In the name (28:19). The singular name shared by the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. The apostles baptized in the name of Jesus Christ (Acts 2:38), and baptism in Jesus’ name is rightly practiced in that Trinitarian sense, since the triune name is revealed in Him. Neither wording is a test of whether a Christian baptism is valid.
