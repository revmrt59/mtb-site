Philemon Book Outline

Revision 2 • October 23, 2026

Major Movements

I. Greeting and Thanksgiving for Philemon’s Love (Philemon 1:1–7)

Paul, a prisoner of Christ Jesus, writes with Timothy to Philemon, Apphia, Archippus, and the church in Philemon’s house. He thanks God for Philemon’s love and faith toward the Lord Jesus and all the saints, and prays that the sharing of his faith may become effective through acknowledging every good thing that is theirs in Christ Jesus. Philemon’s known ministry of refreshing the hearts of the saints (v. 7) lays the ground for the appeal that follows.

II. The Appeal for Onesimus, a Beloved Brother (Philemon 1:8–16)

Though Paul could be bold in Christ to command, he appeals for love’s sake as Paul the aged and now also a prisoner of Jesus Christ, on behalf of Onesimus, his son begotten in his chains, once unprofitable and now profitable. Paul wished to keep him to minister to him in his chains, but sends him back as his own heart, wanting to do nothing without Philemon’s consent so that his good deed would be voluntary. Perhaps Onesimus departed for a while so that Philemon might receive him forever, no longer as a slave but more than a slave, a beloved brother (v. 16).

III. Receive Him as You Would Me (Philemon 1:17–22)

Paul states the request plainly: if Philemon counts him a partner, he is to receive Onesimus as he would receive Paul. Writing with his own hand, Paul places on his own account whatever wrong or debt there may be, reminds Philemon that he owes Paul even his own self, and asks him to refresh his heart in the Lord. He closes the appeal confident that Philemon will do even more than he says, and asks for a guest room in hope of release through their prayers.

IV. Greetings and Grace (Philemon 1:23–25)

Epaphras, Paul’s fellow prisoner, sends greetings with Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, and Luke, Paul’s fellow laborers. The letter ends with the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ upon the spirit of those who receive it.
