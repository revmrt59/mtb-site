BROTHER - (adelphós, Strong's G80)

Summary:

, The word adelphos primarily refers to a male sibling born of the same parents or parent. In the New Testament, it expands beyond physical family to describe fellow believers in Jesus Christ who share a spiritual family bond through God as their Father. In Philemon 1:1, Paul uses the word to introduce Timothy as "our brother," establishing shared spiritual family ties at the very beginning of a letter focused on reconciliation and Christian brotherhood.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek culture, adelphos described a full or half-brother born from the same mother or father. In Jewish culture, the term was also used more broadly to refer to fellow countrymen, members of the same tribe, or close relatives within the covenant community of Israel.

When the early church formed, believers adopted this family language to describe their new identity in Christ. Followers of Jesus were no longer linked merely by ethnicity, social status, or biological heritage. Through faith in Jesus Christ, they were adopted into God's family, making them true brothers and sisters in the faith.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Adelphos can refer to:

  • A physical male sibling sharing common parentage.

  • A fellow countryman or member of the same nation.

  • A fellow believer or companion in the Christian faith.

In Philemon 1:1, the word is used in this third sense to describe Timothy as a spiritual brother. Timothy was not Paul’s biological brother, but a trusted fellow minister united with him in faith and service to Jesus Christ.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, adelphos transitions smoothly between biological family and spiritual family:

  • It describes physical brothers, such as James and John or Jesus' earthly brothers.

  • It describes physical kin or fellow Israelites, especially when addressing Jewish audiences.

  • It describes believers in Christ, highlighting mutual love, unity, equal standing, and shared responsibility within the church.

The spiritual use of the word demonstrates that Christian fellowship is not a mere organizational membership. It is a genuine family relationship established by God. Believers are called to care for, encourage, warn, and support one another as family members.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of adelphos for fellow believers rests on the truth of adoption and regeneration. God becomes the Father of believers through faith in Jesus, which makes believers spiritual brothers and sisters to one another.

This spiritual family identity reshapes social hierarchies. In the Roman world, social status, wealth, and citizenship defined personal worth and social boundaries. Calling a fellow believer a "brother" leveled those societal barriers under the lordship of Christ. Master and servant, Jew and Gentile, rich and poor shared equal standing as children of God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:1, Paul opens his letter by identifying himself as "a prisoner of Christ Jesus" and referring to Timothy as "our brother." Timothy was present with Paul during his imprisonment and joined him in greeting Philemon.

Using the word "brother" here is contextually significant:

  • It includes Timothy in the opening greeting, showing unity between Christian leaders.

  • It establishes the family atmosphere that governs the entire letter. Paul will soon ask Philemon to welcome back Onesimus—a runaway slave—no longer merely as a bondservant, but as a beloved "brother" in the Lord.

  • By introducing Timothy as "our brother," Paul gently reminds Philemon of their common family bond in Christ before raising a delicate request regarding Onesimus.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 15:27 – "And he said to him, 'Your brother has come, and because he has received him safe and sound, your father has killed the fatted calf.'"

  • 2 Corinthians 12:18 – "I urged Titus, and sent our brother with him. Did Titus take advantage of you? Did we not walk in the same spirit? Did we not walk in the same steps?"

  • Hebrews 2:11 – "For both He who sanctifies and those who are being sanctified are all of one, for which cause He is not ashamed to call them brethren."

  • 1 John 2:9 – "He who says he is in the light, and hates his brother, is in darkness until now."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The word adelphos sets the foundation for the central argument of the letter to Philemon. While Paul uses it in verse 1 to identify Timothy, the reality of spiritual brotherhood becomes the main focus when Paul appeals for Onesimus. The greeting introduces the spiritual reality that governs how Christian relationships must function when grievances arise.
