LABORER - (synergós, Strong's G4904)

Summary:

The word sunergos refers to a co-worker, partner, or fellow laborer who works alongside another person to accomplish a shared task. In Philemon 1:1, Paul calls Philemon his "fellow laborer" to affirm Philemon's active role in advancing the gospel. By using this word, Paul highlights their shared commitment to Christ and establishes a relationship of mutual respect before addressing a delicate personal matter.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The letter to Philemon was written by the apostle Paul while he was imprisoned, likely in Rome. Philemon was a Christian residing in Colosse who hosted a local church gathering in his home. Onesimus, a bondservant belonging to Philemon, had run away, encountered Paul, and come to faith in Jesus Christ. Paul wrote this short letter to ask Philemon to receive Onesimus back, no longer merely as a slave, but as a beloved brother in the Lord.

Right at the beginning of this letter, Paul uses sunergos to describe Philemon. In the ancient Greco-Roman world, social structures were rigid and hierarchical. Masters commanded, and servants obeyed. Yet within the Christian community, Paul introduced a totally different way of relating to one another. Calling a local church leader a "fellow laborer" emphasized that gospel ministry was a team effort carried out by brothers and sisters working side by side.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word sunergos can refer to a helper, partner, co-worker, or fellow laborer. It is formed by joining two basic Greek concepts: sun, which means "with" or "together," and ergon, which means "work" or "deed." Therefore, a sunergos is someone who joins their effort with someone else to accomplish a shared task.

The word itself simply means a worker alongside another. By itself, it does not specify what kind of work is being done. The context determines whether the work involves trade, physical labor, or ministry. In the New Testament, the word is almost always used for partners in Christian ministry—those who preach, teach, build up the church, and care for believers together.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the New Testament, sunergos serves as Paul's standard term of warm endorsement for his ministry partners. He uses it to describe a wide variety of people who served alongside him, including prominent leaders like Timothy, local church hosts like Priscilla and Aquila, and traveling companions like Epaphroditus and Titus.

The word carries several important nuances when used in Scripture:

  • Equality in Mission: Calling someone a sunergos shows that Paul did not view himself as a solitary hero or a dictatorial ruler. He recognized that the spread of the gospel depended on teamwork among equal partners.

  • Shared Hard Work: The root word ergon highlights real labor. Ministry in the early church involved sacrifice, struggle, travel, and personal risk. A sunergos was not merely an associate in title, but someone actively sweating and striving for the cause.

  • Mutual Affection: When Paul labels someone a fellow laborer, it is an expression of deep trust and gratitude.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of sunergos highlights several key truths about the nature of the church and Christian ministry:

First, ministry is designed to be collaborative. God does not call believers to serve Him in isolation. Christ built His church using a body with many interconnected members, each contributing to the growth of the whole.

Second, gospel partnership transcends human status. In Christ, ordinary believers, wealthy homeowners, traveling apostles, and former bondservants labor together on equal footing. Their shared allegiance to Jesus matters far more than their social standing.

Third, believers are invited to participate in God's own work. In his letters, Paul even describes church workers as God's fellow laborers. God is the primary builder of His kingdom, yet He graciously works through human instruments to accomplish His purposes on earth.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:1, Paul opens his letter with these words: "Paul, a prisoner of Christ Jesus, and Timothy our brother, To Philemon our beloved friend and fellow laborer." The word appears in the dative case, identifying Philemon as the recipient who shares this title of honor.

Paul's choice of sunergos here is strategically and pastorally significant for several reasons:

  • Affirming Local Ministry: Philemon was not a full-time traveling missionary like Paul, but a householder in Colosse. By calling him a fellow laborer, Paul honors Philemon's local ministry, acknowledging that hosting a church and serving local saints is true kingdom labor.

  • Establishing Mutual Ground: Paul addresses Philemon as a partner rather than pulling apostolic rank. Instead of commanding Philemon as an absolute superior, Paul grounds his opening appeal in their shared labor for Jesus Christ.

  • Setting the Tone for Restoration: The entire letter centers on reconciliation. By emphasizing that Philemon is his partner in the gospel, Paul sets a warm tone of Christian love, preparing Philemon's heart to act in harmony with gospel principles regarding Onesimus.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Romans 16:3 — "Greet Priscilla and Aquila, my fellow workers in Christ Jesus"

  • 1 Corinthians 3:9 — "For we are God’s fellow workers; you are God’s field, you are God’s building."

  • Philippians 2:25 — "Yet I considered it necessary to send to you Epaphroditus, my brother, fellow worker, and fellow soldier..."

  • Philemon 1:24 — "...as do Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, Luke, my fellow laborers."

  • 3 John 1:8 — "We therefore ought to receive such, that we may become fellow workers for the truth."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Combined Expressions of Honor: In Philemon 1:1, Paul combines two descriptions for Philemon: "beloved friend" and "fellow laborer." The first expresses personal affection, while the second acknowledges active ministry service. Love and labor go hand in hand in the Christian life.

  • Practical Hospitality as Labor: Philemon's identity as a co-worker was largely tied to opening his home for the church to meet and providing refreshing hospitality to traveling saints. This shows that practical acts of generosity and service are recognized by Scripture as vital labor for the gospel.

  • A Shared Title in the Letter: It is striking that Paul uses the plural form of this exact same word at the close of the letter in Philemon 1:24 to refer to Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, and Luke as his "fellow laborers." By placing Philemon in the same category as these well-known gospel workers at both the beginning and end of the letter, Paul frames the entire epistle around shared ministry identity.
