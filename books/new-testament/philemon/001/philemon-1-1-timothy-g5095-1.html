TIMOTHY - (Timótheos, Strong's G5095)

Summary:

Timotheos is a proper personal name of Greek origin meaning "one who honors God" or "valued by God." In the New Testament, it identifies Timothy, the close associate, co-laborer, and spiritual child of the apostle Paul. In Philemon 1:1, Timothy is introduced alongside Paul as "our brother." His inclusion as co-sender of the letter highlights gospel fellowship and adds warm, fraternal support to Paul's appeal to Philemon.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Timothy was a native of Lystra, born to a Jewish mother named Eunice and a Greek father. Raised in the Scriptures by his mother and grandmother Lois, he joined Paul during the apostle's second missionary journey. Paul circumcised Timothy to facilitate ministry among local Jewish communities, after which Timothy became one of Paul's most trusted companions.

Throughout Paul's ministry, Timothy was entrusted with delicate pastoral missions, including serving congregations in Thessalonica, Corinth, and Ephesus. When Paul wrote to Philemon from prison, Timothy was present with him. Rather than operating as an isolated leader, Paul routinely included young co-workers like Timothy in his correspondence, demonstrating shared ministry in the early church.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Timotheos is a masculine proper noun combining two Greek words: timē, meaning honor, value, or respect, and theos, meaning God. The name literally translates to "one who honors God" or "honored by God."

As a proper personal name, it designates a specific historical individual. While the character of the biblical Timothy matched the literal meaning of his name, the name itself functions simply as his personal identifier. The author does not use the name as a secret code or symbolic allegory, but to name the brother who stood alongside him as he wrote.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, the name Timothy consistently refers to Paul’s co-worker. Scripture attaches several warm descriptive titles to his name, illustrating his roles:

  • "Our brother" — Emphasizing equal status in the family of God and co-laboring in ministry.

  • "Son in the faith" or "beloved son" — Reflecting Paul’s spiritual mentorship and deep personal affection for him.

  • "Fellow-laborer" or "minister of God" — Highlighting his proven work in spreading the gospel.

In letter greetings, including Timothy alongside Paul shows that Paul valued companion leadership. While Paul wrote with apostolic authority, Timothy’s presence offered a second credible witness to the care and concern expressed in the letters.

4. Theological Implications

The mention of Timothy in letter openings highlights the relational character of gospel ministry. God accomplishes His work through personal relationships, spiritual mentorship, and shared labor. Paul did not serve as a solitary heroic figure; he intentionally nurtured next-generation leaders and shared his burden with them.

Furthermore, calling Timothy "our brother" underscores the spiritual reality of the church. In Christ, believers from diverse backgrounds—such as a veteran apostle and a younger leader of mixed Jewish and Greek heritage—are united in one family.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:1, Paul writes: "Paul, a prisoner of Christ Jesus, and Timothy our brother, To Philemon our beloved friend and fellow laborer..."

Grammatically, Timothy stands in the nominative case alongside Paul as one of the authors extending greetings. Paul identifies himself as a "prisoner of Christ Jesus," grounding his appeal in his own suffering for the gospel. He then immediately joins Timothy to himself, giving Timothy the honorable title "our brother."

This phrasing is significant because the letter addresses a delicate personal situation: Paul's intercession for Onesimus, a runaway servant who belonged to Philemon. By mentioning Timothy, Paul reminds Philemon that this appeal comes within the circle of shared Christian brotherhood. Timothy was likely known to Philemon and the house church in Colosse. His presence beside Paul adds gentle weight and warm fraternal affection to Paul's personal request.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 2 Corinthians 1:1 — "Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God, and Timothy our brother, To the church of God which is at Corinth..."

  • Philippians 2:19 — "But I trust in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy shortly to you, that I also may be encouraged when I know your state."

  • 1 Thessalonians 3:2 — "...and sent Timothy, our brother and minister of God, and our fellow-laborer in the gospel of Christ, to establish you and encourage you concerning your faith..."

  • 1 Timothy 1:2 — "To Timothy, a true son in the faith: Grace, mercy, and peace from God our Father and Jesus Christ our Lord."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Timothy's heritage positioned him well to bridge cultural divides in the early church. His father was Greek and his mother was Jewish, giving him cultural familiarity with both worlds. This unique background, combined with his genuine care for believers, made him an effective pastor and representative for Paul.

Including Timothy in Philemon shows that even deeply personal matters benefit from accountability. Paul made his appeal to Philemon surrounded by trusted brothers, setting an example of openness, wisdom, and Christian community.
