FOR - (perí, Strong's G4012)

Summary:

The Greek preposition peri fundamentally means "around," "about," "concerning," or "on behalf of." While its physical background refers to surrounding something spatially, in non-physical contexts it marks the central topic or person being discussed. In Philemon 1:10, peri introduces the specific subject of Paul’s passionate intercession: his spiritual child Onesimus. The word directs Philemon's attention directly to the person on whose behalf Paul is appealing, turning an abstract request into a tender personal plea.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek, peri originally referred to physical location, describing something positioned around or encircling an object. Over time, it expanded into common everyday use to designate thematic focus—pointing out the topic, event, or person around which a conversation or written document revolved.

In first-century letter writing, prepositions like peri were regularly employed to introduce new topics, signal specific requests, or highlight particular individuals. When the apostle Paul wrote to Philemon from his imprisonment, he utilized this common preposition to bridge his general opening remarks with the specific, sensitive purpose of his letter: advocating for Onesimus, a runaway slave who had come to faith under Paul's ministry.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Peri functions as a preposition that indicates spatial position or thematic focus. Depending on how it is used in a sentence, it can refer to:

  • Physical proximity or enclosure ("around," "surrounding").

  • Thematic focus or subject matter ("concerning," "about," "regarding").

  • Representation or benefit on behalf of someone or something ("for," "on behalf of").

In Philemon 1:10, the word is translated as "for." Here it identifies Onesimus as the precise topic and beneficiary of Paul's intercessory request. It does not carry an abstract theological meaning in itself; rather, it clearly identifies the person on whose behalf Paul is speaking.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the New Testament, peri serves an essential organizing role in discourse. It helps readers identify what an author or speaker is emphasizing.

In historical narratives, the word frequently introduces the subject of a teaching, a miraculous event, or an inquiry regarding Jesus. In apostolic epistles, peri often signals a transition to a new topic raised by the church, or introduces personal updates about fellow workers in the gospel. When used in connection with prayer, it marks the specific people or needs being brought before God. Rather than making a vague or generalized statement, the preposition singles out a specific subject for direct attention.

4. Theological Implications

Although peri is a standard functional preposition, its context in apostolic intercession highlights important gospel patterns:

  • The Pattern of Intercession: By using peri to introduce his plea "for" Onesimus, Paul models Christlike intercession. Just as Christ intercedes for believers before the Father, Paul steps forward on behalf of a vulnerable brother.

  • Personal Advocacy: Christian fellowship is never merely theoretical; it involves advocating for specific individuals in concrete situations. Peri anchors Paul’s broad expressions of love and faith to a specific human context.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:10, Paul writes, "I appeal to you for my son Onesimus, whom I have begotten while in my chains." The placement of peri ("for") within the sentence structure is crucial to the argument:

  • Careful Delay: Paul opens his appeal with urgency ("I appeal to you"), uses peri to point forward, describes the individual as "my son," and only then states the name "Onesimus." In ancient social structures, a runaway slave faced severe penalties. By placing the preposition "for" before identifying Onesimus as his own spiritual child, Paul frames the entire matter around relational love before Philemon even processes the slave's legal standing.

  • Shift from Right to Request: Paul explicitly refrains from using apostolic command. Instead, peri serves as the doorway into a voluntary, love-driven appeal. It directs Philemon away from legal strictures and toward a personal relationship restored through the gospel.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Corinthians 16:12 — "Now concerning our brother Apollos, I strongly urged him to come to you with the brethren..."

  • 1 Thessalonians 1:9 — "For they themselves declare concerning us what manner of entry we had to you..."

  • 1 Peter 3:18 — "For Christ also suffered once for sins, the just for the unjust, that He might bring us to God..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Simple Words, Deep Weight: Peri illustrates how ordinary Greek grammatical connectors gain rich pastoral depth from their surrounding context. The preposition itself simply means "concerning" or "for," but when placed between an apostle in chains and a forgiven runaway slave, it carries the full weight of gospel reconciliation.

  • Clarity in Intercession: Paul does not leave Philemon guessing about the intent of his letter. By introducing Onesimus directly with peri, Paul demonstrates that Christian intercession should be clear, direct, and unashamed to associate with those in need.
