SON - ( teknon , Strong's G5043 )

Summary:

The word teknon signifies a child born into a family, emphasizing deep relational identity, affection, and spiritual lineage rather than mere legal status, and is used here by Paul to describe Onesimus as his true spiritual offspring.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • Rooted in the biological concept of childbearing and birth, tracing back to a base verb meaning to bring forth or beget.

  • In the Greco-Roman and Jewish worlds, the parent-child relationship carried profound expectations of shared nature, character, obedience, and ultimate inheritance.

  • The term naturally evokes a warm, tender picture of domestic intimacy, distinguishing a close family member from a distant acquaintance or servant.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Refers primarily to a child, descendant, or offspring in relation to parents.

  • Used metaphorically in the New Testament for spiritual progeny—those brought to faith through a mentor or evangelist.

  • Carries a strong nuance of affection, endearment, and deep moral or spiritual likeness.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Paul frequently uses familial language to describe his converts, treating them not merely as converts in a statistical sense, but as beloved members of his own spiritual household.

  • By calling Onesimus his teknon, Paul elevates the runaway slave to the highest possible status within the family of God, bridging the massive social chasms of the Roman world.

  • The designation prepares Philemon to receive Onesimus back no longer merely as a runaway slave, but as a beloved brother in Christ.

4. Theological Implications

  • Highlights the regenerative work of God, wherein believers are born anew into a spiritual family.

  • Underscores the intimate relational bond that exists between spiritual mentors and those they bring to Christ, mirroring the greater relationship between believers and their heavenly Father.

  • Demonstrates how the Gospel radically reorders human relationships, turning social hierarchies into genuine familial bonds.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • Paul introduces Onesimus with the phrase "my son," immediately softening Philemon's heart before delivering his bold appeal for forgiveness and restoration.

  • The term underscores that the transformation in Onesimus is deep and authentic; he has become Paul's child "whom I have begotten while in my chains," pointing to a spiritual birth that happened during Paul's imprisonment.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Corinthians 4:17: "For this reason I have sent Timothy to you, who is my beloved and faithful son (teknon) in the Lord..."

  • Philippians 2:22: "But you know his proven character, that as a son (teknon) with a father he served with me in the gospel."

  • 1 Timothy 1:2: "To Timothy, a true son (teknon) in the faith..."

  • Titus 1:4: "To Titus, a true son (teknon) in our common faith..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • While another common Greek word for son (huios) often emphasizes legal standing, maturity, and inheritance rights, teknon leans more heavily into the affective, relational reality of birth, parentage, and emotional closeness.

  • Paul's choice of teknon over huios in this specific context highlights the tender, affectionate bond forged in suffering and shared faith behind prison walls.
