SON - (téknon, Strong's G5043)

Summary:

The word teknon means a child, offspring, or son. While it frequently refers to biological children in a household, Scripture also uses it figuratively to describe spiritual children born into faith through gospel ministry. In Philemon 1:10, Paul uses the word to introduce Onesimus as his spiritual son, who came to faith in Christ through Paul's ministry while Paul was imprisoned. This word highlights the tender family bond created by the gospel, reframing a runaway slave as a beloved child in God's household.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek society, family bonds defined a person's social identity, legal standing, and household relationships. A child belonged to a family line and inherited household responsibilities. In the Greco-Roman world, slaves held no legal family status or societal rights.

When the gospel spread through the Roman world, early Christians adopted household language to describe their new life together. Believers were born into God's family and shared a deep spiritual brotherhood. Spiritual leaders like Paul naturally used family terminology to express their tender pastoral relationship with those they led to faith. By calling a young convert his child, a leader expressed genuine spiritual nurture, protective care, and enduring affection.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

meaningful term refers to a child, offspring, or son/daughter. It highlights the idea of descent, birth, and close family relation.

The word can mean:

  • A biological child or descendant within a human family.

  • A spiritual child who has received spiritual life or discipleship through a mentor's ministry.

  • Believers generally, as children who share in God's divine favor and family identity.

Unlike formal legal terms that stress official rights or adoption rank, teknon emphasizes origin, intimate kinship, and personal warmth.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the word in several distinct settings:

  • Household relationships: It frequently describes literal children in instructions about family honor, obedience, and parental instruction.

  • Divine relationship: It identifies believers as children of God who have received new spiritual life through faith.

  • Spiritual mentorship: Apostles and leaders use it to refer to converts whom they nurtured in the faith, reflecting a fatherly care for their spiritual growth.

The word carries a warm, tender tone. It describes a connection rooted in spiritual life and ongoing nurture rather than merely formal office or institutional authority.

4. Theological Implications

The gospel creates real family bonds that transcend earthly social classes, ethnic divisions, and economic barriers. In Roman culture, a runaway slave was viewed as property subject to harsh punishment. However, spiritual birth through the gospel completely transforms a person's identity.

When a person comes to faith in Christ, they enter the household of God. Onesimus became a child of God and a spiritual son to Paul. This demonstrates that God's grace completely redefines human worth. In Christ, social barriers dissolve, and believers are united as true brothers and sisters in a single spiritual family.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:10, Paul writes a delicate letter of appeal to Philemon, a Christian slaveholder. Onesimus had run away from Philemon, but while hiding in Rome, he met the imprisoned apostle Paul and accepted Christ.

Paul appeals to Philemon on behalf of "my son Onesimus, whom I have begotten while in my chains." Grammatically, the word appears in the genitive singular neuter form, explicitly linking Onesimus to Paul as his spiritual child.

This word choice carries immense persuasive weight. Paul does not introduce Onesimus as a fugative returning to face judgment, but as his own beloved spiritual child. By calling Onesimus his son, Paul signals to Philemon that harming or rejecting Onesimus would mean mistreating Paul's own child. The word softens Philemon's heart and prepares him to receive Onesimus back not merely as a servant, but as a beloved brother in the Lord.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Corinthians 4:17 — "For this reason I have sent Timothy to you, who is my beloved and faithful son in the Lord, who will remind you of my ways in Christ..."

  • 1 Timothy 1:18 — "This charge I commit to you, son Timothy, according to the prophecies previously made concerning you, that by them you may wage the good warfare..."

  • Romans 8:21 — "...because the creation itself also will be delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of God."

  • Ephesians 6:4 — "And you, fathers, do not provoke your children to wrath, but bring them up in the training and admonition of the Lord."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Even while suffering in prison, Paul remained spiritually fruitful. His chains did not prevent him from leading others to Christ and fathering spiritual children. This shows that God's Word is never bound by difficult human circumstances.

Furthermore, the conversion of Onesimus illustrates a powerful transformation. The name Onesimus means "useful." As an unbelieving runaway, he was considered unprofitable, but as a spiritual son born into God's family, he became truly useful to both Paul and Philemon.
