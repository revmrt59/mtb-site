WISHED - ( boulnomai , Strong's G1014 )

Summary:

In Philemon 1:13, "wished" translates boulnomai, capturing a deep, deliberate desire rooted in thoughtful will rather than a passing emotional whim. Paul expresses that while he desired to keep Onesimus with him to serve in place of Philemon during his imprisonment, he refused to act without Philemon's free consent. This word reveals that Christian fellowship and service must be anchored in voluntary love rather than compelled obligation.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The root describes a settled exercise of the will, denoting a conscious determination or deliberate choice rather than a mere wishful thought.

  • In the Greco-Roman world, this term frequently appeared in legal, political, and personal contexts where individuals weighed options and decided upon a deliberate course of action.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • To will, wish, or desire strongly.

  • To be minded or disposed toward a specific action, implying purposeful intent.

  • To determine or resolve upon something after consideration.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The term emphasizes volition and purposeful intent, distinguishing a deliberate choice from idle dreaming or emotional impulse.

  • When used in the New Testament, it often highlights the inner disposition that precedes outward action.

4. Theological Implications

  • Human desires, even those of the Apostle Paul, are subject to the higher wisdom of God and the preservation of Christian unity.

  • Paul respects the agency and freedom of Philemon, illustrating that true Christian love does not manipulate or override another person's free choice.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • Paul wanted to keep Onesimus with him in chains to minister to him on Philemon's behalf.

  • However, Paul deliberately chose not to exercise apostolic authority to force this outcome, ensuring that any kindness shown by Philemon would be voluntary rather than out of necessity.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 1:19 - Then Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not wanting (boulnomai) to make her a public example, was minded to put her away secretly.

  • Mark 15:15 - So Pilate, wanting (boulnomai) to gratify the crowd, released Barabbas to them; and he delivered Jesus, after he had scourged Him, to be crucified.

  • Acts 5:33 - When they heard this, they were furious and plotted (boulnomai) to kill them.

  • 1 Timothy 2:4 - Who desires (boulnomai) all men to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth.

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The contrast in New Testament Greek between emotional desire and calculated will helps clarify why Paul used boulnomai here: his desire for Onesimus's help was a serious, reasoned proposition that he nonetheless submitted to Philemon's judgment.
