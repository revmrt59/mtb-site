WITHOUT - (chōrís, Strong's G5565)

Summary:

The word choris is a preposition meaning "without," "apart from," or "separate from." It indicates that an action or situation takes place independently of someone or something else. In Philemon 1:14, choris expresses Paul's deliberate choice not to act independent of Philemon's personal consent, ensuring that Philemon's gracious response would spring voluntarily from his own heart rather than from apostolic compulsion.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The letter to Philemon is a deeply personal note written by the apostle Paul while in prison. Onesimus, a bondservant who had fled from Philemon's household, encountered Paul, heard the gospel, and became a follower of Jesus Christ. During his time with Paul, Onesimus became a valuable helper in ministry.

Although Paul wanted to keep Onesimus with him in Rome, he recognized that Philemon had a legitimate personal interest in the matter. The word choris functions in this letter as a spatial and relational marker. It sets a boundary: Paul will not move forward in a way that separates his decision from Philemon's explicit agreement.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word choris means "without," "apart from," "independent of," or "separate from."

It simple describes the absence of a person, object, or condition in relation to an action. When used with a person or their judgment, it indicates acting without their knowledge, involvement, or permission. The word itself does not carry a positive or negative moral value; it simply establishes that one thing is taking place apart from another. In Philemon 1:14, it specifically refers to acting without Philemon's personal consent.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In the New Testament, choris is used to mark clear distinctions and essential conditions across various contexts:

  • Spiritual Dependence: It highlights human powerlessness apart from divine grace, such as Jesus teaching that branches cannot bear fruit apart from the vine.

  • Redemptive History: It establishes boundaries in salvation, such as God's righteousness being revealed apart from the works of the law.

  • Relational Mutual Respect: It describes life within the Christian community, emphasizing that believers are not independent of one another in the Lord.

In every case, choris identifies a separation or exclusion, leaving the immediate context to show why that separation matters.

4. Theological Implications

Paul's use of choris in Philemon 1:14 highlights a vital principle in Christian leadership and ethics: God values willing obedience over forced compliance.

God does not coerce people into acts of righteousness, but transforms their hearts so that they serve gladly. Paul reflects this divine priority in his ministry. Even though he possessed apostolic authority to command Philemon, he refused to override Philemon's will. By choosing not to act without Philemon's consent, Paul protected the moral integrity of Philemon's choice. True Christian virtue cannot be produced by pressure or manipulation; it must grow out of genuine love and voluntary faith.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:14, Paul writes: "But without your consent I wanted to do nothing, that your good deed might not be by compulsion, as it were, but voluntary."

The word choris ("without") defines a strict self-imposed restraint on Paul's part. Paul strongly desired to keep Onesimus to assist him in prison. However, Paul knew that keeping Onesimus without Philemon's agreement would put Philemon in a position where he had no choice in the matter. Any passive agreement from Philemon would then feel like forced compliance rather than a generous choice.

By resolving to do nothing without Philemon's consent, Paul handed the initiative back to Philemon. This elevated Philemon from a passive bystander into an active partner in gospel ministry, allowing his forgiveness and generosity toward Onesimus to be a true, voluntary sacrifice unto God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 15:5 — "I am the vine, you are the branches. He who abides in Me, and I in him, bears much fruit; for without Me you can do nothing."

  • Romans 3:21 — "But now the righteousness of God apart from the law is revealed, being witnessed by the Law and the Prophets..."

  • 1 Corinthians 11:11 — "Nevertheless, neither is man independent of woman, nor woman independent of man, in the Lord."

  • Ephesians 2:12 — "that at that time you were without Christ, being aliens from the commonwealth of Israel..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Paul’s approach shows that how a good result is achieved matters just as much as the result itself. Keeping Onesimus in Rome would have been helpful to Paul's ministry, but securing that benefit by bypassing Philemon would have damaged Christian fellowship and compromised Philemon's opportunity to grow in grace. Honor, respect, and voluntary partnership are fundamental to healthy relationships within the church.
