FOR - (prós, Strong's G4314)

Summary:

Pros is a common Greek preposition indicating direction, relation, proximity, or duration, commonly translated as "to," "toward," "with," "for," or "in relation to." In Philemon 1:15, the word functions temporally to denote a limited span of time ("for a while"). It contributes to Paul's argument by establishing a sharp contrast between the brief, temporary period of Onesimus's physical departure and the permanent, eternal nature of his spiritual restoration as a brother in Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Epistle to Philemon is a personal letter written by Paul during his imprisonment. Onesimus, a bondservant belonging to Philemon, had left his master and eventually encountered Paul, coming to faith in Jesus Christ. Paul wrote this letter to send Onesimus back to Philemon, asking Philemon to receive him no longer merely as a servant, but as a beloved brother in the Lord.

In verse 15, Paul places this human situation within the framework of divine providence. Rather than dwelling on the wrongdoing or legal infraction of the departure, Paul suggests that God permitted a short separation so that a far greater, lasting good might be accomplished. The preposition pros marks the temporary boundary of that separation.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Pros is a versatile preposition used to express orientation, motion, or relationship between subjects, objects, or periods of time. Depending on the grammatical context and case it governs, the word can mean:

  • Direction or movement toward a person, object, or goal ("to," "toward").

  • Close proximity, face-to-face association, or personal presence ("with," "in the presence of").

  • Relation, reference, or applicability ("pertaining to," "with regard to").

  • Temporal duration when paired with time expressions ("for," "for the duration of").

In Philemon 1:15, pros connects directly with a noun denoting a brief period ("an hour" or "a season"), giving the preposition the temporal meaning "for." It marks a bounded span of time rather than an ongoing or permanent state.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, pros connects people, places, and ideas in dynamic relationships.

  • Spatial and Personal Motion: It frequently describes individuals moving toward someone to listen, seek healing, or offer assistance.

  • Relational Proximity: It describes personal, intimate presence and face-to-face fellowship, such as describing Christ's eternal presence with God.

  • Reference and Relation: It highlights how an action or statement relates directly to a specific audience or topic.

  • Temporal Limits: When linked to time words, it limits an activity or event to a designated time frame.

In every case, the specific shade of meaning is established by the surrounding syntax and sentence structure rather than being built into the preposition alone.

4. Theological Implications

In Philemon 1:15, the temporal limit marked by pros serves a vital theological purpose. Paul contrasts human temporal experience with God's eternal design.

The brief disruption in Philemon's household was limited in time ("for a while"), but the spiritual outcome achieved through God's grace endures forever. This reflects a frequent biblical truth: God often permits temporary trials, separations, or delays in order to produce lasting spiritual fruit. The contrast shows that God's sovereign providence rules over earthly disruptions, turning short-term losses into eternal blessings.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:15, Paul writes, "For perhaps he departed for a while, that you might receive him forever."

The expression translated "for a while" uses pros to highlight how brief Onesimus's absence really was in the grand scheme of God's purpose. By framing the departure as taking place "for a short season," Paul gently shifts Philemon's focus away from immediate offense and toward spiritual reality.

The brevity of the separation ("for a while") stands in direct, intentional contrast with the word "forever" in the next clause. Onesimus was gone for a brief moment, but he returns as a brother in Christ whose bond with Philemon will never end. The preposition pros anchors the temporal side of this contrast, highlighting the temporary nature of earthly hardship when compared to eternal spiritual relationships.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Galatians 2:5 — "...to whom we did not yield submission even for an hour, that the truth of the gospel might continue with you." Here pros marks a temporal duration ("for"), stressing that Paul did not compromise gospel truth even for a brief moment.

  • John 1:29 — "The next day John saw Jesus coming toward him, and said, 'Behold! The Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!'" Here pros expresses direct physical orientation and movement ("toward").

  • 1 Thessalonians 2:18 — "Therefore we wanted to come to you—even I, Paul, time and again—but Satan hindered us." Here pros expresses direction and personal presence ("to").

  • Romans 15:17 — "Therefore I have reason to glory in Christ Jesus in the things which pertain to God." Here pros establishes dynamic relation ("to"), marking service directed toward God.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Prepositions in biblical Greek act as vital connectors that reveal how the author structures time, space, and personal relationships.

In pastoral contexts, understanding pros in Philemon 1:15 encourages believers facing earthly disruptions. What feels like a major loss in the present moment is often described in Scripture as lasting only "for a season." Recognizing God's ability to frame temporary hardships within His eternal plans provides comfort and patience during seasons of trial.
