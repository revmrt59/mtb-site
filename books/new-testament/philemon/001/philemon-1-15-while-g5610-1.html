WHILE - (hṓra, Strong's G5610)

Summary:

The Greek noun hōra refers to a measured period of time, such as an hour of the day, an appointed season, or a temporary interval. In Philemon 1:15, the word is translated "while" as part of the phrase "for a while" (pros hōran), describing a brief, limited duration of time. Paul uses this word to show Philemon that the temporary separation from his bondservant Onesimus was brief in comparison to the eternal spiritual relationship they would now share as brothers in Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek culture, hōra originally denoted any well-defined period or season, such as a season of the year or a favorable moment for action. Over time, it came to mean a specific unit of time during the day (an hour) or a specific appointed time when an event takes place.

In the New Testament context, hōra appears in various settings. It frequently designates a precise hour of the day, an appointed moment in God's divine plan, or a short, temporary interval. In Paul's personal letter to Philemon, the word is used in this last sense. Onesimus, Philemon's bondservant, had run away and eventually met Paul while Paul was imprisoned. After coming to faith in Jesus Christ, Onesimus was sent back to Philemon. Paul wrote to encourage Philemon to receive Onesimus back not merely as a slave, but as a beloved brother in the Lord.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Hōra refers fundamentally to a slice or unit of time. Depending on the context, the word can mean:

  • A specific hour of the day (for instance, the sixth or ninth hour).

  • An appointed or set time for a particular event to occur.

  • A short duration or temporary period of time.

In Philemon 1:15, hōra carries this third sense of a brief period. Combined with the preposition pros ("for"), the expression pros hōran functions as an idiom meaning "for a short time" or "for a while." The word itself simply denotes a limited interval, emphasizing that the situation being described was temporary rather than permanent.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, hōra serves several clear communicative purposes:

  • Specific Time of Day: It frequently marks the precise moment an action takes place or a miracle occurs.

  • Appointed Divine Moments: It describes fixed, significant times in salvation history or times of crisis and judgment.

  • Limited Duration: When paired with prepositions, it marks a brief interval or temporary season.

In Philemon 1:15, the usage falls into this third category. Paul is not referring to a sixty-minute period on a clock, nor is he pointing to an eschatological hour of judgment. Instead, he uses hōra to set a clear contrast between human earthly disruptions, which are brief and temporary, and the permanent work of God in the lives of believers.

4. Theological Implications

While hōra itself is a standard temporal noun meaning a short period or hour, its placement in Philemon 1:15 highlights an important biblical reality regarding God's providence. Human events that seem painful, unexpected, or disruptive often occupy only a brief moment (hōra) in the overall scope of God's redemptive work.

In this passage, Paul contrasts the brief period of separation with the word "forever" (aiōnion). The departure of Onesimus was only "for a while," but the result—his salvation and reconciliation—carries results that last eternally. God frequently permits temporary disruptions in earthly circumstances to accomplish lasting spiritual results in human hearts and relationships.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:15, Paul writes with notable pastoral tact: "For perhaps he departed for a while, that you might receive him forever." Paul does not excuse any wrongdoing on Onesimus's part, but he gently invites Philemon to view the entire situation through the lens of divine purpose.

By framing the separation as lasting only "for a while" (pros hōran), Paul minimizes the sense of permanent loss or offense Philemon may have felt. The focus shifts from the temporary absence of a bondservant to the permanent gain of a Christian brother. The contrast between the short time (hōra) and eternity ("forever") helps Philemon see that God used a brief earthly disruption to bring about an eternal family restoration.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The phrasing in Philemon 1:15 provides a clear example of how biblical authors contrast time and eternity. By pairing pros hōran ("for a while") with aiōnion ("forever"), Paul creates a literary contrast that reframes the reader's perspective. What felt like a significant personal and financial trial for Philemon was, in reality, a momentary event that led to an everlasting spiritual relationship. This literary contrast provides a model for how believers can evaluate temporary earthly hardships in light of God's eternal purposes.
