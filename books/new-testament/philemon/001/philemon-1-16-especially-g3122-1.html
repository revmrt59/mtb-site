ESPECIALLY - (málista, Strong's G3122)

Summary:

The Greek word malista is an adverb that means "most of all," "particularly," or "especially." It highlights a specific person, group, or detail out of a larger category for distinct emphasis. In Philemon 1:16, Paul uses the word to describe how deeply he personally values Onesimus as a brother in Christ, while setting up a persuasive argument for Philemon to welcome him with even greater joy and affection.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient Greco-Roman world, social relationships were strictly defined by legal status and civic hierarchy. Slaves were viewed as property under Roman law, lacking personal rights and recognized social honor. When Onesimus, a runaway slave belonging to Philemon, encountered the apostle Paul during Paul's imprisonment, Onesimus heard the gospel and placed his faith in Jesus Christ.

This spiritual transformation completely redefined his relationship to both Paul and Philemon. Writing to Philemon to request a gracious reception for Onesimus, Paul uses the adverb malista ("especially") to highlight a profound shift: the former runaway servant has become a deeply cherished Christian brother.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word malista functions as an adverb meaning "most of all," "above all," "chiefly," or "especially." It serves as the superlative form of the Greek word for "more" (mallon). It signals that while a general statement applies broadly, the highlighted item or person holds a place of primary priority, emphasis, or intensity.

When applied to people or circumstances, malista singles out the primary subject of care, interest, or affection. The word itself is a simple modifier of emphasis rather than a technical term; it directs the reader's attention to whatever detail or individual deserves chief focus in the context.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across New Testament writings, malista regularly narrows the focus of a statement to emphasize a specific subset within a larger group. It is commonly used in two practical ways:

First, it identifies a unique priority within a broader obligation. For example, believers are instructed to perform good works toward all people, but "especially" toward fellow believers. Kindness is owed to everyone, but the church family receives specific priority.

Second, it draws attention to an item or fact of chief importance in a given situation. When Paul requests his belongings from prison, he asks for his cloak and books, but places chief emphasis ("especially") on his parchments.

4. Theological Implications

While malista is a standard adverb of emphasis, its placement in apostolic letters highlights how gospel truth reorders human priorities. Divine grace does not erase general human obligations, but it creates elevated, enduring bonds among God's redeemed people.

By using malista, Scripture shows that spiritual kinship in Christ takes preeminence over worldly distinctions. Love and justice are due to all people, but believers share a unique, eternal unity as members of the household of God. In Christ, former social barriers are superseded by family ties rooted in salvation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:16, Paul asks Philemon to welcome Onesimus back "no longer as a slave but more than a slave—a beloved brother, especially to me, but how much more to you, both in the flesh and in the Lord."

Paul's use of malista in this verse serves a clear purpose:

  • It declares Paul's personal affection: Onesimus has become "especially" dear to Paul as a son in the faith who served alongside him during imprisonment.

  • It strengthens his appeal to Philemon: If Onesimus became so precious to Paul in a short time, he ought to be "how much more" valuable to Philemon, who will now relate to him both in daily life ("in the flesh") and in Christian fellowship ("in the Lord").

Paul uses malista not to claim exclusive rights to Onesimus, but to attest to his high worth, encouraging Philemon to view his returned servant through the eyes of Christian love.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Galatians 6:10 — "Therefore, as we have opportunity, let us do good to all, especially to those who are of the household of faith."

  • 1 Timothy 5:8 — "But if anyone does not provide for his own, and especially for those of his household, he has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever."

  • 2 Timothy 4:13 — "Bring the cloak that I left with Carpus at Troas when you come—and the books, especially the parchments."

  • Acts 26:3 — "especially because you are expert in all customs and questions which have to do with the Jews."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Recognizing how malista functions helps prevent misunderstanding biblical statements that distinguish between general and primary duties. The word does not create a rigid separation that excludes the broader group; rather, it highlights a priority within that group.

For instance, doing good "especially" to believers does not relieve Christians of their duty to show kindness to non-believers. Likewise, calling Onesimus "especially" beloved to Paul does not diminish Paul's affection for Philemon or other believers. It simply highlights the deep, real bond established in the immediate setting.
