MORE - (hypér, Strong's G5228)

Summary:

The word huper is a preposition that broadly means "above," "beyond," "more than," or "on behalf of." Depending on its placement in a sentence, it can express substitution, benefit, or a higher degree of comparison. In Philemon 1:16, Paul uses huper ("more than") in its comparative sense to redefine how Philemon should view his runaway servant Onesimus. Paul asks Philemon to receive Onesimus not merely according to his past status as a slave, but as something far greater—a beloved brother in Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Epistle to Philemon centers on a sensitive personal and spiritual situation. Onesimus, a bondservant belonging to a Christian named Philemon, had left his master and fled to Rome. While there, he encountered the apostle Paul, who was under house arrest. Through Paul's witness, Onesimus became a believer in Jesus Christ.

Paul sent Onesimus back to Philemon, carrying this short personal letter. Rather than demanding that Philemon grant Onesimus freedom using apostolic authority, Paul appeals to Philemon’s Christian love. In Philemon 1:16, Paul sets the groundwork for how Philemon should receive Onesimus. The word huper ("more") serves as the pivot point in Paul's request, shifting the basis of their relationship from legal ownership to spiritual brotherhood.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Huper is a preposition with two primary lines of meaning in biblical Greek:

1. Comparative or Spatial ("above," "beyond," "over," or "more than"): In this sense, the word indicates something that surpasses a standard, limit, or status. It places one thing above another in rank, degree, or value.

2. Representational or Beneficial ("for," "on behalf of," or "in the place of"): In this sense, the word describes an action taken for another person's benefit or as a substitute.

In Philemon 1:16, the word carries the first sense. It functions as a comparative particle translated as "more than" or "beyond." Paul uses it to place Onesimus's new standing in Christ above his former position under human law.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, huper is widely used to highlight key theological and practical truths.

When expressing representation or benefit, the word often highlights Christ’s redemptive work. Scripture frequently speaks of Christ dying "for" (huper) sinners, meaning He acted on their behalf and in their place.

When expressing comparison or elevation, the word highlights superior rank or overflowing grace. For example, God exalted Jesus and gave Him a name that is "above" (huper) every other name. Elsewhere, the word describes things that surpass human measurement, such as God's peace or power.

In Philemon 1:16, the comparative nuance directly impacts social identity. Paul does not ignore the historical reality of Onesimus's bondage, but he places a new spiritual reality above it. Onesimus is returning as "more than" (huper) a servant.

4. Theological Implications

The use of huper in Philemon 1:16 highlights how the gospel transforms personal identity and human relationships.

While redemption in Christ does not immediately erase every earthly circumstance, it redefines the value of every person within the Christian community. In the ancient world, a bondservant was regarded as legal property. By placing the word "more than" before "a slave," Paul introduces a higher category derived from the gospel itself.

In God’s household, faith in Christ creates a spiritual bond that surpasses earthly social hierarchies. Onesimus was now united to Christ, making him a son of God and a brother to both Paul and Philemon. Therefore, Christian brotherhood is not a minor addition to earthly social structures; it is a superior reality that must govern how believers treat one another.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In the immediate context of Philemon 1:15–16, Paul offers a divine perspective on Onesimus's departure. He suggests that perhaps Onesimus was separated from Philemon for a short time so that Philemon might have him back forever.

Paul then specifies the nature of this return: "no longer as a slave, but more than a slave—a beloved brother." The word huper ("more than") marks the transition from the old relationship to the new. Paul does not merely ask Philemon to forgive Onesimus as a master forgives a servant; he asks him to welcome Onesimus as a spiritual equal and a cherished member of the family of God.

Paul reinforces this appeal by noting that Onesimus is "a beloved brother, especially to me, but how much more to you, both in the flesh and in the Lord." By framing the request around what Onesimus has now become, Paul gently calls Philemon to align his household conduct with the truth of the gospel.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 16:8 — "For the sons of this world are more shrewd in their generation than the sons of light."

  • Romans 5:8 — "But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us."

  • Ephesians 1:22 — "And He put all things under His feet, and gave Him to be head over all things to the church."

  • Philippians 2:9 — "Therefore God also has highly exalted Him and given Him the name which is above every name."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The phrasing in Philemon 1:16 shows how Paul uses prepositions intentionally to guide church life. By pairing "no longer" (ouketi) with "more than" (huper), Paul signals a decisive break with past attitudes.

Philemon was legally entitled to punish a runaway servant under Roman law. However, Paul reframes the entire scenario by elevating Onesimus to the status of "beloved brother." This shift demonstrates that Christian love operates on a principle higher than strict legal rights. Love seeks the spiritual good of the other person and honors the new creation God has accomplished in their life.
