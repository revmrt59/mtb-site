REFRESH - ( anapauo , Strong's G373 )

Summary:

In Philemon 1:20, "refresh" translates the Greek verb anapauo, which carries the rich pastoral imagery of bringing physical or spiritual relief, rest, and deep inward satisfaction to weary souls, functioning here as a tender appeal for relational restoration and Christian encouragement.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The term is built upon two roots: ana (up or back) and pauo (to make to cease, or to stop).

  • Historically, the word paints the vivid picture of lifting a heavy burden off a traveler or beast of burden so they can catch their breath and regain their strength.

  • In the ancient Greco-Roman world, it frequently referred to halting a journey to rest at an inn, regain physical equilibrium, and enjoy hospitality.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • To cause to rest, calm, or refresh.

  • To give intermission from labor, pain, or trial.

  • To revive the spirit and bring deep encouragement to the inner person.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The New Testament often uses this word for physical rest after strenuous toil, such as Jesus inviting His disciples to rest awhile away from the crowds (Mark 6:31).

  • It also stretches metaphorically into the relational and spiritual realm, describing how ordinary believers can act as an oasis of grace to fellow saints enduring hardship or sorrow.

4. Theological Implications

  • True refreshment in the body of Christ is ultimately rooted in the rest provided by God Himself, who revives the contrite and weary heart.

  • When believers bring relief and comfort to one another, they mirror the restorative heart of God, who desires to heal broken fellowship and bind up the wounded spirit.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • Paul is writing to Philemon regarding Onesimus, a runaway slave who had become a brother in Christ.

  • By asking Philemon to "refresh" his heart, Paul makes a gentle, deeply personal appeal that bypasses demands of authority and instead calls upon Philemon's known reputation for loving hospitality and grace.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 11:28 - "Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."

  • 1 Corinthians 16:18 - "For they refreshed my spirit and yours. Therefore acknowledge such men."

  • 2 Corinthians 7:13 - "Therefore we have been comforted in your comfort. And we rejoiced the more exceedingly for the joy of Titus, because his spirit has been refreshed by you all."

  • Philemon 1:7 - "For we have great joy and consolation in your love, because the hearts of the saints have been refreshed by you, brother."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Paul uses the noun form or related verb forms in Philemon to emphasize a pattern of ministry where Philemon's very life was characterized by being a source of spiritual oxygen to others.

  • The appeal to "refresh" places the relationship between Philemon and Onesimus squarely in the realm of Christian grace rather than legalistic obligation.
