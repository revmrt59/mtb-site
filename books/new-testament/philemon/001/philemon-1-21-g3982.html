CONFIDENCE - ( peitho , Strong's G3982 )

Summary:

The Greek term peitho fundamentally denotes persuasion, trust, and reliance, functioning in Philemon 1:21 to express Paul's deep assurance that Philemon will exceed expectations in extending forgiveness and grace.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • Rooted in the concept of being persuaded by argument, evidence, or inward conviction until a state of settled trust is reached.

  • In classical and Hellenistic Greek, the active voice often meant to persuade or convince someone else, while the middle and passive voices signified being persuaded, yielding to trust, or having inward confidence.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • To persuade, win over, or induce.

  • To be persuaded, to have confidence, to trust in, or to rely upon a person or thing.

  • In the context of a relational assurance, it speaks of a quiet, settled certainty regarding another person's character and future action.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The verb moves beyond a mere intellectual guess to a moral conviction born of relationship and observed faithfulness.

  • Paul does not lean on mere authority or manipulation; rather, he expresses a relational persuasion that Philemon's life will align with the Gospel.

4. Theological Implications

  • Christian confidence is never ultimately placed in human capability alone, but in the transforming work of the grace of God operating within a believer.

  • Trusting a brother or sister to do the right thing is an echo of the divine trust placed in us by Christ.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Philemon 1:21, Paul writes, "Having confidence in your obedience, I write to you, knowing that you will do even more than I say."

  • Here, the word highlights Paul's pastoral discernment and confidence in Philemon's genuine Christian character regarding the reception of the runaway slave Onesimus as a beloved brother.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 18:9 - "Also He spoke this parable to some who trusted [peitho] in themselves that they were righteous, and despised others."

  • Romans 2:19 - "And are confident [peitho] that you yourself are a guide to the blind, a light to those who are in darkness,"

  • 2 Corinthians 2:3 - "And I wrote this very thing to you, lest, when I came, I should have sorrow over those from whom I ought to have joy, having confidence [peitho] in you all that my joy is the joy of you all."

  • Philippians 1:6 - "being confident [peitho] of this very thing, that He who has begun a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ;"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The passive/middle forms of peitho frequently bridge the gap between persuasion and obedience in the New Testament; to be truly persuaded by the Gospel is to obey it.

  • Paul's diplomatic and gentle approach in Philemon exemplifies how Christian leadership relies on relational persuasion rather than heavy-handed coercion.
