OBEDIENCE - (hypakoḗ, Strong's G5218)

Summary:

The word means attentive listening, compliance, or submissive response to a word or authority. In Philemon 1:21, Paul uses it to express his full confidence that Philemon will yield to the spirit of gospel love and mercy, acting beyond even what Paul explicitly requested concerning the runaway slave Onesimus.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Greek word hupakoe combines two basic concepts: a word meaning "under" and a word meaning "to hear." At its simplest level, the term conveys the picture of listening attentively while placing oneself under what is heard. It is not merely hearing sound, but hearing with an open, responsive heart that leads to action.

In the first-century Roman world, authority and hierarchy were enforced largely through legal obligation and social status. Masters commanded, and slaves obeyed out of external requirement or fear. In contrast, the New Testament transforms obedience by grounding it in a voluntary, love-driven response to God's grace. When Paul writes to Philemon, a wealthy Christian master, he places this letter in the context of Christian brotherhood rather than apostolic dictatorship.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Hupakoe refers to the act of listening attentively, yielding to instruction, or complying with a command or standard.

The word can refer to:

  • Listening with the intent to submit and comply.

  • Willing yield to rightful authority or truth.

  • The outward expression of an inward commitment to God or gospel teaching.

Unlike passive compliance or forced submission, hupakoe implies an active, willing choice to align one's conduct with what is right.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, hupakoe appears in several distinct contexts:

  • Obedience to the Gospel: It describes the initial turn of a believer toward Christ, where accepting the gospel message is seen as an act of faith-filled submission to God's truth.

  • Obedience to God and Christ: It describes the lifelong submission of a Christian to the Lord's will, following the supreme model of Jesus, who obeyed the Father fully.

  • Obedience within Christian Relationships: It describes submission to godly leadership, sound instruction, or spiritual encouragement within the church family.

In Paul's letters, obedience is never presented as a way to earn God's favor, but as the practical expression of faith in action.

4. Theological Implications

In New Testament theology, true obedience flows from love and faith. Paul speaks elsewhere of the "obedience of faith," demonstrating that genuine belief always bears fruit in action.

Furthermore, Christ Himself models perfect submission to the Father's will. For the believer, obedience is not a burdensome legal dynamic, but a loving response to the mercy already received through the cross. It transforms relationships, dismantling social pride and replacing self-interest with sacrificial love for others.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:21, Paul writes: "Having confidence in your obedience, I write to you, knowing that you will do even more than I say."

This verse carries rich contextual significance:

  • An Appeal, Not a Command: Earlier in the letter, Paul explicitly stated that although he had the apostolic right to command Philemon, he preferred to appeal to him on the basis of love. Therefore, when Paul speaks of Philemon's "obedience," he does not mean compulsory submission to Paul's personal order.

  • Submission to God's Heart: Philemon's compliance is ultimately directed toward God and the principles of the gospel. By receiving Onesimus no longer merely as a bondservant, but as a beloved brother, Philemon would display true gospel submission.

  • Exceeding Expectations: Paul adds that he knows Philemon will "do even more than I say." Genuine Christian compliance does not calculate the bare minimum required by duty. It seeks to reflect the lavish grace of God by going above and beyond in love, forgiveness, and restoration.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Romans 1:5 — "Through Him we have received grace and apostleship for obedience to the faith among all nations for His name."

  • Romans 5:19 — "For as by one man’s disobedience many were made sinners, so also by one Man’s obedience many will be made righteous."

  • 2 Corinthians 10:5 — "...casting down arguments and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ."

  • 1 Peter 1:2 — "...elect according to the foreknowledge of God the Father, in sanctification of the Spirit, for obedience and sprinkling of the blood of Jesus Christ."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Philemon 1:21, the word form indicates that Philemon's obedient character is the very sphere and foundation of Paul's confidence. Paul is not taking a wild gamble on Philemon; he rests his hope on the proven spiritual character of a man whose heart has been transformed by grace. This provides a powerful model for pastoral ministry, showing that appeals grounded in Christian identity and love are far more powerful than demands backed by authority.
