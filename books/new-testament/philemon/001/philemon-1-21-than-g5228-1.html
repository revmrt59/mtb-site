THAN - (hypér, Strong's G5228)

Summary:

The preposition huper fundamentally carries the spatial meaning of "over" or "above." When used in comparative expressions, it means "more than," "beyond," or "exceeding." It is also frequently used to mean "on behalf of" or "for the sake of." In Philemon 1:21, huper is translated as "than" in the phrase "more than I say." Paul uses this word to express his firm confidence that Philemon will respond to the return of Onesimus with a level of Christian love, generosity, and forgiveness that exceeds Paul's written request.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word huper is a common Greek preposition that describes spatial positioning, comparative degree, or personal representation. At its root, the word suggests height or overlap—standing "over" or "above" something else.

In ancient Greek writing, huper was used in physical contexts to describe an object situated above another. Over time, it naturally expanded into non-physical contexts. It came to describe standing up "on behalf of" someone else, speaking "concerning" a topic, or rising "above" a specific limit or standard. In the Greek translation of the Old Testament and throughout the New Testament, this preposition routinely marks actions done for the benefit of others or matters that surpass a boundary.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Depending on how it is framed in a sentence, huper can function in three main ways:

  • Comparative or Superiority ("above," "beyond," "more than"): When comparing two things, the word indicates that one item exceeds or surpasses the other in rank, degree, or measure.

  • Representational or Benefactive ("for," "on behalf of," "in place of"): When linked to a person or cause, the word shows that an action is taken to benefit or represent that party.

  • Topical ("concerning," "about"): In certain settings, the word simply introduces the subject matter being discussed.

In Philemon 1:21, the word functions in the comparative sense. Paired with the idea of doing "more," huper sets Paul's spoken and written words as the baseline, indicating that Philemon's actions will move far past that line.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Scripture, huper is used to highlight key spiritual truths depending on its relationship to other words in the sentence:

When describing the work of salvation, the word often highlights substitution or benefit. For instance, Scripture speaks of Christ giving Himself "for" His people or dying "for" sinners. In these places, the word indicates that Jesus acted on behalf of those who could not save themselves.

When describing exaltation or rank, the word highlights supreme authority. It marks Jesus as receiving a name that is "above" every name or being placed "over" all things for the church.

When describing degree or measure, as in Philemon 1:21, the word marks a standard that is surpassed. It shows that grace, love, or action is not confined to a minimum requirement, but overflows beyond the initial boundary.

4. Theological Implications

The comparative use of huper in Philemon 1:21 reflects how Christian love operates under the grace of the gospel. Under strict legal frameworks, duty is measured by minimum requirements: fulfilling what is explicitly commanded and nothing more. However, gospel grace changes the human heart, inspiring generosity that goes beyond basic obligation.

Paul's use of the word highlights that Christian brotherhood creates a dynamic of willing obedience that surpasses legal demands. By expressing confidence that Philemon will do "more than" asked, Paul relies on the inner working of the Holy Spirit in Philemon's life. True Christian charity does not ask, "What is the bare minimum required of me?" Instead, it asks how it can reflect the limitless grace of God in practical ways.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In his letter to Philemon, Paul addresses a deeply sensitive situation. Onesimus, a bondservant who had separated from Philemon, had become a Christian through Paul's ministry. Paul was sending Onesimus back, but he asked Philemon to receive him no longer as a mere servant, but as a beloved brother in the Lord.

Instead of issuing an absolute apostolic command, Paul writes with gentle tact and pastoral confidence. In verse 21, he writes, "Having confidence in your obedience, I write to you, knowing that you will do even more than I say."

Here, huper ("than") serves a vital purpose in Paul's appeal:

  • It establishes Paul's explicit written request as a starting point rather than a ceiling.

  • It leaves room for Philemon to exercise his own willing, loving initiative toward Onesimus.

  • It appeals to Philemon's mature faith, trusting that his personal heart for Christ will lead him to grant complete reconciliation and brotherhood beyond what Paul explicitly framed.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 16:8 — "For the sons of this world are more shrewd in their generation than the sons of light."

  • Romans 5:8 — "But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us."

  • Ephesians 5:25 — "Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for it."

  • Philippians 2:9 — "Therefore God also has highly exalted Him and given Him the name which is above every name."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Prepositions like huper are small words, but they carry significant weight in guiding how a passage is understood. In Philemon 1:21, huper demonstrates the wisdom of biblical leadership. Paul does not micromanage Philemon's household or dictate every detail of Onesimus's future. By stating that Philemon will do "more than" asked, Paul provides a soft nudge that honors Philemon's Christian character while setting a high standard of grace.

This simple word shows how Christian relationships are governed by mutual respect, love, and a shared commitment to exceed standard expectations for the sake of Christ.
