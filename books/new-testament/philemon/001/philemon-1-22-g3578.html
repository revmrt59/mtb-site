GUEST ROOM - ( xenia , Strong's G3578 )

Summary:

The Greek term xenia in Philemon 1:22 refers to a place of hospitality or a lodging prepared for a guest, reflecting Paul's earnest expectation of release and his desire to visit Philemon personally.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The word is rooted in the deep-seated ancient Mediterranean culture of hospitality (xenos), where welcoming travelers was considered a sacred duty and a reflection of one's character and reverence for God.

  • In the Greco-Roman world, homes of believers often served as the primary gathering places for early house churches, making the preparation of a guest room a vital ministry to traveling apostles, teachers, and brothers in the faith.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Refers primarily to hospitality, a lodging, or an accommodation provided for a guest.

  • Encompasses both the act of showing hospitality and the physical space set aside to receive someone into one's home.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Paul uses the term not merely as a polite request, but with an expectant warmth, asking Philemon to have a bed ready for him because he trusts the prayers of the saints will result in his freedom.

  • The preparation of the xenia serves as a tangible expression of Christian fellowship and mutual affection, bridging the gap between apostle and brother.

4. Theological Implications

  • Christian hospitality is portrayed as an active, expectant readiness to receive others, mirroring the grace and welcome God has extended to believers through Christ.

  • The expectation of fellowship underscores the unity and interconnectedness of the body of Christ across geographical distances.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Philemon 1:22, Paul transitions from his appeal regarding Onesimus to a personal appeal for his own accommodation, gently holding Philemon accountable to receive him just as he was asked to receive the returning runaway slave.

  • It acts as a subtle nudge of apostolic presence, reminding Philemon that Paul hopes to inspect firsthand the reconciliation that has taken place between master and slave.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Acts 28:23 - "So when they had appointed him a day, many came to him at his lodging, to whom he explained and solemnly testified of the kingdom of God..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The related verb and noun forms throughout the New Testament emphasize the high virtue of hospitality, commanding believers to practice it without murmuring (Romans 12:13; 1 Peter 4:9).

  • Paul's request for a guest room demonstrates his human side—his desire for physical fellowship, rest, and personal reunion with his beloved coworkers in the faith.
