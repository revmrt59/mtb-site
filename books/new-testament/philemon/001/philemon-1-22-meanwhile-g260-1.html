MEANWHILE - (háma, Strong's G260)

Summary:

The Greek adverb hama denotes temporal simultaneity or joint association, meaning "at the same time," "meanwhile," or "together." It connects actions that occur concurrently or highlights people operating in shared fellowship. In Philemon 1:22, Paul uses the word to transition from his earnest appeal for the runaway slave Onesimus to a concurrent request for personal hospitality. By asking Philemon to prepare a guest room "meanwhile," Paul gently connects the immediate forgiveness of Onesimus with the practical expectation of his own upcoming visit.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Greek word hama served as a common adverb indicating timing or association. In everyday ancient Greek, it communicated that two events were occurring within the same timeframe or that two entities were joined together in an activity or state. Depending on how it was used in a sentence, it translated as "at the same time," "meanwhile," or "together with."

In the New Testament, the word appears in narrative transitions, theological explanations of Christian unity, and personal correspondence. In the short letter to Philemon, Paul writes as a prisoner for Jesus Christ, appealing to Philemon, a house-church leader in Colosse. Paul writes on behalf of Onesimus, Philemon’s former bondservant who had fled but was subsequently converted under Paul’s ministry. As Paul brings his appeal to a close, he uses hama to introduce a concurrent action for Philemon to undertake.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word hama means "at the same time," "simultaneously," or "together." It functions in two primary ways:

First, as a temporal adverb, it connects actions occurring at the same time, often rendered as "meanwhile" or "at the same time." In this sense, it introduces a concurrent duty or circumstance alongside a primary narrative or argument.

Second, when linked with a noun or pronoun, it functions prepositionally to mean "together with" or "along with." In this usage, it denotes complete spatial, corporate, or experiential unity between individuals or groups.

In Philemon 1:22, the word functions temporally as an adverb, translated "meanwhile." It marks a transition where Paul asks Philemon to carry out a practical task alongside his consideration of the request regarding Onesimus.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

New Testament authors use hama to signal concurrent actions or shared spiritual reality.

When used in a temporal sense, the word links two ongoing realities. It indicates that while one situation is developing, another related action ought to take place concurrently. For example, an author may request prayer for an ongoing ministry while simultaneously giving instructions for immediate local action.

When used in a relational sense, the word emphasizes collective unity. It describes believers being gathered together, experiencing judgment together, or ultimately living together with Christ. In every case, hama draws distinct elements into immediate proximity, whether in time or in fellowship.

4. Theological Implications

Though hama is a brief transitional word, its placement in apostolic instruction carries meaningful theological implications for Christian life and leadership.

First, it demonstrates the dynamic relationship between prayer and expectant action. Paul states that he trusts he will be released through the prayers of the church, and he immediately asks for a guest room to be prepared. True faith does not treat prayer as a pious wish, but as an effective means through which God works, prompting believers to make practical preparations in expectation of God's answer.

Second, the word illustrates pastoral accountability wrapped in Christian love. By linking the appeal for Onesimus with the preparation for his own arrival, Paul brings the abstract duty of forgiveness into the realm of face-to-face fellowship. Loving accountability in the church operates best within shared personal relationships rather than distant decrees.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:22, Paul writes: "But meanwhile, also prepare a guest room for me, for I trust that through your prayers I shall be granted to you."

The adverb "meanwhile" (hama) anchors Paul's personal request directly to the preceding appeal. Paul has spent the majority of the letter asking Philemon to forgive Onesimus, receive him back not as a slave, but as a beloved brother, and welcome him as he would welcome Paul himself. Right at this decisive moment, Paul adds "meanwhile," turning Philemon's attention to an additional task.

This concurrence carries profound pastoral wisdom. While Philemon processes the letter and decides how to respond regarding Onesimus, he is simultaneously instructed to prepare a room for Paul. The prospect of Paul's imminent arrival meant that Philemon would soon meet Paul in person. The request was not heavy-handed, but it made clear that the restoration of Onesimus was an urgent matter requiring immediate resolution before Paul arrived.

Furthermore, Paul connects his release from prison directly to the prayers of Philemon's household. The word "meanwhile" bridges the gap between the church's spiritual petitions for Paul's freedom and their tangible preparation for his stay.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The temporal connection made by hama highlights the integrity of Christian fellowship. Paul did not treat his friendship with Philemon and his advocacy for Onesimus as separate compartments. By bringing his own future visit into the same temporal space as Onesimus’s return, Paul creates a seamless environment of grace, hospitality, and mutual responsibility.

Additionally, the request for a guest room reveals Paul’s deep value for Christian hospitality. In the early church, opening one's home to traveling ministers and brothers in Christ was a vital expression of gospel unity. Preparing a room was an act of practical faith that honored the communion of saints.
