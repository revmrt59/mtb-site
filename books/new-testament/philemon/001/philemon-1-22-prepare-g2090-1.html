PREPARE - (hetoimázō, Strong's G2090)

Summary:

The Greek verb hetoimazō means to make ready, put in order, or make necessary arrangements for a person or event. It describes concrete actions that prepare physical accommodations, paths, meals, or spiritual conditions. In Philemon 1:22, Paul uses the word to ask Philemon to arrange a guest room for his expected visit, showing his expectation of being released from prison in answer to the church's prayers.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient Mediterranean world, hospitality was a sacred social obligation and a vital element of life in the early church. Travelling ministers, missionaries, and believers relied heavily on local Christians for food, lodging, and protection during their journeys. Preparing accommodations involved securing a suitable room, arranging bedding, and gathering supplies to welcome a guest warmly.

Paul wrote his letter to Philemon while under house arrest or imprisonment, anticipating release in response to the intercessions of his friends. Philemon was a wealthy Christian living in Colosse who hosted a church assembly in his home. By instructing Philemon to make practical arrangements for a guest room, Paul connected his personal hope of freedom with the everyday hospitality of a Christian household.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb hetoimazō refers to the act of making something ready, putting things in order, or taking practical steps to prepare for a coming event or person.

Depending on the context, the word can mean:

  • To prepare physical quarters, accommodations, or lodging for a guest.

  • To prepare food, meals, or a venue for a gathering.

  • To clear or open a pathway for someone's arrival.

  • To arrange or ordain events or places ahead of time through divine purpose.

In Philemon 1:22, the word carries the straightforward sense of making practical household arrangements to host Paul upon his arrival.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, hetoimazō covers both ordinary household preparations and profound spiritual readiness.

In everyday settings, the word describes physical labor, such as preparing meals or arranging rooms for visitors. In prophetic and gospel contexts, it describes opening the heart or removing obstacles to welcome God's work, such as John the Baptist preparing the way for Christ. In cosmic settings, it describes God's sovereign action in preparing eternal blessings, salvation, or judgment.

The common thread in every context is active arrangement. Hetoimazō does not refer to wishful thinking; it denotes tangible action that aligns present circumstances with a expected future event.

4. Theological Implications

While Philemon 1:22 deals with the practical task of preparing a room, the directive carries meaningful spiritual implications:

  • Faith and Action: Expressing faith in God's answers to prayer involves taking concrete action. Expecting release from prison, Paul asked Philemon to act on that expectation by preparing a room.

  • Christian Hospitality: Housing fellow workers in the gospel is a key practice of Christian fellowship. Opening one's home serves the advance of God's kingdom.

  • Providence and Prayer: Paul expected his release to occur through the prayers of the saints, showing how divine sovereignty and human intercession work together.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:22, Paul writes, "But meanwhile, also prepare a guest room for me; for I trust that through your prayers I shall be granted to you."

This instruction comes immediately after Paul’s appeal on behalf of Onesimus, a runaway servant who had become a Christian under Paul's ministry. Paul asked Philemon to receive Onesimus back no longer as a slave, but as a beloved brother.

Asking Philemon to prepare a room serves two primary functions in the letter:

  • Expectation of Release: Paul expresses real hope that God will answer the church's prayers for his freedom.

  • Gentle Accountability: By announcing his planned arrival, Paul gently reminds Philemon that he will soon visit in person. Preparing a room meant Philemon was preparing to welcome Paul himself, adding a warm and personal incentive for Philemon to act graciously toward Onesimus before Paul arrived.

The command is directed specifically to Philemon as the head of the household, charging him with the personal responsibility of making these preparations.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 3:3 — "The voice of one crying in the wilderness: 'Prepare the way of the LORD; make His paths straight.'"

  • Matthew 25:34 — "Then the King will say to those on His right hand, 'Come, you blessed of My Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world.'"

  • Luke 9:52 — "and sent messengers before His face. And as they went, they entered a village of the Samaritans, to prepare for Him."

  • Luke 12:47 — "And that servant who knew his master’s will, and did not prepare himself or do according to his will, shall be beaten with many stripes."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The request to prepare a room demonstrates Paul’s practical mindset. Even while discussing deep spiritual matters of forgiveness, reconciliation, and Christian brotherhood, Paul remains attentive to everyday details like travel plans and lodging. Authentic faith operates in both spiritual devotion and practical arrangements.
