ROOM - (xenía, Strong's G3578)

Summary:

The Greek word xenia refers to hospitality, temporary lodging, or a guest room provided for a visitor. In Philemon 1:22, Paul uses the word to ask Philemon to prepare guest accommodations for him, expressing his confident hope that God will answer their prayers and grant his release from prison to visit them in person.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Mediterranean society, opening one's home to travelers was a fundamental moral and social duty. Inns were often unsafe and unreliable, so travelers relied heavily on personal hospitality. For early Christians, hosting visiting teachers, apostles, and fellow believers was a vital way to support gospel ministry and build up the local church.

Paul writes his letter to Philemon while under Roman imprisonment. He appeals to Philemon on behalf of Onesimus, a runaway slave who had met Paul, come to faith in Christ, and become useful in ministry. As Paul brings his personal letter to a close, he asks Philemon to make practical arrangements to host him, anticipating his eventual release.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Xenia refers to a lodging place, guest quarters, or hospitality offered to a guest. It derives from the Greek word for a stranger or guest (xenos). Rather than referring to a commercial public establishment, the word focuses on private lodging or guest accommodations arranged to welcome someone into a community or household.

In Philemon 1:22, the word refers to a physical room or guest quarters within Philemon's home that Paul asks him to prepare for his upcoming visit.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses xenia to describe personal lodging where ministers stay while conducting gospel work or meeting with people. The term highlights practical host-guest relationships among believers.

Providing lodging (xenia) was not merely offering a bed for the night. It involved offering personal fellowship, safety, sustenance, and direct partnership in the work of the gospel. When believers provided lodging for ministers, their homes became centers for teaching, encouragement, and community life.

4. Theological Implications

Paul’s request for lodging connects spiritual faith with concrete action. He asks the church to pray for his release from prison, and at the same time, he asks Philemon to prepare a physical room for his arrival. True faith actively expects God to answer prayer and prepares practically for the answer.

Additionally, the request reflects the family nature of the early church. Christian fellowship was lived out in real homes around real tables. By staying in Philemon's guest room, Paul would share in the life of Philemon's household as a spiritual father and brother in Christ.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

The request in verse 22 carries gentle pastoral weight within the letter's main argument. Paul has just asked Philemon to forgive Onesimus and receive him no longer as a slave, but as a beloved brother in the Lord.

By asking Philemon to prepare a room for him, Paul announces his intention to visit Philemon's home soon. This friendly notice provides loving accountability. Philemon knows that Paul will soon arrive in person to see firsthand how the household has embraced Onesimus in Christian love.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Acts 28:23 – While under house arrest in Rome, local Jewish leaders came to Paul's "lodging," where he spent the entire day explaining and testifying about the kingdom of God.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Paul’s request shows a healthy balance between divine sovereignty and human planning. Paul trusts that his release depends entirely on God's answer to the believers' prayers, yet he plans responsibly by asking for guest quarters to be prepared ahead of time.
