TRUST - (elpízō, Strong's G1679)

Summary:

The word elpizo means to look forward with expectation, hope, or confidence. In Philemon 1:22, Paul uses this word to express his confident expectation that God will answer the prayers of the believers and grant him release from prison to visit Philemon.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In everyday Greek usage, waiting for a future event could involve either positive expectation or anxious uncertainty, depending on the circumstances. In the New Testament, however, forward-looking expectation derives its strength from the character, promises, and power of God.

When Paul wrote his letter to Philemon, he was in prison, likely in Rome. Despite being confined in chains, Paul was not paralyzed by doubt or despair. Instead, he maintained an active expectation of freedom and personal ministry. In Philemon 1:22, he asks his friend to prepare lodging for him, demonstrating a practical and expectant faith grounded in God's providence and the power of church prayer.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb elpizo means to expect, hope for, or look forward to something with confidence. It describes a mindset that actively anticipates a future outcome rather than living in resignation.

When God or His promises are the object, elpizo carries the sense of quiet, settled confidence. When used of human plans and travel, it describes a hopeful anticipation that remains subject to God’s sovereign control. In this passage, the word refers to Paul's expectation that he will soon be released from custody and reunited with his Christian brother.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, elpizo describes several shades of expectation:

  • Confident reliance on God for salvation and future resurrection, where hope rests on divine promises.

  • Hopeful expectation regarding personal circumstances, travel plans, or spiritual fruit.

  • Misplaced hope, such as trusting in wealth, human influence, or self-righteousness rather than God.

In Paul's writings, the word often connects present endurance with future joy. Whether speaking of ultimate eternal glory or a short-term plan to visit a friend, Paul’s expectations are anchored in God's faithful oversight.

4. Theological Implications

Paul’s statement highlights the relationship between divine sovereignty and prayer. Paul does not view his future as determined by Roman officials or random circumstances. Rather, he sees his expected release as an answer to the intercessory prayers of the church.

This demonstrates that biblical hope is not passive fatalism. Believers pray with expectation because they know God uses the prayers of His people to accomplish His purposes. At the same time, this expectation remains humble, recognizing that all human plans unfold under God's supreme authority.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:22, Paul writes, "prepare a guest room for me, for I trust that through your prayers I shall be granted to you." This request comes immediately after Paul appeals to Philemon to welcome back his runaway bondservant, Onesimus, no longer as a slave, but as a beloved brother in Christ.

By expressing his expectation of a personal visit, Paul gently reinforces his appeal. Philemon knows that Paul hopes to see him face-to-face, which provides a loving, quiet motivation for Philemon to act with grace toward Onesimus. Furthermore, Paul does not demand his release as a personal right; he frames it as a gracious gift from God ("granted to you") given in response to the faithful prayers of Philemon's household.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 12:21 — "And in His name Gentiles will trust."

  • Luke 24:21 — "But we were hoping that it was He who was going to redeem Israel."

  • 2 Corinthians 1:10 — "...in whom we trust that He will still deliver us."

  • Philippians 2:19 — "But I trust in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy to you shortly..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Paul’s command to prepare a guest room shows that biblical expectation leads to practical action. Paul does not wait until he is formally released to make preparations; he lives with the active conviction that God hears prayer. Furthermore, by framing his release as something "granted" to the church, Paul highlights that answer to prayer is a shared joy that builds up the body of Christ.
