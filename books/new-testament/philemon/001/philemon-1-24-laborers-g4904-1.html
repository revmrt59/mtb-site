LABORERS - (synergós, Strong's G4904)

Summary:

The word sunergos refers to a fellow worker, companion in labor, or partner in a common task. In Philemon 1:24, Paul uses this term to describe Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, and Luke as his active co-workers in gospel ministry. By placing these names at the close of his letter to Philemon, Paul highlights the shared responsibility and unity of the team supporting his prison ministry, reinforcing his appeal for Philemon to receive Onesimus as a brother in Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word sunergos is a compound term combining the preposition sun (meaning "with" or "together") and ergon (meaning "work" or "labor"). In ordinary ancient Greek, it described business partners, military companions, or laborers working together to accomplish a shared project. It emphasized active cooperation toward a common objective rather than passive association.

In the New Testament, Paul adopted this practical everyday word to describe the collaborative nature of Christian ministry. Rather than viewing ministry as a solo enterprise, Paul consistently worked alongside a network of men and women whom he valued as genuine partners. In Philemon 1:24, the term appears in the plural (nominative plural masculine), referring to a specific group of four companions who were serving alongside Paul during his imprisonment.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word sunergos means a fellow worker, partner, or companion in labor. It describes someone who actively shares in exertion and responsibility to complete a task.

When applied to gospel ministry, sunergos indicates more than mere friendship or casual association. It identifies individuals who participate directly in the toil, sacrifice, and spiritual labor of spreading the gospel and caring for the church. In Philemon 1:24, the word refers directly to Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, and Luke, identifying them as Paul's trusted partners in ministry while he was in chains.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, sunergos appears in contexts that highlight Christian partnership and mutual support. Paul uses the word in two main ways:

First, he uses it to identify human co-workers who labor alongside him in planting and strengthening churches. Individuals such as Timothy, Titus, Priscilla, Aquila, Mark, and Luke are called by this title. This shows that early Christian mission relied on dedicated teamwork rather than single isolated leaders.

Second, Scripture elevates the concept of labor by describing believers as co-workers with God or partners working together for the truth. In this sense, human labor in the gospel aligns with God's own sovereign purpose, granting dignity and eternal significance to local church ministry and missionary effort.

4. Theological Implications

The use of sunergos in the New Testament highlights several important biblical truths about ministry and the church:

  • Ministry as Collective Labor: The gospel is advanced through shared work. God designs the church so that believers depend on one another, combining different gifts and roles to fulfill Christ's commission.

  • Unity Across Backgrounds: The team named in Philemon 1:24 represents diverse backgrounds and ministry histories. Gospel partnership creates deep unity among people who might otherwise have little in common.

  • The Dignity of Gospel Service: Describing believers as fellow laborers shows that every task supporting the spread of the gospel—whether preaching, practical care, or personal encouragement—holds real value in God's kingdom.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:24, Paul is concluding his personal appeal to Philemon regarding Onesimus, a runaway slave who had become a Christian under Paul's ministry. Paul asks Philemon to forgive Onesimus and welcome him back not merely as a servant, but as a beloved brother in Christ.

The placement of sunergos here carries intentional weight:

  • Bookended Partnership: Paul begins the letter by calling Philemon himself a "fellow laborer" (v. 1) and ends by sending greetings from his "fellow laborers" in Rome (v. 24). This structural detail reminds Philemon that he and Paul belong to the same ministry family and serve the same Lord.

  • A Shared Voice: By including Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, and Luke as his co-workers, Paul shows that his request is supported by a trusted community of believers. Philemon is encouraged to act in harmony with the spirit of gospel partnership that defines Paul's entire ministry network.

  • Ministry in Chains: Though Paul was a prisoner, he was not isolated. These four men actively labored with him in Rome, proving that imprisonment could not stop the collaborative work of the gospel.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Philemon 1:1 — "Paul, a prisoner of Christ Jesus, and Timothy our brother, To Philemon our beloved friend and fellow laborer,"

  • Romans 16:3 — "Greet Priscilla and Aquila, my fellow workers in Christ Jesus,"

  • 1 Corinthians 3:9 — "For we are God’s fellow workers; you are God’s field, you are God’s building."

  • Philippians 4:3 — "And I urge you also, true companion, help these women who labored with me in the gospel, with Clement also, and the rest of my fellow workers, whose names are in the Book of Life."

  • 3 John 1:8 — "We therefore ought to receive such, that we may become fellow workers for the truth."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The four men designated as "laborers" in Philemon 1:24 offer a vivid picture of the reality of Christian ministry teams:

  • Restoration: John Mark is listed as a co-worker here, despite having previously deserted Paul and Barnabas on an earlier missionary journey (Acts 15:37–39). His presence in Rome shows that past failure does not permanently disqualify someone from effective ministry partnership.

  • Faithfulness and Human Fragility: Luke remained a loyal companion through Paul's final trials (2 Timothy 4:11), and Aristarchus shared Paul's imprisonment (Colossians 4:10). Conversely, Demas is included here as a co-worker, but later Scripture records that he deserted Paul because he loved the present world (2 Timothy 4:10). This contrast provides a sobering reminder that being numbered among gospel laborers requires ongoing endurance and faithfulness.
