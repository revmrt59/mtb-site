PEACE - ( eirene , Strong's G1515 )

Summary:

In Philemon 1:3, "peace" serves as a foundational apostolic greeting representing divine wholeness, relational harmony, and well-being that flows exclusively from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, bridging the gap between estranged individuals like Paul, Philemon, and Onesimus.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The Greek term carries a rich background rooted in both the Greek concept of absence of war and the robust Hebrew concept of shalom.

  • In classical Greek, it primarily described a state of national tranquility or a treaty ending hostilities.

  • In the Septuagint and New Testament, it expands to signify comprehensive well-being, safety, health, and harmonious relationships anchored in divine favor.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • A state of national or individual tranquility; exemption from the rage and havoc of conflict.

  • Harmony, concord, and security in human relationships, particularly within the community of believers.

  • Messianic salvation, encompassing spiritual peace with God and the ultimate wholeness brought by Christ.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • It is frequently used in New Testament epistolary openings as a prayer-wish combining the Greek greeting of grace (charis) with the Hebrew greeting of wholeness (eirene).

  • The word paints a picture of a settled soul and a reconciled community where hostility, division, and anxiety are replaced by divine calm and mutual accord.

4. Theological Implications

  • True peace is never merely a human achievement or a cessation of outward friction, but a divine gift originating jointly from "God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ."

  • In the context of Philemon—a letter dealing with a runaway slave and a wronged master—the invocation of peace lays the necessary theological groundwork for forgiveness, reconciliation, and Christian brotherhood.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • Placed at the very beginning of the letter in the standard apostolic salutation, peace is not just a polite pleasantry.

  • It sets the tone for the appeal Paul is about to make, subtly reminding Philemon that the peace he enjoys with God demands a corresponding peaceful resolution in his relationship with Onesimus.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 14:27 - "Peace I leave with you, My peace I give to you; not as the world gives do I give to you."

  • Romans 5:1 - "Therefore, having been justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ."

  • Ephesians 2:14 - "For He Himself is our peace, who has made both one, and has broken down the middle wall of separation."

  • Philippians 4:7 - "and the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The pairing of grace and peace in apostolic greetings reflects the vertical dimension of divine grace leading to the horizontal dimension of relational peace.

  • In Philemon, this greeting transforms a social crisis into an opportunity to display the reconciling power of the gospel.
