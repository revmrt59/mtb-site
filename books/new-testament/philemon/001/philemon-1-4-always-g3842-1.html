ALWAYS - (pántote, Strong's G3842)

Summary:

The Greek adverb pantote means at all times, always, or on every occasion. It describes actions, conditions, or habits that take place regularly or without failing. In Philemon 1:4, Paul uses the word to describe his faithful practice of giving thanks and remembering Philemon whenever he brings his prayers before God.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Epistle to Philemon is a personal letter written by the apostle Paul while in prison. Philemon was a leader in the church at Colossae who opened his home for believers to meet. Paul wrote to him on behalf of Onesimus, a runaway servant who had come to faith under Paul's ministry.

Before Paul makes his sensitive request concerning Onesimus, he opens the letter with a customary expression of thanksgiving and prayer. In verse 4, Paul assures Philemon that he remembers him consistently before the Lord. Using pantote sets a tone of warm Christian affection, letting Philemon know that his faithful service to the saints is a recurring theme in Paul's personal prayer life.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Pantote is formed by combining the Greek words for "all" (pas) and "at that time" (tote). It simply means "at all times," "always," or "on every occasion."

The word does not mean doing an action every single second without stopping for rest or work. Instead, it refers to a practice that happens consistently whenever the proper time or occasion arises. It indicates an unbroken habit, a steady posture, or an ongoing routine.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses pantote in several distinct contexts:

  • Perfect Obedience: It describes the uninterrupted devotion of Jesus toward His Heavenly Father, as well as the Father's constant willingness to hear the Son.

  • Regular Spiritual Habits: It describes the steady practices of the Christian life, such as praying without giving up, praising God, and giving thanks in all circumstances.

  • Persistent Conditions: It refers to situations or truths that remain ongoing or continuous throughout a given period.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical call to do something "always"—such as praying or giving thanks—points to an ongoing posture of the heart rather than an impossible physical demand. True Christian prayer is not a short-lived reaction to trouble, but a continuous lifestyle of dependence on God.

Giving thanks always demonstrates that gratitude is grounded in God's unchanging character and grace rather than fleeting earthly circumstances. Additionally, Paul's commitment to pray always for fellow believers reflects the constant care God's people ought to have for one another.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:4, Paul writes, "I thank my God, making mention of you always in my prayers." The word pantote directly modifies Paul's practice of giving thanks and remembering Philemon.

Paul is not claiming that he spends twenty-four hours a day in non-stop prayer. Rather, he means that whenever he enters his regular times of prayer, Philemon is consistently brought before God. Philemon has a permanent place in Paul's intercessions.

This detail is important for the passage. Paul establishes a foundation of genuine love and spiritual partnership before bringing up the delicate matter of Onesimus. By telling Philemon that he prays for him always, Paul confirms that his gratitude for Philemon's faith and love is real, ongoing, and deeply rooted in prayer.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 18:1 — Jesus spoke a parable to teach that people ought "always" to pray and not lose heart.

  • John 8:29 — Jesus declared that the Father had not left Him alone because He "always" did those things that pleased Him.

  • 1 Corinthians 1:4 — Paul expressed his steady gratitude for the church, writing that he thanked God "always" concerning them for God's grace.

  • Ephesians 5:20 — Believers are instructed to give thanks "always" for all things to God the Father in the name of Jesus Christ.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the first century, daily prayer was often structured around regular hours throughout the day. When Paul writes about praying pantote, he is reflecting a life disciplined by regular, rhythmic communion with God.

It is also worth noting how Paul connects thanksgiving with intercession. He does not merely present a list of demands to God; his prayer routine is filled with continuous thanksgiving for the work of God in the lives of fellow Christians.
