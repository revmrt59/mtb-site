THANK - ( eucharisteo , Strong's G2168 )

Summary:

The Greek verb translated "thank" in Philemon 1:4 is eucharisteo, which carries the core meaning of giving thanks, expressing gratitude, or being thankful. Far beyond a polite social custom, this word in the New Testament is deeply tied to the recognition of God's grace (charis) and goodness, serving as a fundamental posture of the believer's heart in prayer and fellowship.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The term is built upon two distinct elements: the prefix eu, meaning "good" or "well," and charizomai or charis, meaning "grace," "favor," or "gift."

  • Historically and etymologically, to give thanks (eucharisteo) is literally to acknowledge that one has received a good or gracious gift (eu + charis) and to respond with a well-wishing sense of gratitude toward the giver.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • To be thankful, to give thanks, or to express gratitude.

  • To offer a prayer of thanksgiving, particularly directed toward God for His unearned favor, blessings, or the spiritual state of fellow believers.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • In Hellenistic Greek and the New Testament, eucharisteo is frequently used in the context of meals, miraculous provisions, and especially corporate or private prayer.

  • While the word carries a rich background of grace, it functions in everyday New Testament Greek simply as the standard verb for expressing gratitude.

4. Theological Implications

  • True Christian thanksgiving is never merely self-generated optimism; it is a theological response rooted in the recognition of divine grace.

  • When Paul gives thanks for Philemon, he views Philemon's faith and love not as human achievement, but as evidence of God's grace actively working in a believer's life.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Philemon 1:4, Paul tells Philemon, "I thank my God, making mention of you always in my prayers."

  • Here, the action of thanking God sets a warm, affectionate, and pastoral tone for the letter, immediately binding the apostle and his host together in shared spiritual fellowship before addressing a delicate and difficult social situation regarding the runaway slave Onesimus.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 17:16 - "and fell down on his face at His feet, giving Him thanks. And he was a Samaritan."

  • Romans 1:8 - "First, I thank my God through Jesus Christ for you all, that your faith is spoken of throughout the whole world."

  • 1 Corinthians 1:4 - "I thank my God always concerning you for the grace of God which was given to you by Christ Jesus."

  • Philippians 1:3 - "I thank my God upon every remembrance of you."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Paul routinely begins his letters—with the notable exception of Galatians—with an expression of thanksgiving for the recipients, demonstrating that pastoral correction and robust theology must always be bathed in genuine appreciation for God's work in others.

  • The noun form derived from this root, eucharistia, eventually became an early Christian designation for the Lord's Supper, highlighting how central thanksgiving is to Christian worship and community life.
