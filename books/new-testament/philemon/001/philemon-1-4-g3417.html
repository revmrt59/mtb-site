MENTION - ( mneia , Strong's G3417 )

Summary:

The word mention translates a Greek noun that fundamentally means remembrance, memory, or mention, capturing the warm practice of bringing someone to mind in prayer. In Philemon 1:4, Paul assures Philemon that he continually recalls him before God, establishing a posture of deep pastoral affection and spiritual solidarity.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The root behind the term relates to memory, recollection, and the mental act of retaining or bringing someone to mind.

  • In the ancient Greco-Roman world, epistolary openings frequently included health wishes and expressions of remembrance of friends, but Paul elevates this convention into genuine intercessory prayer before the throne of God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The Greek noun mneia denotes a remembrance, recollection, mention, or record.

  • In biblical Greek, it frequently appears in connection with prayer, signifying the act of bringing a person or community explicitly to mind before God.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The term carries the imagery of holding someone in active thought rather than letting them slip into oblivion.

  • Paired with verbs of prayer or making, it portrays the believer's mind as a sanctuary where brothers and sisters in Christ are regularly named aloud to the Father.

4. Theological Implications

  • Christian fellowship transcends physical distance; even when apart, believers are knit together through the ministry of intercession enabled by the Holy Spirit.

  • Prayerful remembrance acknowledges that our primary connection to one another is mediated through our shared relationship with God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • Paul uses the word to soften and prepare Philemon's heart before making a delicate appeal regarding the runaway slave Onesimus.

  • By assuring Philemon of his constant prayers, Paul builds relational trust and demonstrates that his correction is rooted in genuine love rather than apostolic demand.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Romans 1:9 - "For God is my witness, whom I serve with my spirit in the gospel of His Son, that without ceasing I make mention of you always in my prayers."

  • Ephesians 1:16 - "...do not cease to give thanks for you, making mention of you in my prayers..."

  • 1 Thessalonians 1:2 - "We give thanks to God always for you all, making mention of you in our prayers..."

  • 2 Timothy 1:3 - "I thank God, whom I serve with a pure conscience, as my forefathers did, as without ceasing I have remembrance of you in my prayers night and day..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The phrase translated "making mention" in several Pauline openings highlights a disciplined, habitual practice of intercession rather than a passing thought.

  • Pastors and teachers can draw from this word an example of how to hold their congregations and coworkers affectionately in prayer.
