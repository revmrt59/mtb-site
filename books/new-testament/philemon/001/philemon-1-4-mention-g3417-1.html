MENTION - (mneía, Strong's G3417)

Summary:

The Greek noun mneia refers to memory, remembrance, or the deliberate naming of someone in speech or prayer. In New Testament usage, it frequently appears in apostolic thanksgivings, describing the act of bringing specific believers to mind before the Lord. In Philemon 1:4, Paul uses the word to assure Philemon that he regularly mentions him by name in his personal prayers, anchoring his gratitude for Philemon's love and faith in direct, intentional intercession.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The noun mneia belongs to a ancient family of Greek words associated with memory and mental recall. In broad usage, the term signified recalling a person or event, keeping a memory alive, or making explicit mention of someone in conversation or personal correspondence.

When early Christian writers used mneia, particularly in letters to churches and individual believers, the word took on a warm, intercessory character. Rather than serving as a cold mental log or a formal polite gesture, mneia described the active bringing of brothers and sisters in Christ into the presence of God during prayer. In the apostolic letters, making "mention" of fellow believers is a personal, deliberate practice of prayerful care and Christian fellowship that bridges physical distance.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word mneia refers to remembrance, memory, or the act of mentioning someone or something.

The noun carries two related shades of meaning:

  • Remembrance or memory: keeping someone in mind or holding a grateful recollection of their person and service.

  • Explicit mention: verbally bringing someone's name forward, particularly within prayer and thanksgiving.

In the New Testament, mneia almost always appears alongside a verb of making or doing, forming a standard expression that translates as "making mention" or "holding in remembrance." Here in Philemon 1:4, the noun identifies the deliberate content of Paul's prayer practice: he is consciously bringing Philemon before God in his personal devotions.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, mneia is consistently linked with prayer, thanksgiving, and pastoral affection. It appears predominantly in the opening sections of apostolic letters, establishing a personal bond between the author and the recipients before addressing specific instructions or exhortations.

The term highlights intentionality. Biblical prayer is not a vague sentiment; it involves bringing real individuals, with real names and specific circumstances, before God. When Scripture speaks of making mneia of someone, it emphasizes that the individual is actively remembered and named in prayer. The word reflects both mental faithfulness and spiritual intercession.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of mneia carries clear theological significance for Christian fellowship and intercession:

  • Prayer as active fellowship: Bringing someone to mind in prayer is an act of Christian love that spans physical distance. It demonstrates that the unity of believers is sustained through shared access to God.

  • Gratitude in intercession: In apostolic writing, mentioning believers in prayer is regularly paired with thanksgiving. Intercession is driven by joy and appreciation for what God is working in and through others.

  • Pastoral care and accountability: Explicitly naming individuals before God grounds spiritual leadership in prayerful concern rather than simple administrative duty or personal influence.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:4, Paul writes from prison to Philemon, a beloved friend and leader in the church at Colossae. The apostle begins his letter with a deep expression of gratitude: "I thank my God, making mention of you always in my prayers."

This statement serves a vital purpose in the context of the letter. Paul is preparing to make a delicate and profound request regarding Onesimus, Philemon's bondservant who had fled but has now become a believer in Christ. Before Paul presents his appeal, he assures Philemon of his constant, affectionate prayers.

The phrase shows that Paul's appreciation for Philemon is not mere flattery aimed at manipulating him, but a genuine, regular practice of prayerful intercession. Paul explicitly mentions Philemon because he regularly gives thanks for Philemon's faith in the Lord Jesus and his love toward all the saints. By framing his letter with this warm, prayerful remembrance, Paul grounds his upcoming request in shared grace, love, and spiritual partnership.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Romans 1:9 — "For God is my witness, whom I serve with my spirit in the gospel of His Son, that without ceasing I make mention of you always in my prayers..."

  • Ephesians 1:16 — "...do not cease to give thanks for you, making mention of you in my prayers..."

  • Philippians 1:3 — "I thank my God upon every remembrance of you..."

  • 1 Thessalonians 1:2 — "We give thanks to God always for you all, making mention of you in our prayers..."

  • 2 Timothy 1:3 — "I thank God, whom I serve with a pure conscience, as my forefathers did, as without ceasing I remember you in my prayers night and day..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Philemon 1:4, the noun mneia functions as the direct object of Paul's prayerful action. The grammatical structure emphasizes that Philemon himself—his name and person—is the direct focus of Paul's prayer.

Furthermore, Paul links this mention with the word "always," showing that intercession was not an occasional afterthought for the apostle, but an ongoing practice. Even while confined in chains, Paul's mind and prayers were actively engaged with the spiritual welfare of specific individuals across the early church.
