LOVE - ( agapē , Strong's G26 )

Summary:

In Philemon 1:5, the word translated "love" is agapē, which refers to the selfless, sacrificial, and volitional goodwill that actively seeks the highest good of another. It is not merely a passing emotion or affection, but a deliberate choice of the will expressed in action. In this opening commendation, Paul praises Philemon because this supernatural love is actively flowing out of his faith in Christ and making a tangible difference in the lives of the Christian community.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In classical Greek, agapē and its verb form agapaō were relatively rare and lacked the warmth of phileo (brotherly affection) or the intensity of eraō (romantic desire). Classical writers often used it to describe a general, somewhat colorless regard, preference, or contentment. However, the New Testament writers adopted and elevated this word to describe the unique, sacrificial love of God. It became a distinctively Christian word picture of a love that is independent of the worthiness of its object, initiated by the giver rather than drawn out by the beauty or value of the recipient.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Agapē is benevolent affection and active goodwill. It is a love of choice, commitment, and action. Unlike emotional responses that depend on feelings, agapē is guided by the intellect and the will, choosing to value, care for, and serve others regardless of their response, merit, or reciprocal behavior. It is the highest form of love, defined fundamentally by self-giving.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The New Testament uses agapē to describe the very nature of God and the primary fruit of the Holy Spirit in a believer's life. It is depicted not as a passive state of mind, but as an active force. It "suffers long and is kind," "does not seek its own," and "never fails." Its supreme demonstration is the cross of Jesus Christ, where love was visualized through the ultimate sacrifice. It acts as a binding agent that holds the Christian community together in perfect unity.

4. Theological Implications

  • Agapē is the foundational characteristic of the Christian life because it reflects the character of God Himself, who is love. It serves as the horizontal proof of a vertical relationship with God. True agapē is impossible to generate through human effort alone; it is a supernatural fruit produced in the believer's heart by the Holy Spirit. When believers display this love, they put the invisible God on display to a watching world.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:5, Paul commends Philemon for his agapē, which is paired with "faith." This love is not a private, hidden sentiment; Paul has "heard" of it because it has practical, observable results. Philemon's agapē is active and visible, finding its expression "toward all the saints" by refreshing their hearts and opening his home. By praising Philemon's reputation for practical, self-giving love toward the body of Christ, Paul masterfully prepares the ground for his difficult pastoral appeal: asking Philemon to extend this same sacrificial agapē to his runaway slave, Onesimus, welcoming him back no longer as a slave, but as a beloved brother.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 15:13 - "Greater love has no one than this, than to lay down one’s life for his friends."

  • Romans 5:8 - "But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us."

  • 1 Corinthians 13:1 - "Though I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not love, I have become sounding brass or a clanging cymbal."

  • 1 John 4:8 - "He who does not love does not know God, for God is love."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The structure of Philemon 1:5 features a literary device known as a chiasm (an ABBA pattern). While the verse reads "love and faith... toward the Lord Jesus and toward all the saints," the concepts cross over in their primary application: "faith" is directed toward the "Lord Jesus," and "love" is directed toward "all the saints." However, they remain inseparable; true faith in Christ always expresses itself in active, sacrificial love for His people.
