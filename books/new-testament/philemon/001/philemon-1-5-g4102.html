FAITH - ( pistis , Strong's G4102 )

Summary:

In Philemon 1:5, the word "faith" (pistis) describes a deep, personal trust and absolute reliance upon the Lord Jesus Christ that manifests as active faithfulness toward God's people. It is not a static intellectual assent, but a living, relational confidence that anchors the soul in Christ and naturally flows outward in love and service to the church. For Philemon, this faith was so vibrant and visible that news of it traveled to Paul in prison, serving as a model of how vertical devotion to Christ directly transforms horizontal relationships with fellow believers.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The Greek noun pistis is derived from the verb peitho, which means "to persuade" or "to be persuaded." In classical Greek and historical usage, the word carried the idea of trust, reliability, confidence, and a pledge of fidelity. It represented the binding assurance that made covenants and treaties possible between parties. When the New Testament authors adopted the word, they infused it with a richer, relational reality. Instead of referring merely to a reliable business transaction or intellectual persuasion, pistis became the definitive term for a believer's total surrender, reliance, and trust in the character, promises, and redemptive work of God in Jesus Christ.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Pistis encompasses three distinct but closely integrated dimensions of meaning:

  • Trust/Confidence: An active reliance upon the truth of God's character and promises, placing one's eternal security and daily life entirely in His hands.

  • Fidelity/Faithfulness: The character trait of being trustworthy, loyal, and reliable in one's relationships with God and others.

  • The Christian Creed: Occasionally, the term refers objectively to the content of Christian belief, "the faith" once for all delivered to the saints.

In Philemon 1:5, the primary sense is Philemon's personal, unwavering trust and confidence directed toward the person of the Lord Jesus, which simultaneously produces a life of active loyalty and faithfulness toward the body of Christ.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the New Testament, pistis is described using dynamic imagery that highlights its active nature:

  • A Shield: It is a protective shield that extinguishes the fiery darts of the wicked one (Ephesians 6:16).

  • A Breastplate: Paired with love, faith protects the vital organs of the believer's spiritual life (1 Thessalonians 5:8).

  • An Active Power: Faith is depicted not as a passive possession, but as a force that "works through love" (Galatians 5:6) and "overcomes the world" (1 John 5:4).

  • A Root and Foundation: It is the starting point of Christian character, upon which virtue, knowledge, and self-control are built (2 Peter 1:5).

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, pistis is the sole instrument through which human beings receive the free gift of justification. It is not a meritorious work that earns salvation; rather, it is the empty hand of the soul reaching out to receive the finished work of Christ.

  • Relational Nexus: True biblical faith is inherently relational. It cannot exist in isolation. It binds the believer to Christ in a union that immediately alters how the believer relates to everyone else.

  • Inseparable Companions: In Paul's theology, faith (pistis) and love (agape) are inseparable. Faith is the root, and love is the fruit. Where genuine trust in Christ exists, sacrificial love for the saints will inevitably follow.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:5, Paul commends Philemon, "hearing of your love and faith which you have toward the Lord Jesus and toward all the saints."

  • The Chiastic Structure: The verse employs a literary device known as a chiasm (an ABBA pattern). Philemon’s love (A) and faith (B) are directed toward the Lord Jesus (B') and all the saints (A'). This means Philemon’s vertical faith is rooted in the Lord Jesus, while his horizontal love is poured out toward the saints.

  • Faith Expressed as Faithfulness: Some interpreters suggest that pistis here also carries the nuance of "faithfulness." Understood this way, Philemon’s trust in Jesus expresses itself as reliable, covenantal faithfulness toward his fellow believers.

  • The Appeal's Foundation: This commendation is crucial for Paul's upcoming appeal. Paul is about to ask Philemon to do something incredibly difficult in the ancient Roman world: to forgive and receive his runaway slave, Onesimus, as a brother in Christ. By highlighting Philemon’s famous pistis (faith/faithfulness), Paul gently reminds him that his vertical devotion to Christ must govern his horizontal treatment of Onesimus.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Ephesians 2:8: "For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God,"

  • Hebrews 11:1: "Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen."

  • Galatians 5:6: "For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision nor uncircumcision avails anything, but faith working through love."

  • Romans 10:17: "So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Intellect, Emotion, and Will: True pistis involves the whole person. It requires notitia (understanding the facts of the gospel), assensus (agreeing that these facts are true), and fiducia (personal trust and reliance upon those facts). James 2:19 warns that even the demons have intellectual agreement (assensus), but they lack saving trust (fiducia).

  • The Sharing of Faith: In the next verse (Philemon 1:6), Paul prays "that the sharing of your faith may become effective." The word for sharing is koinonia (fellowship/partnership). Philemon's personal faith was meant to be shared in active partnership with others, proving that biblical faith is never a private matter but a communal reality.
