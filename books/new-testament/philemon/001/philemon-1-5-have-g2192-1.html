HAVE - (échō, Strong's G2192)

Summary:

The Greek verb echō fundamentally means to possess, hold, retain, or experience something. In Philemon 1:5, Paul uses this word to describe the personal love and faith that Philemon possesses toward the Lord Jesus Christ and toward fellow believers.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Apostle Paul wrote his short epistle to Philemon while imprisoned, likely in Rome. Philemon was a beloved Christian leader in Colosse in whose home a local church gathered. Paul opens his letter by thanking God for Philemon after receiving reports of his active faith and personal love.

In Philemon 1:5, Paul highlights the core spiritual attributes that define Philemon's life. The verb echō describes the inward reality and practical possession of those virtues. Before Paul makes his specific request regarding Onesimus—a runaway servant who had become a Christian—he affirms that Philemon already possesses the dynamic love and faith necessary to do what is right.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb echō is a basic Greek term meaning to have, hold, possess, or maintain. It can refer to physical ownership, such as possessing property, money, or food. It also frequently describes non-physical states, such as having peace, possessing a condition like illness, or holding specific inner convictions and spiritual character.

In Philemon 1:5, the word describes holding or possessing spiritual graces. Rather than describing a fleeting feeling, the verb speaks of an established, ongoing possession of love and faith that shapes how a person relates to God and others.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, echō functions in several distinct ways depending on the subject and object:

  • Physical Possession: Holding material items or physical gifts (such as having loaves of bread or financial resources).

  • Spiritual Relation: Possessing a relationship with God, as in having the Father and the Son, or having eternal life through faith in Christ.

  • Internal Disposition: Maintaining attitudes or spiritual qualities, such as having peace, hope, confidence, or love.

  • Personal Condition: Experiencing a physical or spiritual state, such as having a physical infirmity or having rest.

In passages describing Christian character, the word highlights that spiritual virtues are real possessions planted in the believer's heart by God's grace and maintained in daily practice.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of true love and faith in a believer's life is a mark of genuine salvation. Scripture teaches that believers do not produce these virtues from human effort alone; rather, they are gifts from God that become the believer's active spiritual property.

Holding faith in the Lord Jesus and love toward all saints shows the direct link between vertical devotion to Christ and horizontal care for fellow believers. True faith in God produces tangible love for God's people.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:5, Paul writes of "hearing of your love and faith which you have toward the Lord Jesus and toward all the saints." The statement directly addresses Philemon ("you have"), highlighting his personal ownership of these virtues.

This detail is crucial for the argument of the letter. Paul is preparing to ask Philemon to do something difficult: forgive Onesimus and welcome him back as a brother in Christ. By affirming that Philemon already possesses love and faith, Paul grounds his coming appeal in Philemon's established Christian character. Paul does not ask Philemon to manufacture a virtue he lacks, but to express the very love and faith he already holds in Christ.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 6:40 — "And this is the will of Him who sent Me, that everyone who sees the Son and believes in Him may have everlasting life..."

  • John 15:13 — "Greater love has no one than this, than to lay down one’s life for his friends."

  • 2 Corinthians 3:4 — "And we have such trust through Christ toward God."

  • 2 John 1:9 — "He who abides in the doctrine of Christ has both the Father and the Son."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The grammar of the verse directly addresses Philemon in the singular. Paul singles him out to affirm that his personal faith and love are known and appreciated by others.

Furthermore, Paul pairs Philemon's love and faith together. While faith rests on the Lord Jesus, love extends out to "all the saints." Possessing faith toward Christ inevitably expands a believer's heart to possess and express love for the entire Christian community.
