HEARING - (akoúō, Strong's G191)

Summary:

The Greek verb akouō fundamentally means to hear, listen, or receive news and reports. In Philemon 1:5, the word describes Paul receiving news while in prison concerning Philemon’s love and faith. This report prompts Paul to give thanks to God continuously and forms the personal backdrop for his upcoming appeal regarding Onesimus.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The verb akouō is a standard terms in ancient Greek for auditory perception and communication. In everyday ancient life, the concept ranged from physically hearing a sound to receiving news from afar or giving careful attention to spoken instruction.

In the early church, news about individual believers and distant congregations circulated through traveling companions, letters, and personal testimonies. Writing as a prisoner, Paul opens his letter to Philemon by noting that reports of Philemon's personal faith and active love toward Christ and fellow believers regularly reached him.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb akouō can refer to:

  • Physically perceiving sound with the ears.

  • Receiving news, reports, or oral accounts from someone else.

  • Listening with attention, comprehension, or obedience.

In Philemon 1:5, akouō is used in the sense of receiving news or reports. Paul indicates that he has been informed about Philemon’s spiritual character and ongoing conduct within the Christian community.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the New Testament, akouō appears in a wide range of contexts:

  • It describes hearing spoken statements or auditory events directly (Matthew 2:3).

  • It carries the sense of listening attentively or heeding divine truth (Luke 11:28; Revelation 3:20).

  • It is used for mental understanding, such as evaluating instruction (John 6:60).

  • It signifies receiving reports from distant places regarding what God is doing (Acts 11:1).

In Philemon 1:5, the grammatical form functions as a participle linked to Paul’s prayers and thanksgiving. It explains the cause of Paul’s gratitude: as reports of Philemon's love and faith reach Paul, he offers praise to God.

4. Theological Implications

The transmission of good news about believers across geographical boundaries underscores the spiritual unity of the early church.

  • Genuine faith produces visible evidence that generates a report among God's people.

  • News of spiritual growth in others serves as a catalyst for prayer and thanksgiving among church leaders.

  • Christian encouragement relies on sharing reports of God's grace active in individual lives.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:4–5, Paul writes, "I thank my God, making mention of you always in my prayers, hearing of your love and faith which you have toward the Lord Jesus and toward all the saints."

Although Paul is imprisoned, his heart is refreshed by reports coming from the churches. The word "hearing" explains why Paul consistently remembers Philemon in prayer with gratitude. Philemon’s devotion to Christ is expressed in practical love toward fellow believers.

This report provides the foundation for Paul’s subsequent appeal. Before Paul asks Philemon to receive Onesimus—a runaway servant who became a believer through Paul's ministry—he affirms Philemon's established reputation. The news of Philemon’s love and faith is verified evidence of grace, giving Paul confidence in making his appeal.

  • 2 Timothy 1:13 – "Hold fast the pattern of sound words which you have heard from me, in faith and love which are in Christ Jesus."

  • Revelation 3:20 – "Behold, I stand at the door and knock. If anyone hears My voice and opens the door, I will come in to him and dine with him, and he with Me."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Information in the Roman world traveled along trade routes through trusted messengers. Reports of Philemon's faith reached Paul despite the physical boundary of imprisonment.

  • The report Paul received was two-fold: faith directed toward the Lord Jesus and love directed toward all the saints. This balanced report highlights genuine Christian maturity, where personal faith in Christ results in love for His people.
