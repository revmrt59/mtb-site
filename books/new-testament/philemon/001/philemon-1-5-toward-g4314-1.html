TOWARD - (prós, Strong's G4314)

Summary:

The Greek word pros is a common preposition that denotes direction, motion, or orientation toward a person, place, or object. Depending on its context, it can mean "to," "toward," "with," or "before." It often highlights direct movement or relational orientation directed at a specific target.

In Philemon 1:5, pros translated as "toward" describes the personal direction of Philemon's faith, showing that his trust and devotion are aimed directly toward the Lord Jesus.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The preposition pros is one of the most frequent directional words in biblical Greek. In ancient Greek literature and everyday conversation, it marked spatial movement toward a destination, direct address in speech, or proximity between persons.

In the New Testament, prepositions carry important relational clarity. While some prepositions emphasize staying inside a space or resting in a location, pros points outward toward a goal, a place, or a person. In Paul's short letter to Philemon, Paul opens with thanksgiving for Philemon's reputation in the church before offering a personal request on behalf of Onesimus. In this opening commendation, Paul uses pros to describe the direction of Philemon's spiritual life.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word pros basic sense is directional motion or orientation. Depending on how it functions in a sentence, it can refer to:

  • Motion toward a person or place ("to," "toward").

  • Presence alongside or in relationship with someone ("with," "near").

  • Facing or standing before a person ("before").

When pros connects a person's inner attitudes or actions to a recipient, it indicates where that focus is directed. In Philemon 1:5, it means "toward," identifying the Lord Jesus as the recipient and target of Philemon's personal faith.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Scripture, pros serves several distinct narrative and relational functions:

  • Physical movement: It indicates someone traveling or turning toward a location or individual.

  • Spoken address: It routinely introduces spoken words addressed directly to another person.

  • Personal orientation: It marks the target of a person's devotion, purpose, or attitude.

When used to describe faith or heart commitment, pros emphasizes that faith is not a passive feeling or vague sentiment. It is an active orientation focused directly on a person.

4. Theological Implications

The use of pros in expressions of faith highlights the personal nature of Christian devotion. New Testament faith is defined by its object. Believers do not merely possess general religious belief; their trust is directed toward the living Person of Jesus Christ.

This directional sense shows that genuine faith moves toward Christ as its anchor and source. Recognizing Christ as the direct recipient of faith establishes the proper foundation for Christian behavior and fellowship within the church family.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:5, Paul writes that he continually gives thanks, "hearing of your love and faith which you have toward the Lord Jesus and toward all the saints." Paul highlights two expressions of Philemon's spiritual health: his faith directed toward Christ and his love extended to fellow believers.

By using pros in connection with the Lord Jesus, Paul specifies that Philemon's faith finds its direct target in Christ. This vertical commitment to Jesus provides the source and strength for Philemon's horizontal love toward his fellow Christians. Before Paul asks Philemon to forgive and welcome back his former slave Onesimus as a brother, Paul reminds Philemon of this deep directional commitment he already has toward the Lord.

  • Galatians 2:5 — "to whom we did not yield submission even for an hour, that the truth of the gospel might continue for you."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In biblical Greek, prepositions often work together to paint a complete picture of Christian living. In Philemon 1:5, Paul uses directional language to show where spiritual life begins and where it flows. Faith is directed upward toward the Lord Jesus, and out of that relationship, practical love flows outward to all the saints.
