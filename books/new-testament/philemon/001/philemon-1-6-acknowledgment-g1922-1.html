ACKNOWLEDGMENT - (epígnōsis, Strong's G1922)

Summary:

Epignōsis means precise knowledge, full recognition, clear practical understanding, or conscious discernment of truth. Rather than referring to a simple awareness of facts, it describes a personal, clear, and active recognition of reality. In Philemon 1:6, the word describes Philemon's conscious recognition and deep appreciation of every spiritual blessing and good thing given to believers in Christ, which energizes and empowers his effective sharing of faith and Christian fellowship.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word epignōsis combines the common noun for knowledge with an intensifying prefix. In ancient Greek, adding this prefix often pointed toward a focused, direction-oriented, or fuller recognition of a subject. It conveys the idea of moving from simple awareness to a clear, established, and personal perception of truth.

In the Greek New Testament, the word appears in contexts dealing with spiritual growth, the gospel message, and the practical outcome of Christian doctrine. Paul frequently uses this word in his prayers for churches and individuals, expressing his earnest desire that believers would move beyond basic conversion into a full, mature recognition of God's will and grace.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Epignōsis can refer to:

  • Precise or full knowledge

  • Personal recognition or acknowledgment

  • Discerning or experiential perception of truth

  • Clear practical insight into divine realities

The word goes beyond holding abstract intellectual opinions. It describes an active mind and heart coming to grips with objective truth. When applied to God or spiritual matters, it refers to a clear, settled recognition of who God is and what He has accomplished. When applied to truth or goodness, it means recognizing and owning that truth in a way that shapes understanding and conduct.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In biblical usage, epignōsis frequently carries a practical and moral dimension. It is not used merely for academic learning or speculative philosophy. Instead, Scripture connects this form of knowledge directly with godliness, spiritual fruitfulness, and Christian behavior.

Throughout the New Testament epistles, the word appears in several key settings:

  • Knowing God or Jesus Christ personally through the gospel.

  • Coming to the recognition of the truth necessary for salvation and repentance.

  • Growing in the full discernment of God's will to walk worthily before Him.

  • Acknowledging the internal spiritual riches and spiritual gifts supplied by God to the church.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of epignōsis highlights several important theological truths:

  • True Christian knowledge is rooted in objective divine revelation. Spiritual life does not depend on human imagination, but on recognizing what God has revealed in Christ.

  • Spiritual understanding is dynamic and productive. Knowing the truth produces tangible changes in how a believer loves, forgives, and serves others.

  • Spiritual growth involves an increasing awareness of gospel realities. Believers grow not by inventing new doctrines, but by deepening their recognition and appreciation of the salvation and grace they have already received in Christ Jesus.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:6, Paul offers a heartfelt prayer for his friend and ministry partner, Philemon: "that the sharing of your faith may become effective by the acknowledgment of every good thing which is in you in Christ Jesus."

The target word appears in a singular feminine dative form, pointing to the means or environment through which Philemon's faith-sharing becomes active and spiritually powerful. Paul is not asking Philemon to give passive intellectual assent to theological concepts. Rather, Paul wants Philemon's active partnership, generosity, and fellowship (his "sharing of faith") to be energized by a rich, clear recognition of all the spiritual treasures God has placed within the Christian community.

This spiritual recognition is essential to the specific pastoral situation Paul addresses. Paul is preparing to ask Philemon to forgive and welcome back Onesimus—a runaway slave who had become a believer through Paul's ministry—not as a servant, but as a beloved brother. By emphasizing the "acknowledgment of every good thing," Paul reminds Philemon of the immense grace, mercy, brotherhood, and forgiveness he himself possesses in Christ. When Philemon clearly recognizes these divine gifts, his heart will naturally extend that same grace and fellowship to Onesimus.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Ephesians 1:17 – Paul prays that God may give believers "the spirit of wisdom and revelation in the knowledge of Him," showing that true wisdom brings a deeper recognition of God Himself.

  • Colossians 1:9 – Paul prays for believers to "be filled with the knowledge of His will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding," linking spiritual insight directly with a life pleasing to God.

  • 2 Timothy 2:25 – Paul instructs ministers to correct opponents humbly, hoping God will grant repentance so that "they may know the truth," where the phrase describes a heart-awakening recognition of God's saving truth.

  • Titus 1:1 – Paul describes his apostolic mission as serving "the faith of God’s elect and the acknowledgment of the truth which accords with godliness," linking genuine spiritual understanding with a godly life.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Philemon 1:6, Paul demonstrates a profound principle of Christian motivation. He does not begin by commanding Philemon through apostolic authority or imposing legalistic pressure. Instead, he prays that Philemon's generosity and forgiveness will flow from an awakened heart that recognizes the richness of God's grace.

When believers live in constant recognition of the forgiveness, love, and spiritual riches granted to them in Christ, act of love and mercy toward others become a natural expression of that inner reality.
