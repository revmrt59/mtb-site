BECOME - (gínomai, Strong's G1096)

Summary:

The Greek word ginomai means to come into existence, to happen, to take place, or to enter into a new state or condition. In Philemon 1:6, Paul uses the word to express his prayer that Philemon's sharing of faith may "become" active and fruitfully effective as he grows in recognizing all the good gifts believers have in Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Apostle Paul wrote his short epistle to Philemon while imprisoned for the gospel. Philemon was a leader in the house church at Colosse, known for his love and faith toward the Lord Jesus and all the saints. Onesimus, a bondservant who belonged to Philemon, had fled to Rome, met Paul, and converted to faith in Christ. Paul wrote this personal letter to encourage Philemon to welcome Onesimus back—no longer merely as a bondservant, but as a beloved brother in the Lord.

Before presenting his specific request regarding Onesimus, Paul opens with thanksgiving and prayer for Philemon. In verse 6, Paul asks God that the fellowship or sharing of Philemon's faith may become dynamic and effective. The verb ginomai marks this transition, expressing Paul's desire for Philemon's faith to produce visible, fruitful results in his community and relationships.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Ginomai can refer to coming into being, happening, taking place, or entering a specific condition. Depending on how it is used, the word can mean:

  • To happen or take place (describing events or circumstances)

  • To come into existence or be created

  • To become or turn into something (describing a change of state or function)

In Philemon 1:6, the word describes a change or development in state. Paul is praying that Philemon's active partnership in the faith may come to be fruitfully effective. The verb itself simply denotes this process of coming into a particular condition rather than remaining static.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, ginomai is used in a wide variety of contexts to describe transitions, historical events, and personal changes. Unlike the basic verb for existence (eimi, which means "to be"), ginomai highlights movement, emergence, or coming to pass. It marks the difference between simply being present and becoming something active or realized.

When used in spiritual contexts, ginomai often describes the practical outworking of faith in a believer's life. It shows that spiritual qualities, relationships, and service are dynamic rather than fixed. Faith is meant to express itself through tangible actions, taking on visible form in daily conduct and ministry.

4. Theological Implications

The use of ginomai in Paul's prayer reflects several important biblical truths:

  • Faith is designed to grow and produce results. True faith does not remain passive; it comes to life and expresses itself in love and generosity.

  • Christian fruitfulness relies on spiritual understanding. Paul prays that Philemon's faith will become effective as he acknowledges every good thing that God has provided in Christ Jesus.

  • Spiritual progress involves an ongoing transformation where internal convictions manifest in outward, Christlike actions within the Christian community.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:6, Paul writes, "that the sharing of your faith may become effective by the acknowledgment of every good thing which is in you in Christ Jesus." The verb form used here expresses Paul's request and desire for Philemon.

This prayer serves a vital role in the argument of the letter. Before Paul asks Philemon to extend forgiveness and fellowship to Onesimus, he prays that Philemon's faith-sharing will become dynamic and active. Paul grounds this desired effectiveness in a growing appreciation of "every good thing" that belongs to believers in Christ. By recognizing God's rich grace toward him, Philemon would be empowered to show extraordinary grace to Onesimus. The word "become" here points to the practical realization of Philemon's faith as he responds to God's goodness.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Galatians 4:16 — "Have I therefore become your enemy because I tell you the truth?"

  • 1 Thessalonians 2:14 — "For you, brethren, became imitators of the churches of God which are in Judea in Christ Jesus."

  • Hebrews 10:33 — "partly while you were made a spectacle both by reproaches and tribulations, and partly while you became companions of those who were so treated."

  • 1 Corinthians 14:20 — "Brethren, do not be children in understanding; however, in malice be babes, but in understanding be mature."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The distinction between mere existence and active becoming is vital in pastoral ministry. Scripture presents the Christian life not as a static standing alone, but as a journey of becoming more fully what God has called believers to be in Christ. Paul does not demand that Philemon manufacture effectiveness on his own; instead, he prays that Philemon's faith will become effective as a natural outcome of knowing and receiving Christ's grace.
