EVERY - (pâs, Strong's G3956)

pas ( pas , Strong’s G3956 )

Summary:

The Greek word pas is a foundational adjective meaning "all," "every," "whole," or "entire." It indicates either the total sum of something or every single individual item within a group. In Philemon 1:6, the word is translated "every" and describes the entire collection of spiritual blessings, virtues, and graces that God has worked into the life of the believer through Jesus Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The adjective pas is one of the most frequent words in the Greek New Testament. It serves as the standard term for expressing totality, completeness, or individual items within a whole. Depending on how it is used with nouns and articles, it can mean "all people," "every person," "the whole," or "every kind of."

Paul wrote his letter to Philemon, a leader in the church at Colosse, while imprisoned. Philemon was a generous believer whose love and faith refreshed the church. Paul wrote to ask Philemon to receive back his runaway servant Onesimus, who had recently become a Christian through Paul's ministry. In his opening prayer, Paul asks God that Philemon's active sharing of the faith would become effective as he comes to appreciate every good work and grace given to believers in Christ.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of pas covers three related ideas:

  • Comprehensive totality: "all," "the whole," or "everything."

  • Individual distribution: "every single" or "each."

  • Broad variety: "every kind of" or "all types of."

In Philemon 1:6, the word appears in a neuter singular form in the genitive case, agreeing directly with the word for "good thing." Here it means "every single" or "all," pointing to the entire range of spiritual goodness that exists in believers because of their relationship with Jesus Christ.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses pas across every genre to indicate comprehensive scope. However, the exact boundaries of "all" or "every" are always defined by the immediate passage. When Scripture says "all," context determines whether it means every human being without exception, every person within a specific group, or every kind of thing within a category.

When applied to God's gifts and works, pas stresses that nothing necessary is left out. God does not grant partial grace or incomplete equipment for Christian living. In Paul's letters, the word frequently emphasizes the thoroughness of God's provision for His people and the complete devotion expected in return.

4. Theological Implications

In Philemon 1:6, the use of "every" reinforces that all spiritual goodness in a Christian comes from union with Jesus Christ. Believers possess genuine capacities for forgiveness, mercy, generosity, and love. These virtues are not self-generated human merits; they are real spiritual assets planted in the believer by Christ.

Furthermore, Paul connects full spiritual awareness with practical Christian action. Faith becomes effective when believers recognize and put into practice every good thing God has placed within them. Christian fruitfulness does not come from searching for missing resources, but from acknowledging and applying what Christ has already supplied.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Paul uses "every good thing" strategically in his prayer for Philemon. Before asking Philemon to do something humanly difficult—forgiving and welcoming back a servant who wronged him—Paul reminds Philemon of the abundance of divine grace already present in his life.

The word "every" functions here as a pastorally wise encouragement. Philemon already possesses the spiritual capacity to forgive, show mercy, and welcome Onesimus as a brother because Christ has placed "every good thing" within him. By recognizing all that God has done in his life, Philemon will find the strength to act with gospel generosity.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Acts 18:4 – Paul reasoned in the synagogue "every" Sabbath, showing a continuous pattern of ministry.

  • Titus 1:15 – Paul notes that to the pure "all" things are pure, describing a renewed moral outlook.

  • 1 John 3:20 – John reassures believers by reminding them that God knows "all" things.

  • John 17:21 – Jesus prayed that His followers "all" may be one, expressing His heart for Christian unity.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Understanding that "every" good thing in us comes from Christ provides a healthy balance for Christian living:

  • It guards against spiritual pride, because any goodness found in a believer is rooted in Jesus Christ, not personal merit.

  • It overcomes feelings of inadequacy, because God has not held back any necessary spiritual blessing needed to obey His calls.
