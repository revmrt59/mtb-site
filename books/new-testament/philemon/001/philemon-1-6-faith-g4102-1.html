FAITH - (pístis, Strong's G4102)

Summary:

The word pistis means trust, reliance, conviction, or faithfulness directed toward God and Jesus Christ. In Philemon 1:6, it refers to Philemon's living trust in Christ, a faith that expresses itself in active, generous fellowship with other believers and bears fruit through a deeper understanding of Christ's blessings.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Paul wrote his short letter to Philemon from prison, addressing a personal friend and house-church leader in Colosse. A enslaved man named Onesimus had separated from Philemon, but after meeting Paul in prison, Onesimus came to faith in Christ. Paul wrote to ask Philemon to welcome Onesimus back not merely as a slave, but as a beloved brother in Christ.

Before making this direct request, Paul opens his letter with thanksgiving and prayer. He praises God for Philemon’s love and faith toward the Lord Jesus and toward all the saints. In verse 6, Paul expands on this praise with a specific petition: he prays that the sharing or fellowship flowing from Philemon's faith will become effective as Philemon recognizes all the good things God has given believers in Christ.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The Greek word pistis refers primarily to personal trust, reliance, and confidence. Depending on context, it can describe:

  • Personal reliance and trust placed in God or Jesus Christ.

  • Faithfulness, fidelity, or trustworthy character.

  • The Christian belief or message itself (the content of what is believed).

When a person exercises pistis toward God, it involves an active commitment of the heart, trusting in God's promises and character. In Philemon 1:6, the word refers to Philemon’s personal, lived trust in the Lord Jesus Christ.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, pistis is used in several complementary ways:

  • Saving Trust: Relies entirely on Jesus Christ for justification and rescue from sin rather than human merit.

  • Daily Reliance: Describes the ongoing lifestyle of walking by trust in God through life's trials and duties.

  • Active Expression: Works through love, manifesting as practical care, generosity, and hospitality toward others.

The biblical concept of pistis is never a passive mental opinion. True trust in God moves the heart toward obedience and active love for God's people.

4. Theological Implications

Faith in Jesus Christ unites a believer to Christ and simultaneously unites that believer to the rest of God's family. Trust in God is never meant to remain isolated or private; it naturally produces community, generosity, and mutual support.

In Paul's theology, the trust a believer places in Christ transforms how that person views every other human relationship. Because faith links believers together in Christ, social divisions and past grievances yield to forgiveness, brotherhood, and shared grace.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:6, Paul speaks of "the sharing of your faith." The phrase links Philemon’s personal trust in Christ (pistis) directly with Christian fellowship and generosity (koinōnia).

Paul prays that this active expression of faith may become fully "effective." The effectiveness Paul desires grows as Philemon acknowledges and appreciates "every good thing which is in you in Christ Jesus."

This prayer prepares the groundwork for Paul's upcoming appeal. Before Paul asks Philemon to forgive Onesimus and receive him as a brother, Paul reminds Philemon that his faith in Christ naturally expresses itself through generous fellowship and spiritual maturity. The genitive form "of your faith" indicates that while this trust is Philemon's personal commitment to Christ, its practical outflow benefits the entire Christian community around him.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 8:10 — "When Jesus heard it, He marveled, and said to those who followed, 'Assuredly, I say to you, I have not found such great faith, not even in Israel!'"

  • Romans 3:22 — "even the righteousness of God, through faith in Jesus Christ, to all and on all who believe. For there is no difference;"

  • Galatians 2:16 — "knowing that a man is not justified by the works of the law but by faith in Jesus Christ, even we have believed in Christ Jesus, that we might be justified by faith in Christ and not by the works of the law;"

  • Hebrews 11:1 — "Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen."

  • James 2:22 — "Do you see that faith was working together with his works, and by works faith was made perfect?"

7. Other Relevant Insights

Paul’s use of pistis in Philemon 1:6 shows how prayer, theology, and practical church life intertw. Paul does not ask Philemon to perform a difficult act of forgiveness in a vacuum. Instead, he grounds his appeal in the shared life flowing from their common trust in Christ.

When believers grow in their understanding of the spiritual riches and good gifts given to them in Christ, their trust in God matures into visible acts of mercy and reconciliation.
