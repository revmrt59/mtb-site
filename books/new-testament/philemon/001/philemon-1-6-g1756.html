EFFECTIVE - ( energes , Strong's G1756 )

Summary:

The word energes describes something that is active, operative, and powerfully productive rather than idle or merely theoretical. In Philemon 1:6, Paul prays that Philemon's sharing of faith may become effective through the full acknowledgment of every good thing in Christ, meaning his faith actively accomplishes tangible results in daily relationships.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • Rooted in the concept of ergon, meaning work, labor, or action.

  • The prefix en- combines with the root to indicate power "at work within" something or someone.

  • The historical development carries the sense of being energetically engaged, operational, and producing observable fruit.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Active, operative, at work, powerful, or effectual.

  • Refers to a force or quality that is not dormant or passive, but fully functioning and achieving its intended purpose.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Often used in ancient contexts to describe medicine that actually heals, tools that properly function, or persons who are actively productive.

  • In New Testament usage, it points away from abstract belief and toward dynamic spiritual vitality that accomplishes real-world good.

4. Theological Implications

  • Spiritual knowledge and faith are designed by God to be dynamic and operational, never static or merely intellectual.

  • The effectiveness of a believer's witness and love is rooted entirely in union with Christ and the recognition of what God has already provided in Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • Paul prays that Philemon's practical expression of fellowship (koinonia) will be actively powerful and productive.

  • The effectiveness is unlocked and experienced as Philemon deepens his personal understanding and appreciation of all good things given by Christ.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Corinthians 16:9 - "For a great and effective door has opened to me, and there are many adversaries."

  • Hebrews 4:12 - "For the word of God is living and powerful, and sharper than any two-edged sword..." (Note: energes is translated as "powerful" here).

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The English word "energetic" derives from this Greek word family, capturing the sense of vigor and focused motion.

  • True Christian fellowship generates an outward, productive energy that refreshes the hearts of the saints, just as Philemon had done.
