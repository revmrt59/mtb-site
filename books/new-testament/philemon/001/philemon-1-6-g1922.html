ACKNOWLEDGMENT - ( epignōsis , Strong's G1922 )

Summary:

The word epignōsis refers to a precise, thorough, and experiential knowledge that goes beyond mere intellectual apprehension to active, personal recognition and submission to the truth. In Philemon 1:6, it represents the vital, conscious awareness and deep appreciation of all the spiritual blessings and virtues that Christ has deposited in the believer's life. This is not passive information, but an active, transforming recognition that directly influences how a Christian behaves toward others.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun epignōsis is a compound word formed from the preposition epi (upon, or used as an intensifier meaning "fully") and the noun gnōsis (knowledge). While gnōsis represents the general acquisition of information or awareness, the prefix epi elevates the word to express a localized, fully realized, and personally claimed knowledge.

The historical word picture is that of a traveler who does not merely know about a destination from a map (gnōsis), but has actually arrived at the location, stood upon its ground, and experienced it firsthand (epignōsis). It is knowledge that has found its target, leading to a deep, internal conviction and an outward acknowledgment of that reality.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term signifies a precise, correct, and complete knowledge. It is the state of having fully processed and embraced a truth so that it becomes an active force in one's life. Rather than a dry academic understanding, it carries the nuance of experiential recognition and confession.

In the Epistles, this word is frequently utilized to denote the deep, saving knowledge of God and His truth. It is a relational term; to have epignōsis of something means to own it, to submit to its implications, and to live in light of its reality. Therefore, translating it as "acknowledgment" capturing the active, confessing, and appreciative side of this knowledge is highly accurate.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the New Testament, epignōsis occurs 20 times and is used exclusively in the Epistles. It is often paired with "the truth" or "God," representing the ultimate spiritual breakthrough where a person moves from ignorance or superficial awareness to a decisive, life-changing embrace of the gospel.

A vivid way to picture epignōsis is to compare it to a key clicking into a lock. Intellectual knowledge (gnōsis) knows what a key is and what a lock is; epignōsis is the actual turning of the key that opens the door. It is the moment truth "clicks" in the heart of a believer, transforming their perspective and empowering their actions.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, epignōsis demonstrates that biblical knowledge is never intended to be merely cognitive or speculative. Scripture guards against the pride of abstract intellectualism, noting that mere knowledge can puff up. In contrast, epignōsis humbles and transforms because it brings the believer into direct, relational contact with the holiness and grace of God.

Furthermore, this concept indicates that spiritual power and Christian maturity are directly linked to our perception of spiritual reality. Believers do not need to beg for new spiritual resources; rather, they must grow in the epignōsis—the full, conscious acknowledgment—of what God has already given them in Christ. Sanctification is the progressive realization and application of our position in Christ.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:6, Paul prays "that the sharing of your faith may become effective by the acknowledgment of every good thing which is in you in Christ Jesus."

The pastoral urgency of this letter is immense. Paul is writing to Philemon, urging him to forgive and receive back his runaway slave, Onesimus, not as a criminal, but as a beloved brother. This was a counter-cultural, incredibly difficult demand. To make this radical leap of grace, Philemon’s faith had to become "effective" (active, powerful, working).

Paul reveals the secret to this spiritual power: it comes through Philemon’s acknowledgment (epignōsis) of every good thing Christ has placed within him. If Philemon looks only at his natural human emotions, he will feel resentment and demand justice. But if he actively recognizes, appreciates, and yields to the supernatural deposits of Christ's love, mercy, and forgiveness already residing in his heart, he will find the power to forgive Onesimus. The key to horizontal grace (toward others) is the vertical acknowledgment of the grace we have already received.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Colossians 1:9 - "For this reason we also, since the day we heard it, do not cease to pray for you, and to ask that you may be filled with the knowledge of His will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding;"

7. Other Relevant Insights

A practical distinction can be made between knowing about our wealth in Christ and acknowledging it. It is possible for a believer to possess an immense spiritual inheritance while living like a spiritual pauper because they lack epignōsis.

When we pray for other believers, we should follow Paul's apostolic pattern. Rather than merely praying for a change in their external circumstances, we ought to pray that they would grow in the deep, experiential acknowledgment of the spiritual riches, power, and identity they already possess in Christ Jesus.
