JESUS - (Iēsoûs, Strong's G2424)

Summary:

The word Iēsous is the proper name Jesus, the Greek equivalent of the Hebrew name Joshua (Yěhôšûaʿ), which means "Yahweh is salvation" or "Yahweh saves." Throughout the New Testament, it identifies Jesus of Nazareth as the divine Savior and promised Messiah. In Philemon 1:6, Paul uses the name alongside "Christ" to anchor every good action, fellowship, and grace within the Christian life directly in the personal reality and work of Jesus Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The name Iēsous is the Greek adaptation of the common Hebrew and Aramaic name Yēšûaʿ or Yěhôšûaʿ (Joshua). In ancient Jewish culture, the name was relatively common among men, carrying a rich heritage connected to Joshua, the successor of Moses who led Israel into the Promised Land.

In the New Testament writings, however, the name overwhelmingly refers to Jesus of Nazareth. While the Gospels frequently use "Jesus" by itself to record His historical life, earthly ministry, death, and resurrection, the apostolic letters often combine His personal name with His official messianic title, yielding expressions such as "Christ Jesus" or "Jesus Christ."

Paul wrote the letter to Philemon while imprisoned for the gospel. Philemon was a believer in Colosse known for his hospitality and love for the church. Paul wrote to pray for Philemon and to urge him to welcome back his runaway bondservant, Onesimus, who had recently become a believer. In Philemon 1:6, Paul frames the entire Christian life around what believers possess in Jesus.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Iēsous is a proper personal name. It represents the Greek form of the Hebrew name Yěhôšûaʿ, combining the divine name Yahweh with the root meaning "salvation" or "to save." The established meaning of the name is "Yahweh is salvation" or "Yahweh saves."

The identification of this meaning is well established across ancient Jewish and early Christian usage. In the Gospels, an angel explicitly connects the name to His divine mission: He was named Jesus because He would save His people from their sins.

In Philemon 1:6, the name identifies the person of Jesus Christ. Paul is not using the name as a general concept, but as the specific, living Lord in whom believers are united. By referring to "Christ Jesus," Paul highlights both His royal messianic office ("Christ") and His historical identity as the Savior ("Jesus").

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In the Gospels, "Jesus" functions primarily as a proper personal name emphasizing His genuine humanity and historical life. Readers see Jesus walking through Galilee, teaching, healing, speaking with disciples, suffering, and dying on the cross.

In the Epistles, the Apostles frequently pair "Jesus" with "Christ." This combination preserves two critical truths:

  • The title "Christ" (Christos, meaning "Anointed One") points to His divine appointment as the promised King and Savior of Old Testament prophecy.

  • The personal name "Jesus" keeps the reader anchored in the real historical person who lived, died, and rose again.

In Paul's letters, being "in Christ Jesus" describes the central relationship of the believer. Every spiritual blessing, transformation, and capacity for good flows from union with Him.

4. Theological Implications

The name Jesus serves as a constant reminder that salvation is anchored in history. Faith is not built on abstract philosophy or moral ideals, but on the person and work of Jesus Christ. The meaning "Yahweh saves" expresses the core of the gospel: salvation is initiated and accomplished by God Himself through His Son.

Furthermore, Paul’s frequent use of "Christ Jesus" links the personal work of salvation with the exalted authority of the Lord. The believers' new identity, mutual relationships, and capacity to forgive one another are rooted in who Jesus is and what He has done.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:6, Paul prays "that the sharing of your faith may become effective by the acknowledgment of every good thing which is in you in Christ Jesus."

Paul is laying a gentle yet firm foundation for the request he will make regarding Onesimus. Before asking Philemon to extend extraordinary forgiveness and brotherhood to a former runaway, Paul reminds him where all genuine goodness comes from. The expression "in Christ Jesus" shows that the good traits, love, and generosity seen in Philemon are not self-generated human virtues. They exist because Philemon belongs to Jesus Christ.

The grammatical structure places "in Christ Jesus" at the conclusion of Paul's thought, showing that every good thing active within the believer points back to Jesus as its source and goal. True Christian fellowship (koinōnia) becomes powerful and effective when believers recognize and acknowledge that every good work they perform originates from their connection to Jesus.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 1:1 — "The book of the genealogy of Jesus Christ, the Son of David, the Son of Abraham."

  • Acts 17:3 — "explaining and demonstrating that the Christ had to suffer and rise again from the dead, and saying, 'This Jesus whom I preach to you is the Christ.'"

  • 2 Corinthians 4:14 — "knowing that He who raised up the Lord Jesus will also raise us up with Jesus, and will present us with you."

  • Philippians 1:8 — "For God is my witness, how greatly I long for you all with the affection of Jesus Christ."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the short letter to Philemon, Paul repeatedly grounds his pastoral counsel in the Lord Jesus. Rather than issuing bare apostolic commands, Paul appeals to Philemon's heart on the basis of Christian love and shared fellowship. By emphasizing that every good thing in Philemon exists "in Christ Jesus," Paul reminds Philemon that forgiving Onesimus is simply the natural expression of the grace Philemon himself has received in Jesus.
