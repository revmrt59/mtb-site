SHARING - (koinōnía, Strong's G2842)

Summary:

The Greek word koinōnia means close fellowship, common participation, partnership, or generous sharing of resources. It describes a deep bond where people share common life, commitments, or material goods. In Philemon 1:6, Paul uses the word to describe Philemon’s active participation in the faith—his practical generosity and shared life with fellow believers. Paul prays that this active sharing will prove powerful and fruitful as Philemon recognizes all the good God has given believers in Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek culture, koinōnia described relationships where people held something in common. It was used for business partnerships, joint family ventures, civic unions, or close friendships where participants shared mutual responsibilities and benefits. The word always implied active involvement rather than passive association.

In the New Testament, koinōnia takes on rich spiritual meaning. It describes the common life believers share because they are joined to Jesus Christ. It is used both for spiritual communion with God and for the practical, tangible expressions of unity among Christians, such as financial assistance and shared ministry efforts. In his letter to Philemon, Paul writes from prison to a Christian brother known for his hospitality and love toward God's people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to several related ideas depending on how it is used:

  • Shared Life and Fellowship: A deep spiritual connection among believers based on their common salvation.

  • Partnership and Cooperation: Working together toward a shared spiritual goal or gospel effort.

  • Generous Sharing or Contribution: Giving financial aid or material support to meet the needs of others.

In Philemon 1:6, the word emphasizes active participation and practical generosity flowing from faith. It is not merely a warm feeling of unity, but faith put into practice through concrete deeds that bless other Christians.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, koinōnia bridges the gap between inner spiritual reality and visible outward action. Believers enter into koinōnia first with God the Father and His Son Jesus Christ, and this spiritual bond immediately creates a shared bond with other Christians.

The word frequently appears in connection with practical generosity. Giving money to relieve poor saints or support gospel workers is called koinōnia because material giving is a visible manifestation of shared Christian life. True Christian fellowship in Scripture is never limited to casual conversation; it includes mutual support, shared suffering, common mission, and open-handed generosity.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical idea of koinōnia shows that faith in Christ is never meant to be lived in isolation. When a person turns to Christ, God places them into a body of believers who share a single life, hope, and calling.

Generosity and hospitality flow directly from this common life. When Christians care for one another, host fellow believers, or share their resources, they are demonstrating the reality of Christ working in them. Practical fellowship is both an evidence of genuine faith and a primary means through which Christ reveals His goodness to the church and the world.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:4–7, Paul opens his letter with thanksgiving for Philemon’s love and faith toward the Lord Jesus and all the saints. In verse 6, Paul writes his prayer for Philemon: "that the sharing of your faith may become effective by the acknowledgment of every good thing which is in you in Christ Jesus."

Here, "the sharing of your faith" refers to Philemon’s active partnership in the gospel and his generous hospitality toward fellow Christians. Paul prays that Philemon's outward expressions of faith will become fully effective and productive. This effectiveness grows as Philemon gains a deeper, clearer recognition of all the good gifts God has placed within believers through union with Christ.

This prayer lays the foundation for Paul’s primary request later in the letter. Paul asks Philemon to receive back Onesimus—a runaway servant who became a believer through Paul's ministry—not as a slave, but as a beloved brother in Christ. Philemon’s readiness to welcome Onesimus back into full fellowship would be the ultimate test and demonstration of his koinōnia.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Acts 2:42 — "And they continued steadfastly in the apostles’ doctrine and fellowship, in the breaking of bread, and in prayers."

  • Romans 15:26 — "For it has pleased those from Macedonia and Achaia to make a certain contribution for the poor among the saints who are in Jerusalem."

  • 2 Corinthians 9:13 — "while, through the proof of this ministry, they glorify God for your obedience to the confession of the gospel of Christ, and for your liberal sharing with them and all men..."

  • Philippians 1:5 — "for your fellowship in the gospel from the first day until now..."

  • 1 John 1:3 — "that which we have seen and heard we declare to you, that you also may have fellowship with us; and truly our fellowship is with the Father and with His Son Jesus Christ."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The word appears in Philemon 1:6 as a noun functioning as the subject of Paul's prayer clause. Paul does not pray simply that Philemon will hold correct doctrine, but that his active fellowship—his living practice of sharing faith and resources—will be fruitful.

Understanding koinōnia as practical partnership illuminates the whole letter to Philemon. Paul calls Philemon his "partner" later in verse 17 using a closely related word. By linking Philemon’s faith to practical fellowship, Paul reminds every reader that true Christian faith naturally opens one's heart, home, and hands to welcome fellow believers without partiality.
