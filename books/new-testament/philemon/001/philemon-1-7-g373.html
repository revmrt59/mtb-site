REFRESHED - ( anapauō , Strong's G373 )

Summary:

The Greek verb anapauō combines the prefix ana (again, back, or up) with pauō (to cease or stop). It means to cause to rest, to give relief, or to refresh after toil or strain. In Philemon 1:7, it captures how Philemon's active, loving ministry brought profound spiritual and emotional relief to the hearts of weary believers, rejuvenating them for continued service.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

Historically, the word carries a vivid military and agricultural word picture. It refers to an army taking a temporary halt during a march to regain strength, or oxen being unyoked to rest. The prefix ana suggests a reversal or turning back of pressure. It does not refer to a permanent retirement from work, but rather a temporary cessation of labor that restores energy, breathing room, and vitality so one can resume a journey or task with renewed strength.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

In the active voice, anapauō means to cause someone to rest, to give rest, or to refresh. In the middle and passive voices, it means to take rest, to sleep, or to be refreshed. The lexical focus is on the restoration of physical, emotional, or spiritual strength after a period of exertion, burden, or distress. It signifies the relief of tension and the restoration of inner quiet.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The New Testament uses anapauō in several key ways:

  • Physical Rest: It describes physical rest, such as Jesus inviting the disciples to rest after a period of intense ministry (Mark 6:31).

  • Spiritual Rest: It describes the deep spiritual rest that Jesus promises to those who are weary and heavy laden under the law or the burdens of life (Matthew 11:28).

  • Relational Refreshment: It refers to the emotional and spiritual rejuvenation experienced through Christian fellowship, hospitality, and mutual encouragement (1 Corinthians 16:18; 2 Corinthians 7:13).

4. Theological Implications

Scripturally, rest and refreshment are divine provisions. God designed human beings to need rhythm, renewal, and relief. In the New Covenant, this refreshment is often mediated through the body of Christ. When believers practice active, self-sacrificing love, they become the hands and feet of Jesus, delivering His promised rest to those who are weary. Refreshment is a tangible demonstration of grace in action within the community of faith.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:7, Paul praises Philemon because the "hearts" (literally splagchna, meaning bowels or the deepest seat of emotions) of the saints have been "refreshed." The Roman world was harsh, and early Christians faced extreme social, financial, and physical pressure. Philemon used his wealth, home, and generous spirit to create an oasis of peace. His love acted as a refreshing tonic, relieving the anxieties of local believers and restoring their spiritual vigor. This specific contextual commendation sets the stage for Paul's upcoming appeal regarding the runaway slave, Onesimus. Paul asks Philemon to extend this same refreshing grace to him as well (Philemon 1:20).

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 11:28 - "Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."

  • Mark 6:31 - "And He said to them, 'Come aside by yourselves to a deserted place and rest a while.'"

  • 1 Corinthians 16:18 - "For they refreshed my spirit and yours; therefore acknowledge such men."

  • 2 Corinthians 7:13 - "And we rejoiced exceedingly more for the joy of Titus, because his spirit has been refreshed by you all."

  • Philemon 1:20 - "Yes, brother, let me have joy from you in the Lord; refresh my heart in the Lord."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The perfect passive indicative form used in Philemon 1:7 (anapépautai) indicates an ongoing state resulting from a completed past action. Philemon’s acts of hospitality and love were not isolated incidents. They had established a lasting climate of refreshment for the local church. This consistent, proven reputation of grace gave Paul the pastoral confidence to make his difficult request regarding Onesimus.
