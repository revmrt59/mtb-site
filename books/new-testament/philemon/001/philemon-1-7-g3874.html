CONSOLATION - ( paraklesis , Strong's G3874 )

Summary:

The English word consolation in Philemon 1:7 translates the Greek noun paraklesis, which fundamentally conveys the idea of calling someone to one's side to offer encouragement, comfort, appeal, or strengthening support. In this personal letter, Paul rejoices deeply because Philemon has refreshed the hearts of the saints, demonstrating a living, active love that brings profound encouragement to the Christian community.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The noun paraklesis derives from the verb parakaleo, which literally means to call someone alongside (para meaning "beside," and kaleo meaning "to call").

  • In the ancient Greco-Roman world, this word group was frequently used in legal and military contexts, describing an advocate who comes alongside a person in court to defend them, or a commander moving among his troops to rally their courage before a battle.

  • Over time, the term shifted from a physical summons to a relational reality, capturing the posture of one who steps into another person's burden to lift, urge, comfort, or fortify them.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Paraklesis spans a semantic range that includes comfort in affliction, strong personal encouragement, exhortation to godly living, and earnest appeal.

  • Unlike a purely emotional sympathy, it involves an active intervention that supplies strength, courage, or moral direction to the recipient.

  • In biblical usage, it often bridges the gap between comfort and challenge—not only soothing a wounded heart but also rousing it to faithful action.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The word evokes the imagery of a trusted companion stepping into a brother's distress, placing an arm around his shoulder, and whispering words of steadfast hope and courage.

  • It pictures active ministry where one believer's faithfulness becomes a tangible source of replenishment for another.

  • The term carries a rich warmth, pointing away from sterile religious duty and toward vibrant, relational care within the body of Christ.

4. Theological Implications

  • All genuine human encouragement flows ultimately from the God of all comfort (paraklesis), who ministers to His people so they can comfort others (2 Corinthians 1:3-4).

  • When believers refresh one another, they put the ministry of Christ on display, acting as visible channels of His invisible grace.

  • Christian love (agape) is not merely an internal sentiment; it translates into concrete deeds that produce deep joy and courage in fellow believers.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Philemon 1:7, Paul writes, "For we have great joy and consolation in your love, because the hearts of the saints have been refreshed by you, brother."

  • Here, Philemon's practical display of love and hospitality toward the believers (the saints) served as a profound source of encouragement to Paul himself while in chains.

  • Paul presents Philemon's character not as an isolated virtue, but as a dynamic force that actively strengthened and heartened the wider Christian community in Colossae.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 2:25: "And behold, there was a man in Jerusalem whose name was Simeon, and this man was just and devout, waiting for the Consolation of Israel, and the Holy Spirit was upon him."

  • Romans 15:4: "For whatever things were written before were written for our learning, that we through the patience and comfort [paraklesis] of the Scriptures might have hope."

  • 2 Corinthians 1:4: "Who comforts us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort those who are in any trouble, with the comfort [paraklesis] with which we ourselves are comforted by God."

  • Philippians 2:1: "Therefore if there is any consolation [paraklesis] in Christ, if any comfort of love, if any fellowship of the Spirit, if any affection and mercy..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The related verb parakaleo and noun parakletos (used of the Holy Spirit in John's Gospel) share this same rich root of being called alongside to help.

  • Philemon's reputation for refreshing the saints laid the vital relational groundwork for the delicate appeal Paul was about to make regarding the runaway slave Onesimus.

  • True Christian fellowship creates an environment where discouragement gives way to resilient, hope-filled endurance.
