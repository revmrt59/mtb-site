GREAT - (polýs, Strong's G4183)

Summary:

The Greek word polys basic meaning refers to a high degree, large amount, or extensive quantity. Depending on the context, it translates into English as "much," "many," "great," or "abundant." In Philemon 1:7, the word describes the overwhelming degree of joy and comfort Paul experienced upon hearing how Philemon refreshed the hearts of fellow believers.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Apostle Paul wrote his short letter to Philemon while imprisoned, likely in Rome. Philemon was a leader in the church at Colosse who hosted believers in his home. Paul was preparing to make a delicate personal request: that Philemon receive back his runaway slave, Onesimus, no longer merely as a bondservant, but as a beloved brother in Christ.

Before introducing this sensitive request, Paul opens his letter with sincere thanksgiving. He commends Philemon for his faith toward the Lord Jesus and his love toward all the saints. In verse 7, Paul explains why Philemon's reputation brings him such encouragement: hearing about Philemon's practical care for believers brought Paul immense personal joy and comfort in the midst of his imprisonment.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word polys indicates abundance in extent, size, intensity, or number. When describing countable items or people, it means "many." When describing non-countable qualities, emotions, or measures, it means "much," "great," or "abundant."

In this passage, the word is attached to "joy" and "consolation." It does not describe a mere trickle of satisfaction or polite gratitude. Instead, it highlights an extensive, full measure of gladness and relief that fills Paul's heart despite his physical chains.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, polys is used in two primary ways:

  • Quantitative Abundance: It describes large crowds, many days, or numerous items.

  • Qualitative Intensity: When describing inward qualities such as mercy, grace, fear, sorrow, or joy, it highlights the intense degree or depth of that experience.

When used qualitatively, as it is in Philemon 1:7, the word shows that a particular spiritual or emotional reality is present in rich abundance. It distinguishes a deep, impactful experience from something partial, small, or insignificant.

4. Theological Implications

Paul's use of polys in this opening commendation highlights how deeply connected believers are within the body of Christ. Even though Paul was in prison far away, the tangible love shown by Philemon to local Christians directly supplied Paul with abundant joy and comfort.

This reveals that faithful Christian service creates ripples of encouragement far beyond a local church. When a believer ministers to others, the spiritual benefit is not small or isolated. It generates widespread comfort and strengthens leaders and fellow saints across the broader Church.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:7, Paul writes, "For we have great joy and consolation in your love, because the hearts of the saints have been refreshed by you, brother."

The word appears in the feminine accusative singular form, directly describing the measure of joy and comfort Paul received. Paul identifies the source of this abundant joy: Philemon had "refreshed" the hearts of God's people.

This setting gives Paul strong spiritual common ground with Philemon. By acknowledging the vast amount of comfort Philemon has already brought to the church, Paul lays a warm foundation of mutual love before asking Philemon to extend that same forgiving love to Onesimus.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Paul’s imprisonment provided plenty of reasons for physical discomfort and distress. Yet, his emotional and spiritual state was not dictated by his physical circumstances. Hearing about Philemon's active love provided a powerful counterweight to the hardship of his chains. The presence of abundant joy in the apostle's heart demonstrates that spiritual refreshment within the community of faith can overpower severe personal trials.
