HEARTS - (splánchnon, Strong's G4698)

Summary:

In ancient Greek, splanchnon referred literally to the internal organs and metaphorically to the seat of deep emotions, affections, and tender mercy. In Philemon 1:7, the word refers to the inner emotional and spiritual life of fellow believers—the seat of their feelings and spiritual vitality—which was cheered, revived, and comforted through Philemon's practical love and ministry.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient world, people viewed the internal organs—such as the heart, liver, and lungs—as the seat of intense human emotion. Just as modern language uses "heart" or "gut feeling" to describe deep sentiment, ancient speakers spoke of the inner organs to describe strong personal feelings.

In biblical writing, the word shifted primarily from literal physical anatomy to express profound inward compassion, tender care, and deep affection. In Paul's short letter to Philemon, written while Paul was in prison, the apostle commends Philemon for his active faith and love before raising a sensitive request regarding the runaway slave Onesimus. Paul notes that Philemon's love had brought great joy to the church because Philemon consistently brought comfort and relief to the innermost spirits of fellow Christians.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word splanchnon can refer literally to physical internal organs. More frequently in Scripture, it refers metaphorically to the emotional core, the seat of deep affection, or tender mercy.

When applied to God or Christ, the word highlights divine compassion and tender mercy extended toward suffering humanity. When applied to people, it describes genuine love, sympathy, and personal concern for others. In Philemon 1:7, the word means the inner emotional and spiritual core of believers—their inner spirits—which experienced tangible comfort and relief through Philemon's care.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

The word carries a strong emphasis on felt compassion rather than mere duty or intellectual sympathy. It describes love that reaches the deepest part of a person's life.

Across Scripture, the word connects inward feeling with outward action. It indicates that biblical love is not passive emotional feeling, nor is it cold, formal obedience. Instead, it is an intense inward care that moves a person to act for another's good. In Paul's personal letters, the term often highlights the deep bond between believers, showing how the kindness of one Christian directly revived the spirit of another.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of this word reveals that God's compassion toward humanity is personal and tender. Divine mercy is not distant or detached; God responds to human need with deep care.

Furthermore, the word illustrates the nature of Christian fellowship within the church. Believers are connected at a spiritual and emotional level. The suffering or relief of one member directly affects others. Gospel ministry goes beyond organized effort; it brings real inward refreshment and rest to the human spirit. Philemon's hospitality and generosity showed how divine compassion flows through believers to revive fellow servants of Christ.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:7, Paul expresses the great joy and comfort he received upon hearing about Philemon's love and faith. Paul writes that "the hearts of the saints have been refreshed by you, brother."

The word translated "refreshed" was often used in military contexts to describe troops resting after exhausting labor or combat. Philemon's love provided genuine rest and relief to the tired spirits ("hearts") of the saints facing hardship.

This verse sets up the central appeal of the letter. Paul later uses the same word to describe Onesimus as his very "heart" and asks Philemon to refresh his "heart" in Christ by accepting Onesimus back as a brother. By noting how Philemon previously ministered to the inner lives of the saints, Paul grounds his request in Philemon's established character of heartfelt Christian love.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Colossians 3:12 — NKJV Span: "tender" — Describes the deep compassionate character believers are called to put on as God's chosen people.

  • 2 Corinthians 7:15 — NKJV Span: "affections" — Highlights the deep inward love and care Titus felt toward the Corinthian church as he remembered their obedience.

  • Philemon 1:12 — NKJV Span: "heart" — Refers to Onesimus as Paul's own inner self or deep affection, expressing his intense personal bond with him.

  • 1 John 3:17 — NKJV Span: "heart" — Warns against closing off inner compassion when seeing a fellow believer in material need.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The nominative plural neuter form in Philemon 1:7 identifies "the hearts" of the saints as the subject receiving refreshment, placing the focus on the actual emotional and spiritual restoration experienced by the believers.

The term appears repeatedly in this short letter, creating a unifying theme centered on heart-level affection and restoration. Philemon refreshed the hearts of the saints, Paul sent Onesimus as his own heart, and Paul asked Philemon to refresh Paul's heart in the Lord. This literary connections shows that true Christian fellowship relies on deep inward empathy and mutual care.
