JOY - (cháris, Strong's G5485)

Summary:

The word charis basic meaning refers to favor, grace, kindness, or pleasantness that produces delight and gratitude. In the New Testament, it most often describes God's unmerited favor toward undeserving sinners, but it can also refer to human expressions of goodwill, practical acts of kindness, or the joy and thanksgiving generated by such goodwill. In Philemon 1:7, the word describes the deep spiritual joy and heart-refreshing delight that Paul experienced upon hearing about Philemon's love and ministry to fellow believers.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek usage, charis described qualities that win favor, evoke delight, or demonstrate gracious goodwill. It carried the idea of attractive charm, unearned favor, or a generous gift given without expectation of return. It was also commonly used to express gratitude or thanks in response to a benefit received.

when the New Testament writers used charis, they filled it with rich gospel meaning. Most frequently, it highlights God's free, unmerited salvation given to sinners through Jesus Christ. However, the Apostles also used the word in everyday practical contexts to describe human acts of generosity, expressions of heartfelt thanksgiving, or the joyful delight experienced when believers care for one another.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to several related concepts depending on the setting:

  • God's unmerited favor and spiritual blessing given to believers.

  • Goodness, favor, or kindness shown between human beings.

  • Gratitude or thanksgiving offered in response to a blessing.

  • Spiritual delight, gladness, or joy produced by a gracious gift or act of love.

In Philemon 1:7, the word refers to the gladness and spiritual joy that filled the Apostle Paul's heart as he heard about Philemon's faithful service to the church.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, charis appears in several distinct practical ways:

  • Divine Grace in Salvation: It describes God's free gift of eternal life, where sinners are saved not by personal merit or works, but entirely through God's kindness in Christ.

  • Goodwill and Favor: It refers to favorable acceptance shown by God or people, such as Mary finding favor with God or early Christians finding favor with neighbors.

  • Expressions of Gratitude: It is often translated as "thanks" or "thankfulness," describing the natural response of a redeemed heart toward God.

  • Generous Giving and Christian Character: It marks the practical generosity of believers who support those in need or speak words that build others up.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of charis in Christian fellowship reveals that grace is not merely an abstract doctrine; it is a living reality that produces practical fruit. When God's grace transforms a person's heart, that person becomes a source of refreshment and joy for others.

Paul's joy in Philemon was not rooted in worldly comfort, but in witnessing the visible fruit of God's grace working through Philemon. True Christian joy multiplies within the community of faith as believers actively love and serve one another.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In his letter to Philemon, Paul is preparing to make a delicate personal request. He is asking Philemon to forgive and welcome back Onesimus, a runaway servant who had become a Christian through Paul's prison ministry. Before Paul brings up this sensitive matter, he expresses sincere gratitude for Philemon's established reputation.

Paul tells Philemon that hearing about his faith and love brought him great joy and comfort. Philemon had opened his heart and home to care for the needs of local believers, refreshing their weary spirits. This tangible evidence of Philemon's love was a source of deep joy to Paul in his imprisonment.

By highlighting this joy, Paul gently reminds Philemon of who he is in Christ. The joy produced by past acts of love forms the foundation for Paul's upcoming appeal, encouraging Philemon to extend that same spirit of grace toward Onesimus.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 1:30 — The angel said to Mary, "Do not be afraid, Mary, for you have found favor with God."

  • Ephesians 2:5 — Paul writes, "even when we were dead in trespasses, made us alive together with Christ (by grace you have been saved)."

  • Colossians 3:16 — Believers are exhorted to sing "with grace in your hearts to the Lord."

  • 2 Timothy 1:3 — Paul writes, "I thank God, whom I serve with a pure conscience."

  • 1 Peter 2:19 — Peter notes, "For this is commendable, if because of conscience toward God one endures grief, suffering wrongfully."

7. Other Relevant Insights

It is worth noting how closely joy, grace, and thanksgiving are bound together in the New Testament. The core root behind charis is shared with the Greek word for joy (chara). In Christian experience, grace is the cause, and joy is the result. When believers experience or extend grace, joy naturally follows. Paul's heart was sustained during imprisonment not by comfortable circumstances, but by the glad news that God's grace was actively working in believers like Philemon.
