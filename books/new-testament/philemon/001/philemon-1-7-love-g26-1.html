LOVE - (agápē, Strong's G26)

Summary:

The word agapē refers to love, affection, goodwill, and practical care for others. In the New Testament, it frequently describes the purposeful, benevolent concern that believers express toward God and one another. In Philemon 1:7, the word refers to Philemon's active, practical love toward fellow believers, which brought great joy and comfort to Paul because it refreshed the hearts of God's people.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Apostle Paul wrote his short letter to Philemon while imprisoned, likely in Rome. Philemon was a Christian brother and host of a house church, probably in Colosse. Before addressing a delicate personal request concerning Philemon’s runaway slave, Onesimus, Paul opens with prayer and thanksgiving. He commends Philemon for his dynamic faith and the love he consistently demonstrated toward the Christian community. In verse 7, Paul highlights how Philemon’s love brought him personal joy and strengthened the broader church family.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Agapē can refer to affection, goodwill, benevolent concern, or devoted care. In general Greek usage, it described regard or warm appreciation for someone. In the New Testament, the word frequently takes on a rich sense of deliberate, seeking-the-good-of-others love.

It is important to remember that the word itself means love or goodwill; its full Christian character—such as self-sacrificing service or unmerited grace—is filled out by how Jesus and the apostles demonstrate and teach it in Scripture. Here in Philemon 1:7, the word designates the genuine, operational care that Philemon extended to fellow Christians.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, agapē describes several key relationships:

  • God’s love for humanity, expressed supreme in sending His Son.

  • Believers’ responsive devotion toward God and Christ.

  • The active care and unity that Christians show toward one another.

Rather than being restricted to inward feeling, agapē routinely expresses itself in visible, practical help. It is described as a spiritual fruit produced in believers and the defining mark of Christian fellowship.

4. Theological Implications

In the life of the church, agapē is not merely an abstract feeling; it is the concrete vehicle through which God’s grace touches human lives. When believers express agapē to one another, it produces real comfort, joy, and spiritual refreshment within the body of Christ. Furthermore, past faithful expressions of love create the atmosphere for present spiritual appeals. Paul uses Philemon’s established reputation for love as the moral foundation to ask Philemon to receive Onesimus back as a brother in Christ.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:7, Paul connects Philemon’s love directly to tangible results: "For we have great joy and consolation in your love, because the hearts of the saints have been refreshed by you, brother."

The expression of Philemon's love was so clear and practical that Paul, sitting in a prison cell, received personal comfort and gladness simply hearing about it. The verse indicates that Philemon refreshed the "hearts" (literally, the inner life or spirits) of the believers. His love met needs, alleviated suffering, and encouraged weary souls. This contextual detail proves that Philemon’s love was active hospitality and kindness, laying the groundwork for Paul’s upcoming appeal for reconciliation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Colossians 1:4 — "...since we heard of your faith in Christ Jesus and of your love for all the saints..."

  • Galatians 5:22 — "But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faithfulness..."

  • 1 John 4:7 — "Beloved, let us love one another, for love is of God; and everyone who loves is born of God and knows God."

  • Hebrews 6:10 — "For God is not unjust to forget your work and labor of love which you have shown toward His name, in that you have ministered to the saints, and do minister."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The relationship between love and spiritual refreshment is central to Paul’s argument. Loving action restores strength to believers who are weary or suffering. Philemon’s ministry of love was not an isolated gesture, but a pattern of life that invigorated the local church. This reminder reinforces that Christian love is designed to build up, comfort, and restore people within the community of faith.
