VERY - (polýs, Strong's G4183)

Summary:

The Greek adjective polys indicates a large quantity, extensive degree, or great amount. Depending on context, it is translated as "many" when referring to countable items or people, and as "much," "great," or "very" when describing degree, intensity, or extent. In Philemon 1:8, the word describes the great extent of Paul's boldness and authority in Christ, setting up a striking contrast with his choice to appeal to Philemon out of love rather than demanding compliance.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Epistle to Philemon is a personal letter written by the apostle Paul from prison. Paul wrote to Philemon, a Christian leader in Colosse, on behalf of Onesimus, a runaway servant who had become a believer through Paul's ministry.

In verse 8, Paul addresses the nature of his relationship with Philemon and his spiritual standing as an apostle. Before making his request regarding Onesimus, Paul notes that he possesses full apostolic freedom and authority to issue a direct command. The word polys is used here to describe the high degree of confidence and official weight Paul carried "in Christ" to require what was fitting.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Polys fundamentally signifies abundance in quantity, size, degree, or intensity.

When used with countable nouns, the word means "many" or "numerous." When paired with uncounted or abstract concepts—such as fear, joy, grace, peace, or confidence—it means "much," "great," "abundant," or "very."

In Philemon 1:8, the word describes an extensive degree of boldness or freedom of speech. Rather than describing a physical amount, it highlights the full measure of apostolic authority Paul had at his disposal.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, polys appears in a wide variety of settings. It describes large crowds, extensive periods of time, heavy burdens, deep emotions, and spiritual blessings.

When applied to personal speech or official authority, polys stresses that the speaker is not acting with hesitant or partial confidence. It indicates complete freedom and unreserved authority to speak directly. In Paul's letters, high degrees of confidence or boldness are grounded not in personal pride, but in the authority granted by Jesus Christ.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of polys in Philemon 1:8 helps establish an important principle regarding Christian authority and leadership:

  • True spiritual authority is real and extensive. Paul did not lack the right to command; his authority in Christ was complete and "very bold."

  • Authority in the church is meant to be exercised with restraint and love. Possessing authority does not mean one must always enforce it by mandate.

  • Voluntary obedience driven by love is far more valuable in the kingdom of God than forced compliance driven by duty or pressure.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philemon 1:8–9, Paul deliberately sets his authority alongside his love for Philemon. He begins by acknowledging that he has "very bold" confidence in Christ to command Philemon to do what is proper.

By emphasizing how great his authority actually was, Paul highlights the grace of his chosen approach. He intentionally sets aside his prerogative to command and instead appeals to Philemon on the basis of love. The word polys makes the contrast clear: Paul's apostolic right was great, but his desire for Philemon's willing, love-motivated response was greater.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Philemon 1:8, the word appears in an accusative singular feminine form, functioning grammatically to modify the noun representing boldness or freedom of speech. This form directly measures the intensity of Paul's confidence.

Understanding this word helps readers avoid two misinterpretations: Paul was neither timid nor uncertain about his authority, nor was he dictatorial. By explicitly recognizing his "very bold" authority before declining to use it as a weapon, Paul models pastoral wisdom and Christlike humility.
