AFFECTION - (splánchnon, Strong's G4698)

Summary:

The Greek word splanchnon originally referred to the internal organs—such as the heart, liver, and lungs—which ancient people regarded as the seat of deep, visceral feelings. In the New Testament, it is used metaphorically to describe deep tender affection, compassion, and heartfelt love. In Philippians 2:1, Paul lists "affection" alongside divine encouragement, love, fellowship, and mercy as a spiritual reality already active among believers in Christ, serving as the ground for their Christian unity.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek thought, the human body was used to describe emotional life. While the mind was viewed as the center of thought, the deep physical interior—the internal organs—was seen as the origin of intense feeling and compassion. Rather than referring merely to surface-level emotions, the word pointed to an inward response arising from the core of a person.

When New Testament writers used this word metaphorically, they retained that sense of inward depth. It speaks of a warm and tender concern that moves a person from within. In Scripture, this term expressed both the deep mercy of God toward humanity and the genuine personal care that believers ought to feel for one another.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Literally, splanchnon refers to the noble upper internal organs. Metaphorically, it refers to deep tender affection, tenderheartedness, compassion, or heartfelt love.

Depending on who is expressing it, the word can refer to:

  • Deep visceral pity or compassion that moves someone to act for another in distress.

  • Warm, heartfelt love felt toward spiritual brothers and sisters.

  • The seat of personal care and deep emotion within a person, often translated as "heart" or "affections."

In Philippians 2:1, the noun is nominative, plural, and neuter, appearing in a list of spiritual blessings that flow from life in Christ. Here it refers to the tender affection and heartfelt compassion that God produces among His people.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across its biblical occurrences, splanchnon carries the idea of love that is felt deeply at the innermost level. It is not cold, detached, or merely formal. Instead, it describes a personal, inward warmth toward others.

The word appears in several distinct settings:

  • Divine Mercy: It describes the tender mercy of God, such as the mercy that brought salvation in the arrival of Christ.

  • Apostolic Devotion: Paul uses the word to express his deep personal longing and love for churches and ministry partners, showing that Christian ministry involves real emotional connection.

  • Christian Fellowship: It describes the genuine affection believers have for one another, which can be opened wide in love or closed off through selfishness.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of splanchnon in Christian teaching demonstrates that the gospel transforms human affections. God's grace does not merely correct a person's thinking or regulate outward behavior; it renews the human heart so that believers feel true compassion for one another.

First, tender affection originates in God Himself. Human compassion in the Christian life reflects the deep mercy God has shown toward sinners in Christ. Second, this affection is produced by the Holy Spirit in the body of Christ. Christian unity is not held together merely by organizational structure or social similarity, but by shared spiritual life that produces authentic personal care. Finally, spiritual affection manifests in visible action. Biblical compassion begins as an inward movement of the heart and leads naturally to practical expressions of mercy.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:1, Paul presents a series of four conditional statements that assume a positive answer: "Therefore if there is any consolation in Christ, if any comfort of love, if any fellowship of the Spirit, if any affection and mercy..." Paul is not questioning whether these realities exist; he is reminding the Philippians of what they already possess in Christ.

By pairing "affection" (splanchnon) with "mercy," Paul highlights the warm, internal root of unity alongside its outward expression. The Philippian church was experiencing internal strains and personal disagreements. Rather than issuing a harsh command to get along, Paul appeals to the spiritual reality they share. Because they are united to Christ, they have received divine comfort and fellowship, and the Holy Spirit has placed genuine tender affection within them. This shared inward life forms the foundation for the command that follows in verse 2: to be like-minded, having the same love, and being of one accord.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 1:78 — "Through the tender mercy of our God, with which the Dayspring from on high has visited us..."

Here the word is translated "tender" to modify "mercy," highlighting the warm, compassionate heart of God in sending the Savior.

  • Philippians 1:8 — "For God is my witness, how greatly I long for you all with the affection of Jesus Christ."

Paul uses the word to express his deep emotional longing for the church, showing that his love for them springs from Christ's own love in him.

  • Colossians 3:12 — "Therefore, as the elect of God, holy and beloved, put on tender mercies, kindness, humility, meekness, longsuffering..."

The word appears as "tender" in the phrase "tender mercies," describing the inward compassion believers are called to wear as part of their new identity in Christ.

  • 1 John 3:17 — "But whoever has this world's goods, and sees his brother in need, and shuts up his heart from him, how does the love of God abide in him?"

The word is translated "heart," warning against closing off one's deep inward compassion when faced with a brother in clear physical need.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The plural neuter form of splanchnon in Philippians 2:1 reflects the ancient Greek convention of treating the internal organs as a collective whole representing deep human emotion. Translating it as "affection" captures the warm internal bond that Paul assumes exists among believers who share in the Spirit.

Understanding this word helps clarify the nature of biblical Christian unity. Unity in the New Testament is never presented as mere outward compliance or formal agreement. It involves a genuine renewal of the heart's affections. The gospel creates a spiritual family where believers care deeply for one another from the inside out.
