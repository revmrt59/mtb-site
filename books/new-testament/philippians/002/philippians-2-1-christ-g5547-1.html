CHRIST - (Christós, Strong's G5547)

Summary:

The word means "Anointed One" or "Messiah." It originates as a title for God's chosen King and Savior, who fulfills Old Testament prophecy. In Philippians 2:1, Paul refers to "Christ" as the source and sphere of spiritual encouragement ("consolation in Christ"), pointing believers to their shared life in Him as the ultimate foundation for unity and selflessness in the church.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The term Christos comes from a Greek verb meaning to smear or anoint with oil. In the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament, the word was used to translate the Hebrew term Mashiach (Messiah). Anointing with oil was the sacred act by which God set apart kings, priests, and prophets for His divine service.

By the time of the New Testament, "Christ" designated the promised descendant of David whom God would send to redeem His people and rule over an eternal kingdom. As the apostles proclaimed the gospel, the word functioned both as a royal title ("the Christ") and as a personal name for Jesus of Nazareth.

In the Epistle to the Philippians, Paul writes from imprisonment to a local congregation facing external pressure and internal disagreements. In Philippians 2:1, he points the church back to the core realities of their shared salvation, grounding his appeal for unity in what they possess together in Christ.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of the word is "anointed one."

In ancient Greek culture outside the Bible, the term referred broadly to anything smeared or anointed, such as oil applied to a surface. In biblical usage, however, the word takes on a rich redemptive meaning:

  • It refers to the Messiah promised in the Old Testament Scriptures.

  • It identifies Jesus as God's appointed Savior, High Priest, and King.

  • It serves as a title and name for Jesus, frequently paired with prepositions (such as "in Christ") to describe the believer's spiritual connection to Him.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, the word displays a clear development in focus:

  • In the Gospels, the term often appears as a royal title. Jesus' contemporaries debated whether He was indeed "the Christ," God's promised King.

  • In the Book of Acts, early Christian preaching focused on proving from the Old Testament Scriptures that Jesus was the Christ through His life, death, and resurrection.

  • In the Epistles, particularly in Paul's writings, the term is used both as a proper name ("Christ Jesus" or "Jesus Christ") and as a description of spiritual location. Phrases like "in Christ" describe the believer's salvation, Security, and vital union with the Lord.

4. Theological Implications

The title "Christ" connects Jesus directly to God's covenant promises throughout redemptive history. Calling Jesus the Christ affirms that He is God's designated King who fulfills the law and the prophets, establishes God's kingdom, and saves His people from sin.

The phrase "in Christ" highlights the corporate reality of salvation. Believers do not merely follow teachings; they share a real, spiritual connection with the risen Savior. Because all believers share in the same Christ, personal rivalries and divisions in the local church are exposed as contrary to the gospel.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:1, Paul opens a series of conditional statements: "Therefore if there is any consolation in Christ..." In Greek, the noun appears in the dative case as a masculine singular form, connected with the preposition "in." This construction identifies Christ as the sphere in which encouragement and spiritual comfort are found.

Paul is not expressing doubt about whether encouragement exists in Christ. Instead, he assumes it as an undeniable fact. His point is straightforward: since believers genuinely experience comfort and strength in their relationship with Christ, that shared reality ought to produce harmony, affection, and mutual humility among them. The objective blessing of being in Christ creates the moral obligation to live in unity with one another.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 11:27 — "She said to Him, 'Yes, Lord, I believe that You are the Christ, the Son of God, who is to come into the world.'"

  • Acts 17:3 — "...explaining and demonstrating that the Christ had to suffer and rise again from the dead, and saying, 'This Jesus whom I preach to you is the Christ.'"

  • Romans 6:8 — "Now if we died with Christ, we believe that we shall also live with Him..."

  • Philippians 3:8 — "Yet indeed I also count all things loss for the excellence of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The mention of "Christ" in Philippians 2:1 directly introduces the passage that leads to the famous account of Christ's humility in Philippians 2:5-11. Before Paul calls believers to follow Christ's example of self-emptying love, he first reminds them of the comfort and grace they have already received in Him. Spiritual growth and humble service flow out of what Christ has already done for the believer.
