LOVE - (agápē, Strong's G26)

Summary:

The word agape refers to love, warm affection, goodwill, or sacrificial devotion. While the word can refer broadly to affection or commitment, New Testament writers use it most often to describe God's benevolent care for humanity and the self-giving care believers are called to show one another. In Philippians 2:1, love describes the refreshing, encouraging care experienced in Christ and within the Christian community. Paul highlights this experienced love to appeal to the Philippians, urging them to live in practical unity, humility, and mutual care.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient Greek language, several words expressed different aspects of love, ranging from romantic desire to family affection and friendship. The noun agape and its related verb were used in everyday Greek to express affection, regard, or preferring one thing or person over another.

In the New Testament, Christian writers filled this word with deep spiritual meaning. They used agape to describe the unique love revealed through Jesus Christ—a love rooted in God's character rather than the worthiness of the recipient. In his letter to the Philippians, Paul writes from prison to a church he knows and loves deeply. Facing external opposition and potential internal friction, Paul reminds the Philippian believers of the spiritual blessings they share in Christ, using agape to point them toward holy unity and selflessness.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Agape means love, goodwill, warm affection, or devoted care. It describes an active commitment to seek the good of another person.

The word can refer to:

  • God's deep and active concern for lost humanity.

  • A believer's love and devotion toward God.

  • Practical, unselfish love shown between human beings, especially within the church family.

While secular Greek often used the term simply for general affection or preference, the New Testament elevates agape by grounding its meaning in God's character and Christ's sacrificial life. It is not merely an emotion, but a love that chooses to give, serve, and build up others.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, agape appears in several distinct settings:

  • God's Love for Believers: Scripture presents God's love as the supreme source of security and salvation for His people. It is an enduring, unshakeable commitment that safe-guards believers through every trial.

  • The Fruit of the Spirit: The Holy Spirit produces love in the hearts of believers. It stands as the primary evidence of spiritual life and growth.

  • The Bond of Christian Community: Agape serves as the standard for how Christians treat one another. It seeks peace, practice patience, overcomes personal offense, and promotes unity within the local assembly.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical teaching on agape reveals vital truths about God and Christian living:

  • God is the Source: Human beings cannot manufacture true Christian love on their own strength. Genuine love flows from God, who demonstrated His love supreme by sending His Son to redeem sinners.

  • Love Demands Practical Expression: Biblical love is never purely theoretical. It expresses itself through concrete acts of service, forgiveness, kindness, and humility.

  • Love Is Essential for Church Unity: Sound doctrine and zealous ministry must be accompanied by love. Without love, spiritual gifts and church activities lose their true value and purpose.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:1, Paul builds a powerful appeal based on the shared spiritual experiences of the Philippians. He writes: "Therefore if there is any consolation in Christ, if any comfort of love, if any fellowship of the Spirit, if any affection and mercy..."

Paul's use of "if" does not express doubt. Rather, it means "since these things are true." He assumes the Philippians have truly tasted these blessings.

The phrase "comfort of love" pairs agape with encouragement or tender relief. Paul asks the believers to remember the soothing relief and strength they have received from Christ's love and from loving brothers and sisters in the faith. By bringing this comforting love to their minds, Paul sets the stage for his direct command in verse 2: that they be like-minded, have the same love, and live in harmony. The love they have experienced becomes the motive to put away self-centered ambition and serve one another humbly.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 15:10 — "If you keep My commandments, you will abide in My love, just as I have kept My Father’s commandments and abide in His love."

  • Romans 8:35 — "Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? Shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword?"

  • Galatians 5:22 — "But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faithfulness..."

  • 1 John 4:7 — "Beloved, let us love one another, for love is of God; and everyone who loves is born of God and knows God."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Grammatical Function: In Philippians 2:1, the noun love is in the genitive singular feminine form. It functions grammatically to describe the nature and source of the "comfort" Paul mentions. It is a comfort that comes directly from love.

  • Contextual Connection: Notice how Paul returns to this word in verse 2, telling the church to have "the same love." The love that comforts believers in verse 1 becomes the exact love they must extend to each other in verse 2.
