BOW - (kámptō, Strong's G2578)

Summary:

The word kamptō means to bend, flex, or bow down, especially referring to bending the knee as a gesture of submission, reverence, or prayer. In Philippians 2:10, Paul uses the word to declare that every creature in all of creation will ultimately bow before the exalted Jesus, acknowledging His absolute Lordship and sovereign authority over all things.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient cultures, physical posture expressed the heart's attitude toward authority. Bending the knee was a standard bodily gesture of humble submission, reverence, and allegiance before a king or deity. Refusing to bow signaled rebellion, while bowing demonstrated submission and worship.

When Paul wrote to the church at Philippi, he echoed the words of Isaiah 45:23, where God declared that every knee would bow to Him alone. By applying this image to the exalted Lord Jesus, Paul presented Christ as sharing the very authority and glory of God. In a Roman colony like Philippi, where citizens were expected to render ultimate allegiance to the emperor, Paul's declaration that every knee must bow to Jesus carried clear spiritual and practical weight.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word kamptō means to bend or bow, specifically used for bending a joint such as the knee.

In general usage, it describes the physical movement of bending something flexible or bowing down before a superior authority. When a person is the subject, bowing the knee signifies an act of personal submission, reverence, or earnest prayer.

In Scripture, the word regularly appears alongside the word for "knee." The physical posture serves as an outward expression of yielding oneself completely to the authority of another.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses kamptō to highlight submission and worship before the living God. The physical act of bowing the knee communicates several distinct biblical postures:

First, it describes complete religious devotion and refusal to worship false gods. In Romans 11:4, God speaks of the remnant who had not bowed their knee to Baal. Here, refusing to bow to an idol demonstrates undivided allegiance to the Lord.

Second, it expresses earnest and reverent prayer. In Ephesians 3:14, Paul writes that he bows his knees before the Father in deeply focused, humble intercession for the believers.

Third, it represents universal, sovereign submission. In Romans 14:11 and Philippians 2:10, bowing the knee describes the final, universal acknowledgment of God's righteous authority. Every knee will bow before the sovereign Lord.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of kamptō in Philippians 2:10 centers on the supreme exaltation of Jesus Christ. Paul places this action immediately following Christ's humiliation—His willingness to take the form of a servant and endure death on a cross. Because of Christ's perfect obedience, God highly exalted Him and gave Him the name above every name.

The requirement that every knee bow to Jesus establishes His full authority and divine identity. Paul draws directly from Isaiah 45:23, where Yahweh swears that every knee shall bow to Him. By connecting this prophecy to Jesus, the New Testament proclaims that Jesus shares the divine title and sovereign reign of God.

Furthermore, the scope of this submission is all-encompassing: "of those in heaven, and of those on earth, and of those under the earth." Heavenly beings, living human beings, and those who have passed away will all bow. The context shows that no entity in the universe can resist or deny the supreme authority of Jesus Christ.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:10, kamptō describes the intended outcome of Christ's exaltation. Grammatically, the verb is in the aorist tense, active voice, subjunctive mood, and singular number, agreeing with the collective subject "every knee." This presents the bowing as the direct result of God exalting Jesus and giving Him the supreme name.

The immediate context of Philippians 2:5–11 provides a powerful pastoral framework. Paul is encouraging believers to adopt the same humble mindset that Christ displayed. Jesus emptied Himself, took the posture of a servant, and submitted to death. In response, God exalted Him to the highest place.

The word "bow" emphasizes that humility before God leads to ultimate vindication. While believers voluntarily bow their knees to Jesus now in faith and worship, a day is coming when the truth of Christ's Lordship will be openly recognized across the entire creation.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The combination of bending the knee (kamptō) and confessing with the tongue creates a complete picture of submission. Bending the knee represents physical alignment and bodily yielding, while confessing with the tongue represents verbal acknowledgment. Together, they demonstrate total submission to Christ.

For believers, bowing the knee to Jesus is not a forced obligation, but a joyful privilege of worshiping the rightful King. Pastoral comfort comes from knowing that the Savior whom believers serve today in quiet faith is the very Lord before whom all creation will eventually bow.
