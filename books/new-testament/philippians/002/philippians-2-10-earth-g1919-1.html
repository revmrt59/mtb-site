EARTH - (epígeios, Strong's G1919)

Summary:

The word epigeios describes that which exists on or belongs to the earth, distinguishing earthly realms and inhabitants from heavenly or subterranean ones. In Philippians 2:10, the word refers to those who dwell upon the earth, emphasizing that every living human being in the earthly realm will ultimately bow before the supreme authority of the exalted Lord Jesus Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Greek word epigeios is a compound form joining the preposition epi (meaning "upon") with gē (meaning "earth" or "land"). At its most fundamental level, the word refers to that which is situated upon the earth or belongs to the physical sphere of human existence.

In ancient Greek literature, the word was used in a neutral sense to describe earthly creatures, physical terrain, or human affairs, contrasting them with the sky, stars, or divine realm above. In the New Testament, it serves primarily as a descriptive term comparing or contrasting physical, temporal realities with heavenly, spiritual ones.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Epigeios means "earthly," "terrestrial," "existing on earth," or "belonging to the earth." Depending on its context, the word can refer to:

  • Physical objects or natural bodies belonging to the physical world.

  • Human beings currently living upon the earth.

  • Earthly thoughts, perspectives, or wisdom bound to temporal existence.

The word itself is a descriptive term of location or nature. It does not inherently carry a negative or sinful meaning. Any moral tone—whether neutral, honorable, or foolish—comes entirely from the context in which it is used rather than from the lexical definition of the word itself.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses epigeios across a variety of contexts to draw distinctions between the physical world and heavenly reality:

  • Physical Nature: It describes mortal human bodies in contrast to heavenly, resurrected bodies.

  • Human Perspective: It can describe earth-bound human wisdom or thinking when set in contrast to divine wisdom from above.

  • Inhabitants of the World: It describes humanity living upon the physical earth alongside spiritual beings in heaven and those beneath the earth.

4. Theological Implications

In Philippians 2:10, Paul arranges created existence into three comprehensive realms: those in heaven, those on earth, and those under the earth. By including those on earth, Scripture establishes that human physical history and the material creation are explicitly included under Christ's universal sovereignty.

The humiliation of Jesus—His willing obedience to take the form of a servant and die on a cross—resulted in His supreme exaltation by God the Father. God bestowed upon Him the name above every name, demanding worship and submission from every level of creation. Earthly human life is not neutral territory; every person living upon the earth stands under the sovereign authority of Jesus Christ.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:5–11, Paul presents the grand arc of Christ's humiliation and subsequent exaltation. After Jesus humbled Himself to the point of death on a cross, God highly exalted Him and declared that at the name of Jesus every knee must bow.

The target word appears in verse 10 within the phrase translated "of those on earth." The authoritative morphology identifies this specific word form as genitive, plural, and masculine. In this sentence, the masculine plural form functions as a noun referring to "earthly ones" or "those on earth"—that is, human beings dwelling in the present physical world.

Paul places earthly inhabitants between heavenly beings above and subterranean beings below. This three-fold classification highlights the absolute scope of Christ's authority. No person currently living on earth falls outside Christ's realm. Whether willingly in faith now or ultimately in judgment, every human knee on earth will bow in acknowledgment of His lordship.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 3:12 — "If I have told you earthly things and you do not believe, how will you believe if I tell you heavenly things?" Here the word (rendered "earthly") describes basic physical illustrations and earthly realities that Jesus used to teach spiritual truths.

  • 1 Corinthians 15:40 — "There are also celestial bodies and terrestrial bodies; but the glory of the celestial is one, and the glory of the terrestrial is another." Paul uses the word (rendered "terrestrial") to contrast physical, earthly bodies with heavenly bodies.

  • 2 Corinthians 5:1 — "For we know that if our earthly house, this tent, is destroyed, we have a building from God, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens." Here the word (rendered "earthly") describes our present mortal physical body in contrast to our future eternal heavenly home.

  • Philippians 3:19 — "whose end is destruction, whose god is their belly, and whose glory is in their shame—who set their mind on earthly things." In this context, the word (rendered "earthly") describes a mind focused exclusively on temporal, worldly desires rather than on heavenly hope.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The declaration in Philippians 2:10 echoes Old Testament passages where God alone receives universal worship, such as Isaiah 45:23, where YHWH declares that to Him every knee shall bow. By applying this total submission to Jesus Christ, Paul asserts the full deity and universal Lordship of Christ over all earthly and heavenly creation.

For believers living on earth today, this passage provides great assurance and clear direction. Worship of Christ is not reserved merely for a future heavenly realm; it defines the present reality and purpose of human life on earth.
