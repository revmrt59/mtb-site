EARTH - (katachthónios, Strong's G2709)

Summary:

The word katachthonios refers to that which is located under the earth or subterranean. In Philippians 2:10, it describes "those under the earth"—beings residing in the realm beneath the earth. By combining this word with heavenly and earthly realm designations, Paul establishes that the supreme authority of the exalted Jesus Christ extends over every realm of existence without exception.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Philippians 2:5-11 contains one of the most magnificent declarations of Christ's identity and work in the New Testament. Often called the "Christ Hymn," this passage traces Jesus’ voluntary condescension from divine glory to humble human servant, culminating in His obedient death on a Roman cross. Because of His complete submission, God highly exalted Him and bestowed on Him the name above every name.

In verse 10, Paul describes the universal response to Christ's exaltation. Every knee must bow and every tongue confess His Lordship. To emphasize that no area of creation is excluded, Paul outlines a three-part scope of creation: those in heaven, those on earth, and those under the earth. The term katachthonios identifies this third group, completing the comprehensive scope of Christ's sovereign reign.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Katachthonios combines the preposition kata ("down" or "under") with the root chthōn ("earth" or "ground"). It describes something situated underground, subterranean, or existing beneath the surface of the earth.

When used of beings or inhabitants, the word refers to those who occupy the realm under the ground. In this passage, the word is used in a masculine plural form, functioning as a noun phrase to mean "those under the earth" or "subterranean beings." The word itself specifies location—under the ground—rather than defining the exact spiritual nature of every individual entity in that realm.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In Philippians 2:10, katachthonios acts alongside two other realm-designating descriptions: "those in heaven" (epouranios) and "those on earth" (epigeios). Together, these three descriptive terms map out all created space:

  • The heavenly realm above

  • The earthly surface realm

  • The subterranean realm below

Grammatically, the word is formatted in the genitive case, plural number, and masculine gender. This indicates that Paul is not merely talking about physical dirt or underground geology, but about entities or beings associated with that realm. By stringing these three terms together, Paul leaves no room for any created being to claim exemption from Christ's authority.

4. Theological Implications

The inclusion of "those under the earth" carries significant theological weight regarding the absolute sovereignty of Jesus Christ:

  • Total Universal Lordship: Christ’s exaltation is not local, regional, or partial. His authority covers every square inch of the universe, including the dark and hidden places below.

  • Forced and Voluntary Submission: While believers on earth and angels in heaven bow in joyful worship, spiritual enemies, demoted powers, and fallen entities under the earth are ultimately compelled to recognize His supreme authority. Every creature will acknowledge Jesus as Lord.

  • Overcoming the Power of Death: In the biblical worldview, the underworld is frequently associated with the realm of the dead and demonic forces. By explicitly declaring that those under the earth bow to Jesus, Scripture affirms that Christ's victory on the cross completely disarmed host powers and shattered the supreme hold of death.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In the immediate context of Philippians 2, Paul introduces this glorious vision of Christ to serve a direct pastoral purpose among believers. In verses 1-4, Paul urges the Philippian church to practice genuine humility, selflessness, and unity. He then presents Jesus as the ultimate model: the One who lowered Himself to the extreme shame of the cross was raised by God to the absolute height of cosmic ruler.

The mention of "those under the earth" marks the extreme lower boundary of creation. Jesus descended into the lowest depths through death, and as a result, God placed Him over every realm, from the highest heaven to the deepest abyss. If every spirit and power in the universe must bow before Jesus, believers can trust His supreme power and follow His example of humble service without fear.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • This term is unique to this context and does not appear elsewhere in the biblical corpus.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The language Paul uses in Philippians 2:10 echoes the prophetic declaration of Isaiah 45:23, where God proclaims that every knee will bow and every tongue will take an oath to Him alone. By taking an Old Testament passage that describes the exclusive worship due to God and applying it directly to the exalted Jesus—including "those under the earth"—Paul makes an unmistakable statement about the divine deity and supreme rule of Christ. No power in the underworld can alter or escape God's ultimate decree.
