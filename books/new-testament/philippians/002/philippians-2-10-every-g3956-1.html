EVERY - (pâs, Strong's G3956)

Summary:

The word pas is the standard Greek adjective for totality, meaning "all," "every," or "the whole." When paired with a singular noun, it points to each individual member of a group without exception. In Philippians 2:10, pas modifies "knee," emphasizing that every single created entity without exception will ultimately bow before the exalted name of Jesus Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Greek word pas is one of the most common words in the New Testament. It expresses the idea of absolute totality, completeness, or universal scope. It can refer to all people, every item in a category, or the entirety of a matter.

In Philippians 2:5–11, Paul presents a profound description of Jesus Christ. He traces Christ's journey from supreme divine glory down to the deepest humility of taking on human form and dying on a Roman cross. Because of Christ's voluntary humiliation and perfect obedience, God highly exalted Him and gave Him the name above every name. In verse 10, Paul uses pas to describe the universal response that this exaltation demands from all of creation.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word pas means "all," "every," "whole," or "every kind of." Its precise rendering depends on how it connects to surrounding words in a sentence:

  • When used with a singular noun without an article, it usually means "every" individual unit of that noun (for example, "every knee" or "every tree").

  • When used with a plural noun, it means "all" members of that collective group.

  • When used as a standalone noun, it means "everyone" or "everything."

The word itself simply establishes full scope. It leaves no exceptions within whatever class or group the writer is discussing.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across New Testament writings, pas serves as an essential tool for expressing total scope. Writers use it to stress that a truth applies universally, without partiality or distinction.

For example, when Scripture speaks of God's sovereign knowledge, pas indicates that He knows "all" things. When describing human sinfulness, it clarifies that "all" have sinned. When describing the gospel offer, it assures readers that "everyone" who calls on the Lord will be saved. In every case, pas ensures that the reader does not narrow down a truth that God intended to be comprehensive.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of pas highlights the vast scope of God's sovereign plan. God does not deal with humanity in partial or fragmented ways; His kingdom power, His gospel offer, and His ultimate judgment apply to the entire created world.

However, the word pas itself does not carry a hidden theological doctrine. It simply means "all" or "every." The spiritual weight comes from what pas modifies. When attached to God's promises, it assures believers that no hardship falls outside His care. When attached to Christ's authority, it reminds the world that no person or power can escape His ultimate rule.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:10, pas functions as an adjective in the neuter nominative singular form, directly modifying the word "knee." The phrase reads "every knee should bow."

Paul immediately expands this phrase to show just how far "every" extends. He lists three distinct realms: "those in heaven, and of those on earth, and of those under the earth." By combining pas with these three realms, Paul leaves no room for any exception:

  • Heavenly beings (angels and spiritual hosts)

  • Earthly beings (living humanity)

  • Beings under the earth (the dead and fallen powers)

The contrast in the passage is striking. The One who humbled Himself to the point of a shameful death is now the very One before whom every single knee will bow. The word "every" reinforces that Christ's victory is absolute. Whether willingly in faithful worship or reluctantly under divine judgment, every created subject will acknowledge Jesus as Lord.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 13:17 — "And when He said these things, all His adversaries were put to shame; and all the people rejoiced for all the glorious things that were done by Him."

  • Acts 18:4 — "And he reasoned in the synagogue every Sabbath, and persuaded both Jews and Greeks."

  • Ephesians 3:21 — "to Him be glory in the church by Christ Jesus to all generations, forever and ever. Amen."

  • 1 John 3:20 — "For if our heart condemns us, God is greater than our heart, and knows all things."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Paul’s phrasing in Philippians 2:10 directly echoes the Old Testament passage in Isaiah 45:23, where God declares, "To Me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall take an oath." In Isaiah, Yahweh asserts His sole authority as the only true God and Savior of the world.

By applying this exact language to Jesus Christ, Paul presents a clear testimony to the divinity of Christ. The absolute universal submission that belongs exclusively to God the Father is now shared by the exalted Son. For believers, this brings deep pastoral comfort: the Lord we serve holds ultimate authority over every force, power, and circumstance in the universe.
