HEAVEN - (epouránios, Strong's G2032)

Summary:

The word epouranios means "heavenly" or "belonging to the realm of heaven." It describes what exists in, originates from, or characterizes the spiritual domain above the earthly realm. In Philippians 2:10, translated as "those in heaven," the word refers to the spiritual inhabitants of the heavenly realm, demonstrating that Christ's exalted authority commands submission from every created entity in heaven.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word epouranios combines the preposition epi (meaning "upon" or "in") with ouranos (meaning "heaven"). It describes that which is positioned in heaven, originates from heaven, or belongs to the higher celestial realm. In ancient Greek literature and biblical writing, it contrasts directly with earthly or physical things that belong strictly to the temporary, lower realm of human existence. While earthly things are subject to decay and limited vision, heavenly things belong to the enduring, invisible realm where God dwells in glory.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word epouranios can function as an adjective or as a noun-like expression. When used as an adjective, it means "heavenly," "celestial," or "existing in heaven." It describes places, bodies, gifts, or spiritual realities associated with God's dwelling place. When used as a noun-like expression, as in Philippians 2:10, it describes the inhabitants of heaven—the angelic forces, spiritual beings, and redeemed souls dwelling in the divine realm.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, epouranios describes distinct aspects of God's revealed truth:

  • Spiritual Realm: It highlights the unseen spiritual realm where Christ reigns at the right hand of God and where believers are blessed with every spiritual blessing.

  • Contrast of Bodies: It contrasts the temporary, mortal body of the present age with the glorified, imperishable resurrection body promised to believers.

  • Inhabitants of Heaven: It designates spiritual beings and celestial forces who occupy the heavenly places.

  • Divine Calling and Kingdom: It describes the origin and character of the believer's spiritual calling, hope, and ultimate eternal city prepared by God.

4. Theological Implications

The use of epouranios highlights the distinction between the created earthly order and the divine heavenly realm. While humanity often focuses on visible, earthly circumstances, Scripture uses this word to direct attention to the greater reality of God's unseen domain. It establishes that ultimate authority, eternal inheritance, and spiritual blessings originate in heaven. Furthermore, describing Christ as the ruler over heavenly things confirms His divine identity, showing that His lordship is not merely earthly or local, but cosmic and absolute.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:5–11, Paul presents the dramatic humiliation and exaltation of Jesus Christ. After describing how Christ humbled Himself by taking the form of a servant and enduring death on a cross, Paul announces that God super-exalted Him and gave Him the name above every name. In verse 10, Paul outlines the scope of this supreme exaltation by naming three distinct groups: "those in heaven, and of those on earth, and of those under the earth."

The target occurrence is a genitive plural masculine form used substantively to mean "those belonging to heaven." In this context, the word refers to all created spiritual beings in the celestial realm, including angels, archangels, and heavenly hosts. By placing those in heaven first among the three creation categories, Paul stresses that even the highest created spiritual authorities must bow before Jesus Christ. His victory on the cross leads to universal recognition of His supreme divine lordship.

  • 1 Corinthians 15:40 — "There are also celestial bodies and terrestrial bodies; but the glory of the celestial is one, and the glory of the terrestrial is another."

  • Ephesians 1:20 — "...which He worked in Christ when He raised Him from the dead and seated Him at His right hand in the heavenly places..."

  • Hebrews 3:1 — "Therefore, holy brethren, partakers of the heavenly calling, consider the Apostle and High Priest of our confession, Christ Jesus..."

  • 2 Timothy 4:18 — "And the Lord will deliver me from every evil work and preserve me for His heavenly kingdom. To Him be glory forever and ever. Amen."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Paul's phrase in Philippians 2:10 uses a three-part cosmic description—heaven, earth, and under the earth—to express total, unreserved completeness. This language reflects Old Testament passages like Isaiah 45:23, where God declares that every knee will bow to Him alone. By applying this universal bow to Jesus, Paul demonstrates that the exalted Christ receives the worship due to God Himself, acknowledged by every spiritual entity in the celestial realm.
