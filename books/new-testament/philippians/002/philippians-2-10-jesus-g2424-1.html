JESUS - (Iēsoûs, Strong's G2424)

Summary:

Iēsous is the proper personal name of the incarnate Son of God, corresponding to the Hebrew name Yēšûaʿ or Yĕhôšûaʿ, which means "Yahweh is salvation" or "Yahweh saves." Throughout the New Testament, it identifies the historical man from Nazareth who lived, died, rose from the dead, and ascended to heaven. In Philippians 2:10, Paul uses this personal name to declare that the very Jesus who humbled Himself to the death of the cross is now exalted to the highest place, so that every creation must bow before Him.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Iēsous is the Greek form of the Old Testament Hebrew name Yĕhôšûaʿ (Joshua) or its shortened post-exilic form Yēšûaʿ. In the Old Testament, Joshua was the leader who brought Israel into the Promised Land after the death of Moses. In the New Testament, God commanded that the Son of Mary be named Jesus because He would save His people from their sins. While the name was relatively common among Jewish men in the first century, it gained supreme, unique significance through the person, earthly work, resurrection, and ascension of Christ.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name Iēsous refers directly to the personal human name of the Savior. It expresses the core truth that salvation comes from God. In the context of the New Testament, the name functions as a proper noun designating Jesus of Nazareth. It is frequently combined with titles such as Christ (the Anointed Messiah) or Lord. The name highlights His true humanity alongside His divine mission to bring God's salvation to mankind.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In the Gospels, Iēsous is the primary name used to narrate Jesus' life, teaching, miracles, trials, and death. It anchors the gospel narrative in real human history. In the book of Acts and the Epistles, the name continues to identify the exalted Lord who now reigns in heaven. The persistent use of this personal name reminds believers that the risen, glorified Lord reigning at the right hand of God is the same Jesus who walked the roads of Galilee and gave His life on the cross.

4. Theological Implications

The name Iēsous grounds the gospel in historical reality. Salvation did not arrive through an abstract idea or an impersonal philosophy, but through a real person who took on human flesh. His earthly name connects His redeeming work directly to the reality of the incarnation. Furthermore, the exaltation of Jesus demonstrates that God honors humility. The one who bore a humble human name and submitted to an agonizing death is now revealed as the supreme Ruler of the universe.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:10, Paul writes within the context of Christ's deep humiliation and supreme exaltation. After explaining that Christ lowered Himself to the point of dying on a cross, Paul explains that God exalted Him and granted Him the name above every name. In verse 10, Paul declares that "at the name of Jesus every knee should bow." Grammatically, the name appears in a genitive singular masculine form, identifying Jesus as the one to whom this exalted name and status belong. The remarkable contrast in the passage is that the personal human name—Jesus—is directly linked to cosmic lordships where every creature in heaven, on earth, and under the earth must bow. The earthly servant who suffered is the exalted Sovereign over all creation.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Philippians 2:10, Paul draws upon language from Isaiah 45:23, where Yahweh declares that to Him every knee will bow and every tongue confess. By applying this Old Testament worship passage to "the name of Jesus," Paul places Jesus in the position of divine honor reserved for God alone, showing that worshiping Jesus honors God the Father.
