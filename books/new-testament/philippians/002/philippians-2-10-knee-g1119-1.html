KNEE - (góny, Strong's G1119)

Summary:

The word gonu refers literally to the human knee, the joint of the leg used for bending, bowing, or kneeling. In biblical usage, bowing or bending the knee signifies submission, reverence, homage, or earnest prayer. In Philippians 2:10, gonu represents the ultimate physical and personal homage that every creature in heaven, on earth, and under the earth will give to Jesus Christ, demonstrating that the exalted Savior receives universal honor as Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek usage and across the ancient biblical world, gonu refers to the knee. Bending the knee was a standard physical gesture signifying honor, deference, or lower status before a sovereign king, ruler, or deity. Rather than remaining standing as an equal, a person who kneeled demonstrated submission, allegiance, or urgent need.

In the Old Testament scriptures, God proclaimed that a day would come when every knee would bow exclusively to Him as the true God (Isaiah 45:23). When the apostle Paul wrote to the church at Philippi, he drew directly upon this Old Testament background. By applying the bowing of every knee to Jesus Christ, Paul presented Jesus as sharing the divine honor and sovereign rule of God Himself.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to:

  • The literal body part: the knee joint between the thigh and lower leg.

  • The physical posture of kneeling or bowing down as an act of prayer, humility, or worship.

  • Human physical strength or endurance, where weak or trembling knees represent bodily exhaustion or fear.

Here in Philippians 2:10, the word refers to the physical joint bent in an act of absolute submission and honor before Jesus Christ.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses gonu across several distinct contexts:

  • Worship and Honor: The word describes bowing down before divine authority in posture of reverence or allegiance.

  • Fervent Prayer: Bending the knees marks deep emotion, humility, and dependence when approaching God in prayer, as seen when believers or apostles knelt down to pray.

  • Bodily Exhaustion: The word is used symbolically for physical stamina or spiritual fatigue, where feeble knees must be strengthened.

  • Mockery: In the passion narrative, Roman soldiers knelt before Jesus in cruel mockery, pretending to give Him royal tribute.

4. Theological Implications

The focus of Philippians 2:9-11 is the exaltation of Jesus Christ following His earthly humiliation and death on the cross. God gave Him the name that is above every name, ensuring that every knee will bow at His name.

This passage establishes key theological truths:

  • The Divinity of Christ: By taking language reserved for God in the Old Testament and applying it to Jesus, Scripture affirms that Jesus possesses full divine dignity and authority.

  • Universal Sovereignty: The target word highlights that Christ's lordship is total. His authority extends over all intelligent creation, leaving no part of the universe outside His rule.

  • Voluntary and Involuntary Submission: Righteous angels and redeemed human beings bow the knee willingly in joy and worship. Hostile powers and unrepentant sinners will ultimately bow in forced recognition of His triumph.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:10, gonu functions as the subject of the clause ("every knee should bow"), grammatically set in the nominative singular form to express a collective reality across all creation.

The immediate passage contrasts Christ's radical self-emptying humility (Philippians 2:5-8) with God's ultimate exaltation of Him (Philippians 2:9-11). Because Christ humbled Himself to the point of death on a cross, God exalted Him to the highest place. The bowing of the knee is not earned by human effort, but is the divine result of Christ's finished work.

The phrase specifies three groups that bow: those in heaven (angelic beings), those on earth (living humanity), and those under the earth (the fallen or departed). The mention of the knee emphasizes that this universal acknowledgement will be visible, concrete, and absolute.

  • Ephesians 3:14 — "For this reason I bow my knees to the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ,"

  • Hebrews 12:12 — "Therefore strengthen the hands which hang down, and the feeble knees,"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Posture Reflects Reality: In Scripture, bodily posture often reflects the state of the heart. While outward kneeling can be faked—as in the mockery of the Roman soldiers—it is designed to reflect genuine devotion and recognition of authority.

  • Final Accountability: The parallel passage in Romans 14:11 links the bowing of the knee to God's final judgment, reminding believers that every creature must ultimately give an account to God.
