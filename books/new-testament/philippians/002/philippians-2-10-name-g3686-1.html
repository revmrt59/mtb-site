NAME - (ónoma, Strong's G3686)

Summary:

The word onoma refers directly to a personal name, proper title, reputation, or the authority represented by a named person. In Philippians 2:10, onoma refers to the supreme title, exalted rank, and divine authority granted by God the Father to the risen Lord Jesus Christ, demonstrating that all creation must ultimately bow in submission to His sovereign reign.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient Greco-Roman world and the Hebrew culture that shaped the New Testament, a name was far more than a convenient vocal label used to get someone's attention. A person's name stood for their full personal identity, character, reputation, status, and legal authority. To act "in the name of" a ruler or magistrate meant to act with that leader's explicit authorization and backing.

When Paul wrote his letter to the Philippians, the Roman imperial cult frequently proclaimed the Caesar as lord and emperor over all the earth. Within that cultural environment, Paul uses onoma in a majestic hymn celebrating Jesus Christ. Although Jesus stooped to the lowest point of human humility and death on a cross, God responded by exalting Him to the highest place and bestowing upon Him the supreme name above every other name.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to:

  • A proper personal name given to identify a specific individual.

  • A person's reputation, fame, or standing before others.

  • A title of rank, honor, or position.

  • The authority, power, or representation associated with someone in official standing.

In Philippians 2:10, the word means the exalted title and royal position bestowed upon Jesus following His resurrection and ascension. It represents the ultimate divine authority to which every creature in heaven, on earth, and under the earth must bow.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, onoma appears in a variety of everyday and spiritual settings. It designates individual people, distinguishes family names, and marks spiritual authority in ministry. Believers gather in Jesus' name, perform ministry in His name, and receive salvation through faith in His name.

Grammatically, in Philippians 2:10 the noun is in the dative case, singular number, and neuter gender. This grammatical form indicates the location or sphere of authority—"at the name of Jesus"—which serves as the focal point for all cosmic worship and submission. The focus is not simply on the sound of the word "Jesus," but on the supreme authority and glorious rank that God gave Him as exalted Lord.

4. Theological Implications

The exaltation of Jesus and the giving of this supreme name highlight the Father's response to Christ's obedience. Jesus took the form of a bondservant and submitted to the humiliation of the cross. God answered this supreme sacrifice by elevating Him to absolute ruler over the cosmos.

This passage echoes the language of the Old Testament prophets, where God declares that every knee will bow and every tongue will confess loyalty to Him alone. By applying this supreme divine worship to Jesus, Scripture affirms His true divine nature and Lordship. Jesus is given the highest honor, showing that worship directed to the exalted Son brings glory to God the Father.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2, Paul presents the self-emptying of Christ as the primary model for Christian humility and unity. He urges the believers in Philippi to look out for the interests of others and to cultivate the mind of Christ. By showing how Jesus humbled Himself and was subsequently exalted by God, Paul grounds Christian conduct in the triumphant reality of Christ's sovereignty.

The phrase "at the name of Jesus" assures the Philippian believers that their Lord reigns above every competing earthly power or hardship. Whether facing persecution from Roman authorities or internal disagreement within the church, the church rests in the guarantee that Jesus holds the highest authority in the universe. Every created being will ultimately acknowledge His sovereign rule.

  • 1 John 3:23 — "And this is His commandment: that we should believe on the name of His Son Jesus Christ and love one another, as He gave us commandment."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The declaration that every knee should bow "at the name of Jesus" establishes both a present reality for the church and a future outcome for all creation. Believers bow their hearts willingly today in joyful worship and obedience. Those who resist His rule in this life will eventually acknowledge His supreme authority when He returns.

Furthermore, calling Jesus "Lord" in connection with this supreme name reflects the translation practice of the ancient Greek Old Testament, where the divine name of God was rendered with the word for "Lord." To confess Jesus Christ as Lord is to declare that the crucified and risen Jesus shares the very authority and majesty of God.
