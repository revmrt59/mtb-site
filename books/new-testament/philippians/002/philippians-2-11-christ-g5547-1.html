CHRIST - (Christós, Strong's G5547)

Summary:

The word christos means "anointed one" and corresponds directly to the Hebrew concept of the Messiah. It identifies a king, priest, or prophet set apart by God for a special divine mission. In Philippians 2:11, the title "Christ" is joined with the personal name "Jesus" to declare that Jesus of Nazareth—the one who humbled Himself unto death—is God's exalted Messiah, openly confessed as Lord by all creation to the glory of God the Father.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The term christos originates from a root that means to smear or apply oil. In the ancient world, anointing with oil was a physical act used to consecrate individuals for specific sacred roles. In ancient Israel, God commanded that kings, high priests, and certain prophets be anointed as a visible sign of divine appointment and empowerment.

Over generations, Jewish hope centered on a promised ruler from the line of David who would be God's ultimate "Anointed One"—the Messiah. By the time of the New Testament, Greek-speaking Jewish readers used the word christos to translate this royal and messianic title. Writing to the believers in Philippi while under Roman imprisonment, Paul highlights Jesus Christ to encourage unity, humility, and steadfastness among Christians facing opposition.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word means "anointed," "anointed one," or "Messiah." It refers to a person formally set apart by God for a divine purpose. In the New Testament, the term functions both as an official title ("the Christ") and as part of a proper name ("Jesus Christ").

When applied to Jesus, the title means that He is God's appointed Savior and King. It does not merely describe an earthly title conferred by human authorities; it marks Jesus as the unique messenger and ruler sent directly by God to fulfill His redemptive promises.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In the Gospels, the term often appears as a title of expectation. People frequently asked whether Jesus was "the Christ"—the long-expected king who would deliver God's people. Jesus affirmed this title, though He redefined messianic kingship around suffering, service, and sacrifice rather than military conquest.

In the New Testament Epistles, "Christ" is frequently combined with "Jesus" (as in "Jesus Christ" or "Christ Jesus"). This combination unites the historical person—Jesus of Nazareth—with His divine office as God's anointed Messiah. The Apostles use the name to emphasize both His earthly ministry and His exalted post-resurrection glory.

4. Theological Implications

The title "Christ" links the ministry of Jesus to the ancient promises made throughout the Old Testament. It shows that God's salvation is not an unexpected detour, but the planned fulfillment of His covenant commitments.

Furthermore, the title highlights the divine authority given to Jesus. As the Anointed One, He holds the supreme offices of Prophet, Priest, and King. His messianic work required Him to suffer and die as a sacrifice for sin before receiving royal exaltation. Recognizing Jesus as Christ means acknowledging His divine appointment and resting in His completed work of redemption.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Philippians 2:5–11 presents a striking declaration of Jesus' humiliation and subsequent exaltation. Verses 6–8 describe how He voluntarily set aside His earthly rights, took the form of a servant, and submitted to death on a cross. Verses 9–11 show God's response: God exalted Him to the highest place and gave Him the name above every name.

In verse 11, Paul writes "that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father." The noun "Christ" appears here in the nominative case, masculine gender, and singular number, working together with "Jesus" as the subject of the confession. By pairing "Jesus" (His human name) with "Christ" (His messianic title) alongside "Lord" (His supreme divine authority), Paul asserts that the humbled, crucified Jesus is the victorious Messiah ruling over all creation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 11:27 — "She said to Him, 'Yes, Lord, I believe that You are the Christ, the Son of God, who is to come into the world.'"

  • Acts 17:3 — "...explaining and demonstrating that the Christ had to suffer and rise again from the dead, and saying, 'This Jesus whom I preach to you is the Christ.'"

  • Romans 6:8 — "Now if we died with Christ, we believe that we shall also live with Him..."

  • Colossians 3:1 — "If then you were raised with Christ, seek those things which are above, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The confession "Jesus Christ is Lord" in Philippians 2:11 draws directly upon the language of Isaiah 45:23, where God declares that every knee will bow and every tongue will take an oath to Him alone. By applying this Old Testament imagery directly to Jesus Christ, Paul places the anointed Son alongside God the Father in divine glory and universal authority.

This cosmic confession also serves a clear purpose for the church. In a Roman world where citizens were expected to proclaim Caesar as lord, confessing that "Jesus Christ is Lord" established the ultimate allegiance of the Christian community. Giving highest honor to the anointed Son brings direct praise and glory to God the Father.
