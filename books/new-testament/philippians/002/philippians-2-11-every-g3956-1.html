EVERY - (pâs, Strong's G3956)

Summary:

The Greek adjective pas fundamental means "all," "every," "whole," or "any." It is a word of total scope used to indicate every individual unit within a group or the entire sum of a collective group. In Philippians 2:11, the word is translated as "every" and grammatically agrees with the word for "tongue." Here, it establishes that the final, verbal acknowledgment of Jesus Christ as Lord will extend to every single tongue across all creation, leaving no creature excluded from honoring the exalted Son of God.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The adjective pas is one of the most frequent words in Biblical Greek for expressing totality, completeness, or comprehensive inclusion. It functions flexibly as an adjective, pronoun, or determiner. Depending on how it is framed grammatically, it can refer to every individual member of a class or the complete whole of a single object or collective body.

In Philippians 2:5–11, Paul presents a profound description of Jesus Christ's self-emptying humility and subsequent exaltation by God the Father. After Christ endured the ultimate humiliation of death on a cross, God highly exalted Him and gave Him the name above every name. In Philippians 2:11, pas appears in a passage describing the inescapable culmination of history, where every knee will bow and "every tongue" will proclaim that Jesus Christ is Lord.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word pas can mean "all," "every," "the whole," or "any." Its precise force depends on whether it appears in the singular or plural, and whether it is attached directly to a noun with or without an article.

  • When used in the singular without a definite article before the noun, pas typically means "every"—pointing to each individual instance or member of a category.

  • When used in the singular with an article, it often means "the whole" or "entirety" of something.

  • When used in the plural, it generally translates as "all," referring to the entirety of a group or collection.

In Philippians 2:11, the word appears in the nominative case, singular number, and feminine gender. It directly modifies the feminine singular noun for "tongue." In this position, the word directly means "every single," indicating that no tongue in existence falls outside the scope of this ultimate statement.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across New Testament writings, pas establishes the boundaries of a given statement. It serves to eliminate exceptions within the context where it is used. However, the exact nature of the totality is always defined by the surrounding passage.

In some contexts, pas describes absolute universality without exception, such as God's exhaustive knowledge of all things or the comprehensive extent of human sinfulness. In other contexts, it refers to all kinds or categories of things within a specific setting, such as preaching to all nations or healing every kind of disease. Understanding how pas functions requires looking closely at the noun it modifies and the central point the writer is making.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of pas in Philippians 2:10–11 carries tremendous theological weight regarding the supreme authority of Jesus Christ. God has set Jesus as Sovereign over the entire cosmos. By declaring that "every knee" will bow and "every tongue" will confess His lordship, Scripture reveals that Christ's rule is universal and inescapable.

This universal acknowledgment does not mean that every individual responds with saving faith, as Scripture clearly distinguishes elsewhere between voluntary worship and forced submission under judgment. Rather, pas highlights that no authority, human being, spiritual entity, or demonic power will be able to deny or avoid Christ's rightful status. Every single voice in the universe will ultimately acknowledge that Jesus Christ is Lord, bringing ultimate glory to God the Father.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:11, Paul deliberately pairs "every tongue" (pasa glossa) with "every knee" (pan gonu) from verse 10. The grammatical form of pas here is nominative, singular, and feminine, directly matching the noun for "tongue."

This pairing creates a vivid picture of physical submission (kneeling) and vocal acknowledgment (confessing). Paul draws this imagery from the Old Testament, specifically God's solemn declaration in Isaiah that every knee will bow and every tongue will take an oath to Him alone. By applying this totalizing language directly to Jesus, Paul demonstrates that Jesus shares the divine name, authority, and honor belonging to the one true God. The word "every" reinforces that there are no neutral observers in God's creation; all will verbally confess Christ's exaltation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Acts 18:4 – "And he reasoned in the synagogue every Sabbath, and persuaded both Jews and Greeks."

  • Titus 1:16 – "They profess to know God, but in works they deny Him, being abominable, disobedient, and disqualified for every good work."

  • 1 John 3:20 – "For if our heart condemns us, God is greater than our heart, and knows all things."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The universal scope introduced by pas in Philippians 2:11 connects directly to Paul's pastoral goal for the church in Philippi. In the opening verses of Philippians 2, Paul urges believers to be united, humble, and focused on the interests of others rather than selfish ambition.

By placing the humiliation and universal exaltation of Christ before the church, Paul shows that self-sacrificing humility is the path to divine honor. Since Christ will inevitably be recognized by "every tongue" across heaven, earth, and under the earth, believers can joyfully submit to His lordship now, knowing that history is heading toward the complete vindication and victory of their Lord.
