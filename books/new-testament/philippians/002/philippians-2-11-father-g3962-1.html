FATHER - (patḗr, Strong's G3962)

Summary:

The word patēr refers to a direct male parent, an ancestor, or God as the sovereign Creator and heavenly Father. In Philippians 2:11, the word identifies God as the Father to whose glory every knee bows and every tongue confesses that Jesus Christ is Lord. It reveals that the ultimate exaltation of Jesus Christ does not compete with God the Father, but brings Him supreme honor and praise.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek, patēr was the standard term for a biological father, a forefather, or a founder of a nation or group. In Jewish thought, God was understood as the Father who created all things, chose Israel, and cared for His people with protective affection.

When Jesus came, He brought this title into central focus, speaking of God as His own unique Father and teaching His followers to address God as their heavenly Father.

When Paul wrote his letter to the Philippians, the believers in Philippi lived in a prominent Roman colony where allegiance to imperial authority was paramount. Within this civic setting, Paul wrote to encourage unity, humility, and steadfast faith in Jesus Christ.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to:

  • A biological male parent or earthly father.

  • An ancestor, forefather, or patriarch in a family line.

  • A spiritual leader or respected elder.

  • God, as the Creator of all life and the divine Father in relation to His Son Jesus Christ and His adopted children.

When applied to God, patēr carries both authority and intimate care. It describes God not as an impersonal force, but as a sovereign, personal Father who initiates salvation and cares for His creation.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, patēr is used both for human parents and for God. When used of human parents, it often carries ideas of parental authority, inheritance, and family responsibility. When used for human ancestors, it connects later generations to their spiritual and historical roots.

When the word describes God, it takes on rich theological depth:

  • It highlights the unique personal relationship between God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

  • It reveals God's tender care, provision, and protection for believers.

  • It emphasizes God's sovereign initiative in sending His Son to accomplish redemption.

4. Theological Implications

The title "Father" brings essential clarity to the work of God in salvation. Salvation is orchestrated by the Father, accomplished by the Son, and applied by the Holy Spirit.

In the exaltation of Jesus Christ, there is perfect divine harmony. The Father exalts the Son because of Christ's perfect obedience, and the universal worship offered to the Son redounds directly to the glory of the Father. Acknowledging Jesus Christ as Lord does not divide divine glory; rather, honoring the Son is the very way the Father is glorified.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:11, patēr appears as part of the phrase "to the glory of God the Father." The word is in the genitive case, singular number, and masculine gender, specifying that the ultimate target of all cosmic praise is God the Father.

This passage forms the triumphant climax of the hymn in Philippians 2:5-11. Paul describes how Christ Jesus, though existing in the form of God, humbled Himself by taking the form of a servant and becoming obedient to the point of death on a cross. Because of Christ's self-emptying obedience, God highly exalted Him and gave Him the name above every name.

When every knee in heaven, on earth, and under the earth bows to Jesus, and every tongue confesses that He is Lord, this worship culminates in giving glory to God the Father. The passage demonstrates that the Son's exaltation and the Father's glory are inseparable.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 28:19 — "...baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit..."

  • Luke 10:22 — "All things have been delivered to Me by My Father..."

  • John 14:20 — "At that day you will know that I am in My Father, and you in Me, and I in you."

  • Galatians 1:3 — "Grace to you and peace from God the Father and our Lord Jesus Christ..."

  • 1 John 2:1 — "...And if anyone sins, we have an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • In Roman Philippi, household patriarchs held total legal authority over their families (paterfamilias), and the emperor was praised as the "father of the country." Paul redirects ultimate allegiance and honor to God the Father.

  • The selfless humility of Jesus in obeying the Father (Philippians 2:8) serves as the primary example for how Christians ought to treat one another in humility and unity (Philippians 2:3-5).
