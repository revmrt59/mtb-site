LORD - (kýrios, Strong's G2962)

Summary:

The word kyrios refers to an owner, master, ruler, or person possessing legal authority and supreme position. In the ancient Greco-Roman world and the Greek translation of the Old Testament, it was used both as a polite title for human superiors and as the divine title for God Himself. In Philippians 2:11, the title declares that Jesus Christ has been exalted by God the Father to absolute cosmic sovereignty, sharing the supreme divine title and authority over all creation.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Greek word kyrios carries the core meaning of legal ownership, authority, and rightful control. In ancient Greek society, a kyrios was the head of a household, a master over servants, or an owner of property. Anyone who held lawful authority or supervisory power over others could be called by this title.

When Jewish scholars translated the Old Testament into Greek, they frequently used kyrios to translate the distinct Hebrew name of God (YHWH) as well as the title Adonai (Master or Lord). Because of this usage, the word came to carry deep theological weight. When early Christians applied kyrios to Jesus Christ, they were not merely calling Him an honored human teacher; they were attributing to Him the title that identified the sovereign Ruler of the universe in the Scriptures.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to a range of figures depending on the context:

  • An owner or master of property and servants.

  • A human superior, magistrate, or official.

  • A respectful form of address similar to "sir."

  • God the Father, as the sovereign Lord of creation.

  • Jesus Christ, as the exalted Lord over all things.

In Philippians 2:11, the noun appears in the nominative case, singular number, and masculine gender. It functions as the ultimate title assigned to Jesus Christ in His exaltation. Here, the word signifies absolute divine sovereignty, presenting Jesus as the exalted ruler over heaven and earth to whom every created entity must bow.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, kyrios is used in several distinct ways:

  • Civil Respect: People sometimes used kyrios simply to address someone with respect, translated as "sir" or "lord" in everyday social contexts.

  • Household Masters: Parables and practical instruction use kyrios for human landowners or household masters who held authority over servants.

  • Divine Title for God: The New Testament frequently uses kyrios when quoting Old Testament Scriptures that refer to the God of Israel.

  • Exalted Title for Jesus: Following His resurrection and ascension, Jesus is confessed as kyrios. This usage encompasses His deity, His authority over the church, His cosmic reign over creation, and His future role as judge.

4. Theological Implications

Confessing Jesus as Lord lies at the core of early Christian belief. In the Roman context, subjects were expected to honor earthly rulers with supreme devotion. To declare that Jesus Christ is Lord was to assert that supreme, ultimate authority belongs to Jesus above every earthly power.

Applying kyrios to Jesus connects Him directly to the divine identity revealed in the Old Testament. Philippians 2:11 echoes Old Testament language where God declares that every knee will bow to Him alone. By applying this standard to Jesus, the passage demonstrates that Jesus shares in the divine authority of God. Far from competing with God the Father, confessing Jesus as Lord brings ultimate honor and glory to the Father.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:5-11, Paul outlines the self-emptying and subsequent exaltation of Jesus Christ. Jesus, though existing in the form of God, took the form of a servant and humbled Himself to the point of death on a cross. In response to His perfect obedience, God highly exalted Him and bestowed on Him the name that is above every name.

The climax of this exaltation appears in verse 11: "and that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father." The title "Lord" is the supreme name granted to Jesus in His exalted state. In this verse, the word shows that universal submission to Jesus is the ultimate goal of God's plan. Every created being—in heaven, on earth, and under the earth—will eventually acknowledge His supreme authority.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 20:28 — "And Thomas answered and said to Him, 'My Lord and my God!'" Here the word is joined with "God" in Thomas's climax of faith upon seeing the resurrected Christ.

  • Ephesians 6:9 — "And you, masters, do the same things to them, giving up threatening, knowing that your own Master also is in heaven, and there is no partiality with Him." Here the same word is rendered "Master" to describe both human household authorities and Christ in heaven.

  • Romans 14:6 — "He who observes the day, observes it to the Lord; and he who does not observe the day, to the Lord he does not observe it." Here the word describes Jesus as the supreme master over every aspect of a believer's daily living and conscience.

  • 1 Peter 2:3 — "...if indeed you have tasted that the Lord is gracious." Here the apostle applies Old Testament language regarding God directly to Jesus as the gracious Lord.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The statement in Philippians 2:11 explicitly links the confession of Jesus as Lord to "the glory of God the Father." This shows that acknowledging the Lordship of Christ does not diminish the Father's glory. Instead, because the Father has appointed the Son to rule over all creation, confessing Jesus as Lord fulfills the Father's supreme purpose and brings Him delight.
