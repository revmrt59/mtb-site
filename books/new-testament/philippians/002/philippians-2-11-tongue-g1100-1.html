TONGUE - (glōssa, Strong's G1100)

Summary:

The word glossa refers to the physical organ of speech, spoken language, or human speech itself. In Philippians 2:11, the word represents every voice and language across humanity making an open spoken declaration that Jesus Christ is Lord to the glory of God the Father.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word glossa was widely used in everyday Greek to describe the physical tongue in the mouth, human speech, and distinct spoken languages or dialects. In biblical literature, the term carries these same primary senses. Speech was understood not merely as noise, but as the vocal overflow of human thought, character, and allegiance.

In Philippians 2:5–11, Paul writes what is widely understood as a early hymn celebrating Jesus Christ. The passage moves from Christ's voluntary self-humiliation in the incarnation and death on the cross to His supreme exaltation by God the Father. In this glorious culmination, Paul quotes and adapts the language of Isaiah 45:23, where God declares that every knee will bow and every tongue will confess to Him. Paul applies this divine prerogative directly to Jesus Christ.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word glossa can refer to:

  • The physical muscle and organ located in the mouth, used for tasting and vocalizing sound.

  • Spoken utterance, speech, or the faculty of speech.

  • A specific foreign language, dialect, or tongue spoken by a particular nation or ethnic group.

  • By figure of speech, the individual speaker or entire language group giving vocal expression to something.

Here in Philippians 2:11, the word refers to vocal speech and expression, representing every person and language group throughout creation issuing a direct verbal declaration.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Scripture, glossa carries several distinct nuances depending on the context:

  • Physical organ: The Gospels describe physical healing when Jesus touched a man's tongue to restore clear speech, or the rich man begging for a drop of water to cool his burning tongue in the afterlife.

  • Moral character and speech control: The letter of James focuses heavily on the tongue as a small organ capable of immense destruction, serving as a key indicator of genuine spiritual maturity. Peter similarly instructs believers to keep their tongue from speaking deceit.

  • Languages and spiritual manifestations: The Book of Acts and First Corinthians use the word for known earthly languages or spiritual gifts of speaking in tongues.

  • Representative of human groups: The Book of Revelation uses the term alongside tribes, peoples, and nations to describe the vast global scope of humanity redeemed by Christ.

  • Direct public confession: In Romans and Philippians, the word joins physical posture ("every knee") with public speech ("every tongue") to describe universal submission to God's ultimate authority.

4. Theological Implications

The focus of Philippians 2:11 is the absolute supremacy and cosmic lordship of Jesus Christ. By taking the Old Testament declaration from Isaiah 45:23—where divine honor belongs exclusively to the Creator—and attributing it to Jesus, Paul clearly highlights Christ's divinity.

Human speech, which is so often misused for rebellion, pride, and false worship, will ultimately be brought into line with divine truth. Whether willingly in faith or unavoidably under judgment, every created voice will eventually utter the foundational truth of reality: "Jesus Christ is Lord." This universal acknowledgement does not compete with God the Father; rather, it achieves the Father's ultimate purpose and brings Him supreme glory.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:11, glossa functions in a nominative, singular, feminine form, paired directly with the totalizing word "every." This combination ensures that the scope is universal. No language, nation, or individual speaker falls outside this mandate.

The movement of Philippians 2 sets up a powerful contrast. In verses 6–8, Jesus refused to cling to His rights, emptied Himself, took the form of a servant, and suffered a humiliating death on a cross. Because of this radical obedience, God highly exalted Him and gave Him the name above every name (verses 9–10). The physical response of bowing knees combined with the vocal response of every "tongue" demonstrates that Christ's exaltation demands both physical submission and explicit verbal acknowledgment.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Mark 7:33 — "And He took him aside from the multitude, and put His fingers into his ears, and He spat and touched his tongue."

  • Luke 16:24 — "Then he cried and said, 'Father Abraham, have mercy on me, and send Lazarus that he may dip the tip of his finger in water and cool my tongue; for I am tormented in this flame.'"

  • Romans 14:11 — "For it is written: 'As I live, says the LORD, Every knee shall bow to Me, And every tongue shall confess to God.'"

  • James 3:6 — "And the tongue is a fire, a world of iniquity. The tongue is so set among our members that it defiles the whole body, and sets on fire the course of nature; it is set on fire by hell."

  • Revelation 5:9 — "And they sang a new song, saying: 'You are worthy to take the scroll, and to open its seals; for You were slain, and have redeemed us to God by Your blood out of every tribe and tongue and people and nation.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The pairing of the bowing knee and the confessing tongue captures the complete harmony of outward posture and vocal expression. Physical submission without spoken honor is incomplete, just as spoken words without physical reverence lack integrity.

Furthermore, Paul's usage in Romans 14:11 and Philippians 2:11 shows how firmly grounded early Christian teaching was in Old Testament theology. By transferring the prophetic expectation of Isaiah 45:23 to Jesus of Nazareth, the passage presents the ultimate destiny of all human speech: to praise and proclaim the exalted Lord.
