ONLY - (mónon, Strong's G3440)

Summary:

The word monon functions as an adverb meaning "only," "merely," "simply," or "alone," serving to restrict or set boundaries around a statement. In Philippians 2:12, Paul uses this word to urge the believers not to limit their obedience merely to times when he is physically present with them. By insisting that their faithfulness must extend beyond his presence, the word underscores that true Christian obedience does not depend on human supervision, but on a lasting commitment before God.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word monon is an adverb derived from the fundamental concept of being single, solitary, or alone. In ancient Greek, it was widely used in daily conversation, letters, and public discourse to narrow the scope of a claim or to draw clear boundaries around an action.

In biblical literature, monon frequently appears in expressions that set limitations or contrast partial actions with complete ones. A common construction pairs a negative with monon ("not only") followed by an addition ("but also"). This pattern allows a writer to acknowledge a initial reality while immediately expanding the reader's view to a larger responsibility or truth.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can mean "only," "merely," "alone," or "simply." It specifies that an action or condition is restricted to a particular boundary.

When used by itself, monon isolates an item or condition, indicating that nothing further is included. When combined with a negative, as in "not only," it functions to break through a potential limitation. Rather than allowing an action to stay confined to one circumstance, it demands that the action apply more broadly.

In Philippians 2:12, the word modifies the phrase describing Paul's physical presence. Paul uses it with a negative ("not as in my presence only") to clarify that the Philippians' history of obedience must not be restricted to the times when he was directly with them to guide and oversee them.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, monon serves to distinguish between limited, external practices and wholehearted devotion. The biblical writers frequently use the word to challenge superficial or conditional behavior.

For instance, the word can highlight a physical boundary that someone seeks to touch in faith, or it can mark the distinction between hearing a command and actually putting it into practice. In apostolic letters, the word often guards against half-measures in the Christian life. By telling believers that an obligation applies "not only" in favorable or monitored conditions, the authors emphasize that divine standards apply universally across all situations.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of monon in Paul's instruction regarding obedience carries important theological weight. It addresses the difference between eyewink service and genuine integrity before God.

When obedience occurs "only" under the watchful eye of an apostle or church leader, it risks becoming dependent on human presence rather than divine authority. True spiritual growth requires believers to recognize that their primary accountability is to God, who sees every action whether human leaders are present or absent.

Furthermore, Paul links this ongoing obedience to the work of salvation being lived out "with fear and trembling." Because God Himself is actively working within believers, their duty to obey is constant. The absence of a human teacher does not lessen God's presence or authority; if anything, it calls for a greater depth of personal responsibility.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:12, Paul writes from confinement, physically separated from the church at Philippi. Prior to this verse, Paul set forth the supreme model of Christ, who humbled Himself and was obedient even to the point of death on a cross.

Building on Christ's example ("Therefore"), Paul commends the Philippians for their past record of obedience. However, his physical separation creates a crucial test for their spiritual maturity. By adding monon—demanding that their obedience continue "not as in my presence only, but now much more in my absence"—Paul cuts off any inclination for them to slacken their spiritual efforts while he is away.

The word monon highlights that his presence was merely the initial context of their obedience, not its permanent requirement. His absence should not lead to spiritual passivity; rather, it should move them to express their faith "much more" diligently, relying directly on the divine power working within them.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 14:36 — "and begged Him that they might only touch the hem of His garment."

  • Romans 3:29 — "Or is He the God of the Jews only? Is He not also the God of the Gentiles?"

  • Galatians 5:13 — "For you, brethren, have been called to liberty; only do not use liberty as an opportunity for the flesh, but through love serve one another."

  • James 1:22 — "But be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The combination of "not only" with "much more" in Philippians 2:12 forms a strong pastoral encouraging contrast. Paul does not dismiss the value of their obedience while he was present; he commends it. Yet he insists that spiritual distance ought to produce greater maturity, not decay.

This reveals an essential principle of spiritual growth: initial faith often benefits from strong spiritual leadership, but mature faith must learn to walk faithfully when human supports are removed. The word monon marks the boundary line where external supervision ends and personal spiritual integrity must stand on its own.
