TREMBLING - (trómos, Strong's G5156)

tromos ( tromos , Strong’s G5156 )

Summary:

energy and physical expression, tromos refers to a trembling, quaking, or shiver caused by awe, deep respect, or fear. In Philippians 2:12, Paul uses this word alongside "fear" to describe the attitude of humble reverence and holy seriousness with which believers should live out their salvation, recognizing that God Himself is actively working within them.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word tromos describes the physical bodily reaction of quaking or trembling, usually brought on by intense emotion, awe, or deep respect. In biblical writing, it often appears paired with the word for fear (phobos), forming the common expression "fear and trembling."

Rather than describing terror that drives a person away from God, this expression conveys deep reverence, severe self-distrust, and a heightened sense of responsibility in the presence of God or when handling sacred duties. It represents the natural reaction of human weakness when brought face-to-face with divine power and holiness.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to a physical shaking or trembling brought on by awe, reverence, or deep emotional weight. In Philippians 2:12, the word is a genitive singular masculine noun.

Here it means a holy awe and sober reverence that leads to careful obedience. When believers consider the majestic holiness of God and the depth of what He has done, this trembling reflects an attitude that turns away from self-confidence and honors God's presence.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In Scripture, tromos describes physical and emotional responses to extraordinary or solemn events. When individuals encounter divine power or carry out serious spiritual duties, trembling highlights their acute awareness of human weakness in light of God's majesty.

In pastoral letters, the pairing of fear and trembling emphasizes humility, earnest effort, and freedom from spiritual pride. It marks a mindset that refuses self-sufficiency, knowing that human wisdom and strength are never enough to accomplish God's work.

4. Theological Implications

This word provides a healthy balance to a believer's understanding of the Christian life. While salvation is entirely a gift of God's grace received through faith, living out that salvation requires utmost seriousness.

Tromos guards believers against spiritual laziness and self-righteous presumption. It does not mean believers should live in anxiety about losing their eternal life, nor does it imply that God is a harsh master seeking to condemn. Instead, it underscores that the God who saves is sovereign and holy. Living before Him demands earnest devotion, deep respect, and constant reliance on His grace.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:12, Paul commands the believers in Philippi to work out their own salvation "with fear and trembling." This directive immediately follows the famous description of Jesus Christ's humble obedience and high exaltation (Philippians 2:5–11). Having set forth Christ as the supreme example of self-emptying humbleness, Paul calls the church to demonstrate practical, unified obedience in their daily lives.

The following verse provides the foundation for this command: "for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure" (Philippians 2:13). The trembling Paul describes is not paralyzing dread. It is the awe-struck humility of realizing that the Almighty God is personally present and active inside the believer. Because God Himself generates both the desire and the strength to obey Him, believers must handle their ongoing spiritual growth with profound care. Tromos reflects the sober awareness that holy ground is beneath their feet as God accomplishes His will in their lives.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Mark 16:8 — "And they went out quickly and fled from the tomb, for they trembled and were amazed." Here the word describes the physical and emotional reaction of the women upon finding the empty tomb.

  • 1 Corinthians 2:3 — "I was with you in weakness, in fear, and in much trembling." Paul uses the word to express his deep humility and reliance on God's Spirit rather than human eloquence when preaching.

  • 2 Corinthians 7:15 — "And his affection for you is all the more abundant while he remembers the obedience of you all, how with fear and trembling you received him." The word highlights the respectful, eager, and earnest attitude of the Corinthian believers when receiving Titus.

  • Ephesians 6:5 — "Bondservants, be obedient to those who are your earthly masters, with fear and trembling, in sincerity of heart, as to Christ." The word describes the respectful diligence and sincere effort believers should display in their daily work, serving as unto the Lord.

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The phrase "fear and trembling" is a characteristic way Paul describes a humble, God-fearing attitude across different areas of life: pastoral ministry, congregational relationships, workplace duties, and personal spiritual growth.

  • Working out salvation with tromos is not working for salvation. The Philippians already possess salvation ("your own salvation"), and the trembling is their reverent response to the God who saved them and continues to work within them.

  • This reverent attitude directly counters the selfish ambition and conceit that Paul warned against earlier in the letter. A heart filled with holy awe toward God naturally becomes humble toward fellow believers.
