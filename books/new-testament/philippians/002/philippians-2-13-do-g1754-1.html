DO - (energéō, Strong's G1754)

Summary:

The word energēō means to operate, produce an effect, or put forth power to accomplish a result. In Philippians 2:13, the word describes God active within believers, giving them the spiritual power to carry out and fulfill the practical actions that bring Him pleasure.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Philippians 2:12–13 forms one of the most significant instructions in Paul's letter to the church at Philippi. In verse 12, Paul commands believers to work out their own salvation with fear and trembling. Immediately in verse 13, he provides the ground and encouragement for that command: "for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure."

The word translated "do" comes from energēō, a verb denoting active power operating to produce results. Writing to a congregation seeking unity and perseverance amid external opposition and internal friction, Paul reminds them that outward Christian living is sustained by God's inward activity.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can mean to operate, to produce an outcome, or to carry out an activity. It refers not to dormant ability or passive intention, but to power actively producing real effects.

When applied to God, the word describes divine power at work within creation or human hearts. When applied to people, it refers to carrying out a task or putting forth active energy.

In Philippians 2:13, the present active infinitive form presents this "doing" as the practical performance and ongoing execution of God's purpose in the daily life of the believer.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses this word to describe energy in motion that achieves a distinct result. It is frequently associated with divine power operating in spiritual service, the ministry of the gospel, and spiritual transformation.

The word contrasts with inactivity or fruitless effort. Rather than describing static power held in reserve, it emphasizes active power bringing about visible, practical outcomes.

4. Theological Implications

The connection between human effort and divine initiative in Philippians 2:12–13 highlights the relationship between God's grace and human obedience. Believers are commanded to work out their salvation, yet their capacity to do so depends entirely on God's active work within them.

God does not merely demand righteous living from a distance; He works directly within His people to produce both the initial desire ("to will") and the practical execution ("to do").

Sanctification is neither pure self-effort nor passive waiting. It is the active obedience of the believer, enabled and sustained by God's continuous internal power.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:13, Paul pairs "to will" with "to do." The first describes the inner desire or inclination of the heart, while the second—our target word—describes the outward performance and practical execution. God transforms both the believer's motives and actions.

The active infinitive structure presents "doing" as the intended goal and result of God's internal working. God provides the internal enablement so that believers can translate holy desires into concrete acts of obedience.

This truth reinforces the larger theme of Philippians 2: humble service patterned after Jesus Christ. Believers do not achieve Christlike humility through unassisted human willpower. God actively works in them to produce the choices and conduct that fulfill His good pleasure.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Galatians 5:6 — Paul writes that in Christ Jesus, true faith expresses itself as "working" through love.

  • Ephesians 3:20 — Paul praises God who is able to do exceedingly abundantly above all that we ask or think, according to the power that "works" in believers.

  • Colossians 1:29 — Paul describes his gospel ministry, laboring according to God's energy which "works" in him mightily.

  • 1 Thessalonians 2:13 — The apostle thanks God that the believers received the gospel as God's word, which effectively "works" in those who believe.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The English word "energy" derives from this Greek word family, capturing the sense of active force accomplishing work.

In Philippians 2:13, this word offers immense comfort to believers struggling with spiritual weakness. It assures Christians that God does not leave them to fulfill His commands in their own strength, but actively supplies the spiritual energy needed to carry out His will.
