FOR - (hypér, Strong's G5228)

Summary:

Hyper is a Greek preposition that commonly means "over," "above," "on behalf of," "for the sake of," or "for." In Philippians 2:13, hyper connects God's active work within believers to its goal and motivation: "for His good pleasure." It identifies that God works in the believer's heart and life for the sake of fulfilling His own good desire and purpose.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Greek word hyper is a common preposition used throughout the New Testament. In its standard spatial sense, it indicates position, meaning "above" or "over." In a relational or figurative sense, it frequently expresses interest, benefit, or representation, translated as "on behalf of," "for the sake of," or "in favor of."

In the context of Paul's letter to the Philippians, hyper serves as a key connective word. It clarifies why God initiates and sustains spiritual transformation in the lives of His people. Rather than leaving believers to rely on their own strength, God works in them to fulfill His gracious intention.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Hyper functions as a preposition indicating direction, benefit, purpose, or position. Depending on the construction and context, its primary senses include:

  • Purpose or motive: "for the sake of," "for," or "on account of."

  • Representation or benefit: "on behalf of" or "for the benefit of."

  • Substitution: "in place of."

  • Comparison or degree: "above," "beyond," or "more than."

In Philippians 2:13, hyper expresses purpose and motivation. Paired with "His good pleasure," it translates as "for," showing that God's internal work in the believer is directed toward accomplishing what pleases Him.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, hyper marks several distinct relationships:

  • Representation and Benefit: The word frequently describes actions taken on behalf of others. For example, Christ's death is repeatedly described as occurring "for us" or "for sinners."

  • Intercession and Concern: It introduces the object of prayer or care, such as praying "for" those who persecute you or making requests "for" all people.

  • Superiority or Elevation: It marks high position or excellence, as when Jesus is given a name "above" every name, or when one thing is greater "than" another.

  • Purpose and Motive: As in Philippians 2:13, it introduces the intention or goal behind an action, indicating that an activity occurs "for the sake of" a specific outcome.

4. Theological Implications

The preposition hyper in Philippians 2:13 highlights that God's work in Christian growth originates in His own desire and serves His own good purpose. God is not an indifferent observer forcing human compliance, nor is He responding to human initiative. He actively works within believers because it brings Him joy to fulfill His good purposes through them.

This perspective centers Christian living on God's initiative and delight. The believer's obedience is both enabled by God's internal grace and directed toward God's sovereign pleasure.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:12, Paul commands believers to "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling." In verse 13, he grounds this command in a comforting reality: "for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure."

The phrase "it is God who works in you both to will and to do" explains where spiritual power and desire come from. God Himself produces both the inward willingness and the outward action. The preposition hyper ("for") then links this divine activity directly to its target: "His good pleasure."

Hyper does not itself carry the complex doctrine of divine power or human responsibility; those truths come from the surrounding verbs describing God working within human willing and acting. What hyper contributes is the goal. It shows that God does not work in believers aimlessly or reluctantly. He acts "for the sake of" His own good delight, ensuring that every step of spiritual growth ultimately serves His good purpose.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 5:44 — "pray for those who spitefully use you"

  • Romans 5:8 — "while we were still sinners, Christ died for us"

  • 2 Corinthians 5:21 — "He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us"

  • Philippians 2:9 — "given Him the name which is above every name"

7. Other Relevant Insights

Understanding hyper helps maintain the proper focus in Christian growth. Believers are called to active effort ("work out your own salvation"), yet this effort rests entirely upon God's prior and ongoing grace ("it is God who works in you"). By ending the sentence with "for His good pleasure," Paul reminds the church that the ultimate aim of all spiritual growth is to bring joy and glory to God.
