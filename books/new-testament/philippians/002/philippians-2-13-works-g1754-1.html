WORKS - (energéō, Strong's G1754)

Summary:

The Greek verb energeō means to be active, to operate, to bring about an effect, or to work effectively. In Philippians 2:13, the word identifies God as the active inner source of spiritual transformation, constantly operating within believers to give them both the desire and the strength to live according to His good pleasure.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In everyday Greek, energeō described power in action. It was used for forces that operate effectively to accomplish a result, such as a medicine taking effect in the body or a physical force producing a clear outcome. It is the root of the English word "energy."

In the New Testament, energeō carries this sense of effective, active power, especially when referring to the activity of God. In Philippians 2:12–13, the Apostle Paul writes to a church experiencing internal friction and external opposition. After commanding the Philippians to "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling," Paul presents the grounding truth in verse 13: the ultimate driver behind all spiritual progress is God Himself, who actively operates within His people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb energeō means to produce an effect, to operate, or to be at work. It does not refer to potential or dormant power, but to power actively being exerted.

When applied to God, energeō describes His living, divine agency accomplishing His purposes within creation and believers. In Philippians 2:13, the word functions as a participle identifying God as "the One working" or "the One who works." Here, it refers to God's continuous, dynamic operation in the human heart, generating both the internal desire ("to will") and the actual practice ("to do") of His holy will.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In New Testament usage, energeō consistently emphasizes active power that yields tangible results. Scripture applies this word across several key settings:

  • Divine power in salvation and spiritual growth: God acts within believers to bestow spiritual gifts, energize faithful service, and transform human desires.

  • Divine power in Christ: God exerted this same active power when raising Jesus Christ from the dead and placing Him above all earthly authority.

  • Spiritual activity in believers: The word describes faith actively expressing itself through love, as well as God's Word actively shaping those who receive it.

  • Unseen or deceptive influences: The word can also describe spiritual hostility or sinful passions working within human flesh, showing that energeō always marks a real, operating force at work behind visible behaviors.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of energeō in Philippians 2:13 highlights the foundational doctrine of divine grace in Christian sanctification. Human effort alone cannot generate spiritual life or sustain holy living.

First, God works at the deepest level of human motivation ("to will"). Grace does not merely compel outward compliance; it transforms inclinations, giving believers new desires that align with God's character.

Second, God works at the level of capability ("to do"). God provides the active strength needed to carry out those transformed desires.

This truth guards against two common errors: passive legalism, which tries to obey God in human strength, and spiritual resignation, which waits for God to act without taking active responsibility. Because God is actively at work within the believer, active obedience becomes both possible and fruitful.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Philippians 2:12–13 forms a essential partnership between divine sovereign agency and human moral responsibility. Verse 12 issues a direct command: believers are to "work out" (katergazomai) their salvation with fear and trembling. Verse 13 then provides the foundation and encouragement: "for it is God who works (energeō) in you."

The relationship between these two verses is cause and effect:

  • Believers are to work out what God has actively worked in.

  • God's inner working does not render human responsibility passive; rather, God's inner working provides the exact energy and foundation that make human obedience possible.

  • The atmosphere of "fear and trembling" is not a terror of abandonment, but a deep, reverent awe before the reality that Almighty God is actively operating inside the believer's life.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Corinthians 12:6 – "And there are diversities of activities, but it is the same God who works all in all."

  • Galatians 5:6 – "For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision nor uncircumcision avails anything, but faith working through love."

  • Ephesians 3:20 – "Now to Him who is able to do exceedingly abundantly above all that we ask or think, according to the power that works in us..."

  • Colossians 1:29 – "To this end I also labor, striving according to His working which works in me mightily."

  • 1 Thessalonians 2:13 – "...the word of God, which also effectively works in you who believe."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Understanding energeō offers lasting pastoral comfort. Believers often experience periods of spiritual weakness, exhaustion, or struggle with persistent sin. In those moments, Philippians 2:13 reassures Christians that spiritual growth is not fueled by human willpower alone.

Because God Himself is the active worker within the heart, believers can approach obedience with confident hope. The desire to please God and the actual power to obey Him are gifts created and sustained by God's effective power inside every child of God.
