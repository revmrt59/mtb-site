DO - (poiéō, Strong's G4160)

Summary:

The Greek verb poieō basic meaning is to make, produce, perform, carry out, or bring about an action or outcome. In Philippians 2:14, the word functions as a direct command to the Christian community, instructing believers to carry out all their daily duties, mutual service, and corporate life without complaining or arguing.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The verb poieō is one of the standard action verbs in the Greek New Testament. It covers a broad semantic range, from creating or crafting an object to performing an assignment or practicing a way of life. While some verbs emphasize mental assent, intent, or speech, poieō centers on concrete execution—putting thought, duty, or instruction into visible action.

In Philippians 2, Paul addresses a church family that enjoyed strong partnership in the gospel yet faced real pressures from outside opposition and subtle internal friction. Prior to verse 14, Paul points the believers to the supreme example of Jesus Christ, who humbled Himself in joyful obedience to the Father. Paul then reminds the church that God is actively working within them to energize both their desires and their actions. Building directly on that foundational reality, Paul uses poieō to direct their focus to practical, day-to-day behavior within the body of Christ.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word poieō can refer to making something new or bringing an action into actual performance. When used of physical production, it means to construct, form, or produce. When used of conduct, it means to accomplish, execute, fulfill, or practice a task or command.

Here in Philippians 2:14, the verb means to carry out or perform ongoing activities. The focus is not on manufacturing a physical object, but on executing the daily work, service, and fellowship of the church. The command specifies not only that the work must be done, but also the spirit in which believers are to carry it out.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the New Testament, poieō serves to separate abstract knowledge from authentic practice. Scriptural faith is never presented as merely internal sentiment; it requires active expression. The verb frequently appears in contexts contrasting those who merely hear or speak God's word with those who actually perform it.

When poieō expresses direct commands, it calls for visible compliance. In moral and ethical contexts, it highlights the transition from knowing what is right to performing what is right. It underscores that spiritual health is demonstrated in tangible conduct, daily choices, and corporate relationships.

4. Theological Implications

In the architecture of Paul's letter, human action is grounded in divine grace. In Philippians 2:13, Paul affirms that God works in believers both to will and to do according to His good pleasure. Immediately following, verse 14 commands believers to "do all things without complaining and disputing."

This sequence carries significant theological weight. Christian obedience is not an attempt to earn God's favor by self-generated effort. Rather, because God is actively at work within His people, believers are empowered and obliged to carry out their calling in daily life. Human action (poieō) acts as the natural, visible fruit of God's prior inner work. Furthermore, the passage shows that God cares deeply about both the work being accomplished and the heart attitude accompanying the work.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:14, poieō appears as a command addressed to the entire assembly. Paul applies the command comprehensively to "all things," leaving no room for selective obedience. Every act of service, every internal church interaction, and every routine duty falls under this instruction.

The immediate context clarifies the character of this action. Paul does not merely say "do all things," but "do all things without complaining and disputing." Grumbling and argumentative spirits destroy church unity and undermine the gospel witness. Therefore, the command to perform all duties is inextricably bound to a spirit of peaceful humility. By carrying out their daily responsibilities without internal friction, believers shine as blameless lights in a dark world.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 1:24 – Joseph obeyed the message of the Lord’s angel and "did" as he was commanded, taking Mary as his wife.

  • Luke 3:11 – John the Baptist instructed the crowds on how to "do" righteousness in practical life by sharing clothing and food with those in need.

  • John 3:21 – Jesus contrasted those who love darkness with the person who "does" the truth and steps into the light.

  • James 4:17 – James highlights personal responsibility, stating that knowing what is good and failing to "do" it is sin.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The grammatical form of poieō in Philippians 2:14 is a present active imperative in the plural. As an imperative, it establishes an explicit command rather than an optional suggestion. The plural form ensures that Paul is addressing the entire corporate community, calling all members to shared accountability. The active voice presents the believers as the ones who must actively carry out this conduct, relying on the internal work of God described in the preceding verse.
