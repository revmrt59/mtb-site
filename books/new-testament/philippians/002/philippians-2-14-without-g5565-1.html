WITHOUT - (chōrís, Strong's G5565)

Summary:

The Greek word choris is a preposition that means "apart from," "separate from," or "without." It indicates complete exclusion or separation between two things. In Philippians 2:14, the word specifies that every action and assignment in the Christian life must be carried out entirely free from grumbling and arguing.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek, choris functioned as an adverb and a preposition that expressed separation or distance. It comes from a root idea of space or distance between items, describing something existing by itself or apart from something else.

In the New Testament, choris serves as a preposition meaning "without," "apart from," or "independent of." It establishes a clear boundary between two entities, conditions, or actions, showing that one exists or occurs completely isolated from the other.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word choris has a straightforward set of meanings:

  • "Apart from" or "separated from" someone or something.

  • "Without," indicating the complete absence of a quality, condition, or accompaniment.

  • "Besides" or "in addition to," when setting a particular group off from a main count.

  • "Independent of," showing that two things do not rely on or belong together.

Here in Philippians 2:14, the word means "without" or "entirely separate from." It marks an absolute exclusion, showing that the actions commanded should contain no trace of the unwanted behavior.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses choris to establish essential distinctions across a variety of spiritual and practical settings.

First, choris describes absolute relational separation. For example, to be choris Christ is to be completely detached from Him and His blessings.

Second, choris marks theological distinction. In discussions of salvation, it separates the righteousness given by God from human efforts to keep the law.

Third, choris sets behavioral boundaries. When used alongside practical commands, it excludes specific sinful attitudes or actions from Christian behavior. In these settings, choris functions like a strict barrier, signaling that certain habits must be completely left outside the door of Christian conduct.

4. Theological Implications

While choris is a simple preposition of separation, its placement in Scripture carries significant theological weight. The word highlights that certain spiritual realities are mutually exclusive.

For instance, true Christian service and persistent grumbling cannot walk hand in hand. Salvation by divine grace stands apart from legalistic human effort. Spiritual fruitfulness cannot occur apart from vital union with Jesus Christ. By using choris, biblical writers show that mixing incompatible spiritual realities ruins the integrity of Christian worship and daily life.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:12–13, Paul instructs the believers in Philippi to work out their own salvation with fear and trembling, reminding them that God is actively working in them both to will and to do for His good pleasure. Immediately following this grand statement of divine enabling, Paul gives a practical command in Philippians 2:14: "Do all things without complaining and disputing."

The word choris ("without") plays a critical role in connecting the power of God with the daily conduct of the church. Paul does not merely say, "Do all things." He adds choris to define the required spirit and manner of their service.

The believers were experiencing internal friction and external pressure. Grumbling (gongysmos) and arguing (dialogismos) threaten unity and dull a church's witness. By placing choris before these two negative behaviors, Paul makes it clear that complaining and disputing are not small flaws to be tolerated. They must be completely removed from the believers' tasks. God's indwelling power provides the precise grace needed to serve completely apart from a bitter or contentious spirit.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 15:5 — "I am the vine, you are the branches. He who abides in Me, and I in him, bears much fruit; for without Me you can do nothing."

  • Romans 3:28 — "Therefore we conclude that a man is justified by faith apart from the deeds of the law."

  • Ephesians 2:12 — "that at that time you were without Christ, being aliens from the commonwealth of Israel..."

  • 1 Timothy 2:8 — "I desire therefore that the men pray everywhere, lifting up holy hands, without wrath and doubting."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Notice the structural similarity between 1 Timothy 2:8 and Philippians 2:14. In both passages, choris introduces a pair of destructive heart attitudes that ruin Christian activity. In 1 Timothy 2:8, prayer must be offered "without wrath and doubting." In Philippians 2:14, service must be done "without complaining and disputing."

This pattern shows that biblical obedience involves both positive execution and negative exclusion. It is not enough simply to perform a duty; the attitude accompanying the performance matters to God. The word choris guards the believer's worship and work from being contaminated by sinful dispositions.
