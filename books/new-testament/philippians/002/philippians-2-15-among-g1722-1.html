AMONG - (en, Strong's G1722)

Summary:

The Greek word en is a basic preposition used to show location, presence, context, or association. Depending on the surrounding sentence, it is translated into English as "in," "among," "at," "by," or "with."

In Philippians 2:15, the word is translated "among." Paul uses it to locate Christians directly within a morally broken society ("a crooked and perverse generation"). The word shows that believers are not called to isolate themselves from the world, but to live faithfully in the very midst of it, shining as lights where God has placed them.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The preposition en is one of the most basic and frequent location words in the Greek New Testament. It is used to mark where someone or something is located, the environment in which an action happens, or the spiritual state or fellowship in which a person lives.

When Paul wrote his letter to the Philippians, he was writing from prison to a church located in a prominent Roman colony. The believers in Philippi faced social pressure, pagan culture, and potential opposition. Paul wrote to encourage them to live worthy of the gospel of Christ, warning them against internal friction and urging them to maintain a pure testimony surrounded by a unbelieving Roman society.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word en indicates spatial location, a sphere of presence, or close connection. Its primary meanings include:

  • "in" — inside a space, city, body, or spiritual state.

  • "among" — surrounded by a group or living in the midst of people.

  • "at" or "on" — present at a specific location or occasion.

  • "by" or "with" — by means of something or accompanied by someone.

The word itself does not carry a complex theological doctrine. Instead, it functions as a functional grammatical pointer, establishing where an event takes place or where a person resides.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the New Testament, en serves a wide variety of grammatical functions:

  • Spatial Location: It identifies physical places, such as being in a city or sitting at a table.

  • Social Presence: When paired with a plural group of people, it means "among" or "in the presence of" that group.

  • Spiritual Union: It frequently describes a Christian's vital connection to Christ, as in the familiar phrase "in Christ."

  • Instrumental Means: It can express the means by which something is done, such as being saved by grace or speaking by the Spirit.

When describing social settings, en places the subject directly inside a specific group or culture, emphasizing real physical presence rather than distance or separation.

4. Theological Implications

While en is simply a preposition, its usage in Philippians 2:15 helps express an important biblical truth about Christian mission. God does not immediately extract believers from the world upon saving them. Instead, He leaves them placed directly inside human society.

Light has no visible purpose unless darkness surrounds it. By placing His children "among" a broken generation, God positions them to reflect His holiness, love, and truth. Faithfulness does not require fleeing the world, but living blameless lives right where dark cultural pressures exist.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:14–16, Paul instructs the church to "do all things without complaining and disputing." Internal friction and murmuring damage the church's unity and weaken its public witness.

Paul explains that harmonious conduct ensures believers remain "blameless and harmless, children of God without fault in the midst of a crooked and perverse generation, among whom you shine as lights in the world."

The preposition "among" sets up a stark contrast between two realities:

  • The moral condition of the surrounding world ("crooked and perverse").

  • The conduct of God's children ("blameless," "harmless," and "shining").

Believers are not to blend into the culture, nor are they to retreat into hidden isolation. The word "among" fixes their position squarely in the mission field, highlighting that their distinct, selfless character must be visible to those around them as they hold fast to the word of life.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Colossians 1:27 — "To them God willed to make known what are the riches of the glory of this mystery among the Gentiles: which is Christ in you, the hope of glory."

  • 2 Corinthians 12:9 — "And He said to me, 'My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness.'"

  • Revelation 1:13 — "and in the midst of the seven lampstands One like the Son of Man, clothed with a garment down to the feet..."

  • Acts 17:16 — "Now while Paul waited for them at Athens, his spirit was provoked within him when he saw that the city was given over to idols."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Understanding en as "among" guards Christians against two equal errors regarding culture:

  • Conformity: Becoming like the dark generation around them, losing their distinction as lights.

  • Withdrawal: Removing themselves entirely from contact with unbelievers, hiding the light from the very place it is needed.

The placement of God's people "among" the world is by divine design. Every workplace, neighborhood, and community serves as the deliberate location where God places His children to manifest the truth of the gospel through holy conduct and gospel proclamation.
