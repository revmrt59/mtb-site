GENERATION - (geneá, Strong's G1074)

Summary:

The word genea refers to a generation, the people living during a particular time period, or a group of contemporaries characterized by shared traits or moral conditions. In Philippians 2:15, Paul uses genea to describe the unbelieving world surrounding the church in Philippi. By placing the pure, faultless character of God's children in contrast with a "crooked and perverse generation," Paul emphasizes that believers are placed directly within a morally broken culture to shine as distinct spiritual lights.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek literature, genea referred primarily to family descent, a birth group, or all the people living within a shared era. The concept routinely measured both chronological time—such as a generation of people living together—and collective cultural identity.

In the Greek translation of the Old Testament, genea frequently translated the Hebrew word dor, which described consecutive generations of humanity or a contemporary population marked by specific moral tendencies. Thus, the term naturally developed both a temporal aspect (a span of contemporary life) and a qualitative aspect (the shared character of the people living during that span).

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word genea can refer to:

  • A group of people born around the same time or living contemporaneously.

  • An age, period, or epoch of human history.

  • A group linked by shared moral or spiritual characteristics.

In Philippians 2:15, the word refers to the contemporary unbelieving society surrounding the Philippian believers, explicitly qualified as morally twisted ("crooked and perverse").

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the New Testament, genea appears frequently in the Gospels when Jesus describes His contemporaries. Jesus often used the term to address the spiritual state of those who witnessed His ministry yet rejected His message. In those settings, the word carries a distinct moral weight, describing not merely people who happened to live in the first century, but a group united in unbelief.

When Paul writes to the churches, genea maintains this qualitative dimension. Rather than viewing the surrounding world as neutral, Scripture views human society apart from God as a distinct "generation" with its own fallen habits, worldviews, and moral trajectories.

4. Theological Implications

The biblically described "generation" highlights the fallen environment in which God's people live. Human history is not composed of isolated individuals, but of fallen cultures that form distinct moral atmospheres.

Scripture does not instruct believers to isolate themselves entirely from their generation, nor does it allow them to conform to it. Instead, God places His children directly inside a fallen generation so that His holy character and truth can be demonstrated. The darkness of the generation serves as the visual backdrop against which the grace and purity of the gospel become clearly visible.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:12–16, Paul instructs believers to work out their own salvation with fear and trembling, maintaining unity and avoiding complaining or disputing. In verse 15, the genitive singular feminine noun genea identifies the environment in which this blameless living must take place: "in the midst of a crooked and perverse generation."

Paul intentionally draws on the background of Deuteronomy 32:5, where Israel was warned for acting unfaithfully like a "perverse and crooked generation." In Philippians, Paul applies those descriptive terms to the surrounding pagan world. While the surrounding culture is twisted (morally crooked) and perverted (turned away from God's truth), believers are called to be blameless, innocent, and faultless children of God. The presence of the church within this "generation" is intentional; their holy conduct causes them to shine like stars or bright lights in a dark world.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 11:16 — "But to what shall I liken this generation? It is like children sitting in the marketplaces and calling to their companions..."

  • Matthew 12:39 — "But He answered and said to them, 'An evil and adulterous generation seeks after a sign, and no sign will be given to it except the sign of the prophet Jonah.'"

  • Mark 8:38 — "For whoever is ashamed of Me and My words in this adulterous and sinful generation, of him the Son of Man also will be ashamed when He comes in the glory of His Father with the holy angels."

  • Luke 1:50 — "And His mercy is on those who fear Him from generation to generation."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The location of God's children "in the midst" of a crooked generation underscores divine purpose in human history. God does not immediately extract believers from corrupt cultural settings. Instead, He places them precisely where moral darkness is prevalent so that the contrast between worldly living and divine truth is clear. The spiritual health of a church is demonstrated not by avoiding contact with the surrounding generation, but by maintaining spotless integrity while living right in its midst.
