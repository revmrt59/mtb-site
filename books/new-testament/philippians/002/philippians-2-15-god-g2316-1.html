GOD - (theós, Strong's G2316)

Summary:

The Greek word theos refers to God, the supreme divine Being, Creator, and ruler of the universe. In Scripture, it identifies the one true God who reveals Himself through His deeds and His Word. In Philippians 2:15, the word appears in the expression "children of God," identifying believers as those who belong to God's family and are called to reflect His holy character in a dark world.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient Greco-Roman world, the word theos was widely used for many pagan deities, mythic gods, and divinized rulers. However, New Testament writers used theos primarily to name the one true God revealed to Israel in the Old Testament and fully made known through Jesus Christ.

When Paul wrote to the church in Philippi, he was speaking to believers living in a Roman colony where emperor worship and idol worship were common. Calling the Creator theos reminded these early Christians that ultimate power, glory, and authority belong to God alone, not to earthly rulers or pagan idols.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word theos means "God" or "god." In biblical usage, it has a few basic applications:

  • It refers to the true God—the sovereign, eternal Creator and Father of Jesus Christ.

  • In a small number of contexts, it refers to false gods or idols worshipped by nations.

  • On rare occasions, it describes human judges or spiritual beings functioning in a limited representative role.

When used of the Lord in the New Testament, theos describes the supreme God in His personal power, holiness, righteousness, and grace.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, theos identifies God in His relation to creation, history, and His people. Scripture uses the word when speaking of God's sovereign acts, such as raising Jesus from the dead, granting salvation, and establishing His kingdom.

In Philippians 2:15, the word is grammatically in the genitive case, singular number, and masculine gender ("of God"). This genitive form indicates ownership and family relationship. Believers are described as "children of God," emphasizing that they belong to Him as His offspring through faith.

4. Theological Implications

The truth that believers are "children of God" carries rich spiritual significance. God is not a distant power or cold ruler; He is a personal Father who claims believers as His own.

This family connection establishes both privilege and responsibility. As children of God, Christians share in divine care and promise. At the same time, children are expected to reflect their Father's nature. Because God is holy, blameless, and good, His children are called to live in a way that shows His family character to the world around them.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:14-15, Paul tells believers to "do all things without complaining and disputing." He follows this command with the goal: "that you may become blameless and harmless, children of God without fault."

Paul places these "children of God" in contrast with their environment: "in the midst of a crooked and perverse generation, among whom you shine as lights in the world." The surrounding society is marked by moral confusion and self-centered conflict. In contrast, those who belong to God are to shine like bright stars against a dark sky. Their conduct, marked by peace and integrity, demonstrates whose family they belong to. Belonging to God gives them both the identity and the obligation to live differently from the world around them.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 1:23 – "They shall call His name Immanuel, which is translated, 'God with us.'"

  • John 6:27 – "Do not labor for the food which perishes, but for the food which endures to everlasting life, which the Son of Man will give you, because God the Father has set His seal on Him."

  • Acts 3:15 – "...and killed the Prince of life, whom God raised from the dead, of which we are witnesses."

  • 1 Timothy 3:5 – "(for if a man does not know how to rule his own house, how will he take care of the church of God?)"

  • 1 John 4:7 – "Beloved, let us love one another, for love is of God; and everyone who loves is born of God and knows God."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Just before verse 15, Paul explains that "it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure" (Philippians 2:13). This connection is vital for understanding what it means to be "children of God."

Believers do not become blameless through human effort alone. God Himself active within His children provides the spiritual desire and strength to obey. The call to live as children of God without fault is supported by the ongoing work of God in their hearts.
