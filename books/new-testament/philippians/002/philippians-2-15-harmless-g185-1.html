HARMLESS - (akéraios, Strong's G185)

Summary:

The word akeraios describes something unmixed, pure, or uncorrupted by foreign elements. In ancient usage, it referred to undiluted wine, unalloyed metals, or an unadulterated substance. Metaphorically, it refers to moral innocence, integrity, and freedom from deceit, guile, or harmful intent. In Philippians 2:15, Paul uses this word alongside "blameless" to urge believers to live lives of uncompromised moral purity and integrity, standing completely free from the corrupt motives and deceitful practices of the surrounding culture.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Greek word akeraios is formed from the prefix a- (meaning "without") and a root verb meaning "to mix" or "to blend." Historically, the term was used in everyday language to describe physical substances that remained pure, whole, and unmixed—such as wine undiluted by water or precious metals unalloyed with cheaper materials.

When applied to human character and moral conduct, the word moved from the physical sphere to the spiritual and ethical realm. It came to describe a person whose character is genuine, uncorrupted, and free from deceitful motives or harmful intent. Rather than carrying hidden agendas or deceptive motives, an akeraios person is marked by moral simplicity, integrity, and guilelessness.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Akeraios can refer to something that is unmixed, pure, innocent, or guileless.

When describing human behavior and heart attitudes, the word carries two primary shades of meaning:

  • Unmixed integrity: Purity of character that is free from corrupting influences or mixed motives.

  • Innocence and harmlessness: Freedom from malicious intent, deceit, or a desire to cause harm to others.

In Philippians 2:15, the word describes the personal character that disciples of Christ are called to maintain. In this verse, akeraios refers to believers maintaining an uncompromised, guileless moral purity, staying untainted by the corrupt attitudes and practices of the surrounding culture.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, akeraios appears in contexts where moral clarity and innocent integrity are contrasted with worldly hostility and deceit.

In Jesus' instructions to His disciples, akeraios describes the simple, guileless character required when serving in a hostile environment. Believers are called to pair wise discernment with an innocent, harmless spirit.

In Paul's letters, the word highlights the contrast between the corrupt, deceptive ways of the fallen world and the transparent purity required of God's children. Rather than adopting the world's scheming methods or moral compromises, believers are called to remain spiritually simple and pure regarding evil, while remaining steadfastly faithful to God.

4. Theological Implications

The call to be akeraios points to the transformative work of the gospel in the life of a believer. Faith in Christ does not merely reform outward behavior; it purifies the inner motives and desires of the heart.

Living as an akeraios person reflects the holy character of God. As children of God, believers represent their heavenly Father in a dark world. God calls His people to be set apart, marked by absolute honesty, transparency, and freedom from malicious intent.

This moral purity is essential for the church's witness. When Christians live with unmixed motives and innocent conduct, their lives provide a striking contrast to a world often characterized by self-seeking, dishonesty, and moral confusion.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:15, Paul commands the believers in Philippi to live without grumbling or disputing, so that they may become "blameless and harmless, children of God without fault."

Paul pairs akeraios ("harmless" or "pure") with amemptos ("blameless"). While "blameless" focuses on the outward reputation before others—being above public reproach—"harmless" focuses on the internal purity and sincerity of the believer's motives. The Philippian believers were facing internal friction and external hostility. Paul warns them that internal strife and grumbling corrupt the church's inner purity.

By remaining akeraios, Christians stand out in sharp contrast to a "crooked and perverse generation." Their uncorrupted lives allow them to "shine as lights in the world," offering the word of life to those around them.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 10:16 — "Behold, I send you out as sheep in the midst of wolves. Therefore be wise as serpents and harmless as doves." Here Jesus commands His followers to combine shrewd discernment with pure, harmless innocence while facing a hostile world.

  • Romans 16:19 — "For your obedience has become known to all. Therefore I am glad on your behalf; but I want you to be wise in what is good, and simple concerning evil." In this verse, the word is translated "simple," describing a heart that remains pure and uncorrupted by evil practices and deceptive schemes.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The word akeraios is built specifically on the physical concept of an unmixed or undiluted substance, such as pure metal or unwatered wine. It refers to an inner nature that has not been contaminated by foreign elements or malicious intent.

Furthermore, being "harmless" or "simple" in the biblical sense does not mean being naive or defenseless. Scriptural innocence is paired with wisdom. Believers are called to understand truth clearly and discern error, while resolutely refusing to participate in deceit, malice, or moral corruption.
