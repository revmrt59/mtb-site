FAST - (epéchō, Strong's G1907)

Summary:

The Greek verb epechō means to hold firmly onto something, to pay close attention to a matter, to offer or present something forward, or to remain in a place. In Philippians 2:16, the word describes believers maintaining a firm grip on the gospel message while holding it out as a guiding light to the surrounding world.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Greek word epechō is formed by combining a preposition meaning "upon" or "toward" with the common verb meaning "to hold." In ancient everyday Greek, it was used in several practical ways: stopping to pay close attention to something, holding out an object or gift to someone, keeping a firm grip on a position, or remaining behind in a location.

When Paul wrote his letter to the Philippian church, he was writing to a Christian community living in a Roman colony surrounded by pagan customs. He urged them to stand out distinctively as children of God. Within this instruction, Paul uses epechō to describe how believers ought to interact with "the word of life"—the life-giving truth of the gospel.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb can refer to several related actions depending on the context:

  • To direct one's mind toward something, paying close attention or taking note.

  • To hold fast or maintain a firm, unbroken grasp on truth or a course of action.

  • To hold out, present, or offer something to others, much like presenting a light in the dark.

  • To stay, delay, or remain in a location for a period of time.

In Philippians 2:16, the word brings together the ideas of gripping tightly to the gospel and offering that same life-giving message to others.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across its various biblical contexts, epechō moves between the ideas of focused attention, physical staying, and steadfast holding. In historical narrative, it describes someone paying fixed attention to a speaker or staying behind in a region. In pastoral writing, it describes giving close, careful attention to one's conduct and doctrine.

In Philippians 2:16, the grammatical form is a present active participle in the nominative plural masculine. The present tense describes an ongoing, continuous practice rather than a single past event. The active voice shows that the believers themselves perform this action, while the plural form addresses the whole congregation together. They are continuously holding fast to and presenting the word of life as a united body.

4. Theological Implications

Holding fast to the word of life highlights that authentic faith involves steady endurance. The gospel is called "the word of life" because it imparts spiritual life and guides daily Christian walk. Clinging to this word keeps believers grounded when surrounded by moral darkness and opposition.

At the same time, maintaining this hold on the gospel serves a public function. When believers hold fast to God's truth, their steady lives shine like stars in a dark world. Their persistent faithfulness displays the transformational power of the gospel to those around them.

Finally, Paul links the believers' perseverance to his future joy at the return of Christ. A congregation that holds fast to the gospel demonstrates that the labor of gospel ministry bears lasting, proven fruit.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:14-15, Paul calls the church to do all things without complaining and disputing, so that they may be blameless and harmless, children of God without fault in the middle of a crooked and perverse generation. He adds that they shine as lights in the world.

Immediately following this image of light, verse 16 uses epechō ("holding fast the word of life"). Their public shining is directly connected to their private allegiance to Scripture. Believers cannot shine effectively as lights unless they remain anchored in the truth of the gospel. Holding fast to the word of life provides the essential energy and content for their witness. Their steadfast stance allows Paul to look forward to the day of Christ with confidence, knowing his efforts among them were fruitful.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 14:7 — "So He spoke a parable to those who were invited, when He noted how they chose the best places..." Here the word describes Jesus taking close notice of the behavior of the feast guests.

  • Acts 3:5 — "So he gave them his attention, expecting to receive something from them." Here the word describes the lame man fixing his undivided attention on Peter and John.

  • Acts 19:22 — "So he sent into Macedonia two of those who ministered to him, Timothy and Erastus, but he himself stayed in Asia for a time." Here the word refers to remaining behind in a geographic location.

  • 1 Timothy 4:16 — "Take heed to yourself and to the doctrine. Continue in them..." Here the word describes paying careful, ongoing attention to personal godly conduct and sound teaching.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The dual sense of epechō—retaining a firm grip and offering something forward—fits the broader message of Philippians. The church guards its doctrine not by hiding it away, but by living it out visibly. Preserving the truth and proclaiming the truth are two sides of the same calling.

Paul's mention of "the day of Christ" emphasizes that daily perseverance has eternal value. Holding fast to God's word today prepares believers to meet the Lord with joy when He returns.
