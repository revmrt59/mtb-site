WORD - (lógos, Strong's G3056)

Summary:

The Greek word logos refers broadly to a spoken word, a statement, a message, a report, or an account. In the New Testament, it frequently designates the proclaimed gospel message. In Philippians 2:16, logos refers to the "word of life"—the life-giving message of the gospel that believers are called to hold fast and display in a dark world.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek culture, logos was a widely used term with a range of meanings depending on the context. It could denote an ordinary spoken word, a reasoned argument, a formal speech, or a financial record.

In the biblical world, logos takes on heightened significance as God communicates His truth to humanity. Rather than remaining distant, God reveals His character and salvation through spoken and written words. In the New Testament, the term often identifies the core Christian message—the good news of salvation through Jesus Christ.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to a simple utterance, a complete speech, a matter under discussion, or a formal financial accounting. It describes direct communication intended to convey meaningful thought.

In gospel proclamation, the word denotes the divine message itself. When paired with the phrase "of life" in Philippians 2:16, logos means the gospel of Jesus Christ, which brings spiritual life to those who receive it and reveals the living God to the world.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses logos in several distinct ways:

  • A Spoken Statement or Teaching: It can refer to an individual saying, a instruction, or a sermon.

  • The Gospel Message: It routinely identifies the message of God's grace, such as "the message of the cross" or "the word of God."

  • An Account or Explanation: It can refer to a financial tally or a rational defense provided to answer an inquiry.

Because logos adapts to its setting, surrounding words clarify whether it refers to standard human speech, a business matter, or the saving message of Jesus Christ.

4. Theological Implications

The description of the gospel as the "word of life" highlights the transforming power of God's truth. The message is not merely human philosophy or moral advice; it carries the life of God to a world dead in sin.

God uses His word to impart life, shape character, and guide conduct. When believers line their lives up with this word, they demonstrate that the gospel is active and effective. The truth proclaimed in the gospel sustains spiritual vitality and equips believers to live as lights in a dark world.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:14–16, Paul addresses how believers ought to live within a crooked and perverse generation. He instructs them to avoid complaining and disputing so that they may prove themselves blameless and harmless. Right in the middle of this exhortation, Paul speaks of "holding fast the word of life."

Grammatically, the word appears in the accusative singular masculine form, acting as the direct object of the action. Believers are the ones who hold onto and present this vital message.

The context suggests a dual emphasis: believers must cling firmly to the truth of the gospel in their own convictions, and they must hold it forth visibly through their lifestyle and witness. Paul links their faithfulness to his own ministry, explaining that their perseverance in the word will give him cause to rejoice in the day of Christ, knowing his labors were not in vain.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 5:1 – "So it was, as the multitude pressed about Him to hear the word of God, that He stood by the lake of Gennesaret..."

  • 1 Corinthians 1:18 – "For the message of the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but to us who are being saved it is the power of God."

  • 2 Corinthians 4:2 – "But we have renounced the hidden things of shame, not walking in craftiness nor handling the word of God deceitfully..."

  • Philippians 4:17 – "Not that I seek the gift, but I seek the fruit that abounds to your account."

  • 1 Peter 3:15 – "But sanctify the Lord God in your hearts, and always be ready to give a defense to everyone who asks you a reason for the hope that is in you..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The phrase "word of life" presents a striking contrast to the surrounding world described in Philippians 2:15. While the culture around them is characterized as dark, crooked, and perverse, the message believers hold is filled with divine light and life.

By grounding their unity and daily conduct in this life-giving word, believers shine as clear beacons. Their transformed lives give visible testimony to the truth of the message they proclaim.
