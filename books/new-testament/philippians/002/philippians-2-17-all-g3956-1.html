ALL - (pâs, Strong's G3956)

Summary:

The word pas refers to the entirety of a group or class, translated as "all," "every," or "whole." In Philippians 2:17, the word appears in the dative masculine plural form to designate the entire congregation at Philippi ("you all"). Paul uses it to emphasize that his apostolic joy and sacrificial ministry extend comprehensively to every believer in the church, ensuring that no member is excluded from his shared rejoicing.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The apostle Paul wrote his letter to the Philippians while imprisoned, likely in Rome. The church at Philippi was the first Christian community established in Europe, and they maintained a warm, supportive relationship with Paul, sending financial relief and aid through Epaphroditus.

Throughout the letter, Paul addresses the reality of gospel advancement amid suffering, while gently exhorting the Philippian believers toward corporate humility, steadfastness, and undivided unity. In Philippians 2, Paul provides the ultimate pattern of self-emptying love in Christ (Philippians 2:5–11) and calls the believers to live as lights in a dark world. The word pas functions in this letter as a vital pastoral tool to embrace every member of the congregation in Paul's affection, prayers, and shared joy.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word pas is the primary Greek term used to express complete scope, totality, or universality. It can refer to an entire quantity, every individual within a group, or a whole entity.

Depending on its grammatical setting, the word carries distinct shades of meaning:

  • When modifying a singular noun without a definite article, it usually means "every" (e.g., every person, every tree).

  • When modifying a singular noun with a definite article, it means "the whole" or "the entire" (e.g., the whole city, the entire law).

  • When used in the plural, it means "all" or "all members" of a specified group.

In Philippians 2:17, the word appears in the dative masculine plural form, functioning as an adjectival modifier linked to the pronoun "you." Here it means "all of you" or "every single one of you," leaving no individual out of Paul's statement.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, pas serves a fundamental role in defining boundaries and scope. Context determines whether the word signifies absolute universality or a comprehensive group within a defined boundary:

  • Absolute Totality: Describing divine attributes or sweeping spiritual truths, such as God knowing all things or creating all things.

  • Comprehensive Inclusion within a Group: Describing every member within a specific assembly, family, or region without exception.

  • All Without Distinction: Indicating all categories or kinds of people (Jew and Gentile alike) rather than every single individual human being.

In apostolic greetings and pastoral epistles, pas regularly underlines corporate unity. When apostles address "all" the saints, they deliberately counter regional, social, or spiritual elitism within local assemblies.

4. Theological Implications

Paul's use of pas in Philippians 2:17 carries significant theological weight for life in the Christian community. The gospel creates a single, unified body of believers bound together by the self-giving sacrifice of Jesus Christ.

In local churches where personal disagreements or social divisions threaten fellowship, emphasizing "all" guards against partiality. Paul refuses to divide the church into factions or reserve his deepest affection for a favored few. Gospel joy and pastoral care are non-exclusive; they belong to the entire body. Furthermore, the suffering endured by gospel workers produces sacrificial fruit that overflows in mutual joy for every member of the church.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:17, Paul writes: "Yes, and if I am being poured out as a drink offering on the sacrifice and service of your faith, I am glad and rejoice with you all."

Paul uses imagery drawn from ancient sacrificial worship. A drink offering (or libation) involved pouring out wine beside or upon a primary altar sacrifice. Paul views the Philippians' faithful service and trust in God as the primary sacrifice offered to God. His own potential martyrdom or continued imprisonment is merely the drink offering poured out alongside their faith.

Rather than viewing his potential death with gloom or self-pity, Paul declares that he is glad and rejoices. By adding "with you all," Paul directly connects his joyful sacrifice to the entire congregation. The dative masculine plural form explicitly identifies the Philippian believers as the co-recipients and companions of his joy. Paul does not limit his rejoicing to church leaders or close personal friends; he shares his apostolic joy with every believer in Philippi, reinforcing their shared identity in Christ.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 17:21 — "that they all may be one, as You, Father, are in Me, and I in You; that they also may be one in Us, that the world may believe that You sent Me."

  • 2 Corinthians 7:15 — "And his affection for you is even greater whenever he remembers the obedience of you all, how with fear and trembling you received him."

  • Colossians 1:4 — "since we heard of your faith in Christ Jesus and of your love for all the saints;"

  • Romans 15:33 — "Now the God of peace be with you all. Amen."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The dative masculine plural form in Philippians 2:17 highlights the personal, relational setting of Paul's joy. The dative case identifies those who share in the experience of Paul's gladness.

Throughout the letter to the Philippians, Paul uses "all" with notable frequency when speaking of the congregation. He prays for "all of you" with joy (Philippians 1:4), holds "all of you" in his heart (Philippians 1:7), and longs for "all of you" with the affection of Jesus Christ (Philippians 1:8). His use of the word in Philippians 2:17 fits a deliberate, letter-wide emphasis on corporate solidarity, ensuring that every member feels valued and included in the life and mission of the gospel.
