FAITH - (pístis, Strong's G4102)

pistis (pistis, Strong’s G4102)

Summary:

The word pistis basically refers to trust, reliance, conviction, or faithfulness. In the New Testament, it describes wholehearted personal reliance on God and Jesus Christ rather than mere mental agreement. In Philippians 2:17, Paul speaks of "the sacrifice and service of your faith." Here, pistis refers to the Philippian believers' active, living trust in Christ, which Paul presents as a sacred worship offering to God, over which he is glad to pour out his own life as a drink offering.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Paul wrote his letter to the Philippians from prison, facing the real possibility of execution. The church at Philippi was also enduring opposition and pressure for their allegiance to Jesus Christ. Throughout chapter 2, Paul encourages them to maintain spiritual unity, steadfastness, and humility by pointing to the supreme example of Jesus Christ, who humbled Himself to the point of death on a cross (Philippians 2:5–11).

In Philippians 2:17, Paul reflects on his own life and prospective martyrdom. To communicate his joy in serving them, he draws upon ancient temple imagery. He pictures the Philippian believers' enduring trust in Christ as a sacred offering presented on God's altar, and he describes his own life and labor as a liquid drink offering poured out beside their offering.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word pistis means trust, belief, reliance, and personal confidence. When used in relation to God, it refers to a wholehearted reliance on His promises, power, and character. Depending on how it is used in a sentence, the word can mean:

  • Personal trust or reliance placed in God or Christ.

  • The quality of being faithful, trustworthy, or loyal.

  • The objective content of the Christian gospel (the Christian faith).

In Philippians 2:17, the word refers to the personal, active trust of the Philippian believers. Their faith is not just a private internal feeling, but a living reliance on God that produces concrete spiritual devotion.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, pistis carries a dynamic range of meaning grounded in personal relationship with God:

  • Personal Reliance: It describes a person turning away from self-sufficiency to rest entirely on God's mercy and power.

  • Active Loyalty: It reflects steady fidelity and devotion to God in the midst of trials.

  • The Gospel Message: It can refer to the core truth of Christianity handed down to the church.

In Paul's writings, pistis is never passive or static. It is a living, working trust. It receives God's salvation as a gift and naturally expresses itself through love, worship, and steadfast service.

4. Theological Implications

Scripture makes clear that faith is not a meritorious work that earns God's favor; it is the instrument through which a person receives God's grace. In Philippians 2:17, Paul highlights an important theological truth: the life of faith itself is an act of spiritual worship that honors God.

When believers trust God during hardship, their reliant trust glorifies Him as dependable, sovereign, and worthy. Faith pleases God because it looks away from human strength and honors God as the sole giver of life and salvation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:17, Paul writes: "Yes, and if I am being poured out as a drink offering on the sacrifice and service of your faith, I am glad and rejoice with you all."

In this verse, the target word is a feminine singular noun in the genitive case, where it identifies the source and character of the sacrificial service.

Paul uses language borrowed from Old Testament worship. In Old Testament ritual, worshippers brought animal or grain sacrifices to the altar, and sometimes a libation or drink offering of wine was poured out alongside or over the main sacrifice (such as in Numbers 15:5, 10).

Paul constructs a striking image that honors the Philippian church:

  • The main sacrifice on the altar is "the sacrifice and service of your faith." The Philippians' steadfast trust in Christ, demonstrated in their holy living and support of the gospel, is presented as the primary act of worship.

  • Paul views his own life and potential death as merely the accompanying "drink offering" poured out over their main sacrifice.

Rather than placing his apostolic ministry above the church, Paul treats their everyday trust in God as the central offering. This reveals the immense value God places on the quiet, enduring trust of ordinary believers.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Romans 10:17 — "So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God."

  • Galatians 2:16 — "...knowing that a man is not justified by the works of the law but by faith in Jesus Christ..."

  • Ephesians 3:17 — "...that Christ may dwell in your hearts through faith..."

  • Hebrews 11:1 — "Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The word translated "service" alongside faith in Philippians 2:17 is leitourgia, the source of the English word "liturgy." In ancient Greek culture, this word referred to public service performed for the community, and in the Scriptures, it refers to priestly service in God's sanctuary. By pairing "faith" directly with "sacrifice and service," Paul shows that Christian trust functions as priestly worship. When believers maintain their trust in God under trial, their reliant heart becomes an altar of genuine spiritual service.
