OUT - (spéndō, Strong's G4689)

Summary:

The Greek verb spendō means to pour out a drink offering or libation as part of a sacrificial ritual. In ancient worship, a drink offering of wine was poured over or alongside an altar sacrifice as an act of devotion to God. In Philippians 2:17, Paul uses this picture to describe his willingness to lay down his life for the church. He views his own imprisonment and potential execution not as a tragedy, but as a glad, worshipful offering poured out beside the sacrificial faith and service of the Philippian believers.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient world, sacrificial ceremonies often included a drink offering, known as a libation. In Old Testament worship, God instructed Israel to accompany daily burnt offerings and grain offerings with a specific measure of wine poured out at the altar. The liquid was completely poured out, symbolizing total surrender and devotion to God.

The apostle Paul draws upon this rich worship image to explain his own suffering and ministry. Writing from prison, under the real possibility of execution, Paul does not speak of his life using terms of defeat or loss. Instead, he uses the sacrificial imagery of the sanctuary to show that suffering endured for Christ and His church is an act of holy worship.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb spendō means to pour out a drink offering or to offer a libation.

When used literally, the word describes the physical act of tipping a vessel to pour liquid—typically wine—onto an altar or onto the ground before God. Once a drink offering is poured out, it cannot be gathered back into the cup. It is completely yielded up.

When used figuratively in the New Testament, the word describes the complete yielding of a person's life, energy, and physical existence in sacrificial service to God. It depicts a life spent entirely for the Lord's purposes, even to the point of physical death.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In the New Testament, spendō is used exclusively by the apostle Paul in figurative contexts dealing with his upcoming or potential martyrdom.

In Philippians 2:17, the verb appears in a present passive form ("am being poured out"). The passive expression shows Paul viewing his life as the offering being placed into God's hands. The present form captures his ongoing situation in prison, where his life was actively on the line for the gospel. Rather than viewing his captors as the ultimate controllers of his fate, Paul presents his life as an offering willingly yielded up to God.

4. Theological Implications

The picture of the drink offering carries profound theological truth regarding Christian service and ministry.

In Israel's sacrificial system, the drink offering was never the main sacrifice; it was an accompanying offering poured out over the primary sacrifice on the altar. In Philippians 2:17, Paul describes the faith and service of the Philippian believers as the primary sacrifice offered to God. Paul sees his own life and apostolic labors as merely the drink offering poured out on top of their faith.

This reveals deep humility. Paul does not view himself as the center of God's work. Instead, he views his suffering as a supporting offering that honors the faithful service of the church. Furthermore, it shows that gospel ministry is ultimately an act of worship. Suffering for the sake of Christ is not meaningless frustration; it is a sacred offering given directly to God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Philippians 2 contains one of the most famous descriptions of Christ’s humility in all of Scripture. Paul describes how Jesus emptied Himself, took the form of a bondservant, and humbled Himself to the point of death on a cross. Paul then calls the Philippians to express that same mind of Christ by living blamelessly and shining as lights in a dark world.

Directly after these exhortations, Paul introduces his own situation using spendō in verse 17. By linking his prospective martyrdom to the drink offering, Paul demonstrates the practical application of Christlike self-giving. He does not ask the Philippians to adopt an attitude of sacrificial humility that he himself is unwilling to practice.

Crucially, Paul links this pouring out of his life with personal joy. He writes that even if he is poured out as a drink offering, he is glad and rejoices with them all, inviting them to share in that same joy. The poured-out life is not presented as a grim burden, but as the fulfillment of Christian joy in union with Christ.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 2 Timothy 4:6 — "For I am already being poured out as a drink offering, and the time of my departure is at hand."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A drink offering presents a helpful picture of totality. Unlike other offerings where portions might be reserved or eaten by the priests, a liquid libation poured upon an altar is entirely absorbed or evaporated. Nothing remains in the vessel. Paul's choice of this word highlights that he held nothing back from his Lord. His time, strength, liberty, and physical life were completely surrendered.
