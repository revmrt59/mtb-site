SERVICE - (leitourgía, Strong's G3009)

Summary:

The Greek noun leitourgia refers to official service, public ministry, or sacred priestly worship offered to God. While the term originally described public works performed for a community, biblical writers used it for sacred duties performed in the temple and for the spiritual worship rendered by believers. In Philippians 2:17, the word describes the living worship and sacrificial devotion of the Philippian believers' faith, which Paul views as a holy offering presented to God.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek society outside the Bible, a leitourgia was a public service or civic duty performed for the community, often at personal expense by a wealthy citizen. Over time, the word moved into religious settings to describe official duties performed in worship.

In the Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint), leitourgia was chosen to translate Hebrew terms for tabernacle and temple service performed by the priests and Levites. The word carried a sense of sacred duty, formal worship, and consecrated work offered to God. By the time of the New Testament, leitourgia expressed both official priestly duties in the temple and the spiritual ministry rendered by Christians as they serve God and one another.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

exact meaning of leitourgia depends on how it is used in a passage:

  • Sacred or priestly service in a sanctuary or temple.

  • Public ministry or practical assistance rendered to meet the needs of God's people.

  • Spiritual worship and sacrificial living offered directly to God.

In Philippians 2:17, the word refers to the active, sacrificial ministry of faith that believers offer to God as a form of genuine worship.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses leitourgia in several distinct contexts across the New Testament:

First, it refers to temple service under the law. In Luke 1:23, the word describes the physical duties Zacharias carried out during his appointed time serving as a priest in the temple.

Second, it refers to the priestly ministry of Jesus Christ and the tabernacle service. In Hebrews 8:6 and 9:21, the word is translated "ministry," describing Christ's superior work as High Priest in the heavenly sanctuary and the ritual service performed under the Old Covenant.

Third, it describes practical, financial ministry among believers. In 2 Corinthians 9:12, Paul calls the collection for needy saints a "service," showing that practical financial relief given to Christians is viewed as an act of worship to God.

Fourth, it describes gospel ministry and Christian devotion. In Philippians 2:17 and 2:30, Paul uses the word for the faith-filled sacrifices and physical care provided by the Philippian church.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of leitourgia reveals that Christian living and ministry are sacred acts of worship. Under the Old Covenant, sacred service was restricted to designated priests performing ritual tasks in a physical temple. Under the New Covenant, the language of priestly service expands to include the everyday faith, financial support, and faithful endurance of believers.

When believers serve Christ and care for His servants, their actions are not merely social human kindness. Scripture views these actions as holy worship rendered to God. The sacrifice of faith offered by God's people is an acceptable offering in His sight.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:17, Paul writes, "Yes, and if I am being poured out as a drink offering on the sacrifice and service of your faith, I am glad and rejoice with you all."

The word leitourgia appears here as a feminine noun in the dative case and singular number. It works together with the word "sacrifice" (thysia) under a single preposition to describe the Philippians' faith. Paul paints a vivid picture drawn from Old Testament worship offerings. In Old Testament sacrificial practice, a drink offering of wine was poured out beside or over a major burnt offering.

Paul pictures the Philippians' faith as the main sacrifice and sacred service offered to God. He views his own potential martyrdom or suffering as merely the drink offering poured out alongside their greater offering. Instead of viewing his hardships as a tragedy, Paul finds deep joy in knowing that his life supports and completes the sacred service of faith being offered by the church.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 1:23 – "So it was, as soon as the days of his service were completed, that he departed to his own house."

  • 2 Corinthians 9:12 – "For the administration of this service not only supplies the needs of the saints, but also overflows through many thanksgivings to God."

  • Philippians 2:30 – "because for the work of Christ he was close to death, not regarding his life, to supply what was lacking in your service toward me."

  • Hebrews 8:6 – "But now He has obtained a more excellent ministry, inasmuch as He is also Mediator of a better covenant, which was established on better promises."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Philippians, leitourgia connects the spiritual conviction of faith with practical financial support. In Philippians 2:30, Paul uses the same word to describe Epaphroditus risking his life to deliver financial aid to Paul on behalf of the church. This shows that the Philippians' "service of faith" in verse 17 was not an abstract feeling, but a active, costly commitment expressed through tangible care for Christ's apostle.
