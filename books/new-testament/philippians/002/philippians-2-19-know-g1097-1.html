KNOW - (ginṓskō, Strong's G1097)

Summary:

Ginosko means to come to know, learn, perceive, or understand through observation, experience, or news received. In Philippians 2:19, Paul uses the word as he expresses his hope to send Timothy to Philippi so that Paul can learn the true condition of the church, allowing his own heart to be refreshed and encouraged by news of their standing in the faith.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek and New Testament usage, ginosko describes coming to know something, gaining awareness of a situation, or recognizing a reality. It moves beyond abstract theoretical speculation to describe active perception, personal acquaintance, or receiving practical information about events and people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Ginosko can mean to learn, perceive, understand, recognize, or become acquainted with facts or people. When a person is the subject, it often refers to discovering news, coming to understand an ongoing situation, or knowing someone personally. In Philippians 2:19, the word describes Paul receiving reliable news about the welfare and spiritual condition of the Philippian believers.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the New Testament, ginosko is used in a wide range of contexts. It can describe standard human perception, such as learning that a crowd has gathered or discovering someone's health or circumstances. It also describes deep spiritual realities, such as coming to know God, recognizing Jesus as the Savior, or understanding divine truth. The unifying idea is an active transition from ignorance or distance to clear understanding and awareness.

4. Theological Implications

Although ginosko in Philippians 2:19 refers to receiving practical news about a local congregation, its presence highlights how Christian fellowship functions. Gospel partnership is never distant or indifferent. True encouragement within the body of Christ relies on real, honest communication rather than vague assumptions. Paul’s longing to know their state shows that mutual care, shared burdens, and informed prayer are essential aspects of life together in Christ.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Writing from prison, Paul faces physical confinement and uncertain circumstances. Yet his primary concern remains the spiritual stability and well-being of the churches he founded. He plans to send Timothy to Philippi with a specific purpose: Timothy will assess how the church is doing and report back to Paul.

In this verse, the word functions as an aorist active participle in the nominative singular masculine form, referring to Paul himself as the subject. The grammatical construction indicates that Paul expects his own spirit to be encouraged as a result of coming to know how the Philippians are faring. Real news of their steadfastness in Christ will bring comfort to the imprisoned apostle.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 16:15 — "And He said to them, 'You are those who justify yourselves before men, but God knows your hearts.'"

  • John 14:20 — "At that day you will know that I am in My Father, and you in Me, and I in you."

  • 1 Corinthians 13:12 — "For now we see in a mirror, dimly, but then face to face. Now I know in part, but then I shall know just as I also am known."

  • Colossians 4:8 — "I am sending him to you for this very purpose, that he may know your circumstances and comfort your hearts."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Paul’s statement in Philippians 2:19 closely parallels his phrasing in Colossians 4:8. In both passages, Paul dispatches a trusted coworker to gather accurate news and bring mutual encouragement. This reveals a consistent pattern in apostolic ministry: spiritual care involves direct personal contact, personal accountability, and an active desire to remain informed about the growth and struggles of God's people.
