STATE - (perí, Strong's G4012)

Summary:

The Greek word peri is a primary preposition that basic meaning includes "about," "concerning," or "around." When combined with an article, as it is in Philippians 2:19, it functions as a phrase referring to a person's life situation, condition, or circumstances. In Philippians 2:19, the word is translated "state" in the NKJV, where Paul uses it to describe the overall condition and spiritual welfare of the Philippian believers, which he eagerly desires to hear about so that his own heart may be refreshed.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Paul wrote his letter to the Philippians while under Roman imprisonment. Despite his own chains and uncertain future, his heart remained deeply tied to the churches he had planted. The church at Philippi was especially dear to Paul, having supported him in his ministry through prayer and financial partnership.

Because Paul was confined and unable to travel to Philippi himself, he planned to send his trusted co-worker Timothy. In Philippians 2:19, Paul explains his purpose for sending Timothy: he wants to receive a complete update about how the Philippian Christians are faring. The word translated "state" comes from the Greek preposition peri, which directs attention specifically to their ongoing situation, trials, unity, and spiritual health.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Peri functions grammatically as a preposition. Its spatial sense means "around" or "encircling," describing something that surrounds a object or person. Its far more common figurative sense is "about," "concerning," or "regarding."

When peri is joined with a definite article and a pronoun (literally, "the things concerning you"), the Greek idiom refers to a person's actual situation or affairs. Here the word means:

  • The circumstances surrounding someone

  • A person's condition or state of affairs

  • The real-life details of someone's situation

In Philippians 2:19, the word points to the complete report Paul hopes to receive regarding the Philippians' life and ministry.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In the New Testament, peri appears across a wide variety of contexts. Its primary usage patterns include:

  • Pointing to a subject of speech, writing, or thought ("about" or "concerning").

  • Highlighting the object of personal concern, prayer, or effort ("on behalf of" or "for").

  • Indicating physical presence or location ("around").

In pastoral passages, the word often introduces the specific focus of a leader's care or instruction. Paul does not merely want general news from Philippi; he wants news that touches directly on their life in Christ—their steadfastness under pressure, their love for one another, and their growth in the gospel.

4. Theological Implications

The use of this preposition in Philippians 2:19 reflects the deeply relational nature of Christian community. Biblical leadership is never detached or bureaucratic. Paul's desire to know their "state" demonstrates that genuine Christian fellowship requires active care and mutual awareness.

This passage also highlights how encouragement works in the body of Christ. Paul mentions that learning about their condition will allow him to "be of good cheer." Even while suffering in prison, Paul's emotional joy was directly connected to the spiritual health of his brothers and sisters. Christian love creates a shared bond where the standing of one believer directly impacts the joy of another.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:19–24, Paul highlights Timothy as a model servant. Unlike others who seek their own interests, Timothy possessed a genuine concern for the welfare of the Philippians. Paul relies on Timothy precisely because Timothy shares his heart for the church.

The preposition peri anchors Paul's intention in sending Timothy. Paul was not asking for a brief, casual greeting. He wanted Timothy to observe their situation firsthand and return with accurate news about their "state." The context shows that Paul anticipated good news regarding their faith, which would serve to cheer his heart in the midst of his confinement.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 24:19 — "So he said to them, 'What things?' So they said to Him, 'The things concerning Jesus of Nazareth...'"

Here the preposition introduces the central topic of discussion among the disciples on the road to Emmaus, focusing on the life and ministry of Jesus.

  • Acts 28:23 — "So when they had appointed him a day, many came to him at his lodging, to whom he explained and solemnly testified of the kingdom of God, persuading them concerning Jesus..."

In this setting, the word marks the grand theme of Paul's preaching to the Jewish leaders in Rome—the truth about Jesus.

  • 1 Thessalonians 3:2 — "and sent Timothy, our brother and minister of God, and our fellow laborer in the gospel of Christ, to establish you and encourage you concerning your faith,"

Just as in Philippians, Paul sends Timothy to focus specifically on the Thessalonians' spiritual condition and strengthen them in their faith.

  • 1 Peter 3:18 — "For Christ also suffered once for sins, the just for the unjust, that He might bring us to God..."

Here the word is translated "for" to express the specific reason and target of Christ's sacrificial death—our sins.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Small grammatical tools like prepositions often carry great pastoral significance in Scripture. By focusing on "the things concerning" the Philippians, Paul demonstrates that biblical ministry pays attention to the concrete details of believers' lives. Pastoral care is not abstract; it seeks to know how Christians are actually enduring hardship, growing in grace, and walking together in the gospel.
