TRUST - (elpízō, Strong's G1679)

Summary:

The word elpizō means to expect, look forward to, or place confident trust in someone or something. In Philippians 2:19, Paul uses this word to express his expectation—grounded in the Lord Jesus—that he will soon send Timothy to the Philippian church to care for their spiritual well-being and bring back an encouraging report.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek, elpizō refers to looking forward to the future with expectation. Depending on the context, this expectation could range from a simple anticipation of upcoming events to a deep, unwavering reliance on a person or promise.

In the New Testament, this forward-looking expectation is transformed by the reality of God's character and promises. Rather than expressing mere wishful thinking or uncertain desire, the word often describes a firm expectation rooted in God's faithfulness. When Paul writes his letter to the Philippians from prison, his personal freedom is restricted, yet his outlook remains defined by confident trust in Christ.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

essential meaning of elpizō is to look forward with expectation, hope, or confidence.

The word can refer to:

  • Expecting a future event to happen.

  • Looking forward to seeing or visiting someone.

  • Placing personal reliance and trust in God or Christ.

In everyday contexts, the word describes expecting a guest or anticipating a report. In spiritual contexts, it describes active faith directed toward God for the future. In Philippians 2:19, the word describes Paul's practical expectation regarding his ministry plans, an expectation anchored in the authority and guidance of Jesus Christ.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, elpizō bridges present circumstances with future outcomes. English translations render elpizō as either "trust" or "hope," depending on whether the emphasis falls on personal reliance or future expectation.

Biblical hope is not an uncertain wish; it is an expectant reliance. When people place their expectation in worldly status, human leaders, or personal accomplishments, that confidence proves fragile. But when expectation is directed toward Christ, it provides steady assurance regardless of immediate hardship.

Paul frequently uses this word when discussing his travel plans and ministry goals. By doing so, he shows that even routine planning is an exercise of trust in God's sovereign oversight.

4. Theological Implications

Paul's statement in Philippians 2:19 offers key insights into how believers approach the future:

  • Planning Under Christ's Lordship: Paul does not say simply "I plan" or "I hope." He specifies that he trusts "in the Lord Jesus." Every practical plan a Christian makes ought to be submitted to Christ's sovereign direction.

  • Mutual Encouragement in the Church: Paul's expectation is linked to his desire to be "encouraged" by news of the Philippians. Faithful pastoral leadership seeks the spiritual health of others and finds joy in their growth.

  • Confidence Amid Uncertainty: Though Paul is imprisoned and uncertain of his own physical outcome, his trust in Christ allows him to plan proactively for the spiritual care of the churches.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:19, Paul expresses his immediate goal: "But I trust in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy to you shortly."

Grammatically, Paul uses the present tense, active voice, and indicative mood to state his current, real expectation. The present tense describes his present outlook, the active voice presents Paul as the one holding this expectation, and the indicative mood expresses it as a genuine reality in his mind.

The phrase "in the Lord Jesus" is critical. Paul does not rely on his own authority or administrative efficiency. His expectation to send Timothy is held in submission to Christ. In the surrounding passage, Paul highlights Timothy's selfless character (Philippians 2:20–22) in contrast to those who seek their own interests. Paul’s trust in Christ leads him to send his most trusted companion to serve the Philippians, echoing the self-giving humility of Christ described earlier in the chapter (Philippians 2:5–11).

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 12:21 — "And in His name Gentiles will trust."

  • John 5:45 — "Do not think that I shall accuse you to the Father; there is one who accuses you—Moses, in whom you trust."

  • 2 Corinthians 1:10 — "...who delivered us from so great a death, and does deliver us; in whom we trust that He will still deliver us,"

  • Philippians 2:23 — "Therefore I hope to send him at once, as soon as I see how it goes with me."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Paul balances confident expectation with realistic awareness. Just a few verses later, in Philippians 2:23, he uses the same word when stating he plans to send Timothy "as soon as I see how it goes with me." This shows that trusting in the Lord does not require knowing every future detail in advance. Paul held a clear conviction about what he ought to do, while remaining fully attentive to how God would unfold his immediate circumstances in prison.
