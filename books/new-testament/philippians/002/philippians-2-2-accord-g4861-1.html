ACCORD - (sýmpsychos, Strong's G4861)

Summary:

The word sumpsuchos means being united in soul, harmonious, or sharing a common inner life and purpose. In Philippians 2:2, Paul uses this word to urge the believers in Philippi to stand united with one shared heart and spirit as they serve Christ together.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Paul wrote his letter to the Philippians while imprisoned, encouraging a beloved church that faced both external pressure and internal friction. Although the Philippian congregation was faithful and generous, personal disagreements among members threatened their Christian witness and church harmony. To guard their unity and spiritual health, Paul urged them to live in a manner worthy of the gospel. In Philippians 2:2, Paul stacks several descriptions of shared heart and mind, calling on the believers to be "of one accord." The term describes a community whose inner desires, choices, and spiritual priorities move in harmony toward a single goal.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word sumpsuchos means united in soul, single-minded, or sharing a common heart and purpose. It describes people who share an inner harmony, moving together with a unified spiritual life rather than pulling in separate, competing directions. When applied to a local church, it refers to believers aligning their personal motives and actions with the mind of Christ. In Philippians 2:2, the word is a masculine nominative plural adjective describing the believers themselves, characterising them as individuals who are joined together in soul and purpose.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In New Testament language, joined terms built with prefixes expressing togetherness emphasize shared partnership and common experience. Here, sumpsuchos stresses an inner alignment of heart and life. True unity in the Christian community is not merely outward agreement or superficial compliance. It involves a genuine, shared spiritual life produced by the Holy Spirit. The word goes beyond organizational alignment to describe deep personal and spiritual harmony among believers in Christ.

4. Theological Implications

Spiritual unity among believers flows directly from their shared union with Jesus Christ. Because Christians share one salvation, one Lord, and one Spirit, their daily lives together ought to reflect that spiritual reality. Division in the church harms gospel witness and disrupts fellowship, whereas soul-level harmony reveals the transforming power of God's grace. This unity does not erase individual personalities or unique spiritual gifts; instead, it directs every gift toward a common purpose: glorifying God and building up the body of Christ.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:1–4, Paul grounds his call to unity in the rich spiritual blessings the Philippians have received: encouragement in Christ, comfort of love, fellowship of the Spirit, and affection and mercy. Because they share these divine gifts, Paul asks them to make his joy complete by living in true harmony. He uses four complementary phrases to express this unity: being like-minded, having the same love, being of one accord, and being of one mind. Positioned near the end of this list, "of one accord" highlights the inner harmony that makes selfless humility possible. Immediately following this verse, Paul presents Christ's supreme example of humbling Himself for others. Inner soul-harmony is both the preparation for and the result of Christlike selflessness.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • This term is unique to this context and does not appear elsewhere in the biblical corpus.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Christian unity requires intentional spiritual effort and constant reliance on God's grace. Being of one accord is not an automatic human accomplishment or a simple personality match. It requires believers to put aside personal ambition and pride, actively choosing to value others ahead of themselves. When a local church cultivates this deep, soul-level unity, it reflects the character of Christ to a divided world.
