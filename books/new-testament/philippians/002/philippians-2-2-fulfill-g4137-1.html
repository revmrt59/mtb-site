FULFILL - (plēróō, Strong's G4137)

Summary:

The Greek verb pleroo means to fill, bring to completion, or make full. In Philippians 2:2, Paul uses this word to urge the believers to make his pastoral joy complete by walking in spiritual unity, shared love, and humble fellowship.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Writing to the church at Philippi from prison, the apostle Paul expresses profound affection for the believers who have partnered with him in the gospel. Though Paul already experiences genuine joy whenever he prays for them, his heart desires to see their fellowship reach its fullest expression. He uses the verb pleroo to ask them to complete or fill up his joy.

In ancient everyday Greek, pleroo was a common term used to describe filling a physical container, such as filling a jar with water or filling a room with fragrance. It was also used for completing a task, fulfilling a duty, or carrying out a promise. In the New Testament, the term moves easily between physical descriptions of fullness and spiritual descriptions of completed purpose, joy, or obedience.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word pleroo can refer to making full, filling to capacity, bringing to completion, or accomplishing a task.

When applied to physical items, it describes filling something until it contains its proper amount, such as a house filled with an aroma. When applied to prophecies, duties, or divine promises, it means to fulfill or bring to full accomplishment what was promised or expected. When applied to abstract qualities like joy, righteousness, or wisdom, it means to bring that quality to its intended measure or full expression.

In Philippians 2:2, the word means to complete or bring to full measure. Paul is not saying he lacks joy entirely, but rather that their visible unity in Christ will bring his pastoral joy to its full capacity.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, pleroo appears in several distinct settings:

  • Physical or Spatial Fullness: The word describes filling physical spaces or containers, such as the fragrance of costly perfume filling an entire house (John 12:3).

  • Fulfilling Prophecy and Divine Purpose: The word frequently marks the accomplishment of Old Testament scriptures or divine plans, such as Jesus fulfilling all righteousness at His baptism (Matthew 3:15) or God bringing Old Testament promises to pass in the life of Jesus (Matthew 1:22).

  • Completing Spiritual Work or Service: The word describes finishing a God-given mission or ministry assignment, such as believers completing the work entrusted to them (Acts 14:26) or Paul carrying out his calling to present God's word (Colossians 1:25).

  • Spiritual Capacity and Abundance: The word describes believers being filled with spiritual virtues, such as being filled with the fruits of righteousness through Jesus Christ (Philippians 1:11), or the full realization of spiritual joy (John 17:13).

4. Theological Implications

The biblical idea of fulfillment shows that God cares about completion, not merely beginnings. In the Christian life, God does not merely initiate work; He brings it to its intended full measure.

Furthermore, Paul's request to "fulfill my joy" highlights an important theological truth about Christian community. Biblical joy is not an isolated, individualistic experience. While joy originates from Christ and salvation, its earthly fullness is deeply connected to the health and unity of God's people. A local church living in harmony, mutual affection, and shared purpose displays the reality of the gospel and brings apostolic and pastoral joy to full completion.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:2, Paul writes, "fulfill my joy by being like-minded, having the same love, being of one accord, of one mind."

The grammatical form of pleroo in this verse functions in the aorist tense, active voice, imperative mood, and plural number. As an imperative, it forms a direct command addressing the entire congregation together. Paul addresses all the believers at Philippi, calling them as a community to carry out this action together.

The immediate context makes clear how this fulfillment is accomplished. In verse 1, Paul lists four foundational spiritual realities: encouragement in Christ, comfort of love, fellowship of the Spirit, and affection and mercy. Because these spiritual realities belong to them in Christ, Paul urges them in verse 2 to act upon them. They complete Paul's joy not by adopting an artificial uniformity, but by sharing the same Christlike mindset, self-sacrificing love, and humble concern for one another.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 3:15 — "But Jesus answered and said to him, 'Permit it to be so now, for thus it is fitting for us to fulfill all righteousness.' Then he allowed Him."

  • John 12:3 — "Then Mary took a pound of very costly oil of spikenard, anointed the feet of Jesus, and wiped His feet with her hair. And the house was filled with the fragrance of the oil."

  • Romans 8:4 — "that the righteous requirement of the law might be fulfilled in us who do not walk according to the flesh but according to the Spirit."

  • Philippians 1:11 — "being filled with the fruits of righteousness which are by Jesus Christ, to the glory and praise of God."

  • Colossians 1:25 — "of which I became a minister according to the stewardship from God which was given to me for you, to fulfill the word of God,"

7. Other Relevant Insights

Pastors and spiritual leaders find deep joy in the spiritual health and selfless unity of the believers under their care. Paul was in prison, facing hardship and uncertain circumstances, yet his primary desire was for the church to walk in Christian love.

This passage reveals that personal joy and church unity are intimately linked. When believers put aside selfish ambition and conceit, treating others as better than themselves, they glorify God and bring full measure to the joy of those who shepherd them.
