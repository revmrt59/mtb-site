HAVING - (échō, Strong's G2192)

Summary:

The Greek verb echo means to have, hold, possess, or maintain. It is a common word used across the New Testament for physical possession, mental attitudes, personal relationships, and ongoing spiritual realities. In Philippians 2:2, Paul uses the word to urge believers to maintain "the same love," calling the congregation to actively hold fast to a shared, practical affection for one another that brings his pastoral joy to completion.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The verb echo is one of the most frequent words in the Greek language. In everyday communication, it was used to describe owning property, carrying an object, experiencing a physical state, or holding a belief. Its basic meaning is to possess or to hold onto something.

In biblical writings, the word spans both external circumstances and internal realities. It describes people having physical items like food or money, having physical ailments, or having spiritual blessings like eternal life, peace, and faith. Because the word is so broad, its exact force depends heavily on what is being held or possessed in any given sentence.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to holding or possessing something in several clear ways:

  • Physical possession: owning, carrying, or holding a material object.

  • Internal condition: experiencing a state of mind or body, such as fear, joy, peace, or sickness.

  • Relational or positional standing: having a master, a father, a friend, or an advocate.

  • Ongoing posture: holding onto an attitude, commitment, or conviction over time.

In Philippians 2:2, the word describes an ongoing, shared posture of heart and mind. Here it means to possess and actively maintain "the same love" within the fellowship of the church.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, echo often moves beyond passive ownership to describe an active, living reality. To "have" faith, for example, is not merely to store a concept in one's head, but to live in active reliance on God. To "have" peace is to experience and walk in divine rest.

When used in commands or pastoral exhortations, the verb frequently emphasizes maintaining what has been given. In Philippians 2:2, the word functions as a description of how believers live out church unity. Paul does not ask the Philippians to create a new kind of human affection from scratch. Rather, he calls them to continuously hold and display the love they have already received in Jesus Christ.

4. Theological Implications

The use of echo in calls to Christian unity highlights several practical truths about life in the church:

  • Unity requires active possession: Christian love is not an abstract ideal. It must be held, owned, and expressed in real community life.

  • Unity flows from shared grace: Believers are able to maintain "the same love" because they all draw from the same divine source mentioned in Philippians 2:1—the encouragement, comfort, and fellowship of the Holy Spirit.

  • Mutual affection guards against division: When every member of a congregation holds fast to the same self-giving love, selfish ambition and conceit are crowded out.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Philippians 2:2 sits within Paul's memorable appeal for gospel-shaped humility and unity. In verse 1, Paul reminds the church of what they possess in Christ: consolation, comfort of love, fellowship of the Spirit, and affection and mercy.

In verse 2, he asks them to complete his joy by being like-minded, "having the same love," and being of one accord. The word "having" shows how the spiritual gifts of verse 1 become visible in verse 2. Because believers have received Christ's love, they are now to hold "the same love" toward one another.

This love is not selective or partial. It is "the same love"—an equal, practical concern for every brother and sister in the assembly. By continuously holding fast to this common affection, the church guards its witness and reflects the mind of Christ.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 15:13 – "Greater love has no one than this, than to lay down one’s life for his friends."

  • 2 Corinthians 3:4 – "And we have such trust through Christ toward God."

  • Philippians 1:30 – "...having the same conflict which you saw in me and now hear is in me."

  • Hebrews 8:3 – "For every high priest is appointed to offer both gifts and sacrifices. Therefore it is necessary that this One also have something to offer."

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a meaningful connection between Philippians 1:30 and Philippians 2:2. In chapter 1, Paul notes that the Philippian believers are "having the same conflict" that he himself faces. In chapter 2, he urges them to be "having the same love."

The repetition highlights the dual nature of Christian fellowship: believers share outer struggles against hardship, and they must meet those struggles by sharing inner, practical love for one another. Mutual hardship without mutual love leads to friction; mutual hardship faced with mutual love produces unbreakable gospel unity.
