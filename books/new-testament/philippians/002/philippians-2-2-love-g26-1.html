LOVE - (agápē, Strong's G26)

Summary:

The word agape refers to love, devotion, or active goodwill toward another person. In the New Testament, it is frequently used for God's love toward humanity and the practical care believers are called to show one another. In Philippians 2:2, Paul uses the word to urge the church to share "the same love," calling believers to demonstrate mutual goodwill and practical care toward every member of the congregation so that the church remains united in spirit.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Paul wrote his letter to the Philippians while imprisoned, likely in Rome. The church at Philippi was dear to Paul, yet they faced subtle pressures that threatened their harmony. Differences in perspective and personal rivalries posed a real danger to the congregation's witness.

In Philippians 2:1–2, Paul grounds his appeal for unity in what the believers have already received from God. Because they have experienced encouragement in Christ, comfort from His love, fellowship in the Spirit, and divine affection, Paul urges them to express these blessings among themselves. His request that they fulfill his joy centers on shared spiritual health, which requires them to maintain unified affection and goal across the entire church.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word agape means love, high regard, affection, or active goodwill. It describes a genuine concern for another person's well-being that expresses itself in practical ways.

While the New Testament often uses this word to describe God's self-giving love for sinners, the basic meaning of the word itself simply denotes love or care. The deep, selfless character associated with Christian love comes from how God reveals love through Jesus Christ, rather than from a secret definition hidden within the vocabulary word itself. When used of believers, it refers to a committed goodwill that seeks the good of others.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, agape describes the central character of Christian love. It is used for God's deep love for the world, Christ's love for His followers, and the love believers owe to God and to one another.

Scripture shows that this love is not merely a passive feeling or emotional response. It is active and expressed through tangible deeds, patience, and forgiveness. In Christian fellowship, this love serves as the central mark of discipleship and the fruit produced in a believer's life by the Holy Spirit. Without it, religious effort and spiritual gifts lose their value before God.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, agape points to the very nature of God, for Scripture declares that God is love. Humans do not originate true spiritual love; rather, believers love because God first loved them. The supreme demonstration of love occurred when God sent His Son into the world to offer salvation to sinners.

This divine love forms the foundation for Christian unity. Because believers have been brought into God's family through the same grace, they are called to reflect that grace in how they treat one another. Unity in the church is not achieved by artificial agreement on every small preference, but by being rooted together in the shared love of Christ.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:2, Paul places "having the same love" alongside three other phrases that emphasize Christian harmony: being like-minded, being of one accord, and being of one mind. Grammatically, the word functions in the accusative singular feminine as the direct object of "having," identifying the shared quality that all believers in the church are called to possess.

The phrase "the same love" is crucial in this passage. Paul is not asking believers to show affection only to their close friends within the church while ignoring others. Instead, he calls for equal, shared goodwill toward every member of the body. The love they express to one another is to be patterned after the love they have all received from Christ.

This call to love prepares the ground for Paul's instructions in verses 3 and 4, where he instructs believers to do nothing through selfish ambition or conceit, but in lowliness of mind to esteem others better than themselves. Shared love creates the practical climate in which humility and consideration for others can flourish.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 15:10 — "If you keep My commandments, you will abide in My love, just as I have kept My Father’s commandments and abide in His love."

  • Galatians 5:22 — "But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faithfulness..."

  • 1 Corinthians 13:13 — "And now abide faith, hope, love, these three; but the greatest of these is love."

  • 1 John 4:7 — "Beloved, let us love one another, for love is of God; and everyone who loves is born of God and knows God."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A common danger in local churches is partiality—loving some believers while remaining cold toward others based on natural preferences, social status, or personality. By instructing the Philippians to have "the same love," Scripture guards against partiality and challenges believers to extend practical goodwill to everyone in the fellowship.

Additionally, Paul reveals a meaningful connection between church unity and pastoral joy. He tells the Philippians that seeing them walk in shared love will fulfill his joy. Faithful leaders are deeply encouraged when believers live together in peace, demonstrating that the gospel has genuinely transformed their relationships.
