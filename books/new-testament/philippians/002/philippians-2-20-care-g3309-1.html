CARE - (merimnáō, Strong's G3309)

Summary:

The Greek verb merimnaō means to give careful thought to, to be concerned about, or to be anxious over something. While the word often describes destructive anxiety or worry over earthly needs, it can also describe legitimate, selfless concern for the spiritual and physical well-being of others. In Philippians 2:20, Paul uses the word to describe Timothy's deep, genuine devotion to the spiritual health of the believers in Philippi.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

When Paul wrote his letter to the Philippians, he was imprisoned for the sake of the gospel. Despite his own difficult circumstances, his heart remained focused on the spiritual growth and unity of the churches he had planted. The church at Philippi faced personal conflicts and external pressures, making Paul eager to send a trusted companion who could encourage them and bring back news of their standing.

Paul chose Timothy for this task. In Philippians 2:20, Paul explains why Timothy was uniquely qualified: Timothy possessed a rare, unselfish concern for their welfare. While others were distracted by their own interests, Timothy shared Paul’s pastoral heart and was ready to expend his energy for the good of the Philippian believers.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Merimnaō carries the primary idea of directing one's mind or attention intensely toward something. Depending on the context and the motive behind the focus, the word can express two distinct concepts:

  • Negative Worry or Anxiety: When directed toward temporal matters with a spirit of fear or self-reliance, the word refers to being anxious, distracted, or overly consumed by worldly cares.

  • Positive Care or Concern: When directed toward the spiritual health and practical needs of others, the word means to have a genuine, active interest in someone's well-being.

The word itself describes an intense mental focus. Whether that focus becomes a sinful distraction or a godly virtue depends entirely on what holds a person's attention and why.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In the Gospels, merimnaō appears frequently in Jesus' teachings about worry. Jesus commands His followers not to be anxious about life, food, or clothing, reminding them that their heavenly Father knows their needs and faithfully cares for them. In those passages, merimnaō describes a divided mind that doubts God's provision.

However, in the New Testament epistles, Paul uses merimnaō to describe the healthy, loving concern that believers ought to have for one another. In 1 Corinthians, for example, Paul speaks of how members of the body of Christ should care for one another equally.

In Philippians 2:20, the verb appears in the future tense, active voice, and indicative mood, translated as "will care." The active voice presents Timothy as the one actively extending this care, while the future form assures the Philippians that Timothy will demonstrate this faithful, attentive concern upon his arrival.

4. Theological Implications

Timothy's care for the Philippians reflects a core truth of the gospel: genuine Christian ministry is marked by self-giving love. Earlier in Philippians 2, Paul calls believers to have the mind of Christ, who did not look only to His own interests but humbled Himself to serve others. Timothy serves as a concrete example of this Christlike mindset in action.

Furthermore, the biblical usage of merimnaō shows a clear spiritual distinction:

  • Godly concern seeks the spiritual good of others and trusts God for the outcome.

  • Ungodly anxiety seeks self-preservation and forgets God's sovereign care.

When believers channel their attention away from self-centered worry and toward loving concern for their brothers and sisters in Christ, they mirror the shepherd-heart of Jesus.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

The context of Philippians 2 highlights why Timothy’s care was so extraordinary. Paul writes that he has "no one like-minded" who will sincerely care for their state. In the very next verse, Paul adds a stark contrast: "For all seek their own, not the things which are of Jesus Christ" (Philippians 2:21).

In this environment, Timothy stood out. His concern was not professional duty or superficial friendliness. The word "sincerely" reinforces that Timothy’s focus on the Philippians was genuine, unmixed with selfish ambition. By using merimnaō here, Paul assures the Philippians that Timothy will hold their spiritual well-being close to his heart, treating their concerns as his own.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 6:25 — "Therefore I say to you, do not worry about your life, what you will eat or what you will drink..."

  • Luke 10:41 — "And Jesus answered and said to her, 'Martha, Martha, you are worried and troubled about many things.'"

  • 1 Corinthians 12:25 — "...that there should be no schism in the body, but that the members should have the same care for one another."

  • Philippians 4:6 — "Be anxious for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A helpful comparison exists between Paul's use of merimnaō in Philippians 2:20 and his command in Philippians 4:6. In chapter 4, Paul instructs believers to "be anxious (merimnaō) for nothing," directing them to turn their burdens over to God in prayer. Yet in chapter 2, Paul commends Timothy because he "will care (merimnaō)" for the church.

This reveals that Scripture does not call Christians to a state of emotional detachment or indifference toward people. Believers are called to release self-centered anxiety to God in prayer, freeing their minds and hearts to give active, loving care to the needs of others.
