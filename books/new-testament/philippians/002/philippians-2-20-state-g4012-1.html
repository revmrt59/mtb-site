STATE - (perí, Strong's G4012)

Summary:

Peri is a Greek preposition that basic usage defines as "about," "concerning," "around," or "with reference to." When combined with a personal pronoun in Greek, it forms a common idiom referring to a person's overall situation, affairs, or circumstances. In Philippians 2:20, the word forms the phrase translated "your state," highlighting Timothy's unselfish, genuine commitment to the practical and spiritual condition of the believers in Philippi.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The preposition peri originally conveyed a spatial sense of "around" or "surrounding." Over time, its common usage expanded metaphorically to mean "concerning," "about," or "with reference to." In this sense, it focuses thought or attention directly upon a particular subject, encircling that topic with mental interest.

In ancient Greek literature and biblical writings, when peri is used with an article and a personal pronoun, it functions as an idiom describing a person's entire environment or situation. Rather than pointing to a single isolated action, it brings into view everything that touches that person's life—their health, spiritual trials, material needs, and personal welfare.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The preposition peri points to the subject, target, or person toward which thought, speech, or concern is directed. Depending on context, it functions in several key ways:

  • "Concerning" or "about": Marking the central topic of a conversation, prayer, teaching, or inquiry.

  • "Around" or "encircling": Describing physical position surrounding an object or group.

  • "With regard to" or "on account of": Expressing the reason, target, or purpose of an action.

In Philippians 2:20, peri appears in the idiomatic expression rendered "your state" in the NKJV. Here the preposition identifies the Philippian believers as the explicit object of Timothy's deep personal interest and active pastoral care.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across biblical literature, peri serves as a primary marker for defining the exact subject under consideration. It frequently introduces the central topic of teaching, apostolic instruction, or communal prayer.

When paired with personal pronouns (such as "you" or "them"), the preposition shifts abstract discussion into concrete relational reality. It indicates that a speaker's care or attention is not general or theoretical, but specifically tailored to the real-life circumstances of particular people. In pastoral contexts, it highlights directed, intentional focus on the practical life and faith of the church.

4. Theological Implications

While peri is a standard grammatical preposition rather than a distinct theological term, its use in Philippians 2:20 serves an important role in expressing Christ-like ministry.

Earlier in the chapter, Paul instructs believers to look out not only for their own interests, but also for the interests of others. Timothy exemplifies this mind of Christ. The preposition peri specifies where Timothy's attention rests: entirely upon the condition and needs of others. True Christian care is not self-absorbed; it turns outward, directing sincere concern toward the spiritual and material circumstances of brothers and sisters in Christ.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:19–24, Paul informs the church at Philippi that he hopes to send Timothy to them soon. Paul is imprisoned and eager for news about how the church is faring, seeking to be encouraged by learning about their condition.

Paul commends Timothy as uniquely qualified for this mission, noting that he has no one else who is "like-minded" or equally devoted. While others seek their own interests, Timothy stands apart because he will "sincerely care for your state."

The preposition peri provides the target for Timothy's pastoral care. The verb for "care" (merimnaō) can sometimes mean anxious worry, but here, modified by "sincerely" and linked via peri to the believers, it describes legitimate, godly, outward-focused concern. Timothy does not view the Philippians as a burden or a stepping stone for his own ministry ambitions; his heart is genuinely occupied with "the things concerning" them.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 24:19 — "concerning" — Two disciples on the road to Emmaus describe the events "concerning" Jesus of Nazareth, showing how the preposition identifies the central subject of their conversation.

  • 1 Thessalonians 3:2 — "concerning" — Paul sends Timothy to strengthen and encourage believers "concerning" their faith, highlighting pastoral action targeted at spiritual growth.

  • 1 Peter 3:18 — "for" — Scripture states that Christ suffered once "for" sins, using the preposition to show the reason and objective of His sacrificial death.

  • Acts 10:19 — "about" — Peter meditates "about" the heavenly vision, demonstrating how the word marks the focus of mental reflection.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The description of Timothy in Philippians 2:20 creates a stark contrast with verse 21, where Paul laments that "all seek their own, not the things which are of Jesus Christ."

By using peri to connect Timothy's sincere care directly to the Philippians' state, Paul shows that seeking "the things which are of Jesus Christ" is inseparable from caring about "the things concerning" Christ's people. Genuine devotion to Christ expresses itself through unselfish concern for the concrete condition of the church.
