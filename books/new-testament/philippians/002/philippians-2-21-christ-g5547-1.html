CHRIST - (Christós, Strong's G5547)

Summary:

The word Christos means "Anointed One" and translates the Old Testament concept of the Messiah, the divine King and Savior chosen by God. In Philippians 2:21, the word appears in the masculine, singular, genitive form ("of Jesus Christ") to express ownership, highlighting the concerns, mission, and people that belong to Jesus in contrast to self-centered human interests.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient Greek world, the verb behind Christos was used for smearing or anointing an object or person with oil or lotion. When the Hebrew Old Testament was translated into Greek, Jewish scholars chose Christos to translate the Hebrew word Mashiach (Messiah). In Israelite history, kings, high priests, and sometimes prophets were anointed with oil as a physical sign that God had appointed and set them apart for holy service. Over centuries of prophetic promise, the title came to designate the ultimate promised King from the line of David who would bring God's salvation and establish His kingdom.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word means "anointed" or "the Anointed One." In the New Testament, it refers specifically to Jesus of Nazareth as God's promised Redeemer. The term functions in two primary ways across the New Testament writings:

  • As a formal title ("the Christ"), emphasizing His identity as the long-awaited Messianic King.

  • As a personal name ("Jesus Christ" or "Christ"), identifying the Lord in His ongoing ministry and divine authority.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In early apostolic preaching, demonstrating that Jesus was "the Christ" formed the core of the Christian message. As the gospel spread throughout the Roman world, the title quickly joined with His personal name, becoming "Jesus Christ" or simply "Christ."

When the word appears in the genitive case, as it does in Philippians 2:21, it marks a relationship of belonging, ownership, or source. Expressions such as "of Christ" identify the things, people, and work that pertain directly to Jesus and His kingdom priorities.

4. Theological Implications

The title Christos grounds Jesus in the fulfillment of God's redemptive promises. Jesus is not merely a moral teacher or wise leader; He is God's anointed King who reigns over all creation and redeems His people through His death and resurrection.

Because Jesus is the Christ, allegiance to Him supersedes all competing earthly loyalties. Believers belong to Him, and their lives are properly oriented around His glory and purpose rather than individual ambition.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2, Paul writes about his hope to send Timothy to the church at Philippi. Paul praises Timothy because he possesses a genuine concern for the believers' spiritual welfare.

In verse 21, Paul sets Timothy's character against a tragic contrast: "For all seek their own, not the things which are of Jesus Christ." Grammatically, the target word is a masculine singular noun in the genitive case. This genitive structure expresses ownership and allegiance—"the things belonging to Jesus Christ."

In this setting, "the things of Jesus Christ" refers to the work of the gospel, the spiritual health of the church, and the glory of the Lord. Paul contrasts two driving motivations:

  • Seeking one's own ease, recognition, and safety.

  • Pursuing the interests, mission, and people of Jesus Christ.

Timothy stands out precisely because his heart is focused on what belongs to Christ rather than his own personal agenda.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 11:27 — "She said to Him, 'Yes, Lord, I believe that You are the Christ, the Son of God, who is to come into the world.'"

  • Acts 17:3 — "...explaining and demonstrating that the Christ had to suffer and rise again from the dead, and saying, 'This Jesus whom I preach to you is the Christ.'"

  • Romans 14:18 — "For he who in these things serves Christ is acceptable to God and approved by men."

  • Philippians 3:8 — "Yet indeed I also count all things loss for the excellence of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord, for whom I have suffered the loss of all things, and count them as rubbish, that I may gain Christ..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast in Philippians 2:21 gains even greater force when read alongside the earlier description of Christ in Philippians 2:5-11. There, Paul describes how Jesus emptied Himself, taking the form of a bondservant for the sake of others. Timothy's willingness to set aside self-interest to serve "the things of Jesus Christ" reflects the very mindset of the Savior He serves.
