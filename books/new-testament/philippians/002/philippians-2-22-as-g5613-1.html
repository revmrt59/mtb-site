AS - (hōs, Strong's G5613)

Summary:

The word hos is a conjunction used throughout the New Testament to connect statements, introduce comparisons, describe manner or capacity, and signal time or degree. Translated as "as," "like," "when," "about," or "how," it helps readers understand how one thing relates to another. In Philippians 2:22, the word introduces a comparison that describes the nature of Timothy’s service alongside Paul, showing that Timothy worked with the apostle in the gospel with the devotion, unity, and deep affection of a son alongside his father.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Paul wrote his letter to the Philippians from prison, encouraging the church toward unity, humility, and joy in Christ. In chapter 2, after presenting Jesus as the ultimate model of humble self-giving, Paul highlights two living examples of Christlike service: Timothy and Epaphroditus.

Paul praises Timothy for possessing a genuine concern for the believers, contrasting him with others who seek their own interests rather than the things of Jesus Christ. To illustrate Timothy's rare character and loyalty, Paul points to their shared history in ministry. The word hos serves as a comparative connector in this verse, helping Paul paint a vivid picture of filial devotion and ministry partnership.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word hos is a flexible conjunction. Depending on the sentence structure and context, it can indicate:

  • Comparison or analogy ("as," "like")

  • Time or sequence ("when," "as soon as")

  • Degree or approximate quantity ("about")

  • Manner or condition ("how," "as")

In Philippians 2:22, the word introduces a simple comparative phrase. It does not carry complex theological meaning in itself; rather, it sets up an analogy between human family relationships and gospel ministry. By using this word, Paul invites his readers to understand Timothy’s loyalty through the familiar lens of a devoted son laboring with his father.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, writers use hos in a variety of grammatical roles:

  • Comparison: Drawing parallels between two subjects or actions to highlight similarity (for example, living "as wise" rather than "as fools").

  • Manner or Quality: Explaining the state, role, or attitude in which an action is performed (for example, remembering prisoners "as if" bound with them).

  • Temporal Marker: Indicating when an action takes place (often translated "when" or "as").

  • Approximation: Giving estimated amounts or counts (often translated "about").

In Philippians 2:22, the word functions clearly in the comparative and qualitative sense. It explains the character and quality of Timothy's partnership with Paul.

4. Theological Implications

While the word hos itself is a basic grammatical connector, its placement in Philippians 2:22 serves an important spiritual truth about ministry and discipleship. Gospel work in the early church was not viewed as a mere professional arrangement or institutional assignment. Instead, it operated through deep, family-like relationships formed by shared faith in Christ.

By describing Timothy as serving "as a son with his father," Paul grounds Christian mentorship in love, honor, and shared labor. Spiritual fatherhood involves training, guiding, and loving younger believers, while spiritual sonship involves willingness to learn, honor leadership, and work side-by-side in the advancing of the gospel.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:22, Paul writes, "But you know his proven character, that as a son with his father he served with me in the gospel." The context provides a striking contrast with verse 21, where Paul laments that many others seek their own interests rather than those of Jesus Christ. Timothy, however, has "proven character"—a tested integrity refined through hardship.

Paul uses hos ("as") to frame Timothy’s service. Yet notice the deliberate phrasing that follows: Paul does not say, "he served me as a master," but rather "he served with me in the gospel." Even while affirming the father-son nature of their relationship through the word "as," Paul maintains that both he and Timothy are fellow servants under the Lordship of Jesus Christ. The conjunction allows Paul to highlight Timothy’s filial devotion without reducing Timothy to a mere subordinate; they are co-workers in a single family mission.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Understanding comparative words like hos helps readers pay attention to the rich imagery biblical writers use. Paul frequently draws on household and family language—fathers, children, brothers, and mothers—to describe spiritual dynamics within the church.

When Paul calls Timothy his child or son in the faith elsewhere, it is not an empty title. Using hos in Philippians 2:22 demonstrates that spiritual kinship was visible in real life through joint sacrifice, mutual respect, and dedicated teamwork for the kingdom of God.
