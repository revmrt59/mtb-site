GOSPEL - (euangélion, Strong's G2098)

Summary:

The Greek word euangelion fundamentally means "good news" or a "joyful message." In secular antiquity, it referred to tidings of military victory or royal declarations, but in the New Testament, it refers specifically to the good news of salvation brought by God through Jesus Christ. In Philippians 2:22, euangelion identifies the active mission and ministry of proclaiming Christ. Paul uses it here to describe the shared labor between himself and Timothy, showing that Timothy's proven character was demonstrated through faithful, son-like partnership in serving the message of Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the broader ancient Greek world, euangelion designated a good report, a reward given for bringing good news, or an official announcement of a major event, such as a military triumph or the birthday of a ruler. When early Christians adopted the term, they filled it with rich biblical meaning derived from the Old Testament announcements of God's coming reign and deliverance.

In the New Testament, the word became the central term for the message of salvation in Jesus Christ—His life, death, resurrection, and exaltation as Lord. When Paul writes to the church at Philippi, he is writing from imprisonment. The Philippian believers knew Timothy well, and Paul highlights him as a living example of humility, loyalty, and Christlike service.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun euangelion combines two basic Greek roots: one meaning "good" or "well" and another meaning "announcer" or "messenger." Thus, the word refers to good news, glad tidings, or a joyful announcement.

In Christian usage, the term refers to the objective truth of God's redeeming work in Jesus Christ. It encompasses both the historical facts of Christ's cross and resurrection and the offer of forgiveness and eternal life to all who believe. In contexts such as Philippians 2:22, the word extends beyond the bare facts of the message to include the active enterprise, ministry, and work of spreading that news to the world.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, euangelion functions in several related ways:

  • As the message itself: the core truth concerning Jesus Christ and the kingdom of God.

  • As the act of preaching: the public proclamation of this saving truth.

  • As the sphere of ministry: the ongoing work and cause in which believers labor together.

In Philippians 2:22, the noun appears in the accusative singular neuter form as the object of a preposition ("in the gospel"). This construction describes the shared domain of ministry in which Paul and Timothy labored. The emphasis falls on their collaborative work for the cause of Christ rather than on a theoretical discussion of doctrine.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of euangelion carries essential theological truths:

  • The gospel originates with God. It is not human philosophy, moral advice, or religious speculation, but God's authoritative announcement of what He has accomplished through His Son.

  • The gospel produces Christian partnership. The message of grace creates a community of co-laborers who are bound together in a common cause.

  • Christian character is proven in service to the gospel. Spiritual maturity is not measured merely by internal feeling or biblical knowledge, but by self-giving service for the advance of Christ's kingdom.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2, Paul provides a profound exposition of Christ's humble, self-emptying service (Philippians 2:5–11) and then offers practical examples of believers who reflect that same mindset. Timothy is the first example Paul presents.

Paul contrasts Timothy with others who seek their own interests rather than the things of Jesus Christ. He reminds the Philippians of Timothy's "proven character." To illustrate this character, Paul uses deep family imagery: "as a son with his father he served with me in the gospel."

Timothy did not view himself as a hireling or a independent worker, but as a devoted son working alongside his spiritual father. The word "gospel" here defines the supreme purpose that united Paul and Timothy. It was for the sake of Christ's message that Timothy shared in Paul's hardship, travels, and imprisonment, demonstrating that true commitment to the gospel leads to humble, sacrificial partnership.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Mark 1:15 — "saying, 'The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand. Repent, and believe in the gospel.'"

  • Romans 1:9 — "For God is my witness, whom I serve with my spirit in the gospel of His Son, that without ceasing I make mention of you always in my prayers..."

  • 1 Corinthians 4:15 — "For though you might have ten thousand instructors in Christ, yet you do not have many fathers; for in Christ Jesus I have begotten you through the gospel."

  • Philippians 1:12 — "But I want you to know, brethren, that the things which happened to me have actually turned out for the furtherance of the gospel..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Mentorship and the Gospel: Paul's statement about Timothy shows that gospel ministry is naturally intergenerational. The advancement of the message relies on seasoned servants passing on the work to faithful younger companions who serve with the same heart and commitment.

  • Practical Service: Timothy's service "in the gospel" involved administrative tasks, personal visitation, and facing hardship alongside Paul. Proclaiming good news requires practical, everyday faithfulness in the details of ministry.
