KNOW - (ginṓskō, Strong's G1097)

Summary:

The word ginōskō means to know, perceive, recognize, or come to understand something, often through personal experience, observation, or interaction. Rather than referring merely to abstract intellectual information, it frequently describes awareness gained through firsthand acquaintance and ongoing experience. In Philippians 2:22, Paul uses the word to appeal to the Philippians' personal, firsthand knowledge of Timothy's tested character, reminding them that they have directly witnessed his faithful service in the work of the gospel.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

When Paul wrote his letter to the Philippians from prison, he wanted to send them encouragement and updates regarding his situation. He planned to send Timothy, his close associate in ministry, to care for their spiritual needs. To assure the church of Timothy's fitness for this mission, Paul points to Timothy's character.

Rather than presenting a list of formal credentials or external recommendations, Paul appeals to what the Philippian believers had already observed themselves. Years earlier, Timothy had traveled with Paul during the initial planting of the church in Philippi. The believers there had seen Timothy's selflessness, humility, and devotion up close. Paul relies on this shared memory, reminding them that they already possess personal knowledge of Timothy's proven worth.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

lowered to everyday understanding, the word means to know, perceive, learn, or recognize. It can refer to:

  • Realizing or becoming aware of a fact.

  • Understanding a truth through instruction or reflection.

  • Knowing someone personally through interaction and experience.

  • Recognizing the true nature or quality of a person or thing.

In Philippians 2:22, the word means personal recognition based on past observation. Paul is not telling the Philippians new information about Timothy; he is pointing them to what they already know to be true through their own past interactions with him.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, ginōskō ranges from basic awareness of events to deep spiritual understanding and personal fellowship. It often carries an experiential nuance—knowledge gained by living through something, watching an event unfold, or walking alongside a person.

In Philippians 2:22, the verb is in the present active indicative form and is plural. This grammatical structure shows that Paul addresses the whole congregation in the present moment, asking them to call to mind their collective, shared understanding. Paul does not need to convince them of Timothy's loyalty; he simply invites the entire church to draw upon what they continuously know to be true about his character.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical view of spiritual discernment and church leadership relies heavily on tested character rather than impressive claims. Anyone can make lofty promises, but genuine Christian character is demonstrated over time through real-life service and trial.

By appealing to what the church knows about Timothy, Paul highlights an important spiritual principle: godly credibility is earned through faithful service and recognized by the community of faith. True knowledge within the body of Christ is rooted in observed reality and shared truth. Just as believers come to know God through His revealed word and faithful actions, believers recognize genuine spiritual leaders by watching their persistent faithfulness in daily life.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:19–24, Paul sets up a sharp contrast between Timothy and others who might seek their own interests. Paul notes that he has no one else like-minded who will sincerely care for the Philippians' state, because many seek their own things rather than the things of Jesus Christ.

Against that backdrop of self-seeking behavior, Paul brings up Timothy. Verse 22 functions as the evidence supporting Paul's choice: "But you know his proven character, that as a son with his father he served with me in the gospel." The word know grounds Paul's recommendation in historical reality. The Philippians had seen Timothy serve alongside Paul like a devoted son working with his father in a family trade. Because they know his past behavior, they can trust his present sending and welcome him with joy.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 6:69 — "Also we have believed and know that You are the Christ, the Son of the living God."

  • John 14:20 — "At that day you will know that I am in My Father, and you in Me, and I in you."

  • 1 Corinthians 13:12 — "For now we see in a mirror, dimly, but then face to face. Now I know in part, but then I shall know just as I also am known."

  • Colossians 4:8 — "I am sending him to you for this very purpose, that he may know your circumstances and comfort your hearts."

  • 1 John 2:29 — "If you know that He is righteous, you know that everyone who practices righteousness is born of Him."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Philippians 2:22, Paul pairs the verb know with the term "proven character" (Greek dokimē). The word dokimē refers to metal that has passed through the fire and proven to be genuine. By placing these concepts together, Paul shows that the Philippians' knowledge of Timothy was not based on rumors or public image, but on seeing him pass through tests of ministry without breaking. Their knowledge was established through proven experience.
