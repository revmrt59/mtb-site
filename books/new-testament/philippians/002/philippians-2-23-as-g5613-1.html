AS - (hōs, Strong's G5613)

Summary:

The Greek conjunction hōs is a versatile connecting word used throughout the New Testament to express comparison ("as," "like"), time ("when," "as soon as"), degree ("about"), or manner ("how"). In Philippians 2:23, the word functions temporally in the phrase translated "as soon as," marking the specific moment Paul intends to dispatch Timothy to Philippi—namely, the instant the outcome of Paul's legal situation in Rome becomes clear.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek, conjunctions like hōs serve as essential sentence connectors. They clarify how one thought relates to another in time, manner, comparison, or degree. Rather than carrying heavy independent theological weight on its own, hōs provides the structural frame that links actions, descriptions, and events.

When writing to the church at Philippi, Paul uses this conjunction to connect his immediate pastoral desires with his surrounding physical circumstances. Bound in Roman custody, Paul relies on clear structural words to communicate his practical ministry plans to a congregation deeply concerned for his well-being.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to several logical connections depending on how a sentence is built:

  • Time: It often indicates when an action occurs, translated as "when," "while," or "as soon as."

  • Comparison: It frequently introduces a comparison or likeness, translated as "as," "like," or "as it were."

  • Manner or Degree: It can describe the way something happens ("how") or give an approximate amount ("about").

In Philippians 2:23, the word functions grammatically as a conjunction introducing time. Combined with a particle indicating conditional outcome, it means "as soon as" or "the moment that."

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

New Testament writers draw upon hōs for a wide range of explanatory purposes:

  • Comparative uses: Writers use the word to draw vivid pictures, such as describing Jesus' glory as the unique Son of God or comparing spiritual growth to the mustard seed.

  • Temporal uses: In historical narratives and personal epistles, the word marks timing, indicating when an event took place or when a leader planned to act.

  • Approximations: When listing groups of people or durations of time, the word can signify an estimate rather than a rigid total.

The flexibility of hōs shows how classical and biblical Greek used light, connecting elements to guide readers through complex narratives and personal correspondence.

4. Theological Implications

Because hōs is a functional conjunction, its theological value comes from the statement it connects rather than from the dictionary definition of the conjunction itself. In Philippians 2:23, the word highlights the balance between divine trust and practical planning in Christian leadership:

  • Trust without presumption: Paul expressed deep faith in God's ultimate care, yet he did not make blind promises. He waited for real information before sending his closest associate.

  • Honest pastoral communication: Paul wanted the Philippian believers to have accurate, timely updates regarding his trial so they would not be weighed down by constant rumor or unnecessary worry.

  • Purposeful partnership: Timothy’s mission was tied directly to Paul’s situation. By coordinating Timothy’s departure with the resolution of his trial, Paul ensured the church received maximum encouragement and clarity.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Philippians 2:19–24 forms a practical section where Paul outlines his hope to send Timothy to Philippi. Paul praises Timothy as a minister of proven character who served alongside him like a devoted son.

In verse 23, Paul writes, "Therefore I hope to send him at once, as soon as I see how it goes with me." Here, hōs introduces the phrase "as soon as." Paul wanted to send Timothy immediately, but wisdom dictated a short delay. He needed to learn the verdict of his Roman trial first. If Paul were released, Timothy could carry the joyful news; if Paul were condemned, Timothy could carry Paul's final instructions. Using hōs allowed Paul to establish a clear timeline for the Philippians, assuring them that Timothy’s arrival was imminent, pending the official outcome of his legal status.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 1:14 – Here the word is rendered "as" in describing Christ's glory "as of the only begotten of the Father," expressing comparison and unique divine nature.

  • John 19:33 – Rendered "when" to mark the precise time Roman soldiers arrived at Jesus on the cross and found He was already dead.

  • Mark 8:9 – Rendered "about" to give an approximate total of the crowd Jesus fed in the wilderness.

  • Hebrews 13:3 – Rendered "as" to instruct Christians to care for imprisoned believers as if bound in chains right alongside them.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Grammatically, hōs functions in Philippians 2:23 strictly as a conjunction. It shows how Paul structured his apostolic ministry around clear logical relationships.

The passage demonstrates that spiritual leaders can hold absolute confidence in God's providence while remaining practical and deliberate in their ministry logistics. Paul did not treat administrative details as unspiritual; instead, he used precise temporal language to keep his beloved church informed and cared for.
