HOPE - (elpízō, Strong's G1679)

Summary:

The verb elpizō means to expect, to look forward to with confidence, or to place trust in someone or something. In biblical usage, it describes both spiritual hope anchored in God's promises and practical expectations for future plans under divine providence. In Philippians 2:23, Paul uses this word to express his present, active intention and confident expectation to send Timothy to the Philippian church as soon as the outcome of his legal trial becomes clear.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek literature, elpizō was used broadly to describe anticipating or looking forward to a future event. Depending on the setting, it could describe positive anticipation or simple expectation regarding the unknown future.

In the New Testament, elpizō carries a strong sense of confidence and trust rather than uncertain wishful thinking. Whether expressing ultimate spiritual confidence in God or describing practical plans for gospel ministry, the word reflects an expectation held in light of God's sovereign care.

When Paul wrote his letter to the Philippians, he was under house arrest, awaiting a formal decision on his legal case. In Philippians 2, Paul expresses his deep pastoral care for the church by explaining his plans for his close companions, Timothy and Epaphroditus. Within this setting, elpizō introduces Paul's practical expectation regarding Timothy's upcoming mission.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb elpizō can mean to look forward to something with expectation, to hope for, or to place trust in someone.

When God or His promise is the object of the verb, elpizō means to place active trust in God's faithfulness. When human plans or travel intentions are in view, the word describes a confident plan or expectation held in submission to God's sovereign will.

Depending on the setting, English Bible translations render elpizō as "hope," "trust," or "look for." In Philippians 2:23, the word means to expect or intend to carry out a practical course of action once present obstacles are cleared.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, elpizō spans a range of practical and spiritual expectations:

  • Spiritual Confidence: It describes unwavering hope in God's salvation, resurrection power, and eternal promises.

  • Personal Trust: It refers to relying upon God's character and ongoing providence in daily life.

  • Practical Expectations: It expresses concrete plans for Christian fellowship, ministry visits, and travel.

In Paul's letters, spiritual hope and everyday planning are closely related. Paul does not treat practical travel plans as detached from his faith. Instead, his expectations are formed in trust and subject to the Lord's guidance and timing.

4. Theological Implications

Paul's use of elpizō for his travel plans reveals an essential balance in Christian stewardship. Believers are called to plan expectantly while leaving the execution and timing of those plans in God's hands.

His expectation is not an aggressive demand or self-confident boast about the future. It is a trust-filled plan formulated by a servant of Christ who recognizes that human circumstances remain under divine authority. Hope in Scripture connects high spiritual truth with daily realities, enabling believers to make real ministry plans while remaining fully surrendered to God's providence.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:19–24, Paul explains his desire to send Timothy to Philippi so that he might receive news of their welfare and offer them encouragement. In verse 19, Paul states that he trusts in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy shortly. Then in verse 23, he repeats this expectation: "Therefore I hope to send him at once, as soon as I see how it goes with me."

The grammatical form of elpizō in verse 23 is a present active indicative verb in the first person singular. This shows that Paul is presently expressing his active, personal expectation.

Paul connects his hope to a specific condition: "as soon as I see how it goes with me." He is waiting to learn the legal verdict of his imprisonment. His expectation to send Timothy is immediate ("at once") upon hearing the news, demonstrating both eager pastoral care for the Philippians and patient submission to his present confinement.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 24:21 — "But we were hoping that it was He who was going to redeem Israel."

  • Romans 8:25 — "But if we hope for what we do not see, we eagerly wait for it with perseverance."

  • Romans 15:24 — "whenever I journey to Spain, I shall come to you. For I hope to see you on my journey..."

  • 2 Corinthians 1:10 — "...who delivered us from so great a death, and does deliver us; in whom we trust that He will still deliver us..."

  • Philippians 2:19 — "But I trust in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy to you shortly..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Philippians 2, the NKJV translates the same Greek verb elpizō as "trust" in verse 19 ("I trust in the Lord Jesus") and as "hope" in verse 23 ("I hope to send him"). This variation highlights how expectation and trust belong together in Biblical thought.

Paul's hope to send Timothy reflects a selfless pastoral heart. Even while facing trial and imprisonment, Paul's active expectations focus on the spiritual health and comfort of the Philippian believers rather than his own ease.
