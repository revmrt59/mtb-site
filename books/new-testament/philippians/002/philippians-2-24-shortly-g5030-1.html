SHORTLY - (tachéōs, Strong's G5030)

Summary:

The word tacheōs is an adverb that means "quickly," "soon," or "shortly." It can describe either the speed with which an action takes place or a time frame in the near future. In Philippians 2:24, Paul uses the word to express his confident hope of visiting the Philippian believers before long, while grounding that personal expectation in his total submission to the Lord's divine guidance.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word tacheōs is derived from a root meaning quick, swift, or rapid. In ancient communication, writers used this term when expressing promptness in carrying out a task, describing an event that would happen without long delay, or warning against hasty, ill-advised decisions.

When Paul wrote his letter to the Philippians, he was under Roman imprisonment. Despite his confinement and uncertain legal circumstances, his heart remained deeply attached to the churches he had planted. In Philippians 2, Paul outlines his ministry plans, explaining how he intends to stay connected with the believers at Philippi through faithful co-workers and, if God permits, a personal visit.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Grammatically, tacheōs functions as an adverb. It modifies verbal actions by specifying either speed or timing. The word generally carries three distinct nuances depending on the context:

  • Time frame: Meaning "soon," "shortly," or "before long," indicating that an anticipated action is expected in the near future.

  • Speed of execution: Meaning "quickly," "swiftly," or "without delay," describing prompt action.

  • Manner or haste: Meaning "hastily," "rashly," or "prematurely," describing an action done too quickly without proper care or thought.

In Philippians 2:24, the word refers to timing. Paul uses it to express his expectation of arriving in Philippi within a relatively short period of time.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across its biblical occurrences, tacheōs shows how urgency and timing interact in various ministry settings:

  • Expectation of Near Arrival: Paul frequently uses the word when outlining his travel plans to churches or requesting colleagues to join him without delay. In these settings, it conveys warm pastoral eagerness and anticipation.

  • Prompt Obedience or Action: The word describes swift, decisive response when a command or invitation is given, emphasizing an action done without hesitation.

  • Unwise Haste or Rashness: In warning passages, the word describes an unhealthy speed, such as turning away too quickly from sound teaching or hastily appointing leaders without proper evaluation.

4. Theological Implications

Paul's use of tacheōs in Philippians 2:24 highlights the biblical balance between human planning and divine providence. Paul expresses a genuine desire and expectation to visit Philippi "shortly," yet he explicitly frames this goal within his belief in Christ's authority: "I trust in the Lord that I myself shall also come shortly."

This reveals that while apostolic planning was active and purposeful, it remained entirely subordinate to the sovereign will of God. Paul's expectation of coming soon was not an absolute prediction or a guarantee he generated on his own authority. Rather, it was a practical hope submitted to the Lord who controls all circumstances, incarcerations, and travel paths.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Philippians 2:19–30 presents a crucial transition in Paul's letter where he discusses three ministers: Timothy, Epaphroditus, and Paul himself.

Paul first notes his desire to send Timothy "shortly" (v. 19) so that he might receive news of their condition. He then explains that he is sending Epaphroditus immediately (v. 25), since Epaphroditus had been sick and was eager to return home. Finally, in verse 24, Paul looks beyond these two messengers to express his own hope: "But I trust in the Lord that I myself shall also come shortly."

The contrast between sending Epaphroditus now, Timothy soon, and Paul hoping to follow shortly demonstrates a orderly ministry plan. The word "shortly" in verse 24 shows that Paul maintained vibrant optimism regarding his release from prison, yet he refrained from overstating his certainty. His sense of timing was hopeful, but his confidence remained anchored in the Lord Jesus.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Philippians 2:19 – "But I trust in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy to you shortly, that I also may be encouraged when I know your state."

  • 1 Corinthians 4:19 – "But I will come to you shortly, if the Lord wills, and I will know, not the word of those who are puffed up, but the power."

  • Galatians 1:6 – "I marvel that you are turning away so soon from Him who called you in the grace of Christ, to a different gospel."

  • 1 Timothy 5:22 – "Do not lay hands on anyone hastily, nor share in other people’s sins; keep yourself pure."

  • 2 Timothy 4:9 – "Be diligent to come to me quickly."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A comparison of how tacheōs is used across Paul's letters illustrates the difference between godly promptness and dangerous haste. When applied to fellowship, service, and ministry travel, tacheōs expresses a healthy, loving eagerness to be together and carry out God's work. However, when applied to spiritual instability or leadership decisions, the same word serves as a warning against acting rashly or prematurely without spiritual discernment.
