CONSIDERED - (hēgéomai, Strong's G2233)

Summary:

The word hegeomai means to think, evaluate, judge, or hold an opinion after careful consideration. It can also describe leading, ruling, or guiding others. In Philippians 2:25, the apostle Paul uses the word to describe his deliberate decision to send Epaphroditus back to the church at Philippi. Paul weighed the circumstances, recognized the deep mutual concern between Epaphroditus and the congregation, and came to the settled conclusion that sending him home was necessary and wise.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The verb hegeomai carries two main senses in biblical usage. In a leadership context, it describes leading, governing, or guiding people. In a mental context, it describes a process of thought—evaluating facts, weighing evidence, and arriving at a settled judgment or perspective. The term does not mean making a quick guess or expressing a vague feeling; it refers to an intentional act of judgment.

When Paul writes to the believers at Philippi, he is writing from prison. The Philippian church had sent Epaphroditus to deliver a financial gift to Paul and to care for his needs. While serving Paul, Epaphroditus became dangerously ill, nearly dying. News of his illness reached Philippi, causing distress to both Epaphroditus and his home church. Upon Epaphroditus's recovery, Paul carefully evaluated the situation and concluded that sending Epaphroditus back to Philippi was the best course of action.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to holding a deliberate conviction, regarding something in a particular light, or deeming a decision necessary. When a person is the subject of the verb in a mental sense, it means:

  • To evaluate or consider something carefully.

  • To deem or judge an action to be appropriate, necessary, or valuable.

  • To regard a person or thing with a specific estimate or value.

When used in a governing sense, it means:

  • To lead, guide, or direct.

  • To hold a position of leadership or authority.

In Philippians 2:25, Paul uses the verb in the first-person singular, describing a personal decision he reached through careful thought. The grammatical form presents his decision as a completed judgment: he weighed the circumstances and concluded that sending Epaphroditus back was necessary.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, hegeomai demonstrates how Christian thought differs from earthly calculation.

When describing leadership, the word emphasizes guidance and spiritual direction rather than self-serving dominance. Leaders are called to lead by serving and setting a godly example.

When describing mental evaluation, the word highlights how believers are called to assess reality through the lens of faith and gospel truth. Rather than reacting based on immediate emotion or worldly value systems, a Christian uses spiritual discernment to determine what is truly important. This involves looking beyond surface appearances to weigh matters according to God's standards and the good of others.

4. Theological Implications

Paul’s use of this word reveals how Christian leadership and pastoral care operate.

First, pastoral decision-making is thoughtful and practical. Paul was inspired by the Holy Spirit, yet his apostolic leadership involved careful practical evaluation of human needs, emotional states, and ministerial practicalities. He assessed Epaphroditus's health and the emotional burden felt by the Philippian congregation, leading him to a wise pastoral conclusion.

Second, the passage highlights the mindset of selflessness. Throughout Philippians, Paul encourages believers to share the mind of Christ, putting the interests of others ahead of their own. Although Paul benefited immensely from Epaphroditus’s companionship and service in prison, Paul evaluated the situation not by his own immediate comfort, but by what would bring relief and joy to the Philippian church. His thoughtful decision reflected the very mindset of Christ he urged the Philippians to emulate.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2, Paul provides concrete examples of the humble, selfless mindset he described earlier in the chapter. After pointing to Jesus Christ as the supreme example of humble service, Paul holds up Timothy and Epaphroditus as living illustrations of Christlike concern for others.

In verse 25, Paul introduces his plan to send Epaphroditus back. He honors Epaphroditus with rich titles: "brother, fellow worker, and fellow soldier," as well as Philippi's "messenger and the one who ministered to my need."

By stating that he "considered" it necessary to send him, Paul makes clear that Epaphroditus was not returning because he had failed in his mission or deserted Paul. Rather, Paul himself evaluated the entire situation, recognized Epaphroditus’s deep longing for his home church, and decided to send him back to bring joy to the Philippians and relief to Epaphroditus. Paul's intentional choice turned what might have looked like a premature return into an act of sacrificial pastoral care.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The word appears multiple times in Philippians, forming an important theme about how believers ought to think and evaluate reality.

  • In Philippians 2:3, believers are commanded to esteem others as more important than themselves.

  • In Philippians 2:6, Christ did not consider equality with God something to be grasped for personal advantage.

  • In Philippians 2:25, Paul considered it necessary to send Epaphroditus back for the sake of the church.

  • In Philippians 3:7-8, Paul counted his former accomplishments as loss for the excellence of knowing Christ.

This repetition shows that spiritual transformation changes how a person evaluates value, priority, and purpose. The Christian life requires a deliberate rethinking of what matters most in light of the gospel.
