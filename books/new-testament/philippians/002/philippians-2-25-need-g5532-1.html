NEED - (chreía, Strong's G5532)

Summary:

The word chreia refers to a state of practical requirement, lack, or pressing necessity. It identifies what is essential for life, health, duty, or well-being. In Philippians 2:25, the word refers to Paul's physical and material necessities while imprisoned in Rome. It highlights Paul's genuine vulnerability and honors Epaphroditus as the faithful messenger sent by the Philippian church to supply those tangible practical needs.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient Roman world, prisoners were rarely provided with adequate food, warm clothing, or medical care by the state. Detainees depended almost entirely on family, friends, or associates to bring them basic supplies. Without outside assistance, a prisoner faced extreme hardship or even starvation.

When Paul was held under guard, the church at Philippi recognized his vulnerable situation. They sent Epaphroditus on a long, hazardous journey to deliver financial relief and personal care. The word chreia sits right at the intersection of this historical reality. It highlights that even an apostle carried real, practical requirements that had to be met through the sacrificial love of fellow believers.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

centered on human dependence, the word can refer to a physical requirement, a practical deficit, or an urgent necessity. It describes the gap between what a person currently possesses and what is required to sustain life, accomplish a task, or carry out a mission.

When applied to physical circumstances, the word points to concrete items such as food, money, shelter, or bodily assistance. When applied to spiritual or relational contexts, it refers to an indispensable requirement—something that cannot be bypassed or ignored. Rather than implying disgraceful poverty, the term simply acknowledges the natural limits of human self-sufficiency.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, chreia appears in several distinct practical settings:

  • Physical Illness and Medical Care: Jesus uses the word to explain that healthy people do not require a doctor, but sick people do. Here the word identifies a clear physical vulnerability that demands a remedy.

  • Daily Material Necessities: The word frequently describes basic human needs like food, clothing, and financial support. In the early church community, believers shared their resources so that no one suffered from unmet daily requirements.

  • Essential Duties and Tasks: The word can indicate that a specific action or person is required for a divine purpose, such as when Jesus sent His disciples to secure a donkey because the Lord had need of it.

In Paul's letters, the word often carries a warm communal tone. It serves as a bridge between believers, showing how God uses one part of the church family to supply what is lacking in another.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of chreia reinforces several important truths about Christian life and ministry:

  • Human Vulnerability is Normal: God did not design human beings to be completely self-sufficient. Having physical, emotional, or material needs is part of being human and is not inherently a sign of weak faith or spiritual failure.

  • Ministry is Concrete and Practical: Christian love is expressed through tangible action. Scriptural care is not limited to spiritual encouragement; it actively addresses bodily and material shortages.

  • Divine Provision through Human Means: God frequently answers prayer and supplies daily necessities through the generosity of His people. The gift sent by the Philippians was both a human act of charity and a divine provision for Paul.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:25, Paul uses the feminine singular genitive form of chreia ("my need") to describe the purpose of Epaphroditus' mission. Paul piles up titles of high honor for Epaphroditus, calling him a brother, fellow worker, fellow soldier, messenger, and minister to Paul's necessity.

This description is striking because of Paul's apostolic authority. Paul does not hide his physical dependence or pretend to be beyond human limitation. He openly acknowledges that he had real material needs in prison, and he gives public credit to Epaphroditus for meeting them.

Furthermore, the context reveals that Epaphroditus became dangerously ill while carrying out this mission, coming near to death for the work of Christ. By using chreia here, Paul elevates practical caregiving to the level of sacrificial Christian service. Helping a brother in physical distress was not a minor errand; it was a holy, gospel-centered military assignment.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 9:12 – "When Jesus heard that, He said to them, 'Those who are well have no need of a physician, but those who are sick.'"

  • Acts 4:35 – "...and laid them at the apostles' feet; and they distributed to each as anyone had need."

  • Romans 12:13 – "...distributing to the needs of the saints, given to hospitality."

  • Philippians 4:19 – "And my God shall supply all your need according to His riches in glory by Christ Jesus."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A meaningful connection exists between Philippians 2:25 and Philippians 4:19. In chapter 2, Paul expresses gratitude that the Philippians sent Epaphroditus to care for Paul's immediate need. Later, in chapter 4, Paul reassures the Philippian church that because they sacrificially gave to meet his lack, God would abundantly supply all of their need according to His glorious riches in Christ.

This thematic pairing shows that giving and receiving within the Christian community form a continuous circle of divine faithfulness. The church looked after the apostle's practical necessity, and the apostle assured the church of God's complete care for their own ongoing requirements.
