GLADNESS - (chará, Strong's G5479)

Summary:

The word chara refers to gladness, joy, or deep spiritual delight. In Philippians 2:29, Paul commands the church in Philippi to receive Epaphroditus back into their fellowship "with all gladness," urging them to welcome their returned messenger with wholehearted Christian joy and honor after his sacrificial service.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek, chara expressed the state of gladness, delight, or happiness triggered by good news, a welcome arrival, or a favorable event. In the New Testament, this term describes the profound delight that springs from God's work of salvation, the presence of the Holy Spirit, and the warm fellowship of believers.

In Philippians 2, Paul writes to a congregation concerned about his imprisonment and troubled by the severe illness of Epaphroditus, the messenger sent from Philippi to care for Paul. After God mercifully healed Epaphroditus, Paul chose to send him back to Philippi. Paul instructs the congregation to receive him with complete chara—unreserved gladness—restoring their peace and affirming Epaphroditus for his faithful labor.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun chara means joy, gladness, or a state of delight. It can refer to the inner emotional response of happiness, the cause of such delight, or a shared experience of gladness among God's people.

In Scripture, chara is not merely a temporary mood dependent on circumstances; it is an abiding gladness rooted in God's goodness and faithful action. In Philippians 2:29, the word describes the full-hearted, joyful welcome the Philippian church should extend to Epaphroditus upon his safe return.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, chara appears in many settings. It describes the joy of the shepherds at the announcement of Jesus' birth, the gladness of disciples experiencing answered prayer, the fruit of the Holy Spirit in a believer's life, and the deep delight believers share in Christian fellowship.

In Philippians 2:29, the noun is feminine, singular, and in the genitive case. Functioning alongside the preposition translated "with," it describes how the Philippians are to receive Epaphroditus—namely, wrapped in complete and genuine gladness.

4. Theological Implications

The joy commanded in Philippians 2:29 reveals how Christian fellowship ought to function. When God preserves a servant who risked his life for the Gospel, the church's response should be rich with thankfulness and gladness.

Christian joy is grounded in God's mercy and expressed through personal care within the community. By calling the church to welcome Epaphroditus with gladness and honor, Paul shows that celebrating faithful Christian workers is an expression of spiritual joy that brings glory to God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In the second chapter of Philippians, Paul emphasizes self-sacrificing humility, pointing first to Jesus Christ, then to Timothy, and finally to Epaphroditus. Epaphroditus had been sent by the Philippians to minister to Paul's needs in prison, but he became so sick he nearly died. Hearing that his home church was anxious about him caused Epaphroditus distress.

Paul sends him back so that the church's anxiety will turn to relief. Paul commands them to receive Epaphroditus in the Lord "with all gladness." The phrase "all gladness" stresses that their welcome should be wholehearted, filled with praise for God's healing mercy, and free from any mistaken impression that Epaphroditus' mission was a failure.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Mark 4:16 — Hearers who receive the seed on stony ground immediately with "gladness" show how the good news can initially produce quick delight.

  • Luke 2:10 — The angel announces to the shepherds tidings of great "joy" that will be to all people because of the birth of the Savior.

  • Acts 12:14 — Rhoda was so overcome with "gladness" when she recognized Peter's voice at the gate that she ran inside without opening the door.

  • Romans 14:17 — Paul explains that the kingdom of God is not eating and drinking, but righteousness, peace, and "joy" in the Holy Spirit.

  • Galatians 5:22 — Paul lists "joy" as part of the spiritual fruit generated in the believer's life by the Holy Spirit.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Exhorting a church to receive a returning minister "with all gladness" guards against misplaced expectations. Epaphroditus had fallen ill and was unable to remain with Paul indefinitely, which might have led some to view his return as premature. By calling for "all gladness," Paul protects Epaphroditus' reputation and directs the congregation to focus on God's kindness in preserving his life.
