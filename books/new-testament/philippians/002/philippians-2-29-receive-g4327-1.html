RECEIVE - (prosdéchomai, Strong's G4327)

Summary:

The Greek verb prosdechomai means to receive favorably, welcome warmly, or wait expectantly for someone or something. Across the New Testament, it describes either accepting people with open hospitality or looking forward with patient hope to God's promised actions. In Philippians 2:29, the word functions as an urgent command for the Philippian church to give Epaphroditus a warm, joyful, and honoring reception upon his return from Rome after risking his life for the gospel.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Apostle Paul wrote his letter to the Philippians while imprisoned, likely in Rome. The church at Philippi had sent one of their own members, Epaphroditus, with financial aid and instructions to care for Paul's personal needs. While carrying out this mission, Epaphroditus fell dangerously ill and nearly died. Word of his illness reached the Philippians, which deeply concerned them, and Epaphroditus was distressed because he knew his home church was anxious on his behalf.

After God mercifully restored Epaphroditus to health, Paul decided to send him back to Philippi. Paul wanted to ease the congregation's worry and bring them joy. However, Paul was aware that some might view Epaphroditus' early return as a failure to complete his long-term mission in Rome. To prevent any misunderstanding, Paul writes to instruct the congregation on how to treat Epaphroditus when he arrives.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb prosdechomai is composed of a preposition meaning "toward" and a base verb meaning "to receive or accept." The word carries two main senses in biblical usage:

First, it means to receive someone or something favorably, welcoming a person into one's presence, home, or fellowship with approval and goodwill.

Second, it means to wait for or look forward to something expectantly, holding a hopeful attitude toward a promised future reality.

In Philippians 2:29, the word is used in the first sense. Paul commands the church to extend a warm, openhearted welcome to Epaphroditus, receiving him with Christian love and deep appreciation for his sacrificial labor.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

When prosdechomai describes interactions between people, it implies genuine acceptance and fellowship rather than cold or formal toleration. To receive a person with this word means to welcome them into full social or spiritual community. When used in connection with promises or future events, it describes a mindset of patient, trusting expectation.

Grammatically, in Philippians 2:29, the verb is in the present tense, middle voice, imperative mood, and plural number:

  • The imperative mood expresses a direct command from Paul to the church.

  • The present tense indicates that this receptive attitude should characterize their immediate and ongoing posture toward him.

  • The middle voice reflects the subjects carrying out the action in direct relationship to themselves as a community.

  • The plural form shows that the instruction is addressed to the entire congregation together, requiring a unified welcoming response.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical call to receive fellow believers reflects the unity of the church as the body of Christ. When Christians welcome faithful servants of the gospel, they honor Christ who called and sent them. Receiving ministers and workers in the Lord demonstrates that kingdom fellowship is built on mutual love, shared faith, and honor for those who labor for the gospel.

Furthermore, God's grace reshapes how believers evaluate one another. Earthly standards often measure people by status, success, or public prominence. In contrast, Scripture calls the church to recognize and cherish those who display Christlike self-sacrifice, even when their service takes place in humble or quiet ways.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:25-30, Paul provides a glowing recommendation for Epaphroditus, describing him as a brother, fellow worker, fellow soldier, messenger, and minister to his needs. Paul highlights that Epaphroditus was so committed to helping him in prison that he came close to death, risking his life to supply what was lacking in the Philippians' service to Paul.

When Paul says, "Receive him therefore in the Lord with all gladness," the word "therefore" ties the command directly to Epaphroditus' faithful service and divine deliverance. Paul instructs the church to greet him not with disappointment or suspicion, but with joy and honor. By using prosdechomai, Paul demands that the believers receive Epaphroditus as a beloved brother who proved his devotion through suffering.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 2:25 — "waiting"

Simeon is described as a righteous man "waiting for the Consolation of Israel," showing the sense of patient expectation for God's promised Messiah.

  • Luke 15:2 — "receives"

The religious leaders complained that Jesus "receives sinners and eats with them," illustrating how the word denotes welcoming people into open fellowship.

  • Acts 24:15 — "accept"

Paul asserts his hope in God regarding the resurrection, a hope that his accusers also "accept," referring to looking forward to a divine promise.

  • Romans 16:2 — "receive"

Paul commands the Roman believers to "receive" Phoebe in the Lord in a manner worthy of the saints, using the same verb for hospitable Christian reception.

  • Hebrews 10:34 — "accepted"

The writer commends believers who joyfully "accepted the plundering of your goods," demonstrating a willing acceptance of hardship for Christ's sake.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Philippians 2, Paul presents four examples of self-giving ministry: Jesus Christ (vv. 5-11), Paul (vv. 17-18), Timothy (vv. 19-24), and Epaphroditus (vv. 25-30). Epaphroditus was not an apostle or a prominent theologian; he was an ordinary member of the Philippian church.

By commanding the church to receive Epaphroditus with all gladness and to hold such men in esteem, Paul shows that high honor in Christ's kingdom belongs to those who serve sacrificial causes with quiet faithfulness. The verse underscores that the church must celebrate and treasure every believer who risks comfort and safety for the sake of the gospel.
