AMBITION - (eritheía, Strong's G2052)

Summary:

The Greek word eritheia refers to self-seeking ambition, rivalry, or a partisan spirit aimed at personal gain and self-advancement. In Philippians 2:3, Paul commands believers to do nothing out of selfish ambition, identifying this self-centered driving force as a direct threat to Christian unity and brotherly love.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek literature outside the Bible, the word eritheia originally connected to hired work and day laborers who worked strictly for personal gain or pay. Over time, the term shifted from mere paid labor to describe the self-seeking political maneuvering seen in public life. It came to denote electioneering, political intrigue, or forming rival factions to secure office and personal prominence.

Rather than seeking the common good, a person characterized by eritheia was driven by self-interest and a willingness to divide a community to advance individual standing. The New Testament adopts this word to describe an attitude of selfish rivalry that disrupts the peace and unity of the church.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word refers to selfish ambition, partisan spirit, rivalry, or self-seeking motive. It describes a disposition that puts personal recognition, status, or preference above the well-being of others and the harmony of the fellowship.

Where genuine Christian love seeks to serve others, eritheia seeks to use circumstances and relationships to exalt oneself. It is not noble aspirational ambition for God's glory, but self-centered ambition that pushes others down to elevate oneself.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, eritheia always carries a negative meaning. It never describes a healthy zeal or a godly desire for excellence. Instead, it consistently appears alongside sins that fracture community life, such as envy, strife, outbursts of anger, and dissensions.

Scripture treats this spirit of self-seeking ambition as a fruit of fallen human nature. It reveals an internal orientation that values individual dominance over collective unity. Whether manifested as political factionalism in the public square or selfish posturing within the church, the word highlights the destructive heart behind personal rivalry.

4. Theological Implications

Theological reflection on eritheia reveals how deeply self-centered ambition opposes the character and gospel of Jesus Christ. Selfish ambition is rooted in pride, seeking glory for self rather than for God.

This spirit threatens the witness of the local church. When leaders or members operate out of self-seeking motives, unity breaks down, and the church mimics the worldly political power struggles around it. True gospel fellowship requires putting away the drive for self-exaltation so that the body of Christ may reflect the selfless love of its Lord.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:3, Paul delivers a clear directive to the church at Philippi: "Let nothing be done through selfish ambition or conceit, but in lowliness of mind let each esteem others better than himself." The target noun appears in the accusative case, singular number, and feminine gender, functioning as the object of a preposition that identifies the unacceptable motivation for any action within the church.

This warning fits directly into Paul's larger appeal for gospel unity in Philippians 1:27–2:4. In the preceding chapter, Paul noted that some were preaching Christ out of eritheia, hoping to cause him distress in prison. Here in chapter 2, he instructs the entire church never to allow such motives to govern their work or relationships.

Paul directly contrasts selfish ambition with "lowliness of mind"—a humble attitude that deliberately considers others as more significant than oneself. Immediately following this verse, Paul sets forth the supreme model of Christ Himself, who emptied Himself and took the form of a servant (Philippians 2:5–8). Self-seeking ambition stands as the complete opposite of the mind of Christ.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Philippians 1:16 – "The former preach Christ from selfish ambition, not sincerely, supposing to add affliction to my chains."

  • Galatians 5:20 – Listed among the works of the flesh as "selfish ambitions," contrasting sharply with the fruit of the Spirit.

  • James 3:14 – "But if you have bitter envy and self-seeking in your hearts, do not boast and lie against the truth."

  • James 3:16 – "For where envy and self-seeking exist, confusion and every evil thing are there."

  • Romans 2:8 – Describes those who face divine judgment as "self-seeking and do not obey the truth."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The biblical contrast between eritheia and Christian humility highlights a profound spiritual truth: worldly success often rewards aggressive self-promotion, but kingdom leadership requires sacrificial service. When believers replace self-seeking ambition with humility, the church demonstrates a powerful counter-cultural testimony to the world.
