FOR - (diá, Strong's G1223)

Summary:

The word dia is a common Greek preposition that points to either the means through which something happens or the reason why an action takes place. Depending on how it functions in a sentence, it can mean "through," "by," "because of," or "for the sake of." In Philippians 2:30, the word expresses cause or motive, showing that Epaphroditus risked his life specifically on account of and for the sake of the work of Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In New Testament Greek, prepositions build bridges between ideas. They show how actions relate to places, instruments, agency, or motives. The word dia is one of the primary prepositions used to express either instrumentality (how an action is accomplished) or cause (why an action is taken).

In the letter to the Philippians, Paul writes from prison to thank the church at Philippi for their partnership in the gospel. The Philippian congregation had sent Epaphroditus as their messenger to deliver a financial gift and to assist Paul in his imprisonment. During this mission, Epaphroditus became dangerously ill. In Philippians 2:25–30, Paul commends Epaphroditus to his home church, instructing them to honor him because he nearly lost his life "for" the work of Christ.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

straightforward preposition, dia connects an action to its cause, instrument, or motive.

The word can refer to:

  • Agency or means: "through" or "by means of," identifying the person or instrument used to accomplish an action.

  • Cause or reason: "because of," "on account of," or "for the sake of," identifying the driving reason or motive behind an action.

Here in Philippians 2:30, the word expresses cause. It identifies "the work of Christ" as the compelling motive behind Epaphroditus' sacrificial service. Epaphroditus did not face death out of mere carelessness or personal ambition; he brought himself to the brink of death on account of Christ's work.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, dia serves a simple yet essential structural purpose. It clarifies either how something happened or why something happened.

When the word highlights agency, it indicates the channel through which God or human beings act. For instance, God often speaks "through" prophets or brings salvation "through" Jesus Christ. In these settings, dia connects the ultimate source of an action to the channel through which it arrives.

When the word highlights cause, as it does in Philippians 2:30, it answers the question "why?" It points directly to the underlying reason for an event, choice, or condition. The preposition itself does not carry complex theological meaning on its own. Instead, its significance comes from the words it connects. When linked to the name or work of Jesus Christ, dia shows that Christ is the true cause and motivation for Christian labor and endurance.

4. Theological Implications

While dia is a simple connecting word, its placement in Philippians 2:30 highlights a key biblical truth about Christian service. Epaphroditus' exhausting effort and life-threatening illness were directly linked "for" (on account of) the work of Christ.

This reveals that practical ministry—such as traveling to care for an imprisoned apostle—is recognized by God as work done directly for Christ. Epaphroditus was serving Paul, but the underlying cause of his sacrifice was the work of Christ Himself. Scripture shows that sacrificial service in the church is not merely human goodwill; it is motivated by and directed toward the honor and advancement of Jesus Christ.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:25–30, Paul addresses the Philippians' anxiety concerning Epaphroditus. The church had heard that their messenger was sick, and Epaphroditus was distressed because his home church was worried about him. Paul assures them that God had mercy on Epaphroditus, restoring his health, and Paul is now sending him back to Philippi.

Paul instructs the church to receive Epaphroditus in the Lord with all gladness and to hold men like him in high esteem. The justification for this high regard hinges on verse 30: "because for the work of Christ he came close to death."

The preposition for (dia) links Epaphroditus' near-fatal illness directly to his gospel mission. He stepped into a dangerous situation to supply what was lacking in the Philippians' physical service to Paul. By using dia, Paul makes it clear that Epaphroditus' suffering was honorable. His risk was taken on account of the work of Christ, elevating his practical aid to the status of sacred service.

7. Other Relevant Insights

It is worth noting how Paul describes practical, physical help as "the work of Christ." Epaphroditus was not preaching on a public stage or writing inspired scripture; he was delivering funds and providing personal assistance to a prisoner. Yet Paul uses dia to frame this practical assistance as action taken directly on account of Christ's work. This shows that in the biblical view, everyday acts of mercy and support for gospel ministry are genuine contributions to the work of Jesus Christ.
