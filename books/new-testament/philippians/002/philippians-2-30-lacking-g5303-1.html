LACKING - (hystérēma, Strong's G5303)

Summary:

The Greek noun hysterēma refers to a deficiency, shortage, requirement, or that which is missing or lacking. It can describe physical poverty, a deficit in material resources, or a gap in personal contact and presence. In Philippians 2:30, the word describes the physical gap created by the Philippian church's distance from Paul while he was in prison. It highlights how Epaphroditus risked his life as their personal representative to supply the practical ministry and personal care that the believers in Philippi could not deliver in person.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The church at Philippi held a special place in Paul's heart, having supported his gospel ministry from its earliest days. When Paul was imprisoned in Rome, the church wanted to care for his practical needs and assure him of their continued love. However, the vast geographic distance between Philippi and Rome made it impossible for the entire congregation to travel and tend to Paul personally.

To solve this problem, the church sent Epaphroditus as their official envoy. He carried their financial gift and was tasked with staying in Rome to serve Paul. While carrying out this mission, Epaphroditus fell gravely ill and nearly died. In Philippians 2:30, Paul commends Epaphroditus to the church, explaining that his life-threatening illness resulted from his sacrificial effort to bridge the physical gap between the Philippians and Paul.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Hysterēma refers to something missing, incomplete, or deficient. Depending on the context, the word can refer to:

  • A shortfall or deficit of material goods, money, or physical necessities.

  • Severe poverty or financial hardship.

  • A gap created by absence or distance, where a personal presence or service is missing.

  • An unfinished or incomplete aspect of something, such as room for growth in knowledge or faith.

The noun itself simply points to a gap or shortage. It does not automatically imply a moral failure or neglect on the part of those associated with the shortage. The reason for the lack is determined by the surrounding context.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, hysterēma appears in contexts involving both physical needs and personal relationships. In the Gospels, the word describes extreme poverty, such as a poor widow giving out of her deep physical deficiency.

In Paul's letters, the word frequently appears in connection with Christian giving and church relationships. It often refers to financial shortfalls that one church helps satisfy for another, promoting mutual support within the body of Christ. In other instances, it describes a personal absence—the unavoidable gap that occurs when believers are separated by distance and cannot enjoy face-to-face fellowship. In every case, the word highlights a real need that requires action to be met or completed.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of hysterēma illustrates how God builds interdependency into the Christian community. No individual believer or local church is entirely self-sufficient. God allows physical, financial, and practical shortages so that His people can express love by helping one another.

Epaphroditus’ willingness to suffer illness for the sake of filling this gap shows the high value Scripture places on sacrificial service. Bridging a practical shortfall for a minister of the gospel is recognized as work done directly for Christ. The passage demonstrates that personal representation and practical care are vital components of Christian fellowship.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:30, Paul uses hysterēma as an accusative singular neuter noun to describe what Epaphroditus came to fulfill: "to supply what was lacking in your service toward me."

Paul is careful not to criticize the Philippian believers. The word "lacking" here carries no tone of rebuke. Their willingness to give was evident in the gift they sent. The only thing "lacking" was their physical presence at Paul's side. Because the entire church could not travel to Rome, there was an unavoidable shortfall in personal, hands-on care.

Epaphroditus stepped into that gap. He acted as the church's hands and feet, delivering their gift and offering personal assistance to the imprisoned apostle. Paul emphasizes that Epaphroditus risked his life to complete this task, transforming a simple delivery into a profound act of sacrificial Christian service.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Corinthians 16:17 – "I am glad about the coming of Stephanas, Fortunatus, and Achaicus, for what was lacking on your part they supplied."

  • 2 Corinthians 8:14 – "that now at this time your abundance may supply their lack, that their abundance also may be a supply for your lack—that there may be equality."

  • 2 Corinthians 9:12 – "For the administration of this service not only supplies the needs of the saints, but also abounds through many thanksgivings to God."

  • 1 Thessalonians 3:10 – "night and day praying exceedingly that we may see your face and perfect what is lacking in your faith?"

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Philippians 2:30, Paul explains that Epaphroditus risked his life to supply this lack. The phrase describing his risk carries the idea of gambling or recklessly exposing oneself to danger. Epaphroditus did not treat his health as paramount when the work of Christ and the needs of Paul were at stake.

This passage reveals the depth of affection between Paul, Epaphroditus, and the Philippian church. By sending Epaphroditus, the Philippians demonstrated that true Christian care is not satisfied merely with good intentions or distant financial transactions; it seeks personal connection and tangible presence wherever possible.
