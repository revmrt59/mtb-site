REGARDING - (parabouleúomai, Strong's G3851)

Summary:

The word refers to exposing oneself to danger, risking one's life, or staking everything on a cause. In Philippians 2:30, it describes how Epaphroditus hazarded his life and health to serve Paul in prison and complete the work of gospel ministry on behalf of the Philippian church.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient Greek world, terms associated with this word family were often used in contexts involving risk, hazard, or putting up a stake, such as in games of chance. Over time, the expression came to describe any action where a person willingly exposed themselves to danger or risked something valuable for a higher purpose.

In the New Testament, Paul uses this word when describing Epaphroditus, a messenger sent by the church at Philippi to care for Paul during his Roman imprisonment. Epaphroditus fell dangerously ill while ministering to Paul, coming close to death in order to carry out his mission.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb means to risk, to endanger, or to hazard oneself. In English, the phrase "not regarding his life" translates an action that means risking one's life or treating personal safety as secondary to a greater goal.

When applied to human actions in Scripture, it describes a person willingly placing their safety or physical well-being on the line to accomplish a God-given task or to minister to someone in need.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

The word describes a specific act of courage and self-sacrifice in the service of Jesus Christ. Epaphroditus did not seek out suffering for its own sake, nor was he reckless with his life. Rather, he considered the work of Christ and the needs of Paul to be worth the personal risk.

The focus of the word is on the real physical danger Epaphroditus faced. His service in a distant city while caring for a prisoner involved serious illness and hardship, yet he pushed forward despite the peril to his health.

4. Theological Implications

The conduct of Epaphroditus illustrates the practical application of Christlike self-giving described earlier in Philippians 2. Just as Christ emptied Himself and took the form of a servant, Epaphroditus demonstrated a willing selflessness that placed the needs of others above his own physical survival.

This word highlights that gospel ministry often carries personal cost. Faithful service to God's people is not always comfortable or safe; at times, it requires believers to place their comfort, health, or safety at risk for the sake of Christ's kingdom and the care of fellow believers.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:25–30, Paul introduces Epaphroditus back to the Philippian congregation. Epaphroditus had fallen ill while serving Paul, and he was deeply concerned because the Philippians had heard about his sickness. Paul sent him back with high commendation, telling the church to receive him with all gladness and hold such men in honor.

Paul specifies that Epaphroditus came close to death "not regarding his life" to supply what was lacking in their service toward Paul. The Philippians could not all travel to Rome to care for Paul personally, so Epaphroditus served as their representative. By placing himself in jeopardy to fulfill their mission of care, Epaphroditus proved his devotion to Christ and to Paul.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • This term is unique to this context and does not appear elsewhere in the biblical corpus.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the early centuries following the apostolic era, groups of Christians in the early church drew inspiration from this word and were called the Parabolani (the "riskers"). These believers traveled into plague-ridden cities and hazardous environments to care for the sick and bury the dead when others fled. They saw their sacrificial labor as a direct continuation of the spirit shown by Epaphroditus in Philippians 2:30.
