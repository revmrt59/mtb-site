TOWARD - (prós, Strong's G4314)

Summary:

The Greek word pros is a preposition that indicates direction, goal, movement toward a target, or relational closeness. Depending on the context, it can be translated as "toward," "to," "with," or "for." In Philippians 2:30, the word identifies Paul as the personal target and intended recipient of the Philippian church's practical care, showing that Epaphroditus' dangerous journey was undertaken to bring their fellowship directly to the apostle in his time of need.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word pros functions in Greek as a preposition, a word that connects an action, movement, or state of being with a person, place, or goal. In ancient Greek communication, prepositions were essential for describing spatial direction, face-to-face interaction, or personal alignment.

While other prepositions might describe general location or movement inside a space, pros frequently emphasizes active direction aimed toward a destination or recipient. In the New Testament, it often marks the orientation of a person's movement, speech, heart, or practical service toward someone else or toward God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word pros can refer to directional movement, personal presence, or thematic reference.

  • When paired with verbs of motion or action, it means "toward" or "to," pointing out the target or recipient of an action.

  • When describing relationships between individuals, it means "with," highlighting face-to-face contact or ongoing personal interaction.

  • When denoting topic or orientation, it means "pertaining to," "for," or "regarding."

In Philippians 2:30, the word functions directionally and relationally. It specifies that the service sent by the Philippians was directed specifically toward Paul.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, pros serves several distinct structural roles in communication:

  • Directional Movement: The word frequently describes physical movement toward a specific person or location, highlighting the intentional destination of a traveler or speaker.

  • Personal Speech: It often introduces the audience receiving a message, indicating to whom words or teachings are explicitly directed.

  • Close Presence: In relational settings, it describes being physically or spiritually present with someone in close fellowship.

  • Service and Orientation: It connects practical acts or personal devotion to the target being served or honored.

4. Theological Implications

The use of pros in passages dealing with Christian ministry highlights the personal and tangible nature of biblical love. Christian service is never presented in Scripture as an abstract idea or a vague feeling. Instead, God's love moves directly toward people in concrete ways.

When believers support missionaries, care for the sick, or encourage leaders, their service is directed toward specific recipients for Christ's sake. Epaphroditus' mission demonstrates that kingdom ministry involves real people bridging real distances to bring help to those in distress.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:25–30, Paul expresses deep gratitude for Epaphroditus, whom the church at Philippi had sent to minister to Paul's physical and practical needs while he was imprisoned. Epaphroditus became dangerously ill during his journey, coming close to death for the sake of Christ's work.

In verse 30, Paul explains that Epaphroditus risked his life to supply what was lacking in the church's service "toward" (pros) him. The phrase does not imply that the Philippian believers were lazy or indifferent. Rather, because of the physical distance between Philippi and Rome, the church as a whole could not be present to hand-deliver their gift to Paul. Epaphroditus acted as their representative, carrying their practical relief directly to Paul's side. The preposition pros anchors this statement, showing that their loving concern reached its intended target through Epaphroditus' sacrificial service.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Luke 9:23 — Then He said to them all, "If anyone desires to come after Me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow Me."

  • 1 Thessalonians 3:4 — For, in fact, we told you before when we were with you that we would suffer tribulation, just as it happened, and you know.

  • Romans 15:17 — Therefore I have reason to glory in Christ Jesus in those things which pertain to God.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The word underscores the active effort required to carry out Christian fellowship. Distance and circumstance often create physical gaps between believers, but genuine love actively moves toward those in need. Epaphroditus' willingness to risk his health demonstrates that directing ministry toward a brother or sister in Christ is worth significant personal sacrifice.
