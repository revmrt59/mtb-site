EACH - (hékastos, Strong's G1538)

Summary:

clear The Greek word transliterated as hekastos means "each," "every," or "each one." It functions as a distributive word, taking a broad group and directing attention to every individual member within it. It ensures that a command, condition, or gift is understood as applying to every single person without exception. In Philippians 2:4, Paul uses "each" to make mutual Christian care the personal responsibility of every believer, ensuring that congregational unity is built on individual active selflessness.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek, hekastos was the standard word used to break down a collective whole into its distinct individual parts. When addressing a crowd, a city, or a household, a speaker used this term to ensure that no single person felt excluded from an instruction or exempt from a duty.

In the Greco-Roman world, social duties were often divided sharply by status, class, or civic standing. However, biblical writers used hekastos to highlight an equal, individual responsibility before God. Whether describing divine judgment, spiritual gifts, or moral obligations, the word brought broad corporate concepts down to the level of personal obedience and individual account.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word means "each," "every single one," or "each individual." It specifies that an action or statement applies to every member of a group considered individually.

When used in Scripture, the word functions in a few clear ways:

  • It individualizes a shared command, showing that a general duty belongs to every person directly.

  • It emphasizes individual accountability, highlighting that God deals with human beings on a personal level.

  • It distributes spiritual gifts or responsibilities across a community, showing that every member has a role to fulfill.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, hekastos frequently balances the relationship between the group and the individual. While God calls believers into one body, He never dissolves individual responsibility within the collective group.

The word appears in contexts dealing with divine evaluation, where God assesses each person's work or conduct. It also appears in practical instructions for church life, where mutual love, truthfulness, and financial generosity are required from every believer. By using this term, Scripture prevents people from hiding behind corporate identity or assuming that someone else will carry out a shared command.

4. Theological Implications

The use of hekastos reflects how God relates to His people. Redemption creates a unified community, yet God knows, equips, and holds accountable each person individually.

Theologically, this word guards against two equal dangers in the life of the church:

  • Individualism without community: The word always operates within a group setting, keeping the individual tied to the life of the broader body.

  • Passivity within community: The word makes shared duties personally binding, preventing believers from expecting the church as a whole to perform duties that require individual obedience.

In Christian fellowship, unity is not created merely by leadership decrees or institutional policies, but by the personal, daily choices of every individual member.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:1–4, Paul appeals to the church at Philippi to maintain complete unity, love, and spiritual harmony. To achieve this, he addresses the practical attitude required of every church member. In Philippians 2:4, he uses the masculine singular form of hekastos, presenting "each" believer as an active participant in maintaining congregational health.

Paul constructs a contrast regarding focus: believers must not look out only for their own interests, but "each" person must look out for the interests of others. By using "each," Paul ensures that no believer exempts themselves from looking after others. Harmony breaks down when church members assume that pastoral leadership or active volunteers will handle mutual care. Paul makes selfless care a universal Christian duty, setting up the ultimate example of self-giving humility seen in Jesus Christ (Philippians 2:5–11).

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Romans 12:3 — "For I say, through the grace given to me, to everyone who is among you, not to think of himself more highly than he ought to think, but to think soberly, as God has dealt to each one a measure of faith."

  • 1 Corinthians 10:24 — "Let no one seek his own, but each one the other’s well-being."

  • Galatians 6:5 — "For each one shall bear his own load."

  • Ephesians 4:25 — "Therefore, putting away lying, 'Let each one of you speak truth with his neighbor,' for we are members of one another."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Philippians 2:4, hekastos reinforces the practical nature of biblical humility. Humility is not an abstract feeling; it expresses itself in concrete actions directed toward other people. By placing "each" in direct connection with looking out for the interests of others, Paul demonstrates that true spiritual maturity shows itself when an individual actively prioritizes the needs, well-being, and burdens of brothers and sisters in Christ.
