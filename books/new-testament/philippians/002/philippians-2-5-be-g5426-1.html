BE - (phronéō, Strong's G5426)

Summary:

The Greek verb phroneō refers to holding an attitude, setting the mind upon a particular focus, or cultivating a personal disposition. Rather than describing a fleeting thought or bare intellectual activity, it refers to a settled mindset and practical outlook that guides how a person thinks and acts. In Philippians 2:5, this command instructs believers to allow the humble, self-giving mindset of Christ Jesus to direct their shared life and community relationships.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek, phroneō described the activity of the mind as it forms judgments, holds convictions, and adopts a particular disposition toward life. It combined thought with inner intent, reflecting not merely what someone believed in theory, but how their underlying outlook shaped their choices and character.

In the New Testament, Paul uses this word extensively when writing to Christian communities. For Paul, the gospel does not simply supply new information; it transforms how believers think, evaluate priorities, and treat one another. In his letter to the Philippians, where personal conflicts threatened church unity, Paul repeatedly uses phroneō to call the church toward a shared mindset marked by humility, mutual care, and devotion to Christ.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word phroneō can mean to think, to set the mind on, to hold a specific disposition, or to harbor a particular attitude.

Depending on the context, the word carries several shades of meaning:

  • Setting one's attention or affection on an objective or value.

  • Harboring a specific mental attitude or opinion.

  • Maintaining a shared agreement or harmony of mind within a group.

The term goes beyond a brief mental impression. It describes an active pattern of thinking that determines a person's priorities, values, and practical behavior.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, phroneō appears in contexts that contrast two opposing ways of thinking: a worldly mindset focused on self-interest and a spiritual mindset focused on God's purposes.

Scripture uses the word to highlight several key aspects of Christian living:

  • Shared Harmony: Paul frequently uses the word to urge churches toward mutual agreement and unity, calling them to "be like-minded" or to "mind the same thing."

  • Mindset Alignment: The word highlights that unity in the church does not come from uniform personalities, but from a common focus and shared attitude rooted in God's truth.

  • Direction of the Soul: The verb shows that a person's spiritual direction is revealed by what their mind routinely dwells upon and values.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of phroneō demonstrates that Christian character begins with the renewal of the mind. Action and fellowship flow directly from inner disposition.

Scripture presents Jesus Christ as the supreme standard for how believers ought to think and live. The call to adopt a Christlike mindset shows that Christian humility is not an abstract virtue, but a concrete imitation of Jesus' self-emptying love.

The target occurrence in Philippians 2:5 carries a present passive imperative form (singular in number). The passive form indicates that believers are to let this mindset be formed and maintained within them, while the imperative makes it a direct apostolic instruction. Believers are called to yield actively to the truth of the gospel, allowing Christ's example to shape their inner attitudes and outward fellowship.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:1–4, Paul appeals to the believers to abandon selfish ambition, vain conceit, and personal rivalry. He urges them to look out for the interests of others and to esteem others above themselves in lowliness of mind.

Verse 5 forms the critical bridge between Paul's practical exhortation and the famous hymn regarding Christ's humiliation and exaltation (Philippians 2:6–11). When Paul writes, "Let this mind be in you which was also in Christ Jesus," phroneō serves as the core instruction.

Paul does not merely tell the Philippians to admire Christ's self-giving sacrifice from a distance. By using phroneō, he commands them to adopt that very same pattern of humble, sacrificial thinking in their daily interactions with one another. The supreme example of Christ putting aside His own privileges to serve others becomes the absolute standard for how the local church must think and act.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 16:23 — Jesus rebuked Peter because he was not "mindful" of the things of God, but the things of men.

  • Romans 8:5 — Paul contrasts those who live according to the flesh with those who set their "minds" on the things of the Spirit.

  • Romans 12:16 — Believers are instructed to be of the same "mind" toward one another, avoiding pride and high-mindedness.

  • Philippians 2:2 — Paul asks the church to fulfill his joy by being like-minded and being of one "mind".

  • Colossians 3:2 — Believers are commanded to set their "mind" on things above, rather than on earthly things.

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Corporate Character: In Philippians 2:5, the phrase translated "in you" refers to the community as a whole. Paul is not only speaking to individual readers in isolation, but instructing the entire church family to cultivate this Christlike mindset together in their shared corporate life.

  • Practical Humility: The mindset described by phroneō in this passage is defined specifically by the verses that follow. Christ's willingness to take the form of a servant and obey even unto death shows that true biblical mindedness is defined by active, self-sacrificing service toward others.
