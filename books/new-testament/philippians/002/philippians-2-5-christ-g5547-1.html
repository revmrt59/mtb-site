CHRIST - (Christós, Strong's G5547)

Summary:

The Greek word Christos means "Anointed One," translating the Hebrew concept of the Messiah. In Philippians 2:5, Paul uses this title and name to point believers to Jesus as the supreme model of humble, self-giving love. Calling Him "Christ Jesus" reminds the church that the sovereign Messiah—the eternal King sent by God—is the very one who humbled Himself for human salvation, providing the pattern for how believers are to think and relate to one another.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word Christos originates from an ancient word meaning to smear or anointed with oil. In the Old Testament context, physical anointing with oil was used to set apart prophets, priests, and kings for God’s special service. Over time, the concept focused on the promised ultimate Deliverer—the Messiah—whom God would send to save His people and establish His righteous kingdom.

By the time of the New Testament, Christos had become the standard Greek word used for the Hebrew title Mashiach (Messiah). In the Gospels, it often appears as a formal title ("the Christ"). In the New Testament letters written by Paul, Christos is frequently combined with the personal name Jesus, functioning both as a divine title of royal authority and as part of a compound proper name, "Christ Jesus" or "Jesus Christ."

When Paul wrote his letter to the Philippians, he was imprisoned for preaching the gospel. The church at Philippi faced external pressure and internal tensions regarding unity and humble service. Paul introduced the example of "Christ Jesus" to resolve these practical relational challenges by pointing to the character and sacrifice of the Lord Himself.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Christos refers to one who has been anointed and appointed by God to accomplish His divine purpose. The word can mean:

  • The promised Messiah of Israel who fulfills Old Testament prophecy.

  • God's anointed King and Savior who reigns over creation and the church.

  • The specific person, Jesus of Nazareth, recognized as God's true Anointed One.

When applied to Jesus, the term carries both royal dignity and official divine authorization. Jesus is not merely a wise teacher or human leader; He is God’s uniquely chosen King who carries out God's redemptive mission on earth.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, Christos appears in two main ways. First, it functions as an official title referring to the Jewish messianic expectation, as seen when people ask whether Jesus is "the Christ." Second, in the epistles, it functions as a personal name and title that defines the identity of the risen Lord and the believer's life in Him.

In Philippians 2:5, the grammatical form is masculine, singular, and in the dative case, forming the expression "in Christ Jesus." This expression denotes the personal model and divine sphere in which Christians live. Paul often uses "Christ Jesus" rather than "Jesus Christ" when he wants to emphasize the exalted status and messianic office of the Savior before mentioning His earthly, human name.

4. Theological Implications

The title Christos carries profound theological significance across the canon of Scripture:

  • Divine Appointment: Anointing signifies that Jesus was explicitly chosen, sent, and empowered by God the Father to accomplish redemption.

  • Royal and Priest-King Office: As the Christ, Jesus fulfills the Old Testament promises made to King David regarding an eternal throne, uniting authority and priestly mediation in one person.

  • Union with Believers: The phrase "in Christ" describes the foundational spiritual reality of Christian life. Believers are joined to Him, sharing in His righteousness, life, and ultimate victory.

  • Redemptive Humility: The New Testament transforms expectations of messianic power. As Christos, Jesus displayed His ultimate kingly authority not by military force, but by humbling Himself to suffer and die on a cross for sinners.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:1–4, Paul appeals to the Philippian church to live in harmony, avoid selfish ambition, and look out for the interests of others. Verse 5 acts as the crucial doorway into the famous passage detailing the self-emptying and exaltation of Jesus (Philippians 2:5–11).

Paul writes, "Let this mind be in you which was also in Christ Jesus." The word Christos here grounds the required mindset in the historical person and royal character of the Lord. The attitude Paul demands of believers—unselfishness, humility, and sacrificial care for others—is not an abstract moral philosophy. It is the very mindset demonstrated by the Messiah Himself.

By identifying Him as "Christ Jesus," Paul highlights a striking contrast. Jesus held the supreme title and status of God's Anointed King, yet He did not exploit His divine rights for personal gain. Instead, as the subsequent verses show, He took the form of a bondservant. The title Christos in verse 5 serves as a powerful reminder that true messianic greatness is revealed through humble service.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 1:1 — "The book of the genealogy of Jesus Christ, the Son of David, the Son of Abraham." Here the title establishes Jesus from the very beginning of the New Testament as the long-awaited Davidic King and descendant of Abraham.

  • Acts 17:3 — "...explaining and demonstrating that the Christ had to suffer and rise again from the dead, and saying, 'This Jesus whom I preach to you is the Christ.'" Paul uses the title in Jewish synagogue discussions to demonstrate from Scripture that the promised Messiah was destined to suffer before entering His glory.

  • Romans 6:8 — "Now if we died with Christ, we believe that we shall also live with Him." In this passage, the term represents the believer's spiritual union with the Savior in His death and resurrection.

  • 2 Corinthians 5:16 — "Therefore, from now on, we regard no one according to the flesh. Even though we have known Christ according to the flesh, yet now we know Him thus no longer." Paul uses the name to show how believing in the resurrected Messiah changes a person's entire perspective on Jesus and on humanity.

  • Colossians 3:1 — "If then you were raised with Christ, seek those things which are above, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God." The title emphasizes the current heavenly reign and exaltation of the Lord, who is the focal point of the Christian's spiritual life.

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Word Order Significance: Paul's placement of "Christ" before "Jesus" in Philippians 2:5 places immediate focus on His divine office and Messiahship. The church is called to imitate the mindset of the Lord of glory who voluntarily walked the path of lowliness.

  • The Grounding of Christian Ethics: Paul does not command humility simply because it creates a pleasant community. He grounds the Christian mindset directly in the historical act of Christos, making Christology the absolute foundation for daily relationships within the church family.
