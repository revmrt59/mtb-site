EQUAL - (ísos, Strong's G2470)

Summary:

The Greek word isos means equal, equivalent, or identical in size, quantity, quality, or rank. In Philippians 2:6, it describes Jesus Christ's essential equality with God the Father. The passage asserts that Christ possessed full equality of nature and divine standing with God prior to His incarnation, yet He did not regard this divine equality as something to be selfishly held onto or exploited for His own advantage.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Philippians 2 contains one of the most profound descriptions of Jesus Christ in the New Testament. Apostle Paul writes to the church at Philippi to encourage unity, harmony, and selflessness among believers. To establish the highest possible standard for humility, Paul points directly to the example of Jesus Christ.

Before taking on human flesh, Christ existed eternally in the form of God. In this pre-incarnate state, He shared full equality with God the Father. The word isos emphasizes that Christ's equality with God was not a future reward to be achieved or a status He illegitimately seized. It was an essential reality He inherently possessed as eternal God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Isos refers to that which is equal, identical, or equivalent in degree, quantity, quality, status, or rank. Depending on the context, the word can refer to:

  • Equal measurement or quantity, such as an identical wage or portion.

  • Agreement or consistency, such as matching testimonies that align with one another.

  • Equal privilege or standing, such as receiving the exact same gift or status.

  • Perfect spatial symmetry, where dimensions are identical in length, width, and height.

  • Essential equality of nature, as seen in descriptions of Christ's relation to God the Father.

In Philippians 2:6, isos describes Christ's complete equality with God in His essential nature, majesty, and divine rights.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In the New Testament, isos is used across several contexts to highlight fairness, consistency, or identical status:

  • It describes financial or material fairness, where two parties receive equal amounts.

  • It describes court witnesses whose testimony agrees without contradiction.

  • It describes spiritual equality, where God imparts the exact same grace or gift to different groups without partiality.

  • It describes full divine equality, where Jesus acts with the same authority and nature as God the Father.

In Philippians 2:6, the word appears in a neuter plural form functioning as an adverb, describing the state of being equal with God ("to be equal with God").

4. Theological Implications

The assertion that Jesus is equal with God is foundational to Christian theology. It establishes the full deity of Jesus Christ. Jesus is not a elevated created being, an angel, or a lower divine entity; He is fully God, sharing the same divine essence with the Father.

Christ's equality with God was not acquired by ambition or theft. As God the Son, equality was inherently His. The marvel of the gospel is that although Christ possessed complete equality with the Father, He willingly set aside the independent exercise of His heavenly rights. He took on the form of a bondservant to accomplish salvation. This demonstrates that true divine glory is expressed through selfless love and service.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:6, Paul highlights a striking contrast. Christ existed in the form of God and possessed full equality with God. Yet He "did not consider it robbery to be equal with God." The word translated "robbery" refers to something seized or held onto tightly for self-advantage.

Christ did not view His equality with God as a privilege to be exploited for personal comfort or honor. Instead, He emptied Himself, taking the form of a servant and coming in the likeness of men.

Paul's pastoral purpose in using isos here is clear: if Jesus Christ, who possessed absolute equality with God, chose to lay aside His rights to serve others, believers must likewise abandon selfish ambition and serve one another with genuine humility.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • John 5:18 — "Therefore the Jews sought all the more to kill Him, because He not only broke the Sabbath, but also said that God was His Father, making Himself equal with God."

  • The religious leaders rightly understood that by claiming God as His own Father in a unique sense, Jesus was claiming identical nature and divine authority with God.

  • Matthew 20:12 — "saying, 'These last men have worked only one hour, and you made them equal to us who have borne the burden and the heat of the day.'"

  • In this parable, the word describes workers receiving the exact same daily wage regardless of hours worked, illustrating God's generous grace.

  • Acts 11:17 — "If therefore God gave them the same gift as He gave us when we believed on the Lord Jesus Christ, who was I that I could withstand God?"

  • Peter uses the word to demonstrate that God granted Gentile believers the identical Holy Spirit given to Jewish believers, proving equal standing in God's kingdom.

  • Revelation 21:16 — "The city is laid out as a square; its length is as great as its breadth. And he measured the city with the reed: twelve thousand furlongs. Its length, breadth, and height are equal."

  • The word highlights the perfect symmetry of the New Jerusalem, where all three spatial dimensions are identical.

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The contrast between Christ and the first Adam is central to biblical theology. Adam was a created man who attempted to grasp equality with God through pride and disobedience, bringing fall and death. Jesus Christ, who eternally possessed true equality with God, humbled Himself through obedience unto death, bringing redemption and life.

  • Christian humility does not require denying one's gifts or position. Rather, following Christ's example means choosing not to leverage power, status, or rights for personal gain, but using them to build up others.
