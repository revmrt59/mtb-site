BONDSERVANT - (doûlos, Strong's G1401)

Summary:

A bondservant or slave is a person subject to the complete authority and ownership of a master. In Philippians 2:7, Paul uses this word to describe the astounding humility of Jesus Christ, who, though existing in the form of God, willingly took on the lowly position and nature of a servant when He became man to accomplish our redemption.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient Greco-Roman world, a bondservant was a person bound to a master in total submission. Unlike a hired laborer who worked for a daily wage and could change employers, a bondservant belonged to another. The master commanded, and the servant obeyed.

When Paul wrote his letter to the church at Philippi, the believers lived in a Roman colony that placed great emphasis on social status, civic honor, and personal prestige. In that culture, slavery occupied the lowest rung of the social hierarchy. By using this familiar term to describe the incarnate Son of God, Paul presented the Philippians with a radical picture of self-giving humility that inverted human standards of greatness.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word refers to a slave, bondservant, or person held in submission to a master.

  • When used of earthly human relationships, it describes someone under the authority and ownership of a human master.

  • When used of religious allegiance, it describes an individual devoted entirely to God or Christ, recognizing Him as Lord.

  • When used of Jesus Christ in His incarnation, it refers to the humble status and position He assumed as a man subject to the Father's will.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the New Testament, the term is used in both literal and spiritual settings. In ordinary historical contexts and parables, it identifies household servants or agricultural workers carrying out the orders of a householder or king.

Spuritually, the word expresses wholehearted devotion. Apostles like Paul, James, and Jude frequently introduce themselves as bondservants of Jesus Christ, indicating that their lives are not their own, but belong entirely to the Lord. Scripture also describes believers as being set free from the slavery of sin in order to become bondservants of God and righteousness. The core idea of the word is not forced oppression, but complete ownership, absolute allegiance, and dutiful obedience to a master.

4. Theological Implications

In Philippians 2:7, the word contributes to one of the most profound Christological passages in all of Scripture. Jesus Christ did not cease to be fully God when He came to earth. Rather, while remaining divine, He voluntarily humbled Himself by taking on the actual position and condition of a bondservant.

This act of self-emptying highlights the depth of divine grace. The Lord of glory came not to be served, but to serve. By stepping into human history as a servant, Christ set the ultimate example of sacrificial love. His servant status was demonstrated in His perfect obedience to the Father, an obedience that ultimately carried Him to the cross.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:5–8, Paul encourages the church to cultivate the same humble mindset that Christ displayed. The apostle sets up a stark contrast between Christ's eternal glory and His earthly humiliation. Though Christ existed in the form of God, He "made Himself of no reputation, taking the form of a bondservant, and coming in the likeness of men."

The grammatical form of the word in this verse is a genitive masculine singular noun, modifying "form" (the form of a bondservant). The phrase "form of a bondservant" parallels "form of God" in verse 6, indicating that just as Christ possessed the true nature of deity, He also took on the true nature and status of a humble human servant. His earthly life was defined by submission, lowliness, and selfless service toward others.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Mark 10:44 — "And whoever of you desires to be first shall be slave of all."

  • John 15:15 — "No longer do I call you servants, for a servant does not know what his master is doing..."

  • Galatians 1:10 — "For if I still pleased men, I would not be a bondservant of Christ."

  • 1 Peter 2:16 — "...as free, yet not using liberty as a cloak for vice, but as bondservants of God."

7. Other Relevant Insights

It is important to distinguish a bondservant from a hired worker. A hired worker contracted out services for personal gain and maintained personal independence. A bondservant had no independent claims to status, rights, or self-determination.

Jesus taking the form of a bondservant links His earthly mission to the Old Testament prophesies of the Lord's Servant, particularly in the Book of Isaiah. The righteous Servant of God was foretold to carry the burdens of His people and submit humbly to the Father's plan of redemption. Christ fulfilled this role perfectly, moving from the lowest position of servant to the highest place of exaltation.
