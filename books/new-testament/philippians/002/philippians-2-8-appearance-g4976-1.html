APPEARANCE - (schēma, Strong's G4976)

Summary:

The Greek word schēma refers to outward form, visible fashion, external appearance, or temporary condition as perceived by an observer. In Philippians 2:8, it describes how Jesus Christ, having taken on human nature, was recognized in His outward appearance and physical life as a man before humbling Himself to die on the cross.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek usage, schēma referred to the visible shape, outer posture, fashion, or external state of a person or object. It described how something appeared to human senses—its outward presentation, visible structure, or temporary bearing. While other terms emphasize the essential nature or permanent character of something, schēma highlights the outward, observable form in which something presents itself at a given time.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word schēma can refer to:

  • Outward shape or external appearance.

  • Visible condition, fashion, or style of life.

  • The visible structure or temporary state of an entity.

When applied to a person, schēma points to the visible reality of outward existence—how someone looks, lives, and presents themselves to observers.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In the New Testament, schēma describes outward forms and visible conditions. It carries the nuance of that which is outwardly perceived and subject to physical observation.

In one instance, the word describes the outward structure and temporary condition of the present physical world, which is changing and passing away. In another instance—specifically in Paul's letter to the Philippians—it describes the outward human appearance and physical reality in which the Son of God was encountered by human eyes. The word does not imply a mere disguise or illusion; rather, it highlights the observable, external reality of human life that Jesus genuinely entered.

4. Theological Implications

Understanding outward appearance (schēma) is helpful when considering the incarnation of Jesus Christ. In Philippians 2, Scripture affirms both Christ's full divine nature and His genuine human existence.

While Christ possessed the essential nature of God, He entered human history as a real man. Anyone who saw Jesus in Galilee or Jerusalem observed a genuine human being with a physical body, human speech, and daily human habits. His outward appearance was fully that of a man. This confirms that Jesus did not merely pretend to be human. He truly experienced the physical conditions, limitations, and outward reality of earthly life, making Him a compassionate and faithful Savior.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:5–8, Paul presents the humility of Christ as the model for Christian unity and selflessness. Paul traces the voluntary lowering of Christ in a clear progression:

  • Existing in the form of God (Philippians 2:6).

  • Taking the form of a bondservant and coming in the likeness of men (Philippians 2:7).

  • Being found in appearance (schēma) as a man, He humbled Himself further to die on a cross (Philippians 2:8).

In verse 8, the expression "found in appearance as a man" describes how human observers encountered Jesus. To anyone looking at Him, He was unmistakably a man. Yet, while living in this visible human condition, Jesus chose the deepest path of humility: He submitted to execution on a Roman cross. The emphasis in verse 8 is not on questioning His humanity, but on highlighting how far the Lord lowered Himself—from heavenly glory to visible human life, and from human life to death on a cross.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Corinthians 7:31 — "and those who use this world as not misusing it; for the form of this world is passing away."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The word schēma emphasizes the outward and physical aspect of Jesus' earthly life. During His earthly ministry, Jesus was legally, physically, and visually recognized as a human being by those around Him.

Additionally, comparing the use of schēma in Philippians 2:8 with its use in 1 Corinthians 7:31 shows that outward forms in this present world are temporary. Earthly structures and physical existence in this age belong to an order that is passing away. Christ willingly entered this temporary, observable earthly condition in order to secure eternal salvation for His people.
