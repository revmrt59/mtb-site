AS - (hōs, Strong's G5613)

Summary:

The Greek particle hōs is a flexible conjunction and adverb used throughout the New Testament to express comparison, manner, role, quality, or time. Depending on context, it can be translated "as," "like," "how," "when," or "about." In Philippians 2:8, hōs functions comparative-descriptively to state that Jesus Christ was observed and recognized in outward human form "as a man." It highlights the historical reality that when Christ lived on earth, He presented Himself in genuine human condition, setting the stage for His ultimate act of humble obedience on the cross.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word hōs is one of the most frequent comparative particles in biblical Greek. It functions as an adverb or conjunction that connects two ideas, showing how one thing relates to another in appearance, function, degree, time, or character.

In first-century Greek, hōs was used across everyday speech, formal literature, and historical narrative. It allowed writers to draw comparisons ("like a tree"), state observed roles ("acting as a servant"), note approximate quantities ("about five thousand"), or indicate temporal sequence ("when He arrived"). In Philippians 2:8, Paul uses hōs within the famous passage describing Christ's self-emptying and humbleness. The apostle uses the word to describe how Jesus appeared to human observers during His earthly life: He was found in outward appearance "as a man."

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Hōs can refer to manner, degree, comparison, quality, time, or role depending on how it is used in a sentence.

Common senses include:

  • Comparison or likeness: meaning "as" or "like."

  • Capacity or observed state: meaning "in the role of" or "as being."

  • Time: meaning "when" or "while."

  • Manner or degree: meaning "how" or "in what way."

  • Approximation: meaning "about" or "roughly."

Here in Philippians 2:8, hōs functions to connect Christ's outward appearance with human nature. It specifies that Jesus was perceived in the recognizable condition and form of a human being.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, hōs serves several distinct structural roles:

  • Comparative comparison: It draws a direct parallel between two actions, objects, or people.

  • Observed capacity: It identifies the role or state in which a person acts or is recognized by others.

  • Temporal marker: It marks the time an action takes place, translated as "when."

  • Numerical approximation: It precedes numbers to express an estimate, translated as "about."

In Philippians 2:8, hōs carries the nuance of observed quality and external condition. When people encountered Jesus in Galilee and Jerusalem, they experienced Him as a real human being with human needs, human appearance, and human limitations, yet without sin.

4. Theological Implications

The comparative word hōs does not by itself define the full doctrine of the incarnation. A single particle cannot carry an entire theological doctrine. Instead, the rich truth of Christ's true deity and true humanity comes from the surrounding passage (Philippians 2:5–11), where Paul balances Christ existing in the "form of God" with His taking the "form of a servant" and being "made in the likeness of men."

The theological significance of hōs in Philippians 2:8 lies in how it describes Christ's earthly presentation. The Eternal Son did not walk the earth displaying visible, unshielded heavenly glory. Instead, He submitted to the normal conditions of human existence. To those around Him, He appeared simply as a man. This earthly lowliness makes His voluntary humble submission all the more profound: though existing above all creation, He was content to be found in appearance as a ordinary man, humbling Himself even to death on a Roman cross.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:8, Paul writes that Christ was "found in appearance as a man."

Each word in this clause builds a vivid picture of Christ's earthly life:

  • "Found" refers to the experience of human observers who encountered Him in history.

  • "Appearance" refers to His outward shape, habits, style, and bodily form.

  • "As" (hōs) links that observable appearance to human identity ("a man").

Paul's purpose in Philippians 2 is to give the church at Philippi a supreme model of humility and unselfish service. Paul urges believers not to pursue selfish ambition, but to share the mind of Christ. By using hōs, Paul emphasizes that Christ genuinely entered into the lowliness of the human condition. He did not insist on exercising His divine privileges for His own comfort, but lived among us as a real man, submitting fully to the Father's plan.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Understanding hōs in Philippians 2:8 helps prevent two equal and opposite theological mistakes regarding the person of Christ:

  • It guards against denying Christ's humanity. Jesus was not pretending to be human; He was genuinely found in appearance as a man, experiencing true human tiredness, grief, hunger, and physical death.

  • It guards against viewing Jesus as merely a human teacher. The context of Philippians 2 establishes that He who was found in appearance as a man was also eternally in the form of God (Philippians 2:6).

Paul presents Christ's willingness to be perceived as a humble man as the ultimate example of servant-hearted love, calling believers to follow His example of lowliness in their relationships with one another.
