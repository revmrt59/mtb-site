MAN - (ánthrōpos, Strong's G444)

Summary:

The word generally means a human being, a person, or humanity as a whole. It distinguishes human beings from God, angels, and the rest of creation. In Philippians 2:8, the word describes Jesus being recognized in outward appearance as a real human being. Paul uses it to show that the eternal Son of God truly humbled Himself to enter genuine human existence before submitting to death on a cross.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek, anthrōpos was the standard word for a human being, regardless of gender or age. It referred broadly to the human race or to an individual person. In common usage, it marked the boundary between humanity and other orders of existence, such as animals or divine beings.

When the Greek New Testament was written, anthrōpos preserved this basic sense. Rather than referring exclusively to an adult male, it often functioned as a general term for mankind. In biblical writing, the word frequently highlights human weakness, mortality, and dependence when set in contrast with God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to an individual person, human beings collectively, or human nature in general.

  • It can mean a person or individual human being.

  • It can refer to humanity as a species, contrasting human life with God or creation.

  • In Christological contexts, it refers to Jesus in His full, real humanity.

In Philippians 2:8, the word refers to human appearance and form as perceived by human observation. It points to the reality that Jesus was recognized as a genuine human being during His earthly ministry.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Scriptures, anthrōpos serves several key functions:

First, it emphasizes the distinction between God and human beings. God is eternal, all-powerful, and holy, while humanity is finite, dependent, and prone to weakness.

Second, it describes humanity in its physical, moral, and spiritual condition. Scripture speaks of human beings in their earthly frailty, as well as their need for divine grace and salvation.

Third, the word plays a central role in describing the incarnation of Jesus Christ. Jesus is called the "Son of Man" and the "Man Christ Jesus." In these contexts, the word does not deny His deity; rather, it highlights His true, uncompromised humanity.

4. Theological Implications

The theological significance of anthrōpos in connection with Jesus lies in the doctrine of the Incarnation. Christianity teaches that Jesus Christ is fully God and fully man.

By entering human history as a real man, Jesus identified directly with human existence. He experienced human limitation, physical needs, and earthly suffering, yet without sin. This true humanity was necessary so that He could serve as the representative and substitute for humanity. As the second Adam, Christ obeyed God where humanity had failed, offering a perfect human life as a sacrifice for sin.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:5–8, Paul presents a grand description of Christ's humility to encourage selflessness among believers. Paul explains that although Jesus existed in the form of God, He did not cling to His divine privileges. Instead, He emptied Himself by taking the form of a servant and coming in the likeness of men.

Verse 8 continues this progression: "And being found in appearance as a man, He humbled Himself and became obedient to the point of death, even the death of the cross."

The word anthrōpos here highlights how Jesus appeared to those around Him. To human observers, He looked like an ordinary human being. His divine majesty was veiled in human flesh. There was no overwhelming outward splendor that forced people to recognize Him as God; He was seen and recognized as a man.

Paul's argument builds on this observation. Being recognized as a man was already a staggering step of condescension for the Creator of the universe. Yet Jesus humbled Himself even further. From the baseline of human existence, He lowered Himself to the status of a servant, and ultimately submitted to a criminal's death on a Roman cross. The use of anthrōpos emphasizes the reality of His human experience and the depth of His self-giving love.

  • 1 Corinthians 15:45 — "And so it is written, 'The first man Adam became a living being.' The last Adam became a life-giving spirit."

  • 1 Timothy 2:5 — "For there is one God and one Mediator between God and men, the Man Christ Jesus."

  • Romans 1:18 — "For the wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness and unrighteousness of men, who suppress the truth in unrighteousness."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The phrase "found in appearance as a man" underscores that Jesus' humanity was not an illusion or a temporary disguise. Ancient heresies sometimes claimed that Jesus only seemed to be human, but Paul's language affirms that when people looked at Jesus, evaluated Him, and lived alongside Him, He was genuinely found to be a man. His physical body, human emotions, and earthly experiences were entirely real, making His sacrificial death genuine and effective for human redemption.
