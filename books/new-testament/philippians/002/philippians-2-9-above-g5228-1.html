ABOVE - (hypér, Strong's G5228)

Summary:

The preposition huper carries two main ideas in Scripture: representation ("on behalf of, for") and comparison or rank ("above, over, beyond"). In Philippians 2:9, Paul uses huper to express supreme elevation and superiority. Here the word shows that God has granted Jesus Christ a rank, authority, and title that surpass every created power and name in existence.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Greek, prepositions help show relationships of direction, place, benefit, or comparison. The preposition huper functions in two primary ways throughout the New Testament. Most frequently, it expresses action done for someone's benefit or in someone's place ("on behalf of" or "for"). In other contexts, it marks position or degree, indicating something that is "above," "over," or "beyond" something else.

In Philippians 2, Paul writes to a church located in a prominent Roman colony. He describes the dramatic transition from Christ's extreme humility to His ultimate elevation. After describing how Jesus humbled Himself to the point of death on a cross, Paul turns to God's decisive response in verse 9, using huper to mark the sheer height of Christ's exaltation.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Depending on the sentence, huper can mean "for," "on behalf of," "concerning," "beyond," "over," or "above."

When expressing spatial position or comparison, the word points to something that excels or sits above another in rank, magnitude, or standing. In Philippians 2:9, huper functions in this comparative sense, translated as "above." It indicates that the name bestowed on Jesus is superior to every other title or authority that can be named.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

The preposition appears in distinct ways depending on its sentence context:

  • Representation and Substitution: The word frequently describes Christ's sacrificial work, where He died "for" sinners or acted "on behalf of" His people.

  • Elevation and Rank: The word also marks distinction and mastery, describing one thing standing "over" or "above" another in importance or authority.

4. Theological Implications

The preposition huper itself indicates spatial position or comparative superiority. When joined to God's action in Philippians 2:9, it carries profound theological weight. Jesus did not merely receive a distinguished title among peers. God exalted Him to a position completely elevated above every earthly and spiritual authority.

This supreme rank is directly tied to His humble obedience. The one who descended to the lowest place of servant sacrifice has been raised to the highest place of ultimate glory. His position above every name highlights His supreme lordship over all creation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Philippians 2:5–11 forms a complete movement from humiliation to exaltation. Verses 5–8 detail Christ's self-emptying, His taking the form of a servant, and His death on the cross. Verse 9 opens with "Therefore," signaling that God's exaltation of Christ is the direct answer to His voluntary humiliation.

In ancient culture, a person's "name" represented their complete character, authority, and official standing. By stating that God gave Jesus a name above every name, Paul asserts that no power—whether political, demonic, or spiritual—can rival Jesus Christ. The immediate context leads directly into verses 10 and 11, where every knee bows and every tongue confesses that Jesus Christ is Lord. The positioning of His name above all others forms the basis for universal worship.

  • Ephesians 1:22 — "And He put all things under His feet, and gave Him to be head over all things to the church..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Roman colony of Philippi, citizens were accustomed to honoring the imperial authority of Caesar, whose name was elevated above all local political titles. For the Philippian Christians, declaring that God gave Jesus a name "above every name" was a bold statement of ultimate allegiance. Earthly rulers and local authorities held temporary power, but Jesus held supreme rank over all creation.

Furthermore, this passage draws upon the Old Testament backdrop of Isaiah 45:23, where God declares that every knee will bow to Him alone. By applying this supreme honor to Jesus, Paul reveals that the exalted Christ shares the full divine glory and sovereignty of God Himself.
