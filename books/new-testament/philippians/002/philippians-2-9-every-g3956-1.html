EVERY - (pâs, Strong's G3956)

Summary:

The Greek word pas basic meaning is "all," "every," "whole," or "entire." It describes total inclusion within a given set, leaving nothing out. Depending on how it is used in a sentence, it can refer to every individual unit of a group or the total sum of something.

In Philippians 2:9, the word is translated as "every" in the phrase "the name which is above every name." Here, it highlights the absolute, supreme exaltation of Jesus Christ. Paul uses the word to demonstrate that no title, authority, rank, or power in creation stands above the exalted name of Jesus.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word pas is one of the most common terms in the Greek language for expressing completeness and totality. In everyday ancient Greek, people used it to speak of whole groups, entire regions, or every single instance of a particular item. It allowed speakers and writers to make comprehensive statements without reservation.

In biblical writing, the word serves a central role in describing the vast reach of God's work, the universal nature of human need, and the absolute authority of Jesus Christ. When scripture speaks of God's sovereignty or Christ's lordship, this word frequently appears to show that divine authority covers everything that exists, without exception.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word pas can mean "all," "every," "any," or "the whole." Its precise nuance depends on the words around it and how it fits into the sentence:

  • When paired with a singular noun without an article, it usually means "every" individual instance of that noun (for example, "every name" or "every tree").

  • When paired with a plural noun, it generally means "all" members of that group (such as "all people" or "all nations").

  • When describing a singular concept, it can mean "the whole" or "entirety" of that thing (such as "the whole body").

In Philippians 2:9, the word appears in the neuter singular form modifying the noun "name." It means "every single name" or "any name that exists." It creates a total and unreserved comparison: among all names or titles that could ever be named, Christ's name stands elevated above them all.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, pas establishes scope. Sometimes scripture uses it in a absolute sense to mean every single unit without exception. At other times, it refers to all types or kinds within a specific category depending on the context.

When applied to divine truth, the word frequently carries absolute weight. It leaves no room for exceptions when describing God's knowledge, Christ's supreme authority, or the universal scope of divine judgment. By using this simple word, biblical writers explicitly shut the door on any competing authority or limitations to God's reign.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of pas in Philippians 2:9 secures the foundational truth of Christ's cosmic lordship. Paul does not merely state that Jesus received a great name or an honored title; he asserts that Jesus received a name positioned above every rival name.

This exaltation is the divine response to Christ's voluntary humiliation. The One who humbled Himself by taking the form of a servant and obeying God even unto death on a cross was exalted by God to the highest position. By declaring His name above "every" name, Scripture reveals that no earthly ruler, demonic power, angelic majesty, or human authority holds a rank that can rival Jesus Christ. His lordship is absolute and supreme over all creation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Philippians 2:9 serves as the turning point in the famous passage known as the Christ Hymn (Philippians 2:5–11). Verses 5 through 8 trace the dramatic downward movement of Christ: from sharing the form of God, to taking the form of a bondservant, to suffering the shameful death of the cross.

Verse 9 marks the dramatic shift back upward with the word "Therefore." Because Christ lowered Himself completely, God exalted Him completely. The accusative singular neuter form of pas fits directly into the precessional statement "above every name."

Paul deliberately places "every" here to establish a complete contrast with Christ's prior humiliation. In His earthly ministry, Jesus surrendered His reputation and took the lowest place. In His resurrection and ascension, God assigned Him the highest place. By stating that His name is above "every" name, Paul prepares the reader for the climax in verses 10–11, where every knee must bow and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Colossians 1:4 – "...since we heard of your faith in Christ Jesus and of your love for all the saints..."

  • Titus 1:16 – "They profess to know God, but in works they deny Him, being abominable, disobedient, and disqualified for every good work."

  • 1 John 3:20 – "For if our heart condemns us, God is greater than our heart, and knows all things."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The phrase "the name which is above every name" links directly to the Old Testament revelation of God's unique identity. In Isaiah 45:23, YHWH declares that to Him every knee shall bow and every tongue take an oath. By applying this exact language to Jesus in Philippians 2:9–11, Paul identifies Jesus with the unique sovereignty of God Himself.

The word "every" reinforces this connection. If any name, title, or authority remained outside or equal to Christ's authority, He would not possess the supreme name above every name. The total inclusion of this word leaves no room for idol worship, ruler cults, or spiritual fear, assuring believers that Christ reigns supreme over every power in existence.
