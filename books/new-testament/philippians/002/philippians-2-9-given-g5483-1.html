GIVEN - (charízomai, Strong's G5483)

Summary:

The verb charizomai means to give freely, bestow favor graciously, or pardon a debt. It describes an act of generous, unmerited giving that flows from goodwill rather than obligation or earned reward. In Philippians 2:9, the word describes how God the Father freely and graciously bestowed upon Jesus Christ—following His humble obedience unto death on the cross—the name that is above every name, exalting Him to the place of supreme honor.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word charizomai comes from a root closely connected to the concept of grace and unearned favor. In everyday ancient Greek, the term was used when someone granted a favor, canceled an outstanding financial debt, or bestowed an unearned gift upon another person. In the New Testament, this background carries over into two main areas of life: God's generous bestowal of spiritual blessings and believers forgiving one another freely.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Charizomai can refer to giving something freely as a gift, bestowing a special favor, or forgiving a wrong or debt. When a person acts out of generous goodwill rather than debt or duty, this word describes that giving. When God is the subject, it describes His actions in giving spiritual blessings, granting deliverance, or bestowing supreme honor. In Philippians 2:9, the word means to graciously grant or bestow upon Jesus Christ a position and title of supreme honor.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the New Testament, charizomai expresses two primary actions, both rooted in free favor:

  • Graciously bestowing gifts or favors: God freely gives believers all things necessary for life and salvation, or grants an individual physical freedom or protection.

  • Canceling debts or forgiving offenses: A creditor pardons a debtor who cannot pay, or believers forgive one another just as God in Christ forgave them.

In both usages, the focus remains on the donor’s generosity rather than any merit or claim held by the recipient.

4. Theological Implications

In Philippians 2:9, charizomai marks a pivotal turning point in the biblical narrative of Jesus Christ. After describing Jesus' voluntary self-emptying, His taking the form of a servant, and His obedience to the point of death on a cross, the text transitions to His exaltation. The Father's bestowal of "the name which is above every name" serves as the supreme divine answer to the Son's earthly humiliation.

This gift is not a wage earned in a commercial transaction, but the Father's supreme, loving vindication of His Son. The supreme authority given to Jesus reflects the Father's delight in the Son's complete obedience.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Philippians 2:5–11, Paul presents the pattern of Christ's humility as a model for Christian fellowship. Jesus did not insist on retaining His high standing, but humbled Himself completely. Because of this self-giving obedience, God exalted Him to the highest place and "given" Him the supreme title.

The grammatical form of the verb presents this action as a single, decisive historical event linked to Christ's resurrection and ascension. The Father graciously bestows upon the incarnate Son the title of "Lord" (Kyrios), declaring to the entire universe that Jesus Christ reigns over all creation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Romans 8:32 — "He who did not spare His own Son, but delivered Him up for us all, how shall He not with Him also freely give us all things?"

  • Philippians 1:29 — "For to you it has been granted on behalf of Christ, not only to believe in Him, but also to suffer for His sake,"

  • 1 Corinthians 2:12 — "Now we have received, not the spirit of the world, but the Spirit who is from God, that we might know the things that have been freely given to us by God."

  • Ephesians 4:32 — "And be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God in Christ forgave you."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The placement of charizomai in Philippians 2 highlights a striking contrast between human ambition and divine action. While human beings often seek honor through self-exaltation and grasping for status, Jesus chose the path of lower servant-humility. God the Father then responded by bestowing upon Him the highest honor imaginable. True honor in God's kingdom comes from divine bestowal, not human grasping.
