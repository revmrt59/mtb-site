NAME - (ónoma, Strong's G3686)

Summary:

The word refers to a proper name, title, reputation, or authority. In Philippians 2:9, it refers to the supreme title and high status that God bestowed upon the exalted Christ after His humble obedience unto death, showing that Jesus possesses the supreme authority that belongs to God Himself.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient Greco-Roman and Jewish worlds, a name was far more than an arbitrary label used to identify someone. A person's name carried their reputation, character, honor, and authority. To act in someone's name meant to carry their authority and act as their representative.

When Paul wrote his letter to the believers in Philippi, he was encouraging a small Christian community living in a Roman colony to remain unified and humble. Roman society was deeply concerned with social status, titles, and public honor. In Philippians 2, Paul turns this cultural obsession with status upside down by pointing to Jesus Christ, who set aside His divine privileges, took the humble form of a servant, and died on a cross. In response, God exalted Him to the highest place.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word onoma refers primarily to a proper name used to identify a specific person or place.

Depending on how it is used, the word can mean:

  • A personal name used to identify an individual.

  • A person's reputation, fame, or standing.

  • Authority or official representation, as when someone speaks or acts under another person's authorization.

  • A title or position of honor and rank.

In Philippians 2:9, the word refers to the supreme title and position of rank that God gave to Jesus following His resurrection and ascension.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the New Testament, onoma appears in several distinct ways:

  • Personal Identification: It is used simply to give the name of a person, such as naming a newborn child or identifying a specific individual.

  • Authority and Agency: It describes doing an action by someone's authority, such as gathering, praying, or commanding spirits in the name of Jesus.

  • Reputation and Honor: It can refer to the standing or fame a person holds among others.

The word itself carries these ordinary senses. The specific nuance—whether a personal label, delegated authority, or high honor—is determined by the surrounding sentence and passage.

4. Theological Implications

In Philippians 2:9, the bestowing of "the name which is above every name" highlights the fatherly vindication of Jesus Christ. Jesus chose the path of lower status, taking on humanity and dying a criminal's death. Because of His obedience, God raised Him up and bestowed upon Him supreme divine honor.

The surrounding verses show that this supreme title is connected to the declaration that "Jesus Christ is Lord" (Philippians 2:11). In the Old Testament, the title "Lord" (Kyrios) was used to translate the sacred personal name of God (Yahweh). By granting Jesus the title above all titles, God publicly confirms that Jesus shares divine authority and reign over all creation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Philippians 2:5–11 forms a powerful movement from humility to exaltation. In verses 5–8, Jesus empties Himself, assumes human nature, acts as a servant, and submits to death on a cross.

Verse 9 marks the turning point in the passage. The focus shifts from what Christ did to what God does: "Therefore God also has highly exalted Him and given Him the name which is above every name."

Grammatically, the word onoma functions as a singular noun in the accusative case, serving as the direct object of God's act of giving. The grammar presents the name as the direct gift granted to Christ by God the Father.

The immediate context shapes the exact weight of the word here:

  • The expression "above every name" establishes that this title surpasses every other earthly or heavenly authority.

  • Verses 10 and 11 explain the consequence of this gift: every knee in heaven, on earth, and under the earth must bow, and every tongue must confess His lordship.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Paul's explanation of Christ's supreme name serves a direct pastoral purpose in Philippians. Paul uses Christ's example to show the Philippians that path to true honor in God's kingdom is humble service, not self-exaltation.

Furthermore, Paul's language in Philippians 2:10–11 explicitly reflects Isaiah 45:23, where Yahweh declares that every knee will bow and every tongue will swear allegiance to Him alone. By applying this Old Testament imagery to Jesus, Paul makes clear that the "name" given to Jesus is nothing less than the share in divine glory and universal sovereignty.
