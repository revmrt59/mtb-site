Chapter Study

Revelation 5

Verse-by-Verse
Revelation 5:1

NKJV

Revelation 5:1 And I saw in the right hand of Him who sat on the throne a scroll written inside and on the back, sealed with seven seals.(NKJV)

NLT

Revelation 5:1 Then I saw a scroll in the right hand of the one who was sitting on the throne. There was writing on the inside and the outside of the scroll, and it was sealed with seven seals. (NLT)

Main Idea


John sees a completely inscribed scroll in God’s right hand, sealed with seven seals, introducing the central question of who has the authority and worthiness to open it and carry God’s purposes forward.

Verse Explanation


“And I saw in the right hand of Him who sat on the throne” — The vision continues directly from chapter 4. God remains seated upon the throne, but John’s attention is now drawn to something in His right hand. The right hand commonly represents power and authority. The scroll therefore belongs to God and remains completely under His control. What is about to unfold in Revelation begins with something held by the One who rules from heaven.

“a scroll written inside and on the back” — The scroll contains writing on both sides. Ancient scrolls were normally written primarily on the inside, making writing on both sides noteworthy. The picture suggests a document filled completely with its contents. John does not yet tell us exactly what is written upon it; its significance will become clearer as the seals are opened and God’s purposes begin to unfold.

“sealed with seven seals” — The scroll is securely closed with seven seals. In the ancient world, seals could authenticate and secure documents, preventing unauthorized access. Revelation’s characteristic use of seven strengthens the picture of complete sealing. No one simply takes this scroll and opens it. The question is not merely whether someone has enough strength to break the seals, but whether anyone possesses the necessary worthiness and authority to do so.

Study Notes


“Scroll” translates biblion (G975), which can refer to a scroll, book, or written document. In John’s setting, a scroll is the natural picture.

“Written inside and on the back” recalls Ezekiel 2:9–10, where Ezekiel receives a scroll written on both sides containing “lamentations and mourning and woe.” The parallel is significant because both passages introduce prophetic revelations involving divine judgment, though the contents and function of Revelation’s scroll should be determined from Revelation itself.

“Sealed” translates katasphragizō (G2696), carrying the sense of sealing securely. This verb occurs only here in the New Testament.

The precise identification of the scroll has produced several interpretations, including a testament, title deed, divine decree, or comprehensive record of God’s redemptive and judicial purposes. Revelation itself does not give the scroll one of those formal titles. What the narrative makes clear is that opening its seals initiates the events through which God’s purposes in judgment and redemption move toward their appointed completion.

Spirit & Life


Chapter 4 established the foundational reality that God is on the throne. Chapter 5 now shows that the unfolding of history is also in His hand.

John will soon see judgments, conflict, suffering, and forces that appear to threaten everything. But before any seal is opened, he sees the scroll securely held by God. History is not a loose scroll lying beyond anyone’s control. What must unfold remains in the hand of the One seated on the throne.

Yet possession of the scroll is only the beginning of the scene. The great question will be: Who is worthy to open it? John will discover that the answer is Jesus—the Lion who has conquered and the Lamb who was slain.

Before Revelation shows us what happens, it establishes who holds history and who alone is worthy to bring God’s purposes to completion.

Revelation 5:2

NKJV

Revelation 5:2 Then I saw a strong angel proclaiming with a loud voice, "Who is worthy to open the scroll and to loose its seals?"(NKJV)

NLT

Revelation 5:2 And I saw a strong angel, who shouted with a loud voice: “Who is worthy to break the seals on this scroll and open it?” (NLT)

Main Idea


A mighty angel issues a challenge throughout creation: who possesses the worthiness and authority necessary to open God’s scroll and break its seals?

Verse Explanation


“Then I saw a strong angel” — John now sees a powerful angel entering the scene. The angel’s strength fits the enormous scope of the announcement he is about to make. Yet even this “strong angel” does not step forward to open the scroll. Strength alone is not sufficient. The central issue will be worthiness.

“proclaiming with a loud voice” — The angel’s proclamation is intended to reach throughout the heavenly scene and beyond. This is not a private question directed toward one particular group. Verse 3 will extend the search through heaven, earth, and under the earth. The loud proclamation creates a universal challenge: is there anyone anywhere who qualifies?

“Who is worthy to open the scroll” — The crucial word is “worthy.” The angel does not ask, “Who is strong enough?” Opening the scroll requires more than power. Someone must possess the rightful character, position, and authority to take what is in God’s hand and carry forward the purposes contained within it.

This prepares for the revelation of Jesus in verse 5. The search is not ultimately for someone who can overpower the seals, but for the One who has accomplished what makes Him uniquely worthy.

“and to loose its seals?” — The seven seals introduced in verse 1 prevent the scroll from being opened. Breaking them will progressively set into motion the events beginning in chapter 6. The authority to loose these seals therefore carries enormous significance. Whoever opens the scroll participates in bringing God’s purposes for judgment, redemption, and the completion of His rule toward their appointed fulfillment.

Study Notes


“Strong” translates ischyros (G2478), meaning strong, powerful, or mighty. Revelation uses the term elsewhere for powerful angelic figures (Revelation 10:1; 18:21).

“Proclaiming” translates kēryssō (G2784), meaning to proclaim or announce publicly. Together with “a loud voice,” the wording presents the angel’s question as a public summons rather than a private inquiry.

“Worthy” translates axios (G514), the same word used in Revelation 4:11, where God is declared worthy to receive glory, honor, and power because He created all things. In chapter 5, worthiness becomes the central question surrounding the scroll and ultimately the Lamb.

The two actions—“open the scroll” and “loose its seals”—belong together. The seals must be broken before the scroll can be opened and its contents brought forward. Revelation 6 will begin describing the consequences as the Lamb opens the seals.

Spirit & Life


The question echoing through heaven is not simply, “Who has power?” It is “Who is worthy?”

That distinction prepares us to understand Jesus correctly when He appears in this chapter. Revelation does not present Him merely as someone stronger than every competing power. He possesses something no other person or heavenly being possesses: the unique worthiness to take the scroll from the hand of God and open it.

Soon John will learn why. Jesus has overcome, and heaven will declare Him worthy because He was slain and has redeemed people to God by His blood (Revelation 5:5, 9).

Before John sees the Lamb, however, the question must be allowed to stand. Heaven, earth, and everything beneath the earth are about to be searched, and no created being will qualify. God’s purposes require someone worthy—and Revelation is preparing to show us that Jesus alone is that One.

Revelation 5:3

NKJV

Revelation 5:3 And no one in heaven or on the earth or under the earth was able to open the scroll, or to look at it.(NKJV)

NLT

Revelation 5:3 But no one in heaven or on earth or under the earth was able to open the scroll and read it. (NLT)

Main Idea


The search extends throughout all creation, but no created being is found capable of opening the scroll or even looking into it.

Verse Explanation


“And no one in heaven or on the earth or under the earth” — The angel’s question from verse 2 receives an initially devastating answer. The search is described in universal terms: heaven, earth, and under the earth. John’s point is comprehensive. No created being, regardless of position or realm, possesses the worthiness required to take and open the scroll.

“was able to open the scroll” — The inability is deeper than simply not having enough physical strength to break the seals. Verse 2 established the essential qualification: “Who is worthy?” No angel, human ruler, spiritual power, or other created being can step forward with the right to open what God holds in His hand. Creation cannot accomplish what God’s purposes require.

“or to look at it” — The inability is so complete that no one can even look into the scroll to examine its contents. The language heightens the absolute failure of the search. No created being can approach the scroll as the one qualified to disclose and execute what it contains.

Study Notes


“No one” translates oudeis (G3762), an emphatic negative meaning no one or nobody. The same word continues prominently through verses 3–4 before the Lamb is revealed as the answer.

“In heaven or on the earth or under the earth” is a comprehensive description of the created order. Similar three-part language appears in Philippians 2:10, where every knee in these realms bows at the name of Jesus.

“Was able” translates a form of dynamai (G1410), meaning to be able or have the capacity to do something. In context, the inability is governed by the question of worthiness in verse 2. No one in creation possesses the qualification necessary to open the scroll.

The phrase “or to look at it” should be read with verse 4, where John weeps because no one is found worthy “to open and read the scroll, or to look at it.” The repeated language intensifies the apparent hopelessness before the identity of the worthy One is revealed.

Spirit & Life


Revelation 5 deliberately allows the search to reach a dead end before revealing Jesus. Heaven is searched. Earth is searched. Everything beneath the earth is searched. No one is found.

That prepares us to understand the absolute uniqueness of Christ.

Jesus is not simply the best candidate among many possible candidates. He does what no one else in all creation can do. No angel can take His place. No earthly ruler can take His place. No spiritual power can take His place. God’s purposes for creation cannot be brought to completion by creation itself.

The emptiness of the search makes the revelation that follows even greater. John is about to hear, “Do not weep. Behold, the Lion of the tribe of Judah… has prevailed” (Revelation 5:5).

Before Revelation shows us that Jesus is worthy, it shows us that Jesus is irreplaceable. What all creation is unable to accomplish, the Lamb alone can do.

Revelation 5:4

NKJV

Revelation 5:4 So I wept much, because no one was found worthy to open and read the scroll, or to look at it.(NKJV)

NLT

Revelation 5:4 Then I began to weep bitterly because no one was found worthy to open the scroll and read it. (NLT)

Main Idea


John weeps deeply because no one has been found worthy to open the scroll, revealing how much depends upon someone being able to carry God’s purposes forward.

Verse Explanation


“So I wept much” — John does not respond to the failed search with mild disappointment. He weeps intensely. He has seen the scroll in God’s hand and heard the universal challenge asking who is worthy to open it, but the search has produced no one. John understands enough about the significance of the scroll to recognize that its remaining sealed would be a devastating outcome.

“because no one was found worthy” — John’s sorrow centers on the absence of someone worthy. This repeats the controlling question of verse 2. The problem is not that someone needs greater physical strength to break the seals. No one within the entire created order possesses the qualification and authority required to take the scroll and open it.

“to open and read the scroll” — John longs for the scroll to be opened and its contents disclosed. The unfolding of God’s purposes represented by the scroll cannot proceed while it remains sealed. At this moment in the vision, the situation appears hopeless.

“or to look at it” — The language repeats and intensifies verse 3. No one has been found who can even approach the scroll as the qualified one who may examine its contents. The search has failed completely. This creates the dramatic tension that will be resolved immediately in verse 5 when one of the elders directs John’s attention to the One who has prevailed.

Study Notes


“Wept much” translates eklaion poly. The verb klaiō (G2799) describes weeping or mourning, while poly intensifies it. John is deeply affected by the apparent impossibility of opening the scroll.

“Was found worthy” continues the emphasis on axios (G514), “worthy.” Revelation 5 repeatedly stresses worthiness because the authority to open the scroll belongs not merely to someone powerful but to someone uniquely qualified.

There is a textual variation involving “and read” in the phrase “to open and read the scroll.” Some manuscripts include the wording reflected in the NKJV, while many modern critical texts read simply “to open the scroll or to look at it.” The central meaning is unchanged: no one has been found qualified to access the scroll.

John’s weeping provides the emotional transition between the unsuccessful search of verses 2–4 and the announcement of Christ’s victory in verse 5. His sorrow makes the elder’s command “Do not weep” especially forceful.

Spirit & Life


For a brief moment, John sees a world in which the scroll remains sealed. God’s purposes appear unable to move forward because no one is worthy. John’s response is tears.

But John is weeping before he has seen the whole picture.

While John sees no solution, heaven already knows there is One. The next words he hears are not an explanation of why the situation is hopeless, but “Do not weep. Behold, the Lion of the tribe of Judah… has prevailed.”

Revelation allows us to feel John’s despair so that we can more fully appreciate the sufficiency of Jesus. The answer is not that another search eventually discovers someone qualified. The answer is Christ. He has already prevailed.

There are moments when what we can presently see appears to offer no answer. Revelation 5:4–5 directs our attention beyond the apparent impossibility to Jesus. John’s tears are real, but they are not the final word. The victorious Christ is.

Revelation 5:5

NKJV

Revelation 5:5 But one of the elders said to me, "Do not weep. Behold, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has prevailed to open the scroll and to loose its seven seals."(NKJV)

NLT

Revelation 5:5 But one of the twenty-four elders said to me, “Stop weeping! Look, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the heir to David’s throne, has won the victory. He is worthy to open the scroll and its seven seals.” (NLT)

Main Idea


John is told to stop weeping because Jesus—the promised Lion from Judah and Root of David—has conquered and is therefore worthy to open the scroll and its seven seals.

Verse Explanation


“But one of the elders said to me, ‘Do not weep.’” — John’s deep sorrow in verse 4 is interrupted by one of the twenty-four elders. The elder does not tell John that the scroll is unimportant or that his concern is misplaced. He tells him to stop weeping because the apparent problem has already been answered. Someone worthy has been found.

“Behold, the Lion of the tribe of Judah” — John is directed to look at Jesus through the imagery of a lion from Judah. This reaches back to Jacob’s blessing over Judah in Genesis 49:9–10, where Judah is compared to a lion and the promise of rule is associated with his line. Jesus is the promised royal Messiah from the tribe of Judah. The title emphasizes His strength, kingship, and victory.

“the Root of David” — This title draws upon the messianic hope of Isaiah 11, where a coming ruler arises from the family line of Jesse, David’s father. Revelation calls Jesus not merely David’s descendant but the “Root of David.” He is the promised Davidic King, yet His identity is greater than ordinary human descent can explain. Revelation will use the title again in 22:16, where Jesus identifies Himself as both “the Root and the Offspring of David.”

“has prevailed” — This is the reason John no longer needs to weep. Jesus has conquered. Significantly, Revelation will immediately reveal that His victory was accomplished through His sacrificial death: John hears about a conquering Lion, but in verse 6 he turns and sees a Lamb as though it had been slain. The Lion’s victory and the Lamb’s sacrifice are not competing pictures. Jesus conquered through His faithful obedience, death, and triumph over death.

“to open the scroll and to loose its seven seals” — Because Jesus has prevailed, He possesses the unique worthiness required to take the scroll and open its seals. The universal search of verses 2–4 has ended. What no one in heaven, on earth, or under the earth could do, Jesus can do. God’s purposes will move forward through the victorious Messiah.

Study Notes


“Lion of the tribe of Judah” draws directly upon Genesis 49:9–10. Jacob describes Judah as a lion and connects Judah with enduring royal rule. Revelation applies this royal-messianic expectation to Jesus.

“Root of David” echoes Isaiah 11:1, 10. Isaiah speaks of a shoot from Jesse and a “Root of Jesse” associated with the coming messianic ruler. Revelation identifies Jesus as the fulfillment of this Davidic expectation.

“Has prevailed” translates enikēsen, from nikaō (G3528), meaning to conquer, overcome, or prevail. This is the same verb repeatedly used in Revelation 2–3 for the one who “overcomes.” Here Jesus is the supreme Overcomer whose victory makes Him worthy to open the scroll.

The movement from Lion in verse 5 to Lamb in verse 6 is crucial to Revelation’s presentation of Christ. John hears that the Lion has conquered, but when he looks, he sees a slain Lamb standing alive. Revelation interprets Christ’s messianic victory through His sacrificial death and resurrection.

Spirit & Life


John was weeping because no one was worthy. Heaven’s answer is not, “Things are not as bad as they seem.” Heaven’s answer is, “Behold”—look at Jesus.

He is the Lion who “has prevailed.” His victory is not something He is still trying to achieve. The decisive work has already been accomplished. Yet when John turns to see this victorious Lion, he will see a Lamb bearing the marks of sacrifice. Jesus conquered in a way the world would never expect: through obedient surrender, sacrificial death, and resurrection victory.

This gives Revelation’s picture of power a distinctly Christ-centered meaning. The greatest victory in history came through the cross. What looked like defeat became the means through which Christ conquered sin and death.

John’s tears give way when his attention is redirected to the victorious Christ. That is the movement of this verse: from weeping to beholding, from apparent impossibility to the One who has already prevailed.

Revelation 5:6

NKJV

Revelation 5:6 And I looked, and behold, in the midst of the throne and of the four living creatures, and in the midst of the elders, stood a Lamb as though it had been slain, having seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God sent out into all the earth.(NKJV)

NLT

Revelation 5:6 Then I saw a Lamb that looked as if it had been slaughtered, but it was now standing between the throne and the four living beings and among the twenty-four elders. He had seven horns and seven eyes, which represent the sevenfold Spirit of God that is sent out into every part of the earth. (NLT)

Main Idea


John turns expecting the conquering Lion and sees Jesus as a slain yet living Lamb, possessing complete power and the fullness of God’s Spirit.

Verse Explanation


“And I looked, and behold, in the midst of the throne” — The elder has just told John to behold “the Lion of the tribe of Judah” who has prevailed. When John looks, his attention is drawn again to the very center of the heavenly throne-room scene. The One who is worthy to open the scroll is not standing at the margins. He appears in the midst of the throne and the worship surrounding it.

“and of the four living creatures, and in the midst of the elders” — The Lamb stands at the center of the heavenly assembly John has been observing since chapter 4. The living creatures and elders surround the throne, and now Jesus appears among them in the place of highest significance. The vision is shifting its focus directly onto Him.

“stood a Lamb as though it had been slain” — Here is the great surprise of the vision. John was told to look for a Lion, but he sees a Lamb. Even more strikingly, the Lamb bears the appearance of having been slain, yet He is standing. Jesus bears the marks of sacrificial death while standing alive and victorious. His death was not the defeat of the Lion; it was central to His victory. The cross and resurrection belong together in Revelation’s picture of the conquering Christ.

“having seven horns” — In biblical imagery, horns frequently represent strength and power. The number seven communicates completeness. The Lamb therefore possesses complete power. His appearance as sacrificed does not mean weakness or helplessness. The slain Lamb possesses perfect authority and power.

“and seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God sent out into all the earth” — John immediately identifies the seven eyes with “the seven Spirits of God,” the same symbolic language used in Revelation 4:5 for the fullness of God’s Spirit. Here the Spirit is described as being sent throughout the earth. The Lamb’s activity is therefore presented in close relationship with the fullness and worldwide work of the Spirit of God.

Study Notes


“Lamb” translates arnion (G721), a diminutive form associated with a lamb. It becomes one of Revelation’s dominant titles for Jesus, occurring repeatedly throughout the book. Revelation intentionally joins sacrificial imagery with royal and victorious authority.

“As though it had been slain” uses sphazō (G4969), meaning to slaughter or slay, especially in a sacrificial or violent sense. The Lamb stands alive while visibly bearing evidence of having been slain, creating a vivid picture of death followed by victory.

“Horns” commonly symbolize power or ruling strength in Old Testament imagery (for example, 1 Samuel 2:10; Daniel 7–8). The Lamb’s seven horns therefore present His power as complete rather than limited.

“Seven Spirits of God” appeared previously in Revelation 1:4; 3:1; and 4:5. Here they are explicitly associated with the Lamb and are “sent out into all the earth.” The wording also recalls Zechariah 4:10, where the “eyes of the LORD” range throughout the whole earth.

The contrast between what John hears in verse 5 and what he sees in verse 6 is significant. He hears “Lion of the tribe of Judah” but sees “a Lamb as though it had been slain.” Revelation does not replace the Lion with the Lamb; it reveals what the Lion’s victory looks like. Christ has conquered through sacrificial death and now stands alive.

Spirit & Life


Revelation 5:6 gives us one of Scripture’s most powerful pictures of Jesus: the Lamb was slain, but the Lamb is standing.

His wounds are evidence of sacrifice, not defeat. The cross did not derail His victory; His sacrificial death was central to it. The One who gave Himself completely now stands in heaven possessing complete power.

This changes the way Revelation teaches us to understand victory. Jesus conquers differently from the powers of this world. The world conquers by crushing its enemies; Jesus conquered by giving Himself. Yet the sacrificed Lamb did not remain in death. He stands.

John had been told, “Behold, the Lion.” When he looked, he discovered the Lion’s strength revealed through the slain Lamb. Revelation wants that image fixed in our understanding before the seals begin to open: the One directing what comes next is Jesus Christ—the sacrificed, risen, all-powerful Lamb.

Revelation 5:7

NKJV

Revelation 5:7 Then He came and took the scroll out of the right hand of Him who sat on the throne.(NKJV)

NLT

Revelation 5:7 He stepped forward and took the scroll from the right hand of the one sitting on the throne. (NLT)

Main Idea


The Lamb steps forward and takes the scroll from God’s right hand, demonstrating that Jesus alone possesses the authority and worthiness to carry God’s purposes forward.

Verse Explanation


“Then He came” — The Lamb John saw standing in the midst of the throne now moves toward the One seated upon it. The entire search of verses 2–5 has been leading to this moment. No one else in heaven, on earth, or under the earth could approach the scroll as the one worthy to open it. Jesus can.

“and took the scroll” — Jesus does what no created being was able to do. He takes possession of the scroll that had remained sealed in God’s hand. This action demonstrates His unique authority. The Lamb is not merely permitted to observe God’s purposes; He is entrusted with carrying them forward. Beginning in chapter 6, He will personally open the seals one by one.

“out of the right hand” — The scroll has been held in the right hand of God since verse 1. Its location emphasized that it remained under God’s sovereign authority. The Lamb taking it does not represent Jesus seizing authority from God. Rather, the Father entrusts the scroll to the Son who has been declared worthy to receive and open it.

“of Him who sat on the throne” — Revelation continues to preserve the throne as the center of the scene. God remains seated upon it while the Lamb receives the scroll from His hand. The action reveals perfect agreement between the One seated on the throne and the Lamb. God’s purposes will now be carried forward through the victorious Christ.

Study Notes


“Took” translates lambanō (G2983), meaning to take, receive, or take into one’s possession. The action is repeated in verse 8—“when He had taken the scroll”—showing that this moment triggers the explosion of heavenly worship that follows.

The movement of Revelation 5 is carefully structured: the scroll is seen in God’s hand (v. 1), a worthy person is sought (vv. 2–4), Christ is announced and revealed as worthy (vv. 5–6), and the Lamb now takes the scroll (v. 7). The remainder of the chapter celebrates His worthiness.

The action also prepares directly for Revelation 6:1, where the Lamb begins opening the seven seals. Taking the scroll and opening its seals belong to the authority He possesses because He has prevailed.

Spirit & Life


Revelation 5:7 is brief, but heaven’s response in the following verses shows how significant this moment is. Jesus takes the scroll. The search is over.

The Lamb who bears the marks of sacrifice is the One into whose hands God places the unfolding of His purposes. The future does not belong ultimately to earthly rulers, hostile spiritual powers, or circumstances that appear uncontrollable. The scroll is placed in the hands of Jesus.

And these are wounded hands—the hands of the Lamb who was slain.

That means the One exercising authority over what follows is the same One who gave Himself for redemption. His power cannot be separated from His sacrificial love, and His sacrifice cannot be mistaken for weakness. The Lamb who gave Himself is now the Lamb who takes the scroll.

Before a single seal is opened, Revelation makes clear whose hands hold what comes next: the hands of the victorious Jesus.

Revelation 5:8

NKJV

Revelation 5:8 Now when He had taken the scroll, the four living creatures and the twenty-four elders fell down before the Lamb, each having a harp, and golden bowls full of incense, which are the prayers of the saints.(NKJV)

NLT

Revelation 5:8 And when he took the scroll, the four living beings and the twenty-four elders fell down before the Lamb. Each one had a harp, and they held gold bowls filled with incense, which are the prayers of God’s people. (NLT)

Main Idea


When the Lamb takes the scroll, heaven responds in worship, and the prayers of God’s people are pictured as precious incense held before Him.

Verse Explanation


“Now when He had taken the scroll” — The response is immediate. Jesus has just done what no one else in all creation was worthy to do: He has taken the scroll from the right hand of God. Heaven recognizes the enormous significance of this act. Before the Lamb opens even one seal, worship erupts around Him.

“the four living creatures and the twenty-four elders fell down before the Lamb” — In chapter 4, these heavenly worshipers fell before the One seated on the throne. Now they fall before the Lamb. Jesus receives the worship and reverence of the heavenly throne room. The Lamb who appeared slain is not treated merely as an honored servant of God; He stands at the center of heaven’s worship.

“each having a harp” — The elders are pictured with harps, instruments associated in Scripture with praise and worship. Their response to the Lamb taking the scroll is therefore not merely physical prostration but worship expressed through music as well.

“and golden bowls full of incense” — Alongside their harps, the elders hold golden bowls filled with incense. Incense had a significant place in Old Testament worship, where its fragrance ascended before God. John does not leave the meaning of these bowls uncertain; he immediately explains what the incense represents.

“which are the prayers of the saints” — The incense represents the prayers of God’s people. This is a remarkable detail in the throne-room vision. The prayers offered by believers on earth are pictured as present before God in heaven. They have not disappeared, been forgotten, or been treated as insignificant. They are held in golden bowls in the very setting where God’s purposes are about to unfold.

Study Notes


“Fell down” translates piptō (G4098), the same verb used of the elders falling before God in Revelation 4:10. Here the living creatures and elders fall before the Lamb, contributing to Revelation’s presentation of Jesus as sharing in the worship given in the heavenly throne room.

“Harp” translates kithara (G2788), a stringed instrument frequently associated with music and worship. Harps appear again in Revelation 14:2 and 15:2.

“Bowls” translates phialai, from phialē (G5357), referring to broad, shallow bowls. Later in Revelation these bowls become associated with God’s judgments, but here they contain incense representing prayer.

“Incense” translates thymiamata, from thymiama (G2368), referring to incense or fragrant material burned as an offering. The connection between incense and prayer also appears in Psalm 141:2 and is developed again in Revelation 8:3–4, where incense and the prayers of the saints ascend before God.

“Saints” translates hagioi, from hagios (G40), meaning holy or set-apart ones. In Revelation, the term regularly refers to God’s people rather than to a special class of unusually holy Christians.

Spirit & Life


Revelation 5:8 brings something deeply personal into this immense heavenly scene: the prayers of God’s people are there.

John has seen the throne, lightning and thunder, living creatures, elders, and the Lamb taking the scroll that will move God’s purposes forward. Yet in the middle of this cosmic scene, God deliberately shows John golden bowls full of the prayers of the saints.

Some of those prayers may have been offered from places of suffering, persecution, waiting, and apparent unansweredness. Revelation does not portray them as lost words spoken into the air. They are precious before God and present before His throne.

That becomes especially important as the seals begin to open. God’s people may not yet see how their prayers relate to what He is doing, but heaven has not forgotten them. What feels unheard on earth may already be held before God in heaven.

The Lamb has taken the scroll, God’s purposes are moving forward, and the prayers of His people are right there before Him.

Revelation 5:9

NKJV

Revelation 5:9 And they sang a new song, saying: "You are worthy to take the scroll, And to open its seals; For You were slain, And have redeemed us to God by Your blood Out of every tribe and tongue and people and nation,(NKJV)

NLT

Revelation 5:9 And they sang a new song with these words: “You are worthy to take the scrolland break its seals and open it.For you were slaughtered, and your blood has ransomed people for Godfrom every tribe and language and people and nation. (NLT)

Main Idea


Heaven sings a new song declaring that Jesus is worthy to open the scroll because through His sacrificial death He purchased people for God from every part of humanity.

Verse Explanation


“And they sang a new song, saying:” — The Lamb taking the scroll produces a new expression of worship. In the Old Testament, a “new song” often celebrates a fresh or decisive act of God’s deliverance. Here heaven sings because the Lamb has accomplished the redemptive victory that makes Him worthy to carry God’s purposes forward.

“You are worthy to take the scroll, And to open its seals” — The question that dominated verses 2–4 is now answered directly. Jesus is worthy. No one else in heaven, on earth, or under the earth qualified to take the scroll, but the Lamb does. The song now explains why His worthiness is unique.

“For You were slain” — The reason begins with the cross. The Lamb is worthy precisely because He was slain. What appeared from an earthly perspective to be humiliation and defeat is revealed from heaven’s perspective as the decisive act of His victory. This explains the connection between the Lion who “has prevailed” in verse 5 and the Lamb “as though it had been slain” in verse 6. Christ conquered through His sacrificial death.

“And have redeemed us to God by Your blood” — Jesus’ death accomplished redemption. His blood refers to His life given sacrificially in death. The Lamb did not merely provide an inspiring example of sacrifice; His death actually secured people for God. Redemption belongs at the center of heaven’s explanation of why Jesus is worthy.

“Out of every tribe and tongue and people and nation” — Christ’s redemptive work reaches across every human boundary. “Tribe” points toward family and ethnic groupings; “tongue” emphasizes languages; “people” describes distinct peoples; and “nation” encompasses the larger nations of the world. The fourfold expression emphasizes the worldwide scope of Christ’s redemption. The Lamb is gathering a people for God from every part of humanity.

Study Notes


“New song” translates ōdēn kainēn. In passages such as Psalm 96:1; 98:1; and Isaiah 42:10, a new song responds to God’s mighty saving acts. Revelation uses the expression again in 14:3.

“Were slain” translates esphagēs, from sphazō (G4969), the same verb used in verse 6 for the Lamb who appeared to have been slain. The repetition explicitly connects the Lamb’s visible wounds with the reason heaven declares Him worthy.

“Redeemed” in the NKJV translates agorazō (G59), literally “to buy” or “purchase.” The underlying picture is acquisition by payment: through His blood, Christ purchased people for God.

There is an important textual variation in “redeemed us to God.” Many early manuscripts read “You purchased for God by Your blood people from every tribe…” without the pronoun “us.” This affects whether the singers explicitly identify themselves as among the redeemed, but it does not alter the central declaration: Christ’s blood purchased people for God from every tribe, language, people, and nation.

The fourfold expression “tribe and tongue and people and nation” recurs in varying forms throughout Revelation and emphasizes the comprehensive international scope of the Lamb’s redeemed people.

Spirit & Life


Heaven answers the question “Why is Jesus worthy?” by pointing directly to the cross: “For You were slain.”

The wounds John saw on the Lamb are not merely reminders of what Jesus suffered. They testify to what His suffering accomplished. By His blood, He purchased people for God.

And look at the people His blood reaches: every tribe, tongue, people, and nation. Around the Lamb, human distinctions do not disappear, but none of them limits the reach of His redemption. Jesus is gathering people from across humanity who belong to God because of what He accomplished at the cross.

Revelation therefore places the cross at the center of heaven’s worship. Heaven does not move beyond the sacrifice of Jesus as though it were merely the beginning of the Christian story. The Lamb stands in glory still identified as the One who was slain, and His blood remains the reason for the song.

The cross that looked like defeat on earth is celebrated as victory in heaven.

Revelation 5:10

NKJV

Revelation 5:10 And have made us kings and priests to our God; And we shall reign on the earth."(NKJV)

NLT

Revelation 5:10 And you have caused them to becomea Kingdom of priests for our God.And they will reign on the earth.” (NLT)

Main Idea


The Lamb has not only redeemed people for God but has given them a royal and priestly identity, with the promise that they will share in His reign upon the earth.

Verse Explanation


“And have made us kings and priests to our God” — The song continues by describing what Christ’s redemption has accomplished for the people He purchased by His blood. They are brought into a new relationship with God and given a remarkable calling: they belong to Him as a royal and priestly people. “Kings” emphasizes participation in God’s rule, while “priests” emphasizes belonging to God and serving in His presence. Redemption is therefore not merely rescue from judgment; Christ restores His people to a life of worship, service, and participation in His kingdom.

“to our God” — Their new position remains completely God-centered. Whatever authority or priestly privilege they possess is “to our God.” This continues the emphasis of verse 9, where the Lamb purchased people “to God” by His blood. Christ’s redemption brings people back into the purpose for which they belong to God.

“And we shall reign on the earth” — The song looks forward to the future reign of God’s redeemed people with Christ. Their present suffering and apparent weakness are not the end of their story. Revelation will later develop this promise, particularly in Revelation 20:4–6 and 22:5. The kingdom toward which Revelation moves does not end with God’s people escaping the earth; it includes their sharing in Christ’s victorious rule.

Study Notes


“Kings and priests” reflects the language of Exodus 19:6, where Israel was called to be a “kingdom of priests and a holy nation.” Revelation applies this royal-priestly calling to those redeemed by Christ. Similar language has already appeared in Revelation 1:6.

There are significant textual variations in this verse. Many early manuscripts read “a kingdom and priests” rather than the NKJV’s “kings and priests.” This wording closely parallels Revelation 1:6 and Exodus 19:6. The central idea remains a redeemed people possessing both royal and priestly identity.

There is also a textual variation between “we shall reign” and “they shall reign.” Many modern critical texts read “they shall reign,” corresponding to the third-person reference to the redeemed in verse 9. These variations affect the grammatical identification of the singers but not the central theological point: the people purchased by the Lamb are made a kingdom and priests for God and will reign upon the earth.

“Reign” translates basileuō (G936), meaning to reign or exercise royal rule. Revelation returns to this theme in 20:4, 6 and 22:5.

The phrase “on the earth” is important. The anticipated reign of the redeemed is explicitly connected with the earth, fitting Revelation’s movement toward the ultimate renewal of creation rather than presenting redemption simply as permanent removal from the created world.

Spirit & Life


The Lamb does more than forgive and rescue His people. He gives them a new identity and purpose: a kingdom and priests to God.

Priests draw near to God and belong to His service. Kings exercise delegated authority under a greater King. Both dimensions keep the redeemed centered upon God—near His presence, serving His purposes, and participating in His rule.

This is especially powerful in Revelation because many of its first readers lived under earthly powers that could make Christians appear insignificant and powerless. Heaven reveals a different reality. Those purchased by the blood of Jesus may presently suffer, but their final identity is not determined by the kingdoms opposing them. They belong to God, and they will reign with Christ.

The movement of verses 9–10 is therefore much larger than personal rescue: the Lamb was slain, He purchased a people for God, He made them a royal-priestly people, and He will bring them into participation in His reign.

Revelation 5:11

NKJV

Revelation 5:11 Then I looked, and I heard the voice of many angels around the throne, the living creatures, and the elders; and the number of them was ten thousand times ten thousand, and thousands of thousands,(NKJV)

NLT

Revelation 5:11 Then I looked again, and I heard the voices of thousands and millions of angels around the throne and of the living beings and the elders. (NLT)

Main Idea


John sees the worship around the Lamb expand dramatically as an innumerable multitude of angels joins the living creatures and elders in praising Him.

Verse Explanation


“Then I looked, and I heard the voice of many angels around the throne” — John’s attention expands outward from the worship immediately surrounding the Lamb. The throne remains the center, but now an enormous company of angels joins the scene. What began with the four living creatures and twenty-four elders becomes a vast heavenly chorus responding to the worthiness of the Lamb.

“the living creatures, and the elders” — John again identifies the groups closest to the throne. The living creatures and elders began the worship in verses 8–10, and now countless angels surround them. The picture develops in widening circles of praise around God and the Lamb.

“and the number of them was ten thousand times ten thousand” — John uses enormous numerical language to communicate a multitude beyond ordinary counting. “Ten thousand” was among the largest common numerical expressions available in Greek. Multiplying it by itself produces the picture of an overwhelmingly vast heavenly host.

“and thousands of thousands” — John adds another expression of immense number. His purpose is not to provide a precise census of the angels but to communicate their innumerable multitude. Heaven is filled with beings whose attention is now directed toward the Lamb.

Study Notes


“Ten thousand” translates myrias (G3461), from which the English word “myriad” derives. It can designate ten thousand specifically or, particularly in plural constructions, an extremely large number.

The language “ten thousand times ten thousand, and thousands of thousands” echoes Daniel 7:10, where thousands minister before the Ancient of Days and “ten thousand times ten thousand” stand before Him. Revelation draws that vast heavenly court into the worship surrounding the Lamb.

The arrangement of the scene is significant. The angels are described as being “around the throne, the living creatures, and the elders.” The vision has expanded outward while maintaining God’s throne and the Lamb as its center.

Verse 11 introduces the angelic multitude; verse 12 records what they proclaim. The two verses therefore belong closely together: John first emphasizes the staggering number of worshipers and then the content of their worship.

Spirit & Life


John began chapter 5 weeping because “no one was found worthy.” Now he hears a multitude too large to count gathering around the One who was found worthy.

The contrast is striking. At first, creation appeared unable to provide anyone who could open the scroll. Then Jesus was revealed—the Lion who conquered as the Lamb who was slain. Now heaven cannot remain silent about Him.

The worship keeps expanding. The living creatures and elders worship the Lamb, and now countless angels join them. In the verses that follow, the circle will widen again until every creature joins the praise.

Revelation is showing us the true center of heaven’s attention: Jesus Christ, the slain and victorious Lamb. What heaven sees clearly, the church is called to see clearly as well. His worthiness is so great that an innumerable heavenly multitude gathers around Him in praise.

Revelation 5:12

NKJV

Revelation 5:12 saying with a loud voice: "Worthy is the Lamb who was slain To receive power and riches and wisdom, And strength and honor and glory and blessing!"(NKJV)

NLT

Revelation 5:12 And they sang in a mighty chorus: “Worthy is the Lamb who was slaughtered —to receive power and richesand wisdom and strengthand honor and glory and blessing.” (NLT)

Main Idea


The countless angels surrounding the throne loudly proclaim that Jesus, the Lamb who was slain, is worthy to receive all power, riches, wisdom, strength, honor, glory, and blessing.

Verse Explanation


“saying with a loud voice:” — The innumerable angels introduced in verse 11 now join together in a thunderous declaration of praise. Their “loud voice” matches the magnitude of the heavenly multitude. Heaven is united in publicly declaring the worthiness of the Lamb.

“Worthy is the Lamb who was slain” — The central question of chapter 5 receives another emphatic answer: the Lamb is worthy. Once again, His worthiness is directly connected with His sacrificial death. Heaven does not celebrate Jesus by moving beyond the cross; it identifies the exalted Christ as “the Lamb who was slain.” His sacrifice remains central to His identity and to heaven’s worship.

“To receive power and riches and wisdom” — The angels begin a sevenfold declaration of what belongs to the Lamb. “Power” acknowledges His sovereign ability and authority. “Riches” recognizes the fullness of all resources belonging to Him. “Wisdom” acknowledges His perfect understanding and ability to accomplish God’s purposes. Nothing required for the fulfillment of those purposes is lacking in Christ.

“And strength and honor and glory and blessing!” — The declaration continues with “strength,” emphasizing might; “honor,” recognizing His supreme worth; “glory,” acknowledging His magnificent greatness; and “blessing,” expressing the praise rightly directed toward Him. Together, these seven terms form a comprehensive declaration that the Lamb deserves the fullest possible worship.

Study Notes


The sevenfold declaration—“power and riches and wisdom, and strength and honor and glory and blessing”—fits Revelation’s repeated use of the number seven to express fullness or completeness. The point is comprehensive praise of the Lamb.

“Worthy” translates axios (G514), the key word running throughout Revelation 4–5. God is “worthy” as Creator in Revelation 4:11, and the Lamb is “worthy” because of His redemptive victory in Revelation 5:9, 12.

“Was slain” again uses sphazō (G4969), the verb already used in verses 6 and 9. Its repetition keeps Christ’s sacrificial death at the center of the chapter: the Lamb appears slain (v. 6), is worthy because He was slain (v. 9), and is worshiped as the Lamb who was slain (v. 12).

There is a notable parallel between the worship of God in Revelation 4:11 and the worship of the Lamb here. God receives “glory and honor and power,” while the Lamb is declared worthy to receive those same expressions of worship along with riches, wisdom, strength, and blessing. Revelation deliberately places the Lamb within the worship of heaven.

Spirit & Life


Heaven looks at Jesus and declares, “Worthy is the Lamb who was slain.”

That description is striking because Jesus is no longer dead. He is standing, victorious, and about to open the seals. Yet heaven continues to identify Him by His sacrifice. The cross is not something heaven has forgotten now that Christ is exalted. His wounds testify forever to the love, obedience, and victory through which He redeemed His people.

And heaven’s response is not restrained. Countless angels proclaim His worthiness “with a loud voice.” Everything about the scene magnifies Jesus.

This gives us a picture of worship centered rightly on Christ. His power deserves our confidence, His wisdom our trust, His glory our attention, and His sacrifice our unending gratitude. The One who gave Himself completely is worthy of complete worship.

The Lamb held nothing back in giving Himself for redemption, and heaven holds nothing back in declaring His worthiness.

Revelation 5:13

NKJV

Revelation 5:13 And every creature which is in heaven and on the earth and under the earth and such as are in the sea, and all that are in them, I heard saying: "Blessing and honor and glory and power Be to Him who sits on the throne, And to the Lamb, forever and ever!"(NKJV)

NLT

Revelation 5:13 And then I heard every creature in heaven and on earth and under the earth and in the sea. They sang: “Blessing and honor and glory and powerbelong to the one sitting on the throneand to the Lamb forever and ever.” (NLT)

Main Idea


The worship surrounding the throne reaches its widest extent as every part of creation joins in blessing, honor, glory, and power directed equally to God on the throne and to the Lamb.

Verse Explanation


“And every creature which is in heaven and on the earth and under the earth” — The circle of worship expands again. The living creatures and elders worshiped in verses 8–10, countless angels joined them in verses 11–12, and now John hears praise arising from the whole created order. The language deliberately reaches across every realm of creation.

“and such as are in the sea, and all that are in them” — John makes the description even more comprehensive by including the sea and everything within it. Nothing is left outside the scope of this vision. The Creator praised in chapter 4 and the Lamb praised in chapter 5 stand at the center of creation’s worship.

“I heard saying:” — John does not merely see this universal gathering; he hears its unified declaration. The many voices of creation become one expression of praise. What began as worship immediately around the throne has now filled the entire created order.

“Blessing and honor and glory and power” — Four expressions of praise are offered. “Blessing” expresses praise and adoration. “Honor” acknowledges supreme worth. “Glory” recognizes divine majesty and greatness. “Power” acknowledges sovereign might and dominion. Creation recognizes that these belong to the One on the throne and to the Lamb.

“Be to Him who sits on the throne, And to the Lamb” — This is one of the most significant features of the verse. The same worship is directed simultaneously to God the Father and to Jesus the Lamb. John does not separate the Lamb from the worship belonging to God. Jesus stands alongside the One seated upon the throne as the recipient of creation’s blessing, honor, glory, and power.

“forever and ever!” — This worship has no expiration. The Lamb’s worthiness is not temporary, and God’s reign does not end. The praise offered to God and the Lamb extends into eternity.

Study Notes


The fourfold description “in heaven and on the earth and under the earth… and in the sea” is comprehensive language encompassing the created order. It also develops the threefold description of Revelation 5:3, where no one in heaven, earth, or under the earth was initially found worthy to open the scroll.

There is an intentional progression through Revelation 5:8–13: the four living creatures and twenty-four elders worship; then myriads of angels join them; finally every creature joins the praise. The worship expands outward from the throne until it encompasses creation.

“Him who sits on the throne, and… the Lamb” places God and the Lamb together as recipients of the same doxology. This is an important part of Revelation’s presentation of Christ’s divine identity: worship that belongs to God is openly given to the Lamb within the heavenly throne-room vision.

The expression “forever and ever” translates eis tous aiōnas tōn aiōnōn, literally “unto the ages of the ages,” a standard biblical expression emphasizing unending duration.

Spirit & Life


Earlier in this chapter, John wept because no one was found worthy. Now the chapter has moved to the opposite scene: all creation is praising the One who was worthy.

And Jesus is not standing somewhere outside the worship directed toward God. The praise is given “to Him who sits on the throne, and to the Lamb.” Heaven does not hesitate to place Jesus at the center of its worship.

This gives us a powerful picture of where all creation is ultimately headed. Revelation contains rebellion, suffering, judgment, and opposition to God, but none of those things receives the final word. The vision moves toward a creation filled with the acknowledgment of God and the Lamb.

For God’s people, worship therefore brings us into agreement with the ultimate reality Revelation reveals. When we give Jesus blessing, honor, glory, and praise, we are joining now in what John sees filling creation.

The Lamb who was rejected and slain on earth is worshiped with the One on the throne forever and ever.

Revelation 5:14

NKJV

Revelation 5:14 Then the four living creatures said, "Amen!" And the twenty-four elders fell down and worshiped Him who lives forever and ever.(NKJV)

NLT

Revelation 5:14 And the four living beings said, “Amen!” And the twenty-four elders fell down and worshiped the Lamb. (NLT)

Main Idea


The throne-room worship reaches its conclusion as the four living creatures affirm creation’s praise and the twenty-four elders fall before God and the Lamb in worship.

Verse Explanation


“Then the four living creatures said, ‘Amen!’” — In verse 13, John heard every creature in heaven, on earth, under the earth, and in the sea offering blessing, honor, glory, and power to God and the Lamb. The four living creatures now respond with “Amen,” affirming that this praise is true and right. The creatures who began proclaiming God’s holiness around the throne in chapter 4 now affirm the worship that has expanded throughout creation.

“And the twenty-four elders fell down” — The elders again respond physically to the glory of God and the Lamb. They had fallen before God in Revelation 4:10 and before the Lamb in 5:8. Their repeated prostration emphasizes humility, submission, and reverence. Although they possess thrones and crowns, their proper place before God is worship.

“and worshiped Him who lives forever and ever” — The scene concludes where the throne-room vision has continually directed attention: worship. God is the eternally living One whose reign has no end. The chapter began with a sealed scroll that no one was worthy to open and ends with heaven and creation overwhelmed in worship because the worthy Lamb has been revealed.

Study Notes


“Amen” transliterates the Hebrew amen, meaning truly, certainly, or so be it. Here it functions as a solemn affirmation of the universal praise offered in verse 13.

“Fell down” translates piptō (G4098), while “worshiped” translates proskyneō (G4352). These same actions characterize the elders’ worship in Revelation 4:10. Their physical posture expresses their recognition of the supreme worth and authority of the One they worship.

The NKJV includes “Him who lives forever and ever.” Some early manuscripts have the shorter reading, simply stating that the elders “fell down and worshiped.” The textual difference does not alter the function of the verse as the concluding response of worship.

There is a deliberate movement across Revelation 5:8–14. Worship begins with the four living creatures and twenty-four elders, expands to myriads of angels, then reaches every creature, and finally returns to the living creatures and elders around the throne. The chapter closes with the entire scene united in worship.

Spirit & Life


Revelation 5 began with John weeping. It ends with heaven worshiping.

What changed was not the throne, God’s purposes, or the existence of the scroll. John was shown Jesus. The Lion had prevailed. The Lamb had been slain but was standing. He took the scroll because He alone was worthy.

Now the response around the throne is simply “Amen” and worship.

That movement captures something central to Revelation. When our attention rests only on what appears impossible, there can be tears like John’s. But heaven keeps directing our attention to the Lamb. Jesus has conquered, His sacrifice has secured redemption, and God’s purposes are in His hands.

The elders therefore finish the chapter where God’s people ultimately belong: bowed before Him in worship. The vision has moved from the question, “Who is worthy?” to the answer that fills heaven and creation: the Lamb is worthy.
