Romans Book Outline

Major Movements

I. Paul’s Calling and the Gospel He Proclaims (1:1–1:17)

Paul introduces his apostolic calling, expresses his desire to visit Rome, and declares his obligation to proclaim the gospel. The gospel is God’s power for salvation to everyone who believes and reveals the righteousness of God through faith.

II. All Humanity Is Accountable to God (1:18–3:20)

Paul exposes humanity’s rejection of God and the inability of either moral judgment or possession of the law to remove guilt. Jews and Gentiles alike are under sin, and the law brings knowledge of sin rather than justification by works.

III. God Justifies Through Faith in Jesus Christ (3:21–5:21)

God’s righteousness is revealed through faith in Jesus Christ, apart from works of the law, and Abraham demonstrates that justification rests on believing God’s promise. Paul unfolds the resulting peace and hope and contrasts the condemnation brought through Adam with the righteousness and life brought through Christ.

IV. New Life in Christ and the Freedom of the Spirit (6:1–8:39)

Union with Christ’s death and resurrection breaks sin’s dominion and calls believers to present themselves to God. Paul explains release from the law’s old jurisdiction, the inability of the flesh, and the Spirit’s work in bringing freedom, adoption, hope, and assurance of God’s love.

V. God’s Faithfulness to Israel and His Mercy to the Nations (9:1–11:36)

Paul addresses Israel’s widespread unbelief in light of God’s promises, sovereign mercy, and the call to righteousness by faith. Israel’s stumbling does not nullify God’s faithfulness, and the inclusion of Gentiles leads to a warning against pride and a declaration of God’s continuing purpose for Israel.

VI. God’s Mercy Shapes the Shared Life of His People (12:1–15:13)

Paul calls believers to offer themselves to God and live with transformed minds, sincere love that blesses even enemies, and responsible conduct toward governing authorities. He applies this life especially to differences over food and days, urging strong and weak believers to receive one another as Christ has received them.

VII. Paul’s Mission Plans and Final Greetings (15:14–16:27)

Paul explains his ministry among the Gentiles, his intended journey through Rome to Spain, and his immediate service to Jerusalem. Commendations, greetings, a warning against divisive teachers, and a closing doxology place the Roman believers within the wider fellowship and mission of the gospel.
