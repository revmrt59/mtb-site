Titus - Book Introduction

Background

Author

The Apostle Paul. The letter identifies Paul as its author and there is no serious question about his authorship. His close, personal relationship with Titus explains the pastoral tone and the apostolic authority behind the concrete instructions for organizing and correcting the churches in Crete.

Audience

Titus and the churches on the island of Crete-Gentile congregations needing organizational leadership, doctrinal clarity, and moral reform.

Date

c. A.D. 63-65 (mid‑60s), most likely written shortly after Paul's first Roman imprisonment.

Occasion

God sent Paul's instructions through Titus because the churches in Crete faced urgent, practical problems: leaders needed to be appointed, teachers spreading false doctrine and moral laxity had to be confronted, and ordinary believers needed clear teaching so sound doctrine would shape daily life and honor the gospel.

Historical Setting

Paul wrote to Titus while Titus was on the island of Crete, a largely Gentile setting known for moral looseness and a poor reputation among neighboring peoples. Paul had left Titus to organize congregations, appoint elders in every town, and confront teachers who blended error with immoral living. The immediate problem was practical: without sound leaders and moral reform, the churches risked dishonoring the gospel and losing credibility in their communities.

Message

Purpose

To equip Titus to establish healthy, self-governing churches by appointing faithful leaders, defending sound doctrine, encouraging lives transformed by God's grace, and promoting good works that publicly honor the gospel.

Book Summary

Paul urges Titus to establish healthy churches in Crete by appointing faithful elders and promoting sound doctrine that counters false teachers and moral laxity. He shows that the gospel's truth-not human myths-produces transformed lives and good works in families and society, so the church's witness honors God.

Major Themes

  • Sound doctrine that leads to godly living
  • Biblical church leadership and elder qualifications
  • Good works as the visible fruit of grace
  • Resistance to false teaching and legalistic myths
  • Practical Christian living in households and society
  • God's saving kindness and the Spirit's renewal

Central Message

God's grace builds and sustains healthy churches by grounding them in sound teaching, faithful leaders, and lives of good works that honor the gospel.

Study Guide

Outline

  • Titus 1:1-16 - Part 1: Leadership Qualifications and Rebuking False Teachers
  • Titus 2:1-15 - Part 2: Sound Doctrine and Godly Living in the Church
  • Titus 3:1-15 - Part 3: Christian Conduct in Society and Final Instructions

Key Verse(s)

  • Titus 1:5 - Paul's charge to Titus to appoint elders so each church has faithful, sound leadership.
  • Titus 2:11-14 - God's grace brings salvation, trains believers in godliness, and presents Christ as our great God and Savior who redeems a people for himself.
  • Titus 3:5-7 - Salvation comes by God's mercy, not works: a washing of rebirth and the Spirit's renewal that gives hope and an eternal inheritance.

Christ in This Book

Titus points to Jesus Christ as the great God and Savior who graciously rescues sinners, purifies a people for himself, and-by his kindness and the renewing work of the Spirit-changes hearts. That saving work shows itself not only in forgiveness but in transformed lives: holiness, self-control, good works, and faithful service that honor the gospel (Titus 2:11-14; 3:4-7).

Introductory Summary

Titus is a short, intensely practical letter from Paul to his young co-worker entrusted with reorganizing churches on Crete. The book's central tension is plain: false teaching and moral laxity threatened the churches, and Paul answers by showing that sound doctrine must lead to changed lives. The letter moves from appointing faithful elders to giving concrete instruction for every age and household so the gospel will be honored in daily life. It closes with a reminder of God's saving kindness and a call to good works. Come ready to see how gospel truth reshapes leadership, community, and ordinary behavior.
