Summary:

GENTLE - ( epieikes , Strong’s [1933]) Summary: The quality of being reasonable, fair, and moderate. It describes a spirit that is willing to yield its legal rights for the sake of mercy and peace, rather than demanding the strict letter of the law.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation - Derived from the preposition epi, meaning "upon" or "fitting," and eikos, meaning "reasonable" or "fair." - It suggests a quality that is appropriate or "fitting" for a given situation, often going beyond the strict requirements of justice to provide what is truly helpful.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition - Fair, moderate, or forbearing. - It refers to the character of a person who is not insistently legalistic but is willing to make allowances for human frailty and circumstances. - It is often contrasted with being harsh or strictly demanding one's due, representing a "sweet reasonableness."

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Equitable Judge: In secular Greek, it was used to describe a judge who looked beyond the "letter of the law" to the "spirit of the law," applying equity where strict justice might be too harsh. - The Considerate Citizen: It pictured a person who does not demand the full measure of their legal rights if doing so would cause unnecessary harm or strife. - The Yielding Spirit: It identifies a person whose character is characterized by a lack of stubbornness and a willingness to listen to the concerns of others.

4. Theological Implications - This quality is a reflection of the character of God, who deals with mankind in mercy rather than according to the strict demands of justice. - It is a required qualification for those in church leadership and a primary characteristic of the wisdom that comes from above. - Gentleness demonstrates a heart that is ruled by the peace of God rather than by personal pride or the desire for vengeance.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:2 - In Titus 3:2, believers are explicitly told to be "gentle" (epieikes) toward all men. - This follows the command to be peaceable, emphasizing that a lack of contention is rooted in a reasonable and yielding spirit. - It ensures that the believer’s interactions with the world are marked by a gracious moderation that represents the faith in a positive and attractive manner.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - 1 Timothy 3:3: “...not greedy for money, but gentle (epieikes), not quarrelsome...” - James 3:17: “But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle (epieikes), willing to yield...” - 1 Peter 2:18: “Servants, be submissive to your masters with all fear, not only to the good and gentle (epieikes), but also to the harsh.” - Philippians 4:5: “Let your gentleness (epieikes) be known to all men. The Lord is at hand.”

7. Other Relevant Insights - This word highlights the balance between truth and grace, where the individual chooses to be kind even when they have the right to be firm. - It implies a strength that is under control, as it takes more strength to yield for the sake of peace than to demand one's own way. - Maintaining this quality serves as a contrast to a society that is often characterized by demands for individual rights and harsh judgments.
