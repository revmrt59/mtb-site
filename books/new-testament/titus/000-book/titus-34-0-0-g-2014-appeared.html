Summary:

APPEARED - ( epiphaino , Strong’s [2014]) Summary: To become visible, to shine upon, or to be clearly known. It describes a purposeful manifestation, like light breaking into darkness, which reveals a new reality or intervention.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation * Derived from the preposition epi, meaning "upon" or "suitably on," and the verb phaino, meaning "to shine" or "to appear." * The combination suggests an appearance that "shines upon" a specific situation with a intended effect or purpose.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition * To show forth, bring to light, or become visible. * In a literal sense, it refers to the appearance of celestial bodies, such as stars. * In a figurative sense, it refers to a person or truth becoming clearly known or showing itself to others.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures * The Rising Sun: In secular literature, it was used for the dawning of the sun or the appearance of stars to sailors navigating the sea, providing necessary light and direction. * The Royal Visit: It described the "epiphany" of a king or a deity who appeared to provide help, rescue, or a display of power to their people. * Breaking the Darkness: It pictured the sudden and decisive interruption of light into a place of deep shadow, changing the environment instantly.

4. Theological Implications * It signifies the decisive intervention of God in human history, specifically through the incarnation of Jesus Christ. * The word emphasizes that salvation is not a hidden or secret truth but has been made visible and accessible to all through a historical event. * It represents the dawning of the Messianic age, fulfilling Old Testament prophecies regarding the "light" that would shine on those in darkness.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:4 * In Titus 3:4, the word marks the dramatic transition from the list of sinful behaviors in verse 3 to the manifestation of God’s grace. * It highlights that while humanity was "foolish" and "hating one another," God’s kindness and love were not merely felt in heaven but "appeared" on earth to change that condition. * The epiphany of God's character (kindness and love) is the catalyst for the washing and renewal described in the following verses.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) * Luke 1:79: “To give light (epiphaino) to those who sit in darkness and the shadow of death...” * Acts 27:20: “Now when neither sun nor stars appeared (epiphaino) for many days...” * Titus 2:11: “For the grace of God that brings salvation has appeared (epiphaino) to all men,”

7. Other Relevant Insights * This word highlights the objective nature of the Gospel; it is based on an "appearing" that can be witnessed and verified. * It suggests that the beauty of God’s character is the "light" that dispels the "darkness" of the previous life’s malice and envy. * To acknowledge that grace has "appeared" is to recognize that a new day has dawned for the believer, requiring a walk that is consistent with the light.
