Summary

ADORN - ( kosmeo , Strong’s [2885])

Summary:

To put in order, arrange, or make attractive. It describes the act of making something beautiful or honorable through proper and harmonious arrangement.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the noun kosmos, which refers to "order," "system," or "the world" as an ordered whole.

It describes the process of taking that which is chaotic or unarranged and placing it into a systematic and functional state.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

To trim, garnish, or embellish.

In a literal sense, it means to put in order or make ready, such as trimming a lamp to ensure it functions correctly.

Metaphorically, it refers to making a person, an office, or a teaching attractive by adding appropriate qualities or behaviors.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- Civic Order: Used to describe the orderly arrangement of a city's government or the decorations used during a public festival.

- Bridal Preparation: Pictured a bride preparing herself with jewels and fine clothing to appear beautiful and honorable for her husband.

- Military Organization: Described the orderly arrangement of troops in their proper ranks and files to ensure efficiency and beauty in formation.

4. Theological Implications

The word points to God's character as the Author of order who arranged the heavens and the earth according to His design.

Believers are called to reflect this order by arranging their conduct in a way that aligns with the character of God.

It suggests that the way a person lives serves as the setting for the truth of God, allowing it to be clearly seen by those who observe.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:10 In Titus 2:10, bondservants are told to "adorn the doctrine of God our Savior in all things." This implies that while the doctrine of God is inherently perfect and beautiful, the faithful and reliable conduct of a believer makes that beauty visible to others. By showing all good fidelity, the servant makes the Gospel undeniable and acts as an ornament for the Word.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Matthew 25:7: “Then all those virgins arose and trimmed (kosmeo) their lamps.”

- 1 Timothy 2:9: “...that the women adorn (kosmeo) themselves in modest apparel...”

- 1 Peter 3:5: “For in this manner, in former times, the holy women... also adorned (kosmeo) themselves...”

- Revelation 21:2: “...prepared as a bride adorned (kosmeo) for her husband.”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights the practical nature of the Christian testimony; it is not just about the profession of truth, but about living in a way that makes the truth attractive to observers.

It suggests that even the most menial tasks, when performed with excellence and integrity, contribute to the overall presentation of the faith.

Proper adornment requires intentionality, ensuring that every area of a person's life is brought into alignment with the standards of the Gospel.
