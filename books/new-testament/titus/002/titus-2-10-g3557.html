Summary

NOT PILFERING - ( nosphizomai , Strong’s [G3557])

Summary: To set apart for oneself, to embezzle, or to secretly keep back what belongs to another. It describes a breach of trust where one takes small amounts for personal use while pretending to be faithful.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

  • Derived from the word nosphis, meaning "aloof" or "apart".

  • It literally means "to set apart for oneself" or "to put away for one's own use".

  • It describes a conviction that results in a settled state of secretly separating a portion of a whole for personal gain.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • To embezzle, purloin, or keep back part of a price.

  • While often associated with large-scale theft, in this context it refers to "petty theft"—the quality of being "untrue to one's word" by taking small items or funds that belong to the master.

  • It is the opposite of "fidelity"; it is the act of being unsteady in one's duty through secret dishonesty.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

  • The Secret Embezzlement: In secular Greek, nosphizomai was used for officials who diverted public funds or property for their private benefit.

  • The Hidden Portion: It pictured someone "setting aside" a part of an agreement or a shipment that gave the other party a false impression of completeness.

  • The Breach: It identifies the "internal hygiene" of a soul that has become "out of tune" with honesty, choosing to "drift" into theft when not observed.

4. Theological Implications

  • Honesty is required because God is "infinitely good" and perfectly faithful; therefore, those who are His must reflect that character.

  • This is the "meaning integrity" of the faith: if we say we trust a faithful God, we must be faithful in how we handle the property of others.

  • It reflects the "sacred fitness" of a life that handles the "small things" of a master's estate with the same integrity as the "great things" of the faith.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:10

  • The NKJV says: “...not pilfering (nosphizomenous), but showing all good fidelity...”.

  • Paul pairs this with "not answering back" to show that Christian "soundness" isn't just about what you say, but about being radically reliable with material things.

  • By not pilfering, the bondservant makes the doctrine of God undeniable to an watching world.

  • The "sound" believer is characterized by absolute honesty.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Acts 5:2: “And he kept back (enosphisato) part of the proceeds, his wife also being aware of it...”.

  • Acts 5:3: “...Why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit and keep back (nosphisasthai) part of the price of the land?”.

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • This word represents the "reproducible" reliability of a believer who is the same in private as they are in public.

  • It is the "action" of being a "safe" person that an employer or master can lean on without fear of loss.

  • When a servant refuses to pilfer, they are effectively "adorning" the Word, demonstrating that the faith produces people who are honest and "sound" in every transaction.
