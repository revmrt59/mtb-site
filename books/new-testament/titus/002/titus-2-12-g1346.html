Summary:

RIGHTEOUSLY - ( dikaios , Strong’s [1346])

In a manner that is just, upright, or in accordance with what is right. In Titus 2:12, it describes the outward conduct of a believer toward others, characterized by integrity and conformity to God's moral standards.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the adjective dikaios (Strong’s [1342]), which means "just" or "righteous," and ultimately from the root dike (Strong’s [1349]), meaning "right," "custom," or "judicial order."

It is the adverbial form, focusing on the quality of the action or the manner of life rather than the state of the person. It implies a life lived in accordance with a straight and unbending rule.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Justly, uprightly, or in a righteous manner.

It signifies conduct that is agreeable to justice and divine law.

In the New Testament, it denotes an ethical standard of living that fulfills all obligations toward fellow men, reflecting the character of a God who is Himself perfectly just.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- The Impartial Scale: In secular Greek, it pictured a merchant’s scale that was perfectly balanced, representing a refusal to cheat or show partiality in dealings with others.

- The Straight Edge: It was used in construction for a tool that ensured a wall was perfectly vertical or a floor was perfectly level, symbolizing a life that does not deviate into corruption or unfairness.

- The Loyal Citizen: It described a person who meticulously fulfilled their duties to the state and their neighbors, representing the horizontal dimension of social and moral responsibility.

4. Theological Implications

Righteousness is the visible outworking of a heart that has been justified by grace.

While "godliness" refers to the vertical relationship (Godward), "righteousness" refers to the horizontal relationship (manward). It is the practice of justice and integrity in all human affairs.

Theologically, it is a fruit of the "teaching" of grace, demonstrating that the Gospel produces people who are honest, fair, and reliable.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:12

In the NKJV of Titus 2:12, the believer is taught to live "righteously (dikaios)" in the present age.

This is the middle term of a triad that defines the Christian life: soberly (internally/self), righteously (externally/others), and godly (upwardly/God).

Living righteously implies that the believer’s conduct in society and in relationships is a testimony to the transforming power of the grace of God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Luke 23:41: “And we indeed righteously (dikaios), for we receive the due reward of our deeds;”

- 1 Corinthians 15:34: “Awake to righteousness (dikaios—righteously), and do not sin;”

- 1 Thessalonians 2:10: “...how holily and justly (dikaios) and blamelessly we behaved ourselves among you...”

- 1 Peter 2:23: “...but committed Himself to Him who judges righteously (dikaios);”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that "good works" (Titus 3:8) must be performed in a "righteous" manner; the ends do not justify unrighteous means.

It suggests that the "maintenance" of a believer’s reputation in the world depends on their commitment to acting "justly" in every situation.

By living righteously, the believer provides a "profitable" example to society, contrasting with the "unprofitable" and "unruly" behavior of the world.
