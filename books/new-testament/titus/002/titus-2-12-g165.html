Summary:

AGE - ( aion , Strong’s [165])

An era, a space of time, or a period of existence. In Titus 2:12, it refers to the current world-system and the specific span of time between the two appearings of Christ, emphasizing the temporary and moral environment in which the believer is called to live.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the adverb aei (Strong’s [104]), which means "always" or "continuously."

The root suggests a duration or a continuous flow of time. While it can mean "eternity" in certain contexts (when used in phrases like "forever and ever"), its primary focus in the New Testament is often on a specific epoch or "segment" of time.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

A period of time, an era, a generation, or the "spirit of the times."

It signifies the world as it exists in a particular time-frame, including its values, philosophies, and moral character.

Lexically, it is often contrasted with kosmos (which emphasizes the physical world or the arrangement of things); aion emphasizes the "time-bound" nature and the ethical "atmosphere" of the world.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- The Human Lifespan: In early Greek literature, it was used to describe the duration of a man's life or his "vital force," representing the finite time allotted to an individual.

- The Sovereign Era: It pictured the reign of a particular king or the duration of an empire, signifying the specific "order" that exists during that ruler's time.

- The Spirit of the Age: It described the zeitgeist or the prevailing thoughts and customs that define a specific period, suggesting that every "age" has its own unique set of distractions and deceptions.

4. Theological Implications

Theology identifies two primary ages: "this age" (ho nyn aion), which is fallen and under the influence of sin, and "the age to come" (ho aion ho mellon), which is the eternal Kingdom of God.

The "present age" is described as "evil" (Galatians 1:4) because it is dominated by a mindset that ignores God.

Theologically, the believer lives "in" this age but is not "of" it; they are to be "transformed" so they no longer conform to its temporary and ungodly patterns.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:12

In the NKJV of Titus 2:12, Paul speaks of living "in the present age (nyn aioni)."

- The Moral Arena: This is the specific "theater" where the grace of God trains the believer. The presence of ungodliness and worldly lusts (verse 12) defines the "atmosphere" of this aion.

- The Finite Reality: By using aion, Paul reminds the reader that the current state of affairs is temporary. It is a "segment" of history that will conclude when the "blessed hope" (verse 13) is realized.

- The Active Testimony: The call to live "soberly, righteously, and godly" is not for a future existence but is a direct challenge to the ungodly spirit that dominates the current aion.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Matthew 13:22: “...and the cares of this world (aionos) and the deceitfulness of riches choke the word...”

- Romans 12:2: “And do not be conformed to this world (aioni), but be transformed by the renewing of your mind...”

- 2 Corinthians 4:4: “whose minds the god of this age (aionos) has blinded...”

- Galatians 1:4: “...that He might deliver us from this present evil age (aionos)...”

- Ephesians 1:21: “...not only in this age (aioni) but also in that which is to come.”

- 2 Timothy 4:10: “for Demas has forsaken me, having loved this present world (aionon)...”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that the Christian life is an "inter-advent" life. We live between the aion of grace and the aion of glory.

It suggests that "worldliness" is a matter of the "time" we are in; to be worldly is to be preoccupied with the temporary aion rather than the eternal one.

By using aion, Paul encourages Titus that the struggle against "worldly lusts" (verse 12) is confined to this present, passing era and will not follow the believer into the next.
