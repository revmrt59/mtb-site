Summary:

TEACHING - ( paideuo , Strong’s [3811])

To train, discipline, or educate. In Titus 2:12, it describes the active, ongoing work of God's grace as a tutor that instructs the believer to reject ungodliness and to live a life characterized by self-control and righteousness.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the noun pais, which means "child."

It is the verbal form related to paideia, which refers to the whole system of training and education.

In its core, it carries the idea of "bringing up a child," encompassing everything from classroom instruction to corrective discipline.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

To train, to educate, to instruct, or to chasten.

In the New Testament, it signifies the process of maturing a person through instruction and, when necessary, through corrective discipline.

It is not merely the transmission of data, but the "training of the will" and the shaping of character toward a specific standard of conduct.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- The Pedagogue: In Greek culture, the paideia was the total system of cultural and physical education designed to produce a virtuous citizen. It pictured a "tutor" who accompanied a child everywhere to correct their behavior and speech.

- The Athletic Coach: It was used for the rigorous "training" an athlete underwent to prepare for the games, involving strict diet, exercise, and the stripping away of anything that would hinder performance.

- The Father’s Discipline: It pictured a father "correcting" a son to prevent him from following a path of ruin, emphasizing that true "teaching" often involves the pain of correction for the sake of future profit.

4. Theological Implications

Theology identifies grace as a "tutor." This contradicts the idea that grace leads to lawlessness; instead, grace "trains" the believer in holiness.

It suggests that salvation is not an end point but the beginning of a "training program" where the Holy Spirit actively works to "renew" the mind (Titus 3:5).

"Teaching" is the mechanism by which the "hope of eternal life" (Titus 3:7) is translated into a "sober, righteous, and godly" life in the present age.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:12

In the NKJV of Titus 2:12, the subject of the verb is "the grace of God" (from verse 11). Paul says grace is "teaching (paideuousa) us."

The verb is a present participle, indicating that grace's work is "continuous and ongoing."

Grace does not just "save" and leave the person alone; it stays with the believer to "train" them to "deny" (arnesamenoi) what is wrong and "live" (zesomen) what is right. It provides the power for the "maintenance of good works" (Titus 3:8).

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Luke 23:16: “I will therefore chastise (paideusas) Him and release Him;”

- 1 Corinthians 11:32: “But when we are judged, we are chastened (paideuometha) by the Lord, that we may not be condemned with the world.”

- 2 Timothy 2:25: “in humility correcting (paideuonta) those who are in opposition...”

- Titus 2:12: “teaching (paideuousa) us that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts...”

- Hebrews 12:6: “For whom the LORD loves He chastens (paideuei), and scourges every son whom He receives.”

- Revelation 3:19: “As many as I love, I rebuke and chasten (paideuo). Therefore be zealous and repent.”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that the "kindness and love of God" (Titus 3:4) is expressed through the "discipline" of His Word.

It suggests that a believer who is not growing in godliness is a student who is ignoring the "tutor" of grace.

By using a word rooted in "child-rearing," Paul emphasizes that believers are the "children" of God who must be "trained" into the maturity required to be "heirs" (Titus 3:7) of the kingdom.
