Summary:

DENYING - ( arnesamenoi , Strong’s [720])

To refuse, reject, or say "no" to. It describes a decisive and personal renunciation of a previous allegiance or lifestyle, signifying a formal break with the values and desires of the ungodly world.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the verb arneomai, which means "to say no," "to refuse," or "to deny."

In the middle voice (as used in Titus 2:12), it carries a reflexive sense, indicating a personal and volitional act where the subject is deeply involved in the decision to separate themselves from the object being denied.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

To disclaim, disown, or abjure.

It signifies a refusal to acknowledge or a formal rejection of a relationship, a claim, or a set of beliefs.

In the New Testament, it is the opposite of "confessing" (homologeo); it is a deliberate refusal to be associated with a person or a way of life.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- The Legal Witness: In secular Greek courts, it was used for a witness "denying" an accusation or "refusing" to acknowledge a previous contract, representing a definitive legal break.

- The Renounced Citizenship: It pictured a person "disowning" their native country or a former master to pledge loyalty to a new one, signifying a transfer of allegiance.

- The Refused Invitation: It was used for "declining" a social offer or an invitation to a feast, suggesting a conscious choice to avoid a specific environment.

4. Theological Implications

Denying is the "negative" aspect of the conversion process; before a believer can "live" for God (verse 12), they must first "deny" the old life.

It represents the "death to self" that is necessary for the "renewing of the Holy Spirit" (Titus 3:5).

Theologically, it is the active response to the "teaching" of grace, where the believer agrees with God’s assessment that "ungodliness" is "unprofitable" (Titus 3:9) and must be discarded.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:12

In the NKJV of Titus 2:12, the word is an aorist middle participle: "denying (arnesamenoi) ungodliness and worldly lusts."

- The Decisive Act: The aorist tense suggests a "once-for-all" decision that sets the direction for all subsequent behavior.

- The Reflexive Choice: The middle voice emphasizes that the believer is not forced into this denial; they personally choose to "disown" their former lusts because they have been "instructed" by grace.

- The Priority: The denial of the old life precedes the "living" of the new life, showing that one cannot "maintain good works" (Titus 3:8) while still clinging to "worldly lusts."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Matthew 10:33: “But whoever denies (arnesetai) Me before men, him I will also deny (arnesomai) before My Father...”

- Luke 9:23: “...If anyone desires to come after Me, let him deny (arnesastho) himself, and take up his cross...”

- Acts 3:13: “...whom you delivered up and denied (arnesasthe) in the presence of Pilate...”

- 2 Timothy 2:12: “...If we deny (arnoumetha) Him, He also will deny (arnesetai) us.”

- Titus 1:16: “They profess to know God, but in works they deny (arnountai) Him...”

- 1 John 2:22: “...He is antichrist who denies (arnoumenos) the Father and the Son.”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights the "fruitfulness" (Titus 3:14) of a life that has successfully "cut ties" with its sinful past.

It suggests that "denying" is not a passive event but an active "watch" (Mark 13:37) against the "unruly" influences that once governed the heart.

By contrasting this with Titus 1:16, Paul shows that true "grace" results in a "denying" through works, rather than a "confessing" through words while the heart remains "disobedient."
