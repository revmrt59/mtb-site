Summary:

UNGODLINESS - ( asebeia , Strong’s [763])

A lack of reverence for God or an active disregard for His authority. It describes an attitude of the heart that ignores the respect and honor due to the Creator, leading to a lifestyle lived in opposition to His character and divine standards.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the alpha intensive (a) meaning "without" or "no," and sebomai, which means "to revere," "to worship," or "to venerate."

It is the direct antonym of eusebeia (godliness or piety). While sebomai refers to the vertical relationship of honor toward God, asebeia describes a total lack of that vertical orientation.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Impiety, irreverence, or wickedness.

It signifies a heart that is devoid of the fear of God. It is not merely the absence of religion, but a positive, active indifference or hostility toward the nature and commands of God.

Lexically, it points to the "root" of sin—the failure to give God His proper place—which then bears the "fruit" of unrighteousness in conduct.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- The Secular Life: In Greek thought, it referred to a person who lived as if the divine did not exist, treating sacred things as common or irrelevant to daily life.

- The Rebellious Son: It pictured a child who refused to show the mandatory honor and weight due to a parent, effectively breaking the most foundational relationship of human society.

- The Desecrated Temple: It was used to describe an act of "profaning" a holy site, treating the dwelling place of a deity with contempt or casual disregard.

4. Theological Implications

Ungodliness is identified as the fundamental reason for the "wrath of God" (Romans 1:18). It is the refusal to acknowledge God as God.

It is a condition of the "old man" before the "washing of regeneration" takes place.

Theologically, it is the state of living "without God in the world," where the self is the final authority rather than the Creator.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:12

In the NKJV of Titus 2:12, Paul states that the grace of God teaches us to "deny ungodliness (asebeian)."

This is the first thing grace instructs the believer to renounce. Before a person can live "godly," they must decisively reject the "ungodly" framework of their previous life.

The context implies that "ungodliness" is the broad mindset of the world that must be disowned in order to become a "purified" people zealous for good works.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Romans 1:18: “For the wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness (asebeian) and unrighteousness of men...”

- Romans 11:26: “...He will turn away ungodliness (asebeias) from Jacob;”

- 2 Timothy 2:16: “...for they will increase to more ungodliness (asebeias).”

- Jude 1:15: “to convict all who are ungodly among them of all their ungodly (asebeias) deeds...”

- Jude 1:18: “...who would walk according to their own ungodly (asebeion) lusts.”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that the problem with humanity is not just bad behavior, but a "vertical" failure to revere God.

It suggests that "grace" is the only instructor capable of teaching a person how to truly "deny" this pervasive mindset.

The contrast between ungodliness and the "blessed hope" (verse 13) shows that those who are saved are looking forward to the King, while the ungodly are looking only at themselves.
