Summary:

GLORIOUS - ( doxa , Strong’s [1391])

The manifestation of God's character, radiance, or majesty. In Titus 2:13, it describes the nature of Christ's second coming, where His divine brightness and supreme authority will be visible to all, contrasting with His first appearance in humility.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the verb dokeo (Strong’s [1380]), which means "to think," "to seem," or "to be of an opinion."

In secular Greek, it originally referred to one's "opinion" or "repute." This evolved into the "good opinion" others held of a person, and eventually came to signify the "majesty," "splendor," and "absolute worth" of God Himself.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Splendor, brightness, majesty, or fame.

It denotes the intrinsic worth and brilliant radiance of God. Unlike human glory, which is often a reflected or temporary reputation, divine doxa is the heavy, substantial, and permanent reality of God’s own perfections.

In the New Testament, it frequently refers to the state of being in heaven or the visible light that accompanies a divine appearance.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- The Radiant Sun: It pictured the "blinding brightness" of the sun at midday, representing a reality so intense that it cannot be looked upon directly without specialized "sight."

- The Royal Court: It described the "pomp and circumstance" of a king’s arrival, where his wealth, power, and dignity were made visible through his attire and entourage.

- The Weighted Scale: Related to the Hebrew concept of kabod, it pictured "weightiness." A person of glory was a person of "weight" whose words and presence carried heavy significance in the community.

4. Theological Implications

Glory is the "outward expression" of God's "inward character." Everything God does is for His own glory—to make His perfections known to His creatures.

Jesus Christ is the "brightness of His glory" (Hebrews 1:3). In His first coming, His glory was veiled by human flesh; in His second coming, His glory will be unveiled.

Theologically, the "glory" is the goal of the "grace" mentioned in verse 11. Grace trains us for glory.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:13

In the NKJV of Titus 2:13, the phrase is "the glorious (doxes) appearing."

- The Noun usage: In the Greek, "glorious" is actually the noun "glory" in the genitive case (tes doxes). It is literally "the appearing of the glory."

- The Contrast: This appearing of "glory" stands in direct contrast to the appearing of "grace" in verse 11. Grace appeared to "all men" in humility; glory will appear to "all men" in majesty.

- The Ultimate Reality: The "glory" is not just an attribute of the appearing; the appearing is the glory of our great God and Savior, Jesus Christ. It is the final, visible proof of His deity and victory.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Matthew 16:27: “For the Son of Man will come in the glory (doxa) of His Father with His angels...”

- John 1:14: “...and we beheld His glory (doxan), the glory (doxan) as of the only begotten of the Father...”

- Romans 3:23: “for all have sinned and fall short of the glory (doxes) of God,”

- 2 Corinthians 3:18: “...are being transformed into the same image from glory (doxes) to glory (doxan)...”

- Colossians 1:27: “...which is Christ in you, the hope of glory (doxes).”

- 1 Peter 4:13: “...that when His glory (doxa) is revealed, you may also be glad with exceeding joy.”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that the Christian's "hope" (verse 13) is not just for a better world, but for the "visible manifestation" of the King.

It suggests that the "denying" of worldly lusts (verse 12) is a "profitable" (Titus 3:8) trade, as the temporary "glory" of the world is nothing compared to the "eternal weight of glory" that is coming.

By calling the return of Christ "the glory," Paul emphasizes that the second coming is the climax of human history and the ultimate vindication of the "faithful saying" of the Gospel.
