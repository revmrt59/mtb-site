Summary:

HOPE - ( elpis , Strong’s [1680])

A confident and favorable expectation. In Titus 2:13, it is not a wishful desire but a certain anticipation of the future return of Christ, providing a stable foundation for the believer's life and conduct in the present age.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the root elpo, which means "to anticipate" or "to expect."

It implies a forward-looking perspective where the mind is fixed on an event yet to occur. In the Greek mind, the root carries the idea of "looking forward with confidence."

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Expectation, hope, or trust.

Unlike the English word "hope," which often implies uncertainty or a mere wish, elpis in the New Testament signifies a "surety."

It is a joyful and certain expectation of good. Lexically, it is the opposite of despair or doubt, functioning as the mental and spiritual "leaning" toward a promised reality.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- The Anchor of the Soul: In maritime imagery, it pictured a ship’s anchor that holds the vessel steady amidst waves, representing the "stability" provided by a certain future.

- The Farmer’s Harvest: It pictured the farmer who sows seeds with the "expectation" of a crop, representing the "patience" required while waiting for a guaranteed result.

- The Watchman’s Confidence: It described a watchman waiting for the morning, where there is no doubt that the sun will rise, only a question of "when."

4. Theological Implications

Biblical hope is grounded in the "faithfulness" of God and the finished work of Christ. It is the "objective" foundation for "subjective" peace.

It is the theological link between the "already" of salvation and the "not yet" of glory.

This hope is what distinguishes the believer from those "who have no hope" (1 Thessalonians 4:13).

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:13

In the NKJV of Titus 2:13, it is called the "blessed hope (elpida)."

- The Motivation for Living: This hope is the "reason" why grace teaches us to deny ungodliness in verse 12. We can say "no" to the present age because we are focused on the "blessedness" of the coming age.

- The Object of Hope: The hope is not an abstract concept; it is the "glorious appearing" of Jesus Christ.

- The Impact: This expectation purifies the life of the believer, providing the "urgent" motivation to be zealous for good works.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Romans 5:5: “Now hope (elpis) does not disappoint, because the love of God has been poured out...”

- Romans 8:24: “For we were saved in this hope (elpidi), but hope (elpis) that is seen is not hope (elpis)...”

- Colossians 1:5: “because of the hope (elpida) which is laid up for you in heaven...”

- 1 Thessalonians 1:3: “...and patience of hope (elpidos) in our Lord Jesus Christ...”

- Hebrews 6:19: “This hope (elpida) we have as an anchor of the soul...”

- 1 Peter 1:3: “...has begotten us again to a living hope (elpida) through the resurrection...”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that the believer’s focus is "future-oriented."

It suggests that the "maintenance of good works" (Titus 3:8) is possible only because we are convinced of the "hope of eternal life" (Titus 3:7).

By describing the hope as "blessed," Paul emphasizes that the fulfillment of this expectation is the ultimate "profit" and "joy" of the Christian life.
