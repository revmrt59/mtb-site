Summary:

APPEARING - ( epiphaneia , Strong’s [2015])

A manifestation, out-shining, or visible arrival. In Titus 2:13, it refers to the climactic and brilliant return of Jesus Christ in glory, serving as the definitive event for which the believer waits with expectant hope.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the verb epiphaino (Strong’s [2014]), which is a compound of epi (upon) and phaino (to shine, to give light, or to appear).

Literally, it describes a "shining upon" or a "bright manifestation," carrying the image of light suddenly breaking through darkness to make something clearly visible.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

An appearance, a manifestation, or a brilliant coming.

In the New Testament, it is used exclusively to describe the interventions of Christ in human history—both His first coming in grace and His second coming in glory.

Lexically, it emphasizes the "visibility" and "splendor" of the event, distinguishing it from other words for "coming" by highlighting the radiant presence of the one appearing.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- The Royal Advent: In secular Greek, epiphaneia was used for the "advent" of a king or emperor to a city, where his majesty was visibly displayed to all the citizens.

- The Dawn of Day: It pictured the "shining forth" of the sun after a dark or stormy night, representing the total dispelling of shadows by the presence of light.

- The Divine Intervention: It was used to describe the "manifestation" of a deity who supposedly intervened to help an army in battle or to bring healing to a person, representing a sudden and supernatural rescue.

4. Theological Implications

Epiphaneia is the "visible" end of the "invisible" wait. It marks the transition from faith to sight.

Theologically, it establishes the deity of Christ; Paul refers to this as the appearing of "our great God and Savior," indicating that the radiance of the Father is fully manifest in the Son.

It represents the "blessed hope" (verse 13) that motivates the "denying" of ungodliness (verse 12), as the believer lives in the constant light of Christ's impending return.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:13

In the NKJV of Titus 2:13, Paul speaks of the "glorious appearing (epiphaneian) of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ."

- The Parallel: There is a deliberate parallel between the "appearing" of grace in verse 11 (epephane—the verb form) and the "appearing" of glory in verse 13.

- The Contrast: The first appearing (Incarnation) was a manifestation of God's kindness and saving grace; the second appearing (Second Coming) will be a manifestation of His supreme majesty and power.

- The Object of Sight: This appearing is what the believer is "looking for" (verse 13). It is the finish line of the "training" (verse 12) provided by grace.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- 2 Thessalonians 2:8: “...whom the Lord will consume... and destroy with the brightness of His coming (epiphaneia),”

- 1 Timothy 6:14: “...keep this commandment... until our Lord Jesus Christ’s appearing (epiphaneias),”

- 2 Timothy 1:10: “...but has now been revealed by the appearing (epiphaneias) of our Savior Jesus Christ,”

- 2 Timothy 4:1: “...who will judge the living and the dead at His appearing (epiphaneian) and His kingdom:”

- 2 Timothy 4:8: “...to all who have loved His appearing (epiphaneian).”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights the "certainty" of the event; light cannot be hidden once it "shines forth."

It suggests that the "maintenance of good works" (Titus 3:8) is the proper response of one who "loves" the light of this appearing.

By using epiphaneia, Paul ensures that the "hope of eternal life" (Titus 3:7) is not viewed as a vague or mystical concept, but as a historical, visible, and "glorious" reality.
