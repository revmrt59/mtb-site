Summary:

BLESSED - ( makarios , Strong’s [3107])

A state of divine favor, fulfillment, and satisfaction. In Titus 2:13, it characterizes the Christian's "hope" as something that originates from God's goodness and provides a transcendent joy and security that the world cannot provide or take away.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the root makar, an ancient Greek term referring to the state of the gods as being "above" the suffering and changes of human life.

In the New Testament, it is transformed to describe the spiritual prosperity and deep-seated well-being of the believer who is a recipient of God’s grace.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Happy, fortunate, or privileged.

It refers to a person who is "well-off" because they have been favored by God.

Unlike common happiness, which depends on "hap" (circumstance), makarios denotes a state of being that is independent of external conditions because it is grounded in a relationship with the Lord.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- The Island of the Blessed: In Greek poetry, this referred to a place of perfect peace and abundance where there was no labor or sorrow, representing a life of total "satisfaction."

- The Transcendent Life: It was used to describe the "unaffected" joy of the divine, suggesting that the "blessed" person possesses a quality of life that is not of this world.

- The Privileged Heir: It pictured someone who had come into a great inheritance, representing the "wealth" and "security" that belongs to those who are heirs of God.

4. Theological Implications

Makarios is the word used in the Beatitudes (Matthew 5) to describe those who are "blessed" in the Kingdom.

It represents the "God-kind of happiness"—a satisfaction that is rooted in God's approval rather than human success.

Theologically, it is the state of the "new man" who has been justified; because the believer’s sins are forgiven, they are truly "blessed" (Romans 4:7).

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:13

In the NKJV of Titus 2:13, Paul refers to the "blessed (makarian) hope."

- The Quality of the Hope: The hope is not just "good"; it is "blessed" because it is a source of divine joy and benefit.

- The Source: It is "blessed" because it comes from the "blessed God" (1 Timothy 1:11).

- The Distinction: This hope is a "privileged" expectation. It provides a sense of well-being even in the "present age" of ungodliness (verse 12) because it is anchored in the certainty of the "glorious appearing" of Christ.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Matthew 5:3: “Blessed (makarioi) are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.”

- 1 Timothy 1:11: “...according to the glorious gospel of the blessed (makariou) God...”

- 1 Timothy 6:15: “...He who is the blessed (makarios) and only Potentate, the King of kings...”

- James 1:12: “Blessed (makarios) is the man who endures temptation...”

- Revelation 1:3: “Blessed (makarios) is he who reads and those who hear the words of this prophecy...”

- Revelation 19:9: “...Blessed (makarioi) are those who are called to the marriage supper of the Lamb!”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that the "hope of eternal life" (Titus 3:7) is a "blessed" reality that should dominate the believer's outlook.

It suggests that "worldliness" (verse 12) is a poor substitute for the "blessedness" found in Christ.

By describing the hope as "blessed," Paul reminds the reader that the "maintenance of good works" (Titus 3:8) is performed by a person who is already spiritually "wealthy" and satisfied in God.
