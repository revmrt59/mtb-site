Summary:

GREAT - ( megalou , Strong’s [3173])

Large, mighty, or of supreme importance. In Titus 2:13, it emphasizes the infinite majesty, dignity, and absolute deity of Jesus Christ, ascribing to Him the highest possible rank of authority and power.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the primary root megas (Strong’s [3173]).

It is a foundational term in the Greek language used to describe greatness in any dimension—physical size, numerical quantity, intensity of force, or rank of dignity.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Large, great, loud, or important.

In the New Testament, when applied to persons, it denotes a position of high rank, excellence, or preeminence.

When used of God, it signifies His transcendence and the "weight" of His glory. It is a superlative of degree that places the subject above all others.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- The High Official: In Greek culture, megas was a title given to kings and commanders (e.g., "Alexander the Great") to distinguish them from subordinate rulers and to emphasize their sovereign power.

- The Mighty Force: It pictured a "great" storm or a "great" wind—forces of nature so powerful that they demand total submission and cannot be resisted by human effort.

- The Resounding Voice: Used for a "loud" (megale) cry or trumpet blast that signifies a royal proclamation or a divine summons that must be heard by everyone.

4. Theological Implications

The use of "great" in relation to God highlights His status as the "Supreme Being" who is incomparable to the idols of the nations.

It represents the infinite "scale" of His attributes, such as His "great" mercy or "great" power.

Theologically, it serves to anchor the believer’s confidence; the One who is coming is not merely a savior, but the "Great" God who has the authority to fulfill every promise.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:13

In the NKJV of Titus 2:13, Paul writes of the "appearing of our great (megalou) God and Savior Jesus Christ."

- The Granville Sharp Rule: According to Greek grammar, the single definite article "the" (tou) governs both "great God" and "Savior." This identifies both titles as belonging to a single person: Jesus Christ.

- Declaration of Deity: By placing "great" before "God," Paul leaves no ambiguity regarding the nature of Christ. He is not a lesser deity but the "Great God" in all His fullness.

- The Contrast of Advents: This greatness stands in contrast to the "emptying" of the first advent (Philippians 2:7). The second appearing will be the visible display of the greatness that was previously veiled.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Luke 1:32: “He will be great (megas), and will be called the Son of the Highest;”

- Hebrews 4:14: “Seeing then that we have a great (megan) High Priest who has passed through the heavens...”

- Hebrews 13:20: “...brought up our Lord Jesus from the dead, that great (megan) Shepherd of the sheep,”

- Revelation 19:17: “...that you may gather together for the supper of the great (megalou) God,”

- Matthew 22:38: “This is the first and great (megale) commandment.”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that the "blessed hope" (verse 13) is a "magnificent" expectation because the One who is appearing is "greater" than the present age.

It suggests that the "teaching" of grace (verse 12) is authoritative because it comes from the "Great" God.

By identifying Christ as "Great," Paul provides the ultimate reason why the "maintenance of good works" (Titus 3:8) is a "profitable" and noble pursuit for every believer.
