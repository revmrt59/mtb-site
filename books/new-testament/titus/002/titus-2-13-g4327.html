Summary:

LOOKING - ( prosdechomenoi , Strong’s [4327])

To receive favorably, to welcome, or to wait for with expectation. In Titus 2:13, it describes the active, continuous posture of the believer who lives in joyful anticipation of the return of Christ, welcoming the fulfillment of God’s promises as a present reality.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

A compound verb derived from the preposition pros, meaning "toward," and dechomai, meaning "to receive," "to take," or "to welcome."

The prefix pros intensifies the word, suggesting a "moving toward" to receive something. It carries the image of someone reaching out to embrace or welcome an expected guest.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

To admit, to receive to oneself, to expect, or to look for.

In the New Testament, it signifies an expectant waiting that is characterized by confidence and desire.

It is not a passive "watching" from a distance, but an active "welcoming" of a person or event that one knows is coming and highly values.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- The Gracious Host: In secular Greek, it pictured a host standing at the threshold of his home, watching the road and ready to "welcome" (prosdechomai) a beloved friend the moment they appear.

- The Legal Acceptance: It was used for "accepting" the terms of a treaty or "receiving" a legacy that had been promised in a will, representing the formal taking possession of what is rightfully one's own.

- The Watchman’s Relief: It described the "waiting" for the dawn or the arrival of a messenger, where the focus is entirely on the object of the search.

4. Theological Implications

"Looking" is the eschatalogical engine of the Christian life. It transforms "waiting" into "expecting."

It is the direct result of the "teaching" of grace (verse 12); grace trains us to look away from "worldly lusts" and toward the "blessed hope."

Theologically, it represents a heart that is already "aligned" with the coming Kingdom, living as if the Master’s return is the defining reality of the present.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:13

In the NKJV of Titus 2:13, it is a present middle participle: "looking (prosdechomenoi) for the blessed hope..."

- Continuous Action: The present tense indicates that this is a habitual, moment-by-moment lifestyle. The believer is always in a state of "welcoming" the return of Christ.

- Personal Involvement: The middle voice emphasizes that the believer is personally and deeply invested in this looking; it is the desire of their own heart.

- The Link: This word links the "denying" and "living" of verse 12 to the "appearing" of verse 13. We are able to live godly in the "present age" because we are "looking" for the "glory" of the next.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Mark 15:43: “...who was himself also waiting (prosdechomenos) for the kingdom of God...”

- Luke 2:25: “...and this man was just and devout, waiting (prosdechomenos) for the Consolation of Israel...”

- Luke 2:38: “...spoke of Him to all those who looked (prosdechomenois) for redemption in Jerusalem.”

- Luke 12:36: “and you yourselves be like men who wait (prosdechomenois) for their master...”

- Jude 1:21: “...looking (prosdechomenoi) for the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ unto eternal life.”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that "hope" in the Bible is never "wishful thinking"; it is the "certainty" of a guest who is already on the way.

It suggests that the "maintenance of good works" (Titus 3:8) is fueled by the "anticipation" of Christ’s reward and presence.

By "looking," the believer demonstrates that they are "sober" (verse 12) and not intoxicated by the "cares of this life" (Luke 21:34) that would dull their spiritual senses.
