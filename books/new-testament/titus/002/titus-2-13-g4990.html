Summary:

SAVIOR - ( soter , Strong’s [4990])

A deliverer, preserver, or rescuer. In Titus 2:13, it refers to Jesus Christ as the One who has decisively rescued His people from the penalty of sin and will return to complete their deliverance from the presence of sin.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the verb sozo (Strong’s [4982]), which means "to save," "to deliver," "to heal," or "to preserve."

The root signifies the act of bringing someone out of a state of extreme danger or death and into a state of safety and wholeness. It is a title that describes the very essence and mission of the Person who bears it.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

A savior, deliverer, or benefactor.

It denotes one who saves another from impending ruin or destruction.

In the New Testament, it is a title reserved for God the Father (the source of salvation) and Jesus Christ (the mediator of salvation), highlighting that salvation is entirely a divine work.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- The Civic Deliverer: In the Greco-Roman world, soter was a title given to emperors or military leaders who saved a city from war or a province from famine, representing a "savior" of the state.

- The Great Physician: It was used for healers who "saved" a patient from a fatal disease, signifying the restoration of life and health.

- The Nautical Rescuer: It pictured a pilot or a rescuer who saved a sailor from a shipwreck or a drowning state, representing the transition from certain death to secure life.

4. Theological Implications

The title "Savior" emphasizes that humanity is in a state of "helplessness" from which it cannot save itself.

It links the Old Testament concept of Yahweh as the only Savior (Isaiah 43:11) to the New Testament Person of Jesus Christ, affirming His full deity.

Theologically, the Savior does not just provide a "plan" for salvation; He is the salvation, and His return is the completion of the "saving" process begun at the cross.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:13

In the NKJV of Titus 2:13, the phrase is "our great God and Savior (soter) Jesus Christ."

- The Deity of Christ: Grammatically (the Granville Sharp Rule), "great God" and "Savior" are governed by a single article, making them titles for one Person—Jesus Christ. This is one of the strongest declarations of Christ's deity in the New Testament.

- The Goal of Looking: The believer is "looking" (verse 13) for the appearing of the Savior. This indicates that our "hope" is not in a concept but in a Person who has proven His "saving" power through His first appearing (verse 11).

- The Fullness of Deliverance: While His first coming provided the "grace" to save us from sin's penalty, His second coming as "Savior" will rescue us from sin's presence in a "glorious" manifestation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Luke 1:47: “And my spirit has rejoiced in God my Savior (soteri).”

- Luke 2:11: “For there is born to you this day in the city of David a Savior (soter), who is Christ the Lord.”

- John 4:42: “...and we know that this is indeed the Christ, the Savior (soter) of the world.”

- Acts 5:31: “Him God has exalted to His right hand to be a Prince and a Savior (sotera)...”

- Philippians 3:20: “...from which we also eagerly wait for the Savior (sotera), the Lord Jesus Christ,”

- Titus 1:4: “...Grace, mercy, and peace from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ our Savior (soteros).”

- Titus 3:6: “whom He poured out on us abundantly through Jesus Christ our Savior (soteros);”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that the "hope of eternal life" (Titus 3:7) is secure because it is guaranteed by the "Savior" who has already "appeared" (verse 11).

It suggests that the "maintenance of good works" (Titus 3:8) is the only appropriate response for a person who has been "rescued" from the "unprofitable" (Titus 3:9) nature of sin.

By using soter, Paul reminds Titus that the Gospel is a message of "rescue," not just "instruction," making it "profitable" for every person in the "present age."
