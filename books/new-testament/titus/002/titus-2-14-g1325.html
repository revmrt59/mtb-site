Summary:

GAVE - ( edoken / didomi , Strong’s [1325])

To yield, bestow, or deliver up of one’s own accord. In Titus 2:14, it refers to the voluntary and sacrificial act of Jesus Christ offering Himself as the ultimate gift to accomplish the redemption of His people.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the primary verb didomi. It is a foundational Greek word for the act of giving.

In Titus 2:14, it appears in the aorist tense (edoken), which signifies a completed, once-for-all action in the past.

The root emphasizes the "bestowal" of something from one person to another, often focusing on the generosity or authority of the giver.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

To give, to grant, to supply, or to deliver up.

In the context of the New Testament, it frequently describes God's gracious activity in providing salvation or the Spirit.

Lexically, when used with a reflexive pronoun (as in "He gave Himself"), it denotes a "self-surrender" or a "voluntary sacrifice" where the giver and the gift are identical.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- The Royal Bounty: In secular Greek, it pictured a king or a benefactor "bestowing" a gift or a title upon a subject, where the focus is on the king's free will and sovereign favor.

- The Legal Transfer: It described the "handing over" of property or a deed to another person, signifying a permanent change of ownership.

- The Willing Captive: It was used for a person who "delivered themselves up" to a specific cause or authority, representing a total commitment of one's life and destiny.

4. Theological Implications

The "giving" of Christ is the ultimate expression of divine love and substitutionary atonement.

It represents the "voluntary" nature of the sacrifice; Christ was not a victim of circumstance but a willing participant in the plan of redemption.

Theologically, this act is the fulfillment of the "grace" mentioned in verse 11, moving from the "appearing" of grace to the "action" of grace on the cross.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:14

In the NKJV of Titus 2:14, the text says Christ "gave (edoken) Himself for us."

- The Substitutionary Purpose: The use of the preposition "for" (hyper) indicates that He gave Himself "on behalf of" or "in the place of" His people.

- The Extent of the Gift: He did not just give His time or His teaching; He gave "Himself"—His life, His blood, and His entire person.

- The Result: This giving was for the specific purpose of "redeeming" us from every lawless deed and "purifying" a people for His own possession.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- John 3:16: “For God so loved the world that He gave (edoken) His only begotten Son...”

- John 10:28: “And I give (didomi) them eternal life, and they shall never perish;”

- Galatians 1:4: “who gave (dontos) Himself for our sins, that He might deliver us...”

- 1 Timothy 2:6: “who gave (dous) Himself a ransom for all, to be testified in due time,”

- 1 John 3:1: “Behold what manner of love the Father has bestowed (dedoken) on us...”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that the "maintenance of good works" (Titus 3:8) is the believer's response to the "gift" they have received.

It suggests that because Christ "gave Himself," the believer is now "His own special people" (verse 14), no longer belonging to the "present age" (verse 12).

By emphasizing the "giving," Paul reminds Titus that the foundation of all "godly" living (verse 12) is the historical and sacrificial event of the cross.
