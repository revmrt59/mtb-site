Summary:

PURIFY - ( katharizo , Strong’s [2511])

To cleanse, make clean, or purge from defilement. In Titus 2:14, it refers to the spiritual washing of the believer by Christ, removing the moral stains of sin to make them holy and fit for His personal possession and service.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the adjective katharos (Strong’s [2513]), which means "clean," "pure," or "unmixed."

The verb katharizo signifies the act of making a person or object katharos. It carries the idea of removing any external or internal impurity that prevents something from being used for its intended purpose.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

To cleanse, to make clean, to purge, or to declare clean.

In the New Testament, it is used in both a physical sense (cleansing a leper) and a moral sense (cleansing the heart or conscience).

Lexically, it describes a process of refinement and sanctification where the "dross" of sin is separated from the "gold" of a person's life, resulting in a state of moral integrity.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- The Winnowed Grain: In agricultural settings, it pictured the process of "cleansing" wheat by throwing it into the air so the wind could blow away the chaff, leaving only the "pure" grain.

- The Refined Metal: It described the "purging" of fire that removes impurities from gold or silver, representing a transformation through heat and pressure.

- The Ceremonial Vessel: In religious life, it pictured the "washing" of a cup or a bowl to make it ritually acceptable for use in a temple, representing the transition from "common" to "consecrated."

4. Theological Implications

Purification is a necessary step for fellowship with a holy God. Since God is light, anything defiled by sin must be "cleansed" before it can enter His presence.

It represents the "sanctifying" work of Christ that follows "justification." While redemption (verse 14) deals with the price of sin, purification deals with the pollution of sin.

Theologically, the blood of Christ is the agent of this cleansing, acting as the spiritual "detergent" that reaches the "conscience" (Hebrews 9:14) where no human effort can go.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:14

In the NKJV of Titus 2:14, it states that Christ gave Himself to "redeem us from every lawless deed and purify (katharise) for Himself His own special people."

- The Owner's Intent: The purification is "for Himself." This indicates that Christ is not just cleaning us for our own benefit, but so that we are suitable to be His "own special possession."

- The Goal of Grace: This is the positive goal that follows the negative "denying" of verse 12. Grace does not just leave us empty; it "purifies" the heart so it can be "zealous for good works."

- The Present Reality: While the full "glory" is future (verse 13), the "purifying" is a work that began at the cross and continues in the life of the believer in the "present age."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Matthew 8:3: “...immediately his leprosy was cleansed (ekatharisthe).”

- Acts 15:9: “...and made no distinction between us and them, purifying (katharisas) their hearts by faith.”

- Ephesians 5:26: “that He might sanctify and cleanse (katharisas) her with the washing of water by the word,”

- Hebrews 9:14: “...how much more shall the blood of Christ... cleanse (kathariei) your conscience from dead works...”

- 1 John 1:7: “...and the blood of Jesus Christ His Son cleanses (katharizei) us from all sin.”

- 1 John 1:9: “...and to cleanse (katharisai) us from all unrighteousness.”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that the "maintenance of good works" (Titus 3:8) requires a "clean" vessel. One cannot be "zealous" for God's works while remaining "defiled" by lawless deeds.

It suggests that the "washing of regeneration" (Titus 3:5) is the inaugural act of this purification process.

By "purifying" a people for Himself, Christ ensures that His followers are no longer "unprofitable" (Titus 3:9) but are instead "profitable and excellent" for the advancement of His kingdom.
