Summary:

GOOD - ( kalos , Strong’s [2570])

Beautiful, noble, or excellent in quality. In Titus 2:14, it describes works that are not only morally right but also aesthetically attractive and honorable, serving as a compelling testimony to the transforming power of God's grace.

Full Study:

  1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from a primary adjective meaning "beautiful" or "excellent." While often translated as "good," it differs from agathos (which emphasizes intrinsic moral goodness) by highlighting the outward manifestation of that goodness as being noble, commendable, and pleasing to the eye. It implies a harmony between inner character and outer conduct.

  1. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Beautiful, handsome, excellent, or eminent.

It signifies that which is fair and honorable in itself, and therefore attractive to others.

In the New Testament, it denotes moral excellence that is seen and appreciated by the world, such as a "good" conscience or "good" works that bring glory to God.

  1. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

    • The Fruitful Tree: In agricultural contexts, it described a tree that produced fruit that was not only edible but also vibrant and visually perfect, representing the healthy output of a healthy source.

    • The Honorable Soldier: It pictured the "noble" conduct of a soldier in battle—one who acts with courage and integrity, making the cause he serves appear attractive and worthy to observers.

    • The Finished Masterpiece: In the arts, it was used for a work that reached the highest standard of craftsmanship, where form and function were perfectly united to create something "beautiful."

  2. Theological Implications

"Goodness" of this kind is a reflection of the character of God, who is the source of all that is beautiful and noble.

The goal of the Christian life is to move beyond mere compliance with rules to a lifestyle that is "beautiful" because it reflects Christ.

Theologically, kalos works are the "ornament" of the doctrine (Titus 2:10), making the message of the Gospel "attractive" to those who do not yet believe.

  1. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:14

In the NKJV of Titus 2:14, the believer is to be "zealous for good (kalon) works."

  • The Contrast: These works stand in direct opposition to the "lawless deeds" (anomia) from which the believer was redeemed.

  • The Purpose of Redemption: Christ did not just save us from the penalty of sin; He saved us to produce "beautiful" fruit that demonstrates the reality of His kingdom.

  • The Witness: By performing works that are kalos, the "special people" (verse 14) provide a visible contrast to the "unprofitable" behaviors of the present age.

  1. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

    • Matthew 5:16: “...that they may see your good (kala) works and glorify your Father...”

    • 1 Timothy 1:18: “...that by them you may wage the good (kalen) warfare,”

    • 1 Timothy 6:12: “Fight the good (kalon) fight of faith...”

    • 2 Timothy 4:7: “I have fought the good (kalon) fight, I have finished the race...”

    • Titus 3:8: “...those who have believed in God should be careful to maintain good (kalon) works.”

    • Titus 3:14: “And let our people also learn to maintain good (kalon) works...”

  2. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that "good works" are intended to be "profitable" (Titus 3:8) for others.

It suggests that the "maintenance" of such works is a learned behavior that requires the "training" of grace (verse 12).

By pursuing kalos works, the believer ensures that their life is "excellent" and "praiseworthy," fulfilling the high calling of being God's "own special people."
