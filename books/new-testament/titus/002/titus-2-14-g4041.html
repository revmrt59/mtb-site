Summary:

SPECIAL - ( periousios , Strong’s [4041])

A people for one’s own possession; a treasured hoard. In Titus 2:14, it describes the Church as God’s private, costly property, chosen and set apart from the rest of the world for His unique purpose and pleasure.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the preposition peri (meaning "above," "beyond," or "around") and the present participle of eimi (ousia), meaning "to be" or "substance/property."

Literally, it means "being beyond" or "having over and above." It refers to that which is superfluous or extra—something a person keeps as a private, special reserve after all other needs are met.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Possessed as a distinctive or costly possession.

It signifies a people who belong exclusively to God.

Lexically, it is the Greek equivalent of the Hebrew segullah, which refers to a king’s private "jewel box" or personal treasure that is distinct from the general treasures of the kingdom.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- The King’s Private Hoard: In ancient times, a king might have a general treasury for the nation’s expenses, but he also had a periousios—a "private treasure" that he kept for himself and valued above all else.

- The Chosen Remnant: It pictured a specific portion of a larger group that was "selected out" because of its superior quality or the special affection of the owner.

- The Encircled Property: From peri (around), it suggests something that God has "put a circle around," claiming it as His own territory where no one else has a right to trespass.

4. Theological Implications

Being a "special people" is the direct result of "redemption" and "purification" (verse 14). We are not special in ourselves, but we are made special by the price Christ paid (lytroo).

It emphasizes the "exclusivity" of the relationship; the Church belongs to Christ in a way the world does not.

Theologically, it points to the "election" of God, where He sets His love upon a specific people to be the recipients of His grace and the reflectors of His glory.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:14

In the NKJV of Titus 2:14, Christ purified for Himself "His own special (periousion) people."

- The Ownership: The addition of "for Himself" emphasizes that the goal of the cross was not just our safety, but His possession.

- The Distinction: This people is no longer part of the "present age" (verse 12) or characterized by "lawless deeds." They are now a "separated" treasure.

- The Purpose: Being "special" is not a status of pride, but a position of service; this unique possession is specifically designed to be "zealous for good works."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV/LXX)

- Exodus 19:5 (LXX): “...then you shall be a special treasure (periousios) to Me above all people;”

- Deuteronomy 7:6 (LXX): “...the LORD your God has chosen you to be a people for Himself, a special treasure (periousion)...”

- Deuteronomy 14:2 (LXX): “...the LORD has chosen you to be a people for Himself, a special treasure (periousion)...”

- Deuteronomy 26:18 (LXX): “Also today the LORD has proclaimed you to be His special people (periousion)...”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that the "kindness and love of God" (Titus 3:4) has resulted in a radical change of "legal status."

It suggests that the "maintenance of good works" (Titus 3:8) is the family business of God's "special" children.

By identifying the Church as a "treasured possession," Paul reminds Titus that every believer is highly valued in the eyes of the Great God and Savior.
