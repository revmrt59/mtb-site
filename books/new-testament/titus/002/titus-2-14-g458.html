Summary:

LAWLESS DEED - ( anomia , Strong’s [458])

A state of being without law or in active defiance of the law. In Titus 2:14, it describes the condition of sin and rebellion from which Christ has liberated His people, moving them from a life of moral anarchy to one of holy devotion.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the alpha privative (a), meaning "without" or "no," and nomos (Strong’s [3551]), meaning "law."

Literally, it means "without law." It is the state of a person who either does not possess the law or, more significantly, lives in total disregard of it.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lawlessness, iniquity, wickedness, or a violation of the law.

It signifies a condition of the heart that rejects the authority of the Lawgiver. It is not merely the accidental breaking of a rule, but a settled disposition of "self-will" that places one's own desires above the divine standard.

Lexically, it is the direct opposite of dikaiosyne (righteousness), representing a life that has no governing principle other than its own impulses.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- The Outlaw: In secular Greek, it described an "anarchist" or an "outlaw" who lived outside the protection and the requirements of the civil law, representing a threat to the order of society.

- The Broken Hedge: It pictured a field where the boundary stones or hedges had been removed, allowing the land to be trampled and defiled by anything that passed by.

- The Rebellious Subject: It was used for a citizen who openly refused to acknowledge the decree of a king, choosing instead to live as if the king had no jurisdiction over their life.

4. Theological Implications

Anomia is the biblical definition of sin; 1 John 3:4 states that "sin is lawlessness."

It describes the "mystery of lawlessness" (2 Thessalonians 2:7) that is currently at work in the world, leading away from the truth of God.

Theologically, lawlessness is the native air of the "old man." It is the root cause of the "unprofitable" disputes and "foolish" genealogies mentioned in Titus 3:9.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:14

In the NKJV of Titus 2:14, Christ gave Himself to redeem us "from every lawless deed (anomias)."

- The Total Scope: The word "every" (pases) emphasizes that Christ’s redemption is comprehensive. No form of lawlessness is too deep for His ransom to reach.

- The Bondage: By using anomia, Paul identifies the specific "master" from which we were redeemed. We were once slaves to our own "self-law," but Christ has loosed those chains.

- The Transition: The goal of this redemption is to move the believer from "lawless deeds" to being "zealous for good works," showing that grace does not lead to a lack of law, but to the fulfillment of the "law of Christ."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Matthew 7:23: “...‘I never knew you; depart from Me, you who practice lawlessness (anomian)!’”

- Matthew 24:12: “And because lawlessness (anomian) will abound, the love of many will grow cold.”

- Romans 6:19: “...For just as you presented your members as slaves of uncleanness, and of lawlessness (anomia) leading to more lawlessness (anomian)...”

- 2 Corinthians 6:14: “...For what fellowship has righteousness with lawlessness (anomia)?”

- Hebrews 1:9: “You have loved righteousness and hated lawlessness (anomian)...”

- 1 John 3:4: “Whoever commits sin also commits lawlessness (anomian), and sin is lawlessness (anomia).”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that the problem of sin is essentially a "legal" and "moral" rebellion against God's sovereignty.

It suggests that the "maintenance of good works" (Titus 3:8) is the outward proof that a person has been successfully redeemed from the "lawless" life.

By identifying the object of redemption as "lawless deeds," Paul reinforces that the Gospel is "profitable" (Titus 3:8) because it addresses the very core of human rebellion.
