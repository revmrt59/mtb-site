BONDSERVANT - ( doulos , Strong’s [1401])

Summary:

A person in a state of permanent servitude, whose will is entirely consumed in the will of another. It describes a relationship of absolute ownership and obligation to a master.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the verb deo, meaning "to bind."

The term describes a person who is bound to another, indicating a condition where the individual’s own interests and choices are subordinated to the requirements of their owner.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

A slave, bondman, or servant.

It refers to one who is in a permanent position of subjection. Unlike a hired servant who works for a wage for a set time, a doulos is the property of the master and is obligated to serve for life.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- Roman Slavery: In the first-century Roman world, the doulos was a common figure with no legal rights, belonging entirely to the head of the household (despotes).

- Absolute Allegiance: It pictures a soldier or servant whose primary duty is to listen for the master’s command and execute it without question or hesitation.

- Total Dependence: It describes a relationship where the servant looks to the master for all provisions, including food, clothing, and shelter.

4. Theological Implications

The term is frequently used by New Testament writers to describe the believer’s relationship with Jesus Christ.

It emphasizes that the Christian has been "bought with a price" and is no longer their own, but is now bound to the will of God.

Christ Himself is described as taking the form of a doulos in His incarnation, showing that the path of the servant is the path of humility and obedience to the Father.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:9

In Titus 2:9, Paul addresses those who were literally in the social position of bondservants.

He instructs them to be submissive to their own masters to demonstrate the power of the Gospel through their work ethic and character.

The goal of the doulos is to live in such a way that their conduct provides a compelling testimony to the doctrine of God their Savior.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Matthew 20:27: “And whoever desires to be first among you, let him be your slave (doulos).”

- Romans 1:1: “Paul, a bondservant (doulos) of Jesus Christ, called to be an apostle...”

- Galatians 1:10: “...For if I still pleased men, I would not be a bondservant (doulos) of Christ.”

- Philippians 2:7: “but made Himself of no reputation, taking the form of a bondservant (doulos)...”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word is distinct from diakonos (deacon/servant), which focuses more on the act of service or ministry.

Doulos focuses on the relationship of subjection and ownership, emphasizing the Master's right to command the servant's life.

A faithful doulos is one who seeks the master’s interests above their own, recognizing that their life belongs to the one who purchased them.
