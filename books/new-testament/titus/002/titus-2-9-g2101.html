Summary

WELL-PLEASING - ( euarestos , Strong’s [2101])

Summary: To be fully agreeable, acceptable, or highly satisfying. It describes an action or attitude that meets the specific standards and approval of a superior.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation A compound word formed from the adverb eu, meaning "well" or "good," and the adjective arestos, meaning "pleasing" or "acceptable." The root verb is aresko, which means "to please" or "to satisfy." It signifies a state of being particularly or fully pleasing to another.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Acceptable, well-pleasing, or gratifying. In the New Testament, it denotes that which is fully acceptable to God or a person in authority. It suggests a quality of service that satisfies the requirements and expectations of the one being served.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - Civic Service: In ancient Greek inscriptions, this term was often used to describe citizens who had provided distinguished and acceptable service to their city. - Sacrificial: Used in religious contexts to describe a sacrifice that met all necessary requirements to be accepted by a deity. - The Approved Standard: It pictured a standard or "pitch" where conduct is perfectly "in tune" with the expectations of a master.

4. Theological Implications The word defines the primary aim of the believer’s life: to be acceptable to God in every thought and action. It is frequently linked to the concept of a "living sacrifice," where the believer's life is offered as a gift that is well-pleasing to the Lord. It emphasizes that obedience should be motivated by a desire to satisfy the heart of God rather than merely performing a duty.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:9 In Titus 2:9, bondservants are exhorted to be "well-pleasing in all things" to their masters. This indicates that their service should be characterized by a proactive spirit that seeks to satisfy the master's interests in every detail. Such behavior serves to make the doctrine of God attractive and undeniable to observers.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Romans 12:1: “...present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable (euarestos) to God...” - Romans 14:18: “For he who serves Christ in these things is acceptable (euarestos) to God...” - 2 Corinthians 5:9: “Therefore we make it our aim... to be well-pleasing (euarestos) to Him.” - Philippians 4:18: “...a sweet-smelling aroma, an acceptable sacrifice, well-pleasing (euarestos) to God.” - Colossians 3:20: “Children, obey your parents in all things, for this is well-pleasing (euarestos) to the Lord.”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights the quality of a believer's reliability and character. It is the action of being a person whose conduct is consistent and reliable in the eyes of their authority. When this standard is met, it demonstrates that sound doctrine produces a life that is steady and honorable in every transaction.
