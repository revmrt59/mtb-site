Summary

EXHORT - ( parakaleo , Strong’s [3870])

Summary:

To call to one’s side for the purpose of urging, encouraging, or instructing. It denotes a persuasive appeal that combines authoritative instruction with a supportive presence.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the preposition para, meaning "beside" or "alongside," and the verb kaleo, meaning "to call."

It describes the act of calling someone to stand near for assistance, advocacy, or to receive a message of encouragement.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

To appeal to, urge, or entreat with earnestness.

While often translated as "exhort" (to urge), it also encompasses "comfort" (to provide strength) and "beseech" (to make an urgent request).

In pastoral epistles, it refers to the act of providing instruction that motivates the hearer toward a specific godly duty.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- The Advocate: Used for a parakletos, an assistant or legal advocate called to stand by a defendant in court to provide support and speak on their behalf.

- The Military Commander: Pictured a general standing beside his troops before a battle to infuse them with courage and remind them of their duty.

- The "Alongside" Help: It describes a person who provides the necessary strength to a weary traveler to keep them from drifting.

4. Theological Implications

God is the primary source of paraklesis (encouragement/comfort), coming alongside His people in their distress.

The Holy Spirit is the Parakletos, the one who stays beside the believer to provide guidance and strength.

Therefore, the act of exhorting is a human reflection of the Spirit’s work, bringing the truth of God into immediate proximity with the believer's life.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:9

In Titus 2:9, the command is to "exhort bondservants." Paul uses this term to show that the instruction given to those in service is not to be a harsh demand, but a persuasive appeal to their will.

It links their daily conduct—being submissive and well-pleasing—to the spiritual motivation of representing the Gospel.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Matthew 5:4: “Blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be comforted (parakaleo).”

- Romans 12:1: “I beseech (parakaleo) you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God...”

- Hebrews 3:13: “but exhort (parakaleo) one another daily, while it is called ‘Today’...”

- 1 Thessalonians 4:18: “Therefore comfort (parakaleo) one another with these words.”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word represents the "active" side of pastoral care—it is the bridge between sound doctrine and sound living.

It is the action of being a supportive presence that provides both the "why" and the "how" of Christian conduct.

When a believer responds to this exhortation, they are walking in alignment with the truth that has been brought alongside them.
