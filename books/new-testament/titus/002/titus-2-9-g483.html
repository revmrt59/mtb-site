Summary

NOT ANSWERING BACK - ( antilego , Strong’s [483])

Summary:

To speak against, contradict, or oppose. In the context of a servant, it refers to a resistant or argumentative spirit that refuses to accept instruction or correction without verbal defiance.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from the preposition anti, meaning "against" or "opposed to," and the verb lego, meaning "to speak."

It literally describes the act of speaking against a command, a person, or a statement.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

To contradict, gainsay, or speak in opposition to.

It implies a refusal to develop a submissive spirit, manifested by verbal resistance or "talking back" when given a directive.

In the New Testament, it is often used to describe those who willfully oppose the truth or the authority of God.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

- Legal Contradiction: In secular Greek settings, it was used to describe a witness or a party in a lawsuit who contradicted the testimony of another.

- Political Defiance: It described those who spoke out against a royal decree or an established law in an attempt to subvert it.

- Social Argumentation: It identifies a person who is habitually contentious, always offering a counter-argument to every statement made by an authority figure.

4. Theological Implications

The term is frequently associated with rebellion and a lack of faith.

It characterizes the "contrary" nature of those who resist God’s messengers and His word.

Because the Gospel calls for a response of faith and submission, the act of "speaking against" represents an internal rejection of God's established order.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:9

In Titus 2:9, Paul explicitly commands bondservants "not to be answering back" (me antilegontas).

This negative prohibition is the counterpart to being "well-pleasing."

It prohibits the habit of making excuses, grumbling, or offering verbal resistance to the master’s instructions, which would otherwise diminish the testimony of the believer.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Luke 2:34: “...and for a sign which will be spoken against (antilego).”

- John 19:12: “...Whoever makes himself a king speaks against (antilego) Caesar.”

- Acts 13:45: “...and contradicting (antilego) and blaspheming, they opposed the things spoken by Paul.”

- Romans 10:21: “All day long I have stretched out My hands to a disobedient and contrary (antilego) people.”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights the importance of the tongue as an index of the heart’s submission.

It suggests that true obedience includes the silence of the ego, where a person does not feel the need to justify themselves or challenge their superior.

To avoid "answering back" is to demonstrate a quiet and stable spirit that trusts in the providence of the roles God has assigned.
