OBEDIENT - ( hypotasso , Strong’s [5293])

Summary:

To place or rank under; to subject oneself to a superior authority. It describes a voluntary attitude of giving in, cooperating, and assuming responsibility under a leader.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

* A compound verb derived from the preposition hypo, meaning "under," and the verb tasso, meaning "to arrange" or "to set in order."

* It describes the act of arranging oneself in an orderly fashion under the authority of another.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

* To be in subjection, to submit, or to obey.

* While often translated as "obedient" in certain contexts, it specifically refers to the act of submitting oneself to a designated authority or order.

* It is a functional term used to describe a voluntary submission to a position of leadership to ensure proper order.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

* Military Rank: In secular Greek, it was primarily a military term used to describe soldiers being arranged in proper order under the command of a leader.

* Political Order: It was used to describe the submission of citizens to the laws and governing authorities of a state.

* Orderly Arrangement: It pictured a structure or system where every part remains in its appointed place to ensure stability.

4. Theological Implications

* Submission is rooted in the recognition of the order established by God within the world.

* Christ Himself modeled this quality by being subject to His earthly parents and ultimately to the will of the Father.

* Believers are encouraged to practice submission as an expression of their trust in God's sovereignty and His arrangement of authority.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 2:9

* In Titus 2:9, bondservants are exhorted to be "obedient" (hypotasso) to their own masters.

* The word suggests a willingness to fit into the structure of the household to ensure peace and efficiency.

* This voluntary submission is intended to be a testimony of the character produced by the Gospel.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

* Luke 2:51: “Then He went down with them and came to Nazareth, and was subject (hypotasso) to them...”

* Romans 13:1: “Let every soul be subject (hypotasso) to the governing authorities.”

* Ephesians 5:21: “submitting (hypotasso) to one another in the fear of God.”

* James 4:7: “Therefore submit (hypotasso) to God. Resist the devil and he will flee from you.”

7. Other Relevant Insights

* This word represents a heart that is at peace with authority and order.

* It is the act of being a person who contributes to harmony rather than chaos within a community or household.

* When this quality is present, it demonstrates a humility that recognizes the functional roles within human relationships.
