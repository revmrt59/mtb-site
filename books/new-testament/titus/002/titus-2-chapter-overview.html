Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 2
Main Idea:

Paul directs Titus to teach every group in the church the conduct that fits sound doctrine, and he grounds that life in the saving grace of God, which trains believers to live godly lives while they wait for the appearing of Jesus Christ.

Setting:

Having exposed teachers whose works deny God (1:16), Paul turns to Titus with “But as for you” (2:1) and describes the healthy life that should mark the Cretan households and churches instead.

Chapter Outline:

  • Titus 2:1–10 — Teaching Fitting for Sound Doctrine

  • Titus 2:11–14 — The Grace of God That Brings Salvation

  • Titus 2:15 — Speak with All Authority

Key Verse:

“For the grace of God that brings salvation has appeared to all men, teaching us that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we should live soberly, righteously, and godly in the present age” (Titus 2:11–12)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Titus 2 answers the false teaching of chapter 1 with healthy teaching. “Sound doctrine” (2:1) is teaching that makes people spiritually healthy, and Paul shows what it produces in each part of the church: older men “sound in faith, in love, in patience” (2:2), older women who are “teachers of good things” (2:3), young women and young men marked by self-control, and Titus himself as “a pattern of good works” (2:7). Even bondservants, enslaved people with the least standing in Cretan society, can “adorn the doctrine of God our Savior in all things” (2:10). All through the section Paul keeps the watching world in view, so that God’s word is not blasphemed (2:5) and opponents have “nothing evil to say” (2:8).

The word “For” (2:11) then opens the reason beneath every instruction. “The grace of God that brings salvation has appeared to all men,” and that same grace teaches believers to deny ungodliness and to live godly lives now (2:11–12). They live between two appearings: grace has already appeared, and they look for the “glorious appearing of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ” (2:13). Christ “gave Himself for us” to redeem and purify a people of His own, “zealous for good works” (2:14). In this chapter God’s grace both rescues His people and remakes the way they live.

Chapter Sections

Titus 2:1–10 — Teaching Fitting for Sound Doctrine

Titus is to teach older men, older women, young women, young men, and bondservants the conduct that fits healthy doctrine, while he himself shows a pattern of good works and sound speech. In every group the aim is a life that keeps God’s word from being blasphemed and makes the doctrine of God our Savior attractive.

Titus 2:11–14 — The Grace of God That Brings Salvation

God’s saving grace has appeared to people of every kind, and it trains believers to deny ungodliness and live godly lives while they wait for the glorious appearing of Jesus Christ, our great God and Savior. Christ gave Himself to redeem and purify a people of His own, eager to do good works.

Titus 2:15 — Speak with All Authority

Titus is to teach these truths, encouraging and correcting with all authority, and to let no one despise him, because his authority rests in the message God has entrusted to him.

Why This Chapter Matters

Titus 2 shows that sound doctrine is measured by the people it produces. Paul does not leave healthy teaching abstract. He describes how it looks in an older man’s patience, a young man’s self-control, a mother’s love for her children, and a slave’s honesty (2:2–10). Godliness is not reserved for leaders or for the socially respected, and the gospel is commended or discredited in ordinary homes.

The chapter also holds grace and good works together without confusing them. Salvation comes from God’s grace, won by Christ’s self-giving (2:11, 14), and good works are the fruit of that redemption, never its price. Because the grace that saves also teaches (2:12), a believer who claims grace while living carelessly has misunderstood it.

Key Themes to Watch For

Healthy teaching. Sound doctrine produces sound faith, love, patience, and speech (2:1–2, 8).

Adorning the doctrine. The everyday conduct of believers either commends the gospel or gives outsiders a reason to speak against it (2:5, 8, 10).

Grace that teaches. God’s saving grace trains believers to renounce ungodliness and live godly lives in the present age (2:11–12).

The blessed hope. Believers live now in light of the glorious appearing of Jesus Christ (2:13).

Zeal for good works. Christ redeemed a people eager to do good, and Titus must model it himself (2:7, 14).

Key Words & Concepts

Sound doctrine (2:1). The word translated “sound” (Greek hygiainō) means to be healthy. Sound doctrine is healthy teaching, teaching that produces spiritually healthy, mature, and godly believers. The NLT renders it “wholesome teaching.”

Self-control. Behind “temperate” (2:2), “discreet” (2:5), “sober-minded” (2:6), and “soberly” (2:12) stands one family of Greek words for a sound, self-controlled mind. Paul asks it of nearly every group, and in 2:12 grace itself teaches it.

Bondservants (2:9–10). These were enslaved people, not simply employees. Paul addresses them directly as believers whose faithful conduct can adorn the gospel. His instructions describe their actual situation; they do not endorse the ownership of people or excuse their mistreatment.

Appearing (2:11, 13). God’s grace “has appeared” in Christ’s first coming, and believers now look for His “glorious appearing.” Paul places believers between these two appearings without setting out a timetable of the end.

“Our great God and Savior Jesus Christ” (2:13). Paul names Jesus Christ as both God and Savior. The NLT reads the verse the same way: “our great God and Savior, Jesus Christ.”

“His own special people” (2:14). Christ purifies a people who belong to Him. The phrase recalls God’s words to Israel, “you shall be a special treasure to Me above all people” (Exodus 19:5), and applies that language to all whom Christ redeems.
