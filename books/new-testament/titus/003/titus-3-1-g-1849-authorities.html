Summary:

AUTHORITIES - ( exousia , Strong’s [1849]) Summary: The delegated right to exercise power or the moral and legal authority to rule. It signifies the jurisdiction granted to an individual or office to act within a specific sphere.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the verb exesti, meaning "it is lawful" or "it is permitted." It describes a state of having the freedom or the right to act based on a legal or moral standing.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition The power of choice, the liberty of doing as one pleases, or the power of authority and of influence. Unlike dynamis (inherent power or ability), exousia specifically refers to the "right" to use that power, often delegated by a higher source. It identifies the legitimate right to command or to govern.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - Judicial Right: In secular Greek, it was used for a magistrate's right to execute laws and pass judgment within their assigned jurisdiction. - Freedom of Action: It pictured the unhindered liberty a person has over their own property or decisions. - The Delegated Rule: It describes a steward who has been given the master's authority to manage the household in his absence.

4. Theological Implications All authority is ultimately derived from God, who is the source of all right and power. Jesus claimed that "all authority" (exousia) had been given to Him in heaven and on earth, signifying His delegated role from the Father. Believers are called to recognize that human authorities are established by God's providence to maintain order and justice in a fallen world.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:1 In Titus 3:1, Paul instructs Titus to remind believers to be subject to "rulers and authorities" (exousiais). This emphasizes that Christians are to respect the office and the delegated right of civil leaders to govern, even in a difficult culture. Recognizing these authorities is a way of acknowledging the order of human society.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Matthew 28:18: “And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, ‘All authority (exousia) has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.’” - John 1:12: “But as many as received Him, to them He gave the right (exousia) to become children of God...” - Romans 13:1: “Let every soul be subject to the governing authorities (exousia). For there is no authority (exousia) except from God...” - Revelation 22:14: “...that they may have the right (exousia) to the tree of life...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights the responsibility of those in power to act according to the law that granted them authority. It serves as a reminder that authority is a trust to be exercised for the good of those under its jurisdiction. For the believer, submission to authority is an act of obedience to God, showing a heart that is aligned with order.
