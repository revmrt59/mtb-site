Summary:

READY - ( hetoimos , Strong’s [2090]) Summary: To be prepared, at hand, or fit for a specific purpose. It describes a state where all necessary preliminary work is complete, allowing for immediate action or service.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the root heteos, which implies fitness or being adjusted. It describes a state of being specifically "set" or "prepared" for a designated task or occasion.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Prepared, ready, or at hand. It signifies that a person or thing is in a state of completion regarding preparation, leaving nothing remaining to be done except the action itself. In the New Testament, it often refers to being morally or spiritually prepared for a divine appointment or for a work of service.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Banquet: In secular Greek, it was used to describe a meal that was fully prepared and the table set, with the host awaiting the arrival of the guests. - The Military Post: It pictured soldiers standing at their assigned positions, fully armed and ready to move the moment a command is issued. - The Clear Path: It described a road that had been prepared and cleared of all obstacles so that a king or traveler could pass without hindrance.

4. Theological Implications Readiness is a fundamental characteristic of a life lived in constant anticipation of God's timing and opportunities for service. It reflects a heart that has been "set in order" by the truth, transforming the individual into a vessel that is fit for the Master's use. A lack of readiness is associated with a lack of watchfulness, whereas being "ready" is the evidence of a life that is actively surrendered to the Lord's will.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:1 In Titus 3:1, Paul instructs Titus to remind believers to be "ready for every good work." This suggests that the believer's preparation should be comprehensive, maintaining a constant state of "fitness" for any good deed rather than waiting for a crisis to prepare. It implies a proactive and available spirit that is prepared to respond to the needs of the community as an expression of the faith.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Matthew 22:4: “...my oxen and fatted cattle are killed, and all things are ready (hetoimos). Come to the wedding.” - Matthew 24:44: “Therefore you also be ready (hetoimos), for the Son of Man is coming at an hour you do not expect.” - Matthew 25:10: “...and those who were ready (hetoimos) went in with him to the wedding; and the door was shut.” - 2 Corinthians 9:5: “...that your generous gift may be ready (hetoimos) as a matter of generosity...” - 1 Peter 3:15: “...and always be ready (hetoimos) to give a defense to everyone who asks you a reason for the hope that is in you...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights the importance of the "preparation phase" of the Christian life, where instruction is translated into practical availability. It implies that a "good work" is not an accidental occurrence but the result of a life that has been intentionally arranged for service. To be "ready" is to demonstrate that priorities are aligned with God's design, ensuring one is never caught "unfit" when an opportunity to represent Him arises.
