Summary:

OBEDIENT - ( peitharcheo , Strong’s [3980]) Summary: To obey authority, particularly a magistrate or ruler. It refers to a conviction that leads to the active execution of commands issued by those in power.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the verb peitho, meaning "to persuade" or "to be convinced," and arche, meaning "rule" or "magistrate." It describes a state of being persuaded of the legitimacy of a ruler, resulting in active compliance with their orders.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition To obey one in authority. While hypotasso (subject) refers to the internal attitude of submission to a rank or order, peitharcheo emphasizes the external and active obedience to specific commands. It signifies a settled state of being convinced to obey the laws or the directives of a superior.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Civil Citizen: In secular Greek, it was used to describe the conduct of a citizen who consistently obeys the directives of the city's magistrates. - The Persuaded Will: It pictured someone who has heard the reasons for a law and is so convinced of its necessity that they obey it freely. - The Specific Order: It describes the action taken by a subordinate when a specific command is given by an officer of high rank.

4. Theological Implications The word implies that the believer's obedience to government should stem from a persuasion that such authority is permitted by God for the sake of order. Obedience to God (peitharcheo) takes precedence over obedience to men if the two conflict, as seen in the apostolic response to the authorities in Jerusalem. It suggests that a life of outward obedience is the result of an inward conviction regarding the truth and the divine arrangement of authority.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:1 In Titus 3:1, Paul links being "subject" (hypotasso) with being "obedient" (peitharcheo). Being subject is the underlying attitude of recognizing authority; being obedient is the practical outworking of that attitude in daily actions. This ensures that the believer is not only respectful of the position of the ruler but also diligent in following the specific laws of the land.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Acts 5:29: “But Peter and the other apostles answered and said: ‘We ought to obey (peitharcheo) God rather than men.’” - Acts 5:32: “...and so also is the Holy Spirit whom God has given to those who obey (peitharcheo) Him.” - Acts 27:21: “...Men, you should have listened (peitharcheo) to me, and not have sailed from Crete...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word represents the active side of a believer's testimony in society. It is the response of a heart that recognizes the functional necessity of leadership and societal order. By being "obedient," the believer avoids the charge of being lawless or rebellious, providing a clear demonstration of the peaceable nature of the faith.
