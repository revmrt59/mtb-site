Summary:

REMIND - ( hypomimnesko , Strong’s [5263]) Summary: To call to mind or bring to remembrance. It involves prompting someone to recollect truths or duties they already know but may have momentarily forgotten or neglected.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the preposition hypo, meaning "under," and the verb mimnesko, meaning "to remind." It suggests a prompting that brings a thought back up to the surface of one's consciousness.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition To cause someone to remember or to bring a matter to their mind. It implies that the knowledge is already present but requires a specific stimulus to be acted upon. In a biblical context, it is the act of reinforcing established apostolic instructions and moral requirements.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Gentle Whisper: In secular Greek, it was used for an assistant who would quietly remind a superior of a name or a fact during a public engagement. - The Signpost: It pictured a marker that directs a traveler back to a path they have previously walked but may have strayed from. - The Mental Trigger: It identifies the process of pulling a specific duty out of memory and placing it into the active will of the individual.

4. Theological Implications The ministry of reminding is a vital work of the Holy Spirit, who brings the words of Christ to the remembrance of the believers. Because humans are prone to spiritual forgetfulness, constant repetition of the Word is necessary for perseverance and consistency. It emphasizes that spiritual growth often involves reclaiming and applying truths already received rather than always seeking new information.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:1 In Titus 3:1, Paul instructs Titus to "remind them to be subject to rulers and authorities." This suggests that the Cretan believers were already aware of their civic duties, but the rebellious environment of their culture necessitated regular reminders to remain obedient. The goal is to ensure their public conduct remains aligned with the standards of the Gospel.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Luke 22:61: “And Peter remembered (hypomimnesko) the word of the Lord, how He had said to him...” - John 14:26: “...He will teach you all things, and bring to your remembrance (hypomimnesko) all things that I said to you.” - 2 Timothy 2:14: “Remind (hypomimnesko) them of these things, charging them before the Lord...” - 2 Peter 1:12: “For this reason I will not be negligent to remind (hypomimnesko) you always of these things, though you know them...” - Jude 1:5: “But I want to remind (hypomimnesko) you, though you once knew this...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights the responsibility of the teacher to reiterate the truth rather than seeking novel doctrines. It serves as a safeguard for the believer, keeping their focus on the foundational aspects of Christian living. Responding to a reminder is an act of humility, acknowledging the need for the Word to be continually set before the mind to guide one's actions.
