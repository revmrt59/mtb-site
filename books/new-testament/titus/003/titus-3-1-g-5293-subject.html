Summary:

SUBJECT - ( hypotasso , Strong’s [5293]) Summary: To place or rank under. It describes a voluntary attitude of cooperation and assuming responsibility under a leader or authority within an established order.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the preposition hypo, meaning "under," and the verb tasso, meaning "to arrange" or "to set in order." It literally means to arrange oneself in an orderly fashion under the authority of another.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition To be in subjection, to submit, or to obey. It refers to the act of submitting oneself to a designated authority or order. It is a functional term used to describe a voluntary submission to a position of leadership to ensure proper order within a system.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - Military Rank: In secular Greek, it was a military term used to describe soldiers being arranged in proper order under the command of a leader. - Political Order: Used to describe the submission of citizens to the laws and governing authorities of a state. - The Arranged Structure: It pictured a building or system where every part remains in its appointed place to ensure stability and function.

4. Theological Implications The term is rooted in the recognition of the order established by God within the created world. Christ Himself modeled this quality by being subject to His earthly parents and ultimately to the will of the Father. Submission does not imply a difference in essence or value, but a difference in function within a divinely appointed hierarchy.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:1 In Titus 3:1, Paul instructs Titus to remind believers to be "subject" (hypotasso) to rulers and authorities. The word suggests a willingness to fit into the civil structure to ensure peace and social order. This voluntary submission is intended to be a testimony of the character produced by the Gospel in the lives of believers.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Luke 2:51: “Then He went down with them and came to Nazareth, and was subject (hypotasso) to them...” - Romans 13:1: “Let every soul be subject (hypotasso) to the governing authorities.” - Ephesians 5:21: “submitting (hypotasso) to one another in the fear of God.” - James 4:7: “Therefore submit (hypotasso) to God. Resist the devil and he will flee from you.”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word represents a heart that is at peace with authority and order. It is the act of being a person who contributes to harmony rather than chaos within a community or household. When this quality is present, it demonstrates a humility that recognizes the functional roles within human relationships.
