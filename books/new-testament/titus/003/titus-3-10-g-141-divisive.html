Summary:

DIVISIVE - ( hairetikos , Strong’s [141]) Summary: One who causes division or creates a faction. It describes a person who chooses their own opinions over revealed truth, leading to a departure from the unity of the faith and the creation of a sectarian spirit.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the verb haireomai (Strong's [138]), which means "to choose" or "to take for oneself." The related noun hairesis refers to a "choice," and by extension, a "sect" or "faction" formed by those who follow a particular choice or opinion.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Causing division, factious, or sectarian. In the New Testament, it refers to a person who creates a rift in the body of Christ by promoting private opinions or false doctrines that lead away from the established truth. While later used to mean "heretic" in a strictly doctrinal sense, its primary biblical emphasis is on the divisive nature of the person's conduct and influence.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Philosophic School: In secular Greek, hairesis was used neutrally for a "school of thought" or a philosophical sect (like the Stoics or Epicureans) chosen by an individual. - The Political Faction: It pictured a group that separated itself from the main body of citizens to pursue its own specific interests or leaders. - The Choice of Path: It described the act of "picking out" a specific way for oneself, often implying a rejection of the "common road" followed by others.

4. Theological Implications A "divisive" person is one who places their own "choice" or "opinion" above the unity and doctrine of the church. This spirit of faction is identified as a "work of the flesh" (Galatians 5:20) and is the opposite of the "washing of regeneration" that should produce humble service. The danger is not just the error itself, but the "self-willed" attitude that refuses correction and insists on drawing others away into a private circle.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:10 In Titus 3:10, Paul commands Titus to "reject a divisive (hairetikon) man after the first and second admonition." This follows the warning against "foolish disputes" (verse 9), identifying the person who refuses to avoid such things and instead uses them to tear the church apart. The instruction is practical: if the person persists in their "choice" of division after being warned, the leader must protect the flock by removing their influence.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Acts 5:17 (Noun): “...and all those who were with him (which is the sect (hairesis) of the Sadducees)...” - Acts 24:14 (Noun): “But this I confess to you, that according to the Way which they call a sect (hairesis)...” - 1 Corinthians 11:19 (Noun): “For there must also be factions (haireseis) among you, that those who are approved may be recognized...” - Galatians 5:20 (Noun): “idolatry, sorcery, hatred, contentions, jealousies, outbursts of wrath, selfish ambitions, dissensions, heresies (haireseis),” - 2 Peter 2:1 (Noun): “...who will secretly bring in destructive heresies (haireseis), even denying the Lord who bought them...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that a person can be "divisive" even if their doctrine is partially correct, if their manner is aimed at creating a personal following. It suggests that the "unity of the Spirit" is a treasure that must be guarded against those who value their own "choice" more than the body of Christ. The "rejection" mentioned in this verse is a protective measure, recognizing that a person who is "warped and sinning" (verse 11) cannot be allowed to continue "striving about the law" within the assembly.
