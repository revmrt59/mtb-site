Summary:

ADMONITION - ( nouthesia , Strong’s [3559]) Summary: A training by word of mouth, encouragement, or correction. It describes a gentle yet firm appeal to the mind and conscience, intended to steer a person away from error and toward a right path through verbal instruction and warning.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation A compound word derived from nous, meaning "mind" or "intellect," and the root of tithemi, meaning "to place" or "to set." It literally means "to place in the mind," signifying the act of depositing a specific thought or instruction into someone's consciousness to influence their behavior.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Admonition, warning, or instruction. It signifies a verbal appeal that seeks to correct a person's thinking or conduct before more severe measures are required. In the New Testament, it is used for the "training" of children and the "counseling" of believers, focusing on the preventive and corrective power of the Word.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Parent’s Guidance: In secular Greek, it described the way a father would sit with his son to "put sense into him" through stories and warnings about the dangers of life. - The Gentle Nudge: It pictured a shepherd using his voice or a slight touch of the staff to guide a sheep back to the path before it reaches a cliff. - The Warning Sign: It was used for a signal or a landmark that "tells" the traveler of a coming hazard, allowing them to adjust their course in time.

4. Theological Implications Admonition is an act of love and mercy; it is the "first line of defense" against spiritual drift. It is not a harsh condemnation but a rational and spiritual plea based on the authority of God’s Word. The goal is restoration-to "place the mind" of Christ into the person so that they might self-correct and avoid the consequences of their current trajectory.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:10 In Titus 3:10, Paul instructs Titus to offer a "first and second admonition (nouthesian)" to a divisive person. This shows that the "rejection" (disciplinary action) is not impulsive; it is preceded by repeated, patient efforts to correct the person’s "choice" of division. It identifies that the problem is in the "mind" (nous) of the divisive man, which needs to be reached by the truth before his influence can be neutralized.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - 1 Corinthians 10:11: “Now all these things happened to them as examples, and they were written for our admonition (nouthesian)...” - Ephesians 6:4: “And you, fathers... bring them up in the training and admonition (nouthesia) of the Lord.” - Colossians 3:16 (Related Verb): “...teaching and admonishing (noutheteountes) one another in psalms and hymns...” - 1 Thessalonians 5:14 (Related Verb): “...warn (noutheteite) those who are unruly, comfort the fainthearted...” - 2 Thessalonians 3:15 (Related Verb): “Yet do not count him as an enemy, but admonish (noutheteite) him as a brother.”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that Christian discipline begins with "teaching" and "warning" rather than "punishing." It suggests that the "faithful saying" of the Gospel should be the content of every admonition, as it is the only thing capable of truly "renewing" the mind. By requiring two admonitions, Paul ensures that the divisive person has been given a fair opportunity to repent, making their subsequent rejection a result of their own "self-condemnation."
