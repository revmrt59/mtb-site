Summary:

WARPED - ( ekstrepho , Strong’s [1612]) Summary: To turn out, twist, or pervert. It describes a person whose inner character has become distorted or turned inside out, resulting in a persistent departure from the straight path of truth.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation A compound verb derived from ek, meaning "out" or "from," and strepho, meaning "to turn" or "to twist." It literally means "to turn inside out" or "to twist out of place," suggesting a dislocation from the original or intended state.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition To turn out of the way, to pervert, or to twist. In the New Testament, it signifies a moral and intellectual distortion where a person is no longer "straight" in their thinking or conduct. It denotes a settled state of being "turned away" from the "faithful saying" of the Gospel.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Dislocated Joint: In medical Greek, it was used for a limb that had been "twisted out" of its socket, making it useless and painful. - The Inside-Out Garment: It pictured a piece of clothing that has been "turned out," showing the seams and the rough interior rather than the finished exterior. - The Diverted Stream: It described a river that has been "turned out" of its natural bed, causing it to flood or dry up, representing a life that has left its proper course.

4. Theological Implications A person who is "warped" has undergone a spiritual inversion; they have replaced the "renewing of the Spirit" with the "lusts of the flesh." This condition is often the result of persistent "foolish disputes" and a refusal to heed "admonition." It identifies a heart that is no longer "upright" before God, having become "twisted" by pride and self-will.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:11 In Titus 3:11, Paul explains why the divisive man must be rejected: "knowing that such a person is warped (exestrapitai)..." The perfect tense indicates a completed state with ongoing results; the man has become so "twisted" that simple instruction is no longer effective. This "warped" condition is the internal cause of the "divisive" external behavior, showing that his "choice" of error has fundamentally changed his nature.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Deuteronomy 32:20 (LXX): “...For they are a perverse (exestrammene) generation, children in whom is no faith.” - Psalm 18:26 (LXX 17:27): “...And with the devious You will show Yourself shrewd (ekstrepho-to twist or turn).” - 1 Timothy 1:10 (Related Concept): “...and if there is any other thing that is contrary (antikeimai-to lie opposite) to sound doctrine,” - Titus 1:14 (Related Concept): “...and commandments of men who turn from (apostrepho) the truth.”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that "heresy" is not just a mistake in facts, but a "twisting" of the soul. It suggests that a "warped" person is someone who has lost their "spiritual alignment" and can no longer walk in the "good works" God prepared. The goal of "rejecting" such a person is to acknowledge that they are "turned out" of the fellowship and are in need of a radical "re-turning" (repentance).
