Summary:

SINNING - ( hamartano , Strong’s [264]) Summary: To miss the mark, to wander from the path, or to forfeit a share. It describes a persistent and willful departure from God’s standard of righteousness, resulting in a state of moral failure and guilt.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the root ha-, meaning "not," and meros, meaning "a part" or "a share." It literally means "to be without a share" or "to miss the prize," suggesting a failure to reach a designated goal or destination.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition To miss the mark, to err, or to do wrong. In the New Testament, it signifies a violation of God's law, whether through commission (doing what is forbidden) or omission (failing to do what is required). It denotes a departure from the straight line of divine truth, resulting in a state of offense against a holy God.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Archer: In secular Greek, it was the technical term for an archer who shoots an arrow but "misses the mark" (hamartia), failing to hit the target. - The Traveler: It pictured a traveler who takes a wrong turn and "wanders from the path," losing their way and failing to reach their destination. - The Forfeited Prize: It described a person who, through their own fault or error, "loses their share" of an inheritance or a reward.

4. Theological Implications Sin is not merely a mistake; it is a spiritual failure to meet the standard of God's glory (Romans 3:23). It identifies a condition of "missing the mark" of the "good works" for which the believer was created (Ephesians 2:10). Because God is the Lawgiver, every sin is a personal rebellion against His authority, requiring the "washing of regeneration" for a person to be restored.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:11 In Titus 3:11, Paul describes the divisive man as one who is "warped and sinning (hamartanei)." The use of the present participle indicates a continuous, habitual action; the man is not merely a victim of a single slip, but is "persistently missing the mark." His sin is specifically tied to his "warped" nature, showing that his outward rebellion flows from an inward distortion that he refuses to correct.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Matthew 18:15: “Moreover if your brother sins (hamartano) against you, go and tell him his fault...” - Romans 3:23: “for all have sinned (hamartano) and fall short of the glory of God,” - Romans 6:15: “What then? Shall we sin (hamartano) because we are not under law but under grace?” - 1 Corinthians 15:34: “Awake to righteousness, and do not sin (hamartano); for some do not have the knowledge of God.” - 1 John 3:6: “Whoever abides in Him does not sin (hamartano). Whoever sins (hamartano) has neither seen Him nor known Him.”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that "heresy" and "division" are not just intellectual differences, but are categorized by God as "sin." It suggests that a person who is "sinning" in this way is actively walking away from the "hope of eternal life" mentioned in verse 7. The "sinning" of the divisive man is what makes him "self-condemned," as his own actions prove that he has rejected the "faithful saying" of the Gospel.
