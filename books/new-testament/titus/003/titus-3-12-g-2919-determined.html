Summary:

DETERMINED - ( krino , Strong’s [2919]) Summary: To decide, resolve, or judge. It describes a settled conclusion reached after mental deliberation, representing a firm intention to carry out a specific course of action.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the root kri-, which suggests a separation or a distinction. It carries the core idea of "sifting" through options or information to arrive at a definitive choice or verdict.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition To judge, to decide, or to resolve. In the New Testament, it is used in a judicial sense (to pass sentence) and in a personal sense (to make up one's mind). It signifies a mental act that "separates" one path from another, resulting in a fixed and purposeful direction.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Sifting of Grain: In secular Greek, it was used for the process of separating the wheat from the chaff, representing the "discernment" needed to find what is valuable. - The Judicial Verdict: It pictured a judge who has heard all the evidence and "reaches a decision" that settles the case and determines the future of the parties involved. - The Architectural Choice: It described a builder "deciding" on the placement of a cornerstone or a boundary, signifying a choice that provides the foundation for everything that follows.

4. Theological Implications The ability to "determine" a right course of action is a result of the "renewing of the mind" (verse 5). While God is the ultimate Judge (krites), He grants the believer the capacity to "judge" what is good and "determine" to pursue it. This word highlights the responsibility of the human will to align itself with the "faithful saying" of the Gospel.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:12 In Titus 3:12, Paul states that he has "determined (kekrika) to spend the winter" at Nicopolis. The perfect tense indicate a decision that has already been made and remains in effect; it is a settled plan. This shows that the "maintenance of good works" involves strategic planning and "determined" leadership to ensure the mission of the church continues effectively.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Acts 15:19: “Therefore I judge (krino) that we should not trouble those from among the Gentiles...” - Acts 20:16: “For Paul had decided (krino) to sail past Ephesus, so that he would not have to spend time in Asia...” - 1 Corinthians 2:2: “For I determined (krino) not to know anything among you except Jesus Christ and Him crucified.” - 1 Corinthians 5:3: “...have already judged (krino), as though I were present, him who has so done this deed:” - 2 Corinthians 2:1: “But I determined (krino) this within myself, that I would not come again to you in sorrow.”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that the Christian life is not governed by "whim" but by "resolution." It suggests that once a believer has "determined" the will of God, they must be "diligent" (verse 12) to carry it out without being distracted by "useless" things. The "determination" of a leader provides the stability and direction needed for the "maintenance of good works" by the whole congregation.
