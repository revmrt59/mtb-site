Summary:

NICOPOLIS - ( Nikopolis , Strong’s [3533]) Summary: A Roman city located in Epirus, Greece. Its name, meaning "City of Victory," identifies the strategic location where the apostle Paul determined to spend the winter and where he summoned Titus to meet him for further instructions and mission planning.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation A Greek proper name derived from nike, meaning "victory," and polis, meaning "city." The name commemorates a specific military triumph, signifying a place of "conquest" or "triumph" in the Roman world.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Nicopolis; a city of Epirus, founded by Augustus Caesar in honor of his victory at Actium (31 BC). In the New Testament, it serves as a geographic "hub" where Paul established a winter base of operations during his travels between his first and second Roman imprisonments.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Memorial City: In secular history, Nicopolis was built as a "monument" to Caesar's victory, designed to be a prominent Roman colony and a center of regional administration. - The Winter Harbor: It pictured a place of "safe haven" during the dangerous winter months when Mediterranean travel by sea was effectively halted, representing a season of "rest and regrouping." - The Strategic Crossroads: It was a "picture" of a vital communication link between the east (Greece) and the west (Italy), making it an ideal location for an apostolic "command center."

4. Theological Implications Nicopolis demonstrates the "strategic" nature of the Gospel mission; Paul used "victorious" Roman cities as platforms to proclaim the ultimate "victory" of Christ. It highlights the "maintenance of good works" (verse 14) through organized leadership, where a "determined" plan (verse 12) provides the stability needed for the church to thrive. The city name, "City of Victory," serves as a subtle reminder of the "hope of eternal life" (verse 7) that provides believers with a triumph over sin and death.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:12 In the NKJV of Titus 3:12, Paul writes, "...be diligent to come to me at Nicopolis (Nikopolin), for I have decided to spend the winter there." This identifies Nicopolis as the designated meeting point for Paul and Titus once the "replacements" (Artemas or Tychicus) had arrived in Crete. By choosing this city, Paul was positioning himself to continue the "profitable" work of the Gospel in a major urban center while waiting for the spring travel season to resume.

6. Biblical Profile - Location: Situated in the province of Epirus on the western coast of Greece, near the site of the Battle of Actium. - Significance in Paul's Life: Mentioned only in Titus 3, it represents a period of "freedom" for Paul after his release from his first Roman house arrest and before his final arrest and execution under Nero. - Role in the Mission: It was a focal point for "setting in order" (Titus 1:5) the various mission teams, as evidenced by his command for Titus to meet him there.

7. Significance - The Victory of Grace: The "significance" of Nicopolis is the irony of an apostle of a "crucified" King establishing a base in a city named for "imperial victory," showing that the "kindness of God" (verse 4) conquers the hearts of the empire. - The Value of Planning: It shows that Paul's "diligent" (verse 12) ministry involved deliberate "decisions" regarding geography and timing to maximize the "profit" of his time. - The Gathering Place: It served as a place of "fellowship" where Paul could "greet" (verse 15) his co-laborers and prepare them for future "urgent needs" (verse 14).

8. Key Insights & Inferences - Insight into Geography: Paul’s presence in Nicopolis (on the Adriatic coast) implies he was looking toward missions in the western part of the Roman Empire, possibly even Rome or Spain again. - Inference of Communication: The fact that Paul could send a letter from an unknown location to Crete, naming a specific city to meet in, suggests a highly "profitable" network of "letter carriers" (like Tychicus) were active in the region. - Insight into Wintering: Because sea travel was closed from November to March, we can infer that Paul intended to spend several months in Nicopolis, using that time to "teach" and "admonish" the believers there. - Inference of Risk: Since Nicopolis was a major Roman colony, Paul’s "determination" to stay there shows he did not hide from authority but continued to "stand at the head" (maintain) of his ministry despite the rising persecution.
