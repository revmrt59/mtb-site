Summary:

DILIGENT - ( spoudazo , Strong’s [4704]) Summary: To make haste, to be eager, or to give maximum effort. It describes a focused and intentional intensity of action, where a person is fully committed to accomplishing a task with speed and excellence.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from spoude (Strong’s [4710]), which means "haste," "earnestness," or "zeal." The root suggests a sense of "moving quickly" because the matter is of great importance and cannot be delayed.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition To hasten, to be earnest, or to give diligence. It signifies an active and energetic pursuit of a goal, characterized by seriousness and a refusal to be idle. In the New Testament, it denotes the spiritual "push" required to fulfill a divine command or to maintain a relationship of service to God.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Courier: In secular Greek, it described a messenger who "hastens" with all their might to deliver a king's decree before it is too late. - The Athlete: It pictured an athlete in a race who is "straining" every muscle to reach the finish line, representing an all-consuming physical and mental effort. - The Earnest Student: It was used for a scholar who "gives diligence" to their studies, poring over texts with a focused mind to ensure they master the subject.

4. Theological Implications Diligence is the necessary response to the "faithful saying" of the Gospel; because salvation is a gift of grace, the believer is motivated to be "diligent" in their service. It is the opposite of "slothfulness" or "apathy," identifying a heart that is on fire for the things of God. The goal of this diligence is to "be found by Him in peace" and to ensure that one's "calling and election" are made sure through a life of good works.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:12 In Titus 3:12, Paul commands Titus to "be diligent (spoudason) to come to me at Nicopolis." This is an imperative call to action, emphasizing that the meeting is time-sensitive and requires Titus to prioritize it above other tasks. It suggests that the "maintenance of good works" (verse 8) includes being "diligent" in supporting the mission and the leaders of the church, showing that personal obedience is tied to corporate cooperation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Galatians 2:10: “...the very thing I also was forward (spoudazo-eager/diligent) to do.” - Ephesians 4:3: “endeavoring (spoudazo-being diligent) to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.” - 2 Timothy 2:15: “Be diligent (spoudazo) to present yourself approved to God, a worker who does not need to be ashamed...” - 2 Timothy 4:9: “Be diligent (spoudazo) to come to me quickly;” - Hebrews 4:11: “Let us therefore be diligent (spoudazo) to enter that rest, lest anyone fall...” - 2 Peter 1:10: “Therefore, brethren, be even more diligent (spoudazo) to make your call and election sure...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that the Christian life is not a "passive" experience but one that requires "haste" and "eagerness." It suggests that "good works" (verse 14) are best performed by those who are "diligent" in their preparation and execution. By calling Titus to be "diligent," Paul reminds him that the "hope of eternal life" (verse 7) should produce a present energy that refuses to be distracted by "unprofitable" things.
