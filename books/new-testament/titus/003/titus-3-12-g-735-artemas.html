Summary:

ARTEMAS - ( Artemas , Strong’s [735]) Summary: A trusted companion and co-laborer of the apostle Paul. His name, likely a contraction of Artemidorus (meaning "gift of Artemis"), identifies a faithful servant whom Paul considered as a potential replacement for Titus in Crete.

+1

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation A Greek proper name, possibly a contraction of Artemidorus, which combines the name of the Greek goddess Artemis with doron, meaning "gift." While the name has pagan origins, it was a common name in the first-century Hellenistic world, and its use here identifies the man rather than his religious background.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Artemas; a Christian at Rome or with Paul, sent by him into Crete. The name itself, in its shortened form, signifies a "gift" or "dedicated to Artemis." In the New Testament, he appears as a dependable member of Paul's inner circle, entrusted with significant responsibility.

+2

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Contracted Name: In secular Greek culture, shortening a long name (like Artemidorus to Artemas) often indicated a level of familiarity or a move toward more practical, everyday usage. - The Representative: Artemas serves as a "picture" of the secondary figures in the New Testament-those who do not have books written to them but are "indispensable" (necessary) for the administration of the church. - The Potential Substitute: He is pictured as a prepared leader, standing "in the wings" to take over the work of a more prominent leader (Titus) so the mission can continue without interruption.

4. Theological Implications Artemas represents the "variety of gifts" within the body of Christ; while Titus was an apostolic delegate, Artemas was a "worker" who was ready to serve where needed. His inclusion in Paul's plans shows that the "maintenance of good works" (verse 14) often involves the quiet, administrative task of "filling a gap" in leadership. It highlights the "faithful saying" that God uses all types of people-including those with pagan names-to advance His "profitable" kingdom.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:12 In the NKJV of Titus 3:12, Paul writes, "When I send Artemas (Arteman) to you, or Tychicus, be diligent to come to me..." This reveals that Paul had two qualified men in mind to relieve Titus of his duties in Crete. The mention of Artemas first may suggest he was the primary choice, showing the high level of trust Paul placed in his character and ability to "set in order the things that are wanting" (Titus 1:5).

6. NT References and Inferences - Titus 3:12: This is the only explicit mention of Artemas in the New Testament. - Inference of Capability: Because Paul was considering sending him to Crete-a difficult and "unruly" environment (Titus 1:12)-it is inferred that Artemas was a man of significant spiritual maturity and leadership "strength." - Inference of Proximity: He was clearly with Paul (likely in Nicopolis or on the way there) at the time this letter was written, making him a close personal aide during Paul's later ministry. - Inference of Teamwork: Like Tychicus, he was a "traveling" minister, a man who lived a life "on the journey" (verse 13) to facilitate the growth of the churches.

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that the "hope of eternal life" (verse 7) produces a community of believers who are willing to go wherever they are "sent." It suggests that Artemas had "learned" (verse 14) to be fruitful in the service of the Gospel, making him a "profitable" asset to Paul's team. Tradition (though not biblical record) identifies him as one of the seventy disciples and later a bishop of Lystra, though Paul's mention here is the only divinely inspired record of his service.
