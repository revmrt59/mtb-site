Summary:

ZENAS - ( Zenas , Strong’s [2211]) Summary: A Christian convert and professional lawyer. His name, a contraction of Zenodorus (meaning "gift of Zeus"), identifies a man whose legal expertise was redeemed for the service of the Gospel, serving as a traveling companion to Apollos and a recipient of the church’s practical support.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation A Greek proper name, likely a contraction of Zenodoros, which combines Zenos (the genitive form of Zeus) with doron, meaning "gift." While his name originally honored the chief deity of the Greek pantheon, Zenas himself is presented as a "gift" to the church through his specialized skills and faithful service.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Zenas; a Christian at Crete and a lawyer. The designation nomikos (lawyer) identifies him as one who is "learned in the law." In the New Testament, he stands as the only individual explicitly identified as a "lawyer" who is also a recognized co-laborer of the apostle Paul.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Professional Jurisconsult: In the Roman world, a nomikos was a professional trained to draft documents, provide legal counsel, and navigate the complex intersection of local and imperial law. - The Redemptive Profession: He is a "picture" of a secular vocation being brought under the Lordship of Christ, where his "legal mind" is used to advance the "faithful saying" (verse 8) rather than create "useless" disputes (verse 9). - The Traveling Partner: He is pictured in the "company" of Apollos, showing the "profitable" synergy between a learned legal expert and a powerful, eloquent preacher.

4. Theological Implications Zenas represents the "sanctification of the intellect"; his background in the law was not discarded upon conversion but was redirected toward "urgent needs" (verse 14). His inclusion proves that "good works" (verse 8) can include professional services that provide "profit" to the mission's logistics and protection. He demonstrates that the "hope of eternal life" (verse 7) is available to the "wise and learned," transforming them into "fellow servants" within the body.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:13 In the NKJV of Titus 3:13, Paul commands, "Send Zenas (Zenan) the lawyer and Apollos on their journey with haste, that nothing be wanting to them." Zenas is at the center of a practical test for the Cretan church: will they "learn to maintain good works" (verse 14) by providing for the needs of this specialized worker? His mention here reveals that even "experts" in the church are occasionally in "lack" and require the "diligent" support of the local congregation to continue their "journey."

6. Biblical Profile - Vocation: Explicitly called a "lawyer" (nomikos). There is scholarly debate on whether he was an expert in Jewish Mosaic Law or Roman Civil Law, though his Greek name and presence in the mission to the Gentiles strongly suggest he was skilled in Roman jurisprudence. - Association: He was a close associate of Apollos, traveling with him as an apostolic team through Crete toward their next destination. - Integrity: Paul’s personal recommendation of Zenas to Titus implies a man of high character who did not use his legal skills for "selfish ambition" or "division" (verse 10).

7. Significance - The Redeemed Professional: Zenas is significant as a model for "professional" Christians. He shows that one does not have to abandon their earthly training to be "fruitful" (verse 14) in the kingdom. - Legal Support for the Mission: His "significance" likely lay in his ability to handle the legal challenges Paul’s teams often faced-such as contracts, property rights for house churches, or defending the group against civil accusations. - The Object of Church Hospitality: He serves as a catalyst for the church's growth in "maintenance"; the church's duty to "send him forward" was a primary way they practiced the "kindness and love of God" (verse 4).

8. Key Insights & Inferences - Insight into the Team: The pairing of Zenas (the lawyer) and Apollos (the orator) suggests a "powerhouse" team capable of addressing both the hearts and the legal standing of the churches they visited. - Inference of Social Standing: As a lawyer, Zenas likely came from a higher social and educational stratum, showing that the Gospel in Crete was reaching across all class lines. - Tradition: Although not mentioned in Scripture, church tradition eventually lists him as one of the seventy disciples and later a bishop of Diospolis (Lydda).
