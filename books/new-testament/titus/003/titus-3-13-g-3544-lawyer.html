Summary:

LAWYER - ( nomikos , Strong’s [3544]) Summary: One who is learned in the law. It specifically describes an expert in the Mosaic Law and its rabbinic interpretations, responsible for the study, teaching, and professional application of legal requirements.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from nomos (Strong's [3551]), which means "law," "statute," or "custom." The suffix -ikos indicates a professional relationship or skill, identifying a person whose life and occupation are centered on the law.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Pertaining to the law, legal, or a lawyer. In the New Testament, it is used almost exclusively for the "Jewish lawyers"-scribes and experts who interpreted the Old Testament Law (Torah) and the oral traditions. While it can refer to a secular jurist in a Greek context, in the context of Titus, it refers to Zenas, an expert in the religious and civil codes of the Jewish people.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Legal Counselor: In secular Greek, a nomikos was a professional consultant who advised citizens on how to navigate the complexities of civil law and contracts. - The Torah Specialist: It pictured a scholar who spent their entire life "bent over the scroll," meticulously analyzing every letter and commandment to ensure proper observance. - The Guardian of Tradition: It described a person who stood as a "gatekeeper" of the law, determining what was "lawful" and what was "unlawful" based on their expertise.

4. Theological Implications The "lawyers" in the Gospels were often criticized for placing "heavy burdens" on people while failing to keep the heart of the law themselves (Luke 11:46). However, the presence of Zenas the lawyer in Titus 3:13 shows that a specialized knowledge of the law can be redeemed for the service of the Gospel. It highlights the transition from a "striving about the law" (verse 9) to a "maintaining of good works" (verse 14), where a lawyer uses his skills to benefit the church rather than divide it.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:13 In the NKJV of Titus 3:13, Paul instructs, "Send Zenas the lawyer (nomikon) and Apollos on their journey with haste..." This is a rare instance of a "lawyer" being spoken of positively in the New Testament. It suggests that Zenas’s expertise was likely used to help navigate the legalities of the mission or to provide "profitable" teaching that focused on the grace of God rather than "foolish disputes."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Matthew 22:35: “Then one of them, a lawyer (nomikos), asked Him a question, testing Him...” - Luke 7:30: “But the Pharisees and lawyers (nomikoi) rejected the will of God for themselves...” - Luke 10:25: “And behold, a certain lawyer (nomikos) stood up and tested Him...” - Luke 11:46: “And He said, ‘Woe to you also, lawyers (nomikoi)! For you load men with burdens hard to bear...’” - Titus 3:9 (Related Adjective): “But avoid foolish disputes... and strivings about the law (nomikas-legal/law-related strivings);”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that God can use "professional" skills within the body of Christ; the church needs those who are "diligent" (verse 12) in their secular expertise as much as their spiritual zeal. It suggests that the "lawyer" who follows Christ is no longer a slave to "useless" technicalities but a servant of the "faithful saying" (verse 8). By specifically mentioning Zenas's profession, Paul emphasizes that every believer, regardless of their background, is called to "stand at the head" of good works.
