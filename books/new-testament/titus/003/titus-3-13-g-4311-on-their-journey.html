Summary:

ON THEIR JOURNEY - ( propempo , Strong’s [4311]) Summary: To send forward, to escort, or to provide for. It describes the act of accompanying someone part of the way or equipping them with the resources and provisions necessary to complete their travel and mission.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation A compound verb derived from the preposition pro, meaning "before" or "forward," and pempo, meaning "to send." It literally means "to send before" or "to send forward."

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition To send forward, to bring on one's way, or to accompany. In the New Testament, it signifies more than just a farewell; it involves the practical and financial support of a traveler, ensuring they have the food, money, and companions needed for their destination. It denotes a communal responsibility to "speed someone on their way" in a manner worthy of God.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Honor Guard: In secular Greek, it was used for an official escort that accompanied a dignitary or king out of a city as a sign of respect and protection. - The Provisioning: It pictured a family "stocking the bags" of a departing member, filling them with bread, water, and funds to ensure they did not suffer "lack" during the trip. - The Parting Escort: It described the emotional and supportive act of a group walking with a departing friend to the outskirts of town or the docks of a ship, refusing to leave them until the last possible moment.

4. Theological Implications The act of "sending someone on their journey" is presented as a vital part of the missionary work of the church. It reflects the "partnership in the Gospel," where those who stay behind participate in the work of those who go by providing the necessary "logistical" support. It is a tangible expression of the "kindness and love of God" (verse 4) working through the hospitality and generosity of the believers.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:13 In the NKJV of Titus 3:13, Paul commands, "Send Zenas the lawyer and Apollos on their journey (propempson) with haste..." This instruction links the "maintenance of good works" (verse 8) to the support of traveling ministers. By using this specific word, Paul is telling Titus and the Cretan church to take personal responsibility for the success of Zenas and Apollos, ensuring their mission is not hindered by physical or financial needs.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Acts 15:3: “So, being sent on their way (propemphthentes) by the church, they passed through Phoenicia and Samaria...” - Acts 20:38: “...And they accompanied (proepempon) him to the ship.” - Romans 15:24: “...I hope to see you on my journey, and to be helped on my way (propemphthenai) there by you...” - 1 Corinthians 16:6: “...that you may send me on my journey (propempsete) wherever I go.” - 3 John 6: “...If you send them forward (propempsas) on their journey in a manner worthy of God, you will do well,”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that "good works" are not always individual acts of charity; they often involve the "corporate maintenance" of the church's mission. It suggests that the "profitable" nature of the church (verse 8) is seen in how it handles its resources to "speed forward" the message of grace. To "send on a journey" with haste implies that the mission of Christ is an urgent matter that requires the "diligent" (verse 12) cooperation of all believers.
