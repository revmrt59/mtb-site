Summary:

HASTE - ( spoudaios , Strong’s [4709]) Summary: With eagerness, diligence, or speed. It describes an earnest and prompt manner of acting, prioritizing a task with a sense of urgency and giving it one's full attention to ensure its timely completion.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from spoude (Strong’s [4710]), which signifies "haste," "earnestness," or "zeal." It is the adverbial form of the word, focusing on the manner in which an action is performed-with a quickened pace driven by intense interest or duty.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Diligently, earnestly, or with speed. In the New Testament, it denotes an activity that is not done sluggishly or as an afterthought but is pushed to the forefront of one's priorities. It signifies a promptness that arises from a recognition of the importance and urgency of the command.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Royal Messenger: In secular Greek, it described the way a servant would run to fulfill a king's request, moving with "haste" because the king’s business takes precedence over all else. - The Eager Host: It pictured a host who prepares a meal "with haste" for an unexpected but honored guest, showing their respect through their speed and care. - The Burning Fuse: It was used for a process that was accelerating toward a conclusion, representing the focused energy needed to finish a task before a window of opportunity closes.

4. Theological Implications "Haste" in the Christian life is the outward expression of a heart that values the "faithful saying" of the Gospel. It is the opposite of the "lazy" or "unprofitable" behavior that Paul warns against earlier in the epistle. True spiritual diligence manifest as a "promptness" to obey, showing that the believer's "hope of eternal life" (verse 7) creates a present urgency in their service to others.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:13 In the NKJV of Titus 3:13, Paul instructs Titus to send Zenas and Apollos on their journey with haste (spoudaios). This emphasizes that the mission of these two men was critical and that any delay would be "unprofitable" to the progress of the Gospel. By using this word, Paul calls for a high level of "carefulness" (verse 8) in logistics, ensuring that the church’s support is provided quickly so the ministers can move on to their next assignment.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Luke 7:4: “And when they came to Jesus, they begged Him earnestly (spoudaios), saying that the one for whom He should do this was deserving,” - Philippians 2:28: “Therefore I sent him the more eagerly (spoudaios), that when you see him again you may rejoice...” - 2 Timothy 1:17: “but when he arrived in Rome, he sought me out very zealously (spoudaios) and found me.” - Titus 3:12 (Related Verb): “Be diligent (spoudazo) to come to me at Nicopolis...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that "good works" (verse 14) are often time-sensitive; a work done late may lose its "profitability." It suggests that a leader’s "haste" in providing for others is a primary way they "stand at the head" of the congregation's ministry. By commanding "haste," Paul reminds Titus that the "kindness and love of God" (verse 4) should be reflected in the church's energetic and responsive hospitality.
