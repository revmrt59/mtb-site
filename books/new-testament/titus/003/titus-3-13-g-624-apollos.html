Summary:

APOLLOS - ( Apollos , Strong’s [624]) Summary: An eloquent and fervent Jewish Christian from Alexandria. His name, a contraction of Apollonius (meaning "belonging to Apollo"), identifies a powerful orator and scholar who became a primary figure in the early church, known for his deep knowledge of the Scriptures and his partnership with the apostle Paul.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation A Greek proper name, likely a contraction of Apollonios, derived from the name of the Greek deity Apollo. Though his name was rooted in pagan mythology, Apollos became a premier "instrument" (vessel) for the true God, using his vast intellectual and oratorical training to "accurately" teach the way of the Lord.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Apollos; a Jewish Christian and an eloquent man. He is defined by his "fervency of spirit" and his status as a "learned" man, particularly in the Old Testament Scriptures. In the New Testament, he represents the convergence of Alexandrian scholarship and the life-giving power of the Gospel.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Eloquent Orator: In Hellenistic culture, logios (the word used to describe him) referred to a man of "letters"-someone who was both well-read and a persuasive public speaker. - The Accurate Teacher: He is pictured as a "diligent" student who, once corrected by Priscilla and Aquila, "accurately" (verse 8) explained the things of Christ. - The Watering Servant: Paul used a "farming" word picture (1 Corinthians 3:6) to describe Apollos's role: "I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the increase."

4. Theological Implications Apollos demonstrates that "eloquence" and "intellect" are "profitable" to the church when surrendered to the Holy Spirit. He highlights the "maintenance" of doctrine, showing that even a gifted leader must be willing to learn "the way of God more accurately" (Acts 18:26). He serves as a model for "fellowship in the Gospel," where different gifts (Paul the pioneer and Apollos the nurturer) work together without "selfish ambition" or "division" (verse 10).

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:13 In the NKJV of Titus 3:13, Paul instructs, "Send Zenas the lawyer and Apollos (Apollon) on their journey with haste..." This reveals that Apollos was traveling through Crete toward a new destination, perhaps return to Alexandria or Corinth. His presence in Crete provided a "powerful" antidote to the "unruly and vain talkers" (Titus 1:10), as his eloquent defense of the faith could "silence" those who disputed the law.

6. Biblical Profile - Origin: A Jew born at Alexandria, Egypt, a major center of learning and biblical study. - Education: Characterized as "eloquent" and "mighty in the Scriptures" (Acts 18:24). - Transformation: Initially knew only the "baptism of John," but was instructed more perfectly by Priscilla and Aquila in Ephesus. - Ministry: He was a powerful apologist who "vigorously refuted the Jews" (Acts 18:28) and a key leader in the Corinthian church. - Humility: Despite his great popularity (to the point that some formed an "Apollos faction"), he maintained a humble and cooperative relationship with Paul.

7. Significance - The Model of Growth: Apollos is significant as a man who was already "mighty" but remained teachable, showing that "good works" (verse 8) include the ongoing "renewing" of one's own understanding. - The Power of the Word: He demonstrated that a "learned" use of the Old Testament could "profitably" prove that Jesus is the Christ. - The Essential Partner: Paul viewed him as an equal-a "fellow laborer"-whose work was "necessary" (urgent) for the stability and growth of the believers.

8. Key Insights & Inferences - Insight into Unity: Paul’s concern that Apollos "lack nothing" (verse 13) proves there was no professional rivalry between them; Paul championed the success of Apollos's ministry as much as his own. - Inference of Respect: Paul later requested Apollos to go to Corinth (1 Corinthians 16:12), though Apollos declined at that time; this implies a relationship built on mutual respect and "liberty" rather than apostolic "domination." - Insight into Impact: Because he was "mighty in the Scriptures," we can infer that his presence in Crete was a "profitable" (verse 8) resource for Titus in establishing "sound doctrine" against the "foolish disputes" of the circumcision party. - Inference of Maturity: His willingness to travel with Zenas (a lawyer) suggests he valued the "different parts of the body," combining his oratorical fire with Zenas's legal precision.

9. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Acts 18:24: “Now a certain Jew named Apollos (Apollos), born at Alexandria, an eloquent man and mighty in the Scriptures, came to Ephesus.”

  • Acts 19:1: “...and it happened, while Apollos (Apollon) was at Corinth, that Paul... came to Ephesus.”

  • 1 Corinthians 1:12: “Now I say this, that each of you says, ‘I am of Paul,’ or ‘I am of Apollos (Apollo),’ or ‘I am of Cephas’...”

  • 1 Corinthians 3:6: “I planted, Apollos (Apollos) watered, but God gave the increase.”

  • 1 Corinthians 16:12: “Now concerning our brother Apollos (Apollo), I strongly urged him to come to you...”
