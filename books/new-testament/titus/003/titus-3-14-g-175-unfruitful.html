Summary:

UNFRUITFUL - ( akarpos , Strong’s [175]) Summary: Barren, useless, or without result. It describes a life or an activity that fails to produce the expected spiritual harvest, representing a state of spiritual stagnation that yields no benefit to God or others.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation A compound word formed by the alpha privative a-, meaning "not" or "without," and karpos, meaning "fruit," "result," or "product." It literally means "without fruit," signifying a plant or a person that consumes resources but yields no harvest.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Barren, unfruitful, or unprofitable. In the New Testament, it is used to characterize both the "unfruitful" works of darkness and the danger of a believer’s life failing to show the evidence of the "renewing of the Spirit." It denotes a lack of practical productivity in the service of Christ and his church.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Barren Orchard: In secular Greek, it was used for a tree that looked healthy and green from a distance but had no fruit when inspected closely, representing a "deceptive" appearance of life. - The Dead Seed: It pictured a seed that fell on rocky ground; it might sprout quickly, but because it had no root, it became "unfruitful" and withered away. - The Empty Hand: It was used for a laborer who returns from the field at the end of the day with nothing to show for their time, signifying a total loss of effort and opportunity.

4. Theological Implications Fruitfulness is the expected outcome of being "justified by His grace" (verse 7); therefore, being "unfruitful" is a sign of spiritual dysfunction. The "unfruitful" life is often one that has been "choked" by the "foolish disputes" and "cares of this world" mentioned in the surrounding context. God’s desire is for His people to be "cleansed" so that they might bear "much fruit," proving that the "kindness and love of God" has truly taken root in their hearts.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:14 In the NKJV of Titus 3:14, Paul warns, "...and let our people also learn to maintain good works... that they may not be unfruitful (akarpoi)." This provides the "negative" motivation for the "maintenance" of good works: to avoid a life of spiritual emptiness. It suggests that if a believer is not "diligent" (verse 12) to meet "urgent needs," they will inevitably become "unfruitful" and "useless" (verse 9).

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Matthew 13:22: “...and the deceitfulness of riches choke the word, and he becomes unfruitful (akarpos).” - 1 Corinthians 14:14: “...my spirit prays, but my understanding is unfruitful (akarpos).” - Ephesians 5:11: “And have no fellowship with the unfruitful (akarpois) works of darkness, but rather expose them.” - 2 Peter 1:8: “...neither be barren nor unfruitful (akarpous) in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ.” - Jude 12: “...late autumn trees without fruit (akarpa), twice dead, pulled up by the roots;”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that "good works" (verse 8) are the "fruit" that justifies the believer's existence on this earth. It suggests that "learning" (verse 14) is a prerequisite for "fruitfulness"; one must be taught how to "stand at the head" of good deeds. By avoiding an "unfruitful" life, the believer ensures that their "hope of eternal life" (verse 7) is reflected in a present life that is "profitable to men" (verse 8).
