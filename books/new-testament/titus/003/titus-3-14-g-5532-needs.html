Summary:

NEEDS - ( chreia , Strong’s [5532]) Summary: Necessity, lack, or duty. It describes a situation where something essential is missing, creating a requirement for provision or action to restore what is necessary for life or service.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the verb chraomai, meaning "to use" or "to make use of." It is also related to chre, meaning "it is necessary" or "it is fitting." The root suggests that a "need" is a gap in the resources required to fulfill a specific "use" or purpose.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Necessity, need, or business. In the New Testament, it signifies a requirement for physical support, spiritual instruction, or the fulfillment of a specific task. It denotes the practical "lack" that exists when an individual or a ministry does not have the tools or resources to proceed.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Empty Pantry: In secular Greek, it was used for a household that had run out of bread or oil, creating a "need" that must be supplied for the family's survival. - The Essential Tool: It pictured a craftsman who is missing the one tool "needed" to finish a project, representing an indispensable requirement for success. - The Official Business: It was used for a "duty" or an "office" that someone was appointed to fulfill, where the "need" was the work itself that required doing.

4. Theological Implications The recognition of "needs" is a call to the "kindness and love of God" (verse 4) to be manifested through His people. While God knows what we "need" before we ask, He has designed the church to be the primary means of meeting "urgent needs" through "good works." It identifies that a believer's resources are not for self-consumption but are a stewardship meant to be "used" where a "need" is identified.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:14 In the NKJV of Titus 3:14, Paul instructs, "...and let our people also learn to maintain good works, to meet urgent needs (chreias)..." This links the "maintenance" of good works to the specific, practical "lacks" (verse 13) found in the community. It suggests that the "profitability" of the church (verse 8) is measured by its ability to identify and fill these "needs" through the power of the Spirit.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Matthew 6:8: “...for your Father knows the things you have need (chreian) of before you ask Him.” - Acts 2:45: “...and divided them among all, as anyone had need (chreian).” - Acts 6:3: “...seven men of good reputation... whom we may appoint over this business (chreias-need/duty);” - Ephesians 4:28: “...that he may have something to give him who has need (chreian).” - Ephesians 4:29: “...but what is good for necessary (chreias-of the need) edification...” - Philippians 4:19: “And my God shall supply all your need (chreian) according to His riches in glory...” - 1 John 3:17: “But whoever has this world’s goods, and sees his brother in need (chreian)...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that "good works" are not abstract concepts but are responses to real, identifiable "needs." It suggests that a church "lacking nothing" (verse 13) is one where every member is "diligent" (verse 12) to meet the "needs" of others. By "meeting urgent needs," the believer moves from "unprofitable" (verse 9) speculation to "profitable" (verse 8) service that proves the reality of their "renewing of the Spirit."
