Summary:

GRACE - ( charis , Strong’s [5485]) Summary: Unmerited favor, divine influence, or a gift. It describes the benevolent act of God toward the undeserving, specifically His power to save, transform, and empower the believer to live a life that is pleasing to Him.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the verb chairo, meaning "to rejoice" or "to be glad." It is also related to chara, meaning "joy," suggesting that "grace" is the source of all true and lasting spiritual gladness.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Favor, kindness, or a gift given without expectation of return. In the New Testament, it signifies the "free gift" of salvation through Jesus Christ and the ongoing "divine influence" upon the heart that strengthens the believer for service. It denotes a quality of "loveliness" and "acceptability" that is bestowed by God rather than earned by man.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The King’s Favor: In secular Greek, it described a ruler who grants a "favor" (charis) to a subject who has no legal claim to it, representing an act of pure generosity. - The Beautiful Gift: It pictured a "charming" or "graceful" gesture that brings delight to the receiver, emphasizing the "beauty" of the giver's heart. - The Recompense of Joy: It was used for a "thank-offering" or an expression of "gratitude" in response to a kindness received, where the joy of the gift is returned to the giver.

4. Theological Implications Grace is the "operating principle" of the Gospel, standing in direct opposition to "works of righteousness" (verse 5) as a means of salvation. It is the power that "appeared" to all men (Titus 2:11), bringing salvation and "teaching" us to deny ungodliness. Grace is both the "foundation" of our justification (verse 7) and the "energy" for our sanctification, enabling us to "maintain good works" (verse 8).

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:15 In the NKJV of Titus 3:15, Paul concludes, "Grace (charis) be with you all. Amen." This final "salutation" is a prayer that the "divine influence" of God would continually rest upon Titus and the entire Cretan church. By ending with "grace," Paul reminds them that everything he has commanded-from "avoiding disputes" to "meeting urgent needs"-is only possible through the strength that God provides.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - John 1:17: “For the law was given through Moses, but grace (charis) and truth came through Jesus Christ.” - Romans 3:24: “being justified freely by His grace (charis) through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus,” - Romans 5:20: “...But where sin abounded, grace (charis) abounded much more,” - Ephesians 2:8: “For by grace (chariti) you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves...” - 2 Timothy 2:1: “You therefore, my son, be strong in the grace (chariti) that is in Christ Jesus.” - Titus 2:11: “For the grace (charis) of God that brings salvation has appeared to all men,” - Titus 3:7: “that having been justified by His grace (chariti) we should become heirs...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that the Christian life begins, continues, and ends with "unmerited favor." It suggests that the "profitable" nature of a believer (verse 8) is a direct result of the "grace" they have received. By closing with "Grace be with you all," Paul emphasizes the "corporate" need for God's favor to protect the church from the "warped" and "divisive" (verses 10-11) influences that seek to destroy its unity.
