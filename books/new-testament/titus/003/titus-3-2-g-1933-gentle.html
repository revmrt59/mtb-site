Summary:

GENTLE - ( epieikes , Strong’s [1933])

A quality of "sweet reasonableness" that is willing to yield its legal rights for the sake of a higher good. In Titus 3:2, it describes the forbearing and considerate attitude that believers must display toward all people, reflecting the grace of God rather than a rigid or litigious spirit.

Full Study:

  1. Greek Roots / Derivation

Derived from epi (upon or unto) and eikos (fitting, seemly, or reasonable). The root points to that which is "appropriate" or "suitable" for a given situation. It suggests a character that moves beyond the letter of the law to find what is truly "fit" or "fair" in the sight of God.

  1. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Gentle, mild, forbearing, or equitable. Lexically, it refers to the virtue of a person who does not insist on the full extent of their legal rights or strict justice. It is often translated as "moderation" or "patient endurance." It denotes a spirit that is not easily provoked and is willing to make concessions for the sake of peace and the Gospel.

  1. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

    • The Equitable Judge: Aristotle famously used epieikes to describe "equity," which he defined as the correction of the law where it is defective due to its universality. It pictured a judge who looks at the heart of the matter rather than just the rigid code.

    • The "Sweet Reasonableness": Matthew Arnold popularized this phrase to describe the word, picturing a person who is eminently reasonable and impossible to pick a fight with because they are so considerate.

    • The Yielding Reed: It was used to describe something that is flexible and supple rather than brittle and hard. It pictured a person who can bend under pressure or when faced with the faults of others without breaking the relationship.

  2. Theological Implications

Theologically, epieikes is an attribute that characterizes the "wisdom from above" (James 3:17). It is the opposite of the self-assertive and contentious nature of the flesh. Because God has dealt with the believer in "equity" and grace rather than strict justice, the believer is called to manifest this same gentleness toward others. It is a hallmark of the Christ-like character, as Jesus Himself was described as "gentle" (epieikeia—the noun form).

  1. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:2

In the NKJV of Titus 3:2, Paul commands believers "to be peaceable, gentle (epieikeis), showing all humility to all men."

  • The Public Witness: This gentleness is not just for fellow believers but for "all men." It is the primary way a Christian survives and thrives in a hostile, ungodly culture without becoming bitter or combative.

  • Contrast to the World: It stands in direct contrast to the "hateful and hating" description of the unregenerate world in verse 3. While the world is litigious and insists on its own way, the believer is to be yielding and fair.

  • Requirement for Leaders: The same word is used as a requirement for overseers in 1 Timothy 3:3, showing that this "sweet reasonableness" is a mark of spiritual maturity and leadership.

  1. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

    • Philippians 4:5: “Let your gentleness (epieikes) be known to all men. The Lord is at hand.”

    • 1 Timothy 3:3: “...not given to wine, not a brawler, not greedy for money, but gentle (epieike), not quarrelsome...”

    • James 3:17: “But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle (epieikes), willing to yield...”

    • 1 Peter 2:18: “Servants, be submissive to your masters with all fear, not only to the good and gentle (epieikesin), but also to the harsh.”

  2. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that being "gentle" is not a sign of weakness but of great strength. It takes more power to yield one's rights than to fight for them.

It suggests that a "legalistic" Christian is a contradiction in terms, as the wisdom of God is defined by its "yielding" nature.

By using epieikes, Paul provides a "profitable" (Titus 3:8) social ethic, showing that the best way to represent Christ to the world is through a spirit of fairness and consideration.
