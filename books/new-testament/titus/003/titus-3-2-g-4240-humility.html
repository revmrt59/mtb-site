Summary:

HUMILITY - ( prautes , Strong’s [4240]) Summary: A disposition that is even-tempered, tranquil, and balanced. It describes a spirit that accepts God’s dealings as good without resisting or disputing, manifesting as a gentle and patient attitude toward others.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the adjective praos, which describes someone who is mild or gentle. It refers to an inward grace of the soul; it is not primarily an external behavior, but a state of heart that results in a specific outward demeanor.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Meekness, gentleness, or mildness. While often misunderstood as weakness, the term signifies "power under control." It is the quality of a heart that is not easily provoked and is characterized by a submissive and teachable spirit toward God and His Word.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Domesticated Animal: In secular Greek, it was used to describe a wild horse that had been broken and trained; the horse retained all its strength, but that strength was now under the control of the rider. - The Soothing Medicine: It pictured a medicinal ointment that took the "sting" out of a wound, providing a cooling and healing effect. - The Gentle Breeze: It was used for a wind that had lost its destructive force and had become a refreshing breeze that brought relief.

4. Theological Implications Humility (or meekness) is a Fruit of the Spirit, indicating it is a supernatural work rather than a natural personality trait. Jesus Christ described Himself as "gentle (praos) and lowly in heart," providing the perfect model of this quality in action. It is the necessary condition for receiving the Word of God, as it requires a heart that is not resistant to divine instruction.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:2 In Titus 3:2, believers are commanded to show "all humility (prautes) to all men." The use of "all" (pas) emphasizes that this gentleness is to be shown toward every person, regardless of their social standing, character, or treatment of the believer. This serves as the final instruction in a list of social behaviors intended to make the Christian community stand out in a culture known for its harshness and rebellion.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Matthew 11:29: “Take My yoke upon you and learn from Me, for I am gentle (praus - adjective) and lowly in heart...” - Galatians 5:23: “gentleness (prautes), self-control. Against such there is no law.” - Galatians 6:1: “...restore such a one in a spirit of gentleness (prautes), considering yourself lest you also be tempted.” - James 1:21: “...and receive with meekness (prautes) the implanted word, which is able to save your souls.” - 1 Peter 3:15: “...and always be ready to give a defense... with meekness (prautes) and fear;”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights the balance between strength and softness; the humble person is firm in their convictions but gentle in their presentation. It suggests that a person has reached a state of internal peace that does not require them to fight for their own rights or prestige. Proper humility allows a believer to represent the truth of the Gospel without the interference of a defensive or argumentative ego.
