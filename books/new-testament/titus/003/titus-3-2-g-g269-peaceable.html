Summary:

PEACEABLE - ( amachos , Strong’s [G269]) Summary: Abstaining from fighting, non-contentious, or not quarrelsome. It describes a person who avoids strife and does not have a combative disposition.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the alpha-privative a, meaning "not," and mache, meaning a "fight," "battle," or "strife." It literally describes a person who is not a fighter or a brawler, signifying the absence of a quarrelsome nature.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition To be peaceable and avoiding of conflict. It refers to the quality of being non-contentious and refusing to engage in verbal or physical strife. In the New Testament, it characterizes a person who is gentle and does not seek to settle matters through aggression or heated argument.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Civilian: In secular Greek, it was used to describe those who were not engaged in military combat or who were unarmed. - The Calm Neighbor: It pictured a person who did not provoke or participate in the typical disputes of the marketplace or community. - Non-Aggression: It identifies an individual who refuses to "strike" back, choosing instead to handle disagreements with restraint.

4. Theological Implications This quality is a required characteristic for church elders, showing that those who lead God's people must not be contentious. It suggests that the believer is to mirror the peace of God by refusing to use worldly methods of strife to defend the faith or personal rights. The absence of a brawling spirit is evidence of the Spirit's work in producing self-control and gentleness.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:2 In Titus 3:2, Paul commands believers to be "not brawlers" or "peaceable." This instruction follows the command to "speak evil of no one," indicating that a peaceable life includes both the restraint of the tongue and the restraint of the temper. Even in a culture known for being "always liars, evil beasts, lazy gluttons" (Titus 1:12), the believer is expected to provide a contrast by remaining non-contentious.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - 1 Timothy 3:3: “...not greedy for money, but gentle, not quarrelsome (amachos), not covetous.”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights the importance of maintaining a peaceable testimony even when faced with opposition or provocation. It implies that one can hold firm convictions without being disagreeable or quarrelsome in the way those convictions are presented. A peaceable spirit serves as a practical demonstration of the Gospel’s power to bring order and harmony to human relationships.
