Summary:

SERVING - ( douleuo , Strong’s [1398]) Summary: To be a slave, to yield obedience, or to be in a state of bondage. It describes a condition where one's life and actions are entirely dominated by a master or a compelling force.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the noun doulos, meaning a "bondservant" or "slave." It describes the active state of being a slave, emphasizing the service rendered to a master to whom one is legally and morally bound.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition To serve as a slave, to be in subjection, or to obey. It refers to a total and exclusive service where the individual’s own will is surrendered to the will of another. In a moral sense, it identifies a life that is controlled and directed by internal or external masters, whether it be God or sinful desires.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Legal Bondage: In secular Greek, it was used for the condition of a slave who was the property of an owner, with no rights of their own. - The Debt Slave: It pictured a person who, due to debt, was forced to serve a creditor until the obligation was met. - The Captive Will: It describes the state of a person whose desires have become their masters, pulling them in directions contrary to their own best interests.

4. Theological Implications The word is used to contrast the two types of service possible for a human being: service to sin or service to God. No one is truly free in an absolute sense; every person serves either the dictates of their fallen nature or the commands of the Lord. Redemption is the process of being transferred from the slavery of sin into the service of Christ, which is the only service that leads to life.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:3 In Titus 3:3, the word describes the former state of believers as "serving (douleuontes) various lusts and pleasures." It portrays sin not as a series of isolated acts, but as a master that demands constant and exhausting service. The plural "lusts and pleasures" suggests a fragmented and chaotic life where the individual is driven by competing and never-satisfied desires.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Matthew 6:24: “No one can serve (douleuo) two masters; for either he will hate the one and love the other...” - Luke 15:29: “...Lo, these many years I have been serving (douleuo) you; I never transgressed your commandment...” - Romans 6:6: “...that the body of sin might be done away with, that we should no longer be slaves of (douleuo) sin.” - Galatians 4:8: “...when you did not know God, you served (douleuo) those which by nature are not gods.” - 1 Thessalonians 1:9: “...how you turned to God from idols to serve (douleuo) the living and true God.”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights the involuntary nature of the bondage of sin; one becomes a slave to what they obey. It suggests that the pleasures sought in rebellion eventually become the chains that bind the individual to a life of futility. The only way to break the service to "various lusts" is through the intervention of a new Master who has purchased the servant.
