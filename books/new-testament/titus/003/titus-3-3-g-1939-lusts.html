Summary:

LUSTS - ( epithumia , Strong’s [1939]) Summary: A strong desire, craving, or longing directed toward an object. In the New Testament, it most often refers to forbidden or sinful desires that originate from the fallen human nature.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the preposition epi, meaning "upon" or "directed toward," and thumos, which refers to "passion," "soul," or "mind." It describes an intense reaching out of the soul toward something that it perceives as desirable.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition A strong desire or craving. While the word is morally neutral in its root-simply meaning a deep longing-it is used in the majority of New Testament contexts to describe the corrupted desires of the flesh. It identifies the internal impulses that drive an individual to seek satisfaction apart from the will of God.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Burning Fire: In secular Greek, it was used to describe the heat of passion or a "burning" appetite that demanded to be fed. - The Reaching Hand: It pictured a person stretching out their hand to grasp something that they believe will provide fulfillment or relief. - The Compelling Force: It describes an internal "driver" that pushes a person toward an action, often overriding their better judgment or logic.

4. Theological Implications Lusts are presented as the primary motivation for sin; James explains that temptation occurs when one is "drawn away by his own desires (epithumia) and enticed." They characterize the "old man" or the unregenerate nature, which is dominated by self-gratification. The goal of the Christian life involves the crucifixion of these lusts through the power of the Holy Spirit, replacing them with godly desires.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:3 In Titus 3:3, Paul describes the former life as "serving various lusts (epithumiais) and pleasures." The use of the plural indicates that these desires are many, diverse, and often contradictory, leading to a life of internal chaos. The word emphasizes that before grace appeared, the individual was not a free agent but was in a state of bondage to their own shifting cravings.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Mark 4:19: “...and the desires (epithumiai) for other things entering in choke the word...” - John 8:44: “You are of your father the devil, and the desires (epithumias) of your father you want to do.” - Romans 1:24: “Therefore God also gave them up to uncleanness, in the lusts (epithumiais) of their hearts...” - Galatians 5:16: “I say then: Walk in the Spirit, and you shall not fulfill the lust (epithumian) of the flesh.” - 1 John 2:16: “For all that is in the world-the lust (epithumia) of the flesh, the lust (epithumia) of the eyes...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that the problem of sin is not merely external behavior, but the internal condition of the heart’s affections. It suggests that the unregenerate mind is constantly seeking a new "master" in the form of a temporary pleasure or desire. The transformation described in Titus 3 involves the redirection of the soul's longings away from "worldly lusts" (Titus 2:12) toward the hope of eternal life.
