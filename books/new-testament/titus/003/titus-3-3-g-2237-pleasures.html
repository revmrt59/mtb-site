Summary:

PLEASURES - ( hedone , Strong’s [2237]) Summary: The enjoyment of sensual gratification or the pursuit of self-indulgence. It describes the state of being driven by personal satisfaction as a primary motivation for one's choices and lifestyle.

Full Study:

  1. Greek Roots / Derivation

    • Derived from the verb handano, which means "to please" or "to be pleasing."

    • It refers to the physical or emotional sensation of pleasure and the internal desires that seek such gratification.

  2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

    • Pleasure, enjoyment, or delight.

    • In the New Testament, it is almost exclusively used in a negative sense to describe the selfish or sensual desires that alienate a person from God.

    • It denotes the governing force of an unregenerate life that seeks fulfillment in temporary, worldly satisfaction.

  3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

    • The Aim of Life: In various Greek philosophical schools, hedone was debated as the potential "chief good" of existence, forming the basis of hedonism.

    • The Lure: It pictured a bait used in a trap, where the immediate promise of pleasure hides the eventual consequence of bondage or ruin.

    • The Internal Strife: It described the chaotic state of a person whose various appetites are in constant competition for satisfaction.

  4. Theological Implications

    • Pleasures are identified as spiritual "choking" agents that prevent the Word of God from reaching maturity in a person's life.

    • They are often linked to the "lusts of the flesh" and are viewed as a source of social conflict and internal war within the individual.

    • Living for pleasure is contrasted with living for God, as the former leads to spiritual dullness and a refusal to submit to divine truth.

  5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:3

    • In Titus 3:3, Paul describes the former condition of believers as "serving various lusts and pleasures" (hedonais).

    • This indicates that the pursuit of pleasure was a form of bondage that dictated their behavior and priorities.

    • It highlights the lack of freedom in the life of one who is ruled by their own changing appetites and desires for gratification.

  6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

    • Luke 8:14: “...and are choked with cares, riches, and pleasures (hedonon) of life, and bring no fruit to maturity.”

    • James 4:1: “Where do wars and fights come from among you? Do they not come from your desires for pleasure (hedonon) that war in your members?”

    • James 4:3: “...that you may spend it on your pleasures (hedonais).”

    • 2 Peter 2:13: “...they count it pleasure (hedonen) to carouse in the daytime.”

  7. Other Relevant Insights

    • This word highlights the deceptive nature of sin, which promises satisfaction but results in a cycle of serving never-satisfied cravings.

    • It identifies a core aspect of the human problem: the misplaced pursuit of joy in the temporary rather than in the eternal.

    • The transition from this state to the one described in the following verses involves a fundamental change in what the soul finds truly satisfying.
