Summary:

DECEIVED - ( planao , Strong’s [4105]) Summary: To wander, go astray, or be led into error. It describes a state of being misled from the truth, often as a result of external deception or the internal influence of sin.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the noun plane, which refers to a "wandering" or a "straying" from the right path. It identifies a condition where an individual has departed from a fixed course or a standard of truth.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition To cause to wander, to lead astray, or to deceive. In the passive voice, as found in Titus 3:3, it means to be led into error or to be misled. It signifies a departure from the straight path of divine instruction into the crooked paths of falsehood and sin.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Wandering Star: In ancient Greek, it was used to describe planets, which were called "wandering stars" because they did not follow the same fixed tracks as other celestial bodies. - The Lost Sheep: It pictured a sheep that had strayed from the flock and the shepherd, becoming vulnerable and lost in the wilderness. - The Navigational Error: It described a ship or a traveler who had lost their way due to a lack of proper guidance or by following a false signal.

4. Theological Implications Deception is presented as a fundamental characteristic of the human condition apart from the intervention of God's grace. It is frequently associated with the work of Satan, who is described as the one who "deceives the whole world," leading humanity away from the knowledge of God. The word emphasizes that being "deceived" is not merely an intellectual mistake but a moral state of being lost and separated from the truth.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:3 In Titus 3:3, Paul lists "deceived" (planomenoi) as part of the shared past of every believer. By using the passive participle, he indicates that they were being led astray by the prevailing influences of their culture and their own lusts. This acknowledgement serves to remind the believer that their current state of "soundness" is entirely due to the mercy and washing of God, not their own wisdom.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Matthew 18:12: “...and one of them goes astray (planao), does he not leave the ninety-nine... and seek the one that is straying (planao)?” - 2 Timothy 3:13: “But evil men and impostors will grow worse and worse, deceiving (planao) and being deceived (planao).” - 1 John 3:7: “Little children, let no one deceive (planao) you. He who practices righteousness is righteous...” - Revelation 12:9: “...that serpent of old, called the Devil and Satan, who deceives (planao) the whole world...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights the vulnerability of the human mind to falsehood when it is not anchored in the Word of God. It suggests that deception is often a progressive state, where one error leads to further wandering away from the truth. The antidote to being "deceived" is the "sound doctrine" and the regenerating work of the Holy Spirit mentioned in the subsequent verses of Titus 3.
