Summary:

ENVY - ( phthonos , Strong’s [5355]) Summary: A feeling of resentment or displeasure at the success or possessions of others. It involves a desire not just to have what another has, but to see them deprived of it.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the root phtheiro, meaning "to corrupt" or "to destroy." It suggests a corruption of the mind that views the well-being of a neighbor as a personal injury.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition The feeling of displeasure produced by witnessing or hearing of the advantage or prosperity of others. It is a "shriveling" of the soul that results in ill-will toward a peer or associate. Unlike zelos (jealousy), which may involve a desire to emulate or possess something for oneself, phthonos is inherently malicious and seeks the downfall of the other.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Eye of Evil: In secular Greek thought, it was often associated with the "evil eye," the belief that a look of malice could bring harm to a prosperous person. - The Internal Decay: It pictured a rot in the bones that consumes the possessor from within, even before it affects the target. - Social Division: It was identified as the primary source of faction and strife within the Greek city-states, as citizens begrudged one another's honors.

4. Theological Implications Envy is a primary manifestation of the fallen nature and is listed among the "works of the flesh." It represents a fundamental rejection of God's sovereignty, as it complains against the way He has distributed His blessings. It is the specific sin attributed to the religious leaders who delivered Jesus to be crucified, showing its potential for ultimate destruction.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:3 In Titus 3:3, it is paired with "malice" to describe the competitive and bitter nature of life before conversion. It characterizes a society where people live "hating one another" because they cannot bear to see others excel. The appearance of grace provides the remedy for this by replacing envy with the kindness and love of God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Matthew 27:18: “For he knew that they had handed Him over because of envy (phthonos).” - Romans 1:29: “...full of envy (phthonos), murder, strife, deceit, evil-mindedness...” - Galatians 5:21: “envy (phthonos), murders, drunkenness, revelries, and the like...” - Philippians 1:15: “Some indeed preach Christ even from envy (phthonos) and strife...” - 1 Timothy 6:4: “...obsessed with disputes and arguments... from which come envy (phthonos), strife...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights the anti-social nature of sin; it makes genuine community impossible because it views others as rivals. It is the state of a heart that is never satisfied with its own portion, always measuring itself against the perceived abundance of others. The removal of envy is essential for the transformation into a life characterized by genuine love and humility.
