Summary:

KINDNESS - ( chrestotes , Strong’s [5544]) Summary: The benevolent disposition of God expressed in helpful and gracious actions. It describes a moral goodness that is gentle and pleasant, seeking the well-being of others without any harshness.

Full Study:

  1. Greek Roots / Derivation

    • Derived from the adjective chrestos, meaning "good," "kind," or "useful."

    • It is related to the verb chraomai, which means "to use." It originally referred to that which is fit for use or well-suited for its purpose.

  2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

    • Goodness, kindness, or benignity.

    • It describes a benevolent disposition that is manifested in helpful and generous actions toward others.

    • It is moral excellence expressed as a gentle and gracious attitude that seeks the well-being of the recipient.

  3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

    • The Mellow Wine: In secular Greek, it was used to describe wine that had aged and lost its harshness, becoming "mellow" and pleasant to the taste.

    • The Well-Fitting Yoke: It pictured a yoke that was custom-made for an animal so that it would not chafe or cause pain during labor.

    • Social Courtesy: It described the refined behavior of a citizen who treated others with genuine consideration and avoided any harshness or brusqueness.

  4. Theological Implications

    • Kindness is a fundamental attribute of God's character, particularly in His dealings with rebellious humanity.

    • It is the specific quality that "leads to repentance," as the kindness of God melts the hardness of the human heart.

    • As a Fruit of the Spirit, it is a quality that is to be reproduced in the lives of believers, reflecting the character of their Savior.

  5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:4

    • In Titus 3:4, the "kindness (chrestotes) and the love of God our Savior" are said to have "appeared."

    • This kindness stands in direct opposition to the "malice and envy" described in the previous verse, showing how God's nature intervenes in the chaos of human sin.

    • It emphasizes that salvation is initiated by God’s gracious disposition rather than by any merit or work found in man.

  6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

    • Romans 2:4: “Or do you despise the riches of His goodness (chrestotes), forbearance, and longsuffering...”

    • Romans 11:22: “Therefore consider the goodness (chrestotes) and severity of God...”

    • Galatians 5:22: “But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace... kindness (chrestotes), goodness...”

    • Ephesians 2:7: “...that in the ages to come He might show the exceeding riches of His grace in His kindness (chrestotes) toward us...”

    • Colossians 3:12: “Therefore, as the elect of God... put on tender mercies, kindness (chrestotes), humility...”

  7. Other Relevant Insights

    • This word represents the "active" side of goodness; it is not just the absence of evil but the presence of positive, helpful grace.

    • It identifies a heart that is not harsh or demanding but is characterized by a "sweet reasonableness" in its interactions.

    • By demonstrating chrestotes, the believer provides a visible proof of the transformation produced by the Gospel.
