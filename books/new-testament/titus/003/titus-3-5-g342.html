Summary:

RENEWING - ( anakainosis , Strong’s [342]) Summary: The process of renovation or a complete change for the better. It refers to the internal restoration and spiritual adjustment of the believer’s mind and heart, brought about by the Holy Spirit.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation A compound word derived from the preposition ana, meaning "up," "again," or "back," and kainos, which describes that which is "new" in quality or nature, rather than simply new in time. It literally describes a "making new again" or a "upward renovation" that results in a state different from the old.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition A renewal, renovation, or a complete change for the better. It signifies a transformation where the old, corrupted patterns of thought and life are replaced by a new, divinely-imparted quality of existence. While palingenesia (regeneration) refers to the new birth itself, anakainosis emphasizes the renovation and developing maturity of that new life.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Renovation: In secular usage, the related concept of kainosis was used to describe the restoration of a building or structure that had fallen into disrepair, making it functional and beautiful once more. - The New vs. The Recent: The root kainos was used for a "new" version of an object (like a chariot) that was of a better design or quality, as opposed to neos, which simply meant something recently produced. - The Fresh Start: It pictured the refreshing of a life or a mind that had been exhausted or darkened, bringing it into a state of vigor and clarity.

4. Theological Implications Renewing is specifically the work of the Holy Spirit within the human soul. It involves the restoration of the "image of God" in the believer, which was defaced by the fall and the former life of sin. The goal of this renewing is to align the believer’s thinking, values, and desires with the mind of God, enabling them to discern and walk in His will.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:5 In Titus 3:5, "renewing" (anakainoseos) is paired with the "washing of regeneration." This indicates that salvation involves both a definitive new birth and a profound internal renovation of the person’s nature. The "renewing of the Holy Spirit" is the supernatural power that cleanses the mind from the "foolish" and "hateful" dispositions of the past, creating a capacity for the "good works" mentioned throughout the epistle.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Romans 12:2: “And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing (anakainosis) of your mind...” - Psalm 103:5 (LXX - related verb anakainizo): “Who satisfies your mouth with good things, so that your youth is renewed (anakainizo) like the eagle’s.” - Lamentations 5:21 (LXX - related verb anakainizo): “Turn us back to You, O Lord, and we will be restored; renew (anakainizo) our days as of old.”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that the Christian life is not merely about external behavior modification but about a deep, internal "makeover" of the soul. It implies a progressive element; as seen in related verbal forms (2 Corinthians 4:16), the inward man is being "renewed day by day." The "renewing" provides the spiritual stamina and vision necessary for the believer to remain "ready for every good work" in a hostile or decaying culture.
