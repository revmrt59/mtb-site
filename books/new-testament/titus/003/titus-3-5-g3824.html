Summary:

REGENERATION - ( palingenesia , Strong’s [3824]) Summary: A new birth, reproduction, or renewal. It describes a complete change of state or a restoration to a pristine condition, specifically referring to the spiritual rebirth of an individual by the Holy Spirit.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation A compound word derived from palin, meaning "again" or "anew," and genesis, meaning "birth," "origin," or "beginning." It literally translates to a "being born again" or a "new beginning" that replaces an old state of existence.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition New birth, renewal, or recreation. It signifies a radical transition from one condition to another, where the old is set aside and a new life is inaugurated. In the New Testament, it is used in two senses: the restoration of the world at the second coming of Christ and the spiritual rebirth of the individual soul.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Cycle of Nature: In secular Greek, it was used to describe the "rebirth" of the earth in the springtime after the death of winter. - The Restoration of a City: It pictured the rebuilding of a city that had been destroyed, restoring it to its former glory and functional life. - The Release from Bondage: It was sometimes used to describe the new life of a slave who had been granted their freedom, marking a total change in their legal and social status.

4. Theological Implications Regeneration is a monergistic work of God; it is something done to the sinner by the Holy Spirit, not something achieved by the sinner. It is the divine act that imparts spiritual life to those who were "dead in trespasses and sins," enabling them to see and enter the kingdom of God. This new birth is the foundation for all subsequent spiritual growth, as it provides the new nature required to live in obedience to God.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:5 In Titus 3:5, Paul connects the "washing" with "regeneration" (palingenesias). This emphasizes that salvation involves more than a legal justification; it involves a creative act that makes the believer a "new creature." It serves as the definitive answer to the "foolish" and "disobedient" state described in verse 3, showing that the only remedy for total depravity is a total rebirth.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Matthew 19:28: “...that in the regeneration (palingenesia), when the Son of Man sits on the throne of His glory...” - 1 Peter 1:3 (Related Verb): “...who according to His abundant mercy has begotten us again (anagennao-related concept) to a living hope...” - 1 Peter 1:23 (Related Verb): “having been born again (anagennao), not of corruptible seed but incorruptible...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights the "discontinuity" between the old life and the new; there is no gradual improvement of the old nature, only the birth of a new one. It identifies the believer as a citizen of the "new creation" even while living in the present age. The "regeneration" of the individual is a down-payment and a foretaste of the future "regeneration" of the entire cosmos when Christ returns.
