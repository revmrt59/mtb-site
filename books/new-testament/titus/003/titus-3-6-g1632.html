Summary:

POURED - ( ekcheo , Strong’s [1632]) Summary: To shed, spill, or distribute in a lavish and abundant manner. It describes the intensive effusion of the Holy Spirit upon the believer, signifying an overflowing supply of divine presence and power.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation A compound verb derived from ek, meaning "out of" or "from," and cheo, meaning "to pour." The root suggests a movement where a substance flows out from a source or a vessel until it is emptied or distributed upon a recipient.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition To pour out, shed forth, or spill. In the New Testament, it is used both literally (as with wine or blood) and figuratively (as with the Holy Spirit or God's love). It signifies a generous or even exhaustive giving, where the amount provided is not measured or restricted but is characterized by fullness.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Sacrificial Libation: In secular and religious Greek contexts, it described the act of pouring out wine or oil upon an altar as an offering to a deity. - The Spilling of Blood: It was commonly used in contexts of warfare or sacrifice where life-blood is "shed" or poured out upon the ground. - The Overflowing Vessel: It pictured a jar or cup being tilted or filled to the point that the contents "burst forth," emphasizing that the supply is more than sufficient for the need.

4. Theological Implications The "pouring out" of the Spirit is the fulfillment of Old Testament promises regarding the New Covenant, specifically Joel 2:28-29. It signifies that the Holy Spirit is no longer reserved for specific individuals for temporary tasks but is now a permanent and abundant gift to all who are in Christ. This act represents the "baptism" of the believer, where they are thoroughly immersed in and saturated by the life and power of God.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:6 In Titus 3:6, Paul notes that God "poured out" (execheen) the Spirit "richly" (plousios) through Jesus Christ. The combination of "poured out" and "richly" emphasizes the staggering abundance of the provision; God did not give the Spirit sparingly or in small portions. This lavish distribution is the effective power behind the "washing" and "renewing" mentioned in verse 5, moving the believer from the "malice" of the old life into the "grace" of the new.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Joel 2:28 (LXX): “And it shall come to pass afterward that I will pour out (ekcheo) My Spirit on all flesh...” - Matthew 26:28: “For this is My blood of the new covenant, which is shed (ekcheo) for many for the remission of sins.” - Acts 2:17: “‘And it shall come to pass in the last days, says God, that I will pour out (ekcheo) of My Spirit on all flesh...’” - Romans 5:5: “...because the love of God has been poured out (ekcheo) in our hearts by the Holy Spirit who was given to us.”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights the Trinitarian nature of salvation: The Father "pours out" the Spirit "through Jesus Christ our Savior." It suggests that the believer’s resources for "good works" are not internal or self-generated but are the result of this external "pouring" of divine life. The abundance implied by ekcheo provides the assurance that no matter how "foolish" or "disobedient" one was, the supply of the Spirit is more than enough to achieve a total renewal.
