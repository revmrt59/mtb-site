Summary:

JUSTIFIED - ( dikaioo , Strong’s [1344]) Summary: A legal or forensic term meaning to declare righteous or to pronounce a verdict of acquittal. It describes the act of God whereby He constitutes a sinner as righteous in His sight, based on the merit of Christ and received through faith.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the adjective dikaios, meaning "righteous" or "just," which is rooted in dike, signifying "right," "custom," or "judicial approval." The verbal suffix -oo typically indicates a declarative action-to treat or account as something-rather than a causative action (to make into something).

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition To declare, pronounce, or judge to be righteous. It signifies a change in legal standing or status before a judge, where a person is cleared of all charges and treated as if they had never violated the law. In the New Testament, it denotes the act of God where He imputes the righteousness of Christ to the believer, resulting in a state of being "right" with Him.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Courtroom Verdict: In secular Greek law, it was the technical term for a judge’s decision to acquit a defendant, signifying that the person was "conformed to the standard" of the law. - The Balanced Scales: It pictured the scales of justice reaching an equilibrium where the person's standing matches the legal requirement. - Vindication: It was used to describe a person whose character or claims had been proven true or "shown to be right" in the face of accusation.

4. Theological Implications Justification is an act of God’s free grace, not a reward for human effort or moral improvement. It is forensic (legal) in nature; it changes the believer’s standing before God's bar of justice from "guilty" to "righteous." Because it is an act of God, it is complete and final, providing the basis for peace with God and the assurance of no condemnation.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:7 In Titus 3:7, Paul links being "justified (dikaiōthentes) by His grace" with the result of becoming "heirs according to the hope of eternal life." This emphasizes that the legal acquittal provided by God is the necessary foundation for receiving the family inheritance of the kingdom. The aorist tense indicates a definitive, completed act that occurred at the moment of salvation, serving as the "doorway" into the status of an heir.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Romans 3:24: “being justified (dikaioo) freely by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus,” - Romans 5:1: “Therefore, having been justified (dikaioo) by faith, we have peace with God...” - Romans 8:30: “...whom He called, these He also justified (dikaioo); and whom He justified (dikaioo), these He also glorified.” - Galatians 2:16: “...knowing that a man is not justified (dikaioo) by the works of the law but by faith in Jesus Christ...” - 1 Timothy 3:16: “...God was manifested in the flesh, justified (dikaioo) in the Spirit, seen by angels...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that salvation is a "gift of status" before it is a "change of behavior." It distinguishes the believer's position (justification) from their process of growth (sanctification), ensuring that the assurance of salvation rests on God’s verdict rather than the believer’s performance. To be "justified" is to have the very righteousness of God credited to one's account, making the believer as "right" as Christ Himself in the eyes of the Law.
