Summary:

HOPE - ( elpis , Strong’s [1680]) Summary: A confident expectation and desire for something good in the future. In the New Testament, it is not a wish or a "maybe," but a settled assurance based on the promises of God and the finished work of Christ.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the root elpo, meaning "to anticipate" or "to welcome." In Greek thought, it refers to the expectation of what is to come, whether good or bad; however, in Biblical usage, it is almost exclusively used for the expectation of good.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Expectation, trust, or confidence. It signifies a favorable and confident expectation of a future event. Unlike the modern English use of "hope" (which often implies uncertainty), the Greek elpis denotes a certainty of the heart that looks forward to the fulfillment of God’s Word.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Anchor: In early Christian art and secular metaphor, it was pictured as an anchor that holds a ship steady in a storm, providing stability because it is "hooked" into something solid. - The Waiting Watchman: It described a watchman who waits for the dawn, fully certain that the sun will rise, though it has not yet appeared. - The Foundation: It pictured the base of a building that supports the weight of the structure, representing the internal "support" that allows a person to endure present trials.

4. Theological Implications Hope is a theological virtue, often grouped with faith and love, and is essential for the believer's perseverance. It is a "living hope" because it is grounded in the resurrection of Jesus Christ, which serves as the guarantee of the believer's own future glory. This hope is "not seen" but is so certain that it produces patience and joy even in the midst of suffering.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:7 In Titus 3:7, Paul speaks of becoming "heirs according to the hope (elpida) of eternal life." This hope is the "standard" or the "measure" (kata) of the heirship; the believer is an heir precisely because they have been given this guaranteed expectation of life with God. It serves as the final contrast to the "hating one another" and "serving various lusts" of verse 3, moving the believer from a life of temporary pleasure to a life of eternal anticipation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Romans 5:5: “Now hope (elpis) does not disappoint, because the love of God has been poured out in our hearts...” - Romans 8:24: “For we were saved in this hope (elpis), but hope (elpis) that is seen is not hope (elpis)...” - Colossians 1:27: “...which is Christ in you, the hope (elpis) of glory.” - Titus 2:13: “looking for the blessed hope (elpida) and glorious appearing of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ,” - Hebrews 6:19: “This hope (elpida) we have as an anchor of the soul, both sure and steadfast...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights the forward-looking nature of the Christian life; the best is always yet to come. It provides the emotional and spiritual "oxygen" necessary for the soul to survive in a fallen world. To have this elpis is to live with the quiet confidence that God’s final "Yes" in Christ is more real than any present "No."
