Summary:

AFFIRM - ( diabebaioomai , Strong’s [1226]) Summary: To assert strongly, state with confidence, or insist upon. It describes a firm and emphatic declaration of truth intended to leave no room for doubt or dispute.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation A compound verb derived from dia, meaning "through" or "thoroughly," and bebaioo, meaning "to make firm" or "to establish." The root bebaios refers to something that is stable, sure, and grounded, suggesting that this affirmation is built on a solid foundation.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition To assert strongly, to state with confidence, or to maintain firmly. It signifies a vigorous and persistent declaration of a particular truth or doctrine. In the New Testament, it is used to describe the authoritative and unwavering teaching of the Gospel message.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Legal Guarantee: In secular Greek, it was used for a legal assurance or a "warranty" that a property or claim was valid and would be defended. - The Firm Stand: It pictured a person planting their feet on solid ground and refusing to be moved, representing an intellectual and spiritual "insistence." - The Confident Witness: It described a witness in a court of law who spoke with such clarity and conviction that their testimony was considered unshakeable.

4. Theological Implications Affirming the truth is the responsibility of those entrusted with the Word of God, especially in the face of false teaching. It implies that the truths of the Gospel are not open for debate or modification; they are to be "thoroughly established" in the hearts of the hearers. This level of confidence is rooted in the "faithful" nature of the message itself, which reflects the unchanging character of God.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:8 In Titus 3:8, Paul instructs Titus to "constantly affirm (diabebaiousthai)" the truths of verses 4–7. The present tense suggests a habitual and persistent activity; Titus is not to mention these things once, but to keep insisting on them as the basis for the believer's life. This constant affirmation is the "fuel" that drives the "carefulness" to maintain good works, showing that right behavior flows from a firm grip on right doctrine.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - 1 Timothy 1:7: “desiring to be teachers of the law, understanding neither what they say nor the things which they affirm (diabebaioomai).” - Hebrews 2:3 (Related Verb): “...which at the first began to be spoken by the Lord, and was confirmed (bebaioo) to us by those who heard Him,” - Hebrews 6:16 (Related Noun): “...and an oath for confirmation (bebaiosis) is for them an end of all dispute.”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that the Christian teacher must be a person of "settled conviction" rather than one who is "tossed to and fro." It suggests that the power of the message is tied to the boldness of its declaration; the truth must be "insisted upon" to be effective. When a leader "affirms" these things, they provide the spiritual "spine" for the congregation, enabling them to stand firm in a culture of confusion.
