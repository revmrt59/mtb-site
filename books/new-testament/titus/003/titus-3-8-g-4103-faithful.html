Summary:

FAITHFUL - ( pistos , Strong’s [4103]) Summary: Trustworthy, reliable, or sure. It describes that which is worthy of belief and can be depended upon because it is inherently true and consistent with reality.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the verb peitho, meaning "to persuade" or "to be convinced." It is the passive form, describing the quality of one who has been so thoroughly persuaded that they have become reliable and constant.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Trustworthy, dependable, or believable. In the New Testament, it is used in two primary ways: subjectively, of a person who is "believing" or full of faith; and objectively, of a thing or person that is "faithful" and can be relied upon. It signifies that which is certain and firm, leaving no room for doubt or suspicion.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Trusted Messenger: In secular Greek, it described a servant or envoy who carried out their instructions exactly, without deviation or personal agenda. - The Reliable Seal: It pictured a stamp or signet that guaranteed the contents of a vessel or the authenticity of a legal document. - The Solid Ground: It was used for a foundation or a path that would not give way under a traveler’s weight, representing that which is "sure."

4. Theological Implications Faithfulness is a primary attribute of God; He is pistos because He is incapable of being inconsistent with His own nature or His word. The "faithful sayings" of the Pastoral Epistles are presented as divine axioms-spiritual truths that are so reliable they form the bedrock of Christian doctrine. For the believer, being pistos is the response to God’s faithfulness, resulting in a life that is dependable in its service and testimony.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:8 In Titus 3:8, the phrase "This is a faithful (pistos) saying" refers back to the glorious summary of the Gospel in verses 4–7. It serves as a solemn affirmation that the doctrine of salvation by grace through the washing of regeneration is absolutely true and trustworthy. By labeling it "faithful," Paul emphasizes that these truths are not mere opinions but are the certain foundation upon which the "good works" of the believer must be built.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - 1 Timothy 1:15: “This is a faithful (pistos) saying and worthy of all acceptance...” - 2 Timothy 2:13: “If we are faithless, He remains faithful (pistos); He cannot deny Himself.” - 1 John 1:9: “If we confess our sins, He is faithful (pistos) and just to forgive us our sins...” - Revelation 19:11: “...and He who sat on him was called Faithful (pistos) and True...” - Revelation 21:5: “...And He said to me, ‘Write, for these words are true and faithful (pistoi).’”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights the objective certainty of the Christian faith; it is not based on "cleverly devised myths" but on faithful sayings. It implies that because the message is pistos, the messenger must also be pistos in handling and declaring it. The faithfulness of the saying provides the necessary confidence for the believer to "insist on these things," knowing they are dealing with eternal realities.
