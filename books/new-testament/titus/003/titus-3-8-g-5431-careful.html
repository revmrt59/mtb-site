Summary:

CAREFUL - ( phrontizo , Strong’s [5431]) Summary: To exercise thought, to be intent upon, or to give heed to. It describes a mental discipline where one purposefully focuses their mind and attention on a specific goal or duty.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from phrontis, meaning "thought" or "care," which is further rooted in phren, referring to the "mind," "intellect," or "seat of mental faculties." It implies a "mindful" approach to life, where actions are preceded by deliberate contemplation and planning.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition To take thought, to be careful, or to be solicitous. It signifies an active mental state of being "bent upon" or "intent on" performing a task. In the New Testament, it denotes a concentrated effort to ensure that one's conduct remains consistent with their stated beliefs.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Mindful Stewardship: In secular Greek, it was used to describe a steward who carefully planned the distribution of resources to ensure nothing was wasted. - The Diligent Workman: It pictured a craftsman who constantly "took thought" of his measurements and materials to ensure the final product met the required standard. - The Anxious Focus: While it could describe "worry" or "brooding" in some contexts, in a positive sense, it pictured a mind that was "captured" by a specific duty, refusing to be distracted.

4. Theological Implications The word highlights that "good works" do not happen by accident; they are the result of intentional, spiritual thinking. It identifies the mind as the "command center" of the Christian life, where the truths of the Gospel are translated into the activities of daily living. Being "careful" is the cognitive bridge between the "washing of regeneration" (verse 5) and the "maintenance of good works" (verse 8).

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:8 In Titus 3:8, Paul instructs those who have believed in God to "be careful (phrontizosin) to maintain good works." This is the only occurrence of phrontizo in the New Testament (a hapax legomenon), used here to underscore a unique intensity of focus. It suggests that because the Gospel described in verses 4–7 is so "faithful," the believer must be equally "thoughtful" and "diligent" in how they live out that truth.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV/LXX) - 1 Samuel 9:5 (LXX): “...lest my father cease caring (phrontizo) about the donkeys and become anxious for us.” - Psalm 40:17 (LXX 39:18): “But I am poor and needy; yet the Lord thinks (phrontizo) upon me.” - Jeremiah 17:8 (LXX): “...and will not be anxious (phrontizo) in the year of drought, nor will cease from yielding fruit.”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights the necessity of "spiritual alertness"; a believer who is not "careful" in their thinking will likely be careless in their conduct. It implies that the "maintenance" of good works requires a recurring mental check-up to ensure that one's motivations remain pure and their actions remain effective. The use of this term emphasizes that the Christian life is an "intellectual" pursuit as much as an emotional or physical one, requiring the full engagement of the phren (mind).
