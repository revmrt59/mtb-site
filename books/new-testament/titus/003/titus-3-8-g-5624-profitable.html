Summary:

PROFITABLE - ( ophelimos , Strong’s [5624]) Summary: Useful, beneficial, or advantageous. It describes that which yields a positive return or provides practical help, particularly in the context of spiritual growth and the well-being of the community.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the verb ophello, meaning "to increase" or "to enlarge," and related to opheleo, meaning "to help" or "to be of service." It carries the core idea of something that adds value or contributes to the improvement of a situation.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Profitable, useful, or helpful. It signifies that which is advantageous or conducive to a desired end. In the New Testament, it is used to distinguish between activities that build up the soul and the church versus those that are "unprofitable" or "vain."

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Fruitful Investment: In secular Greek, it was used for a business venture or a crop that yielded a significant "profit" or "increase" beyond the initial effort. - The Effective Medicine: It pictured a remedy that actually worked to heal a patient, being "useful" for the restoration of health. - The Sharpened Tool: It described a tool that was well-suited for its task, making the workman’s labor more "advantageous" and productive.

4. Theological Implications The concept of "profit" in the New Testament is shifted from material gain to spiritual and communal benefit. Godliness is described as being "profitable for all things," having promise for both the present life and the life to come. Conversely, false teaching and "foolish disputes" are condemned because they are "unprofitable" (anopheleis), producing no spiritual growth or practical love.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:8 In Titus 3:8, Paul states that the constant affirmation of the Gospel and the maintenance of good works are "good and profitable (ophelima) to men." This stands in sharp contrast to the "unprofitable" things mentioned in the very next verse (verse 9). The word emphasizes the "horizontal" benefit of the Gospel: when believers live out the "washing of regeneration," they become a practical blessing to the society around them.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - 1 Timothy 4:8: “For bodily exercise profits (ophelimos) a little, but godliness is profitable (ophelimos) for all things...” - 2 Timothy 3:16: “All Scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable (ophelimos) for doctrine, for reproof...” - Titus 3:9 (Opposite): “But avoid foolish disputes... for they are unprofitable (anopheleis) and useless.”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights the "utilitarian" aspect of Christian doctrine; it is meant to work in the real world. It suggests that the validity of a ministry or a lifestyle can be measured by its "profitability"-does it actually help people and lead them toward God? The goal of the "careful" mind is to discern what is truly "profitable" and to avoid being entangled in things that waste time and spiritual energy.
