Summary:

CONTENTIONS - ( eris , Strong’s [2054]) Summary: Strife, discord, or wrangling. It describes the expression of a heart filled with rivalry and self-assertion, resulting in a breakdown of peace and unity within a community.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the root er- which suggests "to push" or "to stir up." It is related to the idea of a "quarrel" or a "feud" that is actively maintained.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Strife, contention, or wrangling. It signifies a spirit of rivalry that leads to outward conflict and verbal disputes. In the New Testament, it is consistently viewed as a negative trait, identifying a lack of love and a presence of pride that refuses to seek common ground.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Goddess of Discord: In Greek mythology, Eris was the goddess who threw the "apple of discord," sparking the Trojan War. - The Political Faction: In secular Greek, it described the bitter party spirit and fighting between rival groups in a city-state. - The Pushing Match: It pictured two people pushing against one another, neither willing to yield, resulting in a state of constant friction and heat.

4. Theological Implications Strife is listed among the "works of the flesh" and is a primary indicator that a person is acting out of their fallen nature rather than the Spirit. It is the direct opposite of the "peace" and "gentleness" that are the fruit of the Holy Spirit. The presence of eris indicates a heart that is more concerned with being "right" or "first" than with the glory of God or the well-being of the church.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:9 In Titus 3:9, Paul lists "contentions (ereis)" as one of the things to be avoided. It follows "genealogies," suggesting that the speculation about lineage often led to bitter arguments and social division. By commanding Titus to avoid these, Paul emphasizes that a leader's energy should be spent on "good and profitable" works (verse 8) rather than the "useless" heat of religious fighting.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Romans 1:29: “...full of envy, murder, strife (eridos), deceit, evil-mindedness...” - Romans 13:13: “Let us walk properly... not in strife (eridi) and envy.” - 1 Corinthians 1:11: “...that there are contentions (ereis) among you.” - 1 Corinthians 3:3: “For where there are envy, strife (eris), and divisions among you...” - Galatians 5:20: “idolatry, sorcery, hatred, contentions (ereis), jealousies...” - Philippians 1:15: “Some indeed preach Christ even from envy and strife (erin)...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that "argumentativeness" is often a moral failure rather than an intellectual one. It suggests that the goal of the false teachers in Crete was not truth, but the personal victory and social dominance gained through "winning" contentions. The antidote to eris is the "meekness and gentleness" of Christ, which allows a person to "affirm" the truth without becoming "contentious."
