Summary:

DISPUTES - ( zetesis , Strong’s [2214]) Summary: A searching, questioning, or debate. It describes a speculative inquiry or a controversial discussion that often centers on trivial matters rather than the core truths of the Gospel.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the verb zeteo, meaning "to seek," "to search," or "to investigate." The suffix -sis denotes a process or activity, signifying the ongoing act of questioning or the state of being involved in a debate.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition A searching, investigation, or a matter of controversy. It signifies an inquiry or a debate, often with a focus on the philosophical or the speculative. In the New Testament, it is frequently used in a negative sense to describe "empty" questions that do not lead to godliness or the building up of the church.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Intellectual Inquiry: In secular Greek, it was used for the rigorous investigations of philosophers who "sought" after truth through dialogue and logic. - The Legal Investigation: It pictured a magistrate or a court official searching for evidence or questioning witnesses to uncover the facts of a case. - The Maze: It described a mental "pathway" that leads nowhere, where the seeker becomes lost in a labyrinth of their own questions and theories.

4. Theological Implications The Bible contrasts the "seeking" of the kingdom of God with the "disputes" of the worldly or legalistic mind. These disputes are often a symptom of spiritual "sickness," where a person is obsessed with words and arguments rather than the reality of Christ. The presence of zetesis in the church often signals a departure from the "simplicity that is in Christ" toward a pride-filled intellectualism.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:9 In Titus 3:9, Paul identifies "foolish disputes (zeteseis moras)" as things to be avoided. These are the "searchings" that result in nothing but division and strife, distracting the believer from the "good and profitable" works mentioned in verse 8. By calling them "foolish," Paul indicates that these investigations are not true searches for wisdom but are the dull pursuits of a mind not yet fully "renewed" by the Spirit.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - John 3:25: “Then there arose a dispute (zetesis) between some of John’s disciples and the Jews about purification.” - Acts 15:2: “...they determined that Paul and Barnabas and certain others of them should go up to Jerusalem... about this question (zetemata-related root).” - 1 Timothy 6:4: “he is proud, knowing nothing, but is obsessed with disputes (zeteseis) and arguments over words...” - 2 Timothy 2:23: “But avoid foolish and ignorant disputes (zeteseis), knowing that they generate strife.”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that not all "theological" questions are helpful; some are merely "searches" that lead away from the cross. It suggests that the true "investigation" for the Christian is the "searching of the Scriptures" to see the "faithful saying" of the Gospel. The remedy for these disputes is not to win the argument, but to "affirm" the truth and "maintain" good works, which provide the only "profitable" answer to folly.
