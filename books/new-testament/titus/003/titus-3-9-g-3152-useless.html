Summary:

USELESS - ( mataios , Strong’s [3152]) Summary: Empty, vain, or fruitless. It describes that which is devoid of force, truth, or success, representing a pursuit that is fundamentally hollow and leads to no significant or lasting result.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the root mat-, which suggests "searching in vain" or "missing the mark." It is related to the idea of being "idle" or "aimless," pointing to an activity that lacks a solid foundation or a productive conclusion.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Vain, empty, or fruitless. It signifies something that is devoid of reality, power, or purpose. In the New Testament, it often characterizes religious practices or human philosophies that are "hollow" because they are not grounded in the truth of God.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Vapor or Breath: In secular Greek, it was used for a mist or a puff of smoke that appears for a moment but has no substance and quickly vanishes. - The Empty Vessel: It pictured a jar that looked full from the outside but contained nothing but air, representing a "deceptive" appearance of value. - The Fruitless Labor: It described a workman who spends the entire day building something that immediately collapses because it was built on sand or with faulty materials.

4. Theological Implications Mataios is the divine verdict on idolatry and human legalism; both are "vain" because they cannot save or transform the soul. Faith itself would be "vain" if Christ had not been raised from the dead (1 Corinthians 15:17), showing that the "reality" of the object determines the "profit" of the activity. It highlights the tragedy of a life spent on "useless" things when the "washing of regeneration" and "eternal life" are available.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:9 In Titus 3:9, Paul declares that foolish disputes and contentions are "unprofitable and useless (mataioi)." This serves as the final "period" at the end of his warning against religious distractions. While the Gospel (verses 4–7) is "faithful" and "profitable," the bickering over genealogies and the law is "empty"-it has no power to produce the "good works" required of a believer.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Acts 14:15: “...and preach to you that you should turn from these useless (mataion) things to the living God...” - 1 Corinthians 3:20: “The Lord knows the thoughts of the wise, that they are futile (mataioi).” - 1 Corinthians 15:17: “And if Christ is not risen, your faith is futile (mataia); you are still in your sins!” - James 1:26: “...but deceives his own heart, this one’s religion is useless (mataios).” - 1 Peter 1:18: “...you were not redeemed with corruptible things... from your aimless (mataias) conduct...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that some "theological" effort can actually be "aimless" if it doesn't center on the Person of Christ. It suggests that the "maintenance" of good works (verse 8) is the only way to ensure a life is not "useless." By calling these disputes mataioi, Paul warns that the church's time is a stewardship that should not be squandered on "hollow" arguments.
