Summary:

FOOLISH - ( moros , Strong’s [3474]) Summary: Dull, sluggish, or stupid. It describes a lack of spiritual discernment and a rejection of divine wisdom, characterizing thoughts or behaviors that are senseless and devoid of true value.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the root mo-, which suggests being "stopped" or "blocked." It is the source of the English word "moron." It implies a mind that is "closed" to reality or "dull" in its perceptions.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Foolish, impious, or godless. It signifies a person who is intellectually or morally deficient, particularly in their relationship to truth. In the New Testament, it is often used to describe the world’s rejection of the Gospel or the character of those who ignore the warnings of God.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Tasteless Food: In secular Greek, it was used to describe food that had lost its flavor or salt that had become "insipid" and useless. - The Sluggish Mind: It pictured a person who was "slow of heart," unable to grasp the obvious or reach a logical conclusion. - The Shameless Fool: It was used for a person who acted without regard for consequences, often portrayed in Greek comedy as the character who causes trouble through sheer ignorance.

4. Theological Implications The Bible defines "folly" not as a lack of IQ, but as a lack of reverence for God; "the fool (moros) says in his heart, 'there is no God.'" The "wisdom of the world" is characterized by God as foolishness because it fails to account for eternal realities. Conversely, the Gospel is often viewed as "foolishness" by the unregenerate mind because it relies on grace rather than human merit.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:9 In Titus 3:9, Paul warns against "foolish (moras) disputes." These are discussions that are "dull" because they have no life-giving power and are "stupid" because they ignore the "faithful saying" of the Gospel. By labeling these disputes "foolish," Paul strips them of any intellectual prestige they might have claimed, categorizing them as a waste of time for a person of God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Matthew 5:22: “...But whoever says, ‘You fool (more)!’ shall be in danger of hell fire.” - Matthew 7:26: “...will be like a foolish (moro) man who built his house on the sand:” - Matthew 25:2: “Now five of them were wise, and five were foolish (morai).” - 1 Corinthians 1:18: “For the message of the cross is foolishness (moria) to those who are perishing...” - 2 Timothy 2:23: “But avoid foolish (moras) and ignorant disputes, knowing that they generate strife.”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that some "intellectual" pursuits are actually a form of spiritual "dullness." It suggests that the remedy for folly is not more information, but the "renewing of the mind" through the Holy Spirit. Choosing to engage in "foolish" things is a sign that one has temporarily lost sight of the "profitability" of the Gospel.
