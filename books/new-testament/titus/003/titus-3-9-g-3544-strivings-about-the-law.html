Summary:

STRIVINGS ABOUT THE LAW - ( nomikas , Strong’s [3544] / mache , Strong’s [3163]) Summary: Legalistic battles or verbal fights concerning the Mosaic Law and its rabbinic interpretations. It describes a contentious and combative environment where the focus is on technicalities and traditions rather than the grace and truth of Christ.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation

  • Nomikos: Derived from nomos (law). It refers to that which pertains to the law, specifically the Mosaic Law or the legal system.

  • Mache: Derived from the verb machomai (to fight). It refers to a battle, a fight, or a physical or verbal combat.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Nomikas: Pertaining to the law, legal, or a lawyer (one skilled in the law).

  • Mache: A fight, a battle, or a quarrel.

  • Together, the phrase describes "legal fights"-vigorous and often angry verbal battles over the interpretation and application of the Jewish Law.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

  • The Battlefield: In secular Greek, mache was the standard word for a military battle or hand-to-hand combat where the goal is to defeat and destroy the opponent.

  • The Professional Debate: It pictured the sharp, competitive arguments between legal experts in a courtroom, where the focus is on winning the case through technical maneuvering.

  • The Clashing Swords: It identifies a state of relationship where words have become weapons used to strike at an opponent’s position or character.

4. Theological Implications

  • These "strivings" represent the persistent temptation to return to a performance-based relationship with God, characterized by the "works of the law."

  • They are often centered on the "traditions of men" and "commandments of people who turn from the truth," which Paul warned about earlier in Titus 1:14.

  • Such battles are the antithesis of the "rest" found in Christ, as they keep the soul in a state of constant agitation and legalistic uncertainty.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:9

  • In Titus 3:9, Paul explicitly warns Titus to avoid "strivings about the law (machas nomikas)."

  • These battles were likely fueled by the "circumcision party" (Titus 1:10) who sought to impose Jewish regulations on Gentile believers.

  • By labeling these as "unprofitable and useless," Paul argues that no amount of "winning" a legal argument will produce the "washing of regeneration" or the "renewing of the Spirit."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Matthew 22:35 (related): “Then one of them, a lawyer (nomikos), asked Him a question...”

  • 2 Corinthians 7:5: “...we were troubled on every side. Outside were conflicts (machai), inside were fears.”

  • 2 Timothy 2:23: “But avoid foolish and ignorant disputes, knowing that they generate strife (machas).”

  • James 4:1: “Where do wars and fights (machai) come from among you? Do they not come from your desires for pleasure...”

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • This phrase highlights that the "law" can be used as a weapon of pride rather than a tutor to Christ.

  • It suggests that the beauty of the "kindness and love of God" is obscured when the church becomes a courtroom for legalistic debate.

  • The remedy for these strivings is a return to the "faithful saying" of grace, which settles the legal status of the believer once and for all.
