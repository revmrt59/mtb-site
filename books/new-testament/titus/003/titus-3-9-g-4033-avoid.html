Summary:

AVOID - ( periistemi , Strong’s [4033]) Summary: To stand around, to turn away from, or to shun. It describes a deliberate and decisive movement to keep one's distance from something harmful or distracting, effectively "stepping around" an obstacle.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation A compound verb derived from the preposition peri, meaning "around" or "about," and the verb histemi, meaning "to stand." In the middle voice (as used in Titus 3:9), it carries the sense of "placing oneself around" something in a way that avoids direct contact or involvement.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition To turn oneself about, to avoid, or to shun. It signifies a purposeful withdrawal from a person or a topic that is considered dangerous or unprofitable. In the New Testament, it denotes the spiritual discipline of refusing to engage in useless or divisive arguments.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Circle of Distance: In secular Greek, it was used to describe people "standing around" a scene but refusing to get involved in the action. - The Obstacle: It pictured a traveler who sees a pit or a trap in the middle of the road and deliberately "steps around" it to continue their journey safely. - The Social Shun: It described the act of turning one's back on a person or a conversation that was considered shameful or beneath one's dignity.

4. Theological Implications Avoidance is presented as a necessary boundary for the health of the church and the peace of the believer. It recognizes that not every argument is worth having and that some "discussions" are actually spiritual traps that lead to "unliness." It is the negative counterpart to the "careful maintenance" of good works; while the believer "stands at the head" of what is good, they "stand around" what is foolish.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:9 In Titus 3:9, Paul commands Titus to "avoid (periistaso) foolish disputes, genealogies, contentions, and strivings about the law." The present imperative indicates that this should be a constant, habitual stance toward these distractions. This command immediately follows the instruction to "affirm" the Gospel, showing that a leader's time is too valuable to be wasted on "unprofitable" debates that do not lead to godliness.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Luke 24:4 (Related Active Voice): “...behold, two men stood by (ephistemi) them in shining garments.” - John 11:42 (Related Active Voice): “...but because of the people who are standing by (periestota-standing around) I said this...” - 2 Timothy 2:16: “But shun (periistaso) profane and idle babblings, for they will increase to more ungodliness.”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that "spiritual maturity" includes knowing what not to talk about. It suggests that engaging with "foolish disputes" is not a sign of strength but a failure of discernment, as it gives life to "useless" ideas. By "avoiding" these things, the believer preserves their energy for the "profitable" work of serving others and declaring the "faithful saying."
