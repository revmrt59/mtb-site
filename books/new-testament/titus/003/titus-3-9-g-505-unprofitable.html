Summary:

UNPROFITABLE - ( anopheles , Strong’s [505]) Summary: Useless, non-beneficial, or harmful. It describes that which yields no spiritual gain and contributes nothing to the edification of the believer or the health of the church.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation A compound word formed by the alpha privative a-, meaning "not" or "without," and opheleo, meaning "to help," "to profit," or "to be of service." It literally means "not helping" or "devoid of utility," identifying a total absence of positive value.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Useless, unprofitable, or injurious. It signifies a quality that fails to provide any advantage or to achieve a worthy purpose. In the New Testament, it is used to characterize religious activities or teachings that distract from the Gospel and produce no spiritual fruit.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Broken Tool: In secular Greek, it described an instrument that was so damaged it could no longer perform its intended function, rendering it "useless." - The Sterile Soil: It pictured land that had been tilled and sowed but failed to produce a crop, representing a wasted effort that resulted in no "profit." - The Idle Word: It was used for speech that was "empty" or "vain," carrying no weight and providing no help to the hearer.

4. Theological Implications The word serves as a divine verdict on human religious efforts that bypass the grace of God. It identifies that which is "vain" (mataios) in the sight of God, specifically pointing to the inability of legalism or speculation to change the human heart. If an activity is anopheles, it is not merely neutral; it is a thief of time and spiritual energy that should be spent on "good works."

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:9 In Titus 3:9, Paul declares that foolish disputes, genealogies, and contentions are "unprofitable (anopheleis) and useless." This is the direct antithesis of the "good and profitable" works mentioned in verse 8. The contrast is sharp: while the Gospel (verses 4–7) produces "profit" in the form of eternal life and transformed living, religious bickering (verse 9) produces nothing of value for the soul or the community.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Hebrews 7:18: “For on the one hand there is an annulling of the former commandment because of its weakness and unprofitableness (anopheles),” - Titus 3:8 (Opposite): “...These things are good and profitable (ophelima) to men.” - 2 Timothy 2:14 (Related Concept): “...not to strive about words to no profit (ep’ oudeni chresimo-to no use)...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that the value of any theological discussion is measured by its "utility" in producing godliness. It suggests that "winning" a foolish dispute is actually a loss, because the entire endeavor was "unprofitable" from the start. By labeling these things as anopheleis, Paul provides Titus with a clear criterion for leadership: if it doesn't help the "washing and renewing" of the people, it should be avoided.
