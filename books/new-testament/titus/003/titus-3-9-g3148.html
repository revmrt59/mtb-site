Summary:

STRIVINGS - ( mache , Strong’s [3148])

This term refers to verbal battles, fights, or quarrels. In the context of Titus 3:9, it specifically describes intense and persistent conflicts regarding the law that are "unprofitable and useless" for the growth of the believer and the church.

Full Study:

  1. Greek Roots / Derivation

    • Derived from the verb machomai, meaning "to fight," "to strive," or "to quarrel".

    • It signifies a state of active conflict or a specific engagement in combat.

  2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

    • A fight, battle, or combat.

    • In secular Greek, it frequently referred to physical combat or warfare, but in the New Testament, it almost exclusively denotes verbal strife, quarrels, or contentions.

    • It identifies a struggle that has moved beyond a simple disagreement into a full-scale "battle" of words.

  3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures

    • The Battle Ground: In ancient Greek epics like the Iliad, mache described the localized, individual clashes between warriors within the broader theater of a war (polemos).

    • The Physical Duel: It pictured "hand-to-hand" combat, representing the close-quarters and personal nature of a struggle between opponents.

    • The Legal Contest: In civic contexts, it could describe the militant stance of those who "bear arms" or aggressively prosecute a dispute in a public or legal forum.

  4. Theological Implications

    • Verbal strivings are presented as the antithesis of "good and profitable" doctrine.

    • Such conflicts are described as "unprofitable" (vain/empty) and "useless" (worthless) because they lack eternal fruit and fail to build up the body of Christ.

    • Strivings are often the product of human pride and the desire to win a debate rather than a genuine seeking of the truth.

  5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:9

    • In Titus 3:9, "strivings about the law" refers to the petty, legalistic battles involving the minutiae of the Mosaic Law or Jewish traditions.

    • The word is used in the plural (machas), indicating a repetitive and persistent pattern of bickering rather than a one-time disagreement.

    • Believers are instructed to "avoid" these strivings, a term that implies literally "turning the other way" to maintain focus on the Gospel.

  6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

    • Genesis 13:7 (LXX): “And there was strife ( mache ) between the herdsmen of Abram's livestock and the herdsmen of Lot's livestock.”

    • 2 Corinthians 7:5: “...our bodies had no rest, but we were troubled on every side. Outside were conflicts ( machai ), inside were fears.”

    • 2 Timothy 2:23: “But avoid foolish and ignorant disputes, knowing that they generate strife ( machas ).”

    • James 4:1: “Where do wars and fights ( machai ) come from among you?”

  7. Other Relevant Insights

    • Avoiding strivings is a safeguard for the church, preventing the atmosphere of schism and division.

    • Engaging in such verbal battles is a waste of spiritual energy and resources that should instead be dedicated to meeting urgent needs.

    • A focus on sound doctrine naturally displaces the desire for profitless controversy.
