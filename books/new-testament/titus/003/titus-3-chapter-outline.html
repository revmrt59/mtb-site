Titus 3 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Titus 3 urges believers to live as good citizens and gentle neighbors because God saved them by His kindness and mercy, not by their works. It then directs Titus to avoid unprofitable controversies, to warn and then reject the divisive, and to carry out Paul’s closing instructions.

Chapter Sections

1. Titus 3:1–2 — Reminders for Life Among Others

Believers are to be subject to rulers and authorities, ready for every good work, speaking evil of no one, peaceable, gentle, and showing humility to all men.

2. Titus 3:3–8 — Saved by God’s Mercy

Believers were once foolish and enslaved to lusts, but when the kindness and love of God our Savior appeared, He saved them through the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Spirit, not by works of righteousness. This faithful saying is to be affirmed so that believers will be careful to maintain good works.

3. Titus 3:9–11 — Avoid Disputes and Warn the Divisive

Titus is to avoid foolish disputes, genealogies, contentions, and strivings about the law, which are unprofitable and useless. A divisive man is to be rejected after a first and second admonition.

4. Titus 3:12–15 — Final Instructions and Greetings

Paul asks Titus to come to Nicopolis when Artemas or Tychicus arrives and to send Zenas and Apollos on their journey supplied. Believers are to learn to maintain good works, and Paul closes with greetings and grace.
