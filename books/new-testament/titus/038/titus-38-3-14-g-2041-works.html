Summary:

WORKS - ( ergon , Strong’s [2041]) Summary: A deed, action, or labor. It describes the tangible result of physical or mental effort, representing the practical output of a person's life and character in the form of completed tasks or behaviors.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the primary root ergo, which means "to work" or "to do." It is the source of the English word "energy," signifying the power or force required to accomplish a specific performance.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Work, deed, labor, or anything accomplished by hand or mind. In the New Testament, it signifies the actions that define a person's life, whether they are the "works of the law," the "works of darkness," or the "good works" of a believer. It denotes a functional reality where a person's inner state is made visible through their outward activities.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Completed Project: In secular Greek, it described a finished building, a statue, or a piece of craftsmanship that stood as the "work" of the artisan. - The Day's Labor: It pictured the physical toil of a farmer in the field, representing the "sweat and effort" required to produce a harvest. - The Functional Tool: It was used for the "operation" or "working" of a tool or a part of the body, illustrating that something is fulfilling its intended purpose.

4. Theological Implications The New Testament carefully distinguishes between "works" as a means of salvation (which is rejected in Titus 3:5) and "works" as the evidence of salvation (which is commanded in Titus 3:8). "Works" are the "fruit" of the Spirit's "renewing" power, showing that the believer has been "created in Christ Jesus" for the purpose of doing them (Ephesians 2:10). They are the horizontal expression of vertical grace, providing "profit" to others and glory to God.

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:8 & 3:14 In the NKJV of Titus 3:8 and 3:14, Paul uses the phrase "good works (ergon)." This identifies the specific activity that those who have believed in God must be "careful to maintain." By linking "works" to "urgent needs" in verse 14, Paul emphasizes that these are not just religious ceremonies but practical, life-sustaining actions that help others and prevent a life from being "unfruitful."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Matthew 5:16: “...that they may see your good works (erga) and glorify your Father...” - John 6:29: “This is the work (ergon) of God, that you believe in Him whom He sent.” - John 17:4: “I have finished the work (ergon) which You have given Me to do.” - Acts 9:36: “...This woman was full of good works (ergon) and charitable deeds...” - Romans 3:20: “Therefore by the deeds (ergon) of the law no flesh will be justified...” - Ephesians 2:10: “For we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works (ergois)...” - Titus 3:5: “not by works (ergon) of righteousness which we have done, but according to His mercy...”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that "faith" is never intended to be stagnant; it must find its fulfillment in "works." It suggests that a believer's "energy" should be spent on what is "good and profitable" rather than the "unprofitable" disputes of verse 9. By "standing at the head" (maintaining) of these works, the believer fulfills their "duty" and "business" (needs) within the body of Christ.
