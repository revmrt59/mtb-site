Summary:

GOOD - ( kalos , Strong’s [2570]) Summary: Intrinsically good, beautiful, or fair. It describes a quality that is not only morally right but also aesthetically pleasing, excellent in its execution, and beneficial in its effect on others.

Full Study:

1. Greek Roots / Derivation Derived from the root kal-, which suggests beauty or attractiveness. While agathos refers to moral goodness in the abstract, kalos refers to a goodness that is visible, honorable, and "good to look upon."

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition Beautiful, handsome, excellent, or eminent. In the New Testament, it signifies that which is morally honorable, pleasing to God, and useful to men. It denotes a standard of excellence where the "goodness" is demonstrated through practical and effective action.

3. Usage in Greek Culture & Word Pictures - The Masterpiece: In secular Greek, it described a work of art or a building that was "beautifully" constructed, where every part was in its proper place and served its purpose. - The Fruitful Tree: It pictured a tree that was "good" (kalos) because it produced healthy, attractive, and nourishing fruit, contrasting with a "bad" (sapros) tree. - The Noble Soldier: It was used for a soldier who fought "honorably" and "well," representing a character that was both brave and respectable in the eyes of the public.

4. Theological Implications The "goodness" of the believer is a reflection of the "kindness and love of God" (verse 4) manifested through the "washing of regeneration." It identifies that the Christian life is intended to be "attractive" to the world, drawing others to the Gospel through the "beauty" of a transformed life. Being "good" is not a means of earning salvation, but it is the "profitable" result of having "believed in God" (verse 8).

5. Contextual Nuance in Titus 3:8 & 3:14 In the NKJV of Titus 3:8 and 3:14, Paul emphasizes the importance of "good (kala) works." This identifies a specific quality of deeds-those that are intrinsically excellent and provide a visible benefit to the community. By calling these works "good and profitable to men," Paul highlights that the believer's "beauty" is found in their practical service and their readiness to "meet urgent needs."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV) - Matthew 5:16: “Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good (kala) works and glorify your Father...” - Matthew 13:23: “But he who received seed on the good (kalen) ground is he who hears the word and understands it...” - John 10:11: “I am the good (kalos) shepherd. The good (kalos) shepherd gives His life for the sheep.” - 1 Timothy 1:18: “...that by them you may wage the good (kalen) warfare,” - 1 Timothy 4:6: “...you will be a good (kalos) minister of Jesus Christ...” - 2 Timothy 4:7: “I have fought the good (kalon) fight, I have finished the race...” - Hebrews 10:24: “And let us consider one another in order to stir up love and good (kalon) works,”

7. Other Relevant Insights This word highlights that "good works" (verse 8) should be performed with a sense of "excellence" rather than as a mere duty. It suggests that the "maintenance" of these works is what prevents a life from becoming "unfruitful" (verse 14). By "standing at the head" of what is kalos, the believer proves that the "renewing of the Spirit" has resulted in a life that is both morally upright and practically "beautiful."
