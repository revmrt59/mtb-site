Who is Titus?

Titus 1:1;4 Paul, a bondservant of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ, … 4 To Titus, a true son in our common faith: …. NKJV

Titus 1:1;4 This letter is from Paul, a slave of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ…4 I am writing to Titus, my true son in the faith that we share. … NLT

  1. Brief background of Titus

  • He is mentioned 13 times in Paul’s letters but not in Acts

  • He was a Greek who was converted to Christianity (Gal 2:1)

  • Paul calls him a “true son” Titus 1:4 To Titus, a true son in our common faith: … NKJV

  1. The History of Titus in the NT: The Man for Tough Tasks

    1. Galatians 2:1-5 Then fourteen years later I went back to Jerusalem again, this time with Barnabas; and Titus came along, too. 2 I went there because God revealed to me that I should go. While I was there I met privately with those considered to be leaders of the church and shared with them the message I had been preaching to the Gentiles. I wanted to make sure that we were in agreement, for fear that all my efforts had been wasted and I was running the race for nothing.:3 And they supported me and did not even demand that my companion Titus be circumcised, though he was a Gentile.4 Even that question came up only because of some so-called believers there—false ones, really—who were secretly brought in. They sneaked in to spy on us and take away the freedom we have in Christ Jesus. They wanted to enslave us and force us to follow their Jewish regulations. 5 But we refused to give in to them for a single moment. We wanted to preserve the truth of the gospel message for you. NLT - AD48-49

    2. Titus next appears in connection with Paul’s mission to Corinth where Titus was sent into a difficult situation

      1. 2Corinthians 2:3-4 And I wrote this very thing to you, lest, when I came, I should have sorrow over those from whom I ought to have joy, having confidence in you all that my joy is the joy of you all. 4 For out of much affliction and anguish of heart I wrote to you, with many tears, not that you should be grieved, but that you might know the love which I have so abundantly for you. NKJV

      2. 2 Corinthians 7:8 For even if I made you sorry with my letter, I do not regret it; though I did regret it. For I perceive that the same epistle made you sorry, though only for a while. NKJV

        1. After Corinth Titus evidently did now show up as Paul expected

          1. 2Corinthians 2:12-13 Furthermore, when I came to Troas to preach Christs gospel, and a door was opened to me by the Lord, 2Corinthians 2:13 I had no rest in my spirit, because I did not find Titus my brother; but taking my leave of them, I departed for Macedonia. NKJV

        2. Finally, Titus caught up with Paul in Macedonia

          1. 2 Corinthians 7:6-8 But God, who encourages those who are discouraged, encouraged us by the arrival of Titus. 7 His presence was a joy, but so was the news he brought of the encouragement he received from you. When he told us how much you long to see me, and how sorry you are for what happened, and how loyal you are to me, I was filled with joy! 8 I am not sorry that I sent that severe letter to you, though I was sorry at first, for I know it was painful to you for a little while. NLT

        3. Paul then wrote 2 Corinthians and again called upon Titus to carry the letter to the church and take a collection

          1. 2 Cor 8:6 So we have urged Titus, who encouraged your giving in the first place, to return to you and encourage you to finish this ministry of giving. NLT

          2. 2 Cor12:18 When I urged Titus to visit you and sent our other brother with him, did Titus take advantage of you? No! For we have the same spirit and walk in each other’s steps, doing things the same way. NLT

      3. Titus appears in another important role on the island of Crete (Titus 1:4).

        1. Paul’s Ministry Visit (With Titus)

          1. Titus 1:5 For this reason I left you in Crete, that you should set in order the things that are lacking, and appoint elders in every city as I commanded you— NKJV

        2. Crete had a notorious reputation in the ancient world.

          1. Titus 1:12-13 One of them, a prophet of their own, said, Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, lazy gluttons. 13 This testimony is true. Therefore rebuke them sharply, that they may be sound in the faith, NKJV

          2. Crete was religiously diverse with Pagan and Jewish Influences (Titus 1:10–14; 3:9)

        3. Why Paul Left Titus in Crete?

          1. Establish qualified elders (Tit 1:5)

          2. Silence false teachers (Tit 1:10)

          3. Correct people by teaching healthy doctrine (Tit 2:1-10)

          4. Teach the true Gospel transforms lives (Tit 2:11-12)

          5. Encourage good works

      4. The last reference to Titus is at the near the end of Paul’s life Paul remarks in passing that Titus has departed for mission work in Dalmatia (modern Yugoslavia) having faithfully served Paul for a long-time (AD48-63?)

        1. 2 Timothy 4:10 for Demas has forsaken me, having loved this present world, and has departed for Thessalonica—Crescens for Galatia, Titus for Dalmatia. NKJV

  2. Questions for Discussion

    1. What stands out about Titus to you? What qualities do you see in Titus that made Paul trust him with such a volatile situation

    2. Titus was called a “true son.”

      1. When you hear God speak your name, do you see yourself more as a servant, a worker… or as a son or daughter?

      2. How does your self-identity affect the way you walk with God and serve others?

    3. Titus was trusted with volatile, painful, and risky assignments.

      1. How do you usually respond when obedience becomes uncomfortable or relationally costly?

      2. What has pressure revealed about your faith in recent seasons?

    4. Titus stood silently as a living test case for the truth of the gospel.

      1. Have you ever been placed in a situation where your life — not just your words — was the message?

      2. What part of your life currently needs to align more deeply with the gospel you believe?

    5. Titus’ obedience could have cost him acceptance, comfort, and safety.

      1. What has obedience to Jesus cost you personally?

      2. Is there anything you may be protecting that is quietly limiting your obedience?

    6. Paul was not just Titus’ leader, he was his spiritual father.

      1. Who has permission to speak correction, truth, and encouragement into your life?

      2. What keeps you from allowing spiritual fathering or mothering in your walk with God?

    7. Titus was still being trusted decades later.

      1. What do you think sustains spiritual endurance over a lifetime rather than a season?

      2. What rhythms, disciplines, or relationships might God be inviting you into now to deepen your long-term faithfulness?

    8. God entrusted His church to Titus.

      1. What kind of heart do you believe God entrusts His people to?

      2. What might God currently be shaping in you because He plans to entrust more to you later?
