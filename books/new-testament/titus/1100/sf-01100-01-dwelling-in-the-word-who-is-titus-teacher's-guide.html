  1. Intro

    1. Tonight we are starting our journey through the book of Titus

      1. It is an appropriate book in the year of holiness

      2. Holiness is being wholly His - living a life that is genuinely shaped by Him and separated unto Him

      3. Titus helps teach us about practical holiness

      4. “Titus is holiness with its sleeves rolled up.” (Unknown)

      5. Titus teaches us what holiness looks like in daily life

      6. Holiness is the evidence that truth has taken root.”

      7. Holiness is the fruit of the Word becoming flesh in us

    2. Titus belongs to a small group of letters we call the Pastoral Epistles

      1. Pastoral Epistles is the name given to three letters of the apostle Paul: 1 Timothy, 2 Timothy and Titus.

      2. Epistle simply means letter

      3. Pastoral Epistles are distinguished from Paul’s other ten books

        1. Among the last letters Paul wrote - reflect concern of one who is nearing the end of his ministry and life

        2. Addressed to men and not a congregation

          1. Titus 1:4 To Titus, a true son in our common faith…NKJV

          2. 1 Timothy 1:2 To Timothy, a true son in the faith… NKJV

          3. 2 Timothy 1:2 To Timothy, a beloved son… NKJV

          4. 1Corinthians 1:1-2 Paul…2 To the church of God which is at Corinth… NKJV

      4. They are called the pastoral epistles because:

        1. Paul wrote them to two Christian ministers – Timothy and Titus

          1. When Timothy and Titus received these epistles they were not acting, as they had previously done, as missionaries or itinerant evangelists, but had been left by Paul in charge of churches.

            1. Timothy was left having the oversight of the church in Ephesus

            2. Titus was left having the care of the churches in the island of Crete.

          2. The Pastoral Epistles were written to guide them in the discharge of the duties devolving upon them as Christian pastors.

        2. They clearly show Paul’s love and concern as pastor and administrator of several local churches.

        3. They contain instructions concerning the church

      5. Called Pastoral because they seem to be addressed to Pastors of churches and contain instruction for pastoral work in the churches

        1. Outline Pastoral duties

          1. Raising up church leadership

            1. Titus 1:5 For this reason I left you in Crete, that you should set in order the things that are lacking, and appoint elders in every city as I commanded you— NKJV

          2. Teaching sound doctrine

            1. Titus 2:1 But as for you, speak the things which are proper for sound doctrine: NKJV

          3. Preaching the word of God

            1. 2Timothy 4:1 I charge you therefore before God and the Lord Jesus Christ, who will judge the living and the dead at His appearing and His kingdom: 2Timothy 4:2 Preach the word! Be ready in season and out of season. Convince, rebuke, exhort, with all longsuffering and teaching. NKJV

          4. Leading into godliness by example

            1. 1 Timothy 4:12 Let no one despise your youth, but be an example to the believers in word, in conduct, in love, in spirit, in faith, in purity. NKJV

          5. Raise up faithful disciples

            1. 2 Timothy 2:2 And the things that you have heard from me among many witnesses, commit these to faithful men who will be able to teach others also. NKJV

          6. Teach and lead people to pursue godliness

            Titus 2:1-3 But as for you, speak the things which are proper for sound doctrine: 2 that the older men be sober, reverent, temperate, sound in faith, in love, in patience; 3 the older women likewise, that they be reverent in behavior, not slanderers, not given to much wine, teachers of good things— NKJV

          3. Whom is fit to serve in the church 1Ti 3

          4. How can the church care for the widows. 1Ti5

          5. What is the source of my authority - 2Ti 3:16

          6. What should I teach people? Sound doctrine Titus 2

          7. What about prayer? 1Tim 2

      6. Summary of Theme (Nelsons)

        1. The purpose of the Pastoral Epistles is to admonish, instruct, and direct the recipients in their pastoral duties.

        2. These letters deal with the care and the organization of the church, the flock of God.

        3. They contain common injunctions to guard the Christian faith, to appoint qualified officials, to conduct proper worship, and to maintain discipline both personally and in the churches.

        4. They give instructions in the work of the church and show how threats to the doctrinal and moral purity of Christians should be overcome.

    3. Question for reflection and discussion?

      1. What do these letters reveal about how seriously Paul views the health, importance, and holiness of the church?

        1. Paul treats the church as something entrusted, not incidental.

        2. In the Pastoral Epistles, the church is not a gathering that exists for convenience or expression—it is God’s household, Christ’s body, and the steward of truth in the world.

          1. Paul’s seriousness shows up in how carefully he addresses leadership, doctrine, conduct, and correction.

          2. He writes as someone who knows the church can be nurtured or damaged, strengthened or corrupted, depending on whether truth and holiness are guarded.

        3. Paul does not speak casually because he understands that the church is:

          1. the place where truth is preserved

          2. the environment where lives are formed

          3. the witness of God’s character to the world

        4. His tone communicates reverence. Disorder, false teaching, and ungodliness are never treated as minor problems because they distort something God deeply values.

        5. In short, Paul’s seriousness reveals that the church matters to God far more than it often matters to us.

      2. Why do you think Paul spends so much time on order, leadership, and sound doctrine if the church were not deeply precious to God?

        1. Because you protect what you love.

        2. Paul’s attention to order and leadership is not about control—it’s about care.

          1. He knows that without faithful leaders and sound doctrine, people are harmed, truth is diluted, and holiness erodes quietly.

          2. Paul is not trying to build institutions; he is trying to protect souls.

            1. Order matters because truth needs a structure to live in.

            2. Leadership matters because people need examples worth following.

            3. Sound doctrine matters because what people believe shapes how they live.

          3. If the church were disposable, Paul wouldn’t labor this way. He wouldn’t insist on qualified leaders. He wouldn’t warn against false teaching so urgently. He wouldn’t invest years forming people like Timothy and Titus.

        3. Paul’s investment reveals God’s heart: the church is precious enough to guard, fragile enough to protect, and valuable enough to require holy stewardship.

    4. If the church is this precious, who could God trust to care for it? What kind of person could God entrust with that responsibility?

  2. Who was Titus

    1. Titus

      1. Not mentioned in the book of Acts

      2. He is mentioned 13 times in Paul’s letters

      3. He was a Greek who was converted to Christianity (Gal 2:1)

      4. Titus is the Latin form of the Greek name Titos

      5. Titos name means “pleasant” or “agreeable”

    2. Paul calls him a “true son”

      1. Titus 1:4 To Titus, a true son in our common faith: … NKJV

      2. This means most likely Paul led him to the Lord and discipled him like a father

      3. Son implies formation and allowing someone to speak into your life

      4. Ministry flows best from relationship

      5. True - meaning Titus service and faith was genuine and tested

    3. The History of Titus in the NT: The Man for Tough Tasks

      1. Paul first mentions Titus in Gal 2:1-3.

        1. Galatians 2:1-3 Then after fourteen years I went up again to Jerusalem with Barnabas, and also took Titus with me. 2 And I went up by revelation, and communicated to them that gospel which I preach among the Gentiles, but privately to those who were of reputation, lest by any means I might run, or had run, in vain. 3 Yet not even Titus who was with me, being a Greek, was compelled to be circumcised. NKJV

        2. As an uncircumcised Gentile, Titus accompanied Paul and Barnabas to Jerusalem as a living example of a great theological truth: Gentiles need not be circumcised in order to be saved.

        3. His very presence tested whether salvation was truly by grace

        4. Paul intentionally brought Titus into theological conflict

        5. It took courage for Titus to stand with Paul before the Jews at that early stage

        6. Willingness to be examined

        7. Confidence in the gospel, not in cultural acceptance

      2. Titus next appears in connection with Paul’s mission to Corinth.

        1. The church at Corinth was one of the most relationally volatile communities Paul ever pastored.

          1. While Paul was in Ephesus during his third missionary journey, he received disturbing reports from the church at Corinth—reports of division, sexual immorality, leadership challenges, and growing resistance to his apostolic authority (cf. 1 Cor 1:11; 5:1; 2 Cor 10–13).

          2. After writing earlier letters and making a painful visit in which he confronted sin directly and endured personal rejection, Paul wrote a severe letter and sent it to Corinth by the hand of Titus (2 Cor 2:3–4; 7:6–9).

            1. 2Corinthians 2:3-4 And I wrote this very thing to you, lest, when I came, I should have sorrow over those from whom I ought to have joy, having confidence in you all that my joy is the joy of you all. 4 For out of much affliction and anguish of heart I wrote to you, with many tears, not that you should be grieved, but that you might know the love which I have so abundantly for you. NKJV

            2. 2 Corinthians 7:8 For even if I made you sorry with my letter, I do not regret it; though I did regret it. For I perceive that the same epistle made you sorry, though only for a while. NKJV

            1. In doing so, Paul sent Titus into a potential relational landmine.

            2. Titus carried hard words, but he also carried Paul’s heart.

            3. Paul repeatedly sent Titus—not as a distant messenger, but as a trusted representative who could both speak truth and discern the spiritual condition of the people.

        2. When Titus did not return promptly with news of the situation, Paul became deeply troubled.

          1. Leaving Ephesus, Paul traveled to Troas, where a door for ministry was open—but he could not rest because he did not find Titus (2 Cor 2:12–13).

          2. From Troas, Paul went on to Macedonia in a state of inward and outward distress

            1. 2 Corinthians 7:5 For indeed, when we came to Macedonia, our bodies had no rest, but we were troubled on every side. Outside were conflicts, inside were fears. NKJV

            2. 2Corinthians 2:12-13 Furthermore, when I came to Troas to preach Christs gospel, and a door was opened to me by the Lord, 2Corinthians 2:13 I had no rest in my spirit, because I did not find Titus my brother; but taking my leave of them, I departed for Macedonia. NKJV

        3. Finally, in Macedonia, Titus met the anxious apostle with the good news that the church at Corinth had responded with godly sorrow and genuine repentance.

          1. 2Corinthians 7:6-9 Nevertheless God, who comforts the downcast, comforted us by the coming of Titus, 7 and not only by his coming, but also by the consolation with which he was comforted in you, when he told us of your earnest desire, your mourning, your zeal for me, so that I rejoiced even more. 8 For even if I made you sorry with my letter, I do not regret it; though I did regret it. For I perceive that the same epistle made you sorry, though only for a while. 9 Now I rejoice, not that you were made sorry, but that your sorrow led to repentance. For you were made sorry in a godly manner, that you might suffer loss from us in nothing. NKJV

          2. Comforted and relieved, Paul then wrote what we know as 2 Corinthians, likely from Macedonia (possibly Philippi), expressing both joy and continued pastoral concern.

          3. Paul again entrusted Titus with responsibility—this time to help complete the collection for the poor believers in Jerusalem. (2 Cor 7:5-16).

            1. 2 Corinthians 8:6 So we urged Titus, that as he had begun, so he would also complete this grace in you as well. NKJV

            2. 2 Corinthians 12:18 I urged Titus, and sent our brother with him. Did Titus take advantage of you? Did we not walk in the same spirit? Did we not walk in the same steps? NKJV

        4. “Paul entrusted Titus with the most emotionally volatile pastoral assignment in his ministry—because Titus had the character to carry truth with love, firmness with humility, and authority with affection

      3. Titus appears in another important role on the island of Crete (Titus 1:4).

        1. The Origin of the Church in Crete

          1. The church in Crete was founded very early in Christian history, likely around AD 30, in the immediate aftermath of Pentecost.

            1. Acts 2:11 Cretans and Arabs—we hear them speaking in our own tongues the wonderful works of God. NKJV

            2. Most likely these Cretan Jews:

              1. Heard Peters preaching

              2. Witnessed the outpouring of the Holy Spirit

              3. Believed and were baptized (Acts 2:41)

              4. Returned to Crete carrying the gospel

          2. Likely Christianity arrived in Crete at the very birth of the church, through Jewish believers

        2. Paul’s Ministry Visit (With Titus)

          1. Later, Paul ministered freely in Crete with Titus

          2. Titus 1:5 For this reason I left you in Crete, that you should set in order the things that are lacking, and appoint elders in every city as I commanded you— NKJV

          3. This implies:

            1. Multiple churches already existed

            2. Paul exercised apostolic authority

            3. Titus was intentionally stationed to organize leadership

          4. This visit is not recorded in Acts.

        3. Paul’s ministry visit to Crete:

          1. Was not part of the 1st, 2nd, or 3rd missionary journeys

          2. Did not occur during Acts 27

          3. Most likely occurred after Paul’s first Roman imprisonment (Acts 28)

        4. Most scholars conclude:

          1. Paul was released around AD 62

          2. He resumed missionary activity

          3. He visited Crete with Titus

          4. He later wrote the Epistle to Titus (AD 63–65)

        5. This period is often called Paul’s fourth missionary phase which is not recorded in the book of Acts

        6. The Cultural, Religious and Moral Background of Crete

          1. Crete had a notorious reputation in the ancient world.

          2. In Titus 1:12–13, Paul cites a Cretan poet (Epimenides (6th century BC):

            1. Titus 1:12-13 One of them, a prophet of their own, said, Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, lazy gluttons. 13 This testimony is true. Therefore rebuke them sharply, that they may be sound in the faith, NKJV

            2. This environment made discipleship leadership formation challenging.

          3. Crete was religiously diverse:

            1. Pagan Influences

              1. Greek mythology and local cults

              2. Claims that Zeus was born in Crete

              3. Emperor worship under Roman rule

            2. Jewish Influence

              1. Synagogues across the island

              2. Jewish teachers promoting: (Titus 1:10–14; 3:9)

                • Genealogies

                • Myths

                • Legalistic distortions of the Law

          4. The result was a mishmash of Judaism, paganism, and Christianity.

        7. Why Paul Left Titus in Crete?

          1. Paul left Titus to:

            1. Establish qualified elders (Tit 1:5)

            2. Silence false teachers (Tit 1:10)

            3. Correct people by teaching healthy doctrine (Tit 2:1-10)

            4. Teach the true Gospel transforms lives (Tit 2:11-12)

          2. Purpose

          3. Like his first epistle to Timothy, this letter is written to a young preacher assigned a difficult task. Evidently the churches on the island of Crete were in need of maturation, and this letter is designed to assist Titus in that work. Therefore, Paul wrote to encourage Titus:

            1. To see that qualified elders were appointed in every city (1:5-9)

            2. To preach things befitting sound doctrine (2:1)

            3. To exhort the brethren to be zealous for good works (2:14; 3:1,8,14)

      4. A final reference to Titus comes from 2 Tim 4:10, where Paul remarks in passing that Titus has departed for mission work in Dalmatia (modern Yugoslavia).

        1. 2 Timothy 4:10 for Demas has forsaken me, having loved this present world, and has departed for Thessalonica—Crescens for Galatia, Titus for Dalmatia. NKJV

        2. He Faithfully served Paul for decades

  1. Questions for Discussion

    1. What stands out about Titus to you? What qualities do you see in Titus that made Paul trust him with such a volatile situation

    2. Titus was called a “true son.”

      1. When you hear God speak your name, do you see yourself more as a servant, a worker… or as a son or daughter?

      2. How does your self-identity affect the way you walk with God and serve others?

    3. Titus was trusted with volatile, painful, and risky assignments.

      1. How do you usually respond when obedience becomes uncomfortable or relationally costly?

      2. What has pressure revealed about your faith in recent seasons?

    4. Titus stood silently as a living test case for the truth of the gospel.

      1. Have you ever been placed in a situation where your life — not just your words — was the message?

      2. What part of your life currently needs to align more deeply with the gospel you believe?

    5. Titus’ obedience could have cost him acceptance, comfort, and safety.

      1. What has obedience to Jesus cost you personally?

      2. Is there anything you may be protecting that is quietly limiting your obedience?

    6. Paul was not just Titus’ leader, he was his spiritual father.

      1. Who has permission to speak correction, truth, and encouragement into your life?

      2. What keeps you from allowing spiritual fathering or mothering in your walk with God?

    7. Titus was still being trusted decades later.

      1. What do you think sustains spiritual endurance over a lifetime rather than a season?

      2. What rhythms, disciplines, or relationships might God be inviting you into now to deepen your long-term faithfulness?

    8. God entrusted His church to Titus.

      1. What kind of heart do you believe God entrusts His people to?

      2. What might God currently be shaping in you because He plans to entrust more to you later?
