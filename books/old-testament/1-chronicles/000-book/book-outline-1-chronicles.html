1 Chronicles Book Outline

Major Movements

I. The Family of Israel from Adam to the Returned Exiles (1 Chronicles 1:1–9:44)

The book opens with names, tracing the line from Adam through Abraham and the sons of Israel, with special attention to Judah and David’s royal line, to the Levites, and to the tribes. These lists ground a returned people in God’s long dealings with them: Judah was carried away to Babylon “because of their unfaithfulness” (9:1), yet priests, Levites, gatekeepers, and families live again in their cities and serve at the house of God. Saul’s family line closes the movement and leads directly into the account of his death.

II. Saul’s Fall and All Israel Gathers to David (1 Chronicles 10:1–12:40)

Saul dies on Mount Gilboa “for his unfaithfulness which he had committed against the LORD,” and the LORD turns the kingdom over to David the son of Jesse. All Israel comes to Hebron and anoints David king “according to the word of the LORD by Samuel,” and after he takes Jerusalem he grows great because “the LORD of hosts was with him.” Mighty men and warriors from every tribe join him “with a loyal heart” until all Israel is of one mind to make David king.

III. The Ark Comes to Jerusalem (1 Chronicles 13:1–16:43)

David sets out to bring back the ark, neglected “since the days of Saul,” with great joy, but the journey on a cart ends in Uzzah’s death, while the LORD blesses the house of Obed-Edom. After the LORD establishes David’s kingdom and gives him victory over the Philistines, David corrects his approach: the Levites consecrate themselves and carry the ark “as Moses had commanded according to the word of the LORD,” and all Israel brings it up with shouting and music. David sets the ark in the tent he had prepared and appoints Levites to commemorate, thank, and praise the LORD, whose “mercy endures forever.”

IV. The LORD Promises David a House (1 Chronicles 17:1–20:8)

David wants to build a house for the ark, but God answers through Nathan that He will build David a house: David’s son will build God’s house, and his throne will be “established forever.” David responds with humble prayer and praise. The LORD then preserves David wherever he goes, subduing his enemies as promised, and David reigns over all Israel and administers “judgment and justice to all his people.”

V. Judgment, Mercy, and the Place for the House (1 Chronicles 21:1–22:1)

Satan stands up against Israel and moves David to number the people, and judgment falls on the nation. David confesses, “I have sinned greatly,” chooses to fall into the hand of the LORD, “for His mercies are very great,” and asks that the punishment fall on himself rather than on the people. At God’s command he builds an altar on Ornan’s threshing floor, the LORD answers “from heaven by fire,” and David declares, “This is the house of the LORD God.”

VI. David Prepares for the House and Its Service (1 Chronicles 22:2–27:34)

David gathers stone, iron, bronze, cedar, gold, and silver because Solomon is “young and inexperienced,” and he charges Solomon and Israel’s leaders to “arise and build the sanctuary of the LORD God.” Old and full of days, David then organizes the Levites, the priestly divisions, the musicians who “prophesy with harps, stringed instruments, and cymbals,” the gatekeepers and treasurers, and the army and officials of the kingdom. Each has an assigned place in the service of God’s house and His people.

VII. Willing Hearts and Solomon on the LORD’s Throne (1 Chronicles 28:1–29:30)

David assembles Israel’s leaders and tells them that God has chosen Solomon to sit “on the throne of the kingdom of the LORD over Israel.” He charges Solomon to serve God “with a loyal heart and with a willing mind,” hands him the plans he had by the Spirit, and gives his own treasure, and the people rejoice because they “offered willingly.” David blesses the LORD, confessing, “Yours is the kingdom, O LORD,” and Solomon sits “on the throne of the LORD as king instead of David his father” as David dies “in a good old age.”
