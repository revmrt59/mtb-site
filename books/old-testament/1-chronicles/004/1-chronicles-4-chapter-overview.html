Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 4
Main Idea:

The chapter remembers Judah’s wider households and Simeon’s families and towns, and within these lists it pauses on Jabez, who called on the God of Israel and received what he asked.

Setting:

After David’s royal line in chapter 3, the record returns to Judah’s other families and then to Simeon, whose towns lay within Judah’s territory (Joshua 19:1–9).

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

After the royal line of chapter 3, the record turns to ordinary households. Judah’s clans appear with their towns and their trades: craftsmen at Ge Harashim (4:14), linen workers (4:21), and potters who “dwelt with the king for his work” (4:23). Women are named as well, among them Bithiah “the daughter of Pharaoh” (4:18). God’s people included those who built, wove and shaped clay, as well as those who ruled.

In the middle of the list stands Jabez. His mother gave him a name that sounds like pain, saying, “Because I bore him in pain” (4:9), but Jabez “called on the God of Israel” (4:10). He asked for blessing, for enlarged territory, for God’s hand to be with him and for protection from evil, “that I may not cause pain” (4:10). The account ends simply: “So God granted him what he requested” (4:10). A name tied to pain did not decide his life, because he brought his need to the God of Israel.

The chapter then turns to Simeon, a smaller tribe whose families did not “multiply as much as the children of Judah” (4:27), and records its towns and later movements. The expedition to Gedor took place in Hezekiah’s reign; the expedition to Mount Seir is undated. From beginning to end the chapter shows a God who keeps the record of His people’s homes, work and prayers, and who hears those who call on Him.

Chapter Sections

1 Chronicles 4:1–8 — Further Families of Judah

The genealogy returns to Judah’s families beyond the royal line, naming descendants of Shobal and Hur and the two wives of Ashhur the father of Tekoa. The names mix ancestors, clans and towns, tying families to the places they settled.

1 Chronicles 4:9–10 — The Prayer of Jabez

Jabez, more honorable than his brothers, calls on the God of Israel for blessing, enlarged territory, God’s hand with him, and protection from evil and pain. God grants his request, and the account adds no further details about his life.

1 Chronicles 4:11–23 — Clans, Craftsmen, and Potters of Judah

The record continues with Othniel, the sons of Caleb the son of Jephunneh, Bithiah the daughter of Pharaoh, the house of the linen workers and the potters. These notes honor skilled and ordinary work, including service done for the king.

1 Chronicles 4:24–33 — Simeon’s Families and Towns

Simeon’s descendants are listed, with the note that their families did not grow like Judah’s. Their towns, including Beersheba and Ziklag, are named as their dwelling places until the reign of David.

1 Chronicles 4:34–43 — Simeon’s Expansion

Simeon’s leaders seek pasture for their flocks near Gedor, and in Hezekiah’s days they destroy the people living there and settle in their place. Five hundred Simeonites go to Mount Seir, defeat the remaining Amalekites and live there to the writer’s day.

Why This Chapter Matters

Jabez’s prayer is one of the best-known passages in Chronicles, and the chapter shows why it matters: a person marked by pain can bring that need directly to God and ask for His blessing, His presence and His protection. Yet the passage reports one man’s request and God’s answer. It does not promise wealth, land or a painless life to everyone who repeats the words, and it should be read with the wider teaching of Scripture on asking according to God’s will (1 John 5:14).

The chapter also widens our sense of who counts in God’s story. Potters, linen workers, a smaller tribe and families looking for pasture all have a place in the record. Faithful prayer and honest work both belong to what one generation hands to the next.

Key Themes to Watch For

Ordinary households remembered. Judah’s wider families and their towns stand alongside the royal line (4:1–8, 11–23).

Calling on the God of Israel. Jabez brings his request to God, and God grants it (4:9–10).

Work that matters. Craftsmen, linen workers and potters are named for their work (4:14, 21–23).

Simeon within Judah. Simeon’s towns lay inside Judah’s territory, which helps explain why their records stand together (4:24–33; Joshua 19:1–9).

Land and conflict. Simeon’s families expand by force. The Gedor expedition is dated to Hezekiah’s reign; the Seir expedition is undated. The record keeps the violence in view (4:38–43).

Key Words & Concepts

Jabez. His name sounds like the Hebrew word for pain, which gives his request about pain its force (4:9–10).

“Enlarge my territory.” A concrete request for land among Israel’s families (4:10). Applying it to wider responsibility or service is an application, not the original meaning of the words.

Bithiah the daughter of Pharaoh. The wife of Mered, named among Judah’s families (4:18). The wording of 4:17–18 is difficult, and the text tells us nothing more about her.

Ge Harashim. A place whose name refers to craftsmen, as the note “for they were craftsmen” explains (4:14).

Meunites and Amalekites. Peoples displaced or defeated by Simeon’s families (4:41–43). The Amalekites had long been Israel’s enemies (1 Samuel 15).

“To this day.” The writer’s way of describing conditions in his own time (4:41, 43). It does not mean the same families live there now.
