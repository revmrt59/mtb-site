1 Samuel Book Outline

Major Movements

I. Samuel Raised Up Before the LORD (1 Samuel 1:1–3:21)

God answers Hannah’s prayer and gives Samuel, whom she gives back to the LORD for life, and her song proclaims the God who brings low and lifts up and who will exalt the horn of His anointed. While Eli’s sons despise the LORD’s offering and a word of judgment falls on Eli’s house, the boy Samuel grows before the LORD, learns to hear His voice, and is established as His prophet in a time when the word of the LORD was rare.

II. The Ark, Judgment, and Return to the LORD (1 Samuel 4:1–7:17)

Israel carries the ark into battle as though God’s presence could secure victory; the ark is captured, Eli’s sons die, Eli falls at the news, and the glory departs from Israel. The LORD humbles the Philistines and their god Dagon until the ark is sent back, His holiness strikes the men of Beth Shemesh who look into it, and during its long stay at Kirjath Jearim Israel laments after the LORD. At Samuel’s call the people put away their foreign gods, the LORD thunders against the Philistines at Mizpah, and Samuel judges Israel all the days of his life.

III. Israel Asks for a King (1 Samuel 8:1–12:25)

Israel demands a king “like all the nations,” and the LORD identifies the request as a rejection of His own rule, yet He directs Samuel to give them a king. God chooses Saul, Samuel anoints him, and the Spirit empowers him; after his victory at Jabesh Gilead the kingdom is renewed at Gilgal. In his farewell Samuel calls king and people to fear and serve the LORD and promises not to cease praying for them.

IV. Saul’s Disobedience and Rejection (1 Samuel 13:1–15:35)

At Gilgal Saul does not wait for Samuel as commanded but offers the burnt offering himself, and Samuel tells him that he has not kept the commandment of the LORD and that his kingdom will not continue; the LORD has sought a man after His own heart. Jonathan’s faith brings victory at Michmash while Saul’s rash oath endangers his own son. When Saul spares Agag and the best of Amalek’s spoil, Samuel declares that to obey is better than sacrifice, and the LORD rejects Saul from being king.

V. David Anointed and Saul’s Jealousy (1 Samuel 16:1–20:42)

The LORD, who looks at the heart, sends Samuel to anoint David, the youngest son of Jesse; the Spirit of the LORD comes upon David from that day forward and departs from Saul. David enters Saul’s service, defeats Goliath in the name of the LORD of hosts, and behaves wisely because the LORD is with him, while Saul’s fear and jealousy turn to repeated attempts on his life. Jonathan binds himself to David in covenant, warns him, and the two friends part.

VI. David the Fugitive (1 Samuel 21:1–26:25)

Driven from Saul’s presence, David deceives the priest at Nob, a deception whose deadly cost he later owns when Saul slaughters the priests (22:22), and flees on to Gath and the cave of Adullam, gathering the distressed around him and inquiring of the LORD as he delivers Keilah. Twice David has Saul within reach and refuses to stretch out his hand against the LORD’s anointed, leaving judgment with God. Between these accounts Samuel dies, and Abigail restrains David from avenging himself on Nabal.

VII. David Among the Philistines and the Death of Saul (1 Samuel 27:1–31:13)

Despairing of escape from Saul, David takes refuge among the Philistines at Ziklag, where he raids surrounding peoples, leaves no one alive to report it, and deceives Achish. Saul, receiving no answer from the LORD, turns to a medium at En Dor, and the verdict Samuel announced in his lifetime is spoken again: the kingdom has been given to David, and Israel will fall to the Philistines. David, sent back by the Philistine lords, finds Ziklag raided, strengthens himself in the LORD, inquires of Him, and recovers all; Saul and his sons fall on Mount Gilboa, and the men of Jabesh Gilead honor their bodies.
