ELIHU - (ʼĔlîyhûw, Strong's H453)

Summary:

The Hebrew name Elihu translates literally as "He is my God." In 1 Samuel 1:1, Elihu is identified as a direct ancestor of Elkanah, the father of the prophet Samuel. By recording this detailed lineage, Scripture places Samuel's origin within an established Israelite family, showing how God preserved a devout lineage through generations to prepare a prophet and judge during a period of spiritual darkness in Israel.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The name Elihu is a masculine proper noun formed by combining two basic Hebrew elements: 'El, meaning "God," and the personal pronoun hu', meaning "he." Combined, the name forms a short personal confession of faith: "He is my God" or "My God is He."

In the historical setting of 1 Samuel 1:1, Israel was living near the end of the turbulent period of the Judges. It was a time marked by widespread moral decay, spiritual compromise, and unstable leadership. The opening line of 1 Samuel introduces an ordinary family living in the hill country of Ephraim. By naming the ancestors of Elkanah—including his grandfather Elihu—the text grounds the narrative in historical reality and demonstrates that even in times of national decline, God maintained faithful family lines among His people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

As a proper name, the word identifies specific individuals in the Old Testament. Lexically, it functions as a personal name that carries a clear theological statement. The two components reflect a distinct declaration:

  • 'El: The standard ancient Hebrew term for God, emphasizing His power, authority, and divine majesty.

  • hu': The third-person singular pronoun ("he").

When joined, the name expresses personal trust and worship: "He [the LORD] is my God." While the word primarily functions in 1 Samuel 1:1 to identify a specific historical ancestor in a genealogy, the meaning embedded in the name highlights the faith of the family into which Samuel was born.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament historical and wisdom literature, the name Elihu is borne by several distinct figures:

  • An ancestor of Samuel (1 Samuel 1:1): Here, Elihu is listed as the son of Tohu and father of Jeroham, establishing the lineage of Samuel's father, Elkanah.

  • A companion of Job (Job 32:2): In the Book of Job, a younger man named Elihu steps forward to speak after Job's three friends fall silent, attempting to defend God's righteousness and justice.

  • A brave warrior of Manasseh (1 Chronicles 12:20): One of the military leaders who joined David at Ziklag during his flight from Saul.

  • A Levite gatekeeper (1 Chronicles 26:7): A capable man listed among the gatekeepers assigned to sanctuary service during David's reign.

  • A leader in Judah (1 Chronicles 27:18): Identified as an officer over the tribe of Judah and a brother of David (elsewhere called Eliab).

In each instance, the name serves as a proper noun distinguishing a specific person, while testifying to the godward focus of the family that bestowed it.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of Elihu in Samuel's genealogy underscores several key truths about God's work in history:

  • Generational Faithfulness: God often accomplishes His grand redemptive purposes through quiet, generational continuity. Long before Samuel was born to lead Israel back to the LORD, God was at work in the lives of his ancestors, including Elihu.

  • Personal Confession: The name itself ("He is my God") reminds the reader that covenant faith is personal. Israel as a nation was called into covenant with God, but that covenant was lived out through individual families who confessed YHWH as their God.

  • Providential Preparation: During the dark days recorded at the end of Judges, it might have seemed that God's work in Israel had stalled. The genealogy in 1 Samuel 1:1 demonstrates that God was quietly preserving a godly remnant from which He would raise up a deliverer.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:1, Elihu is named in a four-generation patronymic line: "Elkanah the son of Jeroham, the son of Elihu, the son of Tohu, the son of Zuph."

This opening genealogy serves an essential function in the narrative:

  • Historical Grounding: It anchors the story of Samuel in historical reality, showing that Samuel was not a mythic figure but a real prophet descended from a known Israelite family.

  • Lineage and Identity: Providing four generations demonstrates the family's deep roots and respected standing in the region of Ramathaim Zophim.

  • Spiritual Heritage: Noticeably, several names in Samuel's immediate line contain the element 'El (such as Elkanah and Elihu). This naming pattern points to a ongoing tradition of honoring God within the household.

  • 1 Chronicles 26:7 — "The sons of Shemaiah were Othni, Rephael, Obed, and Elzabad, whose brothers were valiant men, Elihu and Semachiah."

  • 1 Chronicles 27:18 — "over Judah, Elihu, one of David’s brothers; over Issachar, Omri the son of Michael;"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The Significance of Naming: In ancient Israel, names were frequently chosen to reflect a parent's prayer, a divine attribute, or a testimony of faith. Giving a child a name meaning "He is my God" was a public witness to the family's allegiance to YHWH alone.

  • Ephraimite and Levite Connections: While 1 Samuel 1:1 describes Elkanah's family as living in the territory of Ephraim ("an Ephraimite"), genealogies in 1 Chronicles 6 indicate that this family had Kohathite Levite origins who resided in the allotted tribal lands of Ephraim. The genealogical record preserved in 1 Samuel 1:1 confirms the family's ancestral identity.
