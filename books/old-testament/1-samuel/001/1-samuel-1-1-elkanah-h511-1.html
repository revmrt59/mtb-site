ELKANAH - (ʼElqânâh, Strong's H511)

Summary:

The name Elkanah is a Hebrew proper noun meaning "God has created" or "God has acquired." It is formed by combining the name for God ('El) with a verb signifying creation or acquisition (qānāh). In 1 Samuel 1:1, the name identifies the father of the prophet Samuel, anchoring the narrative in a specific, devout household through whom God initiates a transformative era for Israel.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The name Elkanah appears at a key turning point in Israel's history. The opening of 1 Samuel marks the end of the chaotic period of the Judges, a time characterized by moral confusion and spiritual decline. In 1 Samuel 1:1, the text introduces a man named Elkanah living in Ramathaim Zophim, located in the hill country of Ephraim.

Although Elkanah resided in the territory of Ephraim, genealogical records in 1 Chronicles clarify that his ancestral lineage belonged to the Kohathite clan of the tribe of Levi. Despite the widespread spiritual decay of his era, Elkanah maintained a personal and family commitment to worship the LORD. He made annual pilgrimages to the sanctuary at Shiloh to offer sacrifices, demonstrating consistent worship during a dark chapter in the nation's life.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Elkanah is a masculine proper noun. The name is a compound word formed from two distinct Hebrew roots:

  • 'El, the foundational word for "God" or "mighty one."

  • qānāh, a verb meaning "to possess," "to acquire," or "to create."

Together, the name translates to "God has created" or "God has acquired." The name expresses a statement of faith, acknowledging divine agency in giving life or claiming ownership over a person or family.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In the Old Testament, the name Elkanah belongs to several distinct individuals, primarily within Levitical lineages:

  • An ancestor in the Korahite family within the tribe of Levi (Exodus 6:24).

  • The father of Samuel the prophet, who is the central figure bearing this name in 1 Samuel.

  • Various Levites mentioned in 1 Chronicles, including temple singers and gatekeepers who served during the reign of King David (1 Chronicles 6:23; 15:23).

  • A high official in the court of King Ahaz of Judah (2 Chronicles 28:7).

Across these contexts, the name functions as an ordinary personal identifier, but its prominence in the priestly and prophetic lines connects it repeatedly with temple service, worship, and faithful leadership.

4. Theological Implications

While a personal name does not by itself establish a doctrine, the meaning "God has created" or "God has acquired" fits the broader message of 1 Samuel. Samuel’s birth was not the result of natural human strength; his mother Hannah was barren, and he was given directly by God in response to prayer. Elkanah's family became the means through which God brought forth a prophet to guide Israel.

The story demonstrates that God works out His redemptive purposes through ordinary, devout families. Long before Samuel was born to minister before the LORD, God had already prepared a household headed by a man whose very name proclaimed God's creative power and sovereign ownership.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:1, Elkanah is introduced with a detailed five-generation genealogy: "the son of Jeroham, the son of Elihu, the son of Tohu, the son of Zuph." Traceable genealogies of this length are uncommon for ordinary individuals in Old Testament narrative. This detailed lineage highlights Elkanah's established place and standing within the community.

The text presents Elkanah as a man of order and devotion amidst national disorder. His household faced real pain—including Hannah's barrenness and domestic tension—yet Elkanah faithfully led his family to worship at Shiloh. The mention of his name at the very beginning of 1 Samuel prepares the reader for God's gracious intervention in the life of his family.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 6:24 – "The sons of Korah were Assir, Elkanah, and Abiasaph. These are the families of the Korahites."

  • 1 Samuel 1:8 – "Then Elkanah her husband said to her, 'Hannah, why do you weep? Why do you not eat? And why is your heart grieved? Am I not better to you than ten sons?'"

  • 1 Chronicles 6:27 – "Eliab his son, Jeroham his son, and Elkanah his son."

  • 1 Chronicles 15:23 – "Berechiah and Elkanah were doorkeepers for the ark."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Elkanah's interactions with his family display genuine personal care alongside religious obligation. When Hannah was deeply grieved by her barrenness and the taunting she endured, Elkanah offered comfort and honored her with a double portion of the sacrifice (1 Samuel 1:5, 8). Furthermore, when Hannah vowed to dedicate Samuel to the LORD, Elkanah supported her vow, saying, "Only let the LORD establish His word" (1 Samuel 1:23). His steady leadership provided a supportive, godly environment where Samuel could be born and raised for divine service.
