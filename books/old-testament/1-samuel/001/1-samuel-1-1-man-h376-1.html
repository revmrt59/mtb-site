MAN - (ʼîysh, Strong's H376)

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'ish basic meaning refers to an individual male human being, a husband, or a person of specific standing. Unlike broader terms for mankind as a collective whole, this word focuses attention on a distinct person or individual.

In 1 Samuel 1:1, the word introduces Elkanah ("a certain man"), grounding the beginning of the book of 1 Samuel in the real life of a specific family. At a time when Israel was spiritually wandering, God initiated a major shift in salvation history through the ordinary domestic life of this individual and his household.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The book of 1 Samuel opens at a major turning point in Israel's history. The nation was coming out of the chaotic, decentralized period of the Judges—a period marked by widespread spiritual decay and moral lawlessness. Israel needed righteous leadership, true worship, and spiritual renewal.

Instead of opening with a grand political speech or a military battle, the sacred text begins in the quiet hill country of Ephraim with a single family. By introducing "a certain man" named Elkanah, the narrative anchors the upcoming spiritual renewal in real historical geography and human lineage. God's major movements in history frequently begin away from the centers of political power, taking root in the everyday lives of ordinary individuals who seek Him.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

targets a specific male individual rather than humanity as a whole. While other Hebrew terms refer to mankind collectively or highlight human mortality, 'ish emphasizes individuality, manhood, marital relationship, or personal role.

Depending on the sentence, the word can mean:

  • An individual adult male human being.

  • A husband within a marriage relationship.

  • A specific person or leader distinguished by character or duty.

  • An individual person in a general or distributive sense ("each one" or "everyone").

In narrative texts, the word often introduces a character whose personal actions or family will drive the unfolding story.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, the word serves several clear grammatical and contextual functions. When used alongside its feminine counterpart, it highlights the distinct role of a husband in relation to his wife. When used in legislative or proverbial passages, it often focuses on personal moral responsibility, emphasizing what an individual person does or thinks.

Grammatically in 1 Samuel 1:1, the noun appears in the singular number and common gender. It identifies Elkanah specifically as one individual man among his people, drawing the reader's eye to his particular story.

4. Theological Implications

The choice to begin 1 Samuel with an ordinary man and his household highlights how God works in redemption. The God of Israel is not distant or concerned only with global empires; He works through specific people, families, and everyday circumstances.

Furthermore, this word highlights individual accountability before God. While national Israel was falling into spiritual ruin under weak religious leadership, individual faithfulness still mattered. God watched the devotion of this family, heard the heartfelt prayer of Elkanah's wife Hannah, and answered by granting them a son, Samuel, who would lead the nation back to God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:1, the opening phrase "Now there was a certain man" establishes a direct contrast with the closing themes of the book of Judges. Judges ends by describing a nation where everyone did whatever seemed right in their own eyes because there was no king.

1 Samuel begins by showing where the true answer to that spiritual crisis would come from. God did not immediately send a king or a grand army; He began with "a certain man" living in Ramathaim Zophim. Though Elkanah lived in a fallen world and faced household rivalries and grief over childlessness, his family faithfully traveled to Shiloh year after year to worship the Lord. The story shows that God's grand plan for Israel was quietly taking shape within the home of an ordinary believer.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:23 — NKJV Span: "Man" — Adam uses the word to distinguish male humanity from female humanity ("Woman"), establishing the foundational God-given relationship between man and woman in creation.

  • Judges 17:6 — NKJV Span: "everyone" — The word is used in a distributive sense to show that every single person was pursuing their own moral standards during the dark era before Israel had a king.

  • 1 Samuel 1:22 — NKJV Span: "husband" — Later in this same story, the word specifies Elkanah's relationship to Hannah as her husband, showing his supportive role in her vow to the Lord.

  • Proverbs 16:2 — NKJV Span: "man" — The word refers to an individual person in a wisdom context, describing how human beings naturally rationalize their own ways while the Lord weighs the motives.

7. Other Relevant Insights

It is worth noting how quiet faithfulness lays the groundwork for national renewal. Elkanah was not a king, a general, or a High Priest. Yet his household provided the setting in which Samuel—Israel's great prophet, priest, and judge—was born and dedicated to God.

This reminds believers that no family or individual is insignificant in God's kingdom. God frequently uses quiet, persistent household obedience as the springboard for extraordinary spiritual deliverance.
