NAME - (shêm, Strong's H8034)

Summary:

The Hebrew word shem refers to a personal name, reputation, family line, or memorial. In 1 Samuel 1:1, the word identifies the individual name of Elkanah at the very beginning of the book. By introducing Elkanah by name and linking him to his ancestral lineage, the text grounds the story of Hannah, Samuel, and the rise of Israel's monarchy in concrete human history rather than ungrounded legend.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israel, a name was far more than an arbitrary label used for identification. A person's name (shem) was closely connected to their personal identity, family line, character, or God's purpose for their life. Preserving one's name within a tribe meant maintaining an enduring inheritance and place among God's covenant people.

The book of 1 Samuel opens during a period of deep spiritual decline in Israel near the end of the era of the Judges. Instead of beginning with grand political events or royal decrees, the narrative opens quietly in the hill country of Ephraim, focusing on an individual man and his household.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word shem can refer to:

  • A personal name or formal designation given to an individual.

  • A person's ancestry, family heritage, or personal identity.

  • A reputation, fame, or honor attached to a person or to God.

  • A memorial or lasting monument that preserves someone's memory.

In 1 Samuel 1:1, the word refers directly to the personal name of a specific man: Elkanah. It serves to identify him as an individual and introduce his family line.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, shem functions in two main settings: human relationships and divine worship. When applied to human beings, a name anchors an individual within a specific family, tribe, and generation. To lose one's name in Israel meant losing one's heritage and place in the community.

When applied to God, His name represents His revealed character, presence, and covenant faithfulness. To honor or call upon the name of the LORD is to worship Him according to who He has declared Himself to be.

Grammatically, in 1 Samuel 1:1, the noun is singular and common in gender. It singles out the specific head of the household around whom the opening narrative revolves.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical practice of naming specific individuals underscores that God works His redemptive plans through real people in actual history. Salvation history does not unfold through abstract ideas, but through named men and women living in specific times and places.

Even during dark historical periods when Israel lacked strong leadership, God was quietly at work within ordinary households. By recording the names of individuals like Elkanah, Hannah, and eventually Samuel, Scripture demonstrates that God knows His people personally and fulfills His promises through ordinary lives.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:1, the word shem introduces Elkanah alongside a detailed list of his ancestors: "and his name was Elkanah the son of Jeroham, the son of Elihu, the son of Tohu, the son of Zuph." This multi-generational lineage establishes Elkanah's standing and deep roots in the land.

This detailed introduction sets up the entire story that follows. Elkanah's household becomes the setting for Hannah's deep sorrow over her barrenness, her earnest prayer at the tabernacle, and God's gracious response in giving her a son. By anchoring the story in a named family, the passage prepares the reader to see how God turns domestic heartbreak into national restoration for Israel.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In ancient Hebrew narrative, introducing a main character with their personal name and an extended genealogy is a sign that a major turning point in Israel's history is about to begin. Tracing Elkanah's ancestral roots links the miraculous birth of Samuel directly back to the ongoing history of God's covenant people.
