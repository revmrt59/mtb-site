TOHU - (Tôchûw, Strong's H8459)

Summary:

Tohu is a masculine proper name used to identify a direct paternal ancestor of Samuel in the opening verse of 1 Samuel. In this passage, the name establishes the historical lineage and family background of Elkanah, showing that God was quietly at work through an ordinary family line to prepare for the birth of Samuel during a dark time in Israel's history.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The name Tohu appears at the very beginning of the First Book of Samuel. The book opens during the historical period of the judges, a time marked by spiritual confusion, moral decay, and leadership failure in Israel. Instead of opening with a great king or a dramatic military victory, the narrative begins with a family genealogy in the hill country of Ephraim.

Tohu is named as the great-grandfather of Elkanah, who would become the father of Samuel. By recording this family line—Zuph, Tohu, Elihu, Jeroham, and Elkanah—the text establishes the setting for one of the most important transition periods in Israel's history. Through this quiet family line, God would raise up Samuel to guide the nation from the period of the judges into the era of the monarchy.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Tohu is a masculine proper noun. It functions strictly as a personal name identifying a specific individual in a Hebrew genealogy.

As a proper name, its primary role in the sentence is simple and direct: it identifies an ancestor. Rather than conveying a complex abstract doctrine by itself, the name serves as a historical marker linking Elkanah and Samuel to their broader family heritage.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In Hebrew record-keeping, personal names in genealogies established a person's legal identity, family heritage, tribal territory, and inheritance rights. By listing Tohu among Elkanah's ancestors, the author gives a grounded historical pedigree to Samuel's family.

This detailed family record shows that Samuel did not appear out of nowhere. His family had deep, established roots in Ramathaim Zophim in the mountains of Ephraim. The inclusion of Tohu emphasizes that biblical faith is rooted in actual history involving real people, families, and places.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of Tohu in this genealogy illustrates how God works in history. Long before Hannah prayed for a child and long before Samuel anointed David as king, God was preserving and guiding a specific family across successive generations.

Tohu was not a famous king, a recorded prophet, or a renowned military hero. Yet his name is permanently recorded in Holy Scripture because he forms part of the chain of generations through which God fulfilled His redemptive plan. This shows that God frequently works through ordinary, unheralded individuals over long periods of time to accomplish His sovereign purpose.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:1, the text introduces Elkanah through a four-generation ancestral chain: "Now there was a certain man of Ramathaim Zophim, of the mountains of Ephraim, and his name was Elkanah the son of Jeroham, the son of Elihu, the son of Tohu, the son of Zuph, an Ephraimite."

The mention of Tohu helps trace the family back to Zuph, the ancestral head of the territory known as the land of Zuph. Tracing the family line back to Tohu and Zuph anchors Elkanah’s lineage in a recognized local heritage. This detailed description contrasts with the spiritual chaos of the period of the judges described in the Book of Judges, showing that even during widespread national decay, God preserved faithful, orderly families in Israel.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • This term is unique to this context and does not appear elsewhere in the biblical corpus.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Genealogies in Scripture serve an important role in distinguishing biblical history from myth. By recording names like Tohu, the Bible emphasizes that God carries out His promises through real historical human timelines.

The quiet mention of Tohu reminds readers that a person does not need to be famous in human eyes to be significant in God's unfolding work. Each generation in a godly family line plays a part in God's larger design, even when their daily lives seem ordinary.
