ZUPH - (Tsûwph, Strong's H6689)

Summary:

The name Zuph refers to an ancestor of Elkanah and Samuel, as well as the district in Ephraim where his family settled. In 1 Samuel 1:1, Zuph identifies the ancestral head of Elkanah's family line, grounding Samuel's priestly and prophetic origin in a concrete, historically verifiable family lineage.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Book of 1 Samuel opens during the closing years of the period of the judges, an era often characterized by spiritual decay and national instability. The narrative introduces Elkanah and his family to set the stage for the birth of Samuel, the leader whom God would use to transition Israel into the era of the monarchy.

To establish Elkanah's place in Israelite society, the passage provides a four-generation genealogy ending with Zuph. The family resided in Ramathaim Zophim in the hill country of Ephraim. The region itself took its name from Zuph and his descendants, showing that the clan had established a recognized heritage in that territory over several generations.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Zuph functions in the Old Testament as a proper masculine noun designating a person and the territory associated with his clan.

The name shares its form with a Hebrew noun meaning "honeycomb" or "flowing honey." As a proper name, it designates a specific individual within the ancestral line of the prophet Samuel.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses Zuph as both a personal name and a regional identifier. In genealogical registers, Zuph represents the patriarchal head of a family branch. Although 1 Samuel 1:1 links his family with the territory of Ephraim, genealogical records in 1 Chronicles clarify that Zuph descended from the Kohathite branch of the tribe of Levi.

The family was granted land in Ephraimite territory while maintaining their Levitical inheritance. Over time, the district where his descendants settled became known as "the land of Zuph," illustrating how prominent families left their mark on the geography of ancient Israel. In parallel genealogical lists, a variation of the name appears as Zophai.

4. Theological Implications

The inclusion of Zuph in the opening sentence of 1 Samuel carries meaningful theological weight:

  • Divine Providence across Generations: God was preparing the way for Samuel long before his birth. By tracing the family back to Zuph, Scripture reminds us that God works quietly through generations of ordinary families to fulfill His redemptive purposes.

  • Historical Grounding: The Bible does not present its heroes as mythical figures appearing out of nowhere. Grounding Samuel's family tree in historical figures like Zuph anchors the account of divine redemption in real history.

  • Unseen Covenant Faithfulness: Even during the chaotic period of the judges, God preserved faithful priestly families who maintained worship and family order.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:1, the text traces Elkanah's lineage: "the son of Jeroham, the son of Elihu, the son of Tohu, the son of Zuph, an Ephrathite." This multi-generational designation gives Elkanah's household clear standing within the community.

The term "an Ephrathite" applies here to the family's geographic residence in the territory of Ephraim, rather than denying their Levitical ancestry. By establishing this historical foundation first, the text contrasts the stable, established lineage of Elkanah with the personal sorrow and barrenness of his wife Hannah in the verses that follow. Before God works the miraculous birth of Samuel, He highlights the real, human history into which Samuel would be born.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Samuel 9:5 — "When they had come to the land of Zuph, Saul said to his servant who was with him, 'Come, let us return, lest my father stop caring for the donkeys and become anxious about us.'"

  • 1 Chronicles 6:26 — "As for Elkanah, the sons of Elkanah were Zophai his son and Nahath his son."

  • 1 Chronicles 6:35 — "the son of Tohu, the son of Zuph, the son of Elkanah..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Meaning of the Name: The root underlying the name Zuph refers to a honeycomb. While ancient Hebrew names often carried descriptive associations, Scripture does not explicitly draw a contrast between the name's meaning and the bitter distress Hannah experienced. Any thematic comparison between the sweetness of honey and Hannah's anguish is a poetic observation rather than a direct claim of the text.

  • Geographic Legacy: The area surrounding Ramathaim Zophim retained Zuph's name long after his death. When Saul later searched for his father's missing donkeys in 1 Samuel 9, he traveled into "the land of Zuph," showing the enduring legacy of this family clan in the region.
