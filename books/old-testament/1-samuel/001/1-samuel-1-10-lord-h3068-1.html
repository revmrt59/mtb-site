LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

Yahweh is the personal proper name of the God of Israel, revealed to His covenant people. In English Bibles, this name is rendered as "Lord" (or "LORD") to distinguish it from general titles for a master or ruler. In 1 Samuel 1:10, this sacred name identifies the living God to whom Hannah turned in her deep sorrow, showing that she brought her personal pain directly to the covenant God who hears and responds to His suffering servants.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The divine name Yahweh is the proper personal name of Israel's God. In the ancient Near East, neighboring nations served various local gods and addressed them by individual proper names. God made Himself known to Israel by His personal name, establishing a personal covenant relationship with them. While general terms like Elohim refer to God in His power as Creator and Ruler of all, Yahweh is the name specifically associated with His personal presence and covenant promises.

1 Samuel opens during a spiritually dark period in Israel's history near the end of the time of the judges. Israel's central sanctuary was at Shiloh, where the tabernacle and the ark of the covenant were located. In this historical setting, Hannah traveled to Shiloh in severe emotional pain because of her childlessness and the persistent provocation of her husband's second wife.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word Yahweh functions as a proper noun, serving as the distinct personal name of God rather than a general title or category. The name is closely connected with the Hebrew verb meaning "to be," highlighting God's self-existence and abiding presence. When God revealed this name to Moses, He expressed His enduring commitment to be present with His people and act on their behalf. The name communicates that God is not a human invention or an abstract force, but the living, constant God who reveals Himself in redemptive history.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, the name Yahweh appears whenever the text emphasizes God's personal relationship with Israel. God uses this name when making covenants, issuing commandments, and extending promises. His people use the name when offering praise, seeking deliverance, and making solemn vows. The prophets speak in the name of Yahweh to declare God's message of judgment or salvation. Calling on the name of Yahweh means turning to the one true God who actively acts in human history, distinguishing Him from the powerless idols of the surrounding nations.

4. Theological Implications

The revelation of God as Yahweh carries several vital theological truths. First, God is personal and accessible to His people, allowing them to know Him by name and address Him directly. Second, God is faithful to His covenant commitments. The name Yahweh reminds Israel that God remembers His promises to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Third, God is sovereign over human circumstances, holding absolute power over life, death, and history. These theological truths are established through God's revealed actions and words throughout Scripture, giving believers confidence that the God who hears prayer is both willing and able to act.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:10, Hannah stands in the tabernacle area at Shiloh in severe grief ("in bitterness of soul"). The passage records that she prayed to the Lord and wept bitterly. Her decision to direct her petition to Yahweh is central to the narrative. She does not resign herself to fate, nor does she turn to pagan idols or superstitious remedies. Instead, she brings her personal heartbreak directly to Yahweh, the God who keeps covenant with Israel.

In the immediate context, Hannah explicitly addresses Him as the "Lord of hosts," acknowledging His authority over all heavenly and earthly powers. Her quiet, desperate prayer before Yahweh stands out against the backdrop of spiritual decay in the priesthood, including Eli's initial misunderstanding of her distress. By invoking the name Yahweh, Hannah grounds her plea in God's character and covenant mercy, trusting that the Lord of the universe pays attention to the grief of an individual worshiper.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Joshua 14:12 – "Now therefore, give me this mountain of which the Lord spoke in that day..."

  • 2 Samuel 22:2 – "And he said: 'The Lord is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer...'"

  • Psalms 25:1 – "To You, O Lord, I lift up my soul."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Grammatical function: In 1 Samuel 1:10, the word functions grammatically as a proper noun (a proper name). It directly names the specific recipient of Hannah's prayer without adding modifying titles in this immediate phrase.

  • Translation conventions: Rendering Yahweh as "Lord" in English Bibles follows a long-standing tradition rooted in Jewish reverence for the sacred name. Readers can recognize that the underlying text contains God's personal covenant name whenever "Lord" appears in this distinct format.

  • Canonical significance: Hannah's prayer to Yahweh marks a major turning point in Israel's history. God's answer to her prayer brings about the birth of Samuel, the prophet who ultimately anoints King David. Hannah's private plea to Yahweh at Shiloh thus serves as the catalyst for God's larger sovereign plan for His kingdom.
