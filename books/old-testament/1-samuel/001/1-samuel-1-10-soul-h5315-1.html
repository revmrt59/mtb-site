SOUL - (nephesh, Strong's H5315)

Summary:

The Hebrew word nephesh fundamental refers to a living being, physical breath, life force, or the inner seat of human emotion, desire, and consciousness. Rather than describing a immaterial soul separate from the body, it often identifies the entire living person or the deep inner life of an individual. In 1 Samuel 1:10, the word describes Hannah's deep emotional anguish as she prays in "bitterness of soul," expressing that her entire inner person is overcome with grief over her childlessness.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The historical setting of 1 Samuel 1 takes place during the period of the judges in Israel, a time marked by spiritual decay and social instability. Elkanah and his family traveled annually to the tabernacle at Shiloh to worship and sacrifice. Hannah, Elkanah's favored wife, suffered from severe distress because she was childless, while her rival Peninnah taunted her continuously.

In the Old Testament world, a woman's childlessness brought not only social stigma but deep personal sorrow. When Hannah went to the sanctuary at Shiloh, her sorrow was so intense that she could not eat. The text highlights her internal condition using the word nephesh to show that her sorrow was not a surface-level sadness, but a grief that touched every part of her living person.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word nephesh can mean a physical creature, a living person, physical life, an appetite or craving, or the inner emotional seat of a human being. The term originates from a primary root associated with breathing or the throat.

Old Testament writers use nephesh in several direct ways:

  • Physical life or breath: The essential life force that keeps a human or animal alive.

  • The whole person: A individual, often translated as "person," "creature," or personal pronouns like "himself" or "myself."

  • The seat of desires and emotions: The inner self that experiences joy, longing, hunger, fear, or deep sorrow.

In 1 Samuel 1:10, the word refers to the seat of human feeling and personal experience, describing Hannah's complete inner self weighed down by deep pain.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament literature, nephesh does not mean a body-less spirit in the Greek philosophical sense. Instead, Scripture views a human as a unified living being. When physical life leaves the body, the nephesh departs; when breath returns, the nephesh returns.

Because nephesh represents the vitality and inner life of a person, biblical writers connect it with strong physical and emotional drives. It is used to express physical hunger and appetite, intense spiritual longing for God, love, fear, and profound grief. The context determines whether the emphasis rests on physical survival, human personal identity, or emotional experience.

4. Theological Implications

Scripture presents human beings as integrated wholes created by God. Pain, grief, and longing are not merely mental ideas; they affect a person's entire physical and spiritual existence.

Hannah’s experience reveals an important truth about prayer: God accepts the genuine, unvarnished sorrow of the human heart. Rather than hiding her pain behind empty religious rituals, Hannah brought her complete inner brokenness before the LORD. Her "bitterness of soul" was not a sinful rebellion against God, but a holy lament laid down at the tabernacle altar. God attends to the deep groanings of the burdened inner person.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:10, the phrase "bitterness of soul" describes Hannah's condition while standing near the doorpost of the tabernacle. Grammatically, the word is a singular common noun. Her bitterness (marat) modifies nephesh, expressing that her grief saturates her entire living identity.

Her grief was so deep that her prayer was silent to human ears; her lips moved, but no voice was heard. This led the priest Eli to mistake her for a drunkard. When confronted, Hannah clarified that she was a woman of a sorrowful spirit who was pouring out her nephesh before the LORD. Her use of the word emphasizes that prayer is the pouring out of one's whole inner life into the presence of God.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's experience illustrates the contrast between destructive bitterness directed against God and honest sorrow poured out to God. When life brings severe grief, the natural human reaction can turn inward toward resentment or outward toward hostility. Hannah took her bitter sorrow and transformed it into intercession, handing her entire life and future over to God's care.
