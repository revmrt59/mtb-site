AFFLICTION - (ʻŏnîy, Strong's H6040)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun 'oni refers to a state of suffering, misery, hardship, or lowliness, often brought on by poverty, oppression, or severe personal anguish. In 1 Samuel 1:11, Hannah uses this word to describe the intense grief and social shame of her childlessness, appealing to God to behold her painful condition and intervene with His mercy.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word 'oni comes from a root associated with being bowed down, humbled, or brought low. In the ancient Near East, personal identity, social security, and legacy were deeply tied to family and offspring. To experience prolonged hardship or social vulnerability was to live under a heavy burden.

In the Old Testament, 'oni describes the state of someone who has been lowered by circumstances, forced to endure misery, poverty, or emotional distress. Rather than describing an abstract concept of bad luck, the word highlights the lived reality of distress—a condition that leaves a person helpless to change their situation by human strength alone.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to deep suffering, affliction, sorrow, poverty, or hardship. When people experience 'oni, they are in a state of distress that exposes their vulnerability.

The term does not merely point to physical pain or financial distress. It carries the sense of being humbled or reduced to a low place. When used in prayer, 'oni brings the sufferer’s helplessness into full view, asking God to pay attention to a situation that human power cannot remedy.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses 'oni across a variety of contexts to portray hardship:

  • National Oppression: The word describes the harsh bondage and suffering of the Israelites in Egypt.

  • Personal Distress: Individuals use 'oni to describe grief, loneliness, rejection, or severe life trial.

  • Social Lowliness: It marks the condition of the poor, marginalized, or destitute who depend on the protection of God and righteous neighbors.

Throughout the Old Testament, when suffering people voice their 'oni, it serves as a cry for God to look, hear, and act. God is repeatedly revealed as One who sees the lowliness of the needy and responds in covenant faithfulness.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of 'oni underscores the compassionate character of God. The Lord is not indifferent to human suffering. When His people are in a state of affliction, He does not turn His face away.

Furthermore, human helplessness in times of 'oni highlights the contrast between human limitation and divine power. When a person reaches the end of their own resources, their affliction becomes the very place where God's mercy is sought. True relief comes not from human self-sufficiency, but from the God who beholds the humble and lifts up the downtrodden.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:11, Hannah stands at the tabernacle in Shiloh, weeping bitterly because she is childless and mocked by her rival. In her prayer, she pleads, "O Lord of hosts, if You will indeed look on the affliction of Your maidservant..."

Here, 'oni describes Hannah's deep emotional torment and social disgrace. By calling her trial an "affliction," Hannah does not hide her pain or pretend to be strong. She presents her brokenness directly to God. The word functions in her prayer as a plea for divine intervention—she asks the Lord to look upon her misery, remember her, and grant her a son. The common singular form presents her specific personal distress as a single, overwhelming reality before the Lord.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 16:11 – "And the Angel of the Lord said to her: 'Behold, you are with child, and you shall bear a son. You shall call his name Ishmael, because the Lord has heard your affliction.'"

  • Genesis 29:32 – "So Leah conceived and bore a son, and she called his name Reuben; for she said, 'The Lord has surely looked on my affliction. Now therefore my husband will love me.'"

  • Exodus 3:17 – "and I have said I will bring you up out of the affliction of Egypt to the land of the Canaanites..."

  • 2 Samuel 16:12 – "It may be that the Lord will look on my affliction, and that the Lord will repay me with good for his cursing this day."

  • Psalms 9:13 – "Have mercy on me, O Lord! Consider my trouble from those who hate me, You who lift me up from the gates of death..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's request in 1 Samuel 1:11 closely parallels Leah's words in Genesis 29:32 and Hagar's experience in Genesis 16:11. In each case, a woman suffering quiet rejection or despair appeals to God, knowing He sees what others overlook.

Hannah's use of 'oni shows that prayer does not require masking deep sorrow. Bringing one's affliction to God is an act of faith, acknowledging that while human help fails, God remains the compassionate Lord who sees and remembers His servants.
