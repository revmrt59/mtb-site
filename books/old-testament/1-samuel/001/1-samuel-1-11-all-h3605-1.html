ALL - (kôl, Strong's H3605)

Summary:

The word kol is the primary Hebrew word used to express totality, completeness, or an entire sum without exception. Depending on how it is paired with other words, it can describe every unit in a group, a whole region, or an unbroken duration of time. In 1 Samuel 1:11, Hannah uses this word in her vow to yield her promised son to the LORD for "all" the days of his life. In this passage, the word establishes a complete, lifelong dedication, marking Samuel out for continuous service to God from infancy until death.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the opening chapter of 1 Samuel, Israel was experiencing a time of spiritual decline under the aging priest Eli and his corrupt sons. Hannah, one of the two wives of Elkanah, suffered deep emotional pain due to her childlessness and the cruel provocation of her rival, Peninnah. Year after year, Hannah went up to the sanctuary at Shiloh, bringing her sorrow before God.

During one of these visits, Hannah prayed in deep anguish and made a solemn vow to God. In ancient Israel, vows were voluntary promises made to God, often pledging a specific gift or service in response to answered prayer. Standard priestly service for Levites normally began around age twenty-five or thirty and ended at age fifty. Hannah’s vow, however, went far beyond ordinary Levite duty. She pledged that if God would grant her a male child, she would return him to the LORD for the entire span of his earthly existence.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word kol refers to the whole sum, entire quantity, or total extent of whatever noun it modifies. It can mean "all," "whole," "every," or "entirety."

When combined with a plural noun representing time, such as "days," it expresses an exhaustive duration—every single day without interruption or limit. Grammatically, the term appears here as a singular noun of common gender linked to the phrase "the days of his life." Rather than indicating a limited term of service, the word signifies a total, unreserved commitment that leaves no part of the child's life outside God's direct ownership.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses kol in a wide variety of settings to indicate comprehensive scope:

  • Total extent of creation or humanity, pointing to God's universal lordship over everything He made.

  • Wholehearted devotion, such as loving God with "all" one's heart and soul.

  • Entire spans of time, marking uninterrupted continuity over days, years, or generations.

When used of time, the word does not merely mean a long time; it means the entire duration available. It excludes the possibility of partial fulfillment or seasonal participation.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah’s vow highlights the principle that life and children are gifts from God, and that the proper response to divine favor is entire surrender. By pledging her son for kol the days of his life, Hannah acknowledged that the child she requested did not belong to her for personal ambition or security.

This lifelong dedication carried great significance for Israel’s history. Samuel was set apart to serve as a prophet, priest, and judge, bridging the gap between the chaotic era of the Judges and the establishment of the kingdom under Saul and David. Because his consecration spanned his whole life, Samuel remained a faithful, uncompromised voice for God through every stage of his ministry. The total scope of his devotion stands as a testimony to God's claim on the entire life of His servants.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:11, kol functions as a crucial term in Hannah’s prayer. She asks God to look on her affliction, remember her, and give her a male child. In return, she promises: "then I will give him to the LORD all the days of his life, and no razor shall come upon his head."

The presence of kol before "the days of his life" works alongside the mention of the razor to establish a lifelong Nazirite framework. Normally, Nazirite vows were taken voluntarily for a temporary, specified period. Hannah’s vow made Samuel's set-apart status permanent from his earliest childhood after weaning until his death. The word stresses that there would be no phase of Samuel's life reserved for secular pursuits, political self-interest, or private life apart from God's house and ministry.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The complete lifelong commitment signified by kol in Hannah's vow contrasts sharply with the spiritual failure surrounding her at Shiloh. The sons of Eli viewed their priestly service as a temporary means of personal gain and self-indulgence. In contrast, Samuel was consecrated before he was even conceived to belong wholly to the LORD for his entire life. This lifelong devotion resulted in a ministry characterized by persistent integrity, intercession, and obedience from childhood to old age.
