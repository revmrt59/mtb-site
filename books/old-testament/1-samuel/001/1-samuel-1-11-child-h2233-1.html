CHILD - (zeraʻ, Strong's H2233)

Summary:

The Hebrew word zera' fundamentally means "seed," referring to agricultural seed, physical offspring, or future generations. In 1 Samuel 1:11, Hannah uses this word within a phrase translated "a male child," asking God to grant her a son whom she promises to dedicate back to the Lord for lifelong service.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israel, farming and family were deeply connected. The word zera' originates in the agricultural world of sowing seed in a field to harvest grain and sustain life. Because seed represents the source of future life, the biblical writers naturally used the same word to describe human offspring, family lines, and future generations.

In Israelite society, having children was not merely a personal desire; it was essential for preserving a family line, passing down inherited land, and carrying forward God's covenant promises. Childlessness was viewed as a sorrowful trial and often brought social hardship. In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah faces severe emotional pain due to her barrenness. Surrounded by misunderstanding and provocation, she travels to the tabernacle at Shiloh to pour out her heart to God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Zera' is a noun that literally means "seed." Depending on the context, the word can refer to:

  • Agricultural seed planted in the soil or grain gathered at harvest.

  • Human biological offspring, including a son, a daughter, or children generally.

  • Future posterity, lineage, or descendants extending across many generations.

In 1 Samuel 1:11, zera' is used for human offspring. Combined with the word for men, the expression rendered "a male child" literally refers to male seed or male offspring.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

across the Old Testament, zera' moves fluidly between the physical realm of farming and the personal realm of family and covenant life. Just as a single seed planted in the ground produces a crop, a child represents the continuation of a family line.

The word can point to an immediate individual child, a group of immediate children, or a vast line of descendants running through centuries. Context always determines whether the focus is on agricultural grain, an individual son or daughter, or an entire nation's future offspring. In Hannah's prayer, the focus is immediate and specific: she asks God to grant her a single son.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of zera' carries rich theological truth. Scripture makes clear that fruitfulness—whether in the field or in the home—is ultimately a gift from God. God is the Creator who gives seed to the sower and opens the womb to bring forth children.

Furthermore, zera' connects directly to God's covenant history. God made promises to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob concerning their seed or descendants. By using this term, Hannah grounds her petition in the ultimate source of life. Her request acknowledges that children are not a natural right to be demanded, but a gracious gift to be received from God's hand. When God grants her request, Hannah recognizes His sovereignty by giving the child back to the Lord for His holy purposes.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:11, Hannah stands before the tabernacle in deep distress. She makes a solemn vow to the Lord, saying, "If You will indeed look on the affliction of Your maidservant and remember me, and not forget Your maidservant, but will give Your maidservant a male child, then I will give him to the LORD all the days of his life."

The word zera' appears here in a singular form, modified by the word for men to express her specific request for a male child. Hannah's prayer is not driven by selfish ambition or pride. While she seeks relief from her personal grief and social reproach, her vow demonstrates a selfless devotion. She does not ask for a son merely to keep him for her own comfort; she promises that if God grants her male offspring, he will be consecrated entirely to God's service as a lifelong Nazirite. God answers her prayer, and the son born to her—Samuel—becomes one of Israel's greatest prophets and leaders.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's prayer highlights how God transforms personal heartbreak into a blessing for many. Hannah requested a single male child to fulfill her longing for a son. Yet, because she dedicated Samuel back to the Lord, God used that child to bring spiritual renewal to the entire nation of Israel. Samuel anointed David as king, establishing a royal line that ultimately pointed to Jesus Christ. Hannah asked for a seed, and God brought forth a harvest of salvation for His people.
