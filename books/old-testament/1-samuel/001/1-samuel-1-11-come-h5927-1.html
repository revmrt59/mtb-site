COME - (ʻâlâh, Strong's H5927)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'alah fundamentally means to ascend, go up, rise, or come upon a surface. In 1 Samuel 1:11, it describes a razor coming up onto or touching the head of Hannah's promised son. By declaring that no razor would come upon his head, Hannah dedicated her child Samuel to lifelong consecration to the LORD, setting him apart for divine service from birth.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The book of 1 Samuel opens during a dark period in Israel's history. The priesthood at Shiloh was corrupt, the people lacked central leadership, and spiritual darkness covered the land. In the middle of this widespread distress was a deeply personal tragedy: Hannah was childless and severely distressed by her rival's taunts.

While praying at the tabernacle in Shiloh, Hannah poured out her soul before God. She made a solemn vow promising that if God granted her a male child, she would return him to the LORD for his entire life. The word 'alah appears in the closing line of her pledge to describe the razor that must never touch or come up onto the child's head.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

'Alah is a common Hebrew action word with a basic physical meaning of upward movement. The word can mean:

  • To go up or ascend an incline, hill, or mountain.

  • To rise up, such as smoke rising or a mist ascending from the ground.

  • To come up onto a surface, place, or person.

In 1 Samuel 1:11, the verb is used in this last sense. It refers to an object—a razor—rising up onto or passing over a person's head. The grammatical form used in this sentence is masculine, singular, and imperfect, describing an action that must not take place throughout the child's life.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, 'alah describes physical elevation, geographical movement, and ritual action. People go up to Jerusalem or ascend a holy mountain to worship. Sacrifices are said to go up on the altar as a pleasing aroma to God. In military settings, armies go up to battle.

When applied to physical items or tools, 'alah indicates bringing something onto a specific location. In the context of vows, the non-arrival of the razor means complete abstinence from cutting the hair. The spatial imagery of the tool coming up onto the head highlights physical contact. Refusing to let a razor come up onto the head keeps the individual in an untouched, holy state.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's vow echoes the law of the Nazirite detailed in the Book of Numbers. Normally, a Nazirite vow was voluntary and temporary, taken for a specific period of self-dedication. During that period, no razor was permitted to touch the person's head as an external sign of complete separation to God.

Hannah extended this commitment to an extraordinary degree. She dedicated her child before he was even conceived, declaring that he would belong to the LORD for his entire life. The uncut hair signified that the child was not under normal earthly obligations but was uniquely claimed by God. The theological focus rests on God's sovereignty: the life that God mercifully grants in response to prayer belongs entirely to Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:11, 'alah serves as the concrete condition of Hannah's vow. Her prayer moves from personal grief to radical surrender. She does not merely ask for a son to resolve her social embarrassment or fulfill her maternal desire. Instead, she offers the child back to God for tabernacle service.

The expression "no razor shall come upon his head" anchors her promise in visible reality. Samuel's outward appearance would forever announce his set-apart status. As Samuel grew, every visible hair served as a quiet testament to his mother's answered prayer and his holy calling. This setting apart prepared Samuel to become Israel's faithful judge, prophet, and priest during a period of national transition.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:6 – "but a mist went up from the earth and watered the whole face of the ground."

  • Exodus 24:18 – "So Moses went up into the mountain into the midst of the cloud."

  • Psalms 68:18 – "You have ascended on high, You have led captivity captive."

  • Jeremiah 16:15 – "but, 'The LORD lives who brought up the children of Israel from the land of the north...'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's prayer demonstrates how deeply personal pain can become the soil for profound spiritual dedication. In her anguish, she did not bargain selfishly with God. Instead, she recognized that any blessing received from the LORD remains His possession.

Her choice to dedicate Samuel completely shows remarkable trust. She was willing to give up the daily joy of raising her son so that he could serve God's higher purpose for the nation. Samuel's life stands as a reminder that what we fully surrender to God can produce far-reaching fruit for His kingdom.
