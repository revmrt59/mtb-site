HEAD - (rôʼsh, Strong's H7218)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun ro'sh basic meaning is "head," referring to the physical head of a person or animal, as well as the top, summit, chief, or beginning of something. In 1 Samuel 1:11, Hannah vows that if God grants her a male child, "no razor shall come upon his head." Here, ro'sh refers to the physical head of her prayed-for child, signaling an uncut mark of lifelong devotion and consecration to God as a Nazirite.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israelite thought and everyday language, ro'sh is a fundamental word used across many settings. Physically, it refers to the human or animal head. Because the head is the highest part of the body, the word naturally expanded to describe physical summits like mountain tops, spatial starting points like the sources of rivers, and social or political positions like chief leaders of tribes or clans.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Ro'sh refers primarily to the physical head of a person or animal. From this foundational sense, the word is used in several related ways depending on context:

  • The physical head of a human being or animal.

  • The top or summit of a mountain, tower, or structure.

  • The beginning, start, or chief division of time or space.

  • A leader, chief, or head of a family, tribe, or group of people.

In 1 Samuel 1:11, the word is used in its simple physical sense. It refers directly to the top of the child's head, which was to remain untouched by a razor as an outward mark of dedication to God.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, ro'sh carries diverse nuances based on its setting:

  • Anatomical: It frequently denotes the human head, where hair grows, where oil is poured during anointing, or where a blessing is pronounced.

  • Geographical and Spatial: It describes the top of a mountain or hill, or the headwaters where a river divides.

  • Leadership and Structure: It refers to leaders, elders, or heads of families who guide and represent their communities.

  • Figurative and Ceremonial: The head is often the focal point for ritual actions, such as laying hands on a sacrifice or refraining from cutting hair during a special vow.

4. Theological Implications

The mention of the head in 1 Samuel 1:11 connects directly to Israel's biblical traditions of consecration. In the Law of Moses (Numbers 6), a Nazirite took a special vow of devotion to the Lord, one key mark of which was letting the hair on the head grow uncut. Hannah's vow that "no razor shall come upon his head" reflects this Nazirite principle, extended to the child's entire life.

By dedicating her unborn son's head—and thus his whole physical person—to God, Hannah acknowledged that the child she prayed for belonged entirely to the Lord. The uncut hair on his head served as a visible reminder that Samuel was set apart for divine service from birth.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:11, Hannah prays in deep anguish at the tabernacle in Shiloh. Barren and provoked by her rival, she brings her sorrow directly to God. Her vow contains a striking devotion: she asks God for a son, yet promises to give him right back to the Lord.

The clause "no razor shall come upon his head" specifies how this total dedication will be visibly realized. The head, as the most visible part of a person, carries the outward sign of his inner consecration. Uncut hair signaled that Samuel was not his own, nor did he belong to his family for ordinary ambitions; his whole life was set apart for God's work during a spiritually dark period in Israel's history.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In ancient Israelite culture, leaving the hair uncut on one's head was an unmistakable public testimony. While most men groomed their hair according to standard cultural customs, a person set apart by a Nazirite vow carried a distinct appearance that everyone recognized. In Samuel's life, this continuous condition of his head stood as a lifelong testimony to Hannah's answered prayer and God's claim over Samuel's service as prophet and leader.
