LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

The word is the personal proper name of the God of Israel, revealing Him as the eternal, self-existent God who remains faithful to His word. In 1 Samuel 1:11, Hannah invokes this personal name in her prayer for a son, anchoring her petition in the reality and authority of the God who sees her suffering and hears her prayer.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israel, a personal name was far more than a convenient label; it revealed identity, character, and authority. The name represented by "Lord" in English translations is God’s unique personal name, revealed to His people as a pledge of His presence and faithfulness.

The events of 1 Samuel begin during a dark chapter in Israel's history near the close of the period of the judges. Spiritual leadership was corrupt, and society was fragmented. In the midst of this national decline, Hannah faced intense personal heartache. She was childless in a society where childlessness brought social disgrace, and she endured regular provocation from her husband's second wife. When Hannah traveled to the sanctuary at Shiloh, she brought her grief directly to the God of Israel, calling upon Him by His personal name.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word is a proper noun that serves as the personal name of the God of Israel. It is closely connected to the Hebrew verb meaning "to be" or "to exist." When God revealed this name to Moses at the burning bush, He explained it with the statement "I AM WHO I AM," pointing to His eternal, uncaused existence.

Unlike general terms for deity that could refer to God or false idols, this word identifies the unique, living God who created the heavens and earth and revealed Himself to Israel. It distinguishes Him from every manufactured god. In English Bibles such as the NKJV, this name is represented by "LORD" or "Lord."

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, the name appears whenever God reveals Himself to humanity, establishes promises, or intervenes in history.

In narrative passages, the name highlights God's personal involvement with His people. It marks His actions in delivering Israel from bondage, guiding them through the wilderness, and establishing them in the promised land. In prayer and worship, the name is used by worshippers calling upon God in times of joy, sorrow, confession, or thanksgiving. In the prophetic books, the name guarantees that God's declarations of judgment and promises of restoration will surely come to pass because His name stands behind His word.

4. Theological Implications

The revelation of this personal name shows that God desires to be known by His people. He is not an impersonal force or an unreachable deity, but a personal God who hears prayer and interacts with human beings.

The name highlights God's self-existence and immutability. God does not depend on creation for His existence, nor does He change over time. Because He remains the same throughout all generations, His people can depend on His promises. Calling upon this name acknowledges His authority over history, creation, and human life, assuring believers that God is both capable and willing to act on behalf of those who seek Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:11, Hannah stands before the tabernacle at Shiloh in deep distress. She addresses God directly as "Lord of hosts." By using this personal name, she grounds her appeal in the character of the God who keeps His word to the humble and needy.

Hannah combines the divine name with "hosts," acknowledging that the personal God who knows her grief is also the supreme Commander of heaven's armies. She asks God to look on her affliction, remember her, and grant her a male child. In return, she vows to dedicate the child back to the Lord for his entire life. Hannah uses the divine name multiple times in her vow, demonstrating that her petition is rooted in trust in God's power to overcome her barrenness and bring life out of grief.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4: "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the Lord God made the earth and the heavens." Here the name appears alongside God as Creator, highlighting His personal care in forming the created order.

  • Exodus 13:5: "And it shall be, when the Lord brings you into the land of the Canaanites..." The name is linked to God's promise to bring His people into the land He swore to give them.

  • Joshua 14:12: "Now therefore, give me this mountain of which the Lord spoke in that day..." Caleb appeals to what God promised years earlier, trusting in God's faithful character to grant strength and victory.

  • Psalms 25:1: "To You, O Lord, I lift up my soul." David calls upon God in personal prayer, placing his full trust and life into God's hands.

  • Proverbs 16:6: "In mercy and truth atonement is provided for iniquity; and by the fear of the Lord one departs from evil." Reverence for God's personal name and authority leads a person away from evil into wisdom.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's address in 1 Samuel 1:11 is the first recorded instance in Scripture where a worshipper uses the exact title "Lord of hosts" in prayer. This title became prominent later in Israel's history, particularly during periods of national crisis and in the writings of the prophets.

This passage demonstrates that God pays attention to individual prayers offered in sincerity. While Israel as a nation was suffering under poor leadership, God heard the silent prayer of a grieving woman in Shiloh. The birth of Samuel in response to this prayer led to the renewal of Israel's spiritual life and the establishment of the monarchy, showing how God uses answered prayer to accomplish His broader purposes.
