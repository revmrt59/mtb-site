MALE - (ʼĕnôwsh, Strong's H582)

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'enosh basic lexical meaning refers to a human being, a mortal man, or mankind collectively. In 1 Samuel 1:11, the plural masculine form of this word combines with the word for "seed" or "offspring" in the phrase translated "male child" in the NKJV. In this passage, Hannah uses the expression to ask God specifically for a male son whom she promises to dedicate entirely to the service of the LORD.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The book of 1 Samuel opens during a dark period in Israel's history near the end of the era of the judges. God's people suffered under moral failure and spiritual compromise. Leadership was weak, and the priesthood at the sanctuary in Shiloh had become corrupt. Against this troubled historical backdrop, the story turns to an ordinary family suffering deep personal grief.

Elkanah had two wives, Peninnah and Hannah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah was childless because the LORD had closed her womb. Year after year, Peninnah provoked Hannah bitterly, causing her severe distress. During an annual pilgrimage to worship and sacrifice at Shiloh, Hannah went to the sanctuary weeping and in deep anguish. There she poured out her heart to God, making a vow that if He answered her prayer with a son, she would give that child back to the LORD for his entire life.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word 'enosh can refer to individual human beings, mankind in general, or groups of men. It frequently carries the sense of human vulnerability, weakness, or mortality when set in contrast to the infinite power and majesty of God.

Depending on the context, the word can mean:

  • A human being or mortal man.

  • Mankind or human society as a whole.

  • Men or male individuals when specifying human beings.

In 1 Samuel 1:11, the masculine plural form is joined to the Hebrew word for "seed" or "offspring" (zera'). Together, the phrase literally describes human offspring specified as male, which the NKJV translates as a "male child."

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, 'enosh emphasizes human frailty and mortality. When poetically contrasted with God, the term highlights how small and temporary human beings are compared to their Eternal Creator.

The word appears in narrative descriptions of groups of men, wisdom reflections on human vulnerability, and prophetic statements about humanity. In poetic texts like the Psalms and Job, it often asks how God could concern Himself with such frail human creatures.

In 1 Samuel 1:11, the word is grammatically masculine and plural. It serves a specific descriptive purpose within Hannah's vow. Rather than asking generally for relief from her barrenness, she specifically requests a male child who can enter into dedicated, lifelong priestly or tabernacle service.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah’s prayer touches on major biblical truths regarding God's sovereignty over human life and the role of selfless devotion in worship.

First, the passage shows that human life and fertility come from God alone. Hannah does not look to human remedies or resign herself to despair; she takes her grief directly to the LORD of hosts, acknowledging Him as the master of life and human destiny.

Second, Hannah's request for a male child is free from personal pride or worldly ambition. In ancient Israelite culture, having a son restored social standing and preserved family lineage. Yet Hannah vows to hand the requested son back to God as a lifelong Nazirite. Her prayer demonstrates that God's gifts are best enjoyed when held with open hands and yielded back to His kingdom purposes.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:11, Hannah prays: "O LORD of hosts, if You will indeed look on the affliction of Your maidservant and remember me, and not forget Your maidservant, but will give Your maidservant a male child, then I will give him to the LORD all the days of his life, and no razor shall come upon his head."

The phrase translated "male child" uses 'enosh to specify the kind of child she desires. Under Old Testament law, male children held specific roles regarding inheritance, covenant lineage, and formal service at the sanctuary. By asking for a male child, Hannah envisions a son who can perform active service for God throughout his life.

The context contrasts Hannah’s sincere faith with the spiritual blindness around her. Eli the priest mistook her silent, tearful praying for drunkenness, revealing how rare genuine spiritual earnestness had become at Shiloh. While Eli's own sons dishonored the sanctuary, Hannah promised to surrender her requested son to God from infancy. God answered her prayer by giving her Samuel, who would grow up to shepherd Israel spiritually during a critical transition in national history.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah’s request for a male child led directly to the birth of Samuel, whose name means "Heard by God." Samuel grew to become Israel's last major judge, a faithful prophet, and the leader who anointed both King Saul and King David.

Hannah's prayer in 1 Samuel 1 and her song of thanksgiving in chapter 2 set a major theological pattern for the rest of 1 and 2 Samuel. God exalts the humble, brings down the proud, restores the barren, and accomplishes His redemptive purposes through those who trust Him completely.
