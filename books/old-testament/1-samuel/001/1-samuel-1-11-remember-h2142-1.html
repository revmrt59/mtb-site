REMEMBER - (zâkar, Strong's H2142)

Summary:

The word zakar basic meaning is to recall, bring to mind, or pay attention to someone or something, often resulting in purposeful action. When used of God, it does not mean He is retrieving a forgotten thought, but rather that He is turning His active, merciful attention toward a person or promise. In 1 Samuel 1:11, Hannah asks God to "remember" her in her painful childlessness, pleading for Him not merely to keep her in mind, but to actively step in and give her a son.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient OT world, remembering was far more than an internal mental exercise. To remember someone meant to fix one's attention upon them with the intention to act on their behalf. The word zakar appears in moments of intense personal distress, covenant renewal, and solemn prayer.

In the opening chapter of 1 Samuel, Israel was experiencing a dark season of spiritual weakness under the priesthood at Shiloh. Hannah lived with the ongoing sorrow of barrenness and the constant taunts of her rival, Peninnah. When Hannah traveled to the tabernacle, her heart was filled with grief. In her prayer, asking God to "remember" her comes from a place of deep sorrow, relying on the God who sees the humble and responds to their petitions.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word zakar can refer to bringing something to mind, mentioning a name, keeping a command in view, or giving active attention to a request.

When human beings are the subject, the word often describes recalling past events, keeping God's commandments, or giving praise by speaking His name in worship. When God is the subject, the word does not imply that He had previously forgotten. God is all-knowing and never loses track of His people. Instead, divine remembering describes the moment God directs His care and power toward a specific person or situation to bring about help, fulfillment, or mercy. In 1 Samuel 1:11, the word expresses a heart crying out for God's gracious intervention.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Scripture, zakar functions in several distinct settings depending on who is acting:

  • Divine Action in Mercy: When God remembers, He acts faithfully toward His promises or shows pity to those who suffer.

  • Human Worship and Remembrance: People remember God by recounting His past mighty deeds, observing His laws, or calling upon His name in prayer.

  • Public or Official Mention: The word can mean formally speaking of something or bringing an issue into public view.

The word ties memory directly to action. God's act of remembering leads to deliverance, relief, or the keeping of a promise, while human remembering leads to obedience, gratitude, and persistent prayer.

4. Theological Implications

In biblical teaching, God's act of remembering highlights His unchanging faithfulness. Because God knows all things perfectly, His remembering is relational and practical rather than mental. When Scripture speaks of God remembering His people, it marks the point where divine mercy breaks into human helplessness.

Hannah's plea in 1 Samuel 1:11 reveals that believers can take their deepest grief directly to God. Her prayer rests on the truth that God is attentive to the lowly. She does not offer a self-centered prayer; rather, she asks God to remember her so that she might surrender the child back to His service. Her request shows that God's intervention in response to prayer often serves His larger purpose in the world.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:11, Hannah stands at the tabernacle praying in soul-crushing anguish. She addresses God as the "LORD of hosts" and asks Him to look on her affliction, "remember" her, and not forget her. In this verse, the verb appears in a singular, masculine, perfect form directed toward God within her vow.

Here, "remember" works together with "look on" and "not forget" to express the intensity of her cry. Hannah is not worried that God has forgotten her existence. Instead, she is asking Him to turn His favor toward her personal suffering. Her request is clear and specific: she asks for a male child and vows that he will be dedicated to the LORD for his entire life. By using this word, Hannah grounds her petition in God's compassionate character.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 8:1 — "Then God remembered Noah, and every living thing, and all the animals that were with him in the ark."

  • Exodus 2:24 — "So God heard their groaning, and God remembered His covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob."

  • Numbers 10:9 — "...then you will be remembered before the LORD your God, and you will be saved from your enemies."

  • Psalms 8:4 — "What is man that You are mindful of him, and the son of man that You visit him?"

  • Psalms 103:14 — "For He knows our frame; He remembers that we are dust."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's prayer is the first time in Scripture that God is called the "LORD of hosts" (Yahweh Tsebaoth), emphasizing His supreme authority over all creation and heavenly powers. Combining this powerful title with her plea for Him to "remember" her creates a striking contrast between her personal helplessness and God's absolute sovereignty.

Furthermore, when God answers her prayer and she gives birth to a son, she names him Samuel, a name that echoes her experience of God hearing her prayer. God's act of remembering Hannah had impact far beyond her individual home. By answering her prayer, God brought forth Samuel, who grew up to serve as a faithful prophet, priest, and judge during a critical turning point in Israel's history.
