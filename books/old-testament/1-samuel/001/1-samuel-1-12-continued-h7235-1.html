CONTINUED - (râbâh, Strong's H7235)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb rabah fundamentally means to multiply, increase, or perform an action abundantly. When paired with another verb describing an activity, it denotes doing that action at great length or repeatedly. In 1 Samuel 1:12, the word describes Hannah lingering long in prayer before the LORD, expressing the great depth and extended duration of her silent plea for a child.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

1 Samuel 1 opens during a period of spiritual decline in Israel, where personal sorrow and national upheaval meet. Hannah, the wife of Elkanah, suffered from barrenness and bitter provocation from her rival Peninnah. During the family's annual pilgrimage to the sanctuary at Shiloh, Hannah brought her deep sorrow directly to God.

As she stood near the doorpost of the tabernacle, she prayed silently in her heart, moving her lips without making an audible voice. The narrative records that she "continued" praying before the LORD while Eli the priest observed her mouth. The Hebrew word rendered "continued" is rabah, a common verb used throughout the Old Testament to convey multiplication and increase. In this setting, the word highlights the long, sustained period Hannah spent in prayer despite her intense agony.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of rabah is to be or become many, to increase, or to grow in number or extent. In causative constructions, it means to make many, to multiply, or to make abundant.

When rabah is used alongside another action, it functions adverbially to indicate doing something abundantly, frequently, or for a prolonged period. In 1 Samuel 1:12, where it accompanies the verb for prayer, it means "to multiply prayer" or "to make much prayer." Thus, English translations render it as "continued praying," capturing the idea of an extended, unhurried outpouring of the soul before God.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, rabah appears in several distinct settings:

  • Creation and Covenant Blessing: God commands living creatures and humanity to "multiply" and fill the earth. God also uses the word when promising Abraham and his descendants that their offspring, days, and blessings will be greatly multiplied.

  • Physical and Material Increase: The word describes the growth of livestock, silver, gold, grain, or national populations.

  • Abundance of Action or Speech: When applied to human speech or action, rabah describes doing something extensively or repeatedly. It can refer to multiplying words, multiplying transgressions, or, as in Hannah's case, multiplying petitions in prayer.

Grammatically, in 1 Samuel 1:12, the verb form is feminine singular in the perfect, agreeing with Hannah as the subject and indicating her sustained, completed effort in lingering before God.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah’s prolonged prayer demonstrates the nature of honest, persistent crying out to God in moments of severe affliction. Scripture does not view extended prayer as a tool to manipulate God or overcome His reluctance, but as a genuine pouring out of a heavy heart.

Hannah did not recite a short, ritualistic formula. Instead, her abundance of grief found its outlet in an abundance of prayer. God receives those who linger in His presence, inviting His people to bring their deepest anxieties to Him without hesitation or haste.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

The use of rabah in 1 Samuel 1:12 creates a striking narrative contrast between human misunderstanding and divine attentiveness. Hannah "multiplied" her prayer, yet she did so in silence, her lips moving while her voice remained unheard.

Eli watched her mouth and assumed she was drunk because she lingered so long at the doorpost moving her lips without sound. Eli misjudged the prolonged duration of her prayer as a sign of intoxication, when in truth it was the mark of a deeply troubled spirit seeking the LORD. Hannah clarified that she was not drunk, but had been pouring out her soul out of the abundance of her grief and provocation. The word rabah emphasizes that her time in God's presence was not brief or superficial, but thorough and unrestrained.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Deuteronomy 11:21 — "that your days and the days of your children may be multiplied in the land of which the LORD swore to your fathers to give them, like the days of the heavens above the earth."

  • Proverbs 4:10 — "Hear, my son, and receive my sayings, and the years of your life will be many."

  • Ezekiel 36:37 — "Thus says the Lord GOD: 'I will also let the house of Israel inquire of Me to do this for them: I will increase their men like a flock.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The choice of rabah to describe prayer illuminates how the Old Testament views silent intercession. Prayer is not measured merely by spoken words or volume, but by the orientation of the heart before the LORD.

Hannah's "multiplying" of prayer consisted of silent heart-cries. This shows that true biblical prayer can be deeply inward and quiet, yet immense in its burden and persistence. Her quiet persistence was eventually answered when God remembered her, giving birth to Samuel, who would become one of Israel's greatest prophets and judges.
