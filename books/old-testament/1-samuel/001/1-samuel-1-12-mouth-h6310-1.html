MOUTH - (peh, Strong's H6310)

Summary:

The Hebrew word peh refers primarily to the physical mouth, as well as the speech, commands, or testimony produced by it. In 1 Samuel 1:12, the word describes Hannah's physical mouth as she prays silently in the sanctuary at Shiloh. While Eli the priest watches the external movement of her mouth and mistakenly assumes she is drunk, God hears the silent cry of her heart, highlighting the contrast between human observation and divine discernment.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the Old Testament, physical actions and organs often carry deep spiritual meaning. The mouth is the organ of speech, breath, eating, and vocal devotion. In the opening chapter of 1 Samuel, Israel is in a period of spiritual decline under the leadership of Eli the priest at Shiloh. Into this setting comes Hannah, a woman burdened by childlessness and bitter grief.

Hannah visits the tabernacle to pour out her heart before the Lord. Unlike traditional public prayers that were spoken aloud, Hannah prays silently. Her prayer occurs in a sanctuary where worship has become formal and spiritually sluggish, making her deeply personal, silent devotion stand out as unusual to the high priest observing her.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of peh is the physical mouth of a human being or animal. Because the mouth is the instrument of speech, the word frequently extends to mean spoken words, mouth-to-mouth communication, a spoken command, or legal testimony. In architectural or geographical contexts, it can also refer to an opening, such as the entrance of a cave or the mouth of a well.

In 1 Samuel 1:12, the word appears in its singular, common-gender noun form and refers literally to Hannah's physical mouth. The text emphasizes that while Eli kept his eyes fixed on her mouth, no audible sound was coming forth.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, peh functions in several related ways depending on the context:

  • Physical Organ: It describes the literal mouth used for eating, drinking, kissing, or speaking.

  • Speech and Utterance: The word frequently stands for the words a person utters, whether praises, complaints, or prayers.

  • Command or Authority: When a leader or king speaks, his peh represents his binding decree or royal instruction.

  • Legal Testimony: In judicial contexts, the testimony given by witnesses is described as coming from their mouth.

  • Divine Revelation: When associated with God, the term points to the unshakeable word and promises that proceed directly from Him.

4. Theological Implications

The focus on Hannah's mouth in 1 Samuel 1 introduces an important biblical truth regarding prayer and divine heart-knowledge. External religious activity often focuses on what can be seen and heard—the posture of the body, the movement of the lips, or the sound of the voice. However, God reads the silent longings of the inner person.

Hannah’s silent prayer demonstrates that true prayer does not depend on loud vocalization or ceremonial eloquence. Her mouth moved without sound, yet her heart was fully articulate before God. This establishes a profound theological point: human religious leaders may evaluate prayer by visible signs, but the Lord responds to the genuine cry of the soul.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:12, Eli the priest sits near the doorpost of the tabernacle while Hannah prays. The narrative notes that as she continued praying before the Lord, Eli was watching her mouth. Verse 13 immediately adds the essential detail: Hannah spoke in her heart, only her lips moved, but her voice was not heard.

Eli's gaze was trapped by surface appearance. Seeing a woman whose mouth moved silently in a setting where festival feasting was common, Eli concluded that she was intoxicated and rebuked her. The word peh marks the point of Eli's misjudgment. He saw the movement of her mouth, but he failed to discern the anguish of her spirit. Hannah had to correct the high priest, explaining that she was not drunk, but pouring out her soul before God.

This interaction sets up a central theme in 1 Samuel: God looks at the heart, whereas human beings look at outward appearances. Eli's spiritual dullness regarding Hannah's mouth foreshadows the tragic decline of his priestly house and God's eventual raising up of Samuel, the child born in answer to Hannah's silent prayer.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's prayer presents one of the earliest examples in Scripture of silent personal prayer. In ancient Near Eastern culture, prayer was almost exclusively recited aloud. The movement of lips without audible sound was so unusual in public worship that Eli interpreted it as disorderly behavior.

God's vindication of Hannah serves as an encouraging model for anyone who lacks the words or emotional strength to speak aloud. The narrative shows that God's ear is attentive even when the mouth is silenced by sorrow.
