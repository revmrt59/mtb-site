PRAYING - (pâlal, Strong's H6419)

Summary:

administrative, devotional, and intercessory petition before God. The Hebrew verb palal is the standard word in the Old Testament for offering prayer, seeking divine favor, or interceding on behalf of oneself or others. In 1 Samuel 1:12, the word describes Hannah's persistent entreaty at the tabernacle in Shiloh, where she poured out her deep personal anguish to God in quiet, heart-felt prayer.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

During the time of the judges, Israel suffered from widespread spiritual decay and inconsistent leadership. Worship at the central sanctuary in Shiloh had been marred by corruption among the priests. In the midst of this darkness, Hannah faced the intense personal grief of childlessness and regular mockery from her husband's second wife. Seeking relief, she went to the tabernacle to bring her sorrow directly to the Lord. In this setting, the verb palal highlights her choice to turn away from human comfort and present her hopeless situation to God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

around bringing an entreaty or request before God. The word can mean to pray, to petition, to intercede, or to entreat. In a few contexts, it carries the sense of arbitrating or stepping in to execute judgment. When human beings are the subject, it describes speaking directly to God with reverence, dependence, and expectation. In 1 Samuel 1:12, the word means offering earnest, continuous prayer to the Lord.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, palal is used whenever people approach God to present their needs, confess their sins, or intercede for others. Prophets use the word when appealing to God on behalf of kings and nations, while ordinary individuals use it when bringing private burdens to God. The word carries a tone of personal humility, acknowledging that human effort is inadequate and that God alone has the power to answer and deliver.

4. Theological Implications

The practice of prayer demonstrates that God is accessible to His people and attentive to their suffering. Prayer is not an attempt to force God's hand, but a humble expression of faith in His sovereign power and compassionate character. By bringing her grief to God in prayer, Hannah demonstrated that true spiritual life persists even during national spiritual decline. God invites the heavy-hearted to come directly to Him with their petitions.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:12, the text notes that as Hannah continued praying before the Lord, Eli the priest observed her mouth. The grammatical form functions as an infinitive construct, describing the ongoing action of her prayer. Hannah was speaking in her heart, her lips moved, but her voice was not heard. Eli misjudged her silent earnestness and assumed she was drunk. This contrast emphasizes that true prayer does not depend on loud words or public demonstration, but on the sincere pouring out of the soul before God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 20:7 — God tells Abimelech that Abraham is a prophet and will "pray" for him so that he may live.

  • 1 Samuel 1:10 — Shortly before verse 12, Hannah was in deep distress and "prayed" to the Lord while weeping bitterly.

  • Job 42:10 — The Lord restored Job's losses after he "prayed" for his friends.

  • Psalms 106:30 — Phinehas stood up and "intervened," which resulted in stopping the plague.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's prayer illustrates that silent, internal cry of the heart is completely heard by God. Human observers, even spiritual leaders, can misinterpret intense personal prayer and spiritual grief. God does not judge prayer by external loudness or polished speech, but looks at the posture of the heart and responds with mercy.
