HEARD - (shâmaʻ, Strong's H8085)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb shama fundamentally means to hear, listen, pay attention, or respond to sound. Depending on who is speaking or listening, it ranges from physical perception to attentive listening and obedient action. In 1 Samuel 1:13, the verb appears in a passive sense indicating that Hannah’s voice was not physically "heard" by Eli the priest. While Eli could not hear her unspoken words and mistook her silent agony for drunkenness, her heart's cry was fully heard by the LORD.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israel, hearing was central to spiritual and communal life. Hearing was not merely an involuntary physical reaction, but an intentional act of giving attention. When people hear God, listening is expected to lead to trust and obedience. When God hears human prayer, His hearing implies gracious attention and deliverance. In the setting of 1 Samuel, Israel was going through a period of spiritual decline under the failing priesthood at Shiloh. Against this gloomy backdrop, Hannah came to the tabernacle in deep sorrow over her barrenness, praying quietly before God in the burden of her soul.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of shama is to hear or listen to an audible sound. In Scripture, the word carries a dynamic range of meaning depending on the context:

  • To perceive sound with the ear.

  • To pay close attention or give heed to what is spoken.

  • To grant a petition or answer a prayer.

  • To obey or act according to what has been communicated.

When used passively, as in 1 Samuel 1:13, it describes a voice or sound that is not audible or perceived by human listeners nearby.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout Scripture, shama connects hearing with active response. Hearing God's voice involves receiving His word with trust and yielding to His instructions. Conversely, when God is said to hear His people, it means He takes notice of their distress, hears their cry, and acts on their behalf. The word often carries an intentional force: to hear is to respond. In prayer and covenant life, hearing forms the fundamental bridge between God and His worshippers.

4. Theological Implications

Scripture presents God as a personal God who hears the prayers of His people. Human ears are limited and fallible, but God's ear is open to the quietest cry of a broken heart. Hannah's experience highlights the contrast between human limitation and divine perception. Eli judged by outward appearances because no sound reached his ears, but God listens directly to the heart. This shows that true prayer does not depend on loud speech or public recognition, but on faith and sincerity before God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:13, Hannah prays in severe grief, her lips moving while her voice is not heard. The verb appears in a masculine singular imperfect form, describing her voice remaining in an unheard state to those standing around her. In the immediate narrative, this lack of audible sound leads Eli to misjudge her, assuming she is drunk. Eli's spiritual dullness is exposed by his inability to distinguish grief-stricken prayer from intoxication. Yet the narrative contains a quiet irony: though her voice was not heard by Eli, her prayer was heard by the LORD. God remembered Hannah and granted her a son, whom she named Samuel, a name that reflects the truth that God hears prayer.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 3:8 — "And they heard the sound of the LORD God walking in the garden in the cool of the day, and Adam and his wife hid themselves from the presence of the LORD God among the trees of the garden."

Here the word describes Adam and Eve physically perceiving the audible approach of God after their disobedience.

  • Psalms 4:3 — "But know that the LORD has set apart for Himself him who is godly; The LORD will hear when I call to Him."

In this psalm, the word expresses confident assurance that God actively listens to and answers the prayer of the godly.

  • Psalms 84:8 — "O LORD God of hosts, hear my prayer; Give ear, O God of Jacob!"

Here the psalmist petitions God for His attentive ear and gracious response during worship.

  • Nehemiah 9:9 — "You saw the affliction of our fathers in Egypt, And heard their cry by the Red Sea."

This historical summary highlights how God attentively heard the desperate cries of His oppressed people and came to save them.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast in 1 Samuel 1 between Eli's inability to hear Hannah and God's hearing of her prayer marks a turning point in Israel's history. Eli, whose sons dishonored the sanctuary, struggled with both spiritual and physical perception. In contrast, Hannah's silent faith resulted in the birth of Samuel, whom God raised up as a prophet through whom all Israel would hear the word of the LORD. The theme of hearing demonstrates that true spiritual vitality begins with quiet, sincere seeking of God.
