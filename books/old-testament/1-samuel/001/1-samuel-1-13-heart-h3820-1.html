HEART - (lêb, Strong's H3820)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun leb refers to the inner self, encompassing a person's thoughts, feelings, intentions, and understanding. In 1 Samuel 1:13, the word describes Hannah speaking silently within her inner self, directing her deepest sorrow and desire to God without uttering audible words.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

1 Samuel opens during a spiritually dark era in Israel's history, the period of the judges. Elkanah and his family made regular journeys to the tabernacle at Shiloh to worship and offer sacrifices. Hannah, one of Elkanah's two wives, suffered deeply because she was childless and faced continuous provocation from her rival, Peninnah. During one visit to Shiloh, overwhelmed by grief, Hannah went to the tabernacle to pray. Eli the priest sat near the doorpost and observed her. Verse 13 explains that Hannah "spoke in her heart," moving her lips in intense prayer while making no sound, which led Eli to mistakenly assume she was intoxicated.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun leb refers broadly to the inner person. In modern English, people often distinguish between the mind (thinking) and the heart (feeling). In biblical Hebrew, however, leb includes both dimensions along with the human will. It can refer to:

  • The center of human thought, reflection, and inner speech.

  • The seat of emotions, such as grief, joy, or anxiety.

  • The source of plans, decisions, and moral intent.

  • Practical understanding, wisdom, or moral clarity.

In 1 Samuel 1:13, the expression "spoke in her heart" refers to internal, unvoiced prayer—deliberately expressing thoughts and emotional distress to God within one's inner self.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Scripture, leb serves as the comprehensive term for the internal life of a person.

  • When describing mental activity, the word refers to thinking, planning, or reasoning within oneself.

  • When describing character, the word points to moral integrity or wickedness, showing where choices originate.

  • In context with skill or decision-making, the word can refer to sound judgment, discernment, or practical wisdom.

Because leb covers the entire hidden life of a human being, Scripture frequently contrasts what happens in the leb with outward appearances or external speech.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of leb carries significant theological truth. First, God sees and evaluates the unseen inner person rather than outward displays. While human observers like Eli judge by external actions, God knows what is in the human heart. Second, prayer does not depend on loud words or public recognition. Hannah's silent petition reached God because true prayer occurs at the level of the inner self. Finally, this passage anticipates a central theme in 1 Samuel: God looks past human appearances to examine the heart.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:13, leb highlights the deeply personal nature of Hannah's prayer. Her distress was so bitter that she could not vocalize her pain. She "spoke in her heart"—her lips moved as her mind formed words, but no sound came out.

This creates a sharp contrast between human perception and spiritual reality. Eli saw only her silent, moving lips and drew a completely wrong conclusion, rebuking her for drunkenness. Hannah had to explain that she was praying out of great grief and grief-filled anguish. Her silent inner prayer was far more genuine than formal outward rituals. The narrative shows that God attends to the unspoken cries of the inner person even when leaders miss what is truly taking place.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 6:5 — "Then the LORD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every intent of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually."

  • 1 Chronicles 28:9 — "As for you, my son Solomon, know the God of your father, and serve Him with a loyal heart and with a willing mind; for the LORD searches all hearts..."

  • Psalms 36:10 — "Oh, continue Your lovingkindness to those who know You, and Your righteousness to the upright in heart."

  • Proverbs 15:21 — "Folly is joy to him who is destitute of discernment, but a man of understanding walks uprightly."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Grammatically, the noun leb in 1 Samuel 1:13 is singular and of common gender. The singular form simply reflects that Hannah was speaking within her individual inner self. It describes the hidden location where her prayer took place—inside her mind and soul—rather than out loud for others to hear. This reminds believers that sincere communion with God does not require elevated speech or public display; God hears the quietest petitions brought before Him in the hidden places of the soul.
