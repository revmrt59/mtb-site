MOVED - (nûwaʻ, Strong's H5128)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb nua' means to move back and forth, quiver, sway, stagger, or wave. It describes irregular, unsteady, or trembling physical motion, such as trees swaying in the wind, a drunkard staggering, or lips quivering without making a sound. In 1 Samuel 1:13, the word describes Hannah’s lips moving in silent, intense prayer at the tabernacle, where her deep grief pours out directly to God without an audible voice, causing Eli the priest to misjudge her devotion as drunkenness.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

1 Samuel 1 takes place at the sanctuary in Shiloh during the era of the judges, a period marked by spiritual decline and formalistic worship in Israel. Elkanah and his family travel annually to worship and offer sacrifices at the tabernacle. Hannah, deeply distressed by her childlessness and provoked by her rival Peninnah, goes into the tabernacle to bring her grief directly to the LORD. Her silent prayer stands in sharp contrast to the spiritual blindness of the priest Eli, who watches her from his seat near the doorpost.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb nua' refers to dynamic, unsteady, or quivering motion. The word can mean to sway back and forth like trees in a storm, to stagger from physical weakness or intoxication, to wander unsteadily without a permanent home, or to quiver or shake. Rather than describing steady, deliberate motion toward a destination, it conveys a repeated, shaking, or unsettled movement. Applied to speech or the mouth, it describes the trembling or physical movement of the lips as words are formed without producing vocal sound.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses nua' in several distinct physical and descriptive settings:

  • Unsteady physical movement: It describes a person staggering under the weight of trouble, storm, or intoxication.

  • Swaying or waving: It describes branches or trees swaying back and forth under the wind or holding sway over other trees.

  • Wandering or unrest: It describes a homeless wanderer roaming from place to place without stability.

  • Silent motion of the mouth: In the target passage, the plural feminine participle describes Hannah's lips quivering and moving continuously while no sound comes out.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah’s prayer demonstrates that God hears the deep longings of the heart even when prayer is silent. While Eli relies on visible evidence and reaches a mistaken conclusion, Yahweh discerns the heart. The physical movement of Hannah's lips shows that prayer is an pouring out of the soul to God, not merely a recited ritual. True petition can occur in quiet, heavy sorrow where words are formed within the heart before God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:13, the word describes the physical motion of Hannah's lips while her voice remains unheard. Hannah speaks in her heart, meaning her mind and affection are directed toward God while her physical lips move in response to her anguish. Because Eli sees her mouth moving continuously without hearing any words, he concludes she must be drunk. This moment highlights a key theme in 1 Samuel: human beings look on the outward appearance, but God looks on the heart. Hannah's moving lips reflect an intense, unreserved cry to God for mercy.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The physical detail of Hannah's moving lips shows how deeply emotional distress affects the body during prayer. In Hebrew thinking, prayer engages the whole person rather than remaining a silent, abstract thought. Hannah's inner grief is so powerful that her physical lips move rapidly to form her inner petitions, even though her vocal cords release no sound.
