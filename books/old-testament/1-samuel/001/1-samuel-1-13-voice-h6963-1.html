VOICE - (qôwl, Strong's H6963)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun qol refers to sound, noise, an audible human or divine voice, or a public report and proclamation. In 1 Samuel 1:13, the word refers to Hannah's audible voice, emphasizing that while her heart was pouring out deep petition before God and her lips were moving, no sound reached Eli's ears.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew word qol is the basic Old Testament term for sound or vocal expression. It covers a wide range of meanings, including the human speaking voice, shouts of joy or grief, thunder, the noise of trumpet blasts, the sound of animals, and public proclamations.

In ancient Israel, prayer was typically offered out loud. Worshippers spoke their petitions audibly when presenting themselves before the Lord at the sanctuary. In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah visited the tabernacle at Shiloh in deep bitterness of soul because of her barrenness. As she prayed, she did something unusual for her culture: she spoke in her heart without producing an audible sound.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to any audible sound or vocal noise. Depending on the setting, it means:

  • The sound produced by human speech, shouting, singing, or weeping

  • The audible voice of God speaking to humanity

  • Natural or atmospheric sounds, such as thunder or wind

  • Animal noises, such as the bleating of sheep

  • A report, news, or official public announcement

In 1 Samuel 1:13, the word refers specifically to the audible sound of the human voice. The text uses the singular form with common grammatical gender, describing the simple physical reality that no audible voice came from Hannah's mouth as she prayed.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, qol communicates presence, message, and spiritual response:

  • Divine Speech and Power: God's voice is often associated with thunder and almighty power, as well as His direct commands given to His covenant people.

  • Human Response: People raise their voice in weeping, joy, or supplication. Listening to the voice of the Lord is standard Biblical language for obedience.

  • Public News or Official Decrees: The word can describe a report carrying news across a camp or a king's public decree sent throughout a land.

  • Sounds of Creation: The word is used for non-human noises, such as animal sounds or environmental rumblings.

4. Theological Implications

The silence of Hannah's voice in 1 Samuel 1:13 highlights how God hears prayer. Human observers rely on visible and audible signs to judge spiritual activity. Eli saw Hannah's moving lips, heard no voice, and incorrectly assumed she was intoxicated.

However, God does not require an audible voice to hear His children. Scripture demonstrates that God hears the unspoken cries of the heart just as clearly as loud cries. Hannah's silent prayer was fully understood by God, who granted her request and gave her a son. This passage shows that genuine prayer is fundamentally an alignment of the heart with God, not merely the production of outward sound.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:13, the narrative creates a strong contrast between Hannah's inner devotion and Eli's outward misjudgment. Hannah "spoke in her heart," her lips moved, but her voice was not heard.

The absence of an audible voice sets up the misunderstanding that follows. Eli, the high priest, observed her behavior and judged her by external appearance, accusing her of drunkenness. Hannah had to correct the priest, explaining that she was pouring out her soul before the Lord out of great grief and affliction.

The detail that her voice was not heard emphasizes the intimate, private nature of her agony. She was not praying to impress human onlookers or following a rigid external ritual; she was pouring out her inner heart directly to God.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The silence of Hannah's voice stands at a key turning point in Israel's history. Her quiet, desperate prayer resulted in the birth of Samuel, the prophet who would bring God's word back to a nation suffering from spiritual decline. Later in 1 Samuel, the text notes that the word of the Lord was rare in those days, but God raised up Samuel to speak His word faithfully. It is significant that the era of Samuel's ministry began with an unspoken prayer that reached the ear of God when religious leaders failed to discern what was happening.
