AWAY - (çûwr, Strong's H5493)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb sur means to turn aside, depart, withdraw, or cause something to be removed. It is used throughout the Old Testament for physical detours, moral turning from evil, and the physical removal of objects or practices. In 1 Samuel 1:14, the word carries the direct sense of removing or discarding something considered harmful or inappropriate. Eli uses it to command Hannah to put away the wine he mistakenly assumes has caused her erratic behavior, highlighting his tragic misjudgment of her deep prayer in the sanctuary.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The book of 1 Samuel opens at a time when worship in Israel was centered at the tabernacle in Shiloh, yet spiritual leadership was weakened by corruption and insensitivity. Hannah, barren and grieved by her rival Peninnah, traveled to Shiloh and prayed in deep bitterness of soul for a male child. As she prayed silently, moving her lips without making a sound, Eli the High Priest observed her.

Failing to discern her silent agony, Eli leaped to a hasty conclusion. He assumed her silence and trembling lip movements were the result of overindulgence during the sacrificial feast. In verse 14, Eli steps in to address what he perceives as drunkenness in the sanctuary, issuing a direct command to put away the alcohol causing her condition.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of sur is to turn aside, depart, or move away from a path or position. When used intransitively, it describes someone stepping off a road, leaving a location, or turning away from a course of action.

When the verb functions to express caused motion, it means to remove, take away, discard, or put something out of sight. In this active sense, the word describes an deliberate action: taking off clothing, removing idols from a town, or setting aside food and drink. In 1 Samuel 1:14, the verb expresses this explicit action of setting aside or getting rid of an offensive substance.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses sur in several distinct contexts across the Old Testament:

  • Physical movement: Stepping off a highway, turning toward a sight, or departing from a city.

  • Moral conduct: Turning away from God's commandments or, conversely, turning away from evil to walk in righteousness.

  • Administrative or religious reform: Clearing out pagan altars, removing false worship, or deposing corrupt leaders.

  • Personal habits or items: Taking off mourning clothes, removing shoes, or setting aside drink.

The word emphasizes a clean break or clear distance. Whether turning off a dirt road or removing idols from a shrine, sur marks a definitive change of position or state.

4. Theological Implications

In the Old Testament, the idea of turning aside or removing carries strong spiritual weight. Holiness often requires putting away things that defile or distract from covenant faithfulness. Israel was repeatedly commanded to put away foreign gods and turn away from pagan practices before approaching the Lord in worship.

However, 1 Samuel 1:14 presents a striking contrast. Here, an official minister of the sanctuary applies a holy command to a completely misdiagnosed situation. Eli uses religious authority to demand the removal of sin where no sin exists. This scene illustrates that spiritual leadership requires true discernment, not merely rigid assumptions based on outward appearances.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:14, Eli speaks directly to Hannah using a singular, feminine imperative form of sur, making his command direct, personal, and urgent. He instructs her to put away her wine immediately to restore order to the sacred precinct.

Eli’s command reveals a tragic irony in the narrative. While Eli is quick to rebuke a brokenhearted woman and command her to remove non-existent wine, his own sons were guilty of genuine, unrestrained wickedness in the sanctuary—evils that Eli failed to put away. Hannah’s gentle response reveals that she has not been drinking at all; rather than pouring wine down her throat, she was pouring out her heart to the Lord. The command to remove wine highlights Eli's spiritual dullness and contrasts it with Hannah’s genuine, heart-level devotion.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 8:13 – After the floodwaters subsided, Noah "removed" the covering of the ark to look out upon the dry ground.

  • Joshua 7:13 – God warned Israel that they could not stand before their enemies until they put "away" the accursed thing from among them.

  • 1 Kings 15:13 – During his religious reforms in Judah, King Asa "removed" his grandmother Maachah from her royal position because she had made an obscene image.

  • Proverbs 16:17 – The proverb describes the moral path of the righteous as turning to "depart" from evil.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Eli’s demand for Hannah to put away wine highlights the difference between outward religious judgment and inner spiritual reality. In the sacrificial feasts of Israel, wine was present, and misuse certainly occurred. Yet Eli mistook the quiet intensity of Hannah’s faith for the loud slurring of intoxication.

Hannah’s eventual answer—explaining that she had drunk neither wine nor intoxicating drink—shows that true prayer often looks incomprehensible to those who evaluate faith purely by surface indicators. The word sur marks the line Eli drew, but Hannah's life proved that her heart was already fully positioned toward the Lord.
