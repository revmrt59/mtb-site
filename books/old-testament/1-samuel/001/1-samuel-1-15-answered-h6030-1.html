ANSWERED - (ʻânâh, Strong's H6030)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'anah basic meaning is to answer, respond, or reply to a question, statement, or action. It frequently marks the next turn in a dialogue, a formal testimony, or God's personal response to human prayer. In 1 Samuel 1:15, the word describes Hannah responding directly to Eli's false accusation of drunkenness. Her humble yet firm reply clarifies her true spiritual grief, turning a priest's harsh misunderstanding into a pastoral blessing.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The book of 1 Samuel opens during a spiritually dark period in Israel's history, near the end of the era of the judges. Hannah, one of the two wives of Elkanah, suffered deeply because she was childless and continually provoked by her rival, Peninnah. During an annual visit to the tabernacle at Shiloh, Hannah brought her grief directly to the Lord in earnest prayer.

In her anguish, Hannah prayed silently, moving her lips without uttering an audible sound. Eli the priest witnessed this from his seat by the doorpost of the tabernacle. Misinterpreting her sorrow, Eli wrongly assumed she was intoxicated and sharply rebuked her. Verse 15 introduces Hannah's immediate response with the verb 'anah ("answered"), as she gently refutes the accusation and explains that she is a woman of a sorrowful spirit pouring out her soul before God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

centered around responsive speech or action, 'anah can refer to answering a question, replying to a command, or giving testimony in a legal setting. It can also describe God responding favorably to those who call upon Him.

Here in 1 Samuel 1:15, the word means to reply in self-defense against a mistaken charge. Hannah does not remain silent under Eli's misjudgment, nor does she lash out in anger. Instead, she responds directly to clarify the truth of her situation.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In Hebrew narrative, 'anah functions as a primary speech formula that advances dialogue. It indicates that a speaker is addressing something previously said or done. The verb carries different nuances depending on who speaks and the context in which they speak:

  • Human Dialogue: It marks a direct response to a query, charge, or conversation.

  • Legal or Formal Defense: It refers to giving testimony or answering a formal complaint.

  • Divine Response: When God is the subject, it describes His act of hearing prayer and responding with help or revelation.

In 1 Samuel 1:15, the verb appears in a singular, feminine, imperfect wayyiqtol form. This grammatical form carries the narrative forward, showing how Hannah steps forward to speak into Eli's false assumption with respectful clarity.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's response highlights how God values truth, humility, and persistence in prayer. Eli, the appointed high priest of Israel, lacked spiritual discernment in this moment and jumped to a wrong conclusion. Yet Hannah did not allow the leader's flaw to destroy her faith or drive her away from God's presence.

Her answer demonstrates how a godly person addresses unfair judgment. She speaks truthfully without dishonoring the priesthood, revealing that her inner posture was one of intense devotion rather than rebellion. The passage illustrates that God sees the genuine condition of the heart, even when human authorities misjudge appearance.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

The immediate context of 1 Samuel 1 emphasizes a sharp contrast between appearance and reality. Eli saw Hannah's moving lips and assumed the worst, commanding her to put away her wine. Hannah's response ("answered") immediately corrects this mistake.

She addresses Eli respectfully as "my lord" while firmly correcting his error: "No, my lord, I am a woman of a sorrowful spirit." She explains that her silent lip movement was the overflow of abundant grief and complaint before the Lord. Hannah's gentle answer diffuses Eli's harsh stance. Upon hearing her response, Eli changes his assessment completely, pronouncing a blessing over her: "Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition." Her clear response converted an unjust censure into a spiritual comfort.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's answer provides a practical model for how to handle mischaracterization. Proverbs 15:1 notes that a soft answer turns away wrath, and Hannah's reply to Eli reflects that wisdom. Had she responded with harsh insults, the situation might have escalated into conflict. By answering with quiet dignity, respect, and transparent truth, she enabled Eli to see his mistake and speak a word of peace over her life.
