BEFORE - (pânîym, Strong's H6440)

Summary:

The Hebrew word panim fundamentally refers to the face or personal presence of a human being or God. When combined with prepositions, it functions to mean "before," "in front of," or "in the presence of."

In 1 Samuel 1:15, Hannah defends her silent, passionate prayer to the priest Eli by explaining that she was pouring out her soul "before" the LORD. Here, the word highlights her direct, transparent posture in God's personal presence, laying open her deep anguish before the One who sees the heart.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Hebrew thought, a person's face represented their entire personal presence and accessibility. The word panim stems from a root connected with turning toward someone or facing a direction. To see someone's face was to enter their direct personal presence, while turning one's face away meant withdrawing relationship or favor.

In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah suffers severe distress over her childlessness and the cruel provocation of her rival, Peninnah. She travels to the sanctuary at Shiloh, where the ark of God was located. In Hebrew grammar at 1 Samuel 1:15, the word is masculine plural, which is the standard form this noun takes to express the complex features of a personal face or presence. Hannah's presence at Shiloh was not merely a visit to a physical building; she specifically positioned herself "before" the LORD to seek His personal attention and mercy.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meaning of panim is the face or countenance. Because a person's face is where personal encounter takes place, the word naturally expanded to mean presence, front, or surface.

Depending on its grammatical context and attached prepositions, the word functions in several common ways:

  • The literal face of a person, animal, or object.

  • The physical surface or front of something.

  • Personal presence or company.

  • Prepositionally as "before," "in front of," or "in the sight of."

When used of God, the word speaks of standing directly in God's presence or under His watchful care. In 1 Samuel 1:15, the word functions prepositionally to describe Hannah standing in the immediate, personal presence of Yahweh.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, standing "before" God describes an intentional act of gathering, worshiping, serving, or praying. The sanctuary was the place where Israel came "before" the LORD, bringing their sacrifices and prayers into the focal point of God's covenant presence.

To be "before" God carries both comfort and solemnity. It implies complete exposure—nothing can be hidden from God's sight. When believers pray "before" the LORD, they are not merely reflecting inward or reciting ritual words; they are addressing the living God who faces them with personal attentiveness.

4. Theological Implications

While Scripture teaches that God is spirit and does not possess a physical body, it uses human language like His "face" to teach us about His relational availability. God is not an impersonal force or an aloof deity. He turns His face toward His people to grant peace, hear prayers, and offer help.

Standing "before" the Lord means coming into a real, personal encounter with the Creator. Worshippers do not need to earn God's attention through theatrical performance or loud speeches. Instead, approaching God "before" His face grounds prayer in His willingness to hear and His divine capability to respond.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:15, the word highlights a powerful contrast between human misunderstanding and divine presence. As Hannah prayed silently, moving her lips without making an audible sound, Eli the priest observed her outward appearance and mistakenly judged her as drunk.

Hannah corrects Eli's misjudgment by clarifying her true condition: she had drunk no wine, but was pouring out her soul "before the LORD." Eli could judge only by outward expressions, but Hannah had taken her grief directly into the presence of the God who searches the inner heart. Her posture "before" the LORD demonstrates that true prayer is an outpouring of genuine burden into the open, loving presence of God, unconcerned with human disapproval.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 1:2 — "And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was on the face of the deep."

  • Genesis 40:9 — "Then the chief butler told his dream to Joseph, and said to him, 'Behold, in my dream a vine was before me...'"

  • Leviticus 14:11 — "Then the priest who makes him clean shall present the man who is to be made clean, and those things, before the LORD, at the door of the tabernacle of meeting."

  • Psalms 62:8 — "Trust in Him at all times, you people; Pour out your heart before Him; God is a refuge for us."

  • Proverbs 22:29 — "Do you see a man who excels in his work? He will stand before kings; He will not stand before obscure men."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's phrase "poured out my soul before the LORD" closely mirrors the invitation found in Psalm 62:8 to "pour out your heart before Him." In both passages, the same Hebrew word describes bringing vulnerable emotions and hidden grief into the immediate presence of God.

Furthermore, Hannah's quiet encounter reveals that coming "before" God does not require elevated status or polished words. Though Eli was the high priest, Hannah was the one truly standing in God's presence in that moment. Her heart was completely bare before the Lord, proving that God's presence is accessible to any hurting believer who seeks Him in sincere faith.
