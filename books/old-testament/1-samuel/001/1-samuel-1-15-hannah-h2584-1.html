HANNAH - (Channâh, Strong's H2584)

Summary:

Hannah is a feminine proper name derived from a Hebrew root meaning "favor" or "grace." In 1 Samuel 1:15, the name identifies the grief-stricken woman who respectfully corrects Eli the priest after he misinterprets her silent prayer, demonstrating her deep grief and transparent devotion before the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The narrative of 1 Samuel opens during a spiritually dark period in Israel's history. The judges had ruled with mixed success, and the priesthood at Shiloh was sliding into compromise and corruption under Eli's sons. In the midst of this national decline, the text introduces a small, personal family story in the hill country of Ephraim.

Elkanah had two wives: Peninnah, who had children, and Hannah, who was childless. Barrenness carried severe social stigma and personal grief in the ancient near east. Peninnah provoked Hannah relentlessly to cause her distress. During an annual family pilgrimage to the tabernacle at Shiloh, Hannah was overcome with grief, unable to eat, and wept before God. It is in this setting of personal despair that Hannah enters the sanctuary to seek the Lord in prayer.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name Hannah is a feminine proper noun connected to a Hebrew root that means to show favor, display mercy, or be gracious. As a personal name, it translates as "grace" or "favored one."

In ancient Israel, personal names often held intentional meaning, reflecting a family's hope, a circumstance surrounding birth, or an attribute of God. While the name itself simply designates the person, its underlying root highlights the concept of divine favor—a theme that becomes central to Hannah's life story as God hears her prayer and acts on her behalf.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses this specific name exclusively in the opening chapters of 1 Samuel. Across these chapters, Hannah's name is tied directly to the narrative transition from Israel's dark period of the judges to the establishment of the monarchy through the prophet Samuel.

The narrative presents Hannah not as a prominent leader or public figure, but as an ordinary woman who takes her intense pain straight to God. Her life moves through a clear pattern:

  • She begins in deep grief and social reproach due to barrenness.

  • She turns to God in sincere, heartfelt prayer, making a solemn vow to dedicate her child to the Lord's service.

  • She receives God's response with quiet faith, gives birth to Samuel, and faithfully fulfills her vow.

  • She bursts into a song of praise that celebrates God's power to lift up the humble and needy.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's story illustrates how God works through the quiet faith of humble individuals to accomplish His overarching redemptive plans. While Israel's leaders were failing, God heard the private cry of a suffering woman and raised up Samuel, who would anoint Israel's first kings.

Her experience highlights several key truths:

  • God notices and values the sorrow of the lowly, proving that access to God does not depend on social status or religious office.

  • True prayer flows from an honest heart, bringing genuine sorrow directly to God rather than hiding it.

  • Divine grace operates by lifting up those who are helpless, transforming barrenness and grief into fruitfulness and praise.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:15, Hannah stands at a critical moment in the narrative. She has been praying silently in the tabernacle, moving her lips without making a sound because her anguish was so intense. Eli, the high priest sitting by the doorpost, mistook her silent distress for drunkenness and confronted her harshly.

Hannah's response reveals her character. Rather than reacting with anger or bitterness at being falsely accused by Israel's high priest, she replies with respect and clarity: "No, my lord, I am a woman of a sorrowful spirit." She explains that she was not drinking, but was pouring out her soul before the Lord.

Here, the name Hannah marks the shift from misunderstanding to revelation. Eli sees an outward display and assumes the worst; Hannah reveals the inner reality of a soul utterly dependent on God's mercy. Her response demonstrates that true prayer is an outpouring of the heart before God, even when misjudged by human onlookers.

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a striking contrast in 1 Samuel 1 between Eli the high priest and Hannah. Eli held the highest religious office in the land, yet he lacked spiritual perception, mistaking deep prayer for public drunkenness. Hannah held no official title or power, yet she possessed spiritual depth and genuine faith.

Furthermore, Hannah's song of praise in 1 Samuel 2 sets a major theological theme for the rest of 1 and 2 Samuel. She sings of how God reverses human expectations—humbling the proud, weakening the mighty, and exalting the poor and barren. This song serves as a prophetic backdrop for the rise of King David and echoes clearly centuries later in Mary's hymn of praise in the Gospel of Luke.
