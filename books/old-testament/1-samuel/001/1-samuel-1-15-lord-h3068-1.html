LORD - ( ye.ho.vah , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The word is the unique personal name of the God of Israel, usually rendered in English Bibles as "the LORD" with small capital letters.

In 1 Samuel 1:15, Hannah uses this name while explaining her silent, heartfelt prayer to the priest Eli. By testifying that she was pouring out her soul before the LORD, Hannah anchors her deep grief and honest petition directly in the care of the personal God who hears His people.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the Old Testament, God is not described merely by generic titles for divinity. He revealed Himself to Israel by a specific personal name, transliterated into English as YHWH (often vocalized as Yahweh). In English translations, following an ancient Jewish practice of reverence, this personal name is printed as "LORD" in small capital letters to distinguish it from title words meaning "master" or "ruler."

The word functions grammatically as a proper noun, serving as God's distinct personal name throughout the Old Testament Scriptures. In the historical setting of 1 Samuel 1, Israel was experiencing a spiritually low period under the priesthood at Shiloh. Yet even when public worship was compromised and leaders lacked discernment, God's personal name reminded faithful Israelites that God Himself remained accessible to individual believers in prayer.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word is the personal name of the God of Israel. It is closely connected to the Hebrew verb meaning "to be," carrying the sense of God's active, abiding presence with His people.

Unlike descriptive titles such as "God" (Elohim) or "Master" (Adonai), YHWH is a proper name. It identifies the specific God who brought creation into existence, made promises to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and redeemed Israel from slavery in Egypt. When Scripture uses this name, it points to the living, personal God who makes Himself known to human beings and acts on their behalf.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, the personal name of God appears whenever Scripture emphasizes His direct interactions with human history and His personal care for His worshippers.

While generic titles for God emphasize His supreme power, majesty, and ruler status over the universe, His personal name emphasizes His presence, accessibility, and truthfulness to His declared word. Israel invoked this name in worship, in corporate gatherings, and in private distress, calling upon the specific God who had pledged to be near to those who seek Him.

4. Theological Implications

The revelation of God's personal name teaches that God is not a distant or impersonal force. He has made Himself known so that His people may address Him personally.

Scripture reveals God's character through His words and deeds across the entire biblical story. His personal name reminds believers that the Sovereign of the universe is also personally attentive to the cries of the afflicted. Access to God in prayer rests upon His willingness to reveal Himself and invite His people into a genuine relationship with Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:15, Hannah responds to Eli, the high priest, who had wrongly accused her of being drunk because her lips moved without sound as she prayed. Hannah clarifies her posture, explaining that she was not intoxicated, but was a woman of sorrowful spirit who had "poured out my soul before the LORD."

This statement highlights a striking contrast in the passage:

  • Eli, the human leader, looked only on outward appearances and misjudged Hannah's broken heart.

  • Hannah turned past human misunderstanding directly to the LORD, knowing that He looks on the heart and understands silent anguish.

By stating that she poured out her soul before the LORD, Hannah expresses complete honesty and emotional vulnerability before God. She did not look to alcohol for comfort, nor did she present a rehearsed or formal religious performance. She laid her deepest sorrow directly before the personal God of Israel, confident that He was the true recipient of her prayer.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 – "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens."

  • Exodus 13:5 – "And it shall be, when the LORD brings you into the land..."

  • 2 Samuel 22:2 – "The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer..."

  • Psalms 25:1 – "To You, O LORD, I lift up my soul."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A subtle wordplay occurs in 1 Samuel 1:15 that highlights the beauty of Hannah's faith. When speaking respectfully to the priest Eli, Hannah addresses him as "my lord" (using a standard human title of respect). However, when describing the true direction of her heart, she speaks of pouring out her soul before "the LORD" (using God's personal name, YHWH).

This distinction reveals that while Hannah respected civil and religious authority, her ultimate hope rested in God alone. Human leaders may fail, misunderstand, or prove limited in their help, but the LORD receives the unvarnished cries of those who seek Him in truth.
