SAID - (ʼâmar, Strong's H559)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'amar means to say, speak, declare, or answer. It is the fundamental word in the Old Testament for verbal communication, used for informal conversation, formal announcements, and divine decrees. In 1 Samuel 1:15, the word introduces Hannah's humble and clear explanation to Eli the priest, who had mistaken her intense, silent prayer for drunkenness. By speaking up, Hannah clarifies her character, defends her integrity, and transforms a priest's harsh misunderstanding into a pastoral blessing.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The verb 'amar is the primary word used across the Old Testament to describe spoken human and divine speech. It appears throughout every biblical era and genre, from narrative accounts and legal codes to wisdom literature and prophetic declarations.

In 1 Samuel 1, the opening narrative sets the scene at the tabernacle in Shiloh. Hannah, afflicted by barrenness and tormented by her rival Peninnah, traveled with her family to worship the LORD. Overwhelmed by grief, she prayed silently in her heart, moving her lips without making an audible sound. Eli the high priest observed her and assumed she was intoxicated, rebuking her sharply. Verse 15 marks the turning point where Hannah opens her mouth to address the priest's false assumption.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of 'amar is to utter words, speak, or express a thought verbally. Depending on the setting and speaker, it can mean:

  • to say or speak in ordinary conversation;

  • to answer or reply when addressed;

  • to command or declare with authority;

  • to proclaim formally or publicly;

  • to think or say in one's heart.

In narrative passages, 'amar routinely introduces direct dialogue. It signals that a person or God is about to express an internal thought, issue a instruction, or respond to another speaker. In 1 Samuel 1:15, paired with the verb for answering, it functions as "said," introducing Hannah's direct reply to Eli.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses 'amar to capture the full spectrum of spoken interaction:

  • Divine Speech: God uses the word to call the universe into existence, speak to prophets, and communicate His holy will to His people. In Genesis 1:3, God said ("said"), "Let there be light."

  • Human Dialogue: Human characters use the word in everyday conversation, emotional appeals, legal arguments, and personal testimonies.

  • Covenant Declarations: In Deuteronomy 26:18, the word is translated "proclaimed" to describe God's solemn declaration of Israel as His treasured possession.

The word itself does not carry a single emotional intensity or theological weight; rather, its meaning receives specific nuance from the speaker, the audience, and the narrative situation.

4. Theological Implications

Speech in Scripture carries significant moral and spiritual weight. What people say reveals what is inside their hearts. Furthermore, God is a speaking God who communicates truth clearly rather than remaining distant or silent.

Hannah's response highlights how God values honest, truthful speech. Her quiet prayer was not a wild, disorderly outburst, but deeply thoughtful devotion. When falsely accused by an authority figure, she did not retreat into bitter silence or lash out in anger. Her spoken defense demonstrates that humble, truthful speech can dispel spiritual ignorance, bring clarity to leaders, and restore harmony within the worshipping community.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:15, the grammar presents the verb as a feminine singular imperfect form with consecutive narrative force (wayyiqtol), advancing the story directly to Hannah's verbal response.

The context places Hannah in a difficult position. She was already carrying deep personal sorrow, and the high priest—the very custodian of God's sanctuary—wrongly condemned her as a worthless, intoxicated woman. Instead of reacting with contempt or fleeing the tabernacle, Hannah answered and said ("said"), "No, my lord, I am a woman of sorrowful spirit."

Her spoken reply balances reverence with truthfulness. She addresses Eli respectfully as "my lord," while firmly denying his accusation by explaining that she was pouring out her soul before the LORD. Her clear words immediately shifted Eli's perspective, moving him from misinformed judgment to pronouncing a divine blessing over her request.

  • Psalms 27:8 — "When You said, 'Seek My face,' My heart said to You, 'Your face, LORD, I will seek.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's speech to Eli offers an important example of gracious self-defense. Believers sometimes face unjust criticism or misunderstanding, even from respected spiritual leaders. Hannah's respectful explanation shows that correcting a false impression with gentle, truthful speech honors God and can turn a moment of conflict into an occasion for mutual blessing and spiritual peace.
