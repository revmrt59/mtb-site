SORROWFUL - (qâsheh, Strong's H7186)

Summary:

,

The Hebrew word qasheh basically means hard, severe, heavy, or difficult. Depending on what it describes, it can refer to physical labor, harsh speech, a stubborn attitude, or a heavy emotional burden. In 1 Samuel 1:15, Hannah uses the word to describe her internal state, explaining that she is a woman with a "sorrowful" or heavily pressed spirit as she pours out her heart before the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In early Israel, during the period of the judges, the tabernacle was located at Shiloh. Elkanah and his family made annual pilgrimages there to worship and sacrifice. His wife Hannah suffered severe distress due to childlessness and the ongoing provocation of her rival, Peninnah. While at Shiloh, Hannah went to the tabernacle in deep distress to pray for a son.

As she prayed silently in her heart, moving her lips without making a sound, the high priest Eli observed her and mistakenly assumed she was drunk. When Eli confronted her, Hannah respectfully corrected him, using qasheh to describe her spirit. Rather than being intoxicated, her spirit was weighed down with severe grief and pain.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The adjective qasheh describes something that is hard, rigid, severe, or difficult to bear. Its meaning shifts based on the noun it modifies:

  • When describing work or servitude, it refers to hard, grueling, or oppressive labor.

  • When describing speech or behavior, it refers to harsh, stern, or severe treatment.

  • When describing a person's neck or heart, it refers to a stiff-necked or stubborn posture.

  • When describing a person's spirit, as in 1 Samuel 1:15, it refers to an inner condition that is heavy, burdened, or severely troubled.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, qasheh appears in several distinct settings. In the book of Exodus, it describes the severe physical hardship imposed on Israelite slaves. In wisdom and historical narratives, it describes leaders or individuals who speak severe words or rule with an unyielding hand.

When combined with the word for spirit (ruach), as in Hannah's defense, qasheh takes on an emotional and psychological nuance. It conveys the idea of a spirit under intense pressure or heavy sorrow. Rather than indicating bitterness or stubborn rebellion, here the term expresses a heart weighed down by heavy sorrow that seeks relief through honest prayer.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah’s description of herself highlights important biblical truths about prayer and God’s care for the suffering:

  • God welcomes those who carry heavy emotional burdens into His presence. Severe sorrow is not a barrier to prayer, but a driving force to seek God.

  • Outward appearances can be misleading. Eli judged Hannah by her physical movements, but God understood the heavy weight of her spirit.

  • Scripture validates honest human grief. Bringing a sorrowful spirit before God in prayer is an act of deep faith, not spiritual weakness or disrespect.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:15, Hannah responds to Eli's blunt accusation: "No, my lord, I am a woman of a sorrowful spirit." The word qasheh appears here in a singular feminine form, agreeing grammatically with Hannah as the speaker describing her condition.

The word creates a sharp contrast between Eli's perception and Hannah's reality. Eli saw a woman acting foolishly under the influence of wine; Hannah was actually a woman crushed under the heavy weight of sorrow. Her prayer was not the slurred babble of drunkenness, but the desperate, silent outpouring of a heavily burdened heart. Her use of qasheh explains that her outward silence was caused by the overwhelming intensity of her inner grief.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 42:7 – Joseph spoke "roughly" to his brothers, using a stern and severe manner to test them without revealing his identity.

  • Exodus 1:14 – The Egyptians made Israel's lives bitter with "hard" bondage, describing grueling, oppressive physical labor.

  • 1 Samuel 25:3 – Nabal is described as "harsh" and evil in his doings, depicting a crude, severe, and unreasonable personality.

  • 1 Kings 12:4 – The northern tribes asked Rehoboam to lighten the "burdensome" service imposed by King Solomon, referring to heavy taxation and labor.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's response to Eli demonstrates remarkable humility and wisdom. Even though she was wrongly accused by the chief religious leader of Israel, she answered gently while clearly explaining her true state.

Her choice of words shifted the focus from external behavior to internal condition. By describing her spirit as qasheh, she helped Eli recognize that what looked like disorder on the outside was actually deep, sincere crying out to God from the inside.
