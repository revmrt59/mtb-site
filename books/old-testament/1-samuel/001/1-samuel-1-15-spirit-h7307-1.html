SPIRIT - (rûwach, Strong's H7307)

Summary:

The Hebrew word ruach basic meaning refers to wind, breath, or spirit. Depending on context, it can describe the physical blowing of air, the life-giving breath within a creature, the unseen disposition or inner life of a human being, or the personal Spirit of God. In 1 Samuel 1:15, the word describes Hannah's inner emotional and spiritual state as a "sorrowful spirit." It highlights the deep grief and anguish she carries before God, clarifying that her silent, emotional praying was driven by severe heart pain rather than drunkenness.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In 1 Samuel 1, Israel was living through a dark spiritual period under the priesthood of Eli at Shiloh. The chapter introduces Elkanah and his two wives, Peninnah and Hannah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah was unable to conceive. Peninnah provoked Hannah continually, causing her immense distress and driving her to tears.

During the annual sacrificial trip to Shiloh, Hannah entered the tabernacle grounds in bitter anguish to pray for a son. Unable to speak aloud through her weeping, her lips moved as she poured out her heart silently before the Lord. Eli observed her and mistakenly assumed she was drunk from the feast. When Eli confronted her, Hannah respectfully corrected him, explaining that she was not intoxicated but was a "woman of a sorrowful spirit." The setting highlights the contrast between Eli's dull spiritual perception and Hannah's genuine, deep cry to God out of her inner pain.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word ruach can mean wind, breath, disposition, or spirit.

When used of physical creation, the word describes moving air, such as a gentle breeze or a strong storm wind. When used of human beings, it often refers to the vital breath of life or the invisible inner seat of thought, emotion, and inclination. Human beings possess a ruach that experiences joy, anger, discouragement, or humility. When applied to God, the word refers to the divine Spirit who acts in creation, grants wisdom, and empowers His people.

In 1 Samuel 1:15, ruach refers to Hannah's inner emotional disposition and mental state. Modified by the description of grief, her "sorrowful spirit" describes an inner life burdened with heavy pain and severe distress.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, ruach carries distinct nuances depending on the context:

  • Physical Wind: It represents the invisible power of God in nature, driving storm clouds or clearing the air.

  • Physical Breath: It signifies the vital breath that keeps human beings and animals alive.

  • Human Spirit and Disposition: It describes a person's inner mood, moral state, or attitude. Scripture speaks of a humble spirit, a troubled spirit, or an broken spirit.

  • God's Divine Spirit: It refers to the Holy Spirit working in creation, inspiring prophecy, and transforming hearts.

When describing the human inner person, ruach focuses on the force and orientation of a person's emotional and spiritual life. A person with a sorrowful ruach is experiencing deep internal weight that shapes their entire outlook and expression.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's response in 1 Samuel 1:15 carries important theological lessons about how believers bring grief before God:

  • God values honest communication from the heart. Hannah did not try to hide her brokenness or pretend to be strong. She brought her grief straight to the Lord.

  • Internal spiritual reality matters more than external appearances. Eli judged Hannah solely by her outward movements and assumed the worst. God looked at her heart and heard her inner cry.

  • Turning to prayer in deep sorrow is a sign of faith, not rebellion. Rather than seeking relief in physical vices like alcohol, Hannah brought her heavy spirit to the sanctuary of God, turning her emotional pain into earnest prayer.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:15, the word ruach forms the core of Hannah's self-defense against Eli's harsh charge. Eli had commanded her to put away her wine, believing her silent mouth movements were signs of drunkenness.

Hannah’s reply directly contrasts physical intoxication with true spiritual expression. She explains that she has drunk neither wine nor intoxicating drink. Instead, her outward appearance is caused by her "sorrowful spirit." She has been pouring out her soul before Yahweh out of the abundance of her grief and accusation.

The singular form of the noun points directly to her personal, individual inner state at that moment. The contrast shows that while alcohol affects the physical body from the outside, her condition comes from deep inside her heart. Her ruach is grieving, and that inner reality overflows into silent, desperate prayer.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 1:2 — "The earth was without form, and void; and darkness was on the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God was hovering over the face of the waters."

  • Job 7:11 — "Therefore I will not restrain my mouth; I will speak in the anguish of my spirit; I will complain in the bitterness of my soul."

  • Job 32:8 — "But there is a spirit in man, and the breath of the Almighty gives him understanding."

  • Psalms 51:10 — "Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a steadfast spirit within me."

  • Isaiah 57:15 — "For thus says the High and Lofty One who inhabits eternity, whose name is Holy: 'I dwell in the high and holy place, with him who has a contrite and humble spirit, to revive the spirit of the humble, and to revive the heart of the contrite ones.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's experience illustrates how misunderstanding can happen even within religious settings. A spiritual leader mistook deep personal anguish for godless behavior. Yet Hannah answered Eli with grace and respect, defending her character without blowing up in anger.

Her example shows that a heavy or sorrowful spirit is not a mark of spiritual failure. Scripture frequently reveals that God draws near to those whose spirits are crushed or contrite. Hannah took her sorrowful spirit to the right place—into the presence of the Lord in prayer—and God ultimately heard her petition and remembered her.
