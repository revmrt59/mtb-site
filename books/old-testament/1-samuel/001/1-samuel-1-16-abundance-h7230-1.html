ABUNDANCE - (rôb, Strong's H7230)

Summary:

The word rov means a great quantity, abundance, mass, or multitude. It describes a vast amount of something, whether physical objects, abstract qualities, or overwhelming emotions. In 1 Samuel 1:16, the word describes the immense volume of grief and pain that Hannah brought before God in her silent prayer. Rather than speaking out of drunken folly as Eli mistakenly assumed, she spoke out of an overflowing fullness of personal distress.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Hannah traveled each year with her husband Elkanah to worship the Lord at Shiloh. Her life was marked by deep sorrow because God had closed her womb, and her rival Peninnah relentlessly provoked her to make her miserable. Year after year, this ongoing trial weighed heavily upon her.

During one visit to Shiloh, Hannah stood before the sanctuary in deep anguish, weeping bitterly and praying silently to the Lord. Her lips moved, but no sound came from her mouth. Eli the high priest observed her and misjudged her behavior, assuming she was drunk on wine. When Eli confronted her, Hannah defended her moral character and explained the real source of her silent, fervent prayer. She was not intoxicated; she was speaking out of an abundance of internal grief that could no longer be contained.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Rov comes from a root verb that means to be or become many, great, or numerous. The noun refers to a large quantity, vastness, abundance, or multitude.

When used of physical things, the word describes large populations, vast herds of livestock, or abundant harvests and wealth. When used of non-physical qualities, it describes a high degree or vast amount of an abstract reality, such as divine mercy, human wisdom, or personal sorrow.

In 1 Samuel 1:16, rov functions as a mass noun indicating the vast degree or overflowing volume of complaint and sorrow that filled Hannah's heart.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, rov emphasizes sheer volume, vastness, or overwhelming measure. It frequently appears alongside nouns of quantity or quality to underline that something exists in immense measure.

In physical contexts, it highlights numbers that are too great to count easily, such as vast armies, swarms of locusts, or abundant natural resources. In poetry and wisdom literature, the word often describes an overflowing amount of speech, wisdom, or emotional trouble.

Grammatically, rov is a common singular noun. In 1 Samuel 1:16, it functions as a mass noun describing the vast quantity of emotional pain Hannah experienced. It does not alter the nature of her grief, but simply highlights how much of it she carried.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah’s use of rov reveals how God receives the uncensored distress of His people. She did not allow her overwhelming pain to drive her into bitter rebellion or petty retaliation against her rival. Instead, she brought her overflowing grief directly to the tabernacle and laid it before the Lord.

This passage demonstrates that deep, emotional turmoil is not incompatible with genuine faith. God welcomes those who approach Him with honest lament. While Eli judged Hannah by outward appearances and misconstrued her silent lip movements as drunken rowdiness, God understood her heart and accepted her prayer. The contrast highlights the difference between human misjudgment and God's compassionate attentiveness to those who pour out their hearts before Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

When Eli accused Hannah of being drunk, he used strong language, telling her to put away her wine. Hannah responded by asking him not to consider her a "wicked woman" (literally a daughter of worthlessness). To prove her innocence, she contrasted two different sources of behavior: Eli suspected her actions were fueled by alcohol, but she explained that her silent petition was driven by an abundance (rov) of complaint and grief.

The word paired with rov for "complaint" refers to an internal burden or vocalized prayer of distress, while the word for "grief" refers to deep emotional provocation and sorrow. By placing rov before these words, Hannah explained that her silent intensity was caused by an immense reservoir of accumulated heart pain. Her prayer was an outpouring of long-standing sorrow brought into the presence of God.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the preceding verse (1 Samuel 1:15), Hannah explained to Eli that she had "poured out" her soul before the Lord. This image matches her description of an "abundance" in verse 16. Just as a full vessel overflows when turned over, Hannah’s heart was so full of grief that it poured out naturally in prayer.

Her choice of words also reveals her deep respect for the holiness of God and the sanctuary. Far from bringing ungodly behavior into the Lord's house, she brought the highest concentration of her personal pain to the only One who could truly relieve it.
