CONSIDER - (nâthan, Strong's H5414)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb natan basic meaning is to give, place, put, set, or deliver. It is widely used across the Old Testament to describe physical acts of giving gifts, placing objects, or setting people in positions of authority. In 1 Samuel 1:16, Hannah uses the word when pleading with Eli the priest not to "consider" (literally to place or set) her as an ungodly woman, asking him not to misjudge her silent, heartfelt prayer as disgraceful behavior.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah suffers deep emotional pain due to her childlessness and the ongoing taunts of her husband's second wife, Peninnah. Year after year, Hannah travels to the sanctuary at Shiloh, weighed down by grief. During one visit, she stands near the tabernacle and silently pours out her sorrow to God, moving her lips without making a sound.

Eli the priest observes her from a nearby seat. Misinterpreting her silent, intense grief, he assumes she is drunk and rebukes her harshly. In response to his unfair charge, Hannah defends herself with gentleness and respect. She asks Eli not to "consider" her a wicked woman, explaining that her silent praying comes from great grief and distress.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb natan primarily means to give, place, put, or set. Depending on the setting, it can describe giving a gift, placing an object in a location, or granting an assignment or position.

When paired with prepositions indicating location or category, natan can describe how someone evaluates or regards another person. In this sense, to "put" someone before oneself as a certain type of person means placing them into a specific mental category or assigning them a moral reputation. In 1 Samuel 1:16, the translation "consider" captures this exact meaning: Hannah asks Eli not to set her down or class her in his mind as an ungodly or rebellious woman.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Scripture, natan is used in a broad range of everyday, royal, and worship settings:

  • Giving or granting: God gives land, blessings, or authority, and people present gifts or offerings.

  • Placing or setting: Objects are set in place, such as sacred items in the tabernacle, or boundaries established for a nation.

  • Assigning status or evaluation: A person is assigned a specific role, reputation, or mental classification.

In Hannah's petition, the word functions in this third sense. Rather than describing a physical act of handing something over, it refers to Eli's judgment—placing her into the category of a worthless person.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's use of natan highlights the difference between human judgment and divine discernment. Eli looked at her outward appearance—her silent, trembling lips—and reached a mistaken conclusion, classifying her as a drunken woman.

This passage shows that God does not judge by surface appearances. While human religious leaders can misunderstand a believer's deepest sorrow, God knows the true state of the heart. Hannah brought her honest pain directly to the LORD, confident that He understood her devotion even when the priest misjudged her. Furthermore, her respectful answer led Eli to recognize his mistake and offer a blessing, demonstrating how gentle speech can turn away misapplied judgment.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:16, the verb natan appears in a masculine singular imperfect grammatical form as part of Hannah's direct request to Eli. She speaks with deep humility, addressing Eli as her superior and calling herself "your maidservant."

The phrase translated "a wicked woman" literally means "a daughter of Belial," which refers to a worthless, rebellious, or ungodly person. Hannah is saying: "Do not place your servant into the category of a rebellious woman." She explains that her moving lips were caused not by wine, but by the abundance of her grief and pain. Eli thought she was behaving with shameless disrespect in God's house, but she was actually pouring out her broken heart before the LORD.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 39:4 — Potiphar saw that God was with Joseph, so he made him overseer and "put" all that he had into his hand.

  • Deuteronomy 4:8 — Moses highlights the uniqueness of God's revelation, asking what great nation has statutes as righteous as all the law he "set" before Israel.

  • 1 Chronicles 16:18 — In a psalm of thanksgiving, David praises God for His covenant promise to "give" the land of Canaan as an allotted inheritance.

  • Ecclesiastes 2:26 — Solomon explains that to the person who is pleasing in His sight, God "gives" wisdom, knowledge, and joy.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's humble appeal provides a model for responding to spiritual misunderstanding. Rather than reacting with anger when falsely accused, she communicated clearly and respectfully. Her response moved Eli to reconsider his judgment and ask God to fulfill her prayer.

This account also previews a key theme in the book of 1 Samuel: the contrast between spiritual blindness and true piety. Eli's own sons were actual rebellious men who corrupted sanctuary worship, yet Eli initially mistook a devoted woman for one. Hannah's heart was pure before God, proving that genuine faith often expresses itself in quiet humility rather than prominent status.
