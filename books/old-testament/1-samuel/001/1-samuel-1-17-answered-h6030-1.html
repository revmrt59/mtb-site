ANSWERED - (ʻânâh, Strong's H6030)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb anah primarily means to answer, respond, or speak in reaction to a statement, situation, or plea. In 1 Samuel 1:17, the word marks Eli the priest's sudden shift from misjudging Hannah as a drunkard to offering her a solemn pastoral blessing, signaling that her deep sorrow has been heard and recognized.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The book of 1 Samuel opens during a spiritually dark period in Israel's history. The tabernacle rested at Shiloh, but the priesthood under Eli was weak and spiritually dull. Hannah, distressed by years of childlessness and relentless provocation by her rival, traveled to Shiloh to pour out her soul before the Lord.

Because Hannah was praying silently from her heart, moving her lips without making a sound, Eli assumed she was intoxicated and sharply rebuked her. Hannah respectfully defended herself, explaining that she was not a wicked woman, but was praying out of great grief and affliction. Once Eli heard her explanation, he "answered" her with words of peace and blessing.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb anah means to answer, respond, or speak in reply. The word can refer to a simple verbal response in everyday conversation, a judicial reply or testimony given in a legal setting, or a divine response when God answers a person in prayer.

Depending on the context, anah can mean:

  • To give a verbal answer to a question or accusation

  • To speak in response to a situation or request

  • To testify or speak witness in a official setting

  • To hear and grant a humble prayer

In 1 Samuel 1:17, the verb describes a direct verbal reply that turns a confrontation into a word of pastoral comfort and assurance.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, anah describes various forms of verbal response. When one person speaks to another, anah introduces the reply that moves the dialogue forward. It often appears in moments where an explanation settles a dispute or clarifies a misunderstanding.

The word is also used when human beings respond to God's holiness or when God responds to human cries. When God answers His people, His response is not merely words; it is an active display of His power and care. Whether in legal settings where witnesses respond under oath or in personal conversations between priests and worshippers, anah highlights the act of addressing what has just been presented.

4. Theological Implications

The narrative of Hannah highlights a God who hears the quiet cries of the oppressed when leaders fail to see them clearly. Eli initially represented a leadership structure that was spiritually insensitive. Yet when Hannah spoke her truth, Eli's response aligned with God's compassionate character.

Eli's answer in this passage demonstrates how true pastoral care operates: listening to the burdened heart, correcting a false assumption, and speaking a word of blessing. Furthermore, Eli’s blessing serves as a human echo of the divine answer Hannah would soon receive from God Himself, who remembered her and gave her a son.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:17, the verb anah is formed as a masculine singular imperfect with a wayyiqtol structure, carrying forward the narrative sequence. The action immediately follows Hannah's humble explanation of her grief.

The word marks a critical turning point in the chapter. Eli moves from harsh accusation ("How long will you be drunk?") to gentle confirmation ("Go in peace"). His verbal response changes the atmosphere from suspicion to peace. Hannah leaves the tabernacle with her countenance no longer sad, showing the immediate psychological and spiritual relief brought by Eli's response.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 18:27 — "Then Abraham answered and said, 'Indeed now, I who am but dust and ashes have taken it upon myself to speak to the Lord...'"

  • Joshua 7:20 — "And Achan answered Joshua and said, 'Indeed I have sinned against the Lord God of Israel...'"

  • 1 Samuel 7:9 — "Then Samuel cried out to the Lord for Israel. And the Lord answered him."

  • Job 42:1 — "Then Job answered the Lord and said..."

  • Psalms 119:42 — "So shall I have an answer for him who reproaches me, for I trust in Your word."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Eli's response demonstrates the power of a priest speaking God's blessing over a broken worshipper. The contrast between Eli's first spoken words (a rebuke based on sight) and his second spoken words (an answer based on hearing Hannah's heart) shows the importance of careful listening in spiritual leadership.
