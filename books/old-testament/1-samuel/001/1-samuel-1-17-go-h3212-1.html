GO - (yâlak, Strong's H3212)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb means to go, walk, travel, or depart. It describes basic physical movement from one location to another, as well as setting out on a path or taking one's leave.

In 1 Samuel 1:17, Eli uses the word to dismiss Hannah after recognizing the sincerity of her silent prayer. By telling her to go in peace, he releases her from her burden at the tabernacle and sends her home with a priestly blessing.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew verb yalak is one of the most common action words in the Old Testament. It describes simple physical movement—walking along a road, traveling to a destination, moving from one room to another, or departing from a gathering. In ancient Israelite daily life, walking was the primary mode of transportation, so this word naturally connected with journeys, daily habits, and transitions between places.

In the opening chapter of 1 Samuel, Hannah travels to the sanctuary at Shiloh carrying deep personal sorrow over her barrenness. Year after year, she endures mockery and grief. In her distress, she prays silently at the tabernacle, pouring out her soul before the Lord. The priest Eli initially mistakes her quiet, desperate praying for drunkenness and confronts her. After Hannah explains her intense grief, Eli recognizes her genuine piety and responds with words of assurance, using this verb to send her forth in peace.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

straightforwardly describes movement. The word can mean:

  • To walk or travel physically from one place to another.

  • To depart, leave, or set out on a journey.

  • To proceed forward or continue along a given path.

  • To follow a particular way of living or conduct when used figuratively.

In 1 Samuel 1:17, the word is used in a direct instruction of dismissal: "Go in peace." Eli is telling Hannah to leave the sanctuary and return home, assuring her that her prayer has been received by God.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

across the Old Testament, yalak carries several key nuances depending on the setting:

  • Physical Journeys: It describes people moving across land, traveling to cities, or embarking on specific missions commanded by God.

  • Formal Departures: It serves as a word of send-off or permission, telling someone they may leave a place or conclude an interaction.

  • Spiritual and Moral Patterns: It is frequently translated "walk" when describing how a person lives—either walking in God's commandments or walking in self-willed disobedience.

In 1 Samuel 1:17, the word functions as a transition. It marks the moment where Hannah moves from agonizing supplication before the Lord to stepping back into her daily life with a quiet confidence.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah arrived at Shiloh broken, weeping, and unable to eat. She brought her anguish directly to God, seeking Him at the tabernacle. Eli's declaration, "Go in peace," serves as a turning point in the narrative.

The theological significance lies in the movement of faith. Hannah did not yet see the physical fulfillment of her prayer; she had not yet conceived. Yet, receiving the priestly benediction, she turned and went her way. She ate food, and her face was no longer sad. Taking Eli's word to "go in peace" meant trusting that God had heard her cries. The passage demonstrates that true worship and honest prayer allow a believer to release their burden to God and step forward with inner peace before the visible answer arrives.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Eli's initial reaction to Hannah was harsh because he misjudged her silent, lip-moving prayer as drunkenness. Hannah respectfully defended her character, explaining that she was speaking out of the abundance of her grief and accusation.

Once Eli understood her true heart, his tone shifted immediately from rebuke to pastoral blessing. His command to "Go" is paired with the blessing of peace (shalom). In Hebrew thought, peace is not merely the absence of trouble, but complete well-being and rest in God's favor. Eli tells Hannah that her time of desperate wrestling at the altar is complete. She is permitted and encouraged to step away from her anxiety, leave the sanctuary, and return to her household trusting in the God of Israel.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Joshua 1:7 – God commands Joshua to be strong and courageous in keeping the law so that he may prosper wherever he may "go."

  • 1 Samuel 16:1 – The Lord tells Samuel to stop mourning for Saul and "go" to Bethlehem to anoint a new king.

  • 1 Samuel 22:5 – The prophet Gad instructs David to "depart" from the stronghold and move into the land of Judah.

  • Psalms 81:12 – God laments leaving rebellious Israel to "walk" in their own self-willed plans.

  • Ezekiel 11:20 – God promises a restored heart to His people so that they will "walk" in His statutes and obey His laws.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The dramatic change in Hannah's emotional state following this verse highlights the impact of Eli's words. In 1 Samuel 1:18, immediately after Eli tells her to go, Hannah goes on her way, eats, and her countenance changes. The physical act of departing from the tabernacle mirrored an internal release of her burden. She entered the tabernacle overwhelmed by despair, but she went away sustained by faith.
