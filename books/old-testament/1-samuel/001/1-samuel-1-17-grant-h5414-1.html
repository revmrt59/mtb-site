GRANT - (nâthan, Strong's H5414)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb natan is a fundamental word meaning to give, bestow, grant, or place. It describes the action of transferring an object, gift, privilege, or answer from one person to another. In 1 Samuel 1:17, the priest Eli uses this word in a masculine singular imperfect form to express a solemn blessing over Hannah: "the God of Israel grant your petition." In this context, the word marks the pivot in the narrative, shifting Hannah's experience from sorrowful supplication to confident expectation of God's divine provision.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The opening chapter of 1 Samuel introduces Hannah, a woman grieving her barrenness and enduring bitter provocation. During the annual family trip to the sanctuary at Shiloh, Hannah pours out her soul before God in silent, tearful prayer, moving her lips without making a sound. The high priest Eli initially mistakes her quiet desperation for drunkenness and rebukes her. After Hannah clarifies that she is not intoxicated but deeply distressed, Eli recognizes his error and speaks a word of pastoral assurance.

1 Samuel 1:17 records Eli's benediction: "Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition which you have asked of Him." The verb natan appears here as "grant," representing Eli's prayer that the Lord would bestow upon Hannah the child she requested.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb natan carries a broad range of everyday and divine actions centered around the concept of giving. Depending on the context, the word can mean:

  • To give or hand over a physical gift, item, or portion.

  • To grant a request, favor, or petition.

  • To assign a task, duty, or position of authority.

  • To set, place, or establish something in a location.

When a person is the subject, natan describes human sharing, payment, or presentation. When God is the subject, the verb describes divine generosity, sovereign appointment, covenant promises, or the granting of human prayers.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, natan functions as a primary verb for expression of the divine will through giving. God is depicted as the ultimate source of life, land, rain, wisdom, peace, and blessing. The word is frequently used when God promises or awards territory to Israel, showing that the land is an unmerited gift rather than a human acquisition.

The word also routinely appears in sanctuary contexts regarding offerings made to God, as well as God's bestowal of priestly privileges upon the house of Aaron. In wisdom literature, natan highlights God's gift of insight and skill to those who seek Him. In legal and personal contexts, it denotes granting a petition or answering a vow.

4. Theological Implications

The theological force of natan rests on God’s character as the gracious Provider. Human beings do not possess inherent power to force outcomes or demand gifts from God. When God grants (natan) a request, it demonstrates His attentive listening and sovereign freedom to bestow favor on the humble.

Furthermore, Eli's declaration reflects the role of priestly mediation in Israel. While Eli himself lacks the power to open Hannah's womb, his authoritative blessing petitions the One who holds life in His hands. The verb underscores that all spiritual and physical life flows directly from God's gracious action.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:17, the masculine singular imperfect form functions within Eli's spoken blessing. Contextually, this statement serves as the turning point of the chapter. Before this moment, Hannah is consumed by grief, unable even to eat. Upon hearing Eli's pronouncement that God would grant her petition, her posture changes completely: her countenance is no longer sad, and she goes her way in peace.

The verb natan links Hannah's earnest petition directly to God's future action. Eli does not claim that he can grant her request himself; rather, he points her to "the God of Israel." The use of natan framing the request prefigures the exact outcome in verse 27, where Hannah testifies that the Lord granted the petition she asked of Him.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Numbers 18:7 — "I give your priesthood to you as a gift for service..."

  • 1 Chronicles 16:18 — "To you I will give the land of Canaan as the allotment of your inheritance..."

  • Ecclesiastes 2:26 — "For God gives wisdom and knowledge and joy to a man who is good in His sight..."

  • Jeremiah 7:14 — "...the place which I gave to you and your fathers..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The structural repetition of natan throughout the first chapter of 1 Samuel highlights a central theme: the exchange of gifts between God and His servant. Hannah asks God to grant (natan) her a child, promising that if He does, she will give (natan) the child back to the Lord for His service all the days of his life. Eli's blessing in verse 17 stands right in the middle of this vow and its eventual fulfillment, showing that God's gracious giving enables human devotion and rededication.
