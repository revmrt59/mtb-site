ISRAEL - (Yisrâʼêl, Strong's H3478)

Summary:

The name Yisra'el means "he strives with God" or "God prevails." It was first given to the patriarch Jacob after his night of wrestling with God at Penuel, and it later came to describe Jacob's descendants and the nation chosen by God. In 1 Samuel 1:17, priest Eli uses the full divine title "the God of Israel" to speak a blessing over the grieved Hannah, anchoring her personal prayer request in the character of the covenant-keeping God of the entire nation.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The name Yisra'el enters biblical history during a pivotal night in the life of Jacob. As Jacob prepared to meet his brother Esau, he wrestled with a divine messenger and refused to let go until he received a blessing. God renamed him Israel, marking a transformation from Jacob ("grasp-at-heel" or "supplanter") to a prince who persevered with God.

Over time, this personal name expanded to identify Jacob's twelve sons, the twelve tribes that descended from them, and the entire national community. In the opening chapter of 1 Samuel, Israel is living through the troubled era of the judges. Worship centered at Shiloh, where the ark of the covenant was kept, and priests like Eli served as religious leaders for the people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Yisra'el can refer to:

  • The patriarch Jacob as an individual.

  • The twelve tribes descended from Jacob as a collective nation.

  • The physical land and heritage promised to Abraham's line.

The name itself combines elements that suggest striving, ruling, or prevailing together with the divine name El ("God"). It highlights both human wrestling in prayer and faith, and ultimate divine sovereignty over the lives of His people.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, the name functions as both a human identity and a covenant label. Scriptural writers use it to distinguish God's chosen community from the surrounding Gentile nations.

When attached to divine titles, such as "the God of Israel," the term emphasizes God's personal connection to this distinct nation. God is not presented as an abstract global power or a localized pagan deity, but as the living Lord who entered into history, rescued His people from slavery, gave them His law, and promised to dwell among them.

4. Theological Implications

The expression "the God of Israel" carries deep theological significance. It reminds worshipers that God binds His reputation to His promises. When people approach God under this name, they appeal to His revealed faithfulness, past redemptive acts, and covenant commitment.

This title reveals that God cares for individual burdens within the larger scope of His redemptive plans. God is the sovereign Ruler over an entire nation, yet He hears the silent prayers of a single brokenhearted worshiper.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:17, Hannah stands in the tabernacle at Shiloh, weighed down by years of barrenness and provocation. She prays silently, her lips moving without a sound. High priest Eli initially misinterprets her deep sorrow as drunkenness and confronts her. After Hannah humbly explains her grief and longing, Eli realizes his mistake and offers a word of priestly blessing: "Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition which you have asked of Him."

Eli's invocation of "the God of Israel" grounds his blessing in God's established character. Hannah was not asking a favor from an unpredictable local god; she was calling upon the God who had delivered her ancestors and pledged His care to His people. Hearing this word anchored in the God of Israel, Hannah receives comfort, her countenance changes, and she leaves in peace long before her prayer is visibly answered.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 32:28 — "And He said, 'Your name shall no longer be called Jacob, but Israel; for you have struggled with God and with men, and have prevailed.'"

  • Joshua 23:2 — "And Joshua called for all Israel, for their elders, for their heads, for their judges, and for their officers, and said to them: 'I am old, advanced in age.'"

  • 2 Samuel 8:15 — "So David reigned over all Israel; and David administered judgment and justice to all his people."

  • Ezra 10:10 — "Then Ezra the priest stood up and said to them, 'You have transgressed and have taken pagan wives, to add to the guilt of Israel.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's personal story intersects directly with the story of the whole nation. Her anguish and prayer led to the birth of Samuel, the prophet who would guide Israel through a national spiritual crisis and anoint its first kings. God used the private weeping of one faithful woman in Shiloh to prepare leadership for all Israel.
