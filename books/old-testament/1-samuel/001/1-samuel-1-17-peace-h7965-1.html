PEACE - (shâlôwm, Strong's H7965)

Summary:

The Hebrew word shalom denotes far more than the simple absence of war or strife. It conveys a state of wholeness, completeness, health, safety, and well-being produced by being in right relation with God and others. In 1 Samuel 1:17, High Priest Eli speaks this word to Hannah, declaring "Go in peace." In this context, shalom functions as a solemn benediction and reassurance that transforms Hannah's inner grief, reassuring her that her silent, heart-wrenching prayer has been heard by the God of Israel.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The account in 1 Samuel 1 takes place during a dark and chaotic period in Israel's history, near the end of the era of the judges. Israel was spiritual sluggish, and the priesthood at Shiloh was compromised by the corruption of Eli's sons. In the midst of this spiritual climate, an ordinary woman named Hannah suffered deep personal grief due to her childlessness and the persistent provocation of her rival, Peninnah.

When Hannah traveled to the sanctuary at Shiloh, her inner sorrow led her to pour out her soul in silent, tearful prayer before the Lord. Eli, watching her lips move without hearing her voice, mistakenly accused her of drunkenness. After Hannah defended her integrity and explained her profound anguish, Eli recognized her sincerity and pronounced a formal blessing over her, telling her to "Go in peace." This traditional parting blessing carried priestly authority, speaking settled quietness and divine favor over a woman whose life had been filled with turmoil.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Shalom comes from a root that means to be whole, complete, or sound. The word can refer to physical health and safety, emotional composure, harmonious personal relationships, or general prosperity. When used as a greeting or departure, it expresses a desire for the recipient's entire well-being.

Shalom is not merely a subjective feeling of calmness, nor is it defined simply as the lack of visible conflict. It describes a positive condition of soundness where everything is in its proper place and functioning as God intended. When a person experiences shalom, they are resting in a state of completeness, secure under God's care.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament historical books, poetry, and prophecy, shalom carries several practical nuances depending on how it is used:

  • Personal Health and Well-being: It often describes physical wellness, personal safety, or reaching the end of one's life in security.

  • Everyday Greetings and Inquiries: It was routinely used to ask about someone's condition—asking if a person was "well" or sending someone away in safety.

  • Relational Harmony and Reconciliation: It marks the presence of quiet agreement, trust, and order between individuals, families, or nations.

  • Covenantal Security: It frequently denotes the restored alignment between God and His people, where His favor rests upon them.

Grammatically, in 1 Samuel 1:17, shalom is a singular common noun used in an absolute sense following the imperative verb "Go." It functions as an adverbial statement of condition, instructing Hannah to walk forward in a state of quiet rest and security.

4. Theological Implications

In the Old Testament, true shalom is never viewed as a human achievement or a matter of mere luck. God is the sole source of genuine peace. Because God is faithful to His covenant, His presence brings order out of chaos and comfort out of distress.

When God grants shalom, it touches every dimension of human life. It establishes peace between God and humanity, restores internal quietness to a troubled mind, and brings structural stability to a community. Crucially, the prophets later revealed that ultimate shalom would be realized through the Messiah, who would reconcile sinners to God and establish a kingdom characterized by eternal righteousness and harmony.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:17, Eli tells Hannah, "Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition which you have asked of Him." This phrase is pivotal for the narrative. Up to this point, Hannah's life has been defined by grief, bitterness of soul, and misunderstanding. She was misunderstood by her husband, mocked by her rival, and wrongly condemned by the high priest.

When Eli issues the command to "Go in peace," it marks a complete shift in Hannah's narrative. The priest's word functions not merely as polite polite politeness, but as an authoritative word of pastoral assurance. By adding the petition that the God of Israel grant her request, Eli turns her private agony into a matter deposited into God's hands.

The immediate result demonstrates the weight of this word: Hannah's countenance changed, she ate, and her face was no longer sad (1 Samuel 1:18). She received the word in faith, and shalom became her present reality even before she became pregnant with Samuel.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 15:15 — "peace" — God promises Abraham that he will go to his fathers in peace, signifying a full, secure life under divine protection.

  • Psalms 122:6 — "peace" — God's people are called to pray for the peace of Jerusalem, seeking the city's safety, spiritual wholeness, and stability.

  • Isaiah 55:12 — "peace" — God promises His redeemed people that they will go out with joy and be led forth with peace, pointing to restored cosmic order and divine favor.

  • Jeremiah 6:14 — "peace" — The Lord rebukes false prophets who proclaimed peace when the nation remained in unrepentant sin, showing that true peace cannot exist without truth and righteousness.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's response to the word shalom offers a striking biblical model of faith. Before leaving the sanctuary, she had not yet received physical proof that her womb would be opened. Yet, when she heard the priestly blessing to "Go in peace," her spiritual burden lifted.

This highlights an essential truth about biblical peace: shalom is not dependent on seeing the immediate fulfillment of our prayers. It enters the heart the moment a believer entrusts their burden entirely to God and relies on His faithful word.
