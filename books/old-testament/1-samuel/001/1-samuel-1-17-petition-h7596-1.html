PETITION - (shᵉʼêlâh, Strong's H7596)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun she'elah refers to a request, desire, or petition presented to someone in authority or to God. Grammatically, the word in 1 Samuel 1:17 is a feminine singular noun. In this passage, the term describes Hannah's heartfelt plea to the LORD for a son. After initially mistaking her silent, anguish-filled prayer for drunkenness, the priest Eli pronounces a blessing over Hannah, asking God to grant the specific petition she poured out before Him.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word she'elah comes from a common Hebrew verb that means "to ask," "to inquire," or "to request." In ancient Israelite culture, bringing a formal request often involved approaching a superior—such as a king, a priest, or God—with humble dependence. In 1 Samuel 1, the narrative opens at the sanctuary in Shiloh, where Hannah suffers under the sorrow of childlessness and the taunts of her rival, Peninnah. Unable to eat and deeply distressed, Hannah brings her longing directly to God in prayer. When Eli sees her lips moving without sound, he initially misjudges her condition. Upon learning that she is pouring out her soul in grief, Eli speaks words of blessing, asking the God of Israel to grant her request.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

She'elah can refer to a request, an asked-for desire, or a formal petition. The word identifies the content or act of asking for something specific. When used in human courtly settings, it denotes an official plea presented to a king. When directed toward God, it describes a prayer in which a worshiper asks the LORD to fulfill a particular need. In 1 Samuel 1:17, the word functions as a feminine singular noun, referring to the specific personal request Hannah presented to God in secret prayer.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Scripture, she'elah appears in contexts involving both royal courts and divine worship. In courtly narrative, the word describes an official petition brought before a king who holds sovereign authority to grant or deny the request. In poetic literature, the word describes a deep inner longing brought before God during times of hardship. In historical narrative, it often marks the transition between asking God for help and receiving His response. The word does not inherently guarantee that every request will be granted; rather, it highlights the petitioner's dependence upon the goodwill and power of the one being addressed.

4. Theological Implications

The use of she'elah highlights the reality that God invites His people to bring specific, concrete needs to Him in prayer. Hannah did not merely offer general praise; she presented a focused request for a child. Her prayer demonstrates that humble persistence and honest grief are appropriate before God. Furthermore, the narrative reveals that while Eli voiced a priest's blessing, it was God alone who granted the request. Answers to prayer come from God's gracious choice rather than human status or ritual standing.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:17, she'elah forms a key turning point in Hannah's story. Prior to this verse, Hannah is consumed by inner anguish, weeping bitterly at the tabernacle. Eli's mistaken rebuke momentarily adds to her burden, but her respectful explanation reveals her genuine piety. When Eli recognizes his error, he changes his tone completely, blessing her with the words: "Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition which you have asked of Him." Hannah responds with faith, her countenance changes, and her grief lifts even before the physical answer arrives. Her petition is later explicitly echoed in 1 Samuel 1:27, where she testifies, "For this child I prayed, and the LORD has granted me my petition which I asked of Him." The connection emphasizes God's direct response to her prayer.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Samuel 1:27 — "For this child I prayed, and the LORD has granted me my petition which I asked of Him."

  • Esther 5:6 — "At the banquet of wine the king said to Esther, 'What is your petition? It shall be granted you.'"

  • Job 6:8 — "Oh, that I might have my request, that God would grant me the thing that I long for!"

  • Psalms 106:15 — "And He gave them their request, but sent leanness into their soul."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The narrative in 1 Samuel 1 creates a deliberate connection between she'elah and the name of Samuel. The name Samuel is associated in the text with Hannah's act of asking God, and later she describes dedicating Samuel back to the LORD. This wordplay underscores how Hannah's granted petition led directly to dedicated service, as the child born in answer to her prayer was offered back to God for ministry at Shiloh.
