SAID - (ʼâmar, Strong's H559)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb amar is the standard term for speaking, saying, or communicating thoughts through direct words. It ranges from everyday conversations to official decrees and divine declarations.

In 1 Samuel 1:17, the word records Eli's spoken response to Hannah. After initially misjudging her heartfelt, silent prayer as drunkenness, Eli receives her explanation and responds with spoken words of pastoral blessing: "Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition which you have asked of Him." Here, the verb marks the turning point from mistaken judgment to a priestly blessing of peace and hope.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The historical setting of 1 Samuel 1 opens during the period of the judges, a time marked by spiritual decay and leadership instability in Israel. Hannah, who was barren, traveled with her family to worship at the tabernacle in Shiloh. Grievously distressed, she prayed silently before the Lord, moving her lips without making a sound. Eli, the high priest, observed her and mistakenly assumed she was intoxicated.

When Hannah clarified that she was pouring out her soul to God in deep grief rather than acting wickedly, Eli's posture shifted entirely. The narrative uses the verb amar to report Eli's verbal response. His words replaced wrongful accusation with a solemn priestly blessing, reassuring a sorrowful woman of God's attentive care.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

centered on verbal expression, the word can refer to simple speech, answering a question, making a promise, or giving an official order.

When a person is the subject, the word introduces what is spoken in conversation, in prayer, or in formal address. When God is the subject, the word introduces His authoritative decrees, promises, or revelations. In narrative settings, the verb routinely introduces direct speech, bringing the speaker's thoughts and intentions directly into the text.

In 1 Samuel 1:17, the word means "said" or "spoke," serving as the introductory verb for Eli's answer to Hannah.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses this verb in a wide variety of contexts across Old Testament literature:

  • Human Conversation: It introduces everyday dialog, questions, and answers between individuals.

  • Divine Command: When spoken by God, it carries creative power and sovereign authority, such as when God spoke creation into existence.

  • Prophetic and Priestly Declarations: It introduces formal announcements of God's word, blessings, or warnings delivered through His appointed messengers.

  • Internal Thought: On occasion, Hebrew narrative uses the phrase "said in his heart" to describe internal reflection or decision-making.

In narrative prose, the verb frequently appears in a grammatical form that carries the action forward (a masculine singular imperfect with wayyiqtol). This form signals the next sequential step in the story—in this case, Eli's immediate response to Hannah's defense.

4. Theological Implications

Eli served as the high priest at Shiloh, holding a position of sacred responsibility to represent God to the people and the people before God. While Eli's initial human judgment was flawed, his spoken blessing carried the weight of his priestly office.

When Eli declared, "Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition," his words functioned as a ministerial affirmation. God used the priest's spoken blessing to grant Hannah inner peace before her prayer was outwardly answered. This demonstrates that God can work through imperfect human leaders to convey His comfort and peace to afflicted hearts.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

The context of 1 Samuel 1 highlights a sharp contrast between appearance and reality. Eli judged Hannah by outward observation, mistaking intense prayer for drunkenness. Hannah corrected his misinterpretation with respectful honesty, explaining that her grief and sorrow had prompted her wordless prayer.

Eli's spoken answer in verse 17 represents a vital pastoral transition. The verb introduces his retracting of the false charge and his pronouncement of peace. Hannah entered the tabernacle in deep distress, but after hearing Eli's spoken blessing, her countenance changed, and she was no longer sad. The immediate context shows how an authoritative word of blessing can minister peace to a troubled soul.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In ancient Hebrew narrative, reported speech is central to character development and theological emphasis. Direct dialogue reveals the hearts of the speakers much more vividly than simple description.

Eli's speech in 1 Samuel 1:17 provides a clear model of how misunderstandings ought to be resolved in godly community. Upon hearing the truth, Eli did not defend his hasty misjudgment. Instead, his immediate spoken response was to extend peace and ask God to grant Hannah's request. Spoken words, when aligned with God's mercy, become instruments of healing and restoration.
