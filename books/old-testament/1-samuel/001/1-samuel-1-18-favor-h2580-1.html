FAVOR - (chên, Strong's H2580)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun chen means favor, grace, acceptance, or goodwill. It frequently appears in the common Old Testament expression "to find favor in the eyes of" someone, describing a situation where a person receives unexpected kindness or approval from a superior. In 1 Samuel 1:18, the word expresses Hannah's humble appeal to the high priest Eli, asking that he view her with pastoral acceptance and goodwill rather than accusation. Receiving his favorable blessing allows her to leave in peace, freed from the crushing grief that had plagued her.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israel, society was deeply structured around family, sanctuary, and leadership. When someone approached a priest, ruler, or family head to make a request, they often sought acceptance before presenting their burden. A favorable reception depended on the goodwill of the one in authority.

In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah travels with her family to the sanctuary at Shiloh. Distressed by years of childlessness and the cruel taunts of her rival Peninnah, Hannah prays silently to God, her lips moving without sound. Eli, the high priest, observes her and mistakenly accuses her of being drunk. Hannah respectfully defends her character, revealing that she is pouring out her soul to God in deep sorrow. Realizing his error, Eli blesses her, praying that God will grant her request. Hannah responds by asking that she might continue to find favor in his sight.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun chen refers to favor, acceptance, charm, or grace. It describes a quality or disposition that draws a favorable response from another person.

When applied to human relationships, chen means receiving kindness, approval, or acceptance from someone who has no legal obligation to grant it. When applied to God, it refers to His unmerited favor and gracious goodwill extended to human beings.

The word often carries the sense of a superior looking upon an inferior with warmth and protection. It is a gift given out of the giver's kindness rather than something earned by the recipient.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, chen is most commonly used in the relational idiom "to find favor in the sight [or eyes] of" another. This idiom appears in a variety of settings:

  • A vulnerable person seeking protection or kindness from a powerful figure.

  • A worshipper or servant appealing to God for mercy and guidance.

  • A person seeking reconciliation after a rift or misunderstanding.

The term carries a distinct nuance of relief and security. To find chen means that potential hostility, judgment, or indifference has been replaced by favor and acceptance.

4. Theological Implications

While chen frequently operates on a human level, it carries rich theological truth throughout Scripture. Human favor often mirrors or mediates divine grace. When Eli retracts his misjudgment and offers a priestly blessing, he acts as God's representative at the sanctuary. Hannah's request for favor demonstrates how God uses human ministers to bring peace and assurance to brokenhearted believers.

Additionally, the passage demonstrates that true favor is sought through humility. Hannah does not demand respect or react in prideful anger to Eli's false accusation. Instead, she speaks with reverence and gentleness, seeking his goodwill. Scripture consistently teaches that God gives grace and favor to the humble while resisting the proud.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:18, chen serves as the turning point in Hannah's emotional and spiritual crisis. Prior to this moment, Hannah was in deep anguish, weeping bitterly and unable to eat. Eli's initial rebuke only added to her grief.

However, after Hannah clarifies her true condition and Eli issues his blessing ("Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition"), Hannah replies, "Let your maidservant find favor in your sight." Here, chen represents her formal acceptance of Eli's pastoral blessing and her desire that any residual suspicion be completely replaced by his goodwill.

Grammatically, the noun appears as a singular form, pointing directly to this specific extension of goodwill from Eli to Hannah.

The effect of finding this favor is immediate and striking. The narrative notes that Hannah went her way, ate food, and her face was no longer sad. Even before her physical condition changed or her son Samuel was born, the reception of favor and the blessing of God's priest brought total transformation to her heart.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 6:8 — "But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD."

  • Genesis 33:10 — "And Jacob said, 'No, please, if I have now found favor in your sight, then receive my present from my hand...'"

  • Ruth 2:2 — "So Ruth the Moabitess said to Naomi, 'Please let me go to the field, and glean heads of grain after him in whose sight I may find favor.'"

  • 1 Samuel 16:22 — "Then Saul sent to Jesse, saying, 'Please let David stand before me, for he has found favor in my sight.'"

  • Proverbs 3:4 — "And so find favor and high esteem In the sight of God and man."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's request for favor highlights the power of pastoral restoration. Eli began the interaction with a harsh, hasty judgment, assuming the worst about Hannah's silent prayer. Yet, when Hannah explained her grief, Eli listened and corrected his assessment. Hannah's request for chen shows that she harbored no resentment toward Eli for his mistake; she actively sought his continued priestly goodwill.

This interaction shows how genuine favor reestablishes community and peace. Hannah arrived at the sanctuary isolated in her pain, misjudged by the priest, and provoked by her rival. She left the sanctuary carrying the blessing of the priest, covered by favor, and filled with peace.
