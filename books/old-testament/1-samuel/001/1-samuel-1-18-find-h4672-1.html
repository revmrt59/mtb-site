FIND - (mâtsâʼ, Strong's H4672)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb matsa means to come upon, locate, attain, or receive something. In 1 Samuel 1:18, Hannah uses the word to ask that she might obtain favor and acceptance from Eli the priest after pouring out her heart to God in prayer.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israelite daily life, matsa described coming upon something, whether through intentional searching or through an unexpected discovery. It applied to physical objects, missing livestock, spatial locations, and social or relational situations. In the opening chapter of 1 Samuel, Hannah travels to Shiloh burdened by her barrenness and the painful provocation of her rival. As she prays silently in the tabernacle, Eli the priest misjudges her anguish and accuses her of being drunk. After Hannah explains her deep grief and devotion, Eli changes his assessment and speaks a pastoral blessing over her. Hannah responds by asking to find favor in his eyes, placing her hope in God's mercy as reflected through His priest.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Matsa fundamentally means to locate, reach, secure, or attain. When used for physical objects, it means coming upon what was sought or missing. When used in personal relationships, matsa often means obtaining goodwill, acceptance, or kindness from someone in authority or from God Himself. The expression "to find favor" is a common biblical idiom for receiving unmerited goodwill and blessing. In this verse, the verb expresses Hannah's desire to secure Eli's pastoral approval and intercessory goodwill rather than his misunderstanding or judgment.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, matsa describes a wide range of discovery and attainment. It is used when people find physical provisions, locate enemies or friends, or attain spiritual gifts like wisdom and understanding. In relational settings, finding favor is not something earned as a legal right or demanded as compensation. Instead, it is kindness received from one who has the willingness and authority to give it. In Hannah's situation, the word marks a vital turning point where her heavy burden begins to lift as she accepts the spoken blessing of God's minister.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's request to find favor illustrates how believers approach God in humility and faith. She does not demand answers or claim a reward based on her own worthiness. Instead, she seeks grace. The broader biblical message shows that true favor ultimately originates with God, who sees the afflicted and listens to the humble. By recording Hannah's prayer and her desire to find favor, Scripture demonstrates that true peace comes when a person commits their distress to the Lord and receives His gracious promises.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:18, Hannah says, "Let your maidservant find favor in your sight," immediately after Eli tells her to go in peace and asks God to answer her request. The verb appears in a singular, feminine, imperfect form, matching Hannah as she speaks humbly of herself. This request initiates an immediate emotional shift. Before this exchange, Hannah was weeping, bitter of soul, and unable to eat. After asking and receiving this favor, her heart rests in God's care. She goes on her way, eats food, and is no longer downcast. The word here highlights her faith in receiving God's kindness through the priest's word, transforming her internal state even before her prayer is physically fulfilled.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 39:4 — "So Joseph found favor in his sight, and served him."

  • Judges 6:17 — "Then he said to Him, 'If now I have found favor in Your sight, then show me a sign that it is You who talk with me.'"

  • Psalms 119:162 — "I rejoice at Your word as one who finds great treasure."

  • Proverbs 19:8 — "He who gets wisdom loves his own soul; he who keeps understanding will find good."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's experience shows that finding favor in God's eyes can bring peace before external circumstances change. When Hannah left the tabernacle, her prayer had not yet resulted in the birth of a child, yet her grief was gone. Obtaining favor from God through faith grants an immediate quietness of heart that sustains a believer while waiting for the Lord's timing.
