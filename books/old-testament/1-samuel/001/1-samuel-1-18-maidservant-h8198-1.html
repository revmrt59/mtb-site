MAIDSERVANT - (shiphchâh, Strong's H8198)

Summary:

The word refers to a female servant, maid, or handmaid associated with a household or a master. In 1 Samuel 1:18, Hannah uses this term as an expression of humility and respect when speaking to Eli the high priest, expressing her desire to receive his favor and priestly blessing as she trusts God with her burden.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israelite society, a female servant was called a shiphchah. This term often designated a woman who belonged to a household, serving under the authority of a mistress or master. Beyond standard domestic service, the word developed an important social function in personal conversation. When addressing a person of higher authority—such as a priest, prophet, or ruler—an Israelite woman would politely call herself a shiphchah ("your female servant") to display respect, modesty, and submission.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word means a female servant, maid, or handmaid. It can refer to:

  • A personal household servant subordinate to a mistress.

  • A woman owned or employed within a family structure.

  • A humble expression used in address, meaning "your servant."

In 1 Samuel 1:18, Hannah uses the word in this third sense. She refers to herself as Eli’s "maidservant" to show honor to his office as priest and to request his spiritual favor and goodwill.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Scripture, the word appears in household settings and personal dialogues. In domestic contexts, it describes servants who were part of a family’s domestic life. In direct speech, using the word signaled lowliness and courtesy. By identifying as a servant before an authority figure, the speaker acknowledged her dependence on that person's favor or protection. The term highlights both social hierarchy and a posture of humility.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah’s choice of words reflects a heart attuned to divine order and genuine humility.

  • Faith approaches with humility: Hannah does not demand a child or insist on her rights; she brings her sorrow to God and addresses His priest with honor.

  • God receives the humble: Scripture regularly highlights God’s grace toward those who recognize their own weakness. Hannah's submission in prayer aligns with how God relates to the humble.

  • Trust in God’s blessing: By receiving Eli’s word with deferential trust, Hannah demonstrates that true worship involves yields of pride and resting in God’s promise.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah suffers deep emotional distress due to her childlessness and the provocation of her rival. While praying silently at the tabernacle in Shiloh, Eli initially misinterprets her grief as drunkenness and rebukes her. After Hannah explains her heartfelt prayer, Eli responds with a pastoral blessing: "Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition."

Hannah's response in verse 18—"Let your maidservant find favor in your sight"—marks a turning point in the story. Grammatically, the word appears as a feminine singular noun expressing her personal submission. She accepts the priest’s blessing without questioning or demanding immediate visible proof. Calling herself his maidservant shows that she receives his words with quiet trust. As a result, her sorrow lifts immediately; she goes her way, eats, and her face is no longer sad, even before the physical answer to her prayer arrives.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 16:2 – Sarai speaks to Abram regarding her "maid" Hagar, illustrating the primary domestic sense of a female household servant.

  • Ruth 2:13 – Ruth speaks to Boaz, humbly placing herself below the status of his "maidservants," expressing deep gratitude for his kindness and shelter.

  • 1 Samuel 25:27 – Abigail addresses David, referring to herself as his "maidservant" while presenting a gift to prevent unnecessary bloodshed.

  • 2 Samuel 14:12 – The wise woman of Tekoa addresses King David, calling herself his "maidservant" as she seeks royal judgment and mercy.

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • An inner posture before physical change: Hannah’s relief in 1 Samuel 1:18 occurs before she becomes pregnant. Her humble self-identification as a maidservant marks the quiet transition from anxious wrestling to peaceful trust.

  • Consistency in Hannah’s speech: Throughout her prayer and conversation with Eli, Hannah consistently speaks with reverence, using humble titles for herself rather than becoming defensive when misunderstood.
