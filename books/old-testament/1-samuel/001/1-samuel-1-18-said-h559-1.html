SAID - (ʼâmar, Strong's H559)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'amar is the primary Old Testament word for speech, communication, and verbal declaration. It describes the act of expressing thoughts, making statements, uttering commands, or giving a vocal response. In 1 Samuel 1:18, the word marks Hannah’s humble, faith-filled response to the priest Eli after he pronounced a blessing over her sorrowful prayer for a child.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root 'amar is one of the most common verbs in the Hebrew Bible. It forms the foundation of human interaction and divine communication throughout Old Testament narrative, law, poetry, and prophecy. Rather than denoting a specialized rhetorical style or high court address, the word serves as the standard, direct term for expressing thought through spoken words.

In ancient Israelite narrative, dialogue drives the storyline and reveals character. When a biblical narrative introduces direct speech using this verb, it focuses the reader's attention on the specific words exchanged between characters or between God and humanity. In 1 Samuel 1:18, the word introduces Hannah's immediate spoken reply to Eli, transitioning her from silent, desperate petition to confident verbal expression.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of 'amar is to say, speak, utter, or declare. Depending on who is speaking and the context of the speech, the word can refer to simple conversation, formal decrees, quiet inner thought, or authoritative divine commands.

When God speaks, the word highlights His sovereign creative power and divine authority. When human beings speak, it communicates their desires, commitments, requests, or emotional state. The word can also describe speaking within one's own heart—expressing inner intention or self-deliberation before bringing those thoughts into the open. In 1 Samuel 1:18, it refers to direct personal address, as Hannah speaks aloud to express her gratitude and submission.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament narrative, 'amar functions in several practical ways:

  • Direct Dialogue: It introduces explicit spoken words between individuals in everyday and historical settings.

  • Inner Reflection: It is sometimes used with "in his heart" or "to himself" to describe unspoken thoughts, intentions, or reasoning.

  • Command or Decree: When spoken by a king, ruler, or superior, the word carries the weight of an official order or instruction.

  • Prophetic Proclamation: Prophets frequently use the phrase "Thus says the Lord," where the verb carries God's direct, binding revelation to His people.

The grammatical form used in 1 Samuel 1:18 is a feminine singular imperfect verb with a sequential conjunction. This form advances the narrative action smoothly, showing that Hannah's spoken response immediately followed Eli's word of blessing.

4. Theological Implications

Speech in Old Testament theology carries genuine weight. What is spoken by God brings creation into existence and establishes His unbreakable promises. Human speech, in turn, reflects the condition of the human heart, demonstrating faith, humility, rebellion, or trust.

Hannah's vocalized response illustrates how faith communicates with spiritual leadership and with God. Throughout 1 Samuel 1, Hannah moves from deep anguish—where her lips moved without audible sound—to a place of audibly expressing her petition, and finally to speaking words of trust. Her spoken acceptance of Eli's blessing demonstrates that genuine faith resting upon God's word manifests in a changed perspective and clear, humble expression.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:18, Hannah responds to Eli’s benediction ("Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition"). Prior to this moment, Hannah had been praying silently in her heart, weeping bitterly because of her barrenness and the provocation of her rival. Eli had initially misunderstood her quiet, tearful posture, accusing her of drunkenness.

When Hannah explained her sorrow and Eli pronounced God's blessing over her, her immediate spoken reply was, "Let your maidservant find favor in your sight." Her use of speech here marks a crucial turning point in the chapter. By speaking these words, Hannah respectfully accepts the priest's blessing, positions herself as a humble servant, and casts off her heavy burden. The text notes that right after she spoke, she went her way, ate, and her face was no longer sad. Her spoken words signaled an internal release of anxiety grounded in her trust in God.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between silent prayer and spoken faith is prominent in 1 Samuel 1. Earlier in the passage, Hannah "spoke in her heart" while only her lips moved, showing that God hears even unspoken prayers born of intense pain. However, when God's promise was conveyed through Eli, Hannah responded with clear, spoken submission. This sequence illustrates that while God understands our silent cries, voicing our trust in His word can be a vital step in stepping out of sorrow and into peaceful expectation.
