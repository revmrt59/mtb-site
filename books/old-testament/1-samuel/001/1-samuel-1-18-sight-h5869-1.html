SIGHT - (ʻayin, Strong's H5869)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun 'ayin primarily refers to the physical eye, the organ of vision. Beyond literal eyesight, Scripture frequently uses the word idiomatically to express personal presence, evaluation, judgment, or favor. In 1 Samuel 1:18, Hannah uses the word within a common biblical idiom ("find favor in your sight") to ask Eli the priest for his continued blessing and acceptance. Her humble plea acknowledges Eli's spiritual authority in Shiloh and demonstrates her trust that God had heard her silent prayer through His priest's words.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The opening chapter of 1 Samuel records the sorrow of Hannah, who was childless and grieved by her husband's second wife, Peninnah. Year after year at the sanctuary in Shiloh, Hannah wept before the Lord. During one visit, she prayed silently from her heart, her lips moving without a sound. Eli the priest observed her and initially assumed she was drunk, rebuking her sharply.

When Hannah explained her deep anguish and innocence, Eli recognized his mistake and offered a blessing: "Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition which you have asked of Him" (1 Samuel 1:17). In response, Hannah expressed her gratitude using 'ayin in verse 18: "Let your maidservant find favor in your sight." Receiving Eli's blessing with faith, her posture immediately shifted. She went her way, ate, and her face was no longer sad.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word 'ayin refers basicly to the physical eye. Because the eye sees and evaluates the world, the word broadens to describe a person's perspective, judgment, assessment, or presence.

When applied idiomatically, the word takes on distinct nuances depending on the context:

  • Presence or view: Doing something "in the sight" of someone means doing it before their eyes or in their presence.

  • Assessment or judgment: Saying something is "good in the eyes" or "evil in the eyes" of someone describes their personal evaluation or moral judgment.

  • Favor or acceptance: "Finding favor in the sight" of someone means receiving their goodwill, kindness, and gracious approval.

In 1 Samuel 1:18, the target occurrence carries dual grammatical number and common gender, functioning within this standard idiom of acceptance and favor.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, 'ayin appears in a variety of literary settings:

  • Physical Vision: It describes literal human or animal eyes, including physical sight, tears, or failing vision due to age.

  • Appearance and Color: It can refer to the visual appearance or surface look of an object, such as the appearance of manna or gemstone.

  • Moral Evaluation: It frequently marks personal evaluation, showing whether an action is viewed as righteous or wicked by God or men.

  • Divine Attention: It describes God's watchful oversight, representing His attentiveness to the righteous and His active governance over creation.

In 1 Samuel 1:18, the phrase "find favor in your sight" uses the word to express a request for acceptance and peaceful standing in the presence of an authority figure.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's request to find favor in Eli's sight highlights several meaningful truths:

  • God's Sovereign Sight: Human leaders can misunderstand or misjudge, just as Eli initially mistook Hannah's silent prayer for drunkenness. God, however, sees the quiet grief and sincerity of the heart.

  • Grace and Favor: Favor is an unearned gift of grace. Hannah did not demand acceptance; she humbly requested it.

  • Rest Through Faith: Hannah believed the word of blessing spoken by Eli on God's behalf. Her heart was settled before the physical promise was even fulfilled, demonstrating that confidence in God's favor brings peace to troubled souls.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:18, the use of 'ayin serves as a crucial turning point in the story of Hannah. Prior to this moment, Hannah was overwhelmed by bitterness and grief. Eli's misjudgment threatened to add more pain to her heavy burden, but her respectful response opened the way for restoration.

Once Eli pronounced God's peace upon her, Hannah's request to "find favor in your sight" signaled her complete acceptance of his pastoral blessing. She did not debate or demand further proof; she rested in the assurance that she was received with goodwill before Eli and before God. This spiritual shift produced an immediate physical transformation: her sorrow lifted, she ate, and her facial expression changed completely.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 2 Samuel 7:19 — "And yet this was a small thing in Your sight, O Lord God; and You have also spoken of Your servant's house for a great while to come."

  • 2 Chronicles 7:16 — "For now I have chosen and sanctified this house, that My name may be there forever; and My eyes and My heart will be there perpetually."

  • Esther 2:15 — "And Esther obtained favor in the sight of all who saw her."

  • Proverbs 5:21 — "For the ways of man are before the eyes of the Lord, and He ponders all his paths."

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a striking contrast in 1 Samuel between physical sight and spiritual perception. Early in the chapter, Eli's spiritual eyesight was slow, causing him to misjudge Hannah's silent prayer. Later in 1 Samuel, Eli's physical eyes grow dim with age. Yet God's sight is never impaired; He sees the humbled heart and responds with mercy. Hannah's request to find favor in Eli's sight reflects her desire for earthly peace, but her trust was ultimately anchored in the God whose eyes behold all things.
