WAY - (derek, Strong's H1870)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun derek primary refers to a physical path, road, or journey, while also being used throughout the Old Testament to describe a person's course of life, conduct, or habits. In 1 Samuel 1:18, the word describes Hannah physically departing from the sanctuary at Shiloh to begin her travel back home. It marks the decisive turning point where she moves forward in daily life with a heart transformed by faith and peace after pouring out her grief before the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The noun derek comes from a root that means to tread, step, or march. In its most basic physical sense, it describes a ground path beaten flat by the feet of travelers, a marked highway, or the physical distance traveled on a trip. Ancient Israel relied on established roads and tracks to travel between villages, tribal regions, and central places of worship.

In the narrative setting of 1 Samuel 1, Hannah traveled annually with her husband Elkanah to the tabernacle in Shiloh. Burdened by childlessness and bitter provoked distress, she went to the sanctuary to pray. After receiving a word of blessing from Eli the priest, Hannah departed. The word derek is used to describe her physical route home, highlighting her return to ordinary life following her profound encounter with God in prayer.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to a concrete physical path, a travel route, or an actual journey from one place to another. In narrative texts, it often simply describes someone taking to the road or continuing on their traveled route.

By natural extension, the word is also used across the Old Testament to describe a person's moral direction, habitual behavior, or manner of living. When referring to God, it highlights His actions, His righteous standards, or His administrative guidance over human history.

In 1 Samuel 1:18, the word carries its direct physical meaning: Hannah walked out onto her travel route and went on her journey. Contextually, this physical movement signals that her period of weeping and agonizing prayer at Shiloh had concluded.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament historical narratives, poetry, and wisdom literature, derek functions as both a concrete travel term and a rich metaphor for spiritual direction.

When used for physical travel, the word describes setting out on a route, making a day's journey, or traveling along a specific highway. When used morally or wisdom-wise, it describes the contrast between the way of the righteous and the way of the wicked.

The word itself is straightforward and grounded in everyday movement. Its deeper emotional or spiritual significance in any given passage comes from who is traveling, where they are going, and what internal state they carry along the path.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's departure along her way provides a clear picture of faithful prayer. She did not receive an immediate physical manifestation of a child before leaving Shiloh; she received God's assurance through the word spoken by the priest. Her physical departure demonstrates that true trust in God alters how a person steps back into their daily routine.

The passage illustrates that spiritual peace reflects in concrete physical actions. Hannah arrived at Shiloh in deep distress, unable to eat, and weeping bitterly. After laying her petition before the Lord, she stepped back onto her path, ate food, and carried a brightened face. Walking her route home became a demonstration of quiet confidence in God's character and faithful care.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:18, the use of derek completes a dramatic narrative contrast. Earlier in the chapter, Hannah sat in sorrow near the doorpost of the tabernacle, pouring out her soul. Eli mistook her silent, moving lips for drunkenness, but Hannah explained her grief, and Eli dismissed her with the blessing, "Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition."

Hannah accepted the blessing, saying, "Let your maidservant find favor in your sight." The text then notes that she "went her way and ate, and her face was no longer sad." The physical act of going her way shows her complete surrender of the burden. She did not remain lingering at the sanctuary in anxious uncertainty. She returned to her normal routine and journeyed home because she believed God had heard her prayer.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 3:24 — "So He drove out the man; and He placed cherubim at the east of the garden of Eden, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to guard the way to the tree of life."

  • Deuteronomy 1:2 — "It is eleven days' journey from Horeb by the way of Mount Seir to Kadesh Barnea."

  • 1 Samuel 26:3 — "And Saul encamped in the hill of Hachilah, which faces Jeshimon, by the road."

  • Psalms 119:37 — "Turn away my eyes from looking at worthless things, and revive me in Your way."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Grammatically, derek appears in 1 Samuel 1:18 as a singular common noun attached to Hannah's movement ("went her way"). The simplicity of the phrase underscores the ordinary nature of the physical action.

Scripture frequently connects profound inner faith with simple, everyday movements. Rather than recording a complex theological declaration from Hannah, the narrative records her taking her path home, eating a meal, and showing a changed face. Her physical journey back to Ramah serves as the quiet proof of her renewed peace.
