WENT - (yâlak, Strong's H3212)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb means to walk, go, move, or depart from one place to another. It describes basic physical motion, traveling along a path, or setting out on a journey. In 1 Samuel 1:18, the word describes Hannah leaving the place of prayer at Shiloh after receiving Eli's blessing. Her physical action of walking away, eating, and showing a brightened countenance demonstrates that she laid her heavy burden down before the Lord and moved forward in peaceful trust.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the opening chapter of 1 Samuel, Hannah faces deep grief because of her childlessness and the cruel mocking she endures from her husband's second wife, Peninnah. Year after year at the sanctuary in Shiloh, Hannah carried this anguish. During one visit, she wept bitterly and poured out her soul before the Lord in silent, desperate prayer.

The priest Eli initially mistook her quiet mouth movement for drunkenness. Once Hannah explained her deep sorrow, Eli responded with a word of blessing, encouraging her to depart in peace and asking God to grant her petition. Hannah accepted his words with faith, requested continued favor, and then stepped away from the place of prayer. The word "went" marks this decisive turning point from immobilizing sorrow to active trust.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb can mean to walk, go, proceed, depart, or move. It is one of the most common motion verbs in Hebrew, describing ordinary movement from one point to another.

Depending on the context, the word can refer to:

  • Physical movement from one location to another, such as walking down a road or leaving a room.

  • Embarking on a specific journey or setting out on a mission.

  • Moving along with a group, an army, or an object in motion.

  • Figuratively proceeding through life or conducting oneself according to a set of principles (walking in God's ways).

Here in 1 Samuel 1:18, the word refers to Hannah physically turning, stepping away from the tabernacle, and walking back to join her family.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament narrative, this verb frequently signals a shift in action or setting. Rather than functioning merely as filler text, it often highlights a deliberate move from one state of affairs to the next.

When people pray or receive a command in Scripture, their subsequent movement reveals their response. When God tells a prophet or leader to move, using this word emphasizes the obligation to act. When a person leaves a sanctuary or altar using this word, it shows the transition from corporate worship or private prayer back into daily routine.

The word can also describe continuous or steady motion. Whether walking on foot, traveling in a caravan, or drifting upon water, it brings to mind physical progress along a path.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's departure from the place of prayer illustrates how faith expresses itself in practical steps. Prayer is not meant to keep a person trapped in endless internal anxiety; it is designed to hand the burden over to God.

Once Hannah poured out her soul and received Eli's pastoral encouragement, her willingness to step forward showed that she trusted God with the outcome. She did not wait to see a physical change in her body before she moved forward; she walked away in peace while her circumstances outwardly remained unchanged. Scripture shows that walking forward in everyday duty after seeking God is a vital aspect of living by faith.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

The context of 1 Samuel 1:18 highlights a striking contrast between Hannah's condition before and after her prayer. Earlier in the passage, her grief was so overwhelming that she wept continuously and could not bring herself to eat. Her sorrow was completely visible and physically disabling.

When Hannah "went her way," everything changed. The text explicitly connects her leaving with two other key actions: she ate, and her countenance was no longer sad. Her step away from the sanctuary was not a casual departure; it was a physical demonstration of a changed heart. The grief that had immobilized her was lifted. By stepping forward into her normal routine, Hannah visibly demonstrated that she had left her burden at the altar and was resting in God's care.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Hebrew historical narratives, internal spiritual states are frequently paired with outward physical movements. A person's posture, movement, and bodily actions reflect what has occurred within the heart.

Hannah’s action of setting out on her way shows the psychological relief that genuine prayer produces. When she walked back to her quarters, she moved with a restored spirit. This simple action underscores the reality that faith produces tangible peace, enabling a person to re-enter daily life with hope and dignity even before the ultimate answer to prayer arrives.
