WOMAN - (ʼishshâh, Strong's H802)

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'ishshah means an adult female human being, a wife, or a woman in a specific role or relationship. Throughout the Old Testament, it is the primary word used to describe women across all stages of life, from creation and marriage to community participation and worship. In 1 Samuel 1:18, the word describes Hannah at a crucial turning point in her life. After weeping in deep anguish and placing her pain before the Lord, she receives Eli's blessing and goes her way. The narrative records that "the woman went her way and ate, and her face was no longer sad." Here, the simple description highlights Hannah's personal shift: the heart-sick woman who entered the tabernacle leaves with quiet confidence and peace in God.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word 'ishshah is the standard Hebrew noun for a female person. In Hebrew language and culture, the word is closely linked with 'ish (meaning "man"), reflecting the creation account where the first female was created as a companion suited to man.

In ancient Israelite society, a woman's life was centered around family, home, community worship, and passing on the covenant faith to future generations. Within the narrative of 1 Samuel 1, Hannah experiences deep sorrow because of her inability to bear children and the painful provocation she receives from her husband's second wife. Her social and personal pain drives her to God's house at Shiloh, setting the stage for one of the most intimate prayers recorded in the Old Testament.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of 'ishshah is straightforward and broad:

  • An individual female human being ("woman").

  • A married female partner ("wife").

  • A specific female person designated in a narrative.

When the word appears in the context of marriage, it carries the sense of "wife," often indicated by a pronoun such as "his wife." When used generally or with a definite article ("the woman"), it identifies a specific female individual within the story.

In 1 Samuel 1:18, the word functions as a narrative pointer identifying Hannah. The term itself does not carry intrinsic theological attributes; rather, the context supplies the spiritual weight of who this particular woman is and what God is doing in her life.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses 'ishshah across every literary genre in the Old Testament:

  • In creation, it distinguishes the woman formed by God to stand alongside man in life and calling.

  • In covenant law, it ensures that women are recognized within legal, spiritual, and communal regulations.

  • In historical accounts, it identifies individual women taking part in national events, personal struggles, and acts of devotion.

  • In wisdom and poetry, it describes the value of a virtuous wife, the blessings of family, and various human experiences.

Because the word is broad, its precise nuance always depends on the surrounding context. It can describe a queen, a mother, a widow, a prophetess, or an ordinary community member.

4. Theological Implications

While 'ishshah is simply the everyday word for a woman, its place in biblical history reflects significant truths:

  • God relates directly to women as individuals with personal faith, moral responsibility, and direct access to Him in prayer.

  • Hannah’s experience shows that God values the silent grief and personal prayers of a suffering woman when others misjudge or ignore her.

  • True spiritual peace often arrives before visible circumstances change. Hannah walked away refreshed not because she was already holding her baby, but because she had cast her care upon the Lord.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:18, the grammar uses a singular, feminine noun to focus attention directly on Hannah. Up to this point in the chapter, she has been called Elkanah's wife or named directly. Here, after Eli blesses her and prays that God grant her request, the narrator simply calls her "the woman."

This simple designation marks a noticeable contrast between her condition when she arrived and her posture as she leaves:

  • She arrived filled with bitter grief, weeping bitterly, and unable to eat.

  • After praying and accepting the priest’s blessing, "the woman went her way and ate, and her face was no longer sad."

Calling her "the woman" highlights her individual journey of faith. She stepped into the tabernacle burdened and misjudged; she stepped out as a woman resting in God's promise. The narrative uses the word to fix our eyes on her personal transformation before the physical answer to her prayer was fulfilled.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's response in 1 Samuel 1:18 provides an instructive picture of biblical faith. Notice the sequence of events: she prayed honestly, surrendered her grief, received the word of blessing, and changed her physical posture immediately. She ate food and allowed her countenance to brighten before any physical sign of pregnancy existed.

Her narrative illustrates that faith affects the heart and mind ahead of external changes. The text identifies her simply as "the woman" to remind every reader that God deals with real human beings in real emotional pain, offering genuine peace when we leave our burdens at His feet.
