BEFORE - (pânîym, Strong's H6440)

Summary:

The Hebrew word panim literally means "face" or "faces." In biblical Hebrew, it is frequently combined with prepositions to mean "in the presence of," "in front of," or "before." In 1 Samuel 1:19, the word describes Elkanah and Hannah worshiping directly in the presence of the LORD at the tabernacle in Shiloh before returning home, highlighting their reverence and peaceful trust in God after Hannah's earnest prayer.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word panim is a primary noun in ancient Hebrew that refers to the physical face or countenance of a person or creature. Because a person's face is where their presence is met and recognized, Hebrew naturally uses panim to express physical proximity, personal encounter, and presence.

When combined with prepositions like le- (forming lipney), the noun functions prepositionally to mean "to the face of" or simply "before." In the Old Testament narratives and worship settings, doing an action "before the LORD" indicates being in His immediate, recognized presence—frequently connected with the tabernacle, the altar, or the ark of the covenant where God revealed His glory among His people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to several related concepts depending on its grammatical setting:

  • The literal physical face or countenance of a human being or animal.

  • The front side, surface, or outer face of an object, building, or land.

  • Prepositional usage meaning "in front of," "in the presence of," or "before."

  • When referring to God, the word denotes the manifest presence of God. To stand, pray, or worship "before the LORD" means to present oneself intentionally in His presence.

Grammatically in 1 Samuel 1:19, the noun is plural and masculine, functioning in a construct combination that marks position directly relative to God.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, panim carries several clear shades of meaning based on context:

  • Physical countenance: It reflects internal human emotions such as joy, sorrow, anger, or shame visible on a person's face.

  • Surface or front: It describes spatial locations, such as the surface of the earth or the front portion of sacred priestly garments.

  • Audience and presence: In royal and sanctuary contexts, it expresses standing in the direct view of a ruler or of God Himself. Standing "before" a king or God implies submission, reverence, and formal audience.

The expression "before the LORD" appears routinely in sacrificial worship, covenant gatherings, and personal prayer, reminding Israel that God is not a distant, abstract deity, but a living God in whose presence His people actively live and worship.

4. Theological Implications

The concept of doing things "before the LORD" highlights both God's omnipresence and His localized, accessible presence among His covenant people. While God fills heaven and earth, Scripture speaks of His "face" or presence as a place of intimate meeting and holy devotion.

Worshiping "before the LORD" emphasizes that God sees, hears, and responds to His people. It redirects human focus away from personal distress and places it upon God's sovereign care. In Israel's sanctuary history, entering God's presence required reverence, faith, and a heart ready to honor Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah arrived at Shiloh in deep anguish, weeping bitterly and pouring out her soul "before the LORD." After the high priest Eli gave her a word of blessing, her sorrow lifted, and her physical appearance changed.

In 1 Samuel 1:19, early the next morning before journeying back to Ramah, Elkanah and his household gather to worship "before the LORD." Here, panim marks an important transition in the story. Their worship in God's presence is no longer a desperate plea for help, but an act of reverent adoration, quiet confidence, and grateful commitment. They placed the outcome of Hannah's request fully into God's hands. Worshiping before His presence sealed their faith prior to the text noting that the LORD remembered her.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 1:2 — Describes the physical surface of the unformed waters ("face").

  • Leviticus 14:11 — Used in a sanctuary setting for presenting a cleansed person directly in God's presence ("before").

  • Psalms 62:8 — Encourages God's people to bring their honest feelings and prayers directly into His presence ("before").

  • Proverbs 22:29 — Refers to standing in the physical presence and royal service of kings ("before").

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Hebrew thought, a person's face and presence are inseparable. To seek someone's face is to seek their direct personal presence and favor. When Scripture speaks of God turning His face toward a person, it indicates His favor, attention, and blessing. Conversely, when God hides His face, it signifies a withdrawal of felt fellowship or divine displeasure.

Hannah's narrative demonstrates this dynamic clearly. Her journey in 1 Samuel 1 begins with weeping before God's face in heavy grief and finishes with worshiping before His face in peace, showing how drawing near to God brings quiet trust long before outward circumstances change.
