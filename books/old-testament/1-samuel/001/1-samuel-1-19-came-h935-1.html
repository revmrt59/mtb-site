CAME - (bôwʼ, Strong's H935)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb bo' broadly means to come, enter, go, or arrive. In 1 Samuel 1:19, the word describes Elkanah and his family arriving back at their home in Ramah after worshiping at Shiloh. It marks the transition from their pilgrimage of earnest prayer to their ordinary domestic life, where God remembered Hannah and answered her cry.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The verb bo' is a fundamental motion verb in the Old Testament. It describes physical movement from one location to another, such as entering a space, arriving at a destination, or returning home. Depending on its grammatical form, it can also describe bringing or introducing someone or something into a place.

In the opening narrative of 1 Samuel, Elkanah's family made an annual pilgrimage to the tabernacle at Shiloh to worship and sacrifice. After Hannah poured out her heart before the Lord in silent prayer and received a blessing from Eli the priest, the family concluded their worship. The verb bo' captures their arrival back at Ramah, establishing the location where God would answer Hannah's request.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meaning of bo' centers on motion toward a destination. The word can mean:

  • To enter a house, tent, city, or sacred space.

  • To arrive at a specific location after a journey.

  • To return to one's home or homeland.

  • To bring or carry people, animals, or objects into a place.

In 1 Samuel 1:19, the verb refers to physical arrival: Elkanah and his family completed their return travel from Shiloh and came into their house at Ramah.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, bo' appears in many narrative contexts. It describes historical journeys, priests entering the sanctuary, kings entering cities, and messengers delivering news.

Because the word is so widely used, its precise nuance comes from the surrounding narrative:

  • In military contexts, it often describes troops coming to battle or entering a conquered city.

  • In domestic contexts, it frequently describes returning home after travel or daily work.

  • In historical prose, its wayyiqtol form carries the story forward, linking actions together in sequential order.

4. Theological Implications

The movement described by bo' in 1 Samuel 1:19 connects public worship with domestic life. Hannah prayed at the tabernacle in Shiloh, but the direct outcome of her prayer took place after the family arrived back home in Ramah.

This movement shows that God operates in ordinary spaces just as He does in sacred places. The God who heard Hannah at Shiloh remained present when she returned home. Worship before the Lord leads back into daily life, where God unfolds His redemptive purpose through ordinary human affairs.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:19, bo' forms part of a sequence of actions: the family rose early, worshiped before the Lord, returned, and came to their house in Ramah.

The masculine plural imperfect form with a sequential prefix (wayyiqtol) presents this arrival as the direct continuation of their morning devotion. The narrative links their act of worship to their return home. Immediately after they arrived in Ramah, Elkanah knew his wife, "and the LORD remembered her." The verb bo' sets the scene for God's gracious response to Hannah's faith.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:19 — "Out of the ground the LORD God formed every beast of the field and every bird of the air, and brought them to Adam to see what he would call them." Here the verb describes bringing God's creation to humanity.

  • Joshua 6:11 — "So the ark of the LORD circled the city, going around it once. Then they came into the camp and lodged in the camp." The word describes Israel returning to camp after circling Jericho.

  • 2 Samuel 11:22 — "So the messenger went, and came and told David all that Joab had sent by him." The word denotes a messenger arriving to deliver news to the king.

  • Isaiah 19:23 — "In that day there will be a highway from Egypt to Assyria, and the Assyrian will come into Egypt and the Egyptian into Assyria." The prophet uses the word to describe peaceful movement between nations.

  • Jeremiah 43:11 — "When he comes, he shall strike the land of Egypt and deliver to death those appointed for death." The word describes a foreign ruler arriving to execute judgment.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The arrival back at Ramah in 1 Samuel 1:19 provides a striking contrast with the corruption occurring at Shiloh under Eli's sons. While the sanctuary was marred by unfaithfulness, Elkanah and Hannah returned home in godliness and expectation. Their simple journey home highlights a household walking faithfully before God, prepared to raise the child who would lead Israel back to the Lord.
