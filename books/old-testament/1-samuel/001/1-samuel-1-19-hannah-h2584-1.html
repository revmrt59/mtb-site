HANNAH - (Channâh, Strong's H2584)

Summary:

The proper name Hannah comes from a Hebrew root meaning "grace" or "favor." In 1 Samuel 1:19, the personal name Hannah marks the moment where God actively answers her persistent prayer in her season of barrenness, showing that the Lord remembered her deep sorrow and granted her request.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Hannah lived near the end of the period of the Judges, a time marked by widespread spiritual decay and political instability in Israel. She was one of two wives of a man named Elkanah from the mountains of Ephraim. While the other wife, Peninnah, had children, Hannah was childless because the Lord had closed her womb.

In ancient Israelite society, childlessness carried severe social stigma and personal grief. Each year when the family traveled to the tabernacle at Shiloh to worship and sacrifice, Peninnah provoked Hannah to drive her to despair. Instead of turning to bitterness or despair, Hannah brought her sorrow directly to the Lord in silent, weeping prayer at Shiloh, promising that if God gave her a male child, she would dedicate him fully to the Lord's service.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name Channah is a feminine proper noun derived from the Hebrew verb root chanan, which means to show favor, extend mercy, or be gracious. As a proper name, it means "grace," "favor," or "gracious one."

The name itself is a standard personal identifier, but in the context of Old Testament narrative, personal names often mirror the character's life experience or God's purpose through them. In Hannah's case, her name reflects both the divine favor she sought in her distress and the unmerited favor God poured out upon her.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In Scripture, the name refers exclusively to the mother of the prophet Samuel. Her narrative spans the opening chapters of 1 Samuel, tracing her journey from agonizing childlessness to joy and thanksgiving.

Throughout this story, her name carries the nuance of humble dependence upon divine mercy. Hannah does not claim any moral merit or leverage over God; rather, she approaches Him as an afflicted servant asking for grace. Her story demonstrates that God's favor is extended to those who bring their grief directly to Him in faith.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's story introduces the central theological themes of the books of Samuel. Her life shows that God pays attention to the lowly, brokenhearted, and helpless.

The divine response to Hannah establishes that salvation and blessing are rooted in God's unearned favor rather than human power, status, or prominence. When God acts on Hannah's behalf, He turns her barrenness into fruitful joy, illustrating a broader biblical pattern: God humbles the proud and lifts up the downtrodden. Her song of praise in 1 Samuel 2 lays the theological foundation for how God will work through the house of David and ultimately through the Messiah.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:19, after the family worshiped at Shiloh and returned home to Ramah, the text states that Elkanah knew Hannah his wife, "and the LORD remembered her." The use of her specific personal name here highlights God's personal attention to her individual cry. She was not lost in the crowd of worshipers at Shiloh, nor was she defined solely by her social condition.

The phrase "the LORD remembered her" does not mean God had previously forgotten her existence. In biblical thought, when God "remembers" someone, it means He moves actively on their behalf according to His promise and covenant mercy. The explicit mention of Hannah in verse 19 serves as the dramatic turning point of the chapter, shifting the storyline from her long years of suffering to the realization of God's favor.

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a meaningful connection between Hannah's name and the name she gives her son, Samuel (Shemu'el). In 1 Samuel 1:20, Hannah names her child Samuel because she asked for him from the Lord. The Hebrew word for "asked" (sha'al) sounds similar to both Samuel and Saul, linking Hannah's prayer directly to the future leadership of Israel.

Hannah's personal experience of divine favor thus became a national turning point. Her son Samuel became the prophet and judge who anointed David as king, demonstrating how God used the quiet, persistent prayer of one woman to shape the history of His people.
