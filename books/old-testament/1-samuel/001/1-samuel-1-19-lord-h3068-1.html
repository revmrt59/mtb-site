LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

The word refers to the proper, personal name of the God of Israel, revealed in Scripture as the eternal, self-existent God who makes promises and remains faithful to His people. In 1 Samuel 1:19, the name identifies the personal God before whom Elkanah and Hannah worshiped and who personally remembered Hannah by answering her prayer for a son.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The personal name of God, transliterated as YHWH, is the central name by which God made Himself known to Israel. When God appeared to Moses at the burning bush, He revealed this personal name to assure His people that He is the ever-present God who sees their suffering and keeps His promises.

The events of 1 Samuel 1 take place during a difficult period in Israel's history near the end of the era of the judges. Spiritual leadership at the tabernacle in Shiloh had grown weak and compromised under the sons of Eli. Yet even during a time of national decline, humble individuals still sought the LORD in sincere prayer. Hannah, grieving her childlessness and the persistent taunts of her rival, brought her deep sorrow directly to the tabernacle to pray to the LORD.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Grammatically, the word is a proper noun identifying the unique personal name of God.

The name is closely connected to the Hebrew verb meaning "to be." It expresses God's self-existence, eternal reality, and ongoing presence. Unlike titles such as Elohim (which means "God" or "deity"), this word functions as God's proper name. It shows that God is not an impersonal force or an abstract idea, but a living person who interacts with human history, speaks to His people, and hears their petitions.

While the personal name is linked with God's faithful character throughout Scripture, the word itself is simply God's proper name. Who God is and how He acts are revealed through the full record of His actions and words across the biblical text.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, God's personal name is used when He acts directly to rescue, guide, or instruct His people. It is the name used in contexts of worship, prayer, and covenant commitment.

Out of deep reverence for the divine name, ancient Jewish tradition began avoiding speaking the personal name aloud, substituting titles such as Adonai ("Lord"). English translations continue a similar tradition by printing the name as "LORD" in capital letters. When readers see "LORD" in the Old Testament, it indicates that the underlying Hebrew text uses God's proper name rather than a general title.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of God's personal name carries important truths for faith and worship:

  • God is personal and accessible: Believers do not pray to an unknown power. God has revealed His name so that His people may call upon Him directly.

  • God is faithful: The name stands as a constant reminder of God's long record of keeping His promises to His people from generation to generation.

  • God notices the individual: Although He is the sovereign God over all creation, He attends to the private burdens and quiet cries of ordinary believers.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:19, Hannah and her husband Elkanah rise early in the morning to worship before the LORD at Shiloh before returning home to Ramah. The text then states that "the LORD remembered her."

In biblical usage, when God "remembers" someone, it does not mean He had previously forgotten them and suddenly recalled their existence. Rather, it means He moves to act on their behalf according to His character and promises.

By using God's personal name in this verse, the writer emphasizes that the birth of Samuel was not a fortunate coincidence or merely a natural event. It was the direct action of the living LORD responding personally to a faithful woman who poured out her heart before Him.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 — "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens."

  • Joshua 14:12 — "Now therefore, give me this mountain of which the LORD spoke in that day..."

  • 2 Samuel 22:2 — "And he said: 'The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer...'"

  • Psalms 25:1 — "To You, O LORD, I lift up my soul."

  • Proverbs 16:6 — "In mercy and truth atonement is provided for iniquity; and by the fear of the LORD one departs from evil."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The distinction between the proper name YHWH (rendered "LORD") and the title Adonai (rendered "Lord") is helpful for Bible readers. While Adonai emphasizes God's mastership and authority, YHWH highlights His personal identity and presence with His people.

In 1 Samuel 1:19, this distinction highlights a striking contrast. While the official priestly leadership at Shiloh was failing, God Himself remained active and accessible. The turnaround in Israel's history began not with a military campaign or political effort, but with the LORD answering the personal prayer of a woman of faith.
