MORNING - (bôqer, Strong's H1242)

Summary:

Boqer refers to the morning, dawn, or the beginning of daylight. In ancient Israelite life, the morning marked the start of daily work, travel, and worship. In 1 Samuel 1:19, the word describes the morning when Hannah and her family arose early to worship the Lord at Shiloh before returning home, reflecting a heart transformed by faith and peace after prayer.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient Near East, daily life followed the natural rhythms of light and darkness. The arrival of morning (boqer) signaled the end of night and the beginning of human labor, domestic duties, business, and religious service. Because the work day began at dawn, early morning was a key time for seeking God, offering sacrifices, and beginning journeys. In Israelite society, starting the morning with worship placed the day ahead into the Lord's hands.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun boqer means "morning," "dawn," or "daybreak." It refers specifically to the time when light breaks through the darkness at the start of a new day. Depending on the setting, it can refer to the first light of dawn, the early hours of the day, or the morning period as a whole.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses boqer in several consistent ways:

  • Creation Rhythms: The word marks the completion of each day's cycle in the creation account, pairing evening with morning.

  • Sacred Rhythms & Sacrifices: God designated the morning for specific sanctuary duties, including the daily morning offerings.

  • Times of Action & Promptness: Rising early in the morning often indicates readiness, obedience, or urgency in carrying out God's directions or setting out on a journey.

  • Prayer & Devotion: The morning serves as a dedicated time for believers to bring their petitions and praises before God at the start of the day.

4. Theological Implications

The morning carries rich spiritual significance in the Old Testament. It represents God's gift of a new day, fresh mercy, and divine order over creation. Darkness gives way to light, reminding God's people that distress and night do not have the final word. Gathering for worship or prayer in the morning acknowledges God's authority over time and puts the Lord first in daily life.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:19, boqer describes the morning after Hannah poured out her soul before the Lord in Shiloh. Grammatically, the word is a singular common noun, pointing to that specific morning following her time of prayer at the tabernacle.

reached Shiloh brokenhearted, weeping bitterly because of her barrenness and the painful taunts of her rival. After praying honestly and receiving a word of blessing from Eli the priest, her countenance changed and she was no longer sad. Rising early the next morning to worship before the Lord shows that her peace was genuine. Before there was any visible answer to her prayer, she and her family spent the early morning in worship. The use of boqer here highlights a fresh morning marked by trust and praise, showing that true worship springs from faith in God's character rather than waiting for visible proof.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 22:3 — "So Abraham rose early in the morning and saddled his donkey..."

  • Exodus 16:7 — "And in the morning you shall see the glory of the LORD..."

  • Judges 6:28 — "And when the men of the city arose early in the morning, there was the altar of Baal, torn down..."

  • Psalms 5:3 — "My voice You shall hear in the morning, O LORD; in the morning I will direct it to You, and I will look up."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's morning worship provides a powerful contrast to her earlier grief. On the previous day, she was overwhelmed with sorrow and could not eat. By the following morning, after entrusting her heart to God, her entire outlook was transformed. She ate, her face was no longer downcast, and she joined her family in morning praise. This shows how bringing our heavy burdens to the Lord changes our morning from a time of anxiety into a time of praise.
