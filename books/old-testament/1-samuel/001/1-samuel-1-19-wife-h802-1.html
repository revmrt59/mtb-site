WIFE - (ʼishshâh, Strong's H802)

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'ishshah refers to a female human being, rendered as either "woman" or "wife" depending on whether a marital relationship is indicated. In 1 Samuel 1:19, the word identifies Hannah's distinct relational position as Elkanah's wife ("Hannah his wife"). By describing Hannah as Elkanah's wife in the immediate context of their union and the Lord's intervention, the word highlights the legal and intimate bond of marriage through which God brings about the birth of Samuel.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The book of 1 Samuel opens during a period of spiritual and domestic trial in Israel. The priesthood at Shiloh was corrupt under Eli's sons, and Israel lacked faithful leadership. Within this national crisis, the narrative focuses on a quiet household in the hill country of Ephraim. Elkanah had two wives, Peninnah and Hannah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah was barren and suffered deeply under Peninnah's ongoing provocation.

After years of sorrow, Hannah prayed fervently at the tabernacle in Shiloh, pouring out her soul before the Lord and asking for a son whom she would dedicate to divine service. After receiving a word of peace from Eli, Hannah returned with her husband Elkanah to their home in Ramah. 1 Samuel 1:19 records the decisive turn in her story: Elkanah and Hannah worshipped God in the morning, returned home, and Elkanah knew Hannah his wife, leading to the divine remembrance that answered her prayer.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of 'ishshah is a female person. In Hebrew, the word serves as the standard general term for a woman, as well as the specific term for a married woman or wife. Context determines which English word best captures the meaning. When the word appears without a possessive suffix or context of marriage, it usually means "woman." When it is connected to a specific man through syntax or context—such as "his wife" or "wife of"—it designates a married spouse.

The word itself carries grammatical feminine gender and singular number in 1 Samuel 1:19. It directly designates Hannah as Elkanah's wedded partner rather than merely an unnamed female member of the household.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, 'ishshah is used to describe women in every station of life. It identifies individual women, wives within household covenants, mothers, widows, and female members of the community.

When used for a wife, the word highlights the established marital bond between a man and a woman. Hebrew does not possess a separate technical vocabulary word for "spouse" or "bride" distinct from "woman." Instead, the simple term for woman takes on full marital significance when joined to a husband. The word thus anchors marriage in the fundamental distinction and complement created between male and female from the beginning of human history.

4. Theological Implications

In Old Testament narrative, God frequently accomplishes His redemption through the everyday structures of family and marriage. While Hannah's barrenness was a heavy burden, God's ultimate blessing was not brought about through a miraculous creation out of nothing, but through the normal, God-ordained relationship of marriage.

Elkanah's intimacy with Hannah his wife was the human act through which God answered her prayer. The narrative balances human faithfulness within marriage and sovereign divine intervention: Elkanah knew his wife, and the Lord remembered her. God honors the institution of marriage as the proper setting for childbearing and family life, working through faithful human relationships to fulfill His overarching plans for His people.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:19, the term 'ishshah appears at a vital transition point in the opening chapter. Up to this point, the text has repeatedly highlighted Hannah's distress, her rival's harsh treatment, and her husband's attempt to comfort her. In verse 19, after Hannah has cast her burden on the Lord and worshipped Him at Shiloh, the narrative shifts toward resolution.

The phrase "Elkanah knew Hannah his wife" establishes the order and holiness of their home life following their corporate worship. It presents Elkanah and Hannah acting together in covenant harmony. The term "wife" underscores Hannah's rightful position in Elkanah's home, affirming her dignity despite her former years of childlessness. Through this recognized marital union, God displays His grace by remembering Hannah and granting her the child she requested.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The phrase "and the LORD remembered her" directly follows the mention of Elkanah knowing Hannah his wife. In Scripture, when God "remembers" someone, it does not mean He had forgotten them; rather, it means He is moving to act on their behalf according to His covenant mercy. By placing this divine action alongside the physical union of husband and wife, the text illustrates how divine sovereignty and human action work together. God answered Hannah's personal tearful plea, and in doing so, provided Israel with Samuel, the prophet who would anoint King David and herald a new era in salvation history.
