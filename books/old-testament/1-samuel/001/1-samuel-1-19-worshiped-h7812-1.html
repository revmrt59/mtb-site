WORSHIPED - (shâchâh, Strong's H7812)

Summary:

The word shachah fundamentally means to bow down, low to the ground, as a physical posture of submission, honor, and reverence. When directed toward God, it describes the act of worship wherein a person acknowledges His authority and supreme majesty. In 1 Samuel 1:19, the word describes Elkanah and his household rising early to honor the LORD at the tabernacle before returning home, signaling their humble submission to God after Hannah's prayer.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient Near East, personal posture communicated social and spiritual status. To bow low or lay face down before a king, superior, or deity was a standard physical demonstration of allegiance and submission.

In Israelite life, true worship was expressed through both outward posture and inward allegiance. The sanctuary at Shiloh served as the central place where God's people brought offerings, made vows, and bowed before the LORD. The background of 1 Samuel 1 takes place during a dark spiritual period in Israel's history, yet Elkanah's family faithfully made annual trips to Shiloh to offer sacrifices and bow before the Almighty.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to the physical act of bowing down, prostrating oneself on the ground, or showing deep homage to a ruler or superior. When directed toward the God of Israel, it means to worship Him as the sovereign Lord.

  • When a person bows before a human superior (such as a king, prophet, or leader), the word describes an act of respect, honor, or submission.

  • When a person bows before God, it refers to worship—the deliberate humbling of oneself to recognize God's holiness, authority, and divine presence.

  • When turned toward false gods, the word describes the sin of idolatry, where honor due to the Creator is directed toward created things.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses shachah in a variety of settings, ranging from standard social politeness between human beings to the highest expressions of sacred devotion.

The physical posture underlying the word is essential to its biblical nuance. It is not merely an abstract mental concept; it involves lowliness and physical lowering. To bow down is to voluntarily forfeit one's stature in the presence of someone greater. When combined with prayer or sacrifice, shachah emphasizes that approaching God requires humility and reverence.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical concept of worship centers on God's unique authority and glory. Because God alone is Creator and Redeemer, He alone is worthy of ultimate devotion.

When Scripture describes people bowing before the LORD, it highlights the vast distinction between the holy God and frail humanity. Worship in the Old Testament is never presented as an equal exchange between God and human beings. Instead, it is the creature's natural and proper response to the Creator. Physical prostration represents the surrender of self-will and the recognition that human lives depend entirely upon God's sovereign grace.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:19, the word describes the morning routine of Elkanah's family before leaving Shiloh. Hannah had previously been in deep anguish over her childlessness, weeping bitter tears and making a vow to the LORD. After receiving a word of peace from Eli the priest, Hannah's countenance changed, and her sorrow lifted.

The family's decision to bow before the LORD early the next morning highlights their corporate devotion. Before packing up to travel back to Ramah, they prioritize worshiping God at the tabernacle. The grammatical form in this verse presents the action as a plural, masculine movement in narrative sequence, showing that the family acted together in worshiping before the LORD. This collective act of reverence serves as a quiet turning point in the story, directly preceding the statement that the LORD remembered Hannah.

  • Ruth 2:10 — "So she fell on her face, bowed down to the ground, and said to him, 'Why have I found favor in your eyes...?'"

  • 1 Samuel 15:25 — "Now therefore, please pardon my sin, and return with me, that I may worship the LORD."

  • 2 Samuel 24:20 — "Then Araunah looked and saw the king and his servants coming toward him. So Araunah went out and bowed before the king with his face to the ground."

  • Jeremiah 8:2 — "They shall spread them before the sun and the moon and all the host of heaven, which they have loved and which they have served and after which they have walked, which they have sought and which they have worshiped."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The location of this act of worship in 1 Samuel 1:19—"before the LORD"—refers specifically to the presence of God at the sanctuary where the ark and the tabernacle rested. Worshiping "before the LORD" indicates that their reverence was directed intentionally toward the dwelling place of God's name.

Furthermore, the placement of this act of worship immediately before Hannah conceives Samuel demonstrates that worship is not contingent upon receiving the final outcome of a request. Hannah and Elkanah worshiped God while the fulfillment of Hannah's prayer was still in the future, establishing a pattern of faith that honors God for who He is before His work is fully seen.
