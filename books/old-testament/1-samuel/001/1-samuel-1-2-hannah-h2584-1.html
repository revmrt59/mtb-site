HANNAH - (Channâh, Strong's H2584)

Summary:

The name Hannah is a feminine Hebrew proper noun derived from a root meaning "favor" or "grace." In 1 Samuel 1:2, it identifies one of the two wives of Elkanah at the beginning of the Samuel narrative. By introducing Hannah immediately alongside her initial childlessness, the verse establishes her historical identity and sets up the narrative of God's sovereign answer to her grief and prayer.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The opening of 1 Samuel presents the family line of Elkanah, an Ephraimite residing in the hill country of Ephraim. The narrative introduces his household by identifying his two wives: Hannah and Peninnah. This setting reflects the end of the period of the judges, a time marked by spiritual decay in Israel and domestic friction within households.

In ancient Near Eastern culture, a woman's social standing and personal security were heavily linked to her ability to bear children. By opening the narrative with the names and childbearing status of Elkanah's two wives, the author sets the stage for the personal crisis that drives the early chapters of the book. Hannah's name introduces a key historical figure whose personal anguish at Shiloh becomes the context through which God brings forth Samuel, the prophet who would anoint Israel's early kings.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name Hannah (transliterated as Channah) is a proper feminine noun. Lexically, it derives from the Hebrew verbal root chanan, which means "to show favor," "be gracious," or "grant mercy." As a personal name, Hannah literally translates to "grace" or "favored one."

In Hebrew narrative prose, proper personal names serve primarily to identify specific historical individuals. While the underlying root relates to favor, the name itself functions grammatically in the text as a direct personal name rather than an abstract noun for grace.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament narrative, personal names frequently reflect verbal roots associated with divine qualities or human hopes. In the books of Samuel, the name Hannah refers exclusively to this historical woman, the mother of the prophet Samuel.

The text presents Hannah through her personal interactions, her deep emotional burden, her earnest prayer at the tabernacle in Shiloh, and her faithful fulfillment of her vow. While the narrative relates how God granted her request, the word itself simply identifies her as Elkanah's wife and Samuel's future mother.

4. Theological Implications

While the proper name itself functions as an identifier, the surrounding narrative develops profound theological truths:

  • God hears the needy: Hannah's experience demonstrates that God pays attention to the individual suffering of His people. Her quiet, heartbroken prayer in the tabernacle was recognized and answered by the Lord.

  • Divine sovereignty over life: The narrative establishes that human life and barrenness are within God's sovereign hand. The resolution of her childlessness highlights God's power to fulfill His promises.

  • The exaltation of the humble: Hannah's song of praise in 1 Samuel 2 contrasts human pride with divine power, establishing a major canonical theme: God opposes the proud but exalts the lowly.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:2, the text states, "And he had two wives: the name of one was Hannah, and the name of the other Peninnah." The structure of the verse immediately introduces a stark contrast between the two women: "Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no children."

In this initial mention, Hannah is introduced without prior background, placing her immediately in a vulnerable position within her household. Her childlessness created deep distress, made worse by Peninnah's provocation. The author introduces her by name at the very start of the narrative to focus the reader's attention on her journey from barrenness and grief to divine answer and joy.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Samuel 1:5 — "but to Hannah he would give a double portion, for he loved Hannah, although the LORD had closed her womb."

  • 1 Samuel 1:19 — "Then they rose early in the morning and worshiped before the LORD, and returned and came to their house at Ramah. And Elkanah knew Hannah his wife, and the LORD remembered her."

  • 1 Samuel 1:20 — "So it came to pass in the process of time that Hannah conceived and bore a son, and called his name Samuel, saying, 'Because I have asked for him from the LORD.'"

  • 1 Samuel 2:1 — "And Hannah prayed and said: 'My heart rejoices in the LORD, my horn is exalted in the LORD. I smile at my enemies, because I rejoice in Your salvation.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's story marks a crucial transition in Israel's history. Her personal deliverance directly connects to national redemption, as her son Samuel would shepherd Israel out of the chaotic period of the judges and establish the Davidic monarchy. Furthermore, Hannah's prayer in 1 Samuel 2 serves as a direct thematic model for Mary's song of praise (the Magnificat) in Luke 1, linking the birth of Samuel to the broader biblical story of salvation in Jesus Christ.
