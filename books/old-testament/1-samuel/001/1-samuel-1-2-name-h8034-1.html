NAME - (shêm, Strong's H8034)

Summary:

The word refers to an identifying name, proper designation, reputation, or memorial. In 1 Samuel 1:2, it introduces Hannah by name, establishing her personal identity at the beginning of a narrative where God turns personal distress into national blessing.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israelite society, a person's name (shem) carried far more weight than a mere label for identification. A name was intimately tied to a person's identity, family line, individual dignity, and standing within the community. Preserving a family name through descendants was considered essential for maintaining a place within the inheritance of Israel.

The Book of 1 Samuel opens during a spiritually low period in Israel's history, near the end of the time of the judges. Instead of beginning with battles or political leaders, the narrative opens within a quiet, domestic setting in the mountains of Ephraim. By introducing Elkanah's household through the specific names of his family members, the passage grounds the unfolding story of salvation in real historical people experiencing personal struggles.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun shem refers primarily to a proper name or explicit title used to identify a person, place, or object. Depending on how it is used, the word can refer to:

  • A proper name given to an individual, city, or nation.

  • Reputation, fame, or public standing (having a "great name").

  • A memorial or mark of remembrance preserved across generations.

  • The revealed character, presence, or authority of God when used of the Divine Name.

In 1 Samuel 1:2, the word carries its fundamental sense of a personal proper name, identifying the first of Elkanah's two wives.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, shem is used in several distinct ways:

First, it is used for basic identification. It distinguishes one individual or location from another, as seen when places, rivers, or historical figures are introduced.

Second, it relates to lineage and memory. To have one's name remembered meant having children to carry on the family legacy, while having a name cut off was considered a severe tragedy.

Third, it represents character and authority. When applied to God, His "name" stands for His revealed nature, His holiness, and His active presence among His people. Worshipping God, calling upon Him, or speaking in His authority is often described as acting in His name.

4. Theological Implications

The narrative use of personal names in Scripture highlights that God works through specific, individual lives. God does not interact with humanity only in vague, national terms; He knows and calls individuals by name.

Hannah's initial circumstance presents a painful contrast. Though she bears a personal name, her childlessness threatens to leave her without a lasting legacy in Israel. Her rival, Peninnah, has children, while Hannah faces the social stigma and deep sorrow of barrenness. Yet God notices Hannah in her distress. By beginning the story with Hannah's name, Scripture emphasizes that God sees the humble and forgotten, using her personal grief as the catalyst to bring forth Samuel, the prophet who would anoint Israel's kings.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:2, shem appears as a singular common noun introducing the two wives of Elkanah: "the name of one was Hannah, and the name of the other Peninnah." The specific target occurrence identifies Hannah.

By naming both women directly at the very start, the narrative gives each woman an explicit place in the unfolding account rather than treating them as anonymous background figures. The text immediately follows their names with their distinct life situations: Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no children.

This stark contrast sets up the dramatic tension of the chapter. Hannah's name—which means "grace" or "favor"—stands in quiet tension with her current distress, anticipating the gracious answer God will give to her heartfelt prayer.

  • Exodus 28:9 — "Then you shall take two onyx stones and engrave on them the names of the sons of Israel."

  • Isaiah 43:7 — "Everyone who is called by My name, Whom I have created for My glory; I have formed him, yes, I have made him."

  • Daniel 9:18 — "O my God, incline Your ear and hear; open Your eyes and see our desolations, and the city which is called by Your name..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The structural placement of shem in 1 Samuel 1:2 mirrors classic Old Testament narrative openings. When a narrator introduces characters by name before describing their crisis, it signals to the reader that these specific individuals will play an essential role in the upcoming story.

Furthermore, Hannah's prominence in the opening chapters demonstrates how God elevates those who seem powerless in the eyes of society. Peninnah possessed children and immediate earthly security, yet it was Hannah's earnest prayer and vow that God used to reshape the nation of Israel through the birth of Samuel.
