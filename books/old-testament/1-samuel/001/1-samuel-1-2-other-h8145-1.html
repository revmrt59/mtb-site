OTHER - (shênîy, Strong's H8145)

Summary:

The Hebrew word sheni functions as an ordinal number or modifying adjective meaning "second," "other," or "another." It designates the second item in a series, a corresponding counterpart, or an additional person or object. In 1 Samuel 1:2, sheni identifies Peninnah as Elkanah's second wife alongside Hannah. By introducing the two wives in sequence, the word establishes the household structure that immediately becomes the source of intense domestic friction and personal sorrow, setting the historical stage for Hannah's prayer and the birth of Samuel.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word sheni is derived from a root associated with the number two or repeating an action. In ancient Hebrew usage, ordinal numbers counted steps, days, ritual objects, or individuals in social and family sequences. Rather than merely giving a total count, ordinal words like sheni establish order, rank, pairing, or succession.

When used in family contexts, sequential numbering frequently identified birth order or marriage arrangement. In the opening chapter of 1 Samuel, Israel stood in a transitional period spiritually and politically. Elkanah’s household reflects the complex domestic reality of polygamy in the Old Testament, where adding a second wife often brought rivalry, personal grief, and household division.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

sheni primarily means "second" or "other." It specifies a person or item following the first in a listed order. Depending on the context, the word can refer to:

  • The second unit in a sequence of time or creation (such as the "second" day or "second" year).

  • A second or corresponding object in a paired arrangement (such as the "other" side or "other" offering).

  • An additional person in a family or administrative listing (a "second" wife or "another" brother).

  • A repeated action or second occurrence ("again" or a "second" time).

In 1 Samuel 1:2, the word appears in a feminine singular form to refer specifically to the second wife in Elkanah's household, Peninnah.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, sheni serves several clear structural functions. First, it marks temporal progression, such as the second day of creation or the second year of a ruler's reign. Second, it identifies complementary architectural elements, such as matching sides of the tabernacle or sanctuary curtains. Third, it distinguishes individual people within a group, such as a second-born son or a second partner in a marriage.

The word itself carries a straightforward numerical sense, but its placement in narrative accounts frequently highlights contrast or pairing. When two individuals are listed as the "first" and the "second," Scripture often draws attention to the relational dynamic between them—whether harmony, comparison, or conflict.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of sheni in biblical narratives often sets human order against divine purpose. While human sequence names one person first and another second, God frequently works outside expected human arrangements.

Throughout biblical history, the second-born child or the less prominent partner often becomes the focal point of God's redemptive work. Human counting establishes sequence and social rank, but God acts according to His sovereign grace rather than human priority. Furthermore, the mention of a second wife in Elkanah's home highlights the painful consequences of departing from God's creation model for marriage, showing how human marital arrangements produce heartache, while God nevertheless works through broken situations to accomplish His saving plans.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:2, sheni appears in the opening description of Elkanah’s family: "the name of one was Hannah, and the name of the other Peninnah." Hannah is mentioned first, reflecting her primary place in Elkanah’s affection, while Peninnah is introduced second.

However, the narrative immediately presents a painful contrast: Peninnah had children, but Hannah had none. The structural word sheni links the two women together in a single domestic setting where comparison was constant. Peninnah's role as the "other" wife led to ongoing provocation and grief for Hannah, especially during the annual trips to worship at Shiloh. The word thus sets up the domestic frame through which Hannah's suffering is understood. Through this heavy trial, God remembered Hannah and provided Samuel, demonstrating that the Lord sees the distress of the afflicted.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The feminine singular form of sheni in 1 Samuel 1:2 grammatically modifies the identification of Peninnah as the second wife, keeping the focus on the distinct identities of the two women. The opening verses of 1 Samuel carefully contrast Hannah and Peninnah to prepare the reader for Hannah's song of praise in chapter 2, where she celebrates how God lifts up the needy and gives children to the barren. The humble, painful circumstances of the second wife's rivalry become the backdrop for displaying God's mercy and power.
