ASKED - (shâʼal, Strong's H7592)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb sha'al means to ask, request, inquire, demand, or petition. It is used throughout the Old Testament for ordinary questions between people, official inquiries for guidance, and personal petitions brought before God. In 1 Samuel 1:20, Hannah uses this word when naming her newborn son Samuel, testifying that she asked for him from the LORD. Here the word highlights Hannah's earnest prayer in her time of deep distress and acknowledges God as the gracious giver who heard and answered her petition.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The verb sha'al is the standard Hebrew word for asking or seeking something from another person or from God. In ancient Israel, asking was an essential part of everyday life. People asked one another for news, requested favors from neighbors, and sought decisions from leaders. In public and spiritual life, kings and priests used the word when seeking divine guidance before going to war or making critical national choices.

The word covers a broad range of human interaction, from simple everyday conversations to the highest moments of worship. Whether a person was asking a question, begging for help, requesting a gift, or inquiring of God, sha'al expressed the act of turning to another to receive an answer or supply a need.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can mean to ask, request, demand, or inquire, depending on who is speaking and what is being sought.

When people speak to one another, the word means asking a question, requesting a personal favor, or asking to borrow an item. When used in official or military settings, it means inquiring of God for direction or seeking counsel from a leader. When a worshiper comes before God in prayer, it means making a specific petition or placing a personal request before Him.

In 1 Samuel 1:20, the word means requesting or petitioning. Hannah reflects on her intense time of prayer at the tabernacle, declaring that her son came as a direct result of her asking the LORD for a child.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses sha'al in several common situations:

  • Seeking personal information or health news: People used the word when asking about the welfare of family members or travelers.

  • Requesting goods or favors: The word describes asking for gifts, permission, or assistance from someone with the resources to help.

  • Inquiring for divine guidance: Leaders and prophets asked the LORD for direction before major decisions, often seeking a clear "yes" or "no" answer regarding battle strategies.

  • Praying with direct petitions: Worshipers used the word to describe laying their specific burdens, needs, and longings before God.

The surrounding context shows whether sha'al carries the tone of a casual question, a humble request, a royal demand, or a deeply emotional prayer.

4. Theological Implications

The use of sha'al in prayer shows that God invites His people to bring their explicit requests to Him. Asking God is an act of humble dependence. It acknowledges that human effort alone cannot meet every need and that God is the true provider of life, help, and direction.

When people ask God for help, their request reflects faith in His power and willingness to listen. God does not need human information, but He desires His people to express their dependence on Him through prayer. At the same time, the verb sha'al itself simply describes the act of asking; it does not guarantee that every human request will be granted in the exact way requested. The certainty of an answer rests on God's wise and faithful character, not merely on the act of asking.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:20, Hannah gives birth to a son and names him Samuel. She explains the name by saying, "Because I have asked him from the LORD."

Hannah had lived with years of sorrow and childlessness. During her visit to the tabernacle in Shiloh, she wept bitterly and poured out her heart, asking God to give her a male child. The grammatical form here is a singular perfect form ("I have asked"), presenting her petition as a completed personal request that she brought directly to the LORD.

Naming her child based on this verb created a lifelong testimony. Every time Samuel's name was spoken, Hannah and her household were reminded that this child was not the product of chance or human self-sufficiency, but a gracious gift given because a grieving woman asked God for help and God listened.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 24:47 – "Then I asked her, and said, 'Whose daughter are you?'"

  • 1 Samuel 1:17 – "Then Eli answered and said, 'Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition which you have asked of Him.'"

  • 2 Samuel 5:23 – "Therefore David inquired of the LORD, He said, 'You shall not go up; circle around behind them...'"

  • 1 Kings 3:13 – "And I have also given you what you have not asked: both riches and honor..."

  • Psalms 40:6 – "Sacrifice and offering You did not desire; my ears You have opened. Burnt offering and sin offering You have not required."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The narrative in 1 Samuel 1 contains a striking wordplay involving sha'al. The name Samuel (Shemu'el) sounds similar to the Hebrew phrasing for "asked of God." Furthermore, in 1 Samuel 1:27–28, Hannah uses forms of sha'al repeatedly: "For this child I prayed, and the LORD has granted my petition which I asked of Him. Therefore I also have lent him to the LORD; as long as he lives he shall be lent to the LORD."

Later in Israel's history, the nation would ask (sha'al) for a king, leading to the appointment of Saul, whose name (Sha'ul) literally means "asked for." The repetition of this verb across the early chapters of 1 Samuel contrasts Hannah's humble, faithful asking with the nation's later worldly demand for a king.
