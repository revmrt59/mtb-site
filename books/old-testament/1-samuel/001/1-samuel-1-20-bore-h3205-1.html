BORE - (yâlad, Strong's H3205)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yalad basic meaning is to bear, give birth to, bring forth, or beget offspring. It describes the physical ushering of new human life into the world, whether through a mother giving birth or a father fathering a child. In 1 Samuel 1:20, the word describes Hannah giving birth to her son Samuel, marking the direct, visible fulfillment of God answering her tearful prayer and turning her long struggle with childlessness into praise.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The verb yalad is the primary Old Testament word for childbearing and family lineage. In ancient Israel, children were seen as a blessing from the Lord and essential for preserving the family line and covenant inheritance. Childlessness was often accompanied by intense personal grief and social distress.

In the opening chapter of 1 Samuel, the narrative sets up a contrast between Peninnah, who had children, and Hannah, who was childless. Hannah suffered severe emotional anguish and provoked taunting, yet she took her grief directly to God at the tabernacle in Shiloh. When God remembered Hannah, her deep sorrow gave way to the joy of motherhood. The birth of her child was not merely a private family event; it marked a crucial turning point in Israel's history as God raised up Samuel to serve as prophet, priest, and judge.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word yalad describes the process of childbearing and lineage.

  • When a woman is the subject, it means to bear a child, give birth, or go through labor.

  • When a man is the subject, it means to father or beget children.

  • In legal and genealogical settings, it establishes biological descent and family relationships.

  • In figurative contexts, it describes bringing forth or producing an outcome, such as a day bringing forth unexpected events.

Here in 1 Samuel 1:20, the word refers to the physical birth of a child after a completed period of pregnancy.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament narrative, yalad carries strong covenantal weight. It frequently appears in family records and birth stories that show God preserving His people across generations.

The word frequently appears in stories where God intervenes to open a closed womb. When Scripture records that a previously barren woman gave birth, the focus centers on God's power and faithfulness. Human ability alone does not create life; God remains the giver of every child.

4. Theological Implications

The use of yalad in Hannah's story highlights several vital truths about God's character and care:

  • God hears the prayer of the brokenhearted. Hannah was deeply distressed, but her grief moved her to persistent prayer. God heard her cry and granted her petition.

  • God holds ultimate authority over human life. The birth of a child serves as a tangible reminder that life is a divine gift.

  • God works through ordinary family events to accomplish His redemptive plans. The arrival of Samuel answered Hannah's personal prayer while simultaneously providing Israel with a leader who would guide the nation toward God's chosen king.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:20, yalad appears in the feminine, singular, wayyiqtol imperfect form. This grammatical form advances the story directly from God's action to Hannah's response: after the Lord remembered her, Hannah conceived, completed the time of pregnancy, and gave birth to a son.

The immediate context emphasizes the connection between prayer and fulfillment. Hannah named her son Samuel, declaring that she had asked him of the Lord. The act of bearing the boy provided tangible proof that God had not forgotten her. Her childlessness was resolved, her dignity restored, and her vow preserved as she prepared to return the child to the Lord's service once he was weaned.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's experience of childbearing mirrors earlier covenant mothers like Sarah and Rachel, who also faced long seasons of childlessness before God acted on their behalf. In each situation, the birth of a child revealed that God's timing is perfect and that He works through human weakness to demonstrate His power.

Furthermore, Hannah's physical act of bearing Samuel leads directly into her song of praise in 1 Samuel 2. Her quiet endurance and answered prayer produced a hymn of thanksgiving that celebrates God as the one who raises the poor from the dust and gives children to the barren.
