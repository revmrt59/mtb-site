CONCEIVED - (hârâh, Strong's H2029)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb hārah means to conceive or become pregnant. It describes the physical beginning of life in the womb. In 1 Samuel 1:20, the word marks the joyful moment when God answered Hannah's long years of prayer and barrenness, resulting in her pregnancy with Samuel.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The first chapter of 1 Samuel opens with a family in distress. Elkanah had two wives, Peninnah and Hannah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah was childless because her womb was closed. Year after year, Peninnah provoked Hannah bitterly to make her miserable. During an annual pilgrimage to worship the Lord at Shiloh, Hannah was in deep sorrow, weeping and unable to eat.

In her anguish, Hannah prayed directly to God, asking Him to look on her affliction and remember her. She made a vow that if God gave her a male child, she would give him back to the Lord all the days of his life. Eli the priest initially mistook her quiet, desperate praying for drunkenness, but upon learning her genuine heart, he sent her away with a blessing. Hannah left with peace in her heart, and soon after, God answered her prayer through the birth of Samuel.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of hārah is to conceive or become pregnant. It refers directly to the biological start of pregnancy when a woman carries a child in her womb.

While the word usually describes human pregnancy, Scripture occasionally uses it in a figurative sense. In those poetic or wisdom contexts, a person is described as conceiving trouble, mischief, or lies in the heart before bringing forth evil actions. However, in narrative texts like 1 Samuel, the word carries its plain physical meaning: a woman becoming pregnant with a child.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In Old Testament narrative, hārah appears regularly in family records and birth accounts. It often forms a familiar sequence with marital relations and childbirth: a husband knows his wife, she conceives (hārah), and she gives birth to a child.

The word carries special emotional and narrative weight when it follows a prolonged season of barrenness. When Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel, Manoah's wife, or Hannah are said to conceive, the word signals a major turning point in the story. It marks the precise transition from human helplessness and sorrow to divine action and fruitfulness.

4. Theological Implications

In the biblical worldview, human reproduction involves both natural human relations and divine blessing. Scripture repeatedly presents the womb as under God's sovereign oversight. Human effort alone cannot produce life if God does not open the womb.

When the biblical narrative notes that a woman conceived, especially after years of barrenness, it highlights God's kindness and power. The word demonstrates that God hears the cries of the afflicted, remembers His people, and brings life out of human impossibility.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:20, the verb hārah is framed in the feminine singular, imperfect with wayyiqtol form, expressing the next sequential event in the story. Verse 19 records that Elkanah knew his wife Hannah, and "the LORD remembered her." Verse 20 immediately follows by stating that in process of time Hannah conceived and bore a son.

The placement of the word right after "the LORD remembered her" connects human pregnancy directly to divine faithfulness. Hannah’s pregnancy was not an accident of nature; it was God's direct answer to her persistent prayer. The name she chose for her son, Samuel, means "Heard by God," serving as a lifelong reminder of the moment she conceived in response to God remembering her humble petition.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 4:1 — "Now Adam knew Eve his wife, and she conceived and bore Cain, and said, 'I have acquired a man from the LORD.'"

  • Genesis 25:21 — "Now Isaac pleaded with the LORD for his wife, because she was barren; and the LORD granted his plea, and Rebekah his wife conceived."

  • Exodus 2:2 — "So the woman conceived and bore a son. And when she saw that he was a beautiful child, she hid him three months."

  • Hosea 1:6 — "And she conceived again and bore a daughter. Then God said to him: 'Call her name Lo-Ruhamah, for I will no longer have mercy on the house of Israel, but I will utterly take them away.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The structure of 1 Samuel 1 provides a comforting pattern for believers facing delayed answers or suffering. Hannah moved from bitterness of soul to honest prayer, from honest prayer to peaceful faith, and finally from faithful endurance to fulfilled promise when she conceived. Her experience demonstrates that God often works through long seasons of waiting to accomplish His larger purpose in salvation history.
