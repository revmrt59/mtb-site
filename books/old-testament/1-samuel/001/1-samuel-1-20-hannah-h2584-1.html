HANNAH - (Channâh, Strong's H2584)

Summary:

Hannah is a Hebrew proper name meaning "favor" or "grace." Derived from a root associated with unmerited kindness and merciful help, the name belongs to the faithful, once-barren mother of the prophet Samuel. In 1 Samuel 1:20, the name Hannah identifies the woman whose persistent prayer was answered by God through the birth of a son. Her name highlights the shift from her painful trial of childlessness to the realization of divine favor when the LORD remembered her and granted her petition.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The name Hannah belongs to the wife of Elkanah who lived in Ramathaim Zophim in the mountains of Ephraim. Grammatically, the target word is a feminine proper noun. Historically, her story takes place during the end of the period of the Judges, a time marked by widespread spiritual decay, weak leadership, and national disorder in Israel. Against this chaotic backdrop, the narrative introduces a quiet household marked by deep personal suffering and earnest devotion.

Hannah was childless, while Elkanah's other wife, Peninnah, had children and provoked Hannah severely year after year. Hannah took her bitter grief to the tabernacle at Shiloh, pouring out her soul in quiet, fervent prayer before God. The high priest Eli initially mistook her silent movement of lips for drunkenness, but upon learning of her distress, he spoke a blessing over her. Hannah believed God, returned home, and in due time gave birth to Samuel, fulfilling her vow by dedicating him to the LORD's service for life.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name Hannah comes from a Hebrew root that means to show favor, be gracious, or extend mercy. As a proper name, it signifies "grace," "favor," or "gracious one."

Names in the ancient Near East often carried descriptive significance, pointing to character, circumstance, or divine action. In Hannah's case, her name reflects both her posture as a humble petitioner for God's mercy and the defining action of God in her life. Though her story begins in sorrow and reproach, it culminates in a demonstration of divine favor that altered not only her personal life but also the spiritual history of Israel.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses this specific proper name exclusively for this person in the Old Testament. Her name appears throughout the opening chapters of 1 Samuel, where she moves from barrenness and torment to joy and praise.

The narrative uses her name to trace a clear progression:

  • Hannah in distress: tormented by her rival and misunderstood by her husband while she wept before the LORD.

  • Hannah in prayer: making a specific vow to dedicate any male child to God's service.

  • Hannah vindicated: remembered by God, conceiving, and bearing a child.

  • Hannah in worship: singing a hymn of thanksgiving that celebrates God's power to lift the needy and humble the proud.

Her name thus becomes associated with faithful endurance, earnest prayer, and the transformative power of divine favor.

4. Theological Implications

The narrative centered on Hannah highlights several essential truths about God's character and working:

  • God hears the afflicted: Hannah's story demonstrates that God pays attention to the brokenhearted and listens to the prayers of the lowly.

  • Sovereign grace in redemption: Israel's transition from the chaotic period of the Judges to the kingdom under David began not with a military victory, but with God's gracious answer to a humble woman's prayer.

  • God reverses human fortunes: Hannah's experience illustrates a recurring biblical theme where God brings life from barrenness, strength from weakness, and honor from shame.

Her life demonstrates that human helplessness is the setting in which divine grace shines most clearly.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:20, the name Hannah marks the climax of the immediate plot. Up to this point, the story emphasizes her long season of waiting, her earnest vow, and her quiet departure from Shiloh after receiving Eli's blessing.

Verse 20 records the turning point: in the process of time, Hannah conceived and bore a son. By naming her as the person who conceives, gives birth, and names her child, the text underscores the direct fulfillment of her prayer. Her name in this verse stands as a testimony to answered prayer—the gracious turn of events brought about when the LORD remembered her petition.

  • 1 Samuel 1:5 — "But to Hannah he would give a double portion, for he loved Hannah, although the LORD had closed her womb."

  • 1 Samuel 1:15 — "But Hannah answered and said, 'No, my lord, I am a woman of sorrowful spirit. I have drunk neither wine nor intoxicating drink, but have poured out my soul before the LORD.'"

  • 1 Samuel 2:1 — "And Hannah prayed and said: 'My heart rejoices in the LORD; my horn is exalted in the LORD. I smile at my enemies, because I rejoice in Your salvation.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's prayer in 1 Samuel 2 serves as a prominent literary and theological foundation for the New Testament. Her song of praise shares striking structural and thematic parallels with Mary’s song in the Gospel of Luke. Both women celebrate God's mercy to the humble, His power over the mighty, and His gracious intervention on behalf of His people.

Furthermore, the naming of Samuel in 1 Samuel 1:20 reinforces the theme of grace. The name Samuel means "Heard by God," serving as a permanent memorial of the gracious favor ("Hannah") requested and the God who answered.
