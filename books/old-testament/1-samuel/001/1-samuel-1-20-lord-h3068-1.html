LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

The word is the personal, proper name of the God of Israel. Unlike general terms for deity, this name represents God as He makes Himself personally known to His people. In 1 Samuel 1:20, the name identifies the specific God who heard Hannah's silent, heartfelt plea for a child, demonstrating that her son Samuel was a direct gift from the God who remembers and answers those who call upon Him.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In English Bible translations, this personal name is traditionally rendered as "LORD" with small capital letters. This convention follows ancient Jewish custom, where out of deep reverence for God's holy name, readers substituted the title Adonai ("Lord") whenever they encountered the personal name in the biblical text.

Grammatically, the term functions as a proper noun. It is not a generic class word for a god, nor is it a descriptive title like "King" or "Almighty." It is God's distinct personal name, revealed to Israel to distinguish Him from the false idols of surrounding nations. In the setting of 1 Samuel 1, Israel was passing through a period of spiritual and leadership crisis at the end of the era of the judges. Against this dark backdrop, the narrative opens not with high political office, but with a distressed woman praying at the tabernacle in Shiloh to the personal God of Israel.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name is closely connected to the Hebrew verb meaning "to be." When God revealed His name to Moses at the burning bush, He explained it using the phrase "I AM WHO I AM." The name carries the basic sense of "He who is" or "the Existing One."

While the name points to God's continuous existence, it is important to distinguish between the name itself and the full character of God. The name is simply His personal proper name. The broader understanding of who God is—His faithfulness, righteousness, mercy, and power—is revealed through His actions and statements across the whole Bible, rather than being mechanically hidden inside the letters of the name itself.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture makes a deliberate distinction between general terms for deity and this personal name:

  • General titles (such as Elohim) describe God's power, majesty, or role as Creator of the universe.

  • The personal name (Yahweh) is used when Scripture emphasizes God's personal interaction, His promises, and His direct relationships with human beings.

Throughout the Old Testament narrative, when individuals call upon God in prayer, make vows, or give thanks for deliverance, they frequently address Him by this personal name. It marks God as a real, accessible Person rather than an abstract force or distant creator.

4. Theological Implications

The primary theological message of this name is that God makes Himself known. He is not unknown or unreachable; He has given His people a personal name by which they may address Him.

This truth establishes that prayer is a real conversation between a person and a personal God. When believers call upon the LORD, they are addressing the living God who listens, remembers, and acts. The name reassures God's people that the One who created the universe is the same One who pays attention to the individual burdens of humble worshippers.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:20, Hannah names her newborn son Samuel (Shemu'el), offering a explanation rooted in her experience: "Because I have asked for him from the LORD."

The context highlights several important truths:

  • Direct Answer to Personal Prayer: Hannah had prayed in severe emotional anguish, pouring out her soul without making an audible sound. Her answer came directly from the personal God who hears secret prayers.

  • Refusal of Pagan Assumptions: Hannah did not credit fate, luck, or fertility rituals for her pregnancy. She credited the LORD specifically.

  • The Turnaround of Israel's History: Samuel's birth marks a major turning point in Israel's history, leading to the establishment of the monarchy and the prophetic line. By highlighting the LORD in verse 20, the text shows that Israel's renewal began with God answering the private prayer of an afflicted, humble woman.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 — "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens."

  • Exodus 13:5 — "And it shall be, when the LORD brings you into the land of the Canaanites..."

  • Psalms 25:1 — "To You, O LORD, I lift up my soul."

  • Proverbs 16:6 — "In mercy and truth atonement is provided for iniquity; and by the fear of the LORD one departs from evil."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's prayer and her subsequent expression of praise in 1 Samuel 2 set the tone for the rest of the Books of Samuel. Her experience proves that divine power is not reserved only for military leaders or prominent public figures. The God who controls nations and raises up kings is the very same God who looks upon the brokenhearted individual. Her use of God's personal name emphasizes that true worship begins with a heart that knows God personally and trusts Him completely.
