NAME - (shêm, Strong's H8034)

Summary:

The word refers to a name, designation, reputation, memorial, or revealed identity. In 1 Samuel 1:20, it designates the specific name Hannah gives her newborn son—Samuel—commemorating God's gracious answer to her petition.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Hannah suffered years of anguish and barrenness, endured persistent provocation from her rival Peninnah, and carried deep grief before the Lord. During an annual pilgrimage to the tabernacle at Shiloh, she poured out her heart in silent, desperate prayer, making a vow that if God would grant her a male child, she would dedicate him to the Lord for all his days.

When God remembered her, Hannah conceived and gave birth to a son. In ancient Israelite culture, giving a child a name was far more than choosing a pleasing vocal sound. Names frequently recorded the historical circumstances of birth, expressed the parents' faith, or preserved a record of divine intervention. By giving her child a specific name, Hannah tied his personal identity directly to the Lord's response to her prayer.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to:

  • A personal proper name assigned to an individual, family, place, or object.

  • A person's reputation, renown, honor, or character.

  • A memorial, title, or lasting legacy.

  • The revealed identity, authority, and presence of God Himself when used of the Lord.

In 1 Samuel 1:20, the word is used in its fundamental sense of a personal name given to a human child.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, assigning a name marks recognition, ownership, purpose, and relational connection.

When applied to geography or individuals, the word establishes formal identity and historical tracking. When applied to God, it signifies His revealed character and reputation among His people. In family contexts, naming a child often served as a confession of faith or a testimony to what God had accomplished in the parents' lives. Hannah's action falls within this tradition of naming children to testify to God's faithful care.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's act of naming her son connects human prayer with divine sovereignty. The name she chooses functions as a standing witness within Israel that God hears the cries of the afflicted.

Her choice of name demonstrates that Samuel's existence was not an accident of nature or a mere human accomplishment, but a direct gift from God. Theologically, this passage shows that God pays attention to individual pain, attends to the sincere cries of His servants, and works out His redemptive plans through answered prayer.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:20, the word appears in the phrase "called his name Samuel," followed immediately by Hannah's explanation: "Because I have asked for him from the LORD."

Grammatically, the noun is singular and common in form, identifying the distinct designation assigned to the infant. The narrative contrasts Hannah's earlier silent, tearful petition in the tabernacle with her clear, joyful pronouncement at his birth. Every time Samuel's name was spoken throughout his long ministry as prophet, priest, and judge, it recalled the reality that God had heard a humble woman's prayer.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah’s choice to mark her son’s identity with answered prayer sets the tone for the entire book of 1 Samuel. Samuel’s life stands as a turning point in Israel's history, leading from the chaotic period of the judges to the establishment of the Davidic monarchy. The miraculous circumstances of his birth and naming foreshadow how God would use him to bring spiritual renewal to the nation.
