PROCESS - (tᵉqûwphâh, Strong's H8622)

Summary:

Tequfah refers to a completed circuit, a revolution, or the turning point of a regular period of time. In 1 Samuel 1:20, the word describes the passage and completion of the time period between Hannah's prayer for a child and the birth of her son. It highlights that God brought Hannah's request to fulfillment through the full, orderly unfolding of time, transforming her period of waiting into the joyful birth of Samuel.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israel, time was observed and measured primarily through the natural cycles of God's creation. The daily movement of the sun across the sky, the changing of seasons, and the yearly cycle of agricultural harvests provided the calendar by which people lived.

The word tequfah comes from a root that means to go around, surround, or complete a circuit. It captures the idea of an entire cycle running its course and coming back around to its starting point or reaching its full turning point.

In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah visited the sanctuary at Shiloh in deep grief because she was childless and ridiculed by her rival, Peninnah. In her anguish, she prayed earnestly to God and promised to dedicate her child to the LORD's service. Eli the priest blessed her, promising that God would consider her request. Hannah returned home with her family, and in due time, God answered her prayer.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Tequfah can refer to several types of completed cycles:

  • The daily circuit of the sun across the heavens from east to west.

  • The completion or turning point of the solar year, particularly the transition between seasons when annual feasts took place.

  • The full passage of a designated period of time, such as the period required for conception, pregnancy, and childbirth.

The basic idea is not vague or aimless passage of time, but time moving through a complete, orderly cycle toward its appointed end.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses tequfah to emphasize order, completion, and the steady movement of time under divine authority.

When describing nature, the word points to the broad sweep of the sun's journey across the sky. The sun does not wander randomly; it follows an appointed circuit set by the Creator.

When describing seasons, the word marks key turning points in the year. It designated the times when Israel gathered for annual festivals to celebrate the harvest and recognize God's ongoing provision.

When describing human events, as in 1 Samuel 1:20, the word points to the full span of time necessary for a specific purpose to reach completion. It marks the interval between a beginning moment (Hannah's prayer and return home) and its conclusion (the birth of Samuel).

4. Theological Implications

The use of tequfah in redemptive history reveals several key truths about how God acts:

  • God works through time: Although God can work instantly through miracles, He frequently accomplishes His purposes through the steady, natural unfolding of time.

  • Waiting is part of God's order: The interval between a promise or prayer and its realization is not wasted time. Just as pregnancy requires a set period of development, God's work often requires a complete cycle of time before coming to fruition.

  • God oversees natural processes: The ordinary biological timeline of conception and birth is governed by God's sovereign care. The passage of time remains under His direct control.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:20, the NKJV translates the phrase as "in the process of time" (literally, "at the turning of the periods of time").

When Hannah left Shiloh, her emotional state had transformed. She was no longer sad, because she trusted the word spoken by Eli. Yet, she did not immediately hold a child in her arms. She returned home to Ramah, lived her daily life with her husband Elkanah, and underwent the full period of conception and pregnancy.

The word tequfah shows that Samuel's birth was neither immediate nor delayed beyond its proper order. It occurred when the days completed their full cycle. God did not forget Hannah during those months; every day of that "process of time" was bringing her closer to the appointed moment when she would hold her promised son.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 34:22 — "And you shall observe the Feast of Weeks, of the firstfruits of wheat harvest, and the Feast of Ingathering at the year’s end."

  • 2 Chronicles 24:23 — "So it happened in the spring of the year that the army of Syria came up against him..."

  • Psalms 19:6 — "Its rising is from one end of heaven, and its circuit to the other end; and there is nothing hidden from its heat."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Plural Form: The grammatical form in 1 Samuel 1:20 is plural and feminine. The plural highlights the succession of individual days and months that came together to form the complete waiting period.

  • Literary Contrast: The "process of time" forms a stark contrast with the years of Hannah's barrenness mentioned earlier in the chapter. While her years of barrenness felt like endless misery, this specific "process of time" was filled with divine purpose leading to joy.

  • Pastoral Comfort: For believers waiting on God's guidance or promises, this passage provides quiet reassurance. The passage of daily time is not evidence of God's inaction, but the standard path through which He matures His gifts and fulfills His words.
