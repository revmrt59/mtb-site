SAMUEL - (Shᵉmûwʼêl, Strong's H8050)

Summary:

The Hebrew proper name Shemu'el (Samuel) means "Heard of God" or "Name of God." In 1 Samuel 1:20, Hannah gives this name to her newborn son to commemorate God's direct answer to her prayer during a season of deep grief and barrenness. By naming him Samuel, she establishes a lifelong testimony that the LORD hears and answers those who cry out to Him.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The opening chapter of 1 Samuel introduces Hannah, a woman suffering under the weight of childlessness and the taunting of her husband's second wife, Peninnah. During an annual pilgrimage to the tabernacle at Shiloh, Hannah poured out her soul before the LORD in weeping and desperate prayer. She made a vow that if God would grant her a son, she would dedicate him to the LORD's service all the days of his life.

God remembered Hannah, and in due time she conceived and gave birth to a son. 1 Samuel 1:20 records the moment she gave him his name. In ancient Israel, names often carried deep historical and spiritual significance. By giving him the name Samuel, Hannah forever linked her son's identity to the faithful God who heard her petition.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name Shemu'el is a masculine proper noun. It is built from Hebrew elements that express divine attentiveness and identity, standardly translated as "Heard of God" or "Name of God" (combining shama, meaning "to hear," or shem, meaning "name," with El, meaning "God").

In 1 Samuel 1:20, Hannah provides her own explanation for the name: "Because I have asked for him from the LORD." Her explanation plays on the phonetic similarity between the verb sha'al (meaning "to ask") and the name Shemu'el. This intentional wordplay connects the concept of asking with the reality of God hearing and answering.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In terms of grammar, this word functions as a masculine proper noun. Across the Old Testament scriptures, it identifies specific individuals, most notably the great prophet, priest, and final judge of Israel.

While an earlier leader from the tribe of Simeon bears the name in the book of Numbers (rendered "Shemuel"), the overwhelming biblical focus is on Hannah's son. Samuel stood at a critical turning point in Israel's history. He led the nation out of the disorder of the period of the judges and guided the transition into the monarchy, anointing both Saul and David as kings over Israel.

4. Theological Implications

The name Samuel highlights one of the most comforting truths in Scripture: God is a prayer-hearing God. He is not distant, unapproachable, or indifferent to human suffering. Hannah's experience shows that the LORD pays close attention to the personal grief of the lowly and brokenhearted.

Furthermore, Samuel's birth shows how God works out His large-scale redemptive plans through quiet moments of individual worship. Israel was in spiritual decline under the corrupt leadership of Eli's sons, yet God provided deliverance and renewal by answering the humble prayer of a faithful mother. Samuel's very name served as an ongoing reminder that Israel's true hope lay in seeking and obeying the LORD who hears.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:20, the naming of Samuel forms the climax of Hannah's personal struggle. Up to this point in the narrative, Hannah was known primarily for her pain and childlessness. The birth and naming of her child transform her situation from sorrow into testimony.

By stating, "Because I have asked for him from the LORD," Hannah publicly acknowledges that her son was not merely the result of natural order, nor was he her own private achievement. Every time Hannah spoke her son's name, she declared God's faithfulness. The name Samuel functioned as a permanent sign that God answers the prayers of those who trust in Him.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the culture of the ancient Near East, parents often chose names that commemorated a significant event surrounding a child's birth or expressed a hope for the child's future. Hannah's choice was rooted in a distinct act of worship and gratitude.

When Hannah eventually brought young Samuel to serve at the tabernacle in Shiloh, she repeated this theme to Eli the priest, explaining that she was the woman who had prayed there years before. The name Samuel stood as a living monument to answered prayer, reminding Eli, the nation, and Samuel himself that his entire life belonged to the God who had heard his mother's cry.
