SON - (bên, Strong's H1121)

Summary:

The word ben refers primarily to a male child, son, or offspring. In 1 Samuel 1:20, the word identifies Samuel, the male child born to Hannah after years of painful barrenness. Here, the word highlights God's direct and gracious response to Hannah's earnest petition, marking the birth of a child who was dedicated to the Lord's service and chosen to lead Israel through a pivotal period in its history.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The First Book of Samuel opens during a spiritually dark and chaotic period in Israel's history. The nation suffered under weak leadership and widespread disregard for God's law. Against this national backdrop, the narrative introduces the personal tragedy of Hannah, a woman living in Ramathaim Zophim. Though deeply loved by her husband Elkanah, Hannah was unable to have children. Her rival, Peninnah, provoked her severely year after year because the Lord had closed her womb.

In her deep anguish, Hannah traveled to the sanctuary at Shiloh and poured out her heart to God. She made a solemn vow that if the Lord would grant her a male child, she would dedicate him entirely to the Lord's service for all the days of his life. God heard her prayer, remembered her, and enabled her to conceive. The fulfillment of this divine intervention is recorded in 1 Samuel 1:20 with the birth of a son, whom she named Samuel.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

exact basic meaning of ben is a male child, son, or immediate male offspring. The term can be applied in several ways across Scripture:

  • A direct biological son born to parents.

  • A grandson or male descendant within a broader family line.

  • The young male offspring of animals.

  • A descriptive expression identifying a person's character, relationship, or age group.

In 1 Samuel 1:20, the word is used in its most direct sense: a male infant born to a human mother.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture frequently uses ben to highlight family lineage, inheritance, and covenant continuity. In Israelite society, the birth of a son preserved the family name, secured the inheritance of tribal land, and maintained the household from one generation to the next.

The word appears in 1 Samuel 1:20 as a singular common noun, pointing to the individual male infant given to Hannah. The simple word "son" carries profound weight in the narrative. It stands as the physical proof that God heard Hannah's silent cries at Shiloh. What was once an empty womb and a source of bitter mockery became, through God's power, a witness to divine faithfulness.

4. Theological Implications

The arrival of Hannah's son illustrates several key biblical truths:

  • God Answers Prayer: Children are gifts from the Lord, given according to His sovereign will. Hannah's pregnancy was not a random turn of events, but a direct result of God remembering His servant.

  • Integrity in Vows: Hannah recognized that the child belonged ultimately to God. Rather than holding tightly to the son she had desired for so long, she faithfully prepared to carry out her vow by relinquishing him to tabernacle service once he was weaned.

  • Redemptive History: The birth of this particular son was critical for the whole nation. God used Hannah's personal grief and subsequent joy to raise up Samuel, who would serve as a faithful prophet, priest, and judge, eventually anointing David as king over Israel.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:20, the birth of the "son" serves as the turning point of the chapter. The narrative moves from Hannah's deep weeping, fasting, and distress to a moment of joyous fulfillment and quiet gratitude.

The text highlights the link between her prayer and the child's identity. Hannah named the boy Samuel, explaining her choice with the words, "Because I have asked for him from the LORD." The word "son" in this verse connects her earlier petition at the tabernacle—where she specifically asked God for a male child—with the infant now resting in her arms. The physical reality of the child confirms that her faith in the Lord was not in vain.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The dedicate-and-surrender pattern seen in Hannah's response to her son provides a timeless model of faithful stewardship. Hannah recognized that her son was a trust from God rather than a personal possession. Her willingness to give Samuel back to the Lord at Shiloh demonstrates that true faith receives God's gifts with gratitude while keeping God Himself as the highest devotion.

Furthermore, Hannah's joy over her son led directly to her famous prayer of praise in 1 Samuel 2. In that prayer, she celebrates how God lifts up the needy, fills the hungry, and turns barrenness into fruitfulness, anticipating themes that echo throughout the rest of Scripture.
