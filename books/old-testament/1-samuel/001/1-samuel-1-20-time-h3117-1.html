TIME - (yôwm, Strong's H3117)

Summary:

The Hebrew word yom basic meaning is a day, referring to daylight hours, a twenty-four-hour period, or a general span of time. In 1 Samuel 1:20, the plural masculine noun is translated as "time" within the phrase "in the process of time." It describes the regular unfolding of days leading to the fulfillment of Hannah's prayer as she conceived and gave birth to Samuel in God's appointed season.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

1 Samuel 1 opens with the deep distress of Hannah, a woman who suffered from barrenness and the severe provocation of her rival, Peninnah. Year after year, Hannah traveled to the sanctuary at Shiloh to worship. In her sorrow, she poured out her soul to the LORD, asking for a male child and promising to dedicate him to divine service. The priest Eli initially mistook her silent prayer for drunkenness, but upon hearing her genuine plight, he offered a word of blessing, praying that God would grant her petition.

Hannah departed in faith, her heart no longer burdened. The narrative moves quickly from the sanctuary back to her home in Ramah. The text records that after Elkanah and Hannah returned, "it came to pass in the process of time" that Hannah conceived and bore a son. The phrase highlights the quiet transition from fervent prayer to tangible fulfillment, marking the steady passage of human days under God's watchful care.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun yom fundamentally designates a day. In the light of basic Old Testament usage, the word can refer to the daylight period in contrast to night, a full twenty-four-hour calendar day, or an extended period or era.

In the plural form used in 1 Samuel 1:20 (yamim), the word frequently carries the broader sense of "days," "time," "a year," or an ongoing season. When joined with a word referring to a complete cycle or revolution, the expression literally describes "at the turning of the days." Here it means the completion of the regular period of human gestation and time needed for a pregnancy to reach its natural end.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, yom is one of the most frequent time-related words. Its range of application covers several distinct contexts:

  • Daily cycles: Marking the routine pattern of day and night established at creation.

  • Specific calendar dates: Designating holy days, appointed feasts, and historical events.

  • Lifetime and longevity: In the plural form, referring to a person's age or the overall duration of a king's reign.

  • Extended seasons: Representing a broader span of time, such as a season of harvest, trial, or waiting.

In 1 Samuel 1:20, the plural noun emphasizes duration. Promises given by God unfold over ordinary, countable days. The word does not describe a sudden, miraculous flash out of time, but rather God working His miracle through the natural timeline of human life.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical account of Hannah's conception demonstrates that God operates inside human history and respects the timeline He established for creation. Prayer is often answered not by bypassing time, but by sanctifying the days that follow.

Hannah prayed passionately at Shiloh, but the answer required time to develop. The expression "in the process of time" reminds believers that divine faithfulness is actively at work during periods of waiting. God does not neglect His promises during the quiet days between the offering of a petition and its visible outcome. Everyday hours and months are the space in which God matures His gifts and brings His plans to light.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:20, the use of yamim (translated "time") provides a crucial link between Hannah's faith at Shiloh and the birth of Samuel. When Hannah left Shiloh, her emotional state changed immediately—she ate and her face was no longer sad—even though her physical reality had not yet altered.

The text then highlights the progression: Elkanah knew his wife, the LORD remembered her, and in due time, the pregnancy progressed to birth. The passage of days was necessary for the "remembering" of God to become visible in the infant Samuel. The word shows that the arrival of Israel's great prophet and judge began with ordinary, patient days of waiting in a quiet home in Ramah.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 1:5 — "God called the light Day, and the darkness He called Night."

  • Here the word defines the light portion of the daily cycle, establishing order at the very beginning of creation.

  • Deuteronomy 16:8 — "Six days you shall eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day there shall be a sacred assembly to the LORD your God."

  • In this passage, the plural noun specifies an exact duration of calendar time appointed for national worship and remembrance.

  • 1 Chronicles 22:9 — "His name shall be Solomon, and I will give peace and quietness to Israel in his days."

  • Here the plural word refers to an entire epoch or reign, promising sustained rest during the lifetime of the king.

  • Isaiah 61:2 — "To proclaim the acceptable year of the LORD, and the day of vengeance of our God."

  • The prophet uses the singular word to signify an appointed time of divine favor and decisive judgment.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Understanding yom in its plural sense helps readers appreciate the quiet rhythm of biblical faith. Believers often look for sudden, dramatic interventions, yet Scripture regularly highlights how God moves through the accumulation of ordinary days. Hannah named her son Samuel, meaning "Asked of God," because every day of her pregnancy served as a continuous reminder that the child in her womb was the direct answer to her prayer.
