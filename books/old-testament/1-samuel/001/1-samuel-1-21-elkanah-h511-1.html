ELKANAH - (ʼElqânâh, Strong's H511)

Summary:

The name Elkanah combines the Hebrew word for God with a root meaning to acquire or create, yielding the personal name "God has acquired" or "God has created." In 1 Samuel 1:21, the name identifies the father of Samuel as he leads his household up to Shiloh to offer the annual sacrifice and fulfill his vow, demonstrating covenant fidelity and leadership in a time of widespread spiritual breakdown.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The name Elkanah belongs to several men in the Old Testament, most notably the father of the prophet Samuel. Elkanah lived in Ramathaim Zophim in the hill country of Ephraim during the turbulent period of the Judges, when Israel lacked a righteous king and spiritual confusion was widespread.

Although living among the tribe of Ephraim, genealogical records reveal that Elkanah was a Levite from the family of Kohath. During this era, central worship took place at the tabernacle in Shiloh. Elkanah distinguished himself from many of his contemporaries by making regular pilgrimages to Shiloh to worship and sacrifice to the LORD.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Elkanah is a masculine proper noun formed from two Hebrew elements: 'El (meaning "God") and the verb qanah (meaning "to acquire, possess, or create"). Together, the name means "God has acquired" or "God has created."

The name expresses a basic biblical conviction: God is the Creator and Owner of life. When parents named a child Elkanah, they confessed that the child was acquired through God's power or belonged directly to Him. In the context of Israelite family life, proper names often served as short statements of faith regarding God's character and activity.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses this name for a few specific Levites and ancient leaders:

  • The Father of Samuel: The majority of narrative uses refer to the husband of Hannah and Peninnah in 1 Samuel 1–2. Here, Elkanah is depicted as a God-fearing head of household who maintains family worship, offers yearly sacrifices, and treats his distressed wife Hannah with affection and support.

  • Exodus Korahite Lineage: In Exodus, Elkanah appears in the genealogy of the sons of Korah within the tribe of Levi.

  • Chronicles Levitical Lines: Levitical genealogies and worship rosters identify several ancestors and descendants bearing this name, including Temple singers and gatekeepers.

  • Royal Official under Ahaz: In 2 Chronicles, an officer named Elkanah served in a high court position before being killed during a Syrian-Ephraimite war.

Across these uses, the name remains a standard masculine proper noun, specifically identifying covenant men within Israel's historical record.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of Elkanah in 1 Samuel highlights God's preservation of a godly remnant. During a dark era when the priesthood at Shiloh was corrupt under Eli's sons, God was quietly working in an ordinary household to bring forth a prophet who would turn the nation back to Him.

The meaning of the name—"God has acquired"—aligns with the central theme of 1 Samuel 1. Hannah's womb was closed, but God remembered her prayer and granted her a son. When Hannah dedicated Samuel to the LORD, she acknowledged that the child belonged completely to God. Elkanah's own household lived under this truth: life and children are acquired from the LORD and belong to His service.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:21, the name Elkanah appears as the subject of the sentence: "Now the man Elkanah and all his house went up to offer to the LORD the yearly sacrifice and his vow."

This verse presents a pivotal moment in the narrative. Hannah had recently given birth to Samuel and chose to stay behind in Ramah until the child was weaned. Elkanah did not force her to travel, nor did he abandon his spiritual duties. Instead, he led the rest of his household up to Shiloh to perform the prescribed annual sacrifices and to fulfill his personal vow before the LORD.

By identifying "the man Elkanah" carrying out these sacred duties, the text emphasizes his headship and moral consistency. He was not passive in his faith; he ensured his family remained committed to worshipping God at the sanctuary, setting the stage for young Samuel to be brought up in an environment of faithful devotion.

  • 1 Samuel 1:8 — "Then Elkanah his husband said to her, 'Hannah, why do you weep? Why do you not eat? And why is your heart grieved?'"

  • 2 Chronicles 28:7 — "Zichri, a mighty man of Ephraim, killed Maaseiah the king’s son, Azrikam the officer over the house, and Elkanah who was second to the king."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between Elkanah's home and the priestly house of Eli is striking. While Eli failed to restrain his wicked sons who served at the sanctuary, Elkanah maintained holy discipline and worship in his own home.

Furthermore, Elkanah's Levite ancestry explains why Samuel could properly serve at the tabernacle under Eli. Though living in the tribal territory of Ephraim, Elkanah held a Levitical inheritance that dedicated his family line to God's sanctuary service.
