LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

The word refers to the proper, personal name of the God of Israel, traditionally rendered as "LORD" in capital letters in English Bibles. It identifies God not by a generic title, but by His personal covenant name revealed to His people. In 1 Samuel 1:21, the name identifies the God to whom Elkanah brings his family's annual worship and vows. It shows that Elkanah's household was devoted specifically to Yahweh, the God who heard Hannah's prayer and provided her son Samuel.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The name transliterated as YHWH is the proper name of the God of Israel. When God met Moses at the burning bush, He revealed this name to express His self-existence and His personal commitment to redeem His people. Out of reverence for the sacredness of the name, Jewish tradition eventually refrained from pronouncing it aloud, substituting the title Adonai ("Lord"). Most English translations follow this ancient pattern by printing the word in capital letters as "LORD."

The events of 1 Samuel 1 take place during the period of the judges, a spiritual era marked by widespread moral instability in Israel. Despite the general breakdown of religious order across the nation, faithful families still made regular pilgrimages to Shiloh, where the tabernacle and the ark of the covenant were located. In this setting, Elkanah and his household traveled annually to present their sacrifices to the God of Israel.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word functions as a proper name rather than a common noun or title. Generic terms like Elohim describe God in His power and supreme deity, whereas this name presents God as a distinct, personal Being who enters into fellowship with people.

The name is connected to the Hebrew verb for being or existing. It designates God as the one who exists independently, lives forever, and acts faithfully toward His creation. In English translations, using capital letters for "LORD" helps readers distinguish this personal proper name from the common title "Lord" (Adonai), which indicates authority or mastership.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses this proper name whenever God interacts directly with His people in covenant relationship. It appears in contexts of prayer, sacrifice, divine promises, and corporate worship.

Because the name belongs exclusively to the God who delivered Israel from Egypt, its use in worship separates true worship from idolatry. When Israelites offered sacrifices or made vows to the LORD, they were not offering generic devotion to a vague heavenly deity. They were approaching the specific God who had made Himself known through His redemptive acts and written commandments.

4. Theological Implications

The prominent use of God's personal name emphasizes that worship is grounded in a real relationship. God is not an impersonal force or an unreachable deity. He invites His people to address Him by name and to rely on His personal care.

At the same time, the name warns against treating God lightly. Fulfilling vows and offering sacrifices to the LORD required integrity and obedience. The God who reveals His name to His people expects sincere devotion that corresponds to His holy character.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:21, the narrative states that Elkanah and his household went up "to offer to the LORD the yearly sacrifice and his vow." This worship took place shortly after Hannah had prayed passionately at Shiloh for a child, promising to dedicate him to the LORD. God answered her prayer, and she gave birth to Samuel.

Elkanah's journey to Shiloh demonstrates his leadership in leading his entire household to fulfill their spiritual obligations to the God who had shown them mercy. The mention of "his vow" alongside the "yearly sacrifice" suggests that Elkanah was fully aligned with the sacred obligations of his family, including the vow Hannah had made regarding Samuel. Offering these sacrifices specifically "to the LORD" highlights the family's recognition that Samuel's birth was an act of divine grace from their covenant God, requiring faithful thanksgiving and obedience.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 — "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens."

  • Joshua 14:12 — "Now therefore, give me this mountain of which the LORD spoke in that day..."

  • 2 Samuel 22:2 — "And he said: 'The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer...'"

  • Psalms 25:1 — "To You, O LORD, I lift up my soul."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Grammatically, the word is a proper noun identifying a unique personal individual. It does not take a plural form or possessive suffixes, as generic nouns do.

The historical backdrop of 1 Samuel 1 contrasts the faithful devotion of Elkanah's family with the corruption present at the Shiloh sanctuary under the sons of Eli. Even in a time of spiritual decline, Elkanah's annual pilgrimage to worship the LORD shows that true covenant faith persisted among ordinary families in Israel.
