MAN - (ʼîysh, Strong's H376)

Summary:

The word 'ish refers to an individual male human being or husband. It identifies a distinct male person in contrast to humanity as a whole or females. In 1 Samuel 1:21, the word refers directly to Elkanah, designating him as the male head of the household who leads his family to Shiloh to fulfill his annual spiritual duties before God.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israelite society, 'ish was the standard word used to describe an individual man, a husband, or a person holding a specific role in a household, community, or army. Unlike broader terms for humanity that describe collective mankind or mortal weakness, this word brings a specific male individual into view.

The opening chapter of 1 Samuel takes place during the late period of the judges, a time marked by widespread spiritual decay and social disorder in Israel. The narrative focuses on a family living in the hill country of Ephraim. By introducing Elkanah as "the man," the text anchors the upcoming story of Samuel's birth within the steady worship and household life of a specific Israelite.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word 'ish primarily means an individual male person. Depending on the context, it can carry several specific senses:

  • A male human being as an individual.

  • A husband in relation to his wife.

  • A warrior or male leader in a community or military setting.

  • An individual representative, often translated as "everyone" or "each one" when describing a group acting individually.

When paired directly with a proper name, as in the phrase "the man Elkanah," it functions as a clear designator, highlighting the specific individual being described.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, 'ish appears in diverse settings to highlight personal identity, social roles, and individual human responsibility.

In relational contexts, the word describes the marriage union between husband and wife established at creation. It distinguishes a husband from his spouse while highlighting their shared physical humanity. In civic and military contexts, the word describes individual men called to battle or leaders standing in council. In wisdom literature, it highlights individual accountability, contrasting human self-perception with God's perfect judgment.

4. Theological Implications

Scripture uses 'ish to illustrate individual moral responsibility. While God deals with whole nations and families, He also deals directly with individual men and women.

The use of this word in family settings highlights the role of faithful leadership in the home. God's redemptive plans often unfold through ordinary individuals who walk in quiet obedience. In times when national worship was corrupt or declining, as seen in the early chapters of 1 Samuel, God preserved a faithful remnant through God-fearing individuals who maintained their devotion to Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:21, the phrase "the man Elkanah" directs attention back to the father of the household after Hannah's private prayer and vow at the tabernacle. The word is grammatically singular and common in gender, describing Elkanah directly as an individual male.

Elkanah moves forward with his family's established custom of going up to Shiloh to offer the annual sacrifice and perform his vow. His steady devotion provides a firm backdrop for Hannah's dynamic faith. While Hannah remains at home to care for young Samuel until he is weaned, Elkanah carries out his household's corporate worship obligations without hesitation. In the following verse, the same word is translated "husband," showing how his role as an individual male is bound up with his marriage commitment to Hannah.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Judges 17:6 — "In those days there was no king in Israel; everyone did what was right in his own eyes."

  • 1 Samuel 1:22 — "But Hannah did not go up, for she said to her husband..."

  • Proverbs 16:2 — "All the ways of a man are pure in his own eyes, but the LORD weighs the spirits."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Hebrew word 'ish differs from 'adam, which refers broadly to mankind or humanity, and 'enosh, which highlights human frailty and mortality. While 'adam emphasizes collective human nature, 'ish highlights individual identity and social standing.

In 1 Samuel 1, the repetition of this word grounds a pivotal moment in Israel's redemptive history within an ordinary household. Before God raises up Samuel as a prophet and judge to guide the nation, He works through the quiet, consistent faithfulness of "the man Elkanah" and his home.
