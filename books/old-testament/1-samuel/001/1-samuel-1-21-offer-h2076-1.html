OFFER - (zâbach, Strong's H2076)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb zabach means to slaughter an animal for sacrifice or to present an animal offering to God in worship. It describes the tangible action of slaying a sacrificial animal, often followed by a shared fellowship meal before the LORD. In 1 Samuel 1:21, the word describes Elkanah’s purposeful journey to Shiloh to fulfill his spiritual duty by presenting his family's annual sacrificial worship and paying his personal vow to the LORD.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israel, sacrificial worship involved visible, practical acts of devotion. The verb zabach refers to the killing or slaughtering of an animal specifically for a religious offering or sacred feast. Unlike burnt offerings that were entirely consumed on the altar, sacrifices associated with zabach often involved shared meals where worshipping families ate part of the sacrificed animal in God's presence after the priests received their portion.

In the opening chapter of 1 Samuel, Israel was living through a period of spiritual weakness under the leadership of Eli and his corrupt sons. Despite the surrounding spiritual decline, Elkanah maintained a steady routine of household devotion. Every year, he traveled from his home in Ramah to the sanctuary at Shiloh to worship and slaughter animals for the yearly sacrifice. This annual journey set the background for Hannah's earnest prayer for a son and God's gracious response in giving her Samuel.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Zabach means to slaughter, kill, or present an animal as a sacrificial offering. The word centers on the physical act of slaying livestock—such as cattle, sheep, or goats—for religious purposes.

Depending on the setting, zabach can describe different types of offerings:

  • A fellowship or peace offering where worshippers shared a meal in God's presence.

  • A sacrificial meal ratifying a covenant between people or between God and Israel.

  • A sacrifice of thanksgiving or the fulfillment of a vow.

  • On rare occasions, the slaughtering of animals for a common feast.

The defining element of zabach is the sacred act of slaughtering an animal to draw near to God in worship, gratitude, or covenant commitment.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, zabach appears in several key settings:

Early patriarchs used zabach when establishing solemn agreements. When Jacob made a covenant of peace with Laban, he slaughtered animals on the mountain to seal the agreement. During the Exodus, God instructed Moses to demand freedom so the Israelites could go into the wilderness to offer sacrifices to the LORD.

In the law of Moses, zabach appears frequently to guide Israel's worship. God required sacrifices to be brought with proper motives, clean hands, and heartfelt thanksgiving. Later in Israel's history, the word is also used to describe corrupt worship when the people turned away from God to offer sacrifices on pagan high places or to idol gods.

In poetry and wisdom literature, the prophets and psalmists emphasized that the physical act of slaughtering an animal meant nothing to God if the worshipper's heart lacked trust and obedience. While zabach describes the outward action of sacrifice, God always looked past the altar to the condition of the worshipper's heart.

4. Theological Implications

The concept of sacrifice represented by zabach highlights crucial biblical truths about worship, atonement, and fellowship. Approach to a holy God required a life to be given, pointing to the gravity of sin and the costly nature of drawing near to the LORD.

Sacrifice was not merely an obligation; it was a primary way Israel expressed gratitude and enjoyed fellowship with God. By partaking of the sacrificial meal, worshippers celebrated God's provision and renewed their allegiance to Him.

Additionally, Scripture reveals that outward sacrifice without genuine obedience is empty. As Samuel later told King Saul, to obey is better than sacrifice. The physical act of zabach was intended to be an outward expression of inward faith, submission, and love for God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:21, zabach appears in an infinitive construct form that expresses purpose: Elkanah and his household went up "to offer" to the LORD the yearly sacrifice and his vow. This trip occurred shortly after Hannah gave birth to Samuel. Hannah chose to remain home until the child was weaned, promising to bring him to Shiloh afterward to dedicate him to the LORD permanently.

Elkanah supported Hannah's resolve while faithfully continuing his own spiritual responsibilities. His action demonstrates headship rooted in steady, humble obedience. He did not abandon his holy duties or delay his vows because of family changes. Instead, his trip "to offer" the annual sacrifice highlights a quiet, enduring commitment to God's house during a dark era in Israel's history. His faithful worship established a godly home in which the young prophet Samuel would eventually grow.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The verb zabach is directly related to the common Hebrew noun zebach, which means "sacrifice" or "sacrificial meal." In 1 Samuel 1:21, both words appear side-by-side: Elkanah went up to offer (zabach) the yearly sacrifice (zebach). This verbal repetition highlights the completeness and intentionality of his worship.

Furthermore, Elkanah's yearly pilgrimage stands out against the backdrop of Shiloh's priesthood. Eli's sons were abusing the sacrifice by forcibly taking meat for themselves before God received His portion. In contrast to their greedy disrespect for the altar, Elkanah approached the sanctuary with reverence to perform the sacred slaughter properly, proving that God preserves a faithful remnant even when religious institutions suffer from corruption.
