UP - (ʻâlâh, Strong's H5927)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb ‘alah means to go up, ascend, rise, or climb. It basic physical movement from a lower elevation to a higher one. In 1 Samuel 1:21, the word describes Elkanah traveling up to the sanctuary at Shiloh with his household to offer his annual sacrifice and fulfill his vow. In this passage, the word highlights Elkanah's ongoing commitment to worship God, showing a household that maintained faithful rhythm during a time of general spiritual decline in Israel.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the Old Testament, traveling to a place of worship was almost always described as going "up." Geographically, central worship sites like Shiloh—and later Jerusalem—were located in the hill country. Worshippers literally walked uphill to reach the sanctuary.

Beyond geography, the term carries a strong ceremonial background. God commanded Israelite families to gather at the central sanctuary for annual feasts. Heading up to Shiloh was not an ordinary trip; it was a deliberate journey made to meet with the Lord, present sacrifices, and renew covenant promises.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meaning of ‘alah is physical ascension or upward movement. It describes a wide range of actions across the Old Testament:

  • Moving from lower terrain to higher ground.

  • A mist or cloud rising into the air.

  • A person stepping up onto a platform, altar, or mountain.

  • Worshippers traveling up to a holy site.

When used in a worship context, the word naturally connects the physical climb with spiritual drawing near to God. The word itself means to ascend or go up, while the context of worship shows that approaching God requires intentional effort and dedicated movement.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In Israelite life, ‘alah marked key moments of transition and devotion. People went up to battle, went up to consult leaders, and went up to sacrifice.

Because sacrifices burned on the altar produced smoke that drifted upward, the verb also became closely connected with offerings. Worshippers brought their gifts up to the altar, and the smoke ascended toward heaven. The term captures both the physical travel of the worshipper and the elevation of the gift presented to God.

4. Theological Implications

The idea of going up to God's presence carries rich biblical meaning:

  • Intentional Worship: Drawing near to God is rarely accidental. It requires turning away from daily routines and ascending to where God has appointed to meet His people.

  • Household Leadership: Spiritual routine within a family depends heavily on faithful leadership. Elkanah’s regular journey set a clear standard of devotion for his entire home.

  • Persistence in Dark Times: Elkanah made this trip during the period of the judges, when Israel lacked strong leadership and Eli's sons brought dishonor to the priesthood. Yet Elkanah continued to go up to worship, demonstrating that God's people are called to remain faithful regardless of the spiritual climate around them.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:21, the narrative form of the verb (a masculine singular form in the imperfect with wayyiqtol) advances the story after Hannah stayed home to nurse young Samuel. Elkanah leads the rest of his household up to Shiloh to offer the annual sacrifice and fulfill his vow.

The word underscores the regular, unhurried obedience of Elkanah's life. Hannah's absence in this specific year was not a sign of neglect, but a practical delay agreed upon so she could dedicate Samuel fully once he was weaned. Elkanah's journey demonstrates that the family's devotion remained firm. He carried out his headship by going up to complete what had been promised before the Lord.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:6 — "but a mist went up from the earth and watered the whole face of the ground."

  • Exodus 24:18 — "So Moses went into the midst of the cloud and went up into the mountain."

  • 1 Samuel 9:14 — "And when they had come into the city, there was Samuel, coming out toward them on his way up to the high place."

  • Psalms 68:18 — "You have ascended on high, You have led captivity captive;"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The physical requirement of going up to Shiloh involved time, expense, and physical labor. Traveling with livestock and family members through hill terrain was demanding.

This reminds readers that biblical worship cost something. Elkanah did not treat worship as a convenience. The upward journey to Shiloh expressed in real, tangible terms that honoring God was worth every effort required.
