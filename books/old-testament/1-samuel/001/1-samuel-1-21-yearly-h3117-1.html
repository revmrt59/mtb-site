YEARLY - (yôwm, Strong's H3117)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun yōm primary refers to a day, daylight, or a specific period of time. When used in the plural, it can describe a succession of days, a lifespan, or a recurring annual cycle. In 1 Samuel 1:21, the word functions within an idiomatic phrase translated "yearly," highlighting Elkanah's faithful, steady habit of traveling to Shiloh year after year to worship God.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israel, life was organized around the natural rhythm of day and night, as well as the seasonal rhythm of the religious calendar. The standard Hebrew noun for a day is yōm. While the word frequently points to a single twenty-four-hour day or the hours of daylight, it also functions more broadly to describe time itself.

In Hebrew thought, time was often viewed through the lens of appointed seasons and regular observances. When yōm appears in the plural form, it can refer to a period of time, a person's lifetime, or a full cycle of days that completes a solar year. In family life before the central temple was built in Jerusalem, God's people made regular pilgrimages to the sanctuary at Shiloh. The term reflects how early Israelite families marked time through annual religious gatherings.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to daylight as distinct from night, a twenty-four-hour period, a specific historical day, or a broader season. Depending on context and grammatical structure, its meaning shifts across a clear range:

  • A single day or daylight period.

  • A fixed point in time, such as a day of battle or judgment.

  • A general span of time or epoch.

  • In the plural form, a succession of days, a lifespan, or an annual cycle.

In 1 Samuel 1:21, the plural form appears in a standard Hebrew idiom where the phrase "the sacrifice of days" describes an annual or regular yearly sacrifice. Here the word carries the sense of a complete yearly cycle.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament historical narratives, poetry, and law, yōm serves as the basic building block for talking about time. The simple singular form marks individual days, such as the days of creation or specific festival days. The plural form often expands the time frame to express routine, continuous history, or long-term commitment.

When applied to worship, the plural form emphasizes repetition and consistency. Rather than describing a unique, one-time religious experience, it highlights an established pattern. Israelite worship involved both daily sacrifices and annual feasts. The use of this word in connection with family pilgrimages shows that true devotion was measured not merely by spontaneous moments, but by steady observance through the changing seasons of life.

4. Theological Implications

While yōm is fundamentally a common word for time, its biblical usage carries important theological truth. God creates time and places human beings within structured daily and seasonal frameworks. Spiritual life in Scripture is designed to operate within these regular rhythms.

The reliance on recurring annual pilgrimages demonstrates that covenant faithfulness is built on long-term consistency. In 1 Samuel, God works out His redemptive purpose through an ordinary family that maintains regular worship over many years. God often accomplishes His silent, transformative work through the quiet discipline of habitual devotion, long before the fruits of His promises become visible.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:21, Elkanah takes his household up to Shiloh to offer "the yearly sacrifice and his vow." The narrative has already introduced the deep sorrow of his wife Hannah, who was childless and grieved by Peninnah's rivalry. In the preceding verses, God answered Hannah's prayer, and she gave birth to Samuel.

The text specifies that Elkanah continued his habit of going to Shiloh for the annual sacrifice. Grammatically, the target word is a masculine plural noun that joins with the word for sacrifice to describe this recurring event. Its presence in the verse underlines several key aspects of the story:

  • It demonstrates domestic spiritual order: Elkanah maintained his family's religious duties year after year, regardless of home conflicts or national spiritual decline.

  • It highlights covenant continuity: Even during the morally dark period at the end of the judges, faithful households continued their annual worship.

  • It sets the stage for vow fulfillment: Elkanah journeyed to fulfill his regular family obligation while preparing for the day Hannah would bring young Samuel to remain at Shiloh.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The plural form of yōm highlights how small daily units of time accumulate into larger seasons of life. In 1 Samuel 1, the narrative bridges the gap between individual days of private weeping and the longer years of divine preparation.

The quiet discipline of Elkanah's annual pilgrimage forms the background for the birth of Samuel, who would become one of Israel's greatest prophets. Routine obedience carried out year after year provides the context in which God raises up leadership and brings spiritual renewal to His people.
