APPEAR - (râʼâh, Strong's H7200)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb ra'ah fundamentally means to see, look, or perceive, but in sanctuary contexts it describes coming into the presence of God or presenting oneself before Him. In 1 Samuel 1:22, Hannah uses the word to express her commitment to bring her young son Samuel to the sanctuary at Shiloh once he is weaned. There he will appear before the LORD and remain in His service permanently, fulfilling the vow she made in her distress.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the opening chapter of 1 Samuel, Hannah faces deep grief because of her barrenness and the continuous provocation of her rival, Peninnah. During an annual pilgrimage to the tabernacle at Shiloh, Hannah pours out her soul to God in silent, tearful prayer. She makes a vow that if the LORD gives her a male child, she will dedicate him to the LORD for all the days of his life.

God hears her prayer, and she gives birth to Samuel. When the time comes for her husband Elkanah to travel to Shiloh for the annual sacrifice, Hannah decides to remain home temporarily. She explains that once the boy is fully weaned, she will take him to Shiloh so that he may stand before the LORD and live there permanently. This sanctuary setting provides the background for using the verb ra'ah in the sense of presenting oneself before God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of ra'ah is to see or perceive with the eyes. Depending on how it is used in a sentence, it can refer to physical sight, mental observation, or physical inspection.

When used in connection with worship at the tabernacle or temple, the word takes on a specialized meaning: to present oneself or to appear in the presence of God. Worshippers did not simply go to a building; they came to appear before the living God. In 1 Samuel 1:22, the verb describes bringing young Samuel into the sanctuary so that he might be presented before the LORD for lifelong service.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, ra'ah describes several types of seeing:

  • Physical observation: Looking at objects, people, or events in daily life.

  • Examination or inspection: Checking something carefully, such as a priest evaluating health conditions.

  • Divine sight: God watching over His creation or observing human conduct.

  • Sanctuary appearance: Coming to the place of worship to present oneself before the LORD.

When used of sanctuary visits, the word highlights the relational reality of worship. Gathering at the tabernacle was an intentional act of coming before God's sight and placing oneself in His presence.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's use of ra'ah reflects essential truths about Old Testament worship:

  • God sees human suffering: Before Samuel appeared before God, God first saw and remembered Hannah in her hardship.

  • Worship involves presenting oneself to God: True worship is not an abstract exercise. It means bringing one's life directly into God's presence.

  • Faithful vows are expressed in action: Hannah did not treat her promise lightly. Bringing Samuel to appear before the LORD was the concrete fulfillment of her pledge.

  • Divine service is a response to grace: Dedicating Samuel to live at the sanctuary acknowledged that his very life was a gift from God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:22, Hannah tells Elkanah, "I will not go up until the child is weaned; then I will take him, that he may appear before the LORD and remain there forever."

The verb is a masculine singular perfect form that functions in sequence to describe the future action Hannah plans to take. Rather than taking an infant on a long journey only to return home, Hannah waits until Samuel is old enough to remain at Shiloh. Appearing before the LORD in this passage marks a permanent transition. Samuel is not visiting Shiloh for a feast day; he is being brought to live in God's presence as a consecrated servant.

  • Daniel 1:15 — "And at the end of ten days their countenances appeared better and fatter in flesh than all the young men who ate the portion of the king’s delicacies."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In ancient Israel, parents weaning a child typically nursed them for two to three years. Waiting through this period allowed Samuel to be physically ready for life in the sanctuary under Eli's care. Hannah's deliberate delay was not an effort to postpone her vow, but a practical preparation to ensure that when Samuel did appear before the LORD, he could remain there permanently. This act of devotion laid the foundation for Samuel's crucial ministry as a prophet and leader in Israel.
