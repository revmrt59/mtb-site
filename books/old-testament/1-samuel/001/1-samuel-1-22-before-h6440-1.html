BEFORE - (pânîym, Strong's H6440)

panim ( panim , Strong's H6440 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun panim literally means "face" or "presence." When combined with prepositions, it frequently functions as a directional or relational term meaning "before," "in front of," or "in the presence of." In 1 Samuel 1:22, the word describes bringing the young boy Samuel into the tabernacle to appear directly in the presence of the Lord, marking the lifelong dedication of his life to God's service.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israel, worship centered on the sanctuary—first the tabernacle and later the temple—where God revealed His presence among His people. To travel to the sanctuary and participate in its rituals was described as coming into God's presence or standing before Him.

In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah suffers under the burden of childlessness. During an annual pilgrimage to the sanctuary at Shiloh, she prays fervently to God, asking for a son and vowing that if God grants her request, she will give the child back to the Lord for his entire life. God answers her prayer, and she gives birth to Samuel. In 1 Samuel 1:22, Hannah explains to her husband Elkanah that once the child is weaned, she will bring him to the sanctuary so that he may appear "before" the Lord and remain there permanently.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun panim fundamentally refers to the face of a person, animal, or object. Because a person's face expresses who they are and where they are turned, the word naturally expanded in Hebrew to denote personal presence.

When panim is combined with a preposition, as it is in 1 Samuel 1:22, it translates into English as "before," "in front of," or "in the presence of."

  • When referring to human interactions, it indicates standing directly in front of another person or authority figure.

  • When referring to God, it means standing or serving in the explicit, localized presence of the Lord at His holy place.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses panim in several related ways:

  • Literal Face: It describes the physical face of a human being or the surface of an object or landscape.

  • Personal Presence: It signifies being in someone's direct company or sight, where actions are open and recognized.

  • Spatial Location: It indicates the front side of a structure, such as the entrance or front of the tabernacle.

  • Sacred Service: Combined with the divine name ("before the LORD"), it serves as a common spiritual phrase for gathering at the altar, presenting sacrifices, or serving as a priest in God's sanctuary.

4. Theological Implications

To stand "before the LORD" is more than a geographic description; it carries deep spiritual meaning. In the Old Testament, approaching God's presence required ceremonial purity and reverence because God is holy.

Hannah's decision to present Samuel "before the LORD" highlights the reality of a vow kept in faith. By placing Samuel in God's presence, she acknowledges that her child was a gift from God and rightfully belongs to Him. Living "before the LORD" describes a life completely devoted to God, conducted under His constant care, authority, and observation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:22, Hannah chooses to stay home during the annual family pilgrimage until Samuel is fully weaned. In ancient Israel, weaning typically occurred around two to three years of age. Hannah was not delaying her vow out of hesitation, but was preparing Samuel for a permanent transition.

When Hannah speaks of taking Samuel to appear "before" the Lord, she links his appearance at Shiloh with a lifelong commitment. Unlike ordinary worshippers who appeared before God temporarily during annual feasts, Samuel was being brought before the Lord to "remain there forever." The phrase marks the point where Samuel transitions from his mother's care into continuous sanctuary service under Eli the priest.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Grammatically, panim is a masculine plural noun in Hebrew. In the Hebrew language, this word only occurs in the plural form, whether it refers to one individual face, a single divine presence, or multiple faces.

In 1 Samuel 1:22, the phrase "before the LORD" translates the Hebrew expression lipney YHWH, combining the preposition le ("to" or "at") with panim ("face") and the divine name. This standard Hebrew construction highlights that Hannah brought Samuel directly to the place where God chose to meet His people, fulfilling her pledge with absolute integrity.
