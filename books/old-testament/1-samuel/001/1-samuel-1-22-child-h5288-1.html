CHILD - (naʻar, Strong's H5288)

Summary:

The word na'ar broadly refers to a male child, youth, or attendant, spanning from early infancy to young adulthood. In 1 Samuel 1:22, the word refers specifically to young Samuel during his infancy while still nursing. It highlights Hannah's maternal devotion as she cares for her young son until he is weaned, after which she fulfills her vow to present him for lifelong service to the Lord at Shiloh.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

First Samuel 1 describes the birth and early life of Samuel during a spiritually dark period in Israel's history. Hannah had suffered years of barrenness and ridicule. She prayed fervently at the tabernacle in Shiloh for a male child, promising to dedicate him entirely to the Lord all the days of his life. God answered her prayer, and she gave birth to Samuel.

When the time came for the family's annual pilgrimage to Shiloh, Hannah chose to stay home until the child was fully weaned. In ancient Israel, weaning typically occurred between two and three years of age. Hannah intended to nurture her young son through this initial stage before presenting him to Eli the priest for permanent sanctuary service.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word na'ar describes a male in the early stages of life or in a subordinate position. Its specific sense depends on the context:

  • A young child or infant: It can describe a infant or toddler who requires constant physical care, as seen with Samuel in 1 Samuel 1:22 and David's sick infant in 2 Samuel 12:16.

  • An older boy or youth: It can refer to a boy or young man growing toward adulthood, such as Isaac during Abraham's test or David before his battle with Goliath.

  • A servant or attendant: It can serve as a title for an assistant, household servant, or aide, regardless of exact age, such as a field supervisor's assistant or a priest's attendant.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Scripture, na'ar carries nuances of youthfulness, dependence, or social subordination.

When describing an infant or toddler, the word highlights helpless dependence and the need for parental protection. Parents care for the na'ar through the vulnerable early years of growth.

When describing an adolescent or young adult, the word highlights physical vitality, ongoing growth, or lack of full military and social standing. The young man remains under authority and guidance.

When describing a servant or assistant, the word indicates a functional role of service rather than mere physical age. An attendant acts on behalf of a master or leader. In all cases, the word presents someone who stands under the care, direction, or authority of another.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of na'ar highlights several key truths:

  • God uses the weak and young: God frequently chooses and equips the young or overlooked to accomplish His redemptive purposes.

  • Sacred stewardship of children: God values children from their earliest stages of life. Samuel was set apart for divine service while still an infant, demonstrating that life devoted to God is not reserved only for adults.

  • Faithfulness in practical care: Hannah viewed her child not as personal property, but as a gift from God. She ministered to his early physical needs so that he would be prepared to serve the Lord for the rest of his life.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:22, Hannah states that she will not go up to Shiloh "until the child is weaned." Grammatically, the word is singular and common in gender, pointing specifically to her infant son Samuel.

The word highlights Samuel's young age. Hannah was not delaying her promise out of reluctance; rather, she was waiting for the appropriate developmental milestone so that Samuel could remain at the sanctuary permanently without requiring maternal nursing.

By using this word here, the text emphasizes both Samuel's physical dependency and the depth of Hannah's faith. She prepared to entrust her young, vulnerable child into the care of the Lord at the sanctuary, trusting God to sustain him from his earliest years onward.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 22:12 — Abraham is commanded not to harm the "lad," referring to his young son Isaac during the trial on Mount Moriah.

  • 1 Samuel 17:55 — Saul asks Abner about the identity of the "youth" who went out to face Goliath, emphasizing David's young age relative to seasoned soldiers.

  • 2 Samuel 12:16 — David fasts and prays on behalf of the "child" who is gravely ill, using the word for his infant son.

  • Ruth 2:5 — Boaz inquires of his "servant" who was set over the reapers, showing the word used as a title for a field supervisor.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The presentation of Samuel as a young na'ar at Shiloh contrasts sharply with the corrupt priests serving there at the time. Later in 1 Samuel 2:15, the same word describes the "servant" of Eli's wicked sons, who demanded raw meat from sacrifices through greedy intimidation.

While the priests' servants misused their authority for selfish gain, young Samuel grew before the Lord in holy service. This contrast demonstrates how God was preparing a faithful prophet from early childhood to replace a corrupt priestly house.
