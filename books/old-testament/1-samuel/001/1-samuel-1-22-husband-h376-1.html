HUSBAND - (ʼîysh, Strong's H376)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun 'ish refers fundamentally to an adult male person, an individual, or a husband in relation to his wife. In 1 Samuel 1:22, the word refers specifically to Hannah's husband, Elkanah. In this passage, the term highlights the personal marital bond between Hannah and Elkanah, establishing the household context in which Hannah explains her vow to dedicate Samuel to the service of the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The opening chapter of 1 Samuel introduces the family of Elkanah, a Levite living in the hill country of Ephraim. His wife Hannah was childless and grieved deeply over her barrenness. During the family's annual pilgrimage to the sanctuary at Shiloh, Hannah prayed fervently to the Lord, pledging that if God would give her a male child, she would dedicate him to the Lord for all his life.

God answered Hannah's prayer, and she gave birth to Samuel. When the time came for the annual sacrifice the following year, Elkanah prepared to travel to Shiloh with his household. In 1 Samuel 1:22, Hannah explains to her husband that she will remain behind until the boy is weaned, after which she will bring him to appear before the Lord and remain there permanently.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun 'ish is the standard Hebrew word for an adult male human being. It can refer to a man in contrast to a woman, an individual person in contrast to a group, or a husband in relation to his wife.

When paired with a personal pronoun or possessive construction, the word frequently means "husband." In 1 Samuel 1:22, the noun appears with a possessive marker signifying "her husband." The word itself denotes a male individual; the marital connection arises from the possessive relationship established in the sentence.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In the Hebrew Bible, 'ish functions across several key shades of meaning depending on the surrounding sentence:

  • Male Person: It marks an individual adult male human, as seen in the creation account where man is distinguished in origins and relationship.

  • Husband: When used within a marriage context or accompanied by a possessive suffix, it identifies a woman's husband.

  • Representative or Indefinite Pronoun: It frequently serves as an indefinite pronoun meaning "each person," "everyone," or "anyone."

Because the word is so broad, its specific nuance in any sentence comes entirely from the surrounding context rather than an specialized theological definition within the word itself.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical text uses ordinary relational terms like 'ish to display how godliness operates within daily life and family structures. In 1 Samuel 1, the relationship between Hannah and her husband illustrates godly harmony in fulfilling vows made to the Lord.

Under the Mosaic law, a husband had the legal right to confirm or void a vow made by his wife if he spoke up when he learned of it. Rather than creating tension between marital harmony and religious devotion, the narrative shows Hannah communicating openly with her husband, and Elkanah freely supporting her commitment to the Lord. The passage demonstrates how marital unity serves the higher purpose of worshipping God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:21, Elkanah is described as "the man Elkanah" as he prepares to lead his household to Shiloh. In 1 Samuel 1:22, the narrative shifts perspective to Hannah's direct address, referring to Elkanah as "her husband."

This transition highlights the personal household setting. Grammatically, the target noun is a singular common form marked as Hannah's husband. Hannah does not act in secret or in rebellion against her family obligations. Instead, she brings her decision directly to her husband, explaining why she will wait to go up to Shiloh. Elkanah's affirming response in verse 23 confirms his support for her vow, demonstrating a shared devotion to the Lord's will within their home.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The distinction between referring to Elkanah as "the man" in verse 21 and "her husband" in verse 22 illustrates how Hebrew narrative subtly shifts focus. Verse 21 describes Elkanah's public leadership as head of the household carrying out religious duties. Verse 22 turns to the quiet, private agreement between husband and wife concerning the child God had granted them.

This balance between public obedience and private covenant faithfulness sets the stage for Samuel's dedication. Samuel was not merely given away; he was raised in a home where both mother and father honored God's word and supported one another in godly vows.
