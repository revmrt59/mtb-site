LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

formatted as the proper personal name of the God of Israel, revealing Him as the self-existent, faithful God who enters into relationship with His people. In 1 Samuel 1:22, Hannah uses this name as she prepares to present her young son Samuel at the sanctuary, marking the fulfillment of her solemn vow to surrender him permanently into the personal service and presence of the God who answered her prayer.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The personal name of God, rendered as "Lord" in most English Bibles, stands at the heart of Israel's faith and history. Unlike generic terms for deity, this unique proper name identifies the specific God who revealed Himself to Moses at the burning bush and redeemed Israel from Egypt. It distinguishes the living God of Israel from the idols of surrounding nations.

In the historical setting of 1 Samuel, the nation of Israel was passing through a difficult period under the judges. Central leadership was weak, and spiritual life at the sanctuary in Shiloh had been compromised by priestly corruption. The tabernacle at Shiloh nevertheless remained the central sanctuary where worshippers gathered to offer sacrifices and call upon God. In this context of personal grief and national need, Hannah traveled to Shiloh to pray directly to God by His personal name.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word is the proper personal name of God. It is closely connected to the Hebrew verb meaning "to be," pointing to God's self-existence, eternal presence, and unchanging reality. God is the One who simply is, dependent on nothing outside Himself for His existence or power.

English Bibles traditionally translate this proper name as "LORD" using capital or small-capital letters. This practice helps readers distinguish God's proper name from the general title for master or owner (adonai). When Scripture uses this personal name, it emphasizes that God is not a vague or distant divine force, but a living, personal Being who makes Himself known, hears human prayers, and remains faithful to His word.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament narrative, poetry, and prophecy, God's personal name appears whenever the text highlights His active relationship with His people. While general terms like Elohim emphasize God's power as the Creator and Ruler of the universe, this personal name highlights His closeness, availability, and covenant promises to Israel.

God's people call upon this name when making solemn promises, bringing sacrifices, seeking guidance, and offering worship. It is the name invoked in times of deep anguish and in corporate gatherings. To act or worship "before the Lord" means entering into the localized awareness of His personal presence at His appointed place of worship.

4. Theological Implications

The revelation of God's personal name shows that the God of Scripture desires to be known personally. He does not remain an anonymous force in the heavens. By making His personal name known, He invites His people into a genuine relationship grounded in truth and promise.

This personal revelation brings both deep comfort and high accountability. Because God is the living Lord, He hears the quiet prayers of the brokenhearted and remembers those who place their trust in Him. At the same time, because He is holy and sovereign, vows made to Him are serious commitments that require faithful fulfillment. Dedicating a child or a life to the Lord is not a symbolic ritual, but a real entrustment of that life into the hands of the God who governs history.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:22, Hannah tells her husband Elkanah that she will remain at home with the newborn Samuel until he is fully weaned. After that, she explains, "I will take him, that he may appear before the Lord and remain there forever."

This statement reflects the direct fulfillment of Hannah's earlier prayer. When she was barren and grieving, she prayed to the Lord at Shiloh and promised that if He granted her a son, she would give him back to the Lord for his entire life. Once God answered her cry and Samuel was born, Hannah remained fully committed to her promise.

Her phrase "appear before the Lord" meant bringing Samuel to the sanctuary at Shiloh, where the ark rested. In the sanctuary setting, appearing before the Lord meant standing in the place where God chose to reveal His presence among His people. Hannah was not simply leaving her child at a religious center; she was presenting him directly to the personal God who had heard her sorrow and granted her request.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 — "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the Lord God made the earth and the heavens." Here the personal name is joined with God's creative title, identifying the God of Israel as the personal Creator of the universe.

  • Exodus 13:5 — "And it shall be, when the Lord brings you into the land of the Canaanites..." This passage highlights the Lord as the faithful God who fulfills His sworn promises to deliver His people and bring them into their inheritance.

  • Deuteronomy 18:16 — "...according to all you desired of the Lord your God in Horeb in the day of the assembly..." The word recalls the solemn presence of God at Mount Sinai when the people sought a mediator.

  • Joshua 14:12 — "Now therefore, give me this mountain of which the Lord spoke in that day..." Caleb relies on the specific word spoken by the Lord decades earlier, demonstrating trust in God's reliability.

  • Psalms 25:1 — "To You, O Lord, I lift up my soul." David uses this proper name to address God directly in prayer, expressing personal trust and total surrender.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's dedication of Samuel before the Lord marks a major turning point in biblical history. At a time when Eli's sons were corrupting the priesthood, God raised up Samuel to serve before Him from childhood. Samuel's upbringing in the sanctuary served as a fresh light during a dark period, leading eventually to the renewal of God's word in Israel and the establishment of the monarchy under David. Hannah's faithful execution of her vow before the Lord thus served as the catalyst for national spiritual renewal.
