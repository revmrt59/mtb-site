REMAIN - (yâshab, Strong's H3427)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yashav fundamentally means to sit, dwell, stay, or remain in a place. It describes a person settling down, taking a seat, residing in a territory, or abiding in a location over time. In 1 Samuel 1:22, Hannah uses the word to declare her intention for her young son Samuel: once he is weaned, she will present him at the sanctuary in Shiloh so that he may remain there permanently in the service of the LORD. The word highlights the total, lifelong transition of Samuel from his earthly home to a permanent residence in God's presence.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The opening chapter of 1 Samuel takes place during a dark period of spiritual decline in Israel's history, near the end of the time of the judges. Worship at the central tabernacle in Shiloh had grown corrupt under the aging priest Eli and his wicked sons. In the midst of this spiritual low point, an childless woman named Hannah brought her deep grief to God in prayer. She vowed that if the LORD would give her a son, she would give him back to the LORD for all the days of his life.

God answered Hannah's prayer, and she gave birth to Samuel. While her husband Elkanah traveled up to Shiloh for the annual sacrifice, Hannah stayed home to care for the young child until he was weaned. In 1 Samuel 1:22, Hannah explains her plan to her husband: as soon as the child is weaned, she will bring him to appear before the LORD and remain there continually. The word chosen for his lifelong abiding at the sanctuary is yashav.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb yashav is a common Hebrew word with a direct and concrete primary meaning: to sit down or to take a seat. From this physical posture of sitting down, the word naturally expands to mean remaining, abiding, tarrying, or residing in a place for a period of time.

The word can refer to:

  • Sitting down on a seat, chair, throne, or the ground.

  • Staying or tarrying in a location temporarily.

  • Dwelling or residing in a home, city, or country as a settled resident.

  • Inhabiting a region, as a population lives in a land.

Here in 1 Samuel 1:22, the word means to dwell or remain in a settled, long-term capacity. It describes Samuel moving into the sanctuary to reside where God's presence dwells.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament narrative, poetry, and law, yashav covers the full spectrum of posture and settlement. The simplest physical sense appears when individuals sit down to rest, eat, or hold counsel. Judges and kings sit on thrones to administer justice, and elders sit at the town gates to conduct legal affairs.

Beyond bodily posture, yashav frequently describes fixed residence. It marks the difference between a nomad passing through a land and a settled resident who builds a home and remains. When applied to God, Scripture uses yashav to describe Him enthroned in heaven or dwelling among His people above the mercy seat. When applied to people in relation to God, the word often expresses the privilege of abiding continuously in His house or protection.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's use of yashav carries significant theological weight in the story of redemption. While ordinary worshipers traveled to Shiloh for brief annual visits and then returned home, Hannah vowed that Samuel would remain there indefinitely. His residence would not be a temporary pilgrimage, but a permanent life of service.

This lifelong placement of Samuel at the sanctuary marks the fulfillment of a solemn vow and the surrender of a precious gift. Hannah received Samuel from God in answer to prayer, and she returned him completely to the LORD. By staying at Shiloh, Samuel was positioned from his youth to hear God's voice, restore faithful prophetic leadership to Israel, and anoint Israel's first kings. His permanent abiding in God's house prepared the way for spiritual renewal throughout the nation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:22, Hannah tells Elkanah, "Not until the child is weaned; then I will take him, that he may appear before the LORD and remain there forever." The phrase "remain there forever" combines yashav with the expression ad-olam ("forever" or "continually"), emphasizing that Samuel's move to Shiloh was intended to be permanent and lifelong.

The surrounding context contrasts two distinct ways of approaching God's sanctuary. Elkanah and his household went up year by year to offer sacrifices, staying at Shiloh briefly before returning to their home in Ramah. Hannah delay her journey temporarily so that when Samuel finally went up, he would not return home with them. Instead, he would remain at the tabernacle.

Grammatically, the verb appears in a masculine singular perfect form, describing the settled reality that will mark Samuel's life once he is presented to the LORD. The focus is on the steady, ongoing state of dwelling in God's presence rather than a brief visit.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 4:16 — "Then Cain went out from the presence of the LORD and dwelt in the land of Nod on the east of Eden."

Here the word is rendered "dwelt," describing Cain establishing a settled residency away from his original homeland after his rebellion.

  • 1 Samuel 1:9 — "So Hannah arose after they had finished eating and drinking in Shiloh. Now Eli the priest was sitting on the seat by the doorpost of the tabernacle of the LORD."

In this earlier verse in the same chapter, the word is translated "sitting," referring to Eli's physical posture on his chair near the sanctuary door.

  • 2 Samuel 7:1 — "Now it came to pass when the king was dwelling in his house, and the LORD had given him rest all around from all his enemies..."

Here the word is rendered "dwelling," describing King David settled comfortably in his completed palace in Jerusalem.

  • 1 Kings 11:16 — "For six months Joab and all Israel remained there, until he had cut off every male in Edom."

In this military context, the word is translated "remained," referring to an army staying in a conquered region for an extended duration.

  • Psalms 123:1 — "Unto You I lift up my eyes, O You who dwell in the heavens."

In this Psalm of Ascents, the word is translated "dwell," describing God enthroned and reigning in the heavens above.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The commitment Hannah made for Samuel stands in sharp contrast to the conduct of Eli's sons, Hophni and Phinehas, who also lived at Shiloh. While Eli's sons used their place at the sanctuary for selfish gain and corrupt behavior, Samuel was placed there as a pure offering of gratitude.

Hannah's willingness to leave her young son to remain at the tabernacle was not an act of abandonment, but an act of supreme faith. She trusted God to care for the boy in the sanctuary, visiting him each year and bringing him a new robe as he grew. God honored Hannah's selfless vow: not only did Samuel grow up to become one of Israel's greatest judges and prophets, but God also blessed Hannah with three more sons and two daughters.
