SAID - (ʼâmar, Strong's H559)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb amar means to say, speak, tell, or declare. It is the most common Hebrew word used to introduce spoken conversation, formal statements, or personal thoughts. In 1 Samuel 1:22, the word introduces Hannah's direct statement to her husband Elkanah, explaining that she will stay behind until her son Samuel is weaned so she can present him at the sanctuary for lifelong service to the LORD.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The verb amar appears throughout the Old Testament to introduce direct speech between individuals, divine pronouncements, and spoken reflections. In the ancient Hebrew culture, direct discourse was central to storytelling, legal agreements, and personal relationships. Rather than merely summarizing what someone believed, Old Testament narratives regularly present actual speech to display character, faith, and motives.

In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah had suffered years of childlessness and intense grief. After praying fervently at the tabernacle in Shiloh, God granted her request, and she gave birth to Samuel. When the time came for Elkanah and his family to make their annual journey to offer sacrifices to the LORD, Hannah chose to stay home in Ramah until the infant was fully weaned.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word amar refers broadly to vocalized communication. It can mean:

  • To say or tell someone something in ordinary conversation.

  • To declare or announce a decision.

  • To command or instruct someone to act.

  • To think or reason silently ("to say in one's heart").

In 1 Samuel 1:22, the verb simply means "said." It introduces Hannah's explanation to her husband regarding her plan to fulfill her vow to God.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses amar in a wide variety of contexts. It marks the creative speech of God, human dialogue, prophetic announcements, and internal motives.

In 1 Samuel 1:22, the grammatical form is a feminine singular perfect verb. This indicates that the subject is Hannah ("she") and places her spoken words within the past narrative sequence. The verb itself functions smoothly to shift the attention from the general family journey to Hannah's specific conversation with Elkanah. The clarity of her purpose comes from the content of her speech rather than the grammatical form of the verb.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's spoken words in 1 Samuel 1:22 reveal her dedication to the vow she made before the LORD. In 1 Samuel 1:11, she promised that if God gave her a son, she would give him back to the LORD all the days of his life.

Her statement highlights several biblical truths:

  • Faithful stewardship: Hannah took loving care of her child during his infant years, recognizing that her nurture prepared him for his future calling.

  • Integrity in promises: Hannah did not forget her promise once she received the blessing. She communicated clearly with her husband so her vow would be carried out in full.

  • Harmony in the home: Rather than acting independently without communication, she spoke openly with Elkanah, who respected her faith and supported her commitment.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

The context of 1 Samuel 1:22 sets up an important moment in the history of Israel. Elkanah went up with his family to perform the annual sacrifice, but Hannah declined to go at that time.

She told her husband, "Not until the child is weaned; then I will take him, that he may appear before the LORD and remain there forever." Her spoken explanation shows a thoughtful plan. Weaning in ancient Israel often occurred around two to three years of age. Hannah was not avoiding her religious duty; she was preparing to render the ultimate service by bringing Samuel to Shiloh permanently. Elkanah accepted her spoken plan, responding in verse 23 by encouraging her to do what was best and praying that the LORD would establish His word.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's dialogue in 1 Samuel 1 provides a striking contrast to the silent prayer she offered earlier in chapter 1. In 1 Samuel 1:13, she spoke only in her heart, and Eli mistaken her for a drunken woman because her voice was not heard. By 1 Samuel 1:22, her situation has changed completely. She speaks openly and clearly to her husband about God's faithfulness and her intent to honor her promise. Her voice, once choked with sorrow, is now used to outline a plan of dedicated service to God.
