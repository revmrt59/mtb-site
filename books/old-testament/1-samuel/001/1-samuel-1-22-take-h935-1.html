TAKE - (bôwʼ, Strong's H935)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb bo' basic meaning is to come, go, or enter. When used to describe moving an object or person, it means to bring, escort, or conduct someone to a destination. In 1 Samuel 1:22, the word is translated "take" (in the sense of bringing or escorting). It describes Hannah's commitment to bring her young son Samuel to the sanctuary at Shiloh once he is weaned, presenting him before the LORD to fulfill her solemn vow.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The book of 1 Samuel opens during a spiritually low period in Israel's history. The tabernacle sat at Shiloh, where the priesthood had become corrupt under Eli's sons. Yet devout Israelites, such as Elkanah and his wife Hannah, still made annual journeys to offer sacrifices to God.

Hannah suffered deep grief because she was childless and faced continual provocation from Elkanah's other wife. During one annual trip to Shiloh, Hannah wept bitterly before God and made a solemn vow: if God would give her a male child, she would give him back to the LORD for all the days of his life. God graciously remembered Hannah, and she gave birth to Samuel. When the time came for the next annual trip, Hannah stayed behind, explaining that once the child was weaned, she would bring him to Shiloh to live in God's service permanently.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb bo' is one of the most common verbs of movement in Hebrew. It refers to directional motion toward a specific point or person.

Depending on the context, bo' can mean:

  • To come or arrive at a place.

  • To go into a building, city, or territory.

  • To bring, carry, or lead a person, sacrifice, or gift to a destination.

In 1 Samuel 1:22, the verb carries the sense of bringing or leading someone to a place. Hannah uses the word to describe bringing her young child to the tabernacle at Shiloh to present him before the LORD.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, bo' appears across many settings, from historical narratives to prophetic visions:

  • Everyday Movement: It describes people entering houses, entering cities, or returning from journeys.

  • Worship and Offerings: It is frequently used for bringing sacrifices, tithes, or worshipers into the court of the tabernacle or temple.

  • Personal Escort: It describes leading or introducing someone into the presence of an authority or king.

When used in religious settings, bringing someone or something to God's sanctuary represents a formal act of presentation, dedication, or worship.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah’s statement in 1 Samuel 1:22 highlights key theological truths about stewardship and worship:

  • Everything belongs to God: Hannah recognized that Samuel was an answer to prayer and a gift from God. Bringing him back to Shiloh was an act of surrendering what God had graciously provided.

  • Faithful Vows: Scriptural worship involves honoring commitments made to God. Hannah did not hold back her only son once God granted her request.

  • Living Presentation: While most worshipers brought animal sacrifices to Shiloh, Hannah brought her living child to be dedicated to lifelong sacred service.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:22, Hannah explains her plan to her husband Elkanah. She chooses not to join the annual family trip while Samuel is still an infant. Instead, she plans to nurse him until he is fully weaned so that when she does travel, he can remain at Shiloh continuously.

The verb bo' (translated "take" in the NKJV) names the action of bringing Samuel to the sanctuary. Grammatically, the verb form here is singular in number, common in gender, and perfect in form. It functions within Hannah's statement to describe the eventual action she intends to perform after the weaning process is complete.

Hannah's personal devotion and determination to keep her vow are clear from the surrounding narrative and her earlier prayer. The verb bo' simply describes the physical act of presenting Samuel at Shiloh, where he would remain before the LORD for the rest of his life.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In ancient Israel, weaning typically occurred when a child was two or three years old. This extra time allowed the young child to grow strong enough to leave home and live under the care of Eli the priest at the tabernacle.

Hannah’s act of bringing Samuel to Shiloh marks a major turning point in Israel's history. Samuel’s presence at the tabernacle established the prophet who would eventually anoint King David and guide Israel through its transition into a kingdom.
