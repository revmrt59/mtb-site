UP - (ʻâlâh, Strong's H5927)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb alah basic meaning is to ascend, climb, or go up from a lower place to a higher one. It is used throughout the Old Testament to describe physical travel up a hill or mountain, journeys to central places of worship, the rising of smoke, and the offering up of sacrifices. In 1 Samuel 1:22, alah describes Hannah's decision not to go up to the sanctuary at Shiloh for the annual family sacrifice until her son Samuel was fully weaned, after which she would present him permanently to the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the geography of ancient Israel, traveling to a place of central worship, a prominent city, or a mountainous region was almost always described as an upward movement. Topographically, places like Shiloh and Jerusalem were elevated compared to much of the surrounding countryside. Beyond geography, going up to the sanctuary carried strong religious significance. To leave home and travel toward the place where God chose to reveal His presence was an intentional act of devotion, moving upward toward the altar and the house of the Lord.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Alah means to go up, ascend, or move from a lower position to a higher one.

Depending on the context, the word is used in several distinct ways:

  • Moving physically to higher ground, such as climbing a hill, ascending a mountain, or traveling up to a fortified city or region.

  • Journeying to a holy place or central place of worship to appear before the Lord.

  • Rising upward, such as smoke ascending from an altar or vegetation springing up from the ground.

  • Bringing or offering up a sacrifice to God on an altar.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses alah across many contexts to describe physical, ceremonial, and spiritual ascents. When people travel to perform sacred duties, alah reflects their deliberate movement toward God's appointed place. When used for offerings, the word connects the physical rising of smoke with the presentation of a gift to God.

The verb captures both the physical step of climbing and the spiritual purpose of approaching the Lord. Whether describing a leader ascending a mountain to meet God, a family traveling to a yearly festival, or an offering lifted upon an altar, alah emphasizes an upward trajectory toward a specific destination or purpose.

4. Theological Implications

The idea of going up to meet God underscores that worship requires dynamic initiative. Returning to the central sanctuary was a physical reflection of a heart seeking God's presence. Ascending to the house of the Lord reminded Israel that approaching the holy God involved leaving behind the routines of common life.

Additionally, alah links worship with dedication. The same word used for traveling to the sanctuary is used elsewhere for ascending altars and presenting sacrifices. Going up to God's house was meant to culminate in personal surrender and joyful worship before Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:22, Elkanah and his household prepare to make their annual journey to Shiloh to offer the yearly sacrifice and pay his vow. However, the text states that Hannah did not go up. The verb appears here in a feminine singular perfect form, referring directly to Hannah's specific action.

Hannah's choice not to go up in this particular instance was not a spiritual decline or a neglect of worship. Instead, it was an act of thoughtful preparation to fulfill her solemn vow. She explained to her husband that she would wait until the child was weaned. Once Samuel was weaned, she would bring him up so that he could appear before the Lord and remain in His service permanently.

Her temporary delay ensured that when she did make the journey up to Shiloh, she would bring her young son to give him completely to the Lord. What appeared on the surface as missing a seasonal pilgrimage was actually setting the stage for a lifetime of dedicated service.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 24:18 — Moses went "up" into the mountain into the cloud to meet with God and receive His law.

  • 1 Samuel 9:14 — Saul and his servant went "up" into the city, where they met the prophet Samuel on his way to the high place.

  • 2 Samuel 2:2 — David went "up" to Hebron at the Lord's command before being anointed king over the house of Judah.

  • Psalms 68:18 — The Lord "ascended" on high, leading captivity captive and receiving gifts among men.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast in 1 Samuel 1 between Elkanah going up for a temporary visit and Hannah preparing for a permanent presentation highlights the depth of Hannah's devotion. Elkanah went up annually to worship and return home; Hannah waited so that when Samuel finally went up, he would remain in the Lord's house forever. Her short delay served an enduring spiritual objective.
