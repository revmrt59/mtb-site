WEANED - (gâmal, Strong's H1580)

Summary:

The Hebrew word gamal means to bring something to completion, deal fully with a person, yield fruit, or complete a developmental stage. When applied to a child, it specifically refers to weaning—the transition from nursing to eating solid food and achieving independent sustenance. In 1 Samuel 1:22, the word identifies the complete conclusion of Samuel's infancy, marking the exact milestone when Hannah would fulfill her vow by bringing her young son to remain permanently in the service of the Lord at Shiloh.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israel, weaning was a significant milestone in a child's development. Infants were typically nursed until about two or three years of age. Given the high rates of infant mortality in the ancient world, successfully reaching the age of weaning was a cause for celebration and gratitude, signaling that a child had survived the most vulnerable stage of early life.

The historical setting of 1 Samuel 1 highlights Hannah's deep heartache over her long years of childlessness. In her distress, she prayed fervently at the tabernacle in Shiloh, asking God for a male child and vowing to dedicate him entirely to the Lord's service for all the days of his life. When God answered her prayer and Samuel was born, Hannah intentionally stayed home during the annual family pilgrimage until the boy reached the age of weaning. The word gamal sets the physical boundary for when her solemn vow would be fulfilled.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word gamal fundamentally means to finish, carry through to completion, or deal fully with something. Depending on how it is used in a sentence, it carries a few closely related applications:

  • To wean a child: Completing the nursing period so that an infant transitions fully to solid food.

  • To yield or ripen: Bringing a natural process to fruition, such as a branch yielding mature fruit.

  • To deal bountifully or repay: Treating someone fully, whether by extending generous favor or repaying actions according to what is deserved.

In every case, the word points to a process reaching its full outcome or proper maturation rather than remaining partial or incomplete.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses gamal across physical, natural, and moral settings. In passages dealing with childhood, such as the weaning of Isaac or Samuel, gamal highlights the successful completion of an infant's nursing period.

In natural settings, the word describes a biological process reaching maturity. For example, when Aaron's rod sprouted overnight in the tabernacle, it produced blossoms and yielded ripe almonds, demonstrating God's supernatural validation of the priesthood.

In moral and relational settings, gamal describes dealing fully with someone. When used of God's actions toward His people, it reflects His generous dealings, as when a psalmist declares that the Lord has dealt bountifully with his soul. When used of human relationships, it can describe repaying good for evil or rendering back actions that are deserved. Across these uses, the common thread is reaching a complete measure in treatment or growth.

4. Theological Implications

The process of weaning carries rich theological truth in Scripture. Physical weaning illustrates how God leads His children through necessary stages of maturation. A nursing child depends entirely on mother's milk, but a weaned child learns to rest in the mother's presence with quiet trust rather than demanding immediate feeding. Psalm 131 uses this exact imagery to depict a quieted soul that has learned content submission to God.

Furthermore, Hannah's handling of Samuel shows the proper balance between parental duty and sacred devotion. Hannah did not rush to surrender Samuel before he was physically ready, nor did she use his youth as an excuse to break her promise. She faithfully nurtured him through infancy until he was weaned, treating her maternal care as part of her sacred responsibility to prepare him for God's service.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:22, Hannah tells her husband Elkanah that she will not travel to Shiloh "until the child is weaned." The masculine singular imperfect form presents the weaning as an expected, upcoming milestone that must be completed before the next step takes place.

Hannah's decision was practical and faithful. Living at the tabernacle under the care of the aging priest Eli required that Samuel be past total nursing dependence. By waiting until the child was weaned, Hannah ensured that Samuel was healthy and ready to live in the sanctuary. Far from delaying out of reluctance to part with her long-awaited son, Hannah was waiting for the exact moment when her vow could be carried out fully and permanently.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 21:8 — "So the child grew and was weaned. And Abraham made a great feast on the same day that Isaac was weaned."

  • Numbers 17:8 — "Now it came to pass on the next day that Moses went into the tabernacle of witness, and behold, the rod of Aaron, of the house of Levi, had sprouted and put forth buds, had produced blossoms and yielded ripe almonds."

  • 1 Samuel 1:23 — "Then Elkanah her husband said to her, 'Do what seems best to you; wait until you have weaned him. Only let the LORD establish His word.' So the woman stayed and nursed her son until she had weaned him."

  • Psalms 116:7 — "Return to your rest, O my soul, for the LORD has dealt bountifully with you."

  • Psalms 131:2 — "Surely I have calmed and quieted my soul, Like a weaned child with his mother; Like a weaned child is my soul within me."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's patient care stands in sharp contrast to the spiritual decay present at Shiloh during the time of Eli's sons. While the sanctuary was marked by corruption, Hannah was quietly nurturing a child in holiness at home. By bringing Samuel to Shiloh only after he was weaned, she introduced a fully prepared child into God's house, where Samuel would grow up listening to the voice of the Lord and eventually serve as Israel's prophet and judge.
