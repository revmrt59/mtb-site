DO - (ʻâsâh, Strong's H6213)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'asah is a foundational action word in the Old Testament, commonly translated as "do," "make," "perform," or "accomplish." It expresses active participation, carrying out an action, or bringing something to completion. In 1 Samuel 1:23, Elkanah uses a feminine singular imperative form of this verb to give his wife Hannah direct permission to carry out what she believes best regarding the nursing and weaning of her infant son Samuel before fulfilling her solemn vow to the LORD.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The verb 'asah is one of the most common action verbs in the Hebrew Bible. It spans a vast array of practical human activities as well as grand acts of divine creation and providence. Its basic force is concrete rather than abstract. It refers to putting thought or purpose into practical effect, whether that involves shaping physical objects, carrying out duties, fulfilling promises, or executing specific choices.

In Israelite household life, everyday actions were tied to the social and spiritual obligations of the family. In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah had suffered years of childlessness, endured provocation, and poured out her heart before God at the sanctuary in Shiloh. The LORD answered her prayer, giving her a son named Samuel. Hannah then made a vow to dedicate him to the service of the LORD once he was weaned. In this family setting, Elkanah speaks to his wife using 'asah to encourage her to carry out her plan for caring for the infant.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word 'asah can refer to making something physical, performing a task, carrying out a request, or executing a choice. When applied to God, it often describes His work of creation or His sovereign deeds in human history. When applied to people, it describes human action across every sphere of life, including ethical conduct, manual work, worship, and personal decisions.

Here in 1 Samuel 1:23, the word means to act or carry out a planned decision. The grammatical form is a feminine singular imperative, directly addressing Hannah. By saying "Do what seems good to you," Elkanah instructs and authorizes her to take whatever steps she considers proper for the child's care during his infancy.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Scripture, 'asah carries several shades of meaning depending on who is performing the action and what is being brought about:

  • Creating or constructing: The verb often describes crafting physical items, building structures, or fashioning the created world.

  • Fulfilling or accomplishing: It is used for carrying out vows, obeying commands, or putting plans into motion.

  • Exercising choice: It frequently appears in idiomatic expressions where a person is urged or permitted to act according to judgment, wisdom, or practical necessity.

In conversational settings, telling someone to "do what is good in your eyes" is a standard Hebrew idiom expressing consent, trust, or granting freedom to make a decision. The word itself simply denotes action, while the surrounding phrase clarifies that the action is guided by personal discernment.

4. Theological Implications

While 'asah is a standard verb for human action, its use in the opening chapter of 1 Samuel highlights important biblical principles:

  • Human action operating within divine purpose: Hannah’s decision to stay home until Samuel is weaned shows practical wisdom. Her action is not opposed to her devotion to God; rather, sensible human action serves the ultimate fulfillment of her sacred vow.

  • Domestic harmony and spiritual commitment: Elkanah does not overrule his wife's plan. He grants her freedom to act ('asah) while adding a prayer that the LORD may establish His word. This reflects a household where human decision-making and dependence on God go hand in hand.

  • The reality of fulfilled promises: Vows made to God were meant to be completed. Using an imperative of action underlines that Hannah's vow was not merely empty words, but something that would be carried out in tangible, historical reality.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:23, Hannah has chosen not to travel up to the annual sacrifice at Shiloh until the boy is weaned, so that when she presents him, he may remain there permanently. Elkanah responds to her plan by saying, "Do what seems good to you; wait until you have weaned him."

Elkanah’s statement uses the feminine singular imperative form of 'asah, addressing Hannah directly as a singular recipient of this instruction. The command grants explicit marital consent and domestic freedom. In ancient Israelite law, a husband had the legal right to confirm or cancel certain vows made by his wife. By telling Hannah to "do" what seems right to her, Elkanah affirms her judgment and supports her dedication to nursing the child.

Furthermore, Elkanah balances his authorization of her action with a expression of trust in God: "Only let the LORD establish His word." Elkanah recognizes that while Hannah must take practical steps in caring for Samuel, the ultimate success and spiritual destiny of their child rest in the LORD’s hands.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The phrase translated "what seems good to you" literally reads "what is good in your eyes." Combining this common idiom with the imperative form of 'asah shows how biblical Hebrew expresses trust and delegation. Elkanah does not dictate every detail of infant care to Hannah; he trusts her spiritual integrity and motherly wisdom to choose the right timing for presenting Samuel to the LORD.

This interaction shows that faithful obedience in Scripture often involves practical discernment rather than rigid haste. Hannah's delay was not a failure of resolve, but a wise preparation to ensure the child was ready for his lifelong service at the sanctuary.
