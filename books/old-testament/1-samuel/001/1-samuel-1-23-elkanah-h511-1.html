ELKANAH - (ʼElqânâh, Strong's H511)

Summary:

The proper noun Elqanah is a Hebrew name meaning "God has created" or "God has acquired." It is formed by combining the divine name El ("God") with a root verb meaning to create, buy, or possess. In 1 Samuel 1:23, the name identifies the father of Samuel as he lovingly supports his wife Hannah's vow to dedicate their young son to the LORD, placing the fulfillment of God's purpose above personal claim or domestic convenience.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The name Elqanah belongs to several men in the Old Testament, most notably the father of the prophet Samuel. The name joins the noun El ("God") with the Hebrew verb qanah, which means to acquire, create, or possess. Names formed in this way were personal confessions of faith, declaring that the child was created or possessed by God.

Historically, Elkanah the father of Samuel lived during the chaotic final years of the period of the judges. He was a Levite residing in the hill country of Ephraim at Ramathaim Zophim. Despite living in an era marked by moral decay and corrupt priesthood at Shiloh, Elkanah maintained a household grounded in worship, annual sacrifice, and obedience to God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Elqanah is a masculine proper noun. The name is composed of two distinct elements:

  • El: The standard ancient Hebrew word for God, emphasizing His power and majesty.

  • qanah: A verb meaning to acquire, buy, produce, or create.

Together, the name translates to "God has created" or "God has acquired." It carries the underlying sense that the individual bearing the name is a possession of God, brought into existence by divine power. In Hebrew usage, it serves as a personal identifier that honors God's creative ownership and sovereign initiative.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the name Elkanah for several different men across Old Testament history:

  • A son of Korah listed in the Exodus genealogy.

  • The father of Samuel the prophet, who is central to the narrative of 1 Samuel 1–2.

  • An ancestor of Asaph the singer and several other Levites listed in Chronicles.

  • A warrior from the tribe of Benjamin who joined David at Ziklag.

  • A high official in the court of King Ahaz of Judah.

In the early chapters of 1 Samuel, the narrative presents Elkanah as a devout head of his household. He faithfully travels year after year to the tabernacle at Shiloh to offer worship and sacrifice. Even when faced with family friction between his wives Hannah and Peninnah, Elkanah demonstrates affection, spiritual care, and respect for his wife's devotion to God.

4. Theological Implications

The name Elkanah highlights God's rightful ownership over life and family. Because God is the Creator who acquires and keeps His people, every blessing within a family ultimately belongs to Him.

In the life of Samuel's father, this truth is put into practice. When God answered Hannah's desperate prayer for a son, Elkanah did not treat the child merely as his personal heir or legacy. He recognized that Samuel belonged first to the LORD. Elkanah's life illustrates how godliness expresses itself in the family: leading by example in worship, encouraging a spouse's faith, and releasing what is precious into God's service.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:23, Hannah has resolved to remain at home until her young child is weaned, after which she will present him at Shiloh for lifelong service in the sanctuary. Elkanah responds to his wife with remarkable faith and support: "Do what seems best to you; wait until you have weaned him. Only let the LORD establish His word."

In ancient near eastern culture, a husband possessed legal authority to annul a wife's voluntary vow. Elkanah does not use his authority to block Hannah's commitment or keep the child at home for his own comfort. Instead, he yields his paternal rights to God's higher purpose. His spoken desire—"Only let the LORD establish His word"—shows that Elkanah valued the fulfillment of God's promise above all personal interests.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Elkanah's faithful home stands in sharp contrast to the spiritual failure of Eli the priest and his sons, Hophni and Phinehas, recorded in the same chapters. While Eli failed to restrain his sons' wickedness in the sanctuary, Elkanah cultivated an environment of piety and honor at home. God used this quiet household in the hill country of Ephraim to prepare Samuel—the prophet who would lead Israel out of darkness and anoint its first kings.
