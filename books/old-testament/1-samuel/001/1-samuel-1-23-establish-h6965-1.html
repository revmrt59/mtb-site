ESTABLISH - (qûwm, Strong's H6965)

Summary:

The basic meaning of qum is to rise, stand up, or come into a standing position. In its causative form, it means to raise up, set up, or bring something into fulfillment. In 1 Samuel 1:23, Elkanah uses this word to ask that God confirm and fulfill His promise regarding Hannah and her infant son Samuel, showing that human plans and vows ultimately rely on God to bring them to completion.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root word qum fundamentally describes physical posture—moving from a seated or lying position to a standing position. Throughout daily life in ancient Israel, people used the word for getting out of bed, standing up to show respect, or rising to go on a journey.

In public and spiritual settings, the word extended beyond simple movement. Causing something to stand came to mean setting up a monument, ratifying a legal covenant, or bringing a spoken word to visible fulfillment. In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah had prayed in deep distress for a son, promising to dedicate him to the LORD. After Samuel was born, Hannah chose to stay home until the child was weaned before presenting him at the sanctuary. Elkanah supported her decision, adding a prayer that God would establish His word.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to simple physical movement, such as arising, standing, or getting up. When used in its causative sense—where the subject causes an action to happen to someone or something else—the word means to set up, build, confirm, fulfill, or make good on a promise.

When a person is the subject, it often describes someone standing up from a chair, rising to speak, or setting up an altar or pillar. When God is the subject in this causative sense, it describes God bringing His plans to reality, upholding His covenant declarations, or validating a prophetic word.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses qum across a wide variety of settings. It appears in ordinary historical narratives when people get up to travel or perform daily chores. It also carries solemn legal weight when confirming an oath or establishing a contract.

In Israelite thought, a word spoken by God was not merely sound; it carried active authority. To "cause a word to stand" meant ensuring that what God declared did not fall to the ground or fail to materialize. The term contrasts something fleeting or unstable with something firmly rooted and realized in history.

4. Theological Implications

Elkanah’s request highlights a key Biblical truth: God is faithful to confirm and fulfill His promises. Human beings may make vows, express hopes, or make plans, but only God can make a spiritual calling stand firm.

Hannah made a sacred vow, and Eli had declared a blessing over her. Yet Elkanah recognized that the ultimate success of Samuel’s life and calling did not depend merely on Hannah's faithfulness or Eli's priestly position. It required God Himself to actively establish what He had begun.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:23, Elkanah says to Hannah: "Do what seems best to you; wait until you have weaned him. Only let the LORD establish His word."

In the grammatical structure of this verse, the verb appears as a masculine singular imperfect form in the causative stem. It functions here as a respectful request or prayer. Elkanah yields to Hannah's practical decision to stay home during the child's infancy, but he immediately points her attention back to God.

This context shows a healthy blend of human responsibility and reliance on God. Hannah nursed and cared for the child, providing necessary parental care. Meanwhile, Elkanah recognized that God's overarching purpose for Samuel required divine confirmation. The word connects Hannah’s private home life with God’s larger redemption history in Israel.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Jeremiah 28:6 — NKJV Span: "perform" — "...Amen! The LORD do so; the LORD perform your words which you have prophesied..."

  • Proverbs 31:28 — NKJV Span: "up" — "Her children rise up and call her blessed; her husband also, and he praises her."

  • Psalms 1:5 — NKJV Span: "stand" — "Therefore the ungodly shall not stand in the judgment, nor sinners in the congregation of the righteous."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Elkanah’s prayer in 1 Samuel 1:23 anticipates the future ministry of Samuel. Later in the narrative, 1 Samuel 3:19 notes that as Samuel grew, the LORD was with him and "let none of his words fall to the ground." The very dynamic Elkanah prayed for in chapter 1 became the defining feature of Samuel's prophetic ministry: God steadily established His word through Samuel's life.
