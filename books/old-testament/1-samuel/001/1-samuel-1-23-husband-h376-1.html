HUSBAND - (ʼîysh, Strong's H376)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun 'ish refers fundamentally to an adult male human being, a man, an individual person, or a husband in relation to his wife. In 1 Samuel 1:23, the word is translated "husband" and designates Elkanah in his specific marital relationship to Hannah. By identifying Elkanah as her "husband," the text highlights his role as the head of the household who validates and supports Hannah's vow regarding young Samuel, showing his shared commitment to letting God's word be established in their family.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The narrative of 1 Samuel 1 takes place during the close of the period of the judges, a time marked by spiritual instability in Israel. The opening chapter focuses on the family of Elkanah, an Israelite living in the hill country of Ephraim. Elkanah regularly took his family to the tabernacle at Shiloh to offer sacrifices to the LORD.

Within this family context, Hannah suffered from barrenness and severe distress. During one of their visits to Shiloh, Hannah prayed fervently to the LORD, making a vow that if God granted her a son, she would give him back to the LORD for all the days of his life. God answered her prayer, and she gave birth to Samuel. When the time came for the annual pilgrimage, Elkanah prepared to go, but Hannah requested to stay home until the child was weaned. In 1 Samuel 1:23, Elkanah's title as her "husband" marks his active leadership and legal agreement with Hannah's plan.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to an adult male, a husband, an individual person, or a warrior. When contrasted with a woman ('ishshah), it highlights male gender. When paired with a possessive marker ("her husband"), it specifies a married spouse.

In 1 Samuel 1:23, the word means "husband." It identifies Elkanah not merely as a male person, but specifically as Hannah's married partner, holding household responsibility and authority under Old Testament law.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout Scripture, 'ish is the most common Hebrew word used for a man or husband. It appears in a wide variety of contexts:

  • It describes an individual male in distinction from a woman or child.

  • It designates a husband in relation to his wife, forming a pair ('ish and 'ishshah) established in the creation narrative.

  • It functions as an indefinite pronoun meaning "everyone" or "each man."

Grammatically in 1 Samuel 1:23, the noun is singular, referring directly to one man, Elkanah. While the word itself is simple and widespread, the surrounding words clarify its specific meaning in this passage as "husband."

4. Theological Implications

Under Old Testament law, particularly in Numbers 30, a husband possessed the authority to confirm or cancel any vow made by his wife if he learned of it. If a husband raised no objection, the vow remained legally binding before God.

Elkanah's response in 1 Samuel 1:23 carries significant theological weight. Rather than overriding Hannah's vow to dedicate Samuel to Shiloh, he confirms her plan and adds a prayer: "Only let the LORD establish His word." This demonstrates godly headship in the home. His priority was not retaining personal comfort or family inheritance, but aligning his household with God's purpose and divine word.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:23, Elkanah responds to Hannah's proposal to remain home until Samuel is weaned: "So Elkanah her husband said to her, 'Do what seems best to you; wait until you have weaned him. Only let the LORD establish His word.'"

Calling Elkanah "her husband" at this precise moment emphasizes his marital role in approving Hannah's choice. Rather than causing domestic conflict over Hannah's vow, Elkanah offers full support and spiritual encouragement. His main concern is that God's plan for the child be fulfilled. The word highlights his role as a loving spouse and spiritual partner who yields his parental rights over Samuel to the LORD.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:23 – "She shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man." Here the word refers to the first male human in relation to the creation of woman.

  • Judges 17:6 – "In those days there was no king in Israel; everyone did what was right in his own eyes." The word is used broadly to refer to each individual person in the nation.

  • 1 Samuel 1:21 – "Now the man Elkanah and all his house went up to offer to the LORD the yearly sacrifice and his vow." The word identifies Elkanah earlier in the narrative as the head of his household leading his family in worship.

  • Proverbs 16:2 – "All the ways of a man are pure in his own eyes, but the LORD weighs the spirits." The word represents an individual human being whose motives are evaluated by God.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Notice the slight shift in description between 1 Samuel 1:21 and 1 Samuel 1:23. In verse 21, Elkanah is called "the man," emphasizing his general role as head of the worshipping household. In verse 23, he is named "her husband," shifting focus to his intimate, covenantal conversation with Hannah. This movement shows his consistent character both in public family worship and in private marital decisions.
