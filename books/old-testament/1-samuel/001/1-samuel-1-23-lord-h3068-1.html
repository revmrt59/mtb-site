LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

The personal, covenant name of God in the Old Testament, identifying the living God who reveals Himself and keeps His promises to His people. In 1 Samuel 1:23, Elkanah uses this divine name to commit his family's plans to God's sovereign care, asking the LORD to establish and fulfill what He had promised regarding the young child Samuel.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the Old Testament, God revealed Himself to Israel by a personal proper name, transliterated into English as YHWH and traditionally rendered in English Bibles as "the LORD" using capital letters. This title distinguishes the true God of Israel from generic references to deity or false idols.

In the opening chapter of 1 Samuel, Hannah suffers deeply because she is childless. In her distress, she goes to the sanctuary at Shiloh and prays to God for a son, promising to dedicate him entirely to His service. God hears her prayer, and she gives birth to Samuel. When her husband Elkanah prepares to travel to Shiloh for the yearly sacrifice, Hannah decides to remain home until the child is weaned. In verse 23, Elkanah agrees with her decision, invoking the LORD's personal name as he prays that God will bring His divine purpose for Samuel to completion.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name YHWH is the personal name of Israel's God. It is closely connected to the Hebrew verb meaning "to be," expressing God's active, living presence and continuous existence.

Rather than functioning as a descriptive title, the word acts as a personal proper name. When Scripture uses this name, it points to the God who acts in history, makes Himself known through spoken promises, and maintains an enduring relationship with His people.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the personal name YHWH to emphasize God's personal involvement with humanity. While general divine titles emphasize God's supreme power as Creator, the name LORD highlights His accessibility, His holiness, and His active dynamic with His worshippers.

Throughout Israel's history, invoking the LORD's name marked a formal recognition that God was directly involved in specific events, vows, and family decisions. It signaled trust that the God who speaks is also the God who acts to bring His spoken word to pass.

4. Theological Implications

The revelation of God's personal name teaches that God is not an impersonal force, but a living Person who listens to prayer and guides human history. His character as the LORD guarantees that He is faithful to everything He speaks.

At the same time, the name itself is not a magical formula that forces a particular outcome. The divine name reveals who God is, while His sovereign wisdom determines how and when His plans are carried out. Believers call upon the LORD in prayer to align their plans with His holy will, resting in His sovereign character.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:23, Elkanah responds to his wife Hannah's choice to nurse Samuel at home before presenting him at the sanctuary. Elkanah says, "Do what seems best to you; wait until you have weaned him. Only let the LORD establish His word."

By invoking the LORD here, Elkanah acknowledges that Samuel's birth was an answer to prayer and that the boy's future belonged entirely to God. Elkanah does not rely merely on human planning or parental devotion. Instead, he surrenders the child's future to the living God, asking the LORD to confirm and carry out His divine destiny for Samuel's life.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 — "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens."

  • Exodus 13:5 — "And it shall be, when the LORD brings you into the land..."

  • Joshua 14:12 — "Now therefore, give me this mountain of which the LORD spoke in that day..."

  • 2 Samuel 22:2 — "And he said: 'The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer...'"

  • Psalms 25:1 — "To You, O LORD, I lift up my soul."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The practice of printing "LORD" in capital letters in English Bibles preserves an ancient Jewish tradition of treating the sacred personal name of God with exceptional reverence.

In the context of 1 Samuel 1:23, Elkanah's prayer shows how a godly family seeks God's guidance in daily choices. Even when making practical parenting decisions, Elkanah places the outcome in the hands of the LORD, demonstrating that practical wisdom and spiritual dependence belong together in the household of faith.
