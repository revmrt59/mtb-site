STAYED - (yâshab, Strong's H3427)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yashav basic meaning is to sit, remain, dwell, or stay. It describes taking up a position, residing in a place, or remaining in a location rather than moving on. In 1 Samuel 1:23, the word describes Hannah remaining at home to nurse her infant son Samuel until he was fully weaned, highlighting her faithful, practical devotion in caring for the child God provided before presenting him for service to the LORD.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Yashav is one of the most widely used verbs in the Old Testament. It covers a broad range of human actions involving position and residence, from literally sitting down on a chair or stool to staying behind in a home, to residing permanently in a city or land.

In ancient Israelite life, yashav often marked settled living as opposed to nomadic wandering, or remaining behind while others traveled. In household settings, it describes family members staying at home to manage domestic responsibilities, care for loved ones, or complete a specific task before traveling.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of yashav is to sit down or take a seat. From this physical action, the word extends naturally to mean remaining, staying, or abiding in a location.

  • When a person sits down, the word describes the physical posture of sitting on a chair, seat, or ground.

  • When a person remains behind while others move, the word means to stay or tarry.

  • When people settle down in a town or region, the word means to dwell, inhabit, or reside there.

In 1 Samuel 1:23, the word means staying at home—remaining behind in Ramah rather than making the journey to Shiloh for the annual sacrifice.

3. Biblical Usage & Nu

ance

Across the Old Testament narrative and poetry, yashav carries distinct nuances depending on the subject and context:

  • Physical posture: It describes sitting in a chair or at a gate, such as Eli sitting on his seat at the tabernacle doorpost.

  • Domestic residence: It describes families residing together in their homes or ancestral lands.

  • Staying behind: It refers to people remaining back while others travel or go to war.

  • Permanent stability: In poetic literature, it describes remaining securely established, such as Mount Zion which cannot be moved.

In story narratives, the verb frequently marks a settled decision to remain in a place for a specific purpose.

4. Theological Implications

While yashav is a common verb for remaining or dwelling, its place in salvation history shows how God works through ordinary seasons of remaining and waiting. Hannah’s decision to stay at home was not an avoidance of worship, but a necessary preparation for fulfilling her vow. God’s work is not accomplished only during public acts of worship at a sanctuary; He also works through the quiet, daily care of family life. Remaining faithful in a quiet place forms the foundation for future public service to God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:23, Elkanah gives his wife Hannah permission to remain behind from the yearly family pilgrimage to Shiloh: "Do what seems best to you; wait until you have weaned him. Only let the LORD establish His word." The passage records that "the woman stayed and nursed her son until she had weaned him."

The grammatical form of yashav here is a feminine singular imperfect verb with a wayyiqtol narrative structure. This form moves the narrative forward, showing Hannah's direct implementation of her husband's agreement.

Staying home allowed Hannah to devote her full attention to rearing the child who would become Israel’s prophet and leader. Her choice to stay was directly connected to her ultimate commitment to dedicate Samuel fully to the LORD once he was old enough. Her time at home was an act of patient, intentional care aligned with God's long-term plan.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Samuel 1:9 — "Now Eli the priest was sitting on the seat by the doorpost of the tabernacle of the LORD."

  • 2 Samuel 7:2 — "See now, I dwell in a house of cedar, but the ark of God dwells inside tent curtains."

  • 1 Chronicles 20:1 — "But David stayed at Jerusalem."

  • Psalms 125:1 — "Those who trust in the LORD are like Mount Zion, which cannot be moved, but abides forever."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The relationship between Hannah remaining at home in verse 23 and her subsequent journey to Shiloh in verse 24 highlights the intentional nature of her devotion. She did not rush to surrender her young child prematurely, nor did she use her time staying at home as an excuse to avoid her sacred vow. Remaining at home for a quiet season of nurturing was the necessary step that enabled her to carry out her promise to God completely.
