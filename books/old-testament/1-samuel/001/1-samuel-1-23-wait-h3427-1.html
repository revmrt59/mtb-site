WAIT - (yâshab, Strong's H3427)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yashav fundamentally means to sit, stay, remain, or dwell. It describes a person settling in a location, whether for a brief moment, an extended season, or a permanent residence. In 1 Samuel 1:23, the word functions as a command for Hannah to remain at home and nurture her young child until he is weaned before bringing him to the tabernacle at Shiloh.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The opening chapter of 1 Samuel records the birth of Samuel in response to the earnest prayer of his mother, Hannah. Previously childless and deeply distressed, Hannah vowed that if the LORD gave her a son, she would dedicate him to divine service for his entire life. When God granted her request, she named the child Samuel.

When the time came for the annual family trip to Shiloh to offer sacrifices, Hannah chose to stay behind until the boy was fully weaned. In Israelite society, weaning typically occurred between the ages of two and three. In 1 Samuel 1:23, her husband Elkanah supports her choice, instructing her to stay at home during this crucial period of nurture before presenting Samuel to the priest Eli.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb yashav carries a broad range of meanings related to position and location. Depending on the setting, the word can mean:

  • To sit down on a chair, seat, or ground.

  • To stay or remain in a place for a period of time.

  • To inhabit or dwell in a house, city, or land.

  • To abide or continue in a state or condition.

In 1 Samuel 1:23, the word means to stay or remain behind. Elkanah uses it to direct Hannah to stay in Shiloh's local vicinity at home rather than making the journey to the sanctuary until the infant is ready.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, yashav appears in various everyday and formal settings. It describes the bodily action of sitting, such as Eli sitting on his chair near the tabernacle doorpost. It also describes long-term residence, such as Israel dwelling in the land promised by God, or people staying stationary during a time of waiting.

In 1 Samuel 1:23, the word appears in the imperative form, directed to a single female subject. This form makes the expression a clear and affirmative directive. Elkanah is not dismissing Hannah's vow; he is instructing her to stay in place so that the necessary preparation for fulfilling the vow can take place in an orderly manner.

4. Theological Implications

The use of yashav in this passage highlights the balance between spiritual devotion and practical responsibility. Hannah had made a solemn promise to dedicate Samuel to God, but fulfilling that promise required patient, day-to-day care during his infancy.

Elkanah's command for Hannah to stay at home demonstrates that quiet, unseen seasons of care are essential to God's divine plans. Remaining in place to nurse and raise a child was not a delay of spiritual duty, but the very means by which the boy was prepared for a lifetime of service in God's house.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:23, Elkanah responds to Hannah's plan with approval: "Do what seems best to you; wait until you have weaned him. Only let the LORD establish His word." The instruction to stay (yashav) comes alongside a prayer that the LORD would fulfill what had been spoken.

By telling Hannah to remain at home, Elkanah acts as a supportive husband and head of the household, relieving her of any obligation to travel with a small infant. The context shows that staying behind was a deliberate choice aimed at the well-being of the child and the ultimate fulfillment of Hannah's vow. The passage records that Hannah followed this instruction, staying home and nursing her son until the time of weaning was completed.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The directive in 1 Samuel 1:23 uses a feminine singular imperative, directly addressing Hannah. This single word granted her freedom from the annual pilgrimage without guilt, giving her domestic peace to focus entirely on the early upbringing of Samuel. It underscores how domestic faithfulness directly serves the larger purposes of God.
