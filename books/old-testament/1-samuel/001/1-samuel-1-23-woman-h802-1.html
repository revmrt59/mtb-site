WOMAN - (ʼishshâh, Strong's H802)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun ishshah is the general biblical word for an adult female person, translated depending on context as "woman," "wife," or "female." In 1 Samuel 1:23, the word refers specifically to Hannah as she remains at home to nurse her infant son Samuel until he is weaned, fulfilling her quiet role in God's larger plan for Israel.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israelite society, ishshah designated an adult female member of the community. It stands alongside its male counterpart 'ish ("man" or "husband"). Within Old Testament narrative and law, an ishshah participated in household management, child-rearing, religious vows, and corporate worship.

In the opening chapter of 1 Samuel, Hannah's grief over her childlessness drives her to earnest prayer at Shiloh. God answers her petition, giving her a son whom she names Samuel. When her husband Elkanah prepares to travel to Shiloh for the annual sacrifice, Hannah chooses to stay behind until the child is weaned. Elkanah supports her choice, saying, "Do what seems best to you; wait until you have weaned him. Only let the LORD establish His word."

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of ishshah is a female human being. Depending on the grammatical relationship and setting, the word carries three main senses in the Old Testament:

  • An adult female person ("woman")

  • A married female partner ("wife")

  • A female animal or general entity ("female")

In 1 Samuel 1:23, ishshah refers to Hannah. The sentence describes her staying at home after her husband's departure to nurse her infant son. In this context, she is both Elkanah's wife and Samuel's mother.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses ishshah across all genres—law, narrative, poetry, and prophecy. The term can describe individual women, married wives, or groups of women collectively.

Grammatically, the form in 1 Samuel 1:23 is a feminine singular noun. The singular number identifies a single female person, while the immediate narrative context identifies her specifically as Hannah. The passage uses the title "the woman" as it shifts focus from Elkanah's spoken words back to Hannah's home activity of nursing and weaning Samuel.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's role in 1 Samuel 1 illustrates several key biblical truths:

  • Personal devotion before God: The Old Testament shows women engaging directly with God in solemn vows, private prayer, and faithful stewardship of their homes.

  • Fidelity in hidden duties: Nursing and weaning a child at home served a primary spiritual purpose. Hannah's domestic care was essential to preparing Samuel for lifetime service to God at Shiloh.

  • Divine providence through ordinary families: God initiated the transition from the era of the Judges to the reign of the kings through a devout family's faithfulness.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:23, Elkanah encourages Hannah to remain at home until Samuel is old enough to be presented at the sanctuary. He adds the prayerful blessing, "Only let the LORD establish His word."

The narrative then states: "Then the woman stayed and nursed her son until she had weaned him." Referring to Hannah as "the woman" at this juncture highlights her maternal devotion within the home. Her faithful endurance during these early years ensured that Samuel grew strong before being presented to Eli at Shiloh.

This quiet domestic setting stands in direct contrast to the spiritual corruption occurring among the priesthood at Shiloh (1 Samuel 2). God's spiritual renewal of Israel begins not in the formal sanctuary, but in the quiet obedience of a faithful woman raising her child for the Lord.

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The weaning process in Israel: In the ancient Near East, children were typically weaned between the ages of two and three. This meant Hannah spent several years caring for Samuel before leaving him at Shiloh, highlighting the sacrifice involved in fulfilling her vow.

  • Concord in marriage: Elkanah's response in verse 23 shows mutual trust and commitment between husband and wife, as both sought to fulfill Hannah's vow before the Lord.
