WORD - (dâbâr, Strong's H1697)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun dabar refers broadly to a spoken word, message, promise, instruction, or an underlying matter or affair. In 1 Samuel 1:23, Elkanah speaks to his wife Hannah, praying that God will establish His "word." In this setting, the term refers to God's promise and stated purpose regarding Hannah's child, Samuel. Elkanah acknowledges that human decisions and parental plans rely entirely on God confirming and fulfilling His divine promise.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Hebrew thought, a word is not merely a passing sound or an abstract concept. Words carry weight, substance, and purpose. Human words can inform, make covenants, or mislead, but when God speaks, His word carries divine authority and power to accomplish what He declares.

The term dabar connects speaking with reality. In Scripture, what God says directly shapes what occurs. In times of transition—such as the period of the judges moving into the kingdom of Israel—human leaders often failed or spoke vainly. Against that backdrop, God's word stands out as the fixed standard of truth and guidance for His people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word dabar can mean a spoken word, a sentence, a speech, a command, a promise, or a prophetic message. Depending on context, it can also refer to a non-spoken reality such as a matter, an event, a cause, a report, or a business affair.

When a person is the subject, the word usually refers to human conversation, advice, or legal statements. When God is the subject, it refers to divine revelation, divine laws, or prophetic declarations. In 1 Samuel 1:23, the word carries the specific sense of a divine promise or declared purpose that God has spoken concerning Samuel's birth and future life.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

across the Old Testament, dabar bridges speech and action. Human speech can be reliable or deceptive, but God's dabar is completely dependable.

Scripture shows several distinct ways the term is used:

  • Divine Revelation: God delivers His message directly to prophets, who then speak that same message to the people.

  • Covenant Instructions: The Ten Commandments are literally called the "ten words" (debarim), emphasizing that God's laws are His authoritative spoken directives.

  • Historical Events or Matters: The term frequently describes a situation or event being discussed, showing that in Hebrew, a word and the event it describes are intimately tied together.

4. Theological Implications

The fundamental theological truth behind dabar is that God is faithful to what He declares. When God speaks a word, He binds Himself to His character to fulfill it. Human promises are limited by human weakness, but God's word possesses absolute trustworthiness.

Furthermore, when God gives His word, human beings are called to trust and align their actions with it. Seeking the fulfillment of God's word does not mean forcing an outcome; it means placing human plans into God's sovereign hands, asking Him to confirm what He has pledged to do.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:23, Hannah has recently given birth to Samuel after years of barrenness and intense prayer. She tells her husband Elkanah that she will remain at home until the boy is weaned, after which she will present him at the sanctuary in Shiloh to serve the Lord permanently, in fulfillment of her vow.

Elkanah supports her request, saying, "Do what seems best to you; wait until you have weaned him." But he immediately adds a prayer of dedication: "Only let the LORD establish His word." The singular noun form describes the specific message or promise connected to Samuel's birth and calling.

Earlier, the priest Eli had pronounced a blessing on Hannah, asking God to grant her petition. Elkanah recognizes that Samuel is not merely their child, but a child given by divine promise. By praying for God to "establish His word," Elkanah submits their family plans to the Lord, acknowledging that God must bring His declared purpose to complete fruition.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In 1 Samuel 1:23, the phrase "establish His word" uses the Hebrew verb qum, which means to arise, stand firm, or be confirmed. To establish a dabar means to cause a spoken declaration to stand upright and reach its intended fulfillment.

This sets up a key theme for the entire book of 1 Samuel. While the religious leadership under Eli was collapsing, God was quietly raising up Samuel. Later in the narrative, Scripture notes that the Lord was with Samuel and "let none of his words fall to the ground." The confirmation of God's word in 1 Samuel 1:23 serves as the foundation for Samuel's ministry as a true prophet whose words were sustained and established by God Himself.
