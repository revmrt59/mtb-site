BROUGHT - (bôwʼ, Strong's H935)

Summary:

Bo' is a basic Hebrew verb that means to come, enter, go, or bring. When used to describe someone guiding or carrying a person or object to a destination, it means to bring or lead in. In 1 Samuel 1:24, the word describes Hannah physically bringing her young son Samuel to the house of the LORD in Shiloh. By bringing Samuel to the sanctuary, Hannah fulfills her solemn promise, presenting her child to God for lifelong service in His presence.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew verb bo' is one of the most widely used action verbs in the Old Testament. At its foundational level, it describes physical movement from one place into another—entering a tent, approaching a city, or arriving at a destination. Depending on the grammatical context, it can describe moving oneself ("coming" or "going") or causing another person or object to move ("bringing" or "leading").

In the narrative of 1 Samuel 1, Hannah had suffered years of distress because she was unable to bear children. During an annual pilgrimage to the sanctuary at Shiloh, she prayed in deep sorrow and promised God that if He gave her a son, she would give him back to the LORD for all the days of his life. After God answered her prayer with the birth of Samuel, Hannah stayed home until the child was weaned. The verb bo' marks the crucial turning point where Hannah moves from receiving God's gift to fulfilling her holy pledge, bringing her young son directly into the house of the LORD.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Bo' refers directly to spatial movement, entrance, and arrival. Depending on how it functions in a sentence, the verb can mean:

  • To come, go, or enter into a location.

  • To arrive at a setting or destination.

  • To bring, carry, or escort someone or something into a place.

The word itself is a simple verb of motion and does not carry complex religious meaning in its dictionary definition. Instead, its significance comes from the destination and the purpose of the movement. When something or someone is brought to a holy sanctuary, the ordinary act of bringing becomes an act of presentation, worship, or devotion. In 1 Samuel 1:24, the word refers to Hannah escorting young Samuel and carrying sacred offerings into the LORD's sanctuary at Shiloh.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, bo' appears across every genre of Scripture to describe simple and significant movements alike:

  • Everyday Movement: People coming home, going into fields, or entering through city gates.

  • Transporting Sacrifices and Offerings: Bringing animals, grain, wine, or precious metals to the altar or tabernacle.

  • Escorting Persons into New Roles: Bringing individuals into a leader's presence, into a family, or into a sanctuary for service.

The verb simply establishes that a person or object has been moved into a new space. The surrounding story provides the deeper meaning by showing who is being moved, who is doing the bringing, and what takes place upon arrival.

4. Theological Implications

Although bo' is an everyday word for physical motion, its usage in 1 Samuel 1:24 reveals the genuine nature of biblical faith. Hannah's vow was not just a emotional promise made in a moment of pain; it was a commitment that required real, physical action. By physically bringing her son to Shiloh, Hannah showed that her devotion to God was active and sincere.

This act also highlights the transition from receiving a blessing to surrendering it to God. Samuel was not brought to Shiloh for a brief visit; he was brought to live and serve in the presence of the LORD permanently. Hannah recognized that the child given to her by God's grace ultimately belonged to God. Her action shows that true gratitude responds to God's gifts by offering them back to Him for His glory.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:24, the verb form is feminine singular in the narrative sequence (imperfect wayyiqtol), pointing directly to Hannah as the one taking initiative to fulfill her vow. After weaning Samuel, she traveled up to Shiloh with substantial offerings—three bulls, an ephah of flour, and a skin of wine—and she "brought" the child to the tabernacle.

The text emphasizes both the tenderness of the situation and Hannah's unwavering resolve. The passage notes that "the child was young," highlighting the emotional difficulty of a mother leaving her small son behind. Yet Hannah did not hesitate or search for excuses. The word "brought" marks the completion of her sacred vow: the boy who was received in answer to prayer at Shiloh was brought right back to Shiloh to serve the LORD continuously.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:19 — God formed the animals and "brought" them to Adam to see what he would call them.

  • Numbers 32:17 — The tribes promised to go armed before Israel until they "brought" them to their land.

  • 1 Samuel 19:7 — Jonathan spoke with David and "brought" him to Saul, returning him to service.

  • 2 Chronicles 25:12 — The army of Judah captured ten thousand enemies and "brought" them to the top of the rock.

  • Ezekiel 20:37 — God promised to count His people and "bring" them into the bond of the covenant.

7. Other Relevant Insights

It is worth noting that Hannah brought both her young son and sacrificial offerings to Shiloh at the same time. In Israelite worship, offerings brought to the tabernacle represented worship and devotion to God. By bringing Samuel along with these sacrifices, Hannah was treating her long-awaited son as her supreme act of worship. Her willingness to yield her daily fellowship with Samuel demonstrates that she prized God's honor above her own comfort as a mother.
