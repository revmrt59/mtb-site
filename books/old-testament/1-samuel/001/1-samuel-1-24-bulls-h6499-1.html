BULLS - (par, Strong's H6499)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun par refers to a young male bull or steer, an animal selected for agricultural labor, breeding, or sacred sacrificial offerings. In ancient Israel, cattle represented valuable livestock and held a central place in the sacrificial system. In 1 Samuel 1:24, the word refers to the valuable livestock Hannah brought to the sanctuary at Shiloh to accompany the formal dedication of her young son Samuel to the LORD.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The noun par describes a young bull, typically a male calf that has grown into full vigor and strength. In the agrarian culture of ancient Israel, cattle were among the most valuable assets a family or community could own. They provided essential agricultural power for plowing and threshing, as well as significant material wealth.

Because of their value and strength, young bulls were designated as prime sacrificial animals in Israel's worship. Bringing a bull to the altar involved a substantial financial investment. In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah travels from Ramah to the Tabernacle at Shiloh to fulfill the vow she made when praying for a child. Having weaned young Samuel, she travels to the sanctuary with valuable provisions, including these animals, to dedicate her son to God's service for his entire life.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word par refers specifically to a young bull or steer. It is distinguished from older work oxen or newborn calves, identifying a young male bovine in the prime of its strength and value.

In Scripture, the word describes animals used in several key contexts:

  • Sacrificial offerings prescribed under the law, including burnt offerings, sin offerings, and peace offerings.

  • Ceremonies of consecration, such as setting apart priests or dedicating the altar.

  • Wealth and livestock management, reflecting significant herds and agricultural property.

In 1 Samuel 1:24, the plural form identifies the livestock Hannah brought to Shiloh as part of her family's worship and vow fulfillment.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses par when describing major worship occasions, solemn consecrations, and national offerings. While smaller animals like sheep or goats were common for everyday family sacrifices, a bull was required for high priests, leaders, or whole-community offerings.

Because of its size and worth, presenting a bull indicated deep reverence, heavy solemnity, and abundant thanksgiving. The word carries nuances of physical strength, prime quality, and substantial devotion when brought before God at the sanctuary.

4. Theological Implications

Sacrificing a bull demonstrated that true worship involved genuine cost. Offering an animal of such high value acknowledged God's complete ownership over all creation and expressed total trust in His future provision.

In the context of vows and dedications, sacrificial offerings served as visible acts of worship that accompanied personal commitments. God's people were taught not to approach Him empty-handed; their physical gifts reflected their spiritual devotion. Hannah's gift demonstrates that fulfilling a promise to God is an act of joyful worship rather than a reluctant duty.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:24, Hannah travels to Shiloh after weaning Samuel. Years earlier, her prayers at the sanctuary were marked by deep emotional distress as she pleaded for a child and promised to give him back to the LORD. God answered her petition, and Hannah responded with prompt, wholehearted obedience.

Bringing the bulls alongside flour and wine shows that Hannah was determined to fulfill her vow completely and honorably. The livestock provided the necessary burnt offerings and peace offerings for the dedication service. This presentation demonstrates that Hannah did not view giving Samuel to God's service as a painful loss, but as an occasion for praise and worship. The valuable gift underscores the solemnity and joy of dedicating her son to the LORD for life.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 32:15 – Jacob included "bulls" among the costly gifts sent ahead to appease his brother Esau, illustrating the great economic value of these animals.

  • Exodus 29:12 – Moses was commanded to take the blood of the "bull" and place it on the horns of the altar during the consecration of Aaron and his sons for priestly service.

  • Leviticus 4:7 – The anointed priest applied blood from the "bull" sacrificed as a sin offering to make atonement before the LORD for the community.

  • 2 Chronicles 30:24 – King Hezekiah provided thousands of "bulls" for the entire assembly during a massive national celebration of Passover and spiritual rededication.

  • Psalms 69:31 – The psalmist notes that sincere, heart-felt praise pleases the LORD even more than an offering of a "bull" with horns and hooves.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's offering of livestock along with flour and wine aligns with the Old Testament regulations for worship, where grain offerings and drink offerings accompanied major burnt sacrifices. Her actions show that even during a spiritually difficult period in Israel's history—near the end of the time of the judges—godly families maintained careful, faithful familiarity with God's sanctuary laws.
