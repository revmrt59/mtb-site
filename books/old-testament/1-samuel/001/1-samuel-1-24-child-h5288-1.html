CHILD - (naʻar, Strong's H5288)

Summary:

The Hebrew word na'ar refers broadly to a young person, ranging from an infant or small boy to a youth or personal attendant. In 1 Samuel 1:24, the word describes the boy Samuel as Hannah brings him to the sanctuary at Shiloh. In this context, it highlights Samuel's tender age and vulnerability as Hannah fulfills her solemn vow, handing over her long-awaited son to lifelong service before the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the Old Testament, social roles and stages of life were clearly recognized, yet terms for youth often covered a broad range of ages. The word na'ar appears across historical narratives, poetry, and prophetic books to identify young males from early childhood through early adulthood. Depending on the setting, it can describe a small child needing care, a youth capable of carrying out tasks, or a younger man serving as an assistant or retainer to a superior.

In 1 Samuel 1:24, the setting is the sanctuary at Shiloh during the period of the judges. Hannah had suffered years of barrenness and distress, enduring taunts while praying fervently for a male child. She promised God that if He gave her a son, she would dedicate him to sanctuary service for all his days. After giving birth to Samuel and nursing him until he was weaned, Hannah made the journey to Shiloh to present her young child to Eli the priest and to fulfill her pledge to God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word na'ar can refer to:

  • A young child, toddler, or infant boy.

  • A lad or adolescent youth.

  • A young man of working or military age.

  • A servant, attendant, or assistant.

The word does not specify an exact age on its own. Instead, the surrounding story determines whether it indicates physical age, social dependence, or a supportive role under a leader. Here in 1 Samuel 1:24, the word refers directly to young Samuel as he is brought to the house of the Lord.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament narratives, na'ar carries two main nuances depending on whether the emphasis falls on age or function.

When the emphasis is on age, the word brings into view dependence, youthfulness, or physical smallness. For instance, the word describes Elisha's servant running to help a distressed youth in 2 Kings 4:29 ("child") and depicts a young person who is small enough to keep simple records in Isaiah 10:19 ("child"). It is also used for Isaac on Mount Moriah in Genesis 22:12 ("lad"), where it points to his youth relative to Abraham.

When the emphasis is on function or social status, the word describes a personal assistant, subordinate helper, or young warrior. In Ruth 2:5, the word refers to the foreman overseeing the reapers ("servant"). In 1 Samuel 2:17, the same Hebrew word describes the young men who served alongside Eli's priestly household ("men"). Thus, the term naturally transitions between physical youth and functional service.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah’s surrender of her young child to God's service reveals several significant biblical truths:

  • God hears the prayers of the afflicted and brings life out of human helplessness. Samuel's presence at Shiloh was living proof of God's grace to a barren woman.

  • True worship involves keeping vows even when the cost is deeply personal. Hannah did not hold back the child God gave her, but returned him to the Lord for His divine purposes.

  • God prepares leadership for His people from early beginnings. Before Samuel ever spoke as a prophet or judged Israel, he began his calling as a young boy ministering before God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:24, Hannah brings young Samuel to Shiloh along with substantial offerings: three bulls, an ephah of flour, and a skin of wine. The text specifically notes his youth to emphasize the magnitude of Hannah's act. She did not wait until Samuel was grown, self-sufficient, or established before fulfilling her promise. She surrendered him at the very moment he was weaned.

Grammatically, the word is singular and common in gender, pointing specifically to this one young child around whom the future of Israel's leadership would turn. The immediate context contrasts Hannah's faithful dedication of her young na'ar with the corrupt behavior of Eli's adult sons, who abused their sanctuary roles. While Eli's sons dishonored the sanctuary offerings, young Samuel grew in faithful service before the Lord.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The narrative of 1 Samuel highlights a striking irony through this word. The weak and vulnerable na'ar brought by a quiet mother becomes the vessel through which God revives His word in Israel. While established leaders at Shiloh failed in their spiritual duties, God used a young child serving quietly in the sanctuary to speak His word and guide the nation into a new era.
