LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

Yahweh is the personal proper name of the God of Israel, revealing Him as the living God who makes Himself known to His people and enters into covenant relationship with them. Unlike generic titles for deity, this name represents God's personal identity and active presence among His worshippers. In 1 Samuel 1:24, the name identifies the sanctuary at Shiloh as the house of the personal God who heard Hannah's desperate prayer, showing that her sacrifice and the dedication of her son Samuel were rendered directly to the faithful God who gave him to her.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the Old Testament, God is identified by several terms, including generic titles that mean "God" or "Master." However, Yahweh is His unique personal name. English Bible translations traditionally render this personal name using capital letters as "LORD" to distinguish it from the standard word for master or lord.

In ancient Near Eastern culture, a personal name was far more than a convenient label. A name expressed identity, character, and personal presence. By revealing His personal name to Israel, God made Himself known in a concrete way. He showed that He was not an abstract power or distant force, but a personal God who speaks, listens, acts, and relates to human beings.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word functions grammatically as a proper noun, serving as the personal name of the true God of Israel. Where common nouns describe what God is—such as Creator, Ruler, or Judge—His personal name declares who He is.

The name identifies the living God as distinct from all false gods made by human hands. It marks the self-revealing God who acts in history, remembers His promises, and invites His people into personal fellowship with Him.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament scriptures, God's personal name appears in contexts where His personal involvement and covenant faithfulness are emphasized. It is the name used when God calls individuals, makes promises, delivers His people from distress, and establishes worship.

While Israel called God by general titles when highlighting His supreme majesty over all nations, they used His personal name when approaching Him in prayer, presenting sacrifices, and praising Him for His salvation. The name separates the worship of Israel from the idol worship of surrounding nations because Israel did not worship an unknown deity, but the specific God who had made Himself known to them.

4. Theological Implications

The revelation of God's personal name teaches that God desires to be known by His people. Faith is not merely belief in a higher power; it is trust in the living God who has revealed His name and character.

Because God is personal, human beings can cry out to Him in honest prayer, bring Him their deepest heartbreaks, and trust His faithful care. The name reassures believers that God remembers His word across generations, acts with compassionate purpose, and personally receives the devotion of those who seek Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:24, Hannah travels to Shiloh after weaning her young child, Samuel. She brings him alongside generous offerings—three bulls, an ephah of flour, and a skin of wine—to present him at "the house of the LORD."

This context gives rich meaning to the target word. Years earlier, Hannah was barren, brokenhearted, and weeping in silent prayer before God at this same location. God heard her cry and gave her a son. Now, Hannah returns to fulfill the vow she made during her grief.

By describing the sanctuary as the "house of the LORD," the text highlights that Hannah is not surrendering her child to a religious system, a physical building, or human priests. She is delivering Samuel directly to Yahweh, the personal God who personally heard her prayer and granted her petition. Her sacrifice and her child belong to the living God who proved Himself faithful to her.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4: "...in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens..." — The personal name LORD is joined with God to show that the Creator of the universe is the same personal God who enters into relationship with humanity.

  • Joshua 14:12: "Now therefore, give me this mountain of which the LORD spoke in that day..." — Caleb grounds his courage and request in the trustworthy word spoken personally by the LORD.

  • 2 Samuel 22:2: "The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer..." — David uses God's personal name to celebrate his personal experience of divine protection and salvation.

  • Psalms 25:1: "To You, O LORD, I lift up my soul." — The psalmist addresses God by His personal name in intimate, trusting prayer during a time of need.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah’s act in 1 Samuel 1:24 illustrates the joy of surrendering back to God what He has graciously given. When Hannah brought Samuel to the house of the LORD, she was handing over her most precious earthly gift.

She was able to release Samuel into lifelong service because she knew the character of the LORD to whom she was entrusting him. She knew that the God who sustained her in her sorrow would faithfully guard and guide her child. Knowing God by name transforms sacrifice from a painful loss into an act of confident worship.
