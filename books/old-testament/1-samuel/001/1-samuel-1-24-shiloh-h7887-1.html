SHILOH - (Shîylôh, Strong's H7887)

Summary:

The Hebrew word Shiloh is a proper noun referring to the major sanctuary city in the central hill country of Ephraim where Israel's tabernacle was located during the period of the judges and early monarchy. In 1 Samuel 1:24, Shiloh identifies the sacred destination where Hannah brought her young son Samuel, along with generous offerings, to fulfill her vow to dedicate him permanently to the service of the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Shiloh was located in the central mountain region of Ephraim, north of Bethel and south of Lebonah. Following Israel's conquest of Canaan under Joshua, the assembly set up the tabernacle of meeting at Shiloh. For centuries before Jerusalem became the national capital, Shiloh functioned as Israel's principal religious center.

The city was the site of regular annual feasts, sacrifices, and administrative gatherings. The ark of the covenant remained at Shiloh until it was taken into battle against the Philistines during the days of Eli the priest.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Shiloh is a Hebrew proper noun designating a specific geographic city and worship center. The name is related to a Hebrew root associated with rest, tranquility, or peace.

As a place name, Shiloh identifies the location chosen by Israel after entering Canaan to house the tabernacle and the ark of the covenant, serving as the central gathering place where God met with His people.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the name Shiloh in several distinct historical phases:

  • Settlement Era: In the Book of Joshua, Shiloh is the base where the tent of meeting was established and where the remaining land of Canaan was distributed among the tribes by lot.

  • Era of the Judges: In Judges and early 1 Samuel, Shiloh represents the sacred place of annual pilgrimage where Israelite families traveled to worship and offer sacrifices.

  • Prophetic Warning: In later books such as Jeremiah, God refers to Shiloh's ultimate downfall and ruin as a solemn warning to Jerusalem, demonstrating that God will not spare a corrupt holy site if His people abandon true worship.

4. Theological Implications

Shiloh carries significant theological weight in the Old Testament narrative:

  • Divine Accommodation: God appointed Shiloh as a centralized place for His presence among His people, providing a tangible location for corporate worship and covenant renewal.

  • Faithful Surrender: Hannah's journey to Shiloh illustrates the response of a faithful worshipper who returns to God the very blessing He granted, surrendering her child to the sanctuary where her prayer was heard.

  • Worship Beyond Geography: Shiloh's later abandonment shows that sacred space is not a magical talisman. Physical location cannot substitute for genuine obedience, reverent hearts, and covenant faithfulness.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:24, Hannah travels to Shiloh after weaning her young child Samuel. Early in the chapter, Shiloh was the backdrop for Hannah's deep agony and desperate prayer for a son. Having received the answer to her prayer, she returns to the exact same site.

Hannah does not travel to Shiloh empty-handed. She brings three bulls, an ephah of flour, and a skin of wine to present alongside Samuel. In 1 Samuel 1:24, Shiloh represents the place of answered prayer and total consecration. By bringing Samuel to Shiloh, Hannah presents him to Eli the priest so that the child might minister before the Lord for his entire life.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Joshua 18:1 — "Now the whole congregation of the children of Israel assembled together at Shiloh, and set up the tabernacle of meeting there."

  • Judges 18:31 — "So they set up for themselves Micah’s carved image which he made, all the time that the house of God was in Shiloh."

  • 1 Samuel 1:3 — "This man went up from his city yearly to worship and sacrifice to the LORD of hosts in Shiloh."

  • 1 Samuel 3:21 — "Then the LORD appeared again in Shiloh. For the LORD revealed Himself to Samuel in Shiloh by the word of the LORD."

  • 1 Samuel 4:3 — "Let us bring the ark of the covenant of the LORD from Shiloh to us, that when it comes among us it may save us from the hand of our enemies."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Shiloh was the primary predecessor to Jerusalem as Israel's national sanctuary. Its history demonstrates God's unfolding plan to establish a central place for His name.

  • Samuel’s upbringing at Shiloh under Eli prepared him for his future leadership as priest, prophet, and judge over Israel.

  • The revival of God’s spoken word occurred at Shiloh when the Lord revealed Himself to young Samuel, bringing light to a period when prophetic vision had been rare.
