SKIN - (nebel, Strong's H5035)

Summary:

Hannah brought a skin of wine along with her sacrificial offerings when she presented young Samuel to the LORD at Shiloh. In 1 Samuel 1:24, the word refers to a portable leather container made from animal hide, used here to hold wine for sacred worship and feasting in fulfillment of a vow.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient Near East, liquids such as water, milk, and wine were stored and transported in containers made from animal skins. Typically crafted from whole goat or sheep hides that were tanned and sewn shut, these leather vessels provided a flexible, durable, and lightweight way to carry liquids over long distances.

In the Old Testament, the Hebrew term nebel carries two main physical senses based on shape or material. It describes a portable leather skin or earthenware vessel for holding liquids, and it also names a stringed musical instrument—such as a harp or lute—whose wooden sound box resembled the shape of a skin vessel. In 1 Samuel 1:24, the context clearly indicates a leather skin container used to transport wine to the sanctuary at Shiloh.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

spaces

The word nebel can refer to a leather skin-bottle or jar designed to store or transport liquids. It can also refer to a musical instrument, often translated as a harp, lute, or stringed instrument, whose shape echoed that of a vessel.

Here in 1 Samuel 1:24, the word means a leather container holding wine. It describes a practical vessel brought along with flour and livestock for a sacred offering.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Scripture, nebel serves in both daily domestic settings and formal religious worship. As a container, it holds essential supplies for journeys, hospitality, or sacred offerings. Leather skins were vital for travelers and worshippers who needed to carry wine across hilly terrain to worship centers.

As a musical instrument, the word appears in scenes of joy, royal celebrations, and temple worship, where musicians played stringed instruments to accompany songs of praise to God.

The word is also used poetically. In Job 38:37, God compares the rain clouds of heaven to "bottles" or water skins that are tipped over to pour rain onto the dry ground.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah’s gift of a skin of wine alongside three bulls and an ephah of flour highlights the generosity and thoroughness of her worship. When God answered her prayer for a child, she did not return to Shiloh with a minimal gesture. She brought substantial gifts to accompany the presentation of her son.

In Old Testament worship, wine accompanied burnt offerings and fellowship offerings. It represented fruitfulness, joy, and the blessings of the land. Bringing a skin of wine to the sanctuary acknowledged that God is the giver of every good gift. Hannah dedicated both her child and her provisions to the LORD in heartfelt gratitude.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:24, Hannah travels to Shiloh after weaning young Samuel to fulfill the vow she had made in her deep distress. The presence of the skin of wine fits the biblical instructions for sacrifices, where drink offerings of wine were poured out before the LORD alongside animal sacrifices and grain offerings.

The text presents the word as a singular noun of common gender, identifying this specific container of wine prepared for the dedication ceremony. The single skin of wine supplied the necessary drink offering for the sacrifices that marked Samuel's lifelong consecration to divine service.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Samuel 10:3 – "skin" – Three men traveling up to God at Bethel carry provisions, including a skin of wine.

  • 1 Samuel 25:18 – "skins" – Abigail quickly gathers two skins of wine along with other food to bring peace to David and his men.

  • 2 Samuel 16:1 – "skin" – Ziba meets David with supplies for his journey, including a skin of wine.

  • Job 38:37 – "bottles" – God poetically describes the cloud formations of the sky as bottles of rain that He tilts over the earth.

  • Psalms 33:2 – "instrument" – The word refers to a stringed musical instrument used to give thanks and sing praises to the LORD.

7. Other Relevant Insights

A meaningful connection appears between Hannah’s former sorrow and her present worship. Earlier in 1 Samuel 1, the priest Eli mistakenly accused Hannah of being drunk with wine when he saw her lips moving in silent prayer. Hannah corrected Eli, explaining that she had poured out her soul, not wine, before the LORD.

When Hannah returns to Shiloh in verse 24, she brings an actual skin of wine—not for personal drunkenness, but as a sacred drink offering to celebrate God's faithfulness in granting her a son. The vessel that once represented a groundless accusation now stands as part of a joyful offering of thanksgiving.
