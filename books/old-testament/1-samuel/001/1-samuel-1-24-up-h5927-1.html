UP - (ʻâlâh, Strong's H5927)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'alah fundamentally means to ascend, go up, rise, or bring up from a lower position to a higher one. It is used throughout the Old Testament for physical movement up a hill, traveling to a holy site, or lifting up sacrifices to God. In 1 Samuel 1:24, 'alah describes Hannah taking her young son Samuel up to the sanctuary at Shiloh, highlighting her decisive action to fulfill her vow by bringing him into the Lord's presence.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient near eastern world, physical geography and spiritual worship were closely linked. Places of worship were frequently located on elevated ground, such as hills or mountains. Consequently, traveling to a sanctuary or central place of worship was described as an upward journey.

In the historical setting of 1 Samuel, Israel gathered at Shiloh, where the tabernacle and the ark of the covenant rested during the period of the judges. When Hannah journeyed from her hometown in the hill country of Ephraim to Shiloh, she was making a physical ascent to the house of the Lord. The verb 'alah highlights both this geographical ascent and the intentional effort required to travel to God's appointed place of worship.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb 'alah refers to upward motion in a variety of physical and ceremonial contexts.

  • It can mean to go up, climb, or ascend a mountain, staircase, or hill.

  • It can refer to rising up, such as smoke ascending from an altar, plants growing up out of the soil, or a morning mist rising.

  • It can mean to bring up, carry up, or lead up people, animals, or items from one place to another.

  • It is frequently used for offering sacrifices, where an animal or gift is "caused to go up" on the altar as a gift presented to God.

In 1 Samuel 1:24, the verb carries the sense of bringing up or carrying along. Hannah is the one performing the action, bringing young Samuel together with prescribed offerings up to the sanctuary.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, 'alah appears in several common settings:

First, it describes basic physical movement upward. People go up into the mountains, ascend city walls, or climb up to high places.

Second, it functions as a standard technical term for going to a place of covenant worship. Whether traveling to Shiloh in the days of the judges or going up to Jerusalem for the annual feasts in later centuries, worshippers consistently "went up" to meet with God.

Third, the verb expresses bringing or offering something upward. When a priest places a burnt offering on the altar, the sacrifice goes up in fire and smoke. Similarly, when a leader brings the ark of the covenant into a new resting place, or when a parent brings a child to present him to the Lord, 'alah describes the movement of bringing that gift up into God's presence.

4. Theological Implications

The idea of going up to God carries rich biblical meaning. Worship is rarely depicted in Scripture as something that happens by accident or without effort. It requires an intentional departure from everyday life and an upward movement toward God's presence.

When people bring gifts or sacrifices up to God, the movement reflects surrender and dedication. What is brought up to the sanctuary is transferred from common, everyday use into divine service. In the sacrificial system, an offering "going up" in smoke signified that the gift was entirely given over to the Lord.

When applied to dedicated human lives, as in the case of Samuel, this upward movement symbolizes a total handoff into God's care. Samuel was not merely being dropped off at a building; he was being brought up and presented to the Lord for lifelong service.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:24, the grammatical form is feminine singular in a narrative past-action form (wayyiqtol / imperfect), identifying Hannah as the main actor carrying out the verse's action. After years of barrenness and earnest prayer, God answered Hannah by giving her a son. She made a solemn vow that if God gave her a male child, she would give him back to the Lord all the days of his life.

Once Samuel was weaned, Hannah acted without delay. The text states that she took him up with her, along with substantial offerings: three bulls, an ephah of flour, and a skin of wine. The use of 'alah here underscores her faithful obedience. Taking the child up to Shiloh meant surrendering her daily custody of him, trusting God to care for him under Eli the priest. The journey upward to Shiloh was the physical realization of her heart's promise to give her precious child back to the Lord who gave him.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 24:18 — "So Moses went into the midst of the cloud and went up into the mountain. And Moses was on the mountain forty days and forty nights."

  • Psalms 68:18 — "You have ascended on high, You have led captivity captive; You have received gifts among men, even from the rebellious, that the LORD God might dwell there."

  • Jeremiah 16:15 — "but, 'The LORD lives who brought up the children of Israel from the land of the north and from all the lands where He had driven them.' And I will bring them back into their land which I gave to their fathers."

  • Ezra 1:11 — "All the articles of gold and silver were five thousand four hundred. All these Sheshbazzar took with the captives who were brought up from Babylon to Jerusalem."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The physical journey "up" to Shiloh in 1 Samuel 1 prefigures the larger biblical pattern of pilgrimages to Jerusalem. Later in Israel's history, God's people sang the "Songs of Ascents" (Psalms 120–134) as they walked up the elevation toward the temple.

Hannah's journey up to Shiloh with Samuel was her own personal song of ascent. It converted her deep personal joy into public worship and lasting covenant commitment.
