WEANED - (gâmal, Strong's H1580)

Summary:

The word gamal means to carry a process through to completion—whether bringing a child off mother's milk, yielding fully ripe fruit, or dealing completely with someone in repayment or blessing. In 1 Samuel 1:24, the word describes Hannah having fully weaned young Samuel, marking the moment her child reached early childhood and was prepared for dedicated service at the tabernacle in Shiloh.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israelite culture, child-rearing and agriculture were two primary spheres of daily life where growth was carefully watched. Children were typically nursed until around two or three years of age, a period crucial for early survival and bonding. Reaching the end of this period was a significant developmental milestone. Agricultural life likewise depended on crops reaching full maturity and bearing ripe fruit. The Hebrew verb gamal reflects this underlying idea of bringing a process to its complete or natural conclusion. In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah had prayed desperately for a child, promising to devote him to Yahweh for all his days. When God granted her request, the process of nurturing Samuel during his infant years led directly to the fulfillment of her vow once he was old enough to live at the sanctuary.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb gamal can mean to complete, bring to maturity, or deal fully with someone. When used in family contexts, it means to complete the nursing period and bring a child into early childhood. When applied to plants, it means to mature or yield ripe fruit. When used regarding human conduct, it means to render back, repay, or deal with someone according to their actions, whether doing good or repaying evil. In 1 Samuel 1:24, the word refers directly to completing the period of nursing.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses gamal in a few primary ways:

  • Weaning a child: Marking the completion of infancy and the transition to solid food and greater independence.

  • Yielding fruit: Describing a plant carrying its natural growth cycle through to ripe produce.

  • Repaying or dealing with people: Describing full payment or treatment rendered to another person, whether gracious reward or deserved punishment.

The common thread in all these uses is the idea of bringing something to its mature or final outcome rather than leaving it partial or unfinished.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah’s act of weaning Samuel highlights the integrity of vows made to God. God answered Hannah's cry in her time of deep sorrow, giving her a son. In response, Hannah did not view her child as a private possession to hold onto indefinitely. She used his earliest years to nurse and care for him until he reached the age where he could live and serve at Shiloh. Her devotion illustrates how God's gracious gifts are to be received with thanksgiving and dedicated back to His service.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:24, gamal appears in a feminine singular perfect form, presenting Hannah’s action as a completed reality. Earlier in the chapter, her husband Elkanah had agreed that she should remain at home until the boy was weaned. Once that milestone was reached, Hannah immediately carried out her commitment. She traveled up to Shiloh, bringing sacrifices alongside the young boy to present him to Eli the priest. The completion of weaning was not a reason for delay, but the signal that the time had come to fulfill her promise to the Lord.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 21:8 — "So the child grew and was weaned. And Abraham made a great feast on the same day that Isaac was weaned."

  • 1 Samuel 1:23 — "Then Elkanah her husband said to her, 'Do what seems best to you; wait until you have weaned him. Only let the LORD establish His word.' So the woman stayed and nursed her son until she had weaned him."

  • 1 Samuel 24:17 — "Then he said to David: 'You are more righteous than I; for you have rewarded me with good, whereas I have rewarded you with evil.'"

  • Psalms 13:6 — "I will sing to the LORD, because He has dealt bountifully with me."

  • Psalms 131:2 — "Surely I have calmed and quieted my soul, like a weaned child with his mother; like a weaned child is my soul within me."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Psalm 131:2, the image of a weaned child portrays a posture of quiet trust before God. An unweaned infant cries out anxiously for milk, but a weaned child rests contentedly beside its mother, satisfied simply to be in her presence. In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah's attentive care during Samuel's early years brought him to a place of physical and emotional readiness, laying the groundwork for a life lived continually in the presence of the Lord.
