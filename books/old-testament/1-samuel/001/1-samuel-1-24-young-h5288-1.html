YOUNG - (naʻar, Strong's H5288)

Summary:

The word na‘ar refers to a young male, ranging in age from an infant or weaned child to a youth or young attendant. In 1 Samuel 1:24, it describes the child Samuel at the moment his mother Hannah presents him to the priest Eli at Shiloh. The NKJV translates the target word as "young" in the phrase "and the child was young." By pointing out Samuel's tender age and physical dependency, the passage highlights Hannah's extraordinary faith in surrendering her small, freshly weaned boy into the lifelong service of the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israelite culture, na‘ar described a male who had not yet reached full adult standing or independence. The word could refer to a newborn, a newly weaned boy, an adolescent, or a young man serving in a domestic, military, or priestly role. Because the term carried ideas of youthful age as well as subordinate status, its precise meaning depends on who is being described and what role they play in the narrative.

1 Samuel 1 records the backstory of Samuel's birth and dedication. Hannah, who had suffered years of barrenness and provocation, prayed fervently at the sanctuary in Shiloh for a male child. She vowed that if God granted her request, she would yield the boy back to Him for His entire life. After God answered her prayer and gave her a son, Hannah cared for Samuel until he was weaned—likely around two or three years old in ancient Near Eastern practice. She then traveled back to Shiloh to fulfill her solemn promise.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary sense of na‘ar centers on youth, early life, and dependency. Depending on the context, the word can refer to:

  • A baby or small boy.

  • A young lad under parental authority.

  • A youth or young man of military age.

  • A personal servant, assistant, or attendant under a superior's direction.

Here in 1 Samuel 1:24, the word refers to an early stage of childhood. The surrounding narrative makes clear that Samuel was just old enough to be weaned from his mother. By selecting na‘ar to describe Samuel at this moment, the narrative draws attention to his vulnerability and early youth as he enters the sanctuary of the Lord.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, na‘ar adapts to the social and narrative context in which it appears:

  • When describing young children, it emphasizes helplessness, physical weakness, or the need for protection and guidance.

  • When describing adolescents or young adults, it underscores their youthfulness relative to older family leaders or mature warriors.

  • When describing personal attendants or officials, it highlights their subordinate role under a master, king, or leader.

In 1 Samuel 1:24, the context focuses entirely on age and physical vulnerability. Samuel is not an established servant or a young soldier; he is a small child newly separated from his mother's daily care.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's presentation of her young child at Shiloh illustrates the depth of her devotion and trust in God. Surrendering a small, vulnerable child to the care of the sanctuary required immense faith. Hannah did not wait until Samuel was fully grown or self-sufficient before fulfilling her promise. She handed him over while he was still dependent, trusting that the God who granted his birth would preserve his life and guide his future.

This passage also introduces a major theme in the books of Samuel: God works through the weak, the small, and the unexpected to accomplish His purpose. At a time when Israel's spiritual leadership was collapsing under corrupt leaders, God prepared a young child to be His voice for the nation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:24, Hannah brings a generous sacrifice alongside her son: three bulls, an ephah of flour, and a skin of wine. Yet the most striking offering is the child himself. The Hebrew text creates an emphatic statement: "and the child was young." Grammatically, the target occurrence functions as a singular noun of common gender describing Samuel's status at that moment.

This repetition underscores the contrast between the magnitude of Hannah's offering and the smallness of the child. Humanly speaking, a child of such tender years seemed completely helpless. Yet this small boy was brought into the sanctuary to live before the Lord. The passage sets this tender child in quiet contrast to the corrupt adult sons of Eli described in the very next chapter, showing that true spiritual devotion begins with wholehearted surrender to God.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's act of giving up her small child was a pivotal moment in the redemption story of Israel. Samuel's childhood service at Shiloh bridged the gap between the chaotic era of the Judges and the establishment of the monarchy. By surrendering her young son to God, Hannah opened the door for Samuel to grow into a faithful prophet, priest, and leader who would eventually anoint King David.
