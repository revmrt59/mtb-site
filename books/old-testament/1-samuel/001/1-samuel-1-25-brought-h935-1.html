BROUGHT - (bôwʼ, Strong's H935)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb bo basically means to go, come, or enter. When used in a causative sense, it means to bring, introduce, or present a person or object to a specific location or recipient. In 1 Samuel 1:25, Elkanah and Hannah "brought" young Samuel to Eli the priest at the sanctuary in Shiloh, fulfilling Hannah's vow to dedicate her child to the service of the LORD.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew verb bo is one of the most common motion verbs in the Old Testament. In its standard form, it describes simple movement—coming, going, or entering a place. When expressed in a causative action, however, it shifts from self-movement to directing something or someone else: to bring, lead, carry, or present.

In ancient Israel, bringing an offering or presenting a person at the sanctuary was a essential part of corporate and personal devotion. True worship involved tangible presentation before God's presence. In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah had prayed in deep grief for a child, vowing to dedicate him to the LORD if God granted her request. After Samuel was born and weaned, Hannah and her husband Elkanah traveled to Shiloh to fulfill that pledge, bringing the young boy directly to Eli the high priest.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to moving from one point to another, entering a boundary, or presenting something to another person. Depending on the setting, the word carries several clear shades of meaning:

  • Moving toward a goal or destination (to come or go).

  • Entering into a tent, house, city, or land (to enter).

  • Escorting or transporting an object, animal, or person to a recipient (to bring or present).

  • Presenting a sacrifice, gift, or dedicated person before God as an act of worship.

In 1 Samuel 1:25, the word means to escort or formally present a person. Elkanah and Hannah brought young Samuel into Eli's presence, surrendering the boy into priestly care for lifelong service at the house of the LORD.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses bo across every sphere of life, from everyday travel to formal royal decrees and sacred sanctuary rituals. A traveler comes into a home, a king brings gifts, or an army enters a territory.

When the word describes bringing offerings or individuals to God's sanctuary, it carries deep devotional meaning. Bringing a sacrifice was not merely transportation; it signified a transfer of ownership and a formal act of tribute. When parents brought a dedicated child to the sanctuary, they were actively placing that child into God's domain. The action marked a permanent shift: what was cared for in the private home was now presented and given over to the LORD's house.

4. Theological Implications

The act of bringing Samuel to Eli highlights the nature of fulfilled vows and wholehearted devotion. Hannah's earlier prayer was not a casual promise, but a solemn commitment to honor God.

First, bringing Samuel shows that genuine worship returns to God what He has graciously provided. Hannah recognized that her son was a direct gift from the LORD. Bringing him to Shiloh proved that she trusted God's faithfulness more than her own desire to hold onto the child.

Second, the passage reveals unified commitment within the home. The text notes that "they" performed the sacrifice and "brought" the boy. Elkanah supported his wife's vow, and together as a family they brought Samuel to the priest. Faithfulness to God is meant to be lived out cooperatively in the household.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:25, the grammatical form is masculine plural and imperfect with wayyiqtol, driving the historical narrative forward: "Then they slaughtered a bull, and brought the child to Eli."

The surrounding verses highlight the careful worship that accompanied this moment. In verse 24, Hannah brought Samuel along with three bulls, flour, and wine to Shiloh. Verse 25 records the sacrifice of the bull followed immediately by bringing the boy to Eli. The animal sacrifice prepared the way for presenting the dedicated child. By bringing Samuel to Eli, Hannah and Elkanah completed the vow, moving Samuel from a mother's private prayer into public, lifelong consecration before the LORD.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:19 — Out of the ground God formed every beast and "brought" them to Adam to see what he would call them.

  • 1 Samuel 19:7 — Jonathan spoke with David and "brought" him to Saul, restoring him to the king's presence.

  • Esther 1:11 — King Ahasuerus commanded his attendants to "bring" Queen Vashti before the king wearing her royal crown.

  • Ezekiel 20:37 — God declared that He would cause His people to pass under the rod and "bring" them into the bond of the covenant.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Israelite worship, presenting a child at the tabernacle was an intentional, public act. Eli was the chief priest representing God's authority at Shiloh. By bringing Samuel directly to Eli, Hannah was not abandoning her child, but placing him under official sanctuary leadership so he could grow up ministering before the LORD. This deliberate presentation set the stage for Samuel's later calling as a prophet and leader in Israel.
