CHILD - (naʻar, Strong's H5288)

Summary:

The Hebrew word na'ar refers broadly to a young person, ranging from an infant or small child to a youth or personal attendant. In 1 Samuel 1:25, na'ar describes the young child Samuel as Hannah brings him to the tabernacle at Shiloh, fulfilling her vow to dedicate him to the Lord's service for life.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word na'ar is rooted in everyday

ancient Hebrew language, describing a male who has not yet reached full adulthood or maturity. It can refer to an infant, a toddler, a young boy, a teenager, or a young man. Additionally, the word was used for servants or assistants who served under a superior, reflecting their position of reliance and duty rather than strictly their physical age.

In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah endured years of sorrow due to her barrenness and the taunting of her husband's second wife. During an annual pilgrimage to Shiloh, Hannah prayed desperately to God, asking for a son and promising that if God answered her prayer, she would dedicate the child completely to God's service. God answered her prayer, and Hannah gave birth to Samuel. After weaning the boy, she brought him to Shiloh to fulfill her solemn vow.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Na'ar can refer to a young child, a growing youth, or a personal helper. The word does not lock the subject into a single fixed age group. Instead, it marks relative youthfulness, dependence, or a subordinate role.

In 1 Samuel 1:25, the word refers to young Samuel at the point of his weaning. He was a small boy, entirely dependent on his parents, yet designated for a life of sacred service in God's presence.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

across the Old Testament, na'ar highlights different aspects of youth or service. In Genesis 22:12, the word is translated as "lad" when referring to Isaac on Mount Moriah. In Genesis 44:33, Judah pleads for young Benjamin, calling him the "lad." The word is also applied to young adult assistants, such as the servant overseeing Boaz's reapers in Ruth 2:5 or Saul's attendant in 1 Samuel 9:8. When David stepped forward to face Goliath in 1 Samuel 17:55, Saul referred to him as a "youth."

In 1 Samuel 1:25, the nuance focuses on Samuel's early childhood. The word highlights his tender age and vulnerability as his mother relinquishes her day-to-day care of him to place him into God's house under Eli's care.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's presentation of the na'ar Samuel illustrates total devotion and trust in God's promises. Giving up a young child was an extraordinary sacrifice of motherly affection, showing that her devotion to God outweighed every earthly attachment.

Furthermore, God's choice to begin a transformative work in Israel through a helpless child highlights how divine power operates. At a time when the priesthood under Eli was weak and corrupt, God prepared a young child to hear His voice and renew the prophetic witness in Israel. God frequently uses the small, young, and humble to fulfill His redemptive plans.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:25, the text notes that after sacrificing a bull, they brought the "child" to Eli. Grammatically, the word appears in the singular number and common gender. This singular focus centers attention directly on young Samuel as the answered prayer and the object of Hannah's vow.

The immediate context emphasizes the transition from Hannah's private tearful prayer to public fulfillment. Immediately after this verse, Hannah reminds Eli, "For this child I prayed, and the Lord has granted me my petition." The presence of the young boy contrasts sharply with the elderly Eli and his faithless sons. Samuel's childhood innocence and early dedication stand as a sign of spiritual hope for the entire nation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 22:12 – God stops Abraham from harming the "lad" Isaac, proving Abraham's faith while preserving the son of promise.

  • Genesis 44:33 – Judah begs to stay in Egypt as a slave in place of the "lad" Benjamin so their father will not die of grief.

  • 1 Samuel 17:55 – King Saul asks about David's family line, calling the young warrior a "youth" before he fights Goliath.

  • 2 Samuel 12:16 – David fasts and lies on the ground, pleading with God to spare the life of his sick "child."

  • Isaiah 8:4 – The prophecy describes a time before the "child" is old enough to speak basic words, signaling swift divine judgment.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In ancient Israelite culture, children were typically weaned around two to three years of age. When Hannah brought Samuel to Shiloh, he was still very young, making her commitment all the more striking. Leaving such a young child at the tabernacle required immense faith that God would protect and guide him. Samuel grew up in the sanctuary, serving before the Lord even as a child, setting a clear biblical pattern of early devotion to God.
