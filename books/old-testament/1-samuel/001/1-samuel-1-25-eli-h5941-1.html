ELI - (ʻÊlîy, Strong's H5941)

Summary:

The proper name Eli refers to the high priest and judge who served at the sanctuary in Shiloh during the transition from the period of the judges to the kingdom of Israel. The name means "exalted" or "high." In 1 Samuel 1:25, Eli is the priest to whom Hannah and Elkanah bring young Samuel after offering their sacrifice, marking the formal fulfillment of Hannah's vow and placing Samuel into life-long service to the LORD under priestly oversight.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Eli was the chief priest and leader in Israel during the closing years of the period of the judges. He served at the central place of worship in Shiloh, where the tabernacle and the ark of the covenant were located before the temple was built in Jerusalem. Eli belonged to the priestly line of Ithamar, the younger son of Aaron.

The narrative in 1 Samuel opens at a time of spiritual decline in Israel. While the public rituals of sacrifice continued at Shiloh, the leaders were often weak or corrupt. Against this dark background, Hannah came to pray for a child. Eli served as the high priest who observed her praying, pronounced a blessing over her, and later received her son into God's service.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Eli is a proper masculine noun. The name is related to a root meaning "to go up," "ascend," or "be high," giving Eli the meaning of "exalted" or "high."

In biblical usage, the name refers specifically to this high priest of Shiloh. It functions as an personal name identifying the priest who oversaw the sanctuary, guided the worship of Israel, judged the people, and helped raise young Samuel.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the name Eli exclusively for this priest in the book of 1 Samuel. His character is presented with notable nuance and realism:

  • Priestly Authority: Eli sits at the doorpost of the tabernacle as the chief religious official in Israel, responsible for receiving sacrifices and maintaining order at the sanctuary.

  • Spiritual Perception and Blindness: Eli initially misinterprets Hannah's earnest, silent prayer as drunkenness, showing a lack of immediate spiritual discernment. Yet when she explains her sorrow, he readily corrects his judgment and speaks a word of peace and blessing over her.

  • Parental Failure: Eli's sons, Hophni and Phinehas, served as priests alongside him but were deeply corrupt. Scripture notes that Eli failed to restrain their wicked actions, bringing divine judgment upon his family line.

  • Mentorship: Despite his personal and family weaknesses, Eli served as a mentor to Samuel. When God called Samuel in the night, Eli recognized that the LORD was speaking and taught the young boy how to respond to God's voice.

4. Theological Implications

The account of Eli highlights how God accomplishes His purpose through human ministers while remaining holy and uncompromised by human failure.

Eli held a high and exalted office, but his position did not excuse his failure to honor God above his sons. His life demonstrates that genuine spiritual authority requires personal obedience and faithfulness. At the same time, God used Eli to receive Samuel's dedication and teach Samuel how to hear the LORD's voice. God's redemptive work was not stopped by Eli's flaws; instead, God prepared a faithful leader in Samuel right within Eli's household.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:25, Elkanah and Hannah slaughter a bull and bring the young child "to Eli." This moment is the high point of Hannah's story of answered prayer.

By bringing Samuel directly to Eli, Hannah fulfills the vow she made in her distress. She does not keep her son at home or alter her promise once her prayer is granted. Bringing him to Eli represents handing Samuel over to the official priesthood at Shiloh so that he can be dedicated entirely to God's service.

This meeting creates a meaningful contrast with her earlier encounter with Eli. In their first meeting, Eli misunderstood her sorrow and questioned her conduct. Now, Hannah returns to the same priest, bringing the living evidence of God's mercy. Eli receives the child, acknowledging that God has answered the woman he once misjudged.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Eli's willingness to guide Samuel reveals a teachable and humble spirit near the end of his life. When Eli realized that God was speaking directly to the young boy rather than to himself, he did not become bitter or jealous. Instead, he carefully instructed Samuel to answer, "Speak, LORD, for Your servant hears."

Furthermore, Eli accepted the hard message of judgment that God gave to Samuel concerning Eli's own house. Even in judgment, Eli submitted to the sovereign will of God. His role in Samuel's life ensured that proper tabernacle training was passed on to the prophet who would eventually annoint Israel's first kings.
