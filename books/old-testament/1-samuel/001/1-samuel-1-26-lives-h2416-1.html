LIVES - (chay, Strong's H2416)

Summary:

The Hebrew word chai fundamentally means "alive," "living," or "life." It describes the presence of vitality, breath, and active existence in contrast to death or non-existence. Beyond describing physical life, chai frequently appears in biblical oath formulas where a speaker appeals to the living reality of God or another person to solemnize a truth or commitment. In 1 Samuel 1:26, Hannah uses this oath phrase—"As your soul lives"—to assure the high priest Eli of her identity as the once-barren woman who prayed in his presence, confirming that God has faithfully answered her prayer.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israelite society, spoken declarations carried immense weight, especially when making solemn assertions or vows. To ensure that a statement was accepted as absolute truth, a speaker often framed the assertion with a living witness. The Hebrew word chai forms the core of these solemn formulas.

In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah had previously stood before the priest Eli at the tabernacle in Shiloh, weeping in deep distress because she was barren. Eli initially mistook her silent prayer for drunkenness, but Hannah explained her grief, and Eli pronounced a blessing over her. After returning home, Hannah conceived and gave birth to Samuel. Once the boy was weaned, she brought him back to Shiloh to present him to Eli and dedicate him permanently to the service of the LORD. To remind Eli of who she was and to affirm the reality of God's answer, Hannah introduced her testimony with a solemn oath containing chai.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word chai refers broadly to life and vitality. It can describe living human beings, active animals, flowing water, or God Himself as the source and possessor of life.

When applied to physical existence, the word highlights active movement, breath, and present reality in contrast to dead or lifeless things. When used in oaths and solemn declarations, chai means "living" or "as [someone] lives." By invoking the living status of a person or the living reality of God, the speaker connects the truthfulness of their declaration to an undeniable fact: just as surely as the person or God is currently alive, the spoken statement is true.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Scripture, chai appears in a variety of settings:

  • Physical vitality: It describes the ongoing lifespan of individuals or the state of being alive rather than dead.

  • Fresh or active nature: It is used to describe fresh, flowing water ("living water") as opposed to stagnant pools.

  • Divine attribute: God is spoken of as the living God, distinguished from inanimate idols who have no breath or life.

  • Oath formula: The word appears repeatedly in expressions like "As the LORD lives" or "As your soul lives." In these phrases, chai serves as a solemn assertion of certainty.

In terms of grammatical form, the target occurrence in 1 Samuel 1:26 is singular in number and common in gender. This singular form directly aligns with the single individual—Eli—whose present, living existence Hannah invokes to validate her identity and testimony.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical concept of life is rooted in God as the Creator and Sustainer of all existence. Life is not an independent or self-sustaining force; it exists because God bestows breath and keeps creatures alive.

When people in Scripture swear by the life of God or by the life of a person standing before them, they acknowledge that life is an undeniable, concrete reality. Human life is fragile and temporary, yet visible and felt; divine life is absolute, eternal, and sovereign. By linking a statement to life, biblical speakers ground their truthfulness in something that cannot be questioned. Hannah's use of this expression highlights her confidence that the living God acts within human history to give life, answer prayer, and fulfill His promises.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:26, Hannah returns to Shiloh carrying her young son Samuel. Years had passed since her sorrowful prayer at the sanctuary, and Eli might not have immediately recognized the joyful mother standing before him.

To bridge this gap and establish her credibility, Hannah says to Eli, "O my lord! As your soul lives, my lord, I am the woman who stood by you here, praying to the LORD." Her use of "As your soul lives" serves several distinct contextual functions:

  • Respectful declaration: It treats Eli with honor as the priest, appealing to his own living presence as a witness to her truthfulness.

  • Legal and spiritual solemnity: It elevates her statement above casual conversation, signaling that what follows is a serious, holy testimony of answered prayer.

  • Connection between past grief and present joy: By recalling the exact spot where she prayed in anguish, she demonstrates that the living child in her arms is the direct result of the petition she made to the LORD.

Hannah does not invent a new theological category with the word chai; rather, she uses an established conversational oath to ensure Eli recognizes her as the very woman whose tears God transformed into joy.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Kings 18:15 — "As the LORD of hosts lives, before whom I stand, I will surely show myself to him today."

  • 2 Samuel 22:47 — "The LORD lives! Blessed be my Rock! Let God be exalted, The Rock of my salvation!"

  • Jeremiah 2:13 — "For My people have committed two evils: They have forsaken Me, the fountain of living waters, and hewn themselves cisterns—broken cisterns that can hold no water."

  • Psalms 23:6 — "Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life; and I will dwell in the house of the LORD forever."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The expression "As your soul lives" was a customary polite oath in ancient Israel when speaking to a superior or person of authority. By invoking the life of the hearer's "soul" (or inner self), the speaker expressed deep respect while giving a solemn guarantee of truthfulness.

In Hannah's case, this greeting contrasts strikingly with her previous visit. On her earlier visit, her soul was poured out in bitterness and sorrow before the LORD. Now, standing before Eli once more, she appeals to Eli's living soul to confirm that her mourning has ended, her vow is being fulfilled, and Samuel is now being presented for lifelong service to God.
