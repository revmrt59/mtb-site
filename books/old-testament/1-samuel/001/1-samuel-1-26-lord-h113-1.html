LORD - (ʼâdôwn, Strong's H113)

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'adon refers to an owner, master, ruler, or person holding authority over others. It is widely used in Scripture to express honor, leadership, and respect across household, civil, and religious settings. When spoken directly to a superior, it serves as a polite honorific title equivalent to "my lord" or "sir." In 1 Samuel 1:26, Hannah uses this title to address Eli the high priest with deep respect as she presents her young son Samuel to serve at the tabernacle, reminding Eli of her earlier tearful prayer in his presence.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israelite society, social life and worship were organized around clear lines of authority, household headship, and divine calling. The word 'adon was the standard term used to recognize someone of higher station or authority—such as a king, a master of a household, a leader, or a priest.

When spoken in direct conversation, 'adon functioned as a title of courtesy and humility. It signaled that the speaker recognized the listener's God-given role or position. In 1 Samuel 1:26, Hannah approaches Eli at the sanctuary in Shiloh. Years before, Eli had seen her praying in silent grief and misjudged her heart. Now returning with her child, Hannah addresses Eli as 'adon to show appropriate honor to his office as priest while witnessing to the Lord's answer to her prayer.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word 'adon refers to someone who exercises authority, oversight, or ownership. Depending on who is being addressed, the word can mean:

  • A master or ruler over servants or subjects.

  • A human superior, where it functions as a polite address ("my lord" or "sir").

  • God Himself, highlighting His supreme power and ownership over creation.

In 1 Samuel 1:26, the noun is singular and serves as a respectful title directed to Eli, acknowledging his position as the priest serving before the Lord.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses 'adon in several distinct settings:

  • Household and master-servant relationships: It describes a master who has responsibility for a household or servant.

  • Royal and leadership titles: Subjects use it when speaking to or about a king or civil leader to show loyalty and submission.

  • Courteous personal address: People use it to show honor to elders, prophets, or priests during conversation.

  • Divine lordship: It is used of God to affirm His authority as the true Lord over all rulers and nations.

In 1 Samuel 1:26, the word reflects proper spiritual and social protocol. Hannah does not approach the sanctuary priest with pride or self-assertion, even though God has granted her request. Instead, she maintains a humble, respectful posture toward God's appointed servant.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah’s use of 'adon in this passage offers several meaningful theological lessons:

  • Honor for God's appointed leaders: Hannah shows respect to Eli's priestly office, demonstrating that personal piety before God is displayed through godly humility toward human authority.

  • A witness to answered prayer: By addressing Eli as "my lord" and reminding him of her past anguish, Hannah turns Eli's memory into a testimony. The priest who once questioned her now sees the child given by God.

  • Submission behind worship: Hannah’s polite speech reflects a heart completely surrendered to God. She surrenders her most precious gift—her long-awaited son—to the Lord's service without reservation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:26, Hannah returns to Shiloh after Samuel is weaned to fulfill the vow she had made to God. She greets Eli with a solemn oath of respect ("As your soul lives, my lord").

The context highlights a striking contrast between past and present:

  • In her earlier visit, Eli saw her lips moving without sound and wrongly accused her of drunkenness.

  • Hannah responded gently, explaining her sorrow, and Eli blessed her.

  • Now, Hannah uses 'adon to re-establish her identity in Eli's mind: "I am the woman who stood by you here, praying to the LORD."

Her use of 'adon keeps the focus where it belongs. She is not arriving to prove Eli wrong or to boast about her victory. She comes in humble devotion, acknowledging Eli's position while giving all glory to the God who heard her cry.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 24:36 — Abraham's servant speaks of his "master" Abraham receiving great blessings from God, showing the word used for household authority and leadership.

  • Joshua 5:14 — Joshua falls to the earth before the Commander of the army of the LORD and asks what his "Lord" requires of him, showing the term used in worshipful submission.

  • 1 Samuel 24:8 — David calls out to Saul, referring to him as his "lord" the king, demonstrating respect for God's anointed leader even while being unjustly hunted by him.

  • Psalms 136:3 — The psalmist gives thanks to the "Lord" of lords, using the word to praise God's ultimate supreme rule over every power on earth.

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Delegated authority versus absolute authority: Hannah addresses Eli as her human superior ('adon), but her covenant vow was made directly to Yahweh, the supreme Lord. Human authority in Scripture is always under and subject to divine authority.

  • A model of gentle persistence: Hannah's respectful address reminds readers of her quiet strength. She did not retaliate when misunderstood by leadership; instead, she trusted God to vindicate her in His timing.

  • Turning point in Israel's history: This respectful meeting between Hannah and Eli marks the formal presentation of Samuel at the sanctuary. Samuel's life of ministry, born out of Hannah's faithful vow, would bring renewal to Israel during a spiritually dark period.
