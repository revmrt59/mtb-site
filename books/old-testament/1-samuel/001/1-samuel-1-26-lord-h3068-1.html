LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

The personal proper name of the God of Israel, revealing Him as the living, true, and self-existent God who makes and keeps promises to His people. In 1 Samuel 1:26, Hannah uses this personal name to identify the living God to whom she poured out her heart in desperate prayer and who faithfully answered her request by giving her a son.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The name transliterated as YHWH is the distinct personal name of God in the Hebrew Scriptures. Unlike general terms for deity or titles of leadership, this name was revealed directly by God as His personal designation when establishing His relationship with Israel. Out of deep reverence for the holiness of God, ancient Jewish readers traditionally refrained from pronouncing the personal name aloud, substituting the title Adonai (Lord) during public reading. Modern English translations reflect this tradition by rendering the name as "LORD" in capital or small capital letters. In the narrative of 1 Samuel, Israel stood at a transition from the chaotic period of the Judges to the establishment of the monarchy. The open vision and revelation of God were rare, making personal encounters with the living God profound testimonies of His active care.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word is a proper name designating the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. It is not a common noun or an abstract title, but the actual personal name of Israel's God.

The name distinguishes the God of Israel from false idols and from human rulers. While titles like Elohim describe God in His majesty as Creator, and Adonai describes His lordship and authority as Master, YHWH is the specific name by which God invites His people into personal relationship and prayerful dependence.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament narrative, poetry, and prophecy, this divine name functions as the anchor of divine revelation.

  • As a personal name, it identifies God as an active subject who speaks, makes oaths, hears human suffering, and acts in history.

  • As a marker of distinction, it separates the Creator from created beings and human leaders.

  • In address, it forms the basis of direct prayer and worship, where believers approach God not as an unknown force, but as the God who has made Himself known.

Scripture repeatedly uses this personal name when highlighting God's intervention in human weakness, emphasizing that the Almighty condescends to hear the humble.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of God's personal name reminds readers that biblical faith is rooted in a God who reveals Himself personally. God does not remain an abstract concept or an distant deity; He makes Himself known by name so His people may call upon Him.

The name assures believers that God is faithful to His word and attentive to individual human grief. When individuals turn to Him in petition, they pray to a living Person who possesses both the desire and the power to redeem, deliver, and answer prayer according to His sovereign purpose.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:26, Hannah stands before Eli the priest at the tabernacle in Shiloh, presenting her young son Samuel to be dedicated to divine service. Her statement creates a meaningful distinction in English through the titles used: she addresses Eli with human respect as "my lord" (adoni), but identifies the true recipient of her worship and prayer as "the LORD" (YHWH).

Years earlier, Eli had misjudged her silent, sorrowful praying as drunkenness. Human authority figures could neither read her heart nor grant her desire. Yet Hannah directed her anguish to YHWH, the personal God who listens. Standing now with the child in her arms, she testifies that her presence there is direct proof that God hears prayer. The name highlights that her miracle was not accomplished by priest, ritual, or human effort, but by the personal God who mercifully remembered her.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 — "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens."

  • Exodus 13:5 — "And it shall be, when the LORD brings you into the land of the Canaanites... which He swore to your fathers to give you..."

  • Psalms 25:1 — "To You, O LORD, I lift up my soul."

  • Proverbs 16:6 — "In mercy and truth atonement is provided for iniquity; and by the fear of the LORD one departs from evil."

  • Jeremiah 33:1 — "Moreover the word of the LORD came to Jeremiah the second time, while he was still shut up in the court of the prison..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's use of the divine name in 1 Samuel 1:26 highlights the power of quiet, persistent faith. In a time when religious leadership in Shiloh was compromised under Eli's sons, Hannah demonstrated that access to God remains open to every believer. She did not rely on human influence, but directly petitioned the God of Israel by name. Her answered prayer not only granted her a son, but delivered to Israel a faithful prophet who would help guide the nation into a new era.
