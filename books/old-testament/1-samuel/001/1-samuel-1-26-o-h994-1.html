O - (bîy, Strong's H994)

Summary:

The Hebrew word bi is an interjection used to introduce a humble, polite, and earnest petition to someone in authority. It expresses deferential address, often translated as "O" or "please," signaling respect and seriousness when appealing to a leader, an angel, or God Himself. In 1 Samuel 1:26, Hannah uses this word when addressing Eli the high priest as she brings her young son Samuel to be dedicated at the tabernacle. Her use of bi highlights her humble respect and reverent joy as she reminds Eli of her earlier weeping prayer and bears witness that God heard her cry.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Hebrew communication, spoken address to a superior—whether a priest, a king, a prophet, or the Lord Himself—carried careful customs of reverence and humility. The word bi served as an opening particle of entreaty. Rather than bursting into speech or demanding immediate attention, a speaker used this term to open a solemn appeal or polite request.

In the narrative of 1 Samuel 1, Hannah had previously stood at the tabernacle in Shiloh brokenhearted, praying silently with moving lips while carrying the heavy grief of childlessness. Eli had initially misjudged her, accusing her of drunkenness. After she clarified her anguish, Eli pronounced a blessing over her. Years later, having received the child God promised, Hannah returned to Shiloh to fulfill her vow. By beginning her speech with bi ("O my lord"), she approaches the sanctuary with humility, presenting both her son and her testimony of answered prayer.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word bi functions as an interjection of entreaty or supplication. It can refer to a polite petition, an earnest plea, or an expression of deference. In English Bibles, it is rendered with words like "O," "oh," "please," or "allow me."

When a person speaks to a ruler or authority, bi signals respect, acknowledging the position of the hearer. When a worshipper addresses God or His messenger, it expresses solemn desperation or deep reverence. It does not carry complex grammatical forms; rather, it sets the tone of the sentence that follows, signaling that the speaker comes not with self-righteous demands, but with a humble appeal.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament narratives, bi consistently appears in moments of intense personal conviction, urgent pleading, or respectful petition. It is almost always paired with the title "my lord" ('adonai), forming a standard opening phrase for earnest address.

The particle appears in several distinct settings:

  • Appeals for Mercy and Guidance: When individuals face staggering circumstances or divine encounters, they use this term to ask for grace or wisdom before speaking further.

  • Humble Requests before Leadership: Citizens or servants use the word when bringing a delicate or urgent matter to a judge, king, or priest.

  • Reverent Intercession before God: Leaders and parents use the word in prayer when asking God for mercy, help, or clear direction.

In every case, the word reflects an attitude of surrender and restraint, demonstrating that the speaker recognizes the gravity of the moment and the authority of the one being addressed.

4. Theological Implications

While bi is a short interjection, its placement in Scripture highlights key truths about biblical prayer and faithful worship:

  • Humility in God's Presence: The word demonstrates that approaching God or His appointed ministers requires a heart free of arrogance. True prayer comes from a position of humble trust rather than entitled demands.

  • Reverence Joined with Boldness: Hannah's address shows that deep reverence does not prevent bold faith. She speaks deferentially ("O my lord"), yet she boldly testifies to the miraculous work God accomplished in her life.

  • The Transformation of Sorrow into Praise: Hannah previously came to Shiloh in silent grief; she returns with a spoken song of thanksgiving. The opening word of her address marks the transition from private suffering to public testimony of divine faithfulness.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:26, Hannah steps forward to present the young child Samuel to Eli. She opens with: "O my lord! As your soul lives, my lord, I am the woman who stood by you here, praying to the LORD."

Her use of bi ("O") carries clear contextual significance:

First, it establishes courtesy and honors Eli’s priesthood. Hannah does not harbor bitterness over Eli's earlier misunderstanding. Instead, she addresses him with complete respect.

Second, it bridges her past sorrow with her present joy. By calling Eli’s attention back to the exact spot where she stood weeping, she anchors her current testimony in her past supplication. The same woman who once prayed in quiet anguish now stands before him with the living answer to her prayer.

Third, it prepares Eli to hear her solemn vow. She is not merely making small talk; she is delivering her long-awaited son to serve the LORD all the days of his life. The deferential opening sentence underlines the solemnity of dedicating her child to the sanctuary.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Joshua 7:8 — "O Lord, what shall I say when Israel turns its back before its enemies?"

  • Here Joshua uses the word while prostrating himself before the LORD in desperate intercession following Israel's defeat at Ai.

  • Judges 6:13 — "Gideon said to Him, 'O my lord, if the LORD is with us, why then has all this happened to us?'"

  • Gideon uses the word (associated with the rendering "to") when addressing the Angel of the LORD in honest struggle and deep distress regarding Israel's oppression.

  • Judges 6:15 — "So he said to Him, 'O my Lord, how can I save Israel?'"

  • Gideon uses the word again (associated with the rendering "to") to express his feelings of inadequacy when called to deliver his nation.

  • Judges 13:8 — "Then Manoah prayed to the LORD, and said, 'O my Lord, please let the Man of God whom You sent come to us again...'"

  • Samson's father uses the word (associated with the rendering "to") to humbly ask God for divine guidance in raising the child promised to them.

  • 1 Kings 3:26 — "Then the woman whose son was living spoke to the king... and she said, 'O my lord, give her the living child, and by no means kill him!'"

  • The compassionate mother uses the word (associated with the rendering "to") in an urgent, tearful plea before King Solomon to save her infant's life.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The phrase "As your soul lives" immediately follows bi in 1 Samuel 1:26. This was a solemn oath in ancient Israel, emphasizing the reality and truthfulness of what was being said. Hannah’s greeting combines polite entreaty (bi) with a solemn declaration of fact, demonstrating her complete truthfulness as she hands Samuel over to Eli.

Furthermore, Hannah's respectful spirit provides a striking contrast to the wicked behavior of Eli's own sons, Hophni and Phinehas, described in the next chapter. While Eli's sons treated the sanctuary and the LORD's offerings with contempt, Hannah—an ordinary woman from the hill country—approached the tabernacle with profound reverence, honor, and heartfelt devotion.
