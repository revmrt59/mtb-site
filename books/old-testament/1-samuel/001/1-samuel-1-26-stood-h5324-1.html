STOOD - (nâtsab, Strong's H5324)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb natsav means to take a position, station oneself, or stand firm in a specific location. In 1 Samuel 1:26, Hannah uses this word as a feminine singular participle to identify herself to Eli as "the woman who stood by you here, praying to the LORD." By referencing her former posture in that exact location, she connects her past season of desperate prayer with the child now standing beside her, demonstrating that Samuel is the direct answer to her supplication before the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israel, taking a physical stance in a designated location often signified presentation, readiness, or persistent engagement. In the opening chapter of 1 Samuel, Hannah arrives at the tabernacle in Shiloh burdened by years of childlessness and grief. In her distress, she stations herself in prayer before the Lord near the doorway of the sanctuary.

Years later, after the Lord answers her petition and gives her a son, Hannah returns to Shiloh to dedicate young Samuel to divine service. Addressing Eli the high priest, she uses natsav to recall her previous presence at that very spot. The word grounds her testimony in historical reality, pointing Eli back to the exact place where her quiet, tearful petitions were offered.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb natsav refers to taking a position, stationing oneself, standing firm, or being set up in a particular place. Depending on the context, the word can describe:

  • A person taking a physical stance or positioning themselves in a place.

  • An object, such as a pillar or monument, being set upright.

  • An official positioned or appointed in authority over a task.

When used of individuals approaching God or a figure of authority, natsav implies an intentional presence—placing oneself directly before someone to speak, wait, or serve. In 1 Samuel 1:26, the word describes Hannah taking her stance beside Eli at the tabernacle while pouring out her soul in prayer.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, natsav carries distinct nuances depending on who is standing and where they are positioned:

  • Standing in readiness or expectation: Persons station themselves at a location to wait for an outcome or receive a word, such as Abraham standing near his tent entrance when visitors arrive.

  • Standing in divine presence: Servants of God are positioned before Him during sacred encounters, such as Moses standing on the rock at Sinai while God's glory passes by.

  • Set in authority: The word can describe leaders or overseers stationed in charge of tasks, such as officers appointed over reapers.

In 1 Samuel 1:26, the participle form identifies Hannah by her posture of devotion: she was "the woman standing" in focused supplication before the Lord.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's use of natsav in 1 Samuel 1:26 highlights several significant biblical truths:

  • God hears those who present themselves before Him: Taking a stance in prayer expresses dependence and reverence. Hannah's persistent presence at the tabernacle was not ignored by God.

  • Answered prayer yields public praise: The location that once witnessed Hannah's grief now witnesses her gratitude. The posture of sorrow becomes a posture of thanksgiving as she presents Samuel to the Lord.

  • Covenant continuity: Hannah's return to the exact spot where she prayed demonstrates her faithfulness to fulfill the vow she made when she stood there years before.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:26, the context gives natsav rich significance. When Hannah first stood praying at Shiloh, Eli misunderstood her silent, agonizing prayers and accused her of being drunk. Hannah corrected him, explaining that she was pouring out her soul before the Lord.

When she returns with young Samuel, her words—"I am the woman who stood by you here"—instantly bridge the past and the present. She does not merely say "I prayed," but emphasizes her presence at that specific spot. Her physical return to the same location with her child serves as visible proof that her earlier silent prayer was heard and granted. The posture of standing thus marks both the place of her deep need and the place of God's faithful provision.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The grammatical form of natsav in 1 Samuel 1:26 is a feminine singular participle. Rather than describing a fleeting movement, this form functions as a descriptive marker: "I am the woman who was standing here." It anchors Hannah's identity in Eli's memory around that memorable occasion.

Furthermore, this moment at Shiloh represents a pivotal transition in Israel's history. Hannah's private posture of standing in prayer leads to the birth of Samuel, the prophet who would guide Israel from the period of the judges into the monarchy. Her personal act of taking a stand before God directly serves the larger unfolding story of redemption.
