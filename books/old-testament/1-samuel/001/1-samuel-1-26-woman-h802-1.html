WOMAN - (ʼishshâh, Strong's H802)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun 'ishshah means an adult female person or a wife, depending on the context in which it appears. It is the standard Hebrew word for a female human being, used throughout the Old Testament to designate individual women, wives in marriage, or women collectively. In 1 Samuel 1:26, Hannah uses this word to identify herself to Eli the priest ("I am the woman who stood by you here"). By taking this designation, she recalls her previous posture of silent, agonizing prayer in the tabernacle and presents herself now as living evidence of God's answered prayer.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word 'ishshah is the common Old Testament noun for an adult female. It stands as the direct feminine counterpart to 'ish, which means a man or husband. From the opening chapters of Genesis onward, the word describes women across all spheres of ancient Israelite society—as daughters, wives, mothers, widows, and individual members of the covenant community.

In ancient Near Eastern culture, women often had limited public standing outside of their family roles. However, the Hebrew Scriptures consistently present women as morally responsible individuals who relate directly to God. They participate in worship, receive divine promises, bring petitions before the Lord, and play key roles in the history of redemption.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word 'ishshah primarily refers to an adult female human being.

Depending on the context, the word can mean:

  • An individual female person ("woman").

  • A married female partner ("wife").

  • Female persons collectively ("women").

Because Hebrew uses the same noun for both "woman" and "wife," surrounding words and sentence structure indicate which sense is intended. When the word is linked to a specific man or house, it usually means "wife." When it designates a female person generally or identifies someone by her personal actions, it means "woman." In 1 Samuel 1:26, the term identifies Hannah as the specific female individual who had previously stood in Eli's presence pouring out her soul to God.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, 'ishshah reflects the full scope of female life in Israel. It first appears in the creation narrative, where God forms the first female from the man and brings her to him. Throughout the historical and legal books, the term describes wives fulfilling family duties, mothers caring for households, and women participating in religious feasts and offerings.

The word carries no automatic moral qualification by itself. Scripture uses 'ishshah to describe faithful, God-fearing women such as Ruth and Hannah, as well as wicked women such as Jezebel. The character and actions of the person determine the moral tone, while 'ishshah simply identifies her as a female individual.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of 'ishshah demonstrates that God deals directly with women as individual image-bearers and covenant members. Women in Israel were not spiritually invisible; they possessed direct access to God in prayer, received His promises, and were held accountable to His law.

Hannah's interaction with God illustrates this reality clearly. She did not need an earthly mediator to voice her heart's grief to the Lord. Though Eli misjudged her heart when he first saw her praying, God heard her humble petition. The record of God answering Hannah shows that He listens to the quiet, desperate cries of individual believers, regardless of their social power or public position.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:26, Hannah returns to Shiloh bringing the young child Samuel to dedicate him to the service of the Lord. When she approaches Eli, she reminds him of their past encounter by saying, "I am the woman who stood by you here, praying to the LORD."

This reference carries deep contextual weight:

  • Recalling Past Misunderstanding: During her earlier visit, Hannah prayed in her heart with moving lips but no audible voice. Eli had mistakenly judged her as a foolish or intoxicated woman and rebuked her. Now, by using the phrase "the woman who stood by you here," she gently corrects that past judgment without bitterness.

  • Presenting God's Answer: Hannah links her past identity as a sorrowful suppliant to her present reality as a joyful mother. The very woman whom Eli once mistook for a ungodly person now stands before him as a monument of divine mercy.

  • Fulfilling a Vow: By identifying herself as that same woman, Hannah emphasizes her integrity. She promised God that if He gave her a son, she would give him back to the Lord. She returns to the very spot where she prayed to fulfill that pledge completely.

Grammatically, the noun is singular and feminine, fitting Hannah's direct personal testimony as an individual standing before the high priest.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah’s use of 'ishshah' in 1 Samuel 1:26 highlights the personal continuity of faith. She does not claim any title of nobility or public office; she simply refers to herself as "the woman who stood by you here, praying."

This shows that her true identity in that moment was defined by her posture before God: she was a praying woman whom the Lord had heard. Her testimony reminds believers that God remembers the private prayers offered in deep anguish, and He is faithful to complete what He begins in the lives of His people.
