CHILD - (naʻar, Strong's H5288)

Summary:

The Hebrew word na'ar describes a young person across various stages of early life, ranging from an infant or small child to a youth, young man, or attendant. In 1 Samuel 1:27, the word refers to young Samuel, whom God granted to Hannah in response to her earnest prayer. In this verse, the term identifies the actual child standing before Eli as the concrete answer to Hannah's petition, whom she now dedicates to the service of the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israelite life, na'ar was a common term used for young people. It could describe a baby, a young child, an adolescent, or a young man serving in a specific role such as a servant or military assistant. The term highlighted a person's youth, relative position, or dependence on parents or leaders.

The historical setting of 1 Samuel 1 opens with Hannah, a woman suffering under the grief of childlessness. In her distress, she traveled to the tabernacle at Shiloh and prayed fervently to God, asking for a male child and promising to dedicate him to the Lord for all his life. God answered her prayer, and she gave birth to Samuel. After nursing and weaning him, Hannah brought the young boy back to Shiloh to present him to Eli the priest, marking the fulfillment of her vow.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word na'ar can refer to a young child, an infant, a boy, or a young man, as well as a youth who works as a servant or helper. The term naturally emphasizes youthfulness and subordinate status rather than a single specific age boundary.

In 1 Samuel 1:27, the word means a young boy or child. Hannah uses it to point directly to her young son Samuel, identifying him as the physical child she had requested from God during her time of deep sorrow.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament narratives, na'ar covers a broad range of human development:

  • Early childhood: It describes young children and infants who depend entirely on maternal care.

  • Adolescence and early adulthood: It refers to young men capable of serving in households, fields, or military settings.

  • Functional service: It frequently designates an assistant, attendant, or domestic servant under a supervisor.

When Hannah speaks of "this child," she uses the word with deep personal affection and religious reverence. The word identifies Samuel not only by his young age, but as the tangible outcome of her prayer to the Lord.

4. Theological Implications

The use of na'ar in Hannah's prayer highlights several key biblical truths:

  • God hears and answers the prayers of the afflicted: The child standing in the tabernacle serves as direct evidence that God attends to the cries of His people.

  • Human life is a gift from God: Samuel's birth was not merely a natural event, but an explicit act of divine favor toward a woman in distress.

  • Surrender and dedication: Hannah acknowledges that the young child given by God belongs rightfully to God. Her willingness to give him over to tabernacle service shows that true thanksgiving yields what God bestows back into His hands.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:27, Hannah says to Eli, "For this child I prayed, and the LORD has granted me my petition which I asked of Him." The expression "this child" creates a direct link between her past weeping at the tabernacle and her present joy.

The singular form highlights the specific, individual answer God provided. Hannah did not receive a vague spiritual reassurance; she received a living child. The presence of the young boy in the sanctuary demonstrates God's faithfulness and validates Hannah's sacred vow to set him apart for lifelong service to the Lord.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 22:12 — "Do not lay your hand on the lad" — Here the word refers to Isaac as a young man during Abraham's test of faith on Mount Moriah.

  • Ruth 2:5 — "Then Boaz said to his servant who was in charge of the reapers" — In this passage, the word describes a young man serving as an overseer in the harvest fields.

  • 1 Samuel 17:55 — "Whose son is this youth?" — King Saul uses the word to refer to young David as he goes out to face Goliath.

  • 2 Samuel 12:16 — "David therefore pleaded with God for the child" — The word is used for the sick infant born to Bathsheba, over whom David fasted and wept.

  • Isaiah 8:4 — "for before the child shall have knowledge to cry 'My father' and 'My mother'" — Here the word describes a young child before reaching early speaking age.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The word na'ar bridges the story of Hannah's personal family sorrow with the larger history of Israel. Samuel's childhood dedication in Shiloh prepared him for a lifetime of leadership as a prophet, priest, and judge. The child who was granted in response to a mother's private prayer became the man God used to guide Israel through a crucial transition in its history.
