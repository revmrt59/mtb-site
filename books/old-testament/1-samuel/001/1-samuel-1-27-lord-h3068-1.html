LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

The word is the unique personal name of the God of Israel, traditionally rendered as "LORD" (in small capital letters) in English translations like the NKJV. It distinguishes the personal, covenant-making God who reveals Himself to His people from general titles for deity. In 1 Samuel 1:27, the name highlights that Hannah's long-awaited child was neither an accident of nature nor a gift from a distant, unknown god, but the direct response of Israel's faithful God to her specific prayer.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The opening chapter of 1 Samuel takes place during a dark and chaotic era in Israel's history near the end of the period of the judges. Worship centered at the tabernacle in Shiloh, where spiritual leadership was weak and compromised. In the midst of this national decline, the narrative focuses on a humble family from the hill country of Ephraim. Hannah, one of the two wives of Elkanah, suffered deep grief because she was childless and tormented by her rival, Peninnah.

During an annual visit to Shiloh, Hannah poured out her soul in silent prayer before the Lord, asking for a male child and promising to dedicate him to divine service for his entire life. When the priest Eli observed her moving her lips without making a sound, he initially mistook her for a drunkard. Upon realizing her genuine distress, he pronounced a blessing over her. Hannah returned home, conceived, and gave birth to a son, naming him Samuel. Once the boy was weaned, she brought him back to Shiloh to fulfill her vow, speaking directly to Eli and identifying her son as the answer to her prayer.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name Yahweh is a Hebrew proper noun that serves as the personal name of God. In standard English translations, printer conventions use small capital letters ("LORD") to distinguish this personal name from common nouns or titles meaning master or ruler, such as Adonai.

While generic terms for deity refer broadly to divine power or majesty, a proper noun identifies a specific personal identity. In biblical usage, Yahweh is the revealed name by which God entered into relationship with Israel. The name identifies Him as the living God who acts, speaks, hears, and remains faithful to His declarations.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, the name Yahweh appears when God interacts directly with human history and His chosen people. The term carries distinct nuances depending on the context:

  • Personal Accessibility: Unlike abstract deities or localized idols, Yahweh makes Himself known to individuals and invites them to call upon Him by name.

  • Faithful Commitment: The name is closely tied to God's promises and His ongoing commitment to preserve and guide His people.

  • Active Intervention: Yahweh is presented not as a passive observer, but as the active Sovereign who directs human affairs, opens and closes the womb, and fulfills human petitions according to His will.

While terms like Elohim emphasize God's role as Creator and ruler over the nations, Yahweh highlights His close, relational involvement with Israel and individual worshippers.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's use of the personal name of God carries significant theological depth. It shows that the God of Israel is attentive to the personal sorrows of an individual woman, even during a period of widespread national spiritual failure. Divine sovereignty and personal compassion meet in this single verse.

Furthermore, the gift of Samuel demonstrates how God fulfills individual prayers to accomplish broad historical purposes. Samuel would grow up to serve as a judge, priest, and prophet who would anoint Israel's first kings. By attributing Samuel's birth directly to Yahweh, the passage reveals that God builds His kingdom through His sovereign answers to humble prayer.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:27, Hannah stands before Eli holding the young boy Samuel and declares, "For this child I prayed, and the LORD has granted me my petition which I asked of Him." Her choice of words is deliberate and precise. She does not attribute her pregnancy to generic good fortune, natural fertility, or a vague divine power. She names Yahweh directly.

The sentence structure links Hannah's past act of prayer with Yahweh's direct response. The phrase highlights a clear cause and outcome: Hannah asked, and Yahweh granted. The immediate context reinforces this connection through wordplay, as the name Samuel sounds like the Hebrew expression for "asked of God." Because Yahweh gave the child in response to her petition, Hannah willingly hands Samuel over to tabernacle service, recognizing that the boy ultimately belongs to the God who granted him.

  • 2 Samuel 22:2 – "The Lord is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In 1 Samuel 1:27, the word functions grammatically as a proper noun, serving as the explicit subject of the action: Yahweh is the one who granted the request.

Hannah's personal acknowledgment in verse 27 sets the foundation for her celebrated song of praise in 1 Samuel 2. There, she broadens her testimony from her private experience to a general proclamation of Yahweh's character. She praises Yahweh as the holy God who opposes the proud, lifts up the barren and needy, and guards the feet of His faithful saints. Her private experience of answered prayer becomes a prophetic witness to how Yahweh governs the world.
