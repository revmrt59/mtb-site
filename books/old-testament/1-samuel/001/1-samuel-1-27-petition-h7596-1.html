PETITION - (shᵉʼêlâh, Strong's H7596)

Summary:

She'elah means a request, petition, or thing asked for. It refers to a specific ask brought before an authority figure or before God in prayer. In 1 Samuel 1:27, the word describes the precise child Hannah desperately sought from the LORD, showing that God specifically heard and answered her personal plea.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word she'elah comes from a common Hebrew root that means to ask, inquire, or request. In ancient Israelite life, making a formal request to a king, leader, or parent was a standard part of social interaction. When brought before God, a she'elah was an explicit, personal plea offered in worship and prayer.

In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah visited the sanctuary at Shiloh in deep grief because she was childless and ridiculed by her rival. In her anguish, she prayed silently, moving her lips without making a sound, asking God to remember her and give her a male child. Years later, after Samuel was born and weaned, Hannah returned to Shiloh to dedicate him to the LORD, presenting him to the priest Eli with the words, "For this child I prayed, and the LORD has granted me my petition which I asked of Him."

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

She'elah refers to a request made to a human ruler, an earnest desire expressed in prayer to God, or the actual thing granted as a result of that ask.

When someone makes a she'elah, they present a clear wish to an authority who has the power to grant or deny it. The word itself does not automatically guarantee a favorable answer; rather, it identifies the specific matter being asked. In some passages, the noun also refers to the concrete gift received once the request is granted, as seen when Hannah presents Samuel as the very answer to her prayer.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, she'elah appears in both royal court settings and personal prayer.

In civic settings, royal subjects or queens present a she'elah to a king. For example, Queen Esther repeatedly brings her "petition" before King Ahasuerus to save her people. In those settings, the word carries the sense of a formal request brought before higher authority.

In spiritual settings, a she'elah is brought directly to God. When believers bring their requests to God, the word highlights their dependence on Him. People ask for deliverance, relief from suffering, or daily provisions. In Psalm 106, the word is used for Israel's insistent demand for food in the wilderness, showing that a request can be motivated by genuine faith or by selfish impatience.

Grammatically, the target word in 1 Samuel 1:27 is a feminine singular noun, identifying the single, focused plea Hannah made for a child.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's use of she'elah highlights several key truths about prayer and God's character:

First, God hears personal, specific prayers. Hannah did not pray a vague prayer; she asked God specifically for a son. God's response shows that He pays attention to individual pain and hears specific requests.

Second, bringing a petition acknowledges God's sovereignty. Presenting a request to God recognizes that He alone possesses the authority and power to provide what human effort cannot secure.

Third, answered petitions lead to dedicated worship. When God granted Hannah's request, she did not treat the child as merely her own possession. Instead, she brought Samuel back to the sanctuary to fulfill her vow, recognizing that the gift came from God and belonged to Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:27, Hannah speaks to Eli the priest while presenting young Samuel at Shiloh. Earlier in the story, Eli had mistaken her silent, weeping prayer for drunkenness. Once he realized her true distress, Eli said, "Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition which you have asked of Him" (1 Samuel 1:17).

Now, in verse 27, Hannah reminds Eli of that moment. By repeating the word she'elah, she forms a direct link between her past weeping, Eli's blessing, and the living child in her arms. Hannah uses a double expression here: "the LORD has granted me my petition (she'elah) which I asked of Him." This phrasing underscores that Samuel is the concrete, tangible answer to her specific prayer.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Samuel 1:17 — "Then Eli answered and said, 'Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition which you have asked of Him.'"

  • 1 Kings 2:16 — "Now I make one petition of you; do not deny me."

  • Esther 5:6 — "At the banquet of wine the king said to Esther, 'What is your petition? It shall be granted you.'"

  • Esther 7:3 — "Then Queen Esther answered and said, 'If I have found favor in your sight, O king, and if it pleases the king, let my life be given me at my petition, and my people at my request.'"

  • Psalms 106:15 — "And He gave them their request, but sent leanness into their soul."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The name Samuel (Shemu'el) sounds similar to the Hebrew root for asking, creating a memorable connection throughout the early chapters of 1 Samuel. In 1 Samuel 1:28, Hannah goes on to say that Samuel is "given to the LORD," using another form derived from the same root. This highlights how every stage of Samuel's life—from Hannah's earnest prayer to his dedicated service in the sanctuary—is intertwined with asking God and receiving from Him.
