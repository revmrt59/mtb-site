PRAYED - (pâlal, Strong's H6419)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb palal means to pray, ask, make intercession, or seek divine intervention. It describes coming before God to present a petition or entreat Him to act. In 1 Samuel 1:27, Hannah uses the word to identify her child Samuel as the direct answer to her past petition at the tabernacle. By saying, "For this child I prayed," she testifies that the child in her arms is the tangible result of her prayer to the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The narrative of 1 Samuel 1 begins in a time of personal grief for Hannah. She was childless and faced continual provocation from her rival Peninnah. During an annual pilgrimage to the sanctuary at Shiloh, Hannah went to the tabernacle in deep sorrow. There she poured out her soul to God, asking Him to remember her and grant her a male child.

In 1 Samuel 1:27, years have passed. God answered her request, Samuel was born and weaned, and Hannah returned to Shiloh to fulfill her vow. Presenting the young child to Eli the priest, she points to Samuel and uses palal to remind Eli of the specific prayer she offered years earlier in that very place.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Palal is the standard word in Scripture for offering prayer to God. The word can mean:

  • To petition God or ask Him for help.

  • To make intercession on behalf of another person or nation.

  • To entreat God's favor or seek His judgment and intervention in a difficult situation.

When a person prays using palal, they are approaching God as the supreme judge and sovereign ruler who has the power to intervene, grant mercy, and change circumstances.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, palal describes a wide range of prayerful speech. It is used when individuals pray in personal distress, when prophets and leaders intercede for the nation, and when God's people gather at the temple to seek His favor.

The word does not refer to mechanical repetition or ritualistic chanting. Instead, it describes an intentional act of turning to God, laying a specific need before Him, and placing full confidence in His power and willingness to respond.

4. Theological Implications

The use of palal in Scripture highlights several basic truths about God and prayer:

  • God is accessible: Worshippers can approach the Sovereign Lord with their deepest sorrows and explicit desires.

  • God hears and answers: Hannah's story proves that God pays attention to the prayers of the lowly and afflicted.

  • Prayer aligns with God's purpose: Hannah's personal petition for a son fit into God's broader plan to provide a faithful prophet and judge for Israel.

Prayer in Scripture is an act of humble dependence, acknowledging that help comes from God alone.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:27, Hannah says, "For this child I prayed, and the LORD has granted me my petition which I asked of Him."

The grammatical form of the verb here is singular, common in gender, and perfect in form. This reflects a completed action in past time. Hannah is looking back to the moment at the tabernacle when she brought her sorrow to God. The past act of praying has now yielded a present, visible result.

This creates a powerful connection between her past request and her present worship. Samuel was not an accidental blessing or merely a natural turn of events; he was the direct gift of God given in answer to Hannah's prayer. By making this connection clear to Eli, Hannah demonstrates her integrity in fulfilling her promise to dedicate the child back to the Lord for His service.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Samuel 1:10 — "And she was in bitterness of soul, and prayed to the LORD and wept sore."

  • 1 Kings 8:29 — Solomon asks God to hear "the prayer which Your servant makes toward this place," pointing to the temple as a house of prayer for the nation.

  • 2 Kings 19:15 — "Then Hezekiah prayed before the LORD" when Jerusalem faced destruction by Assyria, seeking divine deliverance for God's city and people.

  • Psalms 106:30 — "Then Phinehas stood up and intervened, and the plague was stopped," showing the word used for mediating or taking action to make intercession before God.

  • Job 42:10 — "And the LORD restored Job’s losses when he prayed for his friends," demonstrating the power of intercessory prayer for others.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's use of palal highlights the relationship between prayer and stewardship. She asked God for a child, received the answer to her prayer, and then willingly returned the child to the Lord's service. Her action shows that the answers God gives to prayer are meant to be held with open hands and used for His purposes.
