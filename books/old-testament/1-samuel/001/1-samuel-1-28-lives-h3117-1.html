LIVES - (yôwm, Strong's H3117)

Summary:

Yom fundamentally means a day, a specific period of time, or a duration of human life. In 1 Samuel 1:28, the word appears in a masculine plural form as part of a temporal expression rendered "as long as he lives" (literally, "all the days"). In this context, Hannah uses the word to express the complete, lifelong dedication of her son Samuel to the service of the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Hebrew, yom is the primary word used to measure and describe time. Depending on the sentence, it can refer to a twenty-four-hour period, daylight as distinct from night, a specific point in history, or a person's entire lifetime when paired with terms meaning "all." In 1 Samuel 1, Hannah suffered years of grief due to childlessness and bitter provocation. After praying in deep distress at the tabernacle in Shiloh, God granted her petition, and she gave birth to Samuel. Hannah had vowed that if God gave her a son, she would return him to the Lord for his whole life. In 1 Samuel 1:28, Hannah fulfills her vow, bringing young Samuel to Eli the priest and declaring that his entire earthly existence is dedicated to God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Yom can mean a single calendar day, daylight hours, or an extended period of time. When used in the plural, it often expands to describe a season, an era, or a person's full lifespan.

In 1 Samuel 1:28, the word is grammatically masculine and plural. Within the context of Hannah's statement, it functions as part of a phrase meaning "all the days that he lives." Rather than referring to a single twenty-four-hour day, the word refers to the full duration of Samuel's life on earth.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses yom in several standard ways:

  • A literal day: Marking basic units of time, such as the individual days of creation or weekly sabbaths.

  • A person's lifespan: When combined with "all," the plural form means "throughout one's life" or "as long as someone lives."

  • A historical period: Describing a time of ruling, a reign, or a specific era in Israel's history.

  • An appointed divine time: Referring to moments of divine judgment or salvation, such as the "day of the Lord."

In 1 Samuel 1:28, the emphasis is clearly on total duration. Hannah does not present Samuel for short-term service or a temporary commitment. She surrenders his entire lifetime to God.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's dedication of Samuel highlights key biblical truths:

  • God's Ownership of Life: Everything believers receive from God belongs to Him. Hannah acknowledged that Samuel was a gift from God, and she freely gave him back to the Lord's service.

  • Lifelong Devotion: True commitment to God is not a short-term phase or occasional practice. Samuel's life was consecrated day by day, for all his days.

  • God's Faithfulness and Human Vows: Hannah made a promise to God in her distress, and when God answered her prayer, she remained faithful to her word. Dedicating Samuel for his entire life reflected her deep gratitude and integrity before God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:28, Hannah tells Eli, "Therefore I also have lent him to the LORD; as long as he lives he shall be lent to the LORD." The word translated "lent" carries the idea of giving or surrendering back to God what was requested from Him in prayer.

The immediate setting contrasts Hannah's previous years of desperate petitioning with her immediate, complete surrender of the boy once he was weaned. The plural form yamim ("days") emphasizes every single day Samuel would draw breath. Hannah was not placing Samuel in the tabernacle for temporary training or basic education; she was yielding his whole future to God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 1:5 — "God called the light Day, and the darkness He called Night."

  • Deuteronomy 16:8 — "Six days you shall eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day there shall be a sacred assembly to the LORD your God."

  • 1 Chronicles 22:9 — "I will give peace and quietness to Israel in his days."

  • Job 10:20 — "Are not my days few? Cease! Leave me alone, that I may take a little comfort."

  • Isaiah 61:2 — "To proclaim the acceptable year of the LORD, and the day of vengeance of our God."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The expression "all the days" in 1 Samuel 1:28 is significant for the broader story of Israel. During a period marked by spiritual decay and corrupt priesthood under Eli's sons, God raised up Samuel as a lifelong leader. Samuel served as prophet, priest, and judge from his childhood until his death. Hannah's willing surrender of her son's entire lifetime became the means through which God brought spiritual renewal to the entire nation.
