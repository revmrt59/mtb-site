LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

The word is the personal, revealed name of the God of Israel, identifying Him as the self-existent, living God who faithfully keeps His promises. In 1 Samuel 1:28, Hannah uses this personal divine name to declare that she is surrendering her young son Samuel entirely to the LORD, fulfilling the solemn vow she made when praying for a child.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Hannah lived during a spiritually dark and turbulent era in Israel's history, when the priesthood at Shiloh was corrupt and faithful worship was rare. In her severe distress over being childless, Hannah traveled to the tabernacle to pour out her soul before God. Rather than turning to idols or relying on human solutions, she addressed the living God of Israel directly by His personal name.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word is the personal proper name of the God of Israel, traditionally rendered as "LORD" in capital letters in English translations. The name is closely connected to the Hebrew verb meaning "to be" or "to exist," pointing to God as the eternal, self-existent One who causes all things to exist. Unlike general terms for deity that highlight divine power, this name identifies God in His personal, faithful self-revelation to His people.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, this personal name appears whenever God reveals His character, enters into commitment with His people, or intervenes in human history. It is the name God made known when He redeemed Israel from bondage and established His covenant with them. Calling upon this name signifies trust in the living God who hears prayers, acts in history, and remains faithful to His spoken word.

4. Theological Implications

The divine name reminds readers that God is not an impersonal force, but a living, sovereign Lord who relates personally to individuals. The name itself does not act as a magical formula; rather, it reveals God's self-existent nature and absolute trustworthiness. Hannah’s surrender of Samuel shows a foundational theological truth: every good gift comes from the LORD, and returning those blessings to Him in service is the heart of true worship.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:28, Hannah brings the young child Samuel to Shiloh after he is weaned and presents him to Eli the priest. She recalls her former distress and reminds Eli that she prayed specifically to the LORD for a son. Hannah then uses the name LORD twice in her declaration: "I also have lent him to the LORD; as long as he lives he shall be lent to the LORD." The English word "lent" translates a Hebrew term closely related to the verb "to ask." Because Hannah asked the LORD for a child, she now gives him back as one dedicated entirely to the LORD's service. The verse concludes with glad worship, highlighting that Hannah's yield of her son was not a painful loss, but a joyful act of devotion to the God who answered her prayer.

  • Exodus 13:5 — "And it shall be, when the LORD brings you into the land of the Canaanites..."

  • 2 Samuel 22:2 — "And he said: 'The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer...'"

  • Psalms 25:1 — "To You, O LORD, I lift up my soul."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's dedication of Samuel to the LORD marked a vital turning point for the entire nation of Israel. At a time when spiritual leadership was falling apart, God used Samuel to establish prophetic leadership and guide the nation back to faithful worship. Hannah's private, faithful surrender of her son to the LORD bore long-term fruit that blessed generations beyond her own lifetime.
