CITY - (ʻîyr, Strong's H5892)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun 'ir refers to a city, town, or settled human community, ranging from small regional villages to large fortified strongholds. Unlike temporary tent encampments, a city represents permanent settlement, local social life, and daily civil routine.

In 1 Samuel 1:3, the word refers to Ramathaim Zophim (Ramah), the local home town of Elkanah and his family. Its inclusion establishes the departure point for Elkanah's annual pilgrimage, showing a faithful family intentionally leaving their home city and daily life behind to go up to Shiloh and worship the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew word 'ir is the primary Old Testament term for an inhabited town or city. In ancient Israel, a city was defined less by its population size and more by its permanent dwellings, municipal structure, and often its surrounding walls or defenses that provided protection for local residents. It stood in contrast to nomadic campsites, open fields, and temporary shelters.

In 1 Samuel 1:3, the word sets the scene for the family of Elkanah during the turbulent period of the Judges. Israel as a nation experienced widespread spiritual drift and political instability, yet within Elkanah's home city of Ramah, true personal faith was still practiced. The narrative opens by anchoring this family in their physical home town before describing their regular spiritual journey to God's sanctuary.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

'Ir refers directly to a settled human habitational area. The term can mean:

  • A small local town or village community.

  • A fortified city with walls, gates, and towers.

  • A capital city or seat of political authority.

In 1 Samuel 1:3, the word means Elkanah's home municipality. It represents the place where his household lived, worked, and conducted their daily affairs.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, 'ir is used to describe human settlements across all eras of biblical history. It can refer to early human construction, localized regional centers, or holy sites chosen by God.

The word carries several nuances depending on its setting:

  • Civil and Social Life: A city was the hub of family life, commerce, local elders, and civil administration.

  • Security and Refuge: Cities often provided physical protection behind walls against roving invaders.

  • Regional Identity: People were often identified by the city from which they originated.

When Scripture mentions someone traveling "from his city" to worship, it highlights a deliberate transition from normal municipal routine into dedicated sacred activity.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of 'ir in 1 Samuel 1:3 provides several quiet theological truths:

  • Faithfulness in Ordinary Places: God works His redemptive plan through real families living in ordinary home towns. Elkanah did not live in a spiritual isolation camp; he lived in a local city, yet kept his family devoted to the Lord.

  • Worship Beyond Local Routine: While daily life occurred within his city, Elkanah recognized that local community was not the ultimate focal point of his devotion. True worship required journeying to the place where God had placed His name.

  • God's Sovereign Preparation: The small home city of Ramah became the quiet incubator for Samuel, the prophet whom God would raise up to bring spiritual renewal to the entire nation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:3, the phrase "went up from his city yearly" sets up a clear contrast between two locations: Elkanah's local town of residence (Ramah) and the central place of corporate worship (Shiloh).

During the era of the Judges, central worship at Shiloh was often neglected or corrupted, as indicated by the presence of Eli's wicked sons, Hophni and Phinehas. Yet Elkanah remained committed to covenant obedience. Mentioning "his city" highlights that Elkanah's worship was regular, disciplined, and family-oriented. He did not let the routine demands of town life or the spiritual decline of the wider culture stop him from making his annual pilgrimage.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 4:17 – "And he built a city, and called the name of the city after the name of his son—Enoch." Here the word refers to the earliest recorded permanent human settlement, illustrating humanity's desire for localized community and defense.

  • 1 Samuel 4:13 – "Now when the man came into the city and told it, all the city cried out." Later in 1 Samuel, the word describes Shiloh as news of the captured Ark arrives, showing how a town reacted corporately to tragic spiritual news.

  • Psalms 87:3 – "Glorious things are spoken of you, O city of God!" Here the word describes Jerusalem, contrasting regular earthly towns with the unique city chosen as the site of God's special presence.

  • Isaiah 37:33 – "He shall not come into this city, nor shoot an arrow there..." Here the word refers to Jerusalem under divine protection, demonstrating God's defense of His people living within their urban walls.

7. Other Relevant Insights

It is noteworthy that Ramah, the very "city" Elkanah leaves in 1 Samuel 1:3, later becomes the lifelong ministry base for his son Samuel. First Samuel 7:17 records that after circuiting Israel as a judge, Samuel always returned to Ramah, where he built an altar to the Lord. The quiet home town of a faithful father eventually became the central administrative and spiritual hub for one of Israel's greatest prophets.
