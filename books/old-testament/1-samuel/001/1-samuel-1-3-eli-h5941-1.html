ELI - (ʻÊlîy, Strong's H5941)

Summary:

The name Eli is a Hebrew proper masculine noun meaning "exalted" or "high." In the Old Testament, it belongs exclusively to the High Priest and judge who presided over the central sanctuary at Shiloh during the final years of the period of the judges. In 1 Samuel 1:3, the name identifies the head of the priestly family at Shiloh, framing the spiritual backdrop of Israel's sanctuary and introducing his corrupt sons whose failure prompts God's intervention through the birth and calling of Samuel.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Eli was the High Priest and judge of Israel stationed at Shiloh, where the tabernacle and the ark of the covenant resided prior to the monarchy. During this era, Shiloh served as the central place of pilgrimage for Israelite worship. The narrative of 1 Samuel opens at a time when Israel suffered from deep spiritual decay, moral instability, and compromised leadership.

As a descendant of Aaron through the line of Ithamar, Eli held the highest sacred office in the nation. His leadership represented the old order of judges and priests. The mention of his name at the beginning of 1 Samuel connects the reader to the sanctuary's ongoing ministry while signaling an impending transition in Israel's history from the disorder of the judges to the prophetic and royal era.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The proper name Eli comes from a Hebrew root associated with height, elevation, or going up. It can mean "exalted," "high," or "lofty."

As a proper personal name, the word refers specifically to this individual historical figure. It does not carry additional hidden meanings in its grammar or letters. While the root idea reflects height or distinction, the name functions in the biblical text as the personal identifier of Shiloh's High Priest.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the early chapters of 1 Samuel, the name Eli describes a leader of notable personal piety who nevertheless suffered from severe leadership and parental failures. Eli was not an idolater or an outright apostate. He recognized Yahweh's authority, blessed worshippers, guided young Samuel to hear God's voice, and accepted divine correction with solemn resignation.

However, Scripture repeatedly links Eli's name to his household's corruption. Although he personally honored God's sanctuary, he failed to discipline or restrain his sons, who abused their sacred office. Consequently, Eli's name becomes associated with both the dignity of the priesthood and the tragic consequences of passive oversight in spiritual matters.

4. Theological Implications

The narrative centered on Eli underscores several core biblical truths regarding leadership, holy offices, and spiritual responsibility:

  • Sacred titles do not shield leaders from accountability. Holding an exalted position like High Priest did not excuse Eli when he tolerated wickedness within his household and ministry.

  • Spiritual neglect carries generational consequences. Eli honored his sons above God by allowing them to dishonor the sacrifices of the sanctuary, leading to the collapse of his family's line.

  • God preserves His work despite human failure. When Eli's house fell into spiritual decline, God did not abandon His people. Instead, He raised up a faithful servant, Samuel, to herald a new era of spiritual renewal.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:3, the name Eli appears in a verse describing Elkanah's annual pilgrimage to Shiloh: "And the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, the priests of the LORD, were there."

Here, Eli is introduced as the patriarch and High Priest whose administrative oversight covers the sanctuary. The verse places his name in direct association with his two sons, immediately setting up a tension in the narrative. While worshippers like Elkanah and Hannah came to Shiloh seeking God in sincere devotion, the priesthood under Eli's patriarchate was already tainted by his sons' self-serving behavior.

The mention of Eli in verse 3 establishes the institutional setting into which Hannah prays for a child. It contrasts the widespread spiritual decline under Eli's household with the Quiet faith of Hannah, whose son Samuel would eventually replace Eli's line.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Samuel 1:9 — "So Hannah arose after they had finished eating and drinking in Shiloh. Now Eli the priest was sitting on the seat by the doorpost of the tabernacle of the LORD."

  • 1 Samuel 1:17 — "Then Eli answered and said, 'Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition which you have asked of Him.'"

  • 1 Samuel 2:12 — "Now the sons of Eli were corrupt; they did not know the LORD."

  • 1 Samuel 3:1 — "Now the boy Samuel ministered to the LORD before Eli. And the word of the LORD was rare in those days; there was no widespread revelation."

  • 1 Samuel 3:8 — "And the LORD called Samuel again the third time. So he arose and went to Eli, and said, 'Here I am, for you did call me.' Then Eli perceived that the LORD had called the boy."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Eli's spiritual perception remained partially intact even in his physical and administrative decline. When young Samuel heard a voice calling in the night, Eli recognized that the Lord was speaking and instructed Samuel how to respond.

  • When Samuel delivered God's severe message of judgment regarding Eli's house, Eli responded with humble submission: "It is the LORD. Let Him do what seems good to Him" (1 Samuel 3:18). This reveals a heart that feared God personally, even while bearing the tragic consequences of lax leadership.

  • Eli's death in 1 Samuel 4:18 occurred not merely upon hearing that his sons had died in battle, but specifically when he heard that the ark of God had been captured, demonstrating that God's glory remained his ultimate concern.
