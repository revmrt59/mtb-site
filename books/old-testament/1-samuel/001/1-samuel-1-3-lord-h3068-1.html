LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

The word is the personal, proper name of the God of Israel, traditionally rendered in English Bibles as "LORD" in small capital letters to distinguish it from a general title for a master or ruler. In 1 Samuel 1:3, the name identifies the personal God whom Elkanah and his family traveled to Shiloh to worship. By naming God as the LORD, the text shows that Israel's faithful worship was offered to the living God who revealed Himself to their ancestors, setting the stage for Him to hear Hannah's prayer and raise up a new prophet for the nation.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The name YHWH is the unique, personal name of God in the Hebrew Scriptures. Unlike general words for divinity, such as Elohim (God), this name specifically identifies the personal God who revealed Himself to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and later to Moses at the burning bush.

The setting of 1 Samuel 1:3 occurs during the period of the judges, a time marked by moral breakdown and spiritual confusion in Israel. Worship at this time centered at Shiloh, where the tabernacle rested. In the midst of widespread national decay, Elkanah faithfully traveled every year to worship at the sanctuary. The text records that he went up to worship the LORD, demonstrating that despite the corruption around him, faithful Israelites continued to seek the true God of the covenant.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word functions as a proper noun and is the personal name of God. In ancient Israel, personal names were meaningful designations that expressed identity and relationship. The name YHWH distinguishes the God of Israel from all false gods and idols worshipped by surrounding nations.

The name is historically linked to God's self-revelation to Moses in Exodus, where God declared "I AM WHO I AM." It presents God as the self-existent, living God who acts in human history. While the broader story of Scripture reveals God's faithfulness, mercy, power, and justice, the name itself serves first and foremost as His proper personal identifier.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, the name YHWH appears in contexts of prayer, sacrifice, covenant making, and national praise. It is the name God's people call upon when seeking help, offering thanks, or making vows.

In 1 Samuel 1:3, the name appears as part of a sacred title associated with the sanctuary at Shiloh. Using this name grounds the narrative in reality: the events of Hannah's life and the birth of Samuel did not take place in a generic religious setting, but in direct relationship with the living God of Israel. The passage also notes that Hophni and Phinehas were priests of the LORD, creating a stark contrast between the holy name of God and the wicked conduct of those who served at His altar.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of God's personal name reminds readers that biblical faith is built on personal revelation. God is not an impersonal force or an unreachable deity. He makes Himself known to people and invites them to address Him by name.

In the narrative of 1 Samuel, the name of the LORD brings hope during a dark period of national history. Leaders and priests may fail, but the LORD remains seated on His throne. He is the one who hears the quiet, desperate prayers of suffering individuals like Hannah and brings about redemption for His people.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:3, the personal name of the LORD is introduced at the beginning of the book to set the spiritual tone of the entire narrative. Elkanah's yearly journey to Shiloh highlights his personal commitment to worship the LORD despite the corrupt priesthood led by Eli's sons.

This setting prepares the reader for Hannah's story in the verses that follow. Hannah was barren and deeply grieved, yet she brought her pain directly to the sanctuary of the LORD. She did not pray to a vague power; she poured out her soul before the LORD. The use of God's personal name in verse 3 establishes that the sanctuary was the place where the true God met His people, paving the way for God's gracious answer to Hannah's prayer.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 — "Lord" — Introduces the personal name of God alongside His work as Creator, connecting the God of creation directly with the God who forms a personal relationship with humanity.

  • Joshua 14:12 — "Lord" — Caleb bases his request for the hill country on what the Lord promised years earlier, showing complete trust in God's spoken word.

  • Psalms 25:1 — "Lord" — David addresses his prayer directly to the Lord, expressing wholehearted trust and surrender in times of trouble.

  • Jeremiah 33:1 — "Lord" — Highlights the Lord actively speaking His word to the prophet even while Jeremiah was confined in prison.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Grammatically, this word is classified as a proper noun (proper name). Because it is a proper name, it directly identifies the God of Israel rather than serving as a descriptive title like "king" or "master."

Out of deep reverence for the sacred name, ancient Jewish traditions began reading the word Adonai (Lord) aloud whenever the text contained YHWH. Translators reflected this reverence by printing the name as "LORD" in capital letters in most English Bibles. In 1 Samuel, this holy name stands at the center of the transition from the chaotic period of the judges to the establishment of the kingdom under Samuel, Saul, and David.
