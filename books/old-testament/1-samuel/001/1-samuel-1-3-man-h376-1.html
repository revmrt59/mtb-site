MAN - (ʼîysh, Strong's H376)

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'ish refers primarily to an individual adult male, a husband, or a person in distinction from God or nature. In 1 Samuel 1:3, it introduces Elkanah as an individual man whose regular, faithful worship at Shiloh sets the narrative stage for God's work in his family and in the nation of Israel.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word 'ish is one of the most common terms in Hebrew for an individual man or husband. It appears throughout the Old Testament from creation onward, capturing the individual person in various social, familial, and moral settings.

The historical setting of 1 Samuel begins near the end of the period of the judges, a time characterized by spiritual spiritual drift and moral instability. Introducing Elkanah simply as "this man" places an ordinary individual at the start of a major turning point in Israel's history. While the surrounding culture was marked by instability, this individual household maintained a pattern of seeking the LORD.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to an adult male human being, a husband, a male leader, or an individual representative. It can distinguish a male from a female, a husband from his wife, or a mortal human being from God.

In broader contexts, it is also used distributively to mean "each one," "everyone," or "a certain person." Depending on the sentence, it emphasizes an individual's personal identity, social standing, or specific role within a family or community.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses 'ish in several distinct ways:

  • An Adult Male: It identifies a specific male individual in distinction from women or children.

  • A Husband: When set alongside a wife or household, it carries the sense of a married man who holds headship within his family.

  • An Individual or "Each One": In narrative contexts, it can highlight individual human accountability or single out a person from a larger group.

While another Hebrew word ('adam) often refers to humanity collectively, 'ish frequently highlights individual personality, distinct roles, or specific personal action.

4. Theological Implications

The focus on 'ish in salvation history demonstrates that God carries out His redemptive purposes through individual human lives and families. God does not deal only with large nations or abstract movements; He sees, calls, and works through specific individuals.

In 1 Samuel, the spiritual state of the nation was low, yet God's plan to raise up a prophet and establish a kingdom began with an ordinary man and his family making faithful pilgrimages to worship God. This highlights that individual obedience and domestic devotion are often the quiet soil in which God plants large spiritual renewals.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:3, "this man" refers directly to Elkanah. The passage records that he went up from his city year by year to worship and sacrifice to the LORD of hosts at Shiloh.

The immediate context sets up a notable contrast. In the very same verse, the text mentions Hophni and Phinehas, the corrupt sons of Eli, who served as priests at Shiloh. While spiritual leadership at the tabernacle was compromised, this individual man consistently brought his family to worship God according to the Law. The word underlines Elkanah's personal responsibility and ongoing devotion, forming the quiet backdrop for Hannah's heartfelt prayer and the eventual birth of Samuel.

7. Other Relevant Insights

From a grammatical perspective, the noun in 1 Samuel 1:3 is singular with common gender classification in this form, pointing specifically to Elkanah as one distinct individual taking responsibility for his household.

It is also worth noting how 'ish functions within the larger story of 1 Samuel. While Elkanah is an ordinary man ('ish), his son Samuel will grow to be a prophetic leader, and David will later be sought out as a "man ('ish) after God's own heart." The story moves from an ordinary man faithfully bringing his family to Shiloh to God raising up leaders to guide His people.
