PRIESTS - (kôhên, Strong's H3548)

Summary:

The Hebrew word kohen refers to a priest—an appointed official who serves before God on behalf of the people by offering sacrifices, tending the sanctuary, and directing worship. In 1 Samuel 1:3, kohen identifies Hophni and Phinehas, the sons of Eli, as the official ministers serving at Shiloh. Their sacred title creates a tragic contrast between their holy office and their corrupt lives, highlighting Israel's deep spiritual decline at the end of the period of the judges.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The noun kohen is the standard Hebrew term for a priest. In ancient Israel, the priesthood was established by God to serve at His sanctuary. The priests were set apart from the line of Aaron within the tribe of Levi to care for the tabernacle, present the offerings on the altar, and represent the nation before God.

In 1 Samuel 1:3, the word appears in the historical context of the tabernacle at Shiloh. During this time, before Israel had a king, Shiloh served as the central location where families gathered annually to worship and offer sacrifices to the Lord.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Kohen refers to a sacred minister or priest. The word describes a mediator who stands between God and human worshippers.

When applied to Israel's worship, the word carries several clear duties:

  • Presenting animal offerings, incense, and blood sacrifices on the altar.

  • Guarding the purity and holiness of the sanctuary.

  • Declaring God's blessing over the congregation.

  • Teaching God's statutes and discerning His will for the community.

Although Scripture occasionally uses kohen for non-Israelite priests, its main focus throughout the Old Testament is the Aaronide priest who ministers at God's sanctuary.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, kohen identifies the office charged with maintaining access to God through sacrificial worship. The priest acted as the sanctuary guardian and the people's representative. Without a priest, the worshipper could not present a sacrifice according to the Law of Moses.

However, holding the title of kohen did not automatically guarantee personal righteousness. Scripture records both faithful priests who brought spiritual renewal to Israel and unfaithful priests who corrupted God's worship. The title describes an office and a God-given role, but the moral integrity of the individual minister determined whether that service honored God or brought divine discipline.

4. Theological Implications

The office of kohen shows that sinful human beings cannot approach a holy God without an appointed mediator and an acceptable sacrifice. God established the priesthood so that Israel could live in fellowship with Him while preserving the boundary of His holiness.

The shortcomings of human priests throughout Israel's history highlight the need for a better, permanently righteous mediator. Human priests were flawed, subject to temptation, and limited by death. These Old Testament priests point forward to Jesus Christ, the ultimate High Priest who offered Himself as a perfect sacrifice to open permanent access to God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:3, the term appears in masculine plural form, identifying Hophni and Phinehas together as "priests of the LORD." The verse introduces them while describing Elkanah's annual journey to Shiloh to worship God.

The passage creates a striking contrast. Elkanah is depicted as a devout worshipper faithfully fulfilling his religious obligations. In contrast, Hophni and Phinehas hold the sacred title of "priests of the LORD," yet the surrounding narrative reveals that they abused their authority, stole portions of the sacrifices, and treated the Lord's offering with contempt. Mentioning their title early in the story exposes the tragic state of Israel's sanctuary leadership and establishes the need for God to raise up Samuel as a faithful priest and prophet.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 14:18 – "Then Melchizedek king of Salem brought out bread and wine; he was the priest of God Most High."

  • Leviticus 7:7 – "The trespass offering is like the sin offering; there is one law for them: the priest who makes atonement with it shall have it."

  • Joshua 4:16 – "Command the priests who bear the ark of the Testimony to come up from the Jordan."

  • 2 Chronicles 29:4 – "Then he brought in the priests and the Levites, and gathered them in the east square,"

  • Jeremiah 26:8 – "Now it happened, when Jeremiah had made an end of speaking all that the LORD had commanded him to speak to all the people, that the priests and the prophets and all the people seized him, saying, 'You will surely die!'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The presence of two priests serving together at Shiloh reflects the practical division of duties necessary to manage the heavy volume of sacrifices brought by visiting pilgrims.

  • The contrast between Elkanah's humble devotion and the corruption of Eli's sons shows that God preserved genuine faith among ordinary people even during times of widespread spiritual failure among spiritual leaders.

  • The downfall of Hophni and Phinehas demonstrates that holding a high religious office provides no protection against divine judgment when a leader dishonors God's sanctuary and misleads His people.
