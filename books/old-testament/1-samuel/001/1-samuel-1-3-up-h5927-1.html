UP - (ʻâlâh, Strong's H5927)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb ‘alah fundamentally means to ascend, climb, or move upward from a lower place to a higher one. In 1 Samuel 1:3, it describes Elkanah’s regular journey traveling upward to the sanctuary at Shiloh. The word emphasizes both the physical elevation of the trip to Shiloh and the intentional act of going up to meet God in worship.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The action of "going up" (‘alah) carries strong geographical and religious significance in the Old Testament. Ancient Israel's sanctuary sites and towns were frequently situated on elevated hills or mountain ridges. Traveling to a town, altar, or sanctuary almost always involved a physical ascent.

In 1 Samuel 1:3, the text describes Elkanah leaving his home in Ramathaim Zophim in the mountains of Ephraim to travel to Shiloh, where the Tabernacle and the Ark of the Covenant were located. Shiloh served as Israel’s central place of worship during the period of the judges. Beyond the literal geography, traveling up to a place of worship was understood as an intentional journey into the presence of God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of ‘alah is to move upward, ascend, climb, or rise. Depending on the setting, the word can refer to:

  • Moving from a lower geographic region to a higher elevation, such as climbing a hill or traveling to a mountainous district.

  • Ascending to a high place or altar to offer sacrifices and perform acts of worship.

  • Rising up in a natural or physical sense, such as smoke ascending or mist rising from the earth.

  • Advancing in a military context, where troops go up to engage in battle.

In 1 Samuel 1:3, the verb appears in a masculine singular perfect form, referring to Elkanah's established habit of traveling up to Shiloh year after year.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses ‘alah across many contexts to capture different aspects of upward movement:

  • Physical ascent: The word describes climbing mountains, stepping onto elevated ground, or rising up out of a valley.

  • Ceremonial movement: Worshipers regularly go up to sacred places to present themselves before the LORD, bring offerings, or observe annual feasts.

  • Sacrificial action: The noun form for the burnt offering (‘olah) derives from this same root, conveying the picture of the smoke rising upward toward heaven.

  • Strategic travel: In civic and military contexts, leaders and armies go up to cities or battlefields positioned on higher ground.

4. Theological Implications

In the Old Testament, approaching God is consistently pictured as an ascent. God dwells on high, and His worshipers climb upward to enter His presence at the sanctuary.

This upward journey highlights the holy distinction between common daily life and sacred worship. Going up to worship required deliberate effort, preparation, and devotion. Elkanah's journey up to Shiloh demonstrates faithful devotion during a dark period in Israel's history when many had abandoned true worship. His annual movement upward shows a household devoted to seeking God at His ordained place of meeting.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:3, ‘alah establishes the rhythm of Elkanah's life. The opening chapter of 1 Samuel introduces a period of transition in Israel. The narrative contrasts the spiritual corruption of Eli’s sons, Hophni and Phinehas, with the quiet fidelity of Elkanah’s household.

Despite the breakdown of leadership at Shiloh, Elkanah continued his pattern of going up to worship and sacrifice to the LORD of hosts. The verb highlights his commitment to honor God regardless of the spiritual state of the priests. His ascent to Shiloh creates the setting for Hannah's deep prayer, God's answer, and the birth of Samuel, who would bring renewal to the nation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:6 — "but a mist went up from the earth and watered the whole face of the ground."

  • Exodus 24:18 — "So Moses went up into the mountain, and was on the mountain forty days and forty nights."

  • 1 Samuel 9:14 — "And they went up to the city. As they were coming into the city, there was Samuel, coming out toward them on his way up to the high place."

  • Psalms 68:18 — "You have ascended on high, You have led captivity captive; You have received gifts among men."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The physical topography of the Promised Land reinforced the theological meaning of ‘alah. Major cities like Jerusalem and Shiloh were built in high country, meaning that worshipers literally looked up as they approached and climbed as they arrived. This created a physical experience that mirrored the spiritual reality of entering God's presence.
