GIVE - (nâthan, Strong's H5414)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb natan means to give, grant, place, or bestow. It is one of the most common verbs in the Old Testament, used for actions ranging from handing an object to a person to setting boundaries or granting covenant blessings. In 1 Samuel 1:4, the word describes Elkanah distributing sacrificial portions of meat to his wife Peninnah and her children during their annual worship at Shiloh.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root natan is a foundational Hebrew verb expressing the basic act of moving something from one possessor or place to another. Its core meaning centers on giving, placing, or handing over. Depending on the context, it can describe a gift between human beings, the assignment of a duty, the act of putting an object in a physical location, or God granting a blessing to His people.

In 1 Samuel 1:4, the setting is the tabernacle at Shiloh during the period of the judges. Israelite worshipers traveled to Shiloh for annual feasts. As part of the peace offerings (or fellowship offerings), the worshiper and his family ate a communal meal from the sacrificial meat after the priests received their designated portion. Within this sacred family meal, Elkanah carried out his responsibility as head of the household by distributing portions to his family.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb natan carries a broad range of everyday and religious meanings:

  • To give or present a gift, gift portion, or payment to someone.

  • To set, place, or put an object into a position or place.

  • To grant or bestow an inheritance, favor, land, or offspring.

  • To appoint or assign a person to a task or role.

In 1 Samuel 1:4, the verb means to hand out or distribute food portions from the peace offering to family members gathered for worship.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, natan appears in almost every genre of literature. Its nuance depends on who is performing the action and what is being given:

  • When God is the subject, the verb often highlights divine favor and sovereign authority. God gives the land to Israel, gives rain in its season, gives wisdom to leaders, or gives children to mothers.

  • When human beings are the subject, it describes acts of generosity, worshipful presentation of sacrifices, payment of wages, or everyday sharing of food and goods.

  • When used for physical placement, it describes setting the sun in the sky, placing the ark in the tabernacle, or putting a ring on a hand.

The action can be a simple gesture of daily care or a solemn act with deep spiritual significance.

4. Theological Implications

In the context of worship under the Law of Moses, sharing sacrificial meat during a fellowship offering was an act of communal joy before God. The head of the household did not merely feed his family; he distributed sacred portions that represented God's peace and covenant care.

Human giving in Scripture constantly reflects the prior generosity of God. God gives life, grain, livestock, and land; His people respond by presenting offerings to Him and sharing portions with one another. In the narrative of 1 Samuel, the theme of divine giving becomes central: while human heads of house give earthly food portions, only God can give the gift of new life.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:4, the text describes the routine of Elkanah’s yearly pilgrimage to Shiloh: "And whenever the time came for Elkanah to make an offering, he would give portions to Peninnah his wife and to all her sons and her daughters."

Here, natan describes the systematic distribution of sacrificial meat. Peninnah and her children each receive their portions, underscoring her abundance of children and highlighting her secure place in the family structure. This regular act of distribution sets up the poignant contrast in the next verse, where Elkanah turns to Hannah—who has no children—and gives her a special portion because he loves her.

The grammatical form used here presents the action in a masculine, singular, completed aspect, summarizing Elkanah’s customary procedure at the worship feast. The quiet, repetitive scene of handing out food portions serves as the dramatic backdrop for Hannah’s grief, Peninnah’s provocation, and Hannah’s subsequent prayer for God to grant her a child.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The word natan plays a subtle but vital thematic role throughout 1 Samuel 1. In verse 4, Elkanah gives (natan) food portions to his family. Later in the chapter, Hannah prays desperately that God will give (natan) her a male child (1 Sam 1:11). When God answers her prayer, Hannah responds by giving (natan) Samuel back to the Lord for lifelong service at the sanctuary (1 Sam 1:28).

This repetition connects earthly care with heavenly intervention. The temporary food portion that Elkanah distributes at the feast cannot satisfy Hannah's heart, but when God bestows a child, Hannah freely surrenders that gift back to the Giver.
