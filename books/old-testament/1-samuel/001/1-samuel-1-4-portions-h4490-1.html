PORTIONS - (mânâh, Strong's H4490)

Summary:

The word manah refers to an allotted share, a divided piece, a measure of food, or a designated gift given to a specific person. In 1 Samuel 1:4, the word refers to the individual servings of sacrificial meat that Elkanah distributed to his family members during their annual worship feast at Shiloh.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The noun manah comes from a root that means to count, measure out, assign, or divide into parts. In ancient Israel, when an offering or meal was prepared for a group, the food was divided into specific shares so that every designated individual received an allotted portion.

In the social and religious setting of the Old Testament, receiving a portion at a shared feast was a visible sign of inclusion, honor, and fellowship. The head of a household or the host of a feast was responsible for dividing the meal and handing out the individual portions to the guests or family members present.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Manah can refer to:

  • A divided share or individual serving of food, especially meat from a sacrifice.

  • An assigned gift, allowance, or present sent to another person.

  • A measure or lot set aside for a specific purpose or individual.

  • Figuratively, an allotted destiny or spiritual inheritance assigned by God.

In 1 Samuel 1:4, the word carries the direct physical meaning of an allotted serving of meat from the peace offering.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses manah across several settings:

  • Sacrificial Feasts: During a peace offering, a portion of the animal was burnt on the altar for the LORD, a portion was assigned to the officiating priest, and the remainder was returned to the worshiper to be shared in a sacred meal. The individual servings given to the family members are called portions.

  • Festive Celebrations: On days of holy rejoicing, such as the reading of the Law in Nehemiah's day or the celebration of deliverance in Esther, God's people were commanded to send portions of food to those who were poor or had nothing prepared.

  • Royal and Official Allotments: Higher authorities assigned daily allowances or choice portions of food as acts of favor or royal provision.

  • Spiritual Inheritance: David uses the term figuratively to describe his relationship with God, declaring that the LORD Himself is his chosen portion.

4. Theological Implications

The distribution of portions during sacrificial meals highlights the communal nature of Old Testament worship. Peace offerings were designed to celebrate reconciliation and fellowship with God. God did not consume the entire offering; instead, He invited the worshiper and his household to eat in His presence.

This practice demonstrated that God's blessings were meant to be shared within the family and community. Receiving a portion at the sanctuary table was a tangible reminder that each family member was a recipient of God's covenant provision.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:4, Elkanah travels to the tabernacle at Shiloh to offer his annual sacrifice. After the ritual requirements were met, he divided the remaining sacrificial meat into portions for his family.

The text notes that he gave portions to his wife Peninnah and to all her sons and daughters. Because Peninnah had many children, she received a large number of individual portions. This public distribution visibly highlighted her fertility and high social standing within the household.

This setting forms a direct contrast with the following verse, where Elkanah turns to his childless wife, Hannah. The act of handing out portions—meant to be a joyous occasion of family unity and worship—instead exposed the deep pain, domestic rivalry, and sorrow present within the family.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Leviticus 7:33 – "part" – The priest who offers the blood and fat of the peace offering receives the right thigh as his assigned share or portion.

  • 1 Samuel 9:23 – "portion" – Samuel instructs the cook to serve Saul a special, reserved share of meat at the sacred feast, marking Saul for royal honor.

  • Nehemiah 8:10 – "portions" – The leaders command the people to celebrate God's goodness by eating choice foods and sending shares to those who have nothing prepared.

  • Psalms 16:5 – "portion" – David confesses that the LORD Himself, rather than earthly wealth or land, is his ultimate share and inheritance.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Understanding how sacrificial portions were handed out brings clarity to the family dynamic in 1 Samuel 1. The peace offering feast was one of the few times the entire family gathered for a formal, sacred meal. Because portions were distributed according to the number of children, the meal made Peninnah's large family strikingly obvious, while emphasizing Hannah's childlessness. What should have been a season of pure celebration at Shiloh became a venue for personal anguish, driving Hannah to her earnest prayer before the LORD.
