TIME - (yôwm, Strong's H3117)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun yom basically means a day, a specific point in time, or an appointed occasion. In 1 Samuel 1:4, it describes the recurring occasion when Elkanah brought his family to Shiloh to offer sacrifices to the LORD, setting the scene for Hannah's distress and her earnest prayer before God.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

nearby

The opening of 1 Samuel takes place during the spiritual decline of the judges period. The nation lacked unified godly leadership, and even the sanctuary at Shiloh suffered under corrupt priests. In the middle of this dark era, the text turns to an ordinary Israelite family from the mountains of Ephraim. Elkanah faithful made an annual journey to Shiloh to worship and present sacrifices to the LORD of hosts.

The word yom appears here to mark a specific point in time—the appointed day when the family gathered for their annual sacrificial worship. This routine setting highlights how God accomplishes His great redemptive plans through the humble, daily events of faithful people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun yom can refer to daylight hours as opposed to night, a full twenty-four-hour day, or a broader period or specific occasion. When linked with specific actions or time indicators, it often marks an appointed day, a regular milestone, or a memorable gathering.

In 1 Samuel 1:4, the word refers to the appointed day or time when Elkanah carried out his family sacrifice. It points to a regular occasion on the family calendar when they traveled to Shiloh to offer worship, participate in a fellowship meal, and give praise to God.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, yom adapts easily to its surrounding context. It can describe the initial days of creation, an individual's lifetime, an appointed festival day, or a major event like "the day of the LORD."

When used for religious gatherings, yom singles out a dedicated time when ordinary work stops so the people can focus on God. In 1 Samuel 1:4, the singular noun points to the actual arrival of that designated sacrificial day, highlighting that the events taking place occurred during this solemn religious occasion.

4. Theological Implications

The arrival of this appointed day shows that God works through the routine patterns of worship. Elkanah continued to travel to Shiloh year after year, remaining devoted to the LORD despite the corruption of the priests.

This specific day also reveals the contrast between outward religious performance and genuine personal pain. On this day of sacrifice, Peninnah used the occasion to mock Hannah, while Elkanah tried to comfort her with a double portion of meat. For Hannah, the holy day brought intense grief over her childlessness. God often uses such specific days of trial within normal spiritual routines to strip away human self-reliance and draw His people into deeper prayer.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:4, yom introduces a painful, recurring scene in Elkanah's household. When the time arrived for the sacrifice, Elkanah distributed portions of the offered meat to his wife Peninnah and all her children. He gave Hannah a double portion because he loved her, even though the LORD had closed her womb.

The arrival of this regular day exposed the deep conflict in the home. Year after year, when this sacrificial time arrived, Hannah was reminded of her childlessness and provoked by her rival. The narrative uses yom to show that this burden was not a single isolated moment, but a painful pattern connected to what should have been a joyous holy day. Yet it was on this very day of distress that Hannah took her grief directly to the LORD in prayer, opening the door for God to grant her a son who would serve as Israel's prophet.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 1:5 — "God called the light Day, and the darkness He called Night." Here the word distinguishes the daylight hours from the dark.

  • Leviticus 23:12 — "And you shall offer on that day, when you wave the sheaf, a male lamb a year old without blemish as a burnt offering to the LORD." In this passage, the word marks an appointed holy day set aside for sacrificial worship.

  • Psalms 89:16 — "In Your name they rejoice all the day, and in Your righteousness they are exalted." Here the word describes a continuous, ongoing posture of joyful devotion.

  • Isaiah 61:2 — "To proclaim the acceptable year of the LORD, and the day of vengeance of our God; to comfort all who mourn." Here the word refers to a specific time set by God for His redemptive and judicial work.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The regular arrival of this sacrificial day reminds us that spiritual breakthroughs often occur within ordinary routines. Hannah experienced her deepest grief on a day set apart for celebration, proving that external religious rituals cannot cure internal sorrow. Nevertheless, God met Hannah on that exact day. Her bitter tears during a regular family feast led to a heartfelt vow, showing that God can turn our most painful calendar days into turning points of grace.
