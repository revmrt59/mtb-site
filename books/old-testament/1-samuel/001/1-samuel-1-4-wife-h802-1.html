WIFE - (ʼishshâh, Strong's H802)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun 'ishshah is the standard Old Testament word for a woman or a wife. Depending on the context, it describes an adult female person generally or a married woman in relation to her husband. In 1 Samuel 1:4, the word identifies Peninnah in her lawful position as Elkanah's wife, highlighting her standing in the family as the mother of his children during the sacrificial feast at Shiloh.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word 'ishshah is one of the most foundational nouns in Old Testament Hebrew. It designates the female counterpart to man ('ish). From the earliest chapters of Genesis through the end of the Hebrew Bible, it is used to describe women in every sphere of life—in creation, family, community, worship, and government.

In 1 Samuel 1, the story opens during the period of Israel's transition from the era of the judges to the establishment of the monarchy. The family of Elkanah traveled yearly to Shiloh to offer sacrifices to the Lord. Elkanah had two wives, Peninnah and Hannah. In 1 Samuel 1:4, 'ishshah appears in the description of Peninnah, identifying her as Elkanah's wife as he distributed portions of the sacrificial meal to her and her children.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of 'ishshah is an adult female human being. Ancient Hebrew uses this single word where English uses two distinct words: "woman" and "wife." The precise meaning is made clear by the context.

  • When used generally, it refers to a woman regardless of her marital status.

  • When used with a possessive pronoun or in direct relationship to a husband (such as "his wife"), it carries the specific sense of a married woman or spouse.

In 1 Samuel 1:4, the word means "wife." It identifies Peninnah's formal marital bond to Elkanah, establishing her position within his household.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, 'ishshah displays several shades of usage depending on the setting:

  • Human Identity at Creation: In Genesis, the word first appears when God creates the first female as a companion corresponding to the man.

  • Marital Relationship: The word frequently denotes a lawful wife in covenant marriage, reflecting household structures, inheritance rights, and family life in Israel.

  • Plural Usage: In the plural form, it describes groups of women in various settings, such as corporate worship, social gatherings, or times of national crisis.

Because Hebrew uses the same noun for both "woman" and "wife," the text relies on context—such as possessive pronouns or actions involving a husband—to indicate marriage.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of 'ishshah highlights God's design and care for women throughout redemptive history. From creation onward, women share equal human dignity before God. In the Old Testament narrative, marriage forms a primary building block of Israelite society and covenant life.

At the same time, Scripture honestly records the brokenness introduced into family life when human practice departed from God's creation ideal. Elkanah's household suffered from the friction inherent in a polygamous marriage. Peninnah used her fertility to provoke Hannah, while Hannah grieved her barrenness. The presence of two wives in this passage sets up a poignant contrast, demonstrating how God works His gracious purpose through painful human circumstances to bring about salvation history.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:4, 'ishshah occurs in a feminine singular form referring specifically to Peninnah. The passage describes Elkanah's regular worship practice at the sanctuary in Shiloh: "And whenever the time came for Elkanah to make an offering, he would give portions to Peninnah his wife and to all her sons and her daughters."

The text explicitly connects Peninnah as "his wife" to "all her sons and her daughters." This phrasing underscores Peninnah's established position and motherly fruitfulness within the household. It stands in direct contrast with verse 5, where Hannah receives a special portion despite having no children. Peninnah's title as "his wife" in verse 4 emphasizes the visible, social blessing of children she possessed, which became a source of rival provocation toward Hannah. Through this contrast, the narrative sets the stage for Hannah's deep prayer and God's miraculous answer in the birth of Samuel.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the worship setting of 1 Samuel 1:4, the distribution of portions belonged to the peace offering (shelamim). Under the Mosaic law, the peace offering was a shared feast where the worshiper, his family, and the priests ate together before the Lord in thanksgiving.

Elkanah's distribution of portions to Peninnah "his wife" and her children fulfilled his role as head of the household during this sacred meal. The sacred occasion of joy was overshadowed by personal grief due to domestic strife. This detail illustrates how ordinary household realities and religious devotion intersected in ancient Israel, ultimately leading Hannah to pour out her heart to God at the tabernacle.
