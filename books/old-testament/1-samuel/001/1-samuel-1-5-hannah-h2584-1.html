HANNAH - (Channâh, Strong's H2584)

Summary:

Hannah is a Hebrew proper name meaning "favor" or "grace," derived from a root that expresses showing unmerited favor or kindness. In 1 Samuel 1:5, the name identifies Elkanah's beloved wife, who faces severe heartache because she is childless, creating a poignant contrast between human devotion and divine sovereign delay that ultimately sets the stage for God's gracious intervention.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The name Hannah comes from an ancient Hebrew root associated with favor, mercy, and grace. It carries the idea of being treated with generous kindness or shown undeserved warmth. Names in the Old Testament often carried descriptive significance, reflecting character, context, or divine purpose. In the family setting of 1 Samuel, Hannah is introduced alongside Peninnah as one of Elkanah's two wives. While Peninnah had children, Hannah had none, creating intense personal grief and social distress within her household.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Hannah is a feminine proper noun denoting a specific person in the Old Testament narrative. The name itself means "grace" or "favored one." It is closely related to Hebrew words that describe giving favor, showing kindness, or bestowing mercy upon someone in need. Although the name signifies favor, the character Hannah initially experiences a period of deep sorrow and childlessness before God responds to her prayer.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In Old Testament narrative, Hannah appears as the proper name of Samuel's mother in the opening chapters of First Samuel. Her story is tied directly to the transition of Israel from the period of the judges to the established monarchy. Though her name signifies favor, her life highlights a real paradox: she is deeply favored and loved by her husband Elkanah, yet she suffers years of barrenness and ongoing provocation from her rival. Rather than yielding to despair or resentment, her trial drives her to sincere, quiet prayer before God at the tabernacle in Shiloh.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's story illustrates that experiencing favor from God does not mean an absence of trial or waiting. Her name embodies the truth that divine favor rests upon God's sovereign mercy rather than immediate earthly comfort. God's decision to close her womb was not an act of ultimate rejection, but part of a larger purpose to raise up Samuel as a prophet and judge to guide Israel. Through Hannah's sorrow, vow, and ultimate song of praise, Scripture shows that God remembers the distressed and exalts the humble through His grace.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:5, the repeated mention of Hannah emphasizes both her husband's deep affection and her stark physical affliction. Elkanah gives a double portion of the sacrificial meal to Hannah because he loves her, yet the narrative explicitly records that the Lord had closed her womb. Her name—meaning favor—stands in sharp contrast with her painful circumstances. Elkanah's human effort to comfort her with extra provisions cannot satisfy her deepest longing. The verse highlights her situation, setting up her turning to the Lord in prayer as her true source of hope.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The personal journey of Hannah provides the context for one of the most significant prayers of thanksgiving in the Old Testament in 1 Samuel 2:1–10. Her prayer celebrates God's power to reverse human fortunes, lift up the poor from the dust, and give children to the barren. This song of praise establishes major biblical themes that recur throughout the historical and prophetic books, and it directly foreshadows Mary’s song of praise in the New Testament.
