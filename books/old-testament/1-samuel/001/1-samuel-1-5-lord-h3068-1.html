LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

The divine personal name Yahweh (rendered "LORD" in small capital letters in English Bibles) is the proper name of the God of Israel. It distinguishes the living God from all false deities and identifies Him as the personal God who forms relationships with His people and acts in human history. In 1 Samuel 1:5, the name identifies God as the supreme Lord who oversees human life and physical circumstances, showing that Hannah's childlessness was not a matter of random chance, but a painful condition governed by the LORD for His ultimate redemptive purposes.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the Old Testament, God is identified by several titles and names. General terms like Elohim describe God in His supreme power as Creator, while titles like Adonai acknowledge His rule as Lord and Master. The name Yahweh (H3068) is distinct because it is God's specific personal name revealed to His people Israel. When God revealed Himself to Moses at the burning bush, He made His name known as "I AM WHO I AM," establishing Yahweh as the faithful God who acts to save His people.

In English Bibles, including the New King James Version, Yahweh is customarily translated as "LORD" using capital letters. This practice follows an ancient Jewish tradition of reading the Hebrew word for Lord instead of pronouncing the sacred personal name aloud. In the historical setting of 1 Samuel, Israel was transitioning from the chaotic period of the judges into the establishment of the monarchy. The repeated focus on God's personal name reminded the nation that whether they were ruled by judges or kings, Yahweh remained their true ruler and divine King.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name Yahweh is a proper personal noun. It functions throughout Scripture as the formal name of the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

Rather than serving as a general term for godhead, the name designates God in His personal identity. The word refers to:

  • The personal God who enters into explicit promises and fellowship with His people.

  • The sovereign Creator who governs all events, nations, and individual lives.

  • The living God who listens to prayer and intervenes in human affairs.

In 1 Samuel 1:5, the name identifies the active, sovereign God who exercises control over the private details of Hannah's life and family.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

The personal name Yahweh appears far more frequently in the Old Testament than any other divine title. It spans every genre of biblical literature, including narrative, law, poetry, and prophecy.

Scripture uses this proper name in distinct settings:

  • In worship and prayer, God's people address Him by His personal name when crying out in grief or offering praise.

  • In historical events, the name emphasizes that God Himself is intervening in human affairs rather than leaving events to blind fate.

  • In times of personal suffering, the name reassures believers that God is aware of their trials and acts according to His righteous character.

4. Theological Implications

The name Yahweh reveals fundamental truths about God's character as displayed throughout Scripture.

First, God is personal. He is not a distant force or unfeeling principle. He interacts with individuals, hears their quiet cries, and responds to their needs.

Second, God exercises absolute authority over physical creation and human circumstances. In Israel's understanding, health, childbearing, and daily life were directly under God's control. The narrative presents Hannah's condition not as the work of chance or hostile powers, but as a situation governed by Yahweh.

Third, God works through personal heartbreak to carry out His larger plan of salvation. Even when God permits seasons of grief, He remains active and trustworthy, preparing the way for future blessing and redemption.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:5, the story sets up the deep grief of Hannah. Her husband Elkanah loved her and expressed that love by giving her a double portion during the annual sacrifice at Shiloh. Yet Hannah was heartbroken because "the LORD had closed her womb."

The presence of the name Yahweh in this verse carries several important contextual points:

  • It excludes any pagan idea that Hannah's barrenness was caused by chance, magic, or lesser deities. The text explicitly points to Yahweh's sovereign oversight.

  • It highlights a painful contrast in Hannah's life: she was deeply loved by her husband, yet hindered by the very God her family traveled to worship. This painful reality drove Hannah to deep, earnest prayer in the tabernacle.

  • It prepares the reader for God's merciful response. Because Yahweh was the One who closed her womb, only Yahweh could open it. This sets the stage for the miraculous birth of Samuel, whom Hannah would dedicate back to the LORD for a lifetime of service.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 13:5 — "And it shall be, when the LORD has brought you into the land of the Canaanites..."

Here the name points to God's supreme power to fulfill the ancient promises He made to Israel's ancestors.

  • Joshua 14:12 — "Now therefore, give me this mountain of which the LORD spoke in that day..."

In this passage, Caleb relies directly on the promised word and faithfulness of the LORD to claim his inheritance in his old age.

  • 2 Samuel 22:2 — "The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer;"

David uses God's personal name in a song of praise, celebrating God's active protection and personal salvation throughout his life.

  • Psalms 25:1 — "To You, O LORD, I lift up my soul."

This Psalm shows how God's personal name is used in heartfelt prayer, expressing complete reliance on God in times of trouble.

  • Proverbs 16:6 — "In mercy and truth atonement is provided for iniquity; and by the fear of the LORD one departs from evil."

Here the divine name is connected with personal devotion and holy living, showing that wisdom begins with revering God.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's experience illustrates how the personal name of God connects public worship with private suffering. Elkanah's family traveled to Shiloh to sacrifice to the "LORD of hosts"—a title introduced in 1 Samuel 1:3 that presents God as the Commander of the armies of heaven.

Yet in verse 5, that same almighty God is described as being directly involved in Hannah's personal grief at home. The Commander of heaven's armies is the very Lord who holds sovereign power over a mother's womb and hears her sorrowful prayer.
