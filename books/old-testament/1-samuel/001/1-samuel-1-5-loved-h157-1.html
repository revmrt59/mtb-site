LOVED - (ʼâhab, Strong's H157)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb ahab expresses strong personal affection, tender devotion, or deep delight toward another person, an object, or a principle. In 1 Samuel 1:5, the word describes Elkanah's tender care and personal preference for his wife Hannah, showing that his love for her was steadfast and generous even while she suffered from childlessness.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The opening chapter of 1 Samuel is set during the troubled transition period near the end of the era of the judges. Elkanah lived in the mountains of Ephraim with his two wives, Hannah and Peninnah. In ancient Israelite society, childlessness brought severe social stigma and personal grief. Peninnah had children and routinely provoked Hannah, causing her deep sorrow.

Each year, Elkanah traveled with his family to worship and offer sacrifices to the Lord at Shiloh. When Elkanah distributed portions of the sacrificial meal to his family, he gave Hannah a double portion because of his genuine affection for her. The text uses ahab to highlight the contrast between Hannah's barren status in the household and her husband's constant personal commitment.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb ahab is the primary Hebrew word used for affection and love. It can refer to:

  • Warm affection between family members or spouses

  • Deep friendship and personal loyalty

  • Joyful delight in God's commands or righteousness

  • God's faithful affection for His chosen people

Depending on who is loving whom, the word ranges from emotional affection to practical expressions of loyalty and favor. In domestic settings, it indicates a strong emotional bond and personal preference.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament literature, ahab describes various forms of human and divine love:

  • Parental and sacrificial devotion, such as Abraham's affection for Isaac

  • Marital intimacy and personal preference within domestic relationships

  • Loyal companionship, such as the bond between close friends

  • Faithful reverence for God's revealed instruction and law

Grammatically, the word appears in 1 Samuel 1:5 as a masculine singular verb in the perfect form. This form describes Elkanah's settled and ongoing affection for Hannah as a established reality in their marriage.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of ahab in 1 Samuel 1:5 highlights several key biblical themes:

  • Value independent of productivity: Elkanah's affection for Hannah was not based on her ability to bear sons, demonstrating that a person's worth in God's eyes and within a godly family is not measured by worldly output or social standing.

  • Human affection alongside divine sovereignty: The passage explicitly notes that Elkanah loved Hannah, yet the Lord had closed her womb. Human affection, though comforting, cannot replace or overrule God's sovereign timing and purpose.

  • Compassion in suffering: Elkanah's practical generosity served as a tangible expression of care in the midst of Hannah's ongoing trial, reflecting how genuine affection seeks to honor and comfort those who are suffering.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:5, ahab explains the motivation behind Elkanah's action. During the sacrificial feast at Shiloh, Elkanah gave portions of the sacrifice to Peninnah and her children, but to Hannah he gave a double portion. The narrative explicitly links this extra portion to his feeling toward her: he gave it because he loved her.

This detail is crucial to the narrative setup. While Peninnah used her fertility to torment Hannah, Elkanah used his position as head of the household to honor Hannah. The word emphasizes that despite her inability to produce an heir at that time, Hannah remained the central object of Elkanah's devotion. This domestic backdrop sets the stage for Hannah's desperate prayer to the Lord and the miraculous birth of Samuel.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In ancient Near Eastern cultures, a husband might easily ignore, demote, or divorce a childless wife in favor of one who had borne heirs. Elkanah's actions run contrary to this cultural expectation. By granting Hannah a double portion, he publicly affirmed her dignity and honored her position in the family. The narrative demonstrates that godly devotion within the family can offer significant practical comfort, even before God steps in to resolve a long-standing trial.
