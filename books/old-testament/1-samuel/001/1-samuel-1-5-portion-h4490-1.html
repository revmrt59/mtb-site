PORTION - (mânâh, Strong's H4490)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun manah refers to an allotted share, portion, or measure, often used for a serving of food from a sacrifice, a gift, or an assigned lot in life. In 1 Samuel 1:5, the word describes the special serving of sacrificial meat that Elkanah gave to his wife Hannah, showing his deep love and honor for her despite her painful barrenness.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israel, shared meals often followed peace offerings and religious feasts. When a worshiper brought an offering to the sanctuary, parts of the animal were burned on the altar, parts were given to the priests, and the remaining meat was eaten by the worshiper and his family in a joyful meal.

During these family feasts, the head of the household distributed the food, assigning a specific share to each family member. The noun manah identifies this measured allotment or serving. In 1 Samuel 1, the setting is the annual family pilgrimage to the tabernacle at Shiloh. Elkanah brought his family to worship and sacrifice to the Lord, and at the feast he handed out individual shares of meat to his wives and children.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun manah means a portion, assigned share, serving, or allotment. It comes from a verb root that means to count, number, or assign. The noun focuses on the actual measure or gift that has been counted out for someone.

The word can refer to several distinct types of shares:

  • A physical serving of food, particularly meat reserved from a sacrificial meal or a banquet.

  • A required priestly share or legal allotment from a holy sacrifice.

  • A gift or portion sent to friends, neighbors, or those in need during times of celebration.

  • A figurative allotment assigned by God, whether describing a person's spiritual inheritance or a divine judgment.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses manah across several settings in Israel's history and worship.

In the law of Moses, manah describes the priest's designated share of a sacrifice. When consecration offerings or peace offerings were made, God set aside specific parts of the animal, such as the breast or thigh, as the priests' lawful portion for their service.

In social feasting and worship, manah refers to distributed shares of food. At holy celebrations, heads of households gave shares to their family members. During sacred revivals or national holidays, people sent portions of food to those who had nothing prepared so that the entire community could rejoice together.

In poetic and prophetic writings, manah carries a powerful figurative meaning. David speaks of the Lord Himself as the portion of his inheritance and his cup, meaning that God is the true sum of his security, joy, and blessedness. Conversely, Jeremiah uses the word to describe the sobering lot or portion of judgment assigned to those who forget God.

4. Theological Implications

The idea of an assigned portion highlights God's divine provision for His people. When Israel gathered to feast before the Lord, the distribution of portions served as a visible reminder that every physical blessing came from God's hand.

The word also illuminates the nature of God's holy requirements and worship. God assigned specific portions to the priests who ministered at His altar, demonstrating that ministry and sustenance are upheld under His care.

Furthermore, Scripture moves from physical shares of meat to spiritual realities. While earthly portions of food nourish the body for a day, the ultimate portion of the believer is the Lord Himself. To have God as one's assigned share means having complete contentment, protection, and eternal life, regardless of earthly circumstances or temporary suffering.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:5, manah appears in a scene of deep family pain and sincere devotion. Year after year, Elkanah traveled to Shiloh with his two wives, Peninnah and Hannah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah had none.

When the time came to eat the sacrificial meal, Elkanah gave portions of food to Peninnah and all her sons and daughters. But to Hannah he gave a special portion. Grammatically, the word functions here as a singular feminine noun, identifying the specific share handed directly to Hannah.

Elkanah's action carried clear significance. In ancient near-eastern culture, giving a guest or loved one an honored, enlarged, or choice portion at a feast was a public expression of high esteem and affection. By giving Hannah a distinguished portion, Elkanah publicly affirmed her dignity and declared his love for her, seeking to comfort her heart even while her rival provoked her over her barrenness.

The passage notes that the Lord had closed Hannah's womb. Although Hannah lacked the earthly blessing of children at that moment, Elkanah made sure she did not lack his love or honor at the Lord's table.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The distribution of food at sacred meals was not merely about meeting physical hunger; it was a visible expression of fellowship and relationship. Receiving a portion from the head of the table signified full acceptance and belonging within the family and the worshiping community.

Hannah's choice portion contrasts sharply with her internal grief. The portion nourished her body and showed her husband's love, but it could not heal the ache of her empty arms. This tension drove Hannah from the family table to the tabernacle doors, where she poured out her soul directly to God in prayer. God responded to her sorrow, transformed her grief into praise, and eventually blessed her with a son, Samuel.
