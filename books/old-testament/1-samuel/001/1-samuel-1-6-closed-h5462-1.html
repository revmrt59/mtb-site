CLOSED - (çâgar, Strong's H5462)

Summary:

The Hebrew word sagar basic meaning is to shut, close, isolate, or surrender something into another's power. In 1 Samuel 1:6, the word describes God shutting Hannah's womb, attributing her physical inability to conceive to the sovereign action of the LORD, which sets the stage for her earnest prayer and God's eventual response.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israelite society, daily life involved shutting city gates at nightfall for protection, closing the doors of homes for privacy, and securing boundaries. The word sagar draws from these everyday experiences of closing off an opening or enclosing something securely.

In the narrative of 1 Samuel 1, Hannah lived in a culture where childbearing was deeply tied to family honor and personal joy. Her inability to bear children caused her immense grief, which was made worse by the relentless mocking of Peninnah, Elkanah's other wife. The biblical narrative uses sagar to explain the underlying cause of Hannah's physical condition.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to closing a physical barrier, such as shutting a door or a city gate. It can mean closing up a gap in physical tissue, as when a wound or opening is healed over. It is also used in legal and military settings to mean handing someone over or surrendering them into the power of another.

When God is the subject, the word describes His sovereign action in placing a restraint or shutting off an outcome. Here in 1 Samuel 1:6, the word means shutting or closing the womb, expressing that Hannah's physical barrenness was under divine direction.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses sagar across several distinct settings:

  • Physical enclosures: It describes shutting house doors, closing city gates at dusk, or closing up flesh.

  • Quarantine and isolation: In health laws, it describes isolating a person affected by a skin disease to prevent spread.

  • Military context: It expresses delivering or surrendering an enemy into someone's hand.

  • Divine authority: It describes God placing limits, closing doors, or restricting human capabilities according to His divine purpose.

4. Theological Implications

The use of sagar in 1 Samuel 1:6 highlights God's absolute sovereignty over human life and fertility. Hannah's inability to conceive was not presented as a spiritual punishment or a biological accident, but as an action determined by God Himself.

This truth underscores that God holds ultimate authority over life, death, opening, and closing. While God closed Hannah's womb for a season, His action was not designed to abandon her, but to lead her into deep, transformative prayer. That prayer resulted in the birth of Samuel, who would play a pivotal role in Israel's history. The passage teaches that God's acts of closing off human possibilities can serve as the prelude to a greater divine purpose.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:6, the word appears in a masculine, singular, perfect form ("the LORD had closed her womb"). This action of the LORD is repeated from verse 5 to emphasize the cause of Peninnah's harsh provocation and Hannah's deep sorrow.

Peninnah used Hannah's childlessness to irritate and upset her year after year. The narrator makes clear that while Peninnah saw Hannah's barrenness as an opportunity for cruelty, the underlying situation was established by the LORD. By repeating that the LORD had closed her womb, the text frames Hannah's distress within the larger framework of divine providence. God's temporary closing set the stage for Hannah's vow, bringing her to the end of human strength so that she would entrust her child entirely to God's service.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:21 — "And the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall on Adam, and he slept; and He took one of his ribs, and closed up the flesh in its place."

  • Numbers 12:14 — "If her father had but spit in her face, would she not be shamed seven days? Let her be shut out of the camp seven days, and after that she may be received again."

  • 2 Kings 4:4 — "And when you have come in, you shall shut the door behind you and your sons; then pour it into all those vessels, and set aside the full ones."

  • Isaiah 22:22 — "The key of the house of David I will lay on his shoulder; so he shall open, and no one shall shut; and he shall shut, and no one shall open."

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is an intentional contrast in 1 Samuel between human malice and divine sovereignty. Peninnah's severe provocation was intended to make Hannah miserable, yet Peninnah could not alter what God had determined. Human opposition can multiply emotional distress, but it cannot override God's authority.

Furthermore, what God shuts, He can also open. Later in the chapter, the narrative notes that the LORD remembered Hannah, opening her womb to conceive Samuel. The temporary closing served God's ultimate plan to raise up a faithful prophet for Israel.
