LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

The proper noun Yahweh (translated as "LORD" in English Bibles) is the personal name of the God of Israel, identifying Him as the self-existent, living God who makes Himself known to His people. In 1 Samuel 1:6, the narrator uses this personal divine name to attribute Hannah's barrenness directly to God's sovereign oversight, showing that her deep personal struggle is held within the larger purpose and authority of Israel's Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word rendered "LORD" in standard English Bibles is the unique proper name of Israel's God, Yahweh. Unlike general terms for God, this is a specific personal name revealed in the Old Testament. The historical setting of 1 Samuel 1 takes place near the end of the period of the judges, during a time of moral decay and spiritual weakness in Israel. The sanctuary at Shiloh was serving as the religious center, but the priest Eli's sons were dishonoring their office. Into this context of spiritual breakdown, the narrator introduces a quiet domestic tragedy: Hannah, a godly woman, is unable to bear children and suffers ongoing provoke and cruelty from her husband's second wife, Peninnah.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Yahweh is a proper noun that functions as the personal name of God. It is connected closely with the Hebrew verb meaning "to be" or "to exist," as shown when God revealed His name at the burning bush as "I AM WHO I AM." The name highlights that God is self-existent, active, and present with His creation.

English Bibles traditionally print this name as "LORD" in capital letters to distinguish it from title words like Adonai (which means "Lord" or "Master"). While standard titles express God's role or authority, Yahweh identifies who He is in His revealed personal identity.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament narrative, law, poetry, and prophecy, Yahweh is used whenever Scripture emphasizes God's personal identity and active involvement with His people. While a generic word for deity refers broadly to God's divine power or contrast with humanity, Yahweh identifies the God who speaks, makes promises, acts in history, and demands exclusive devotion.

In Old Testament narrative prose, the name anchors the storyline to the living God who directs events behind the scenes. Its presence signals to the reader that the events taking place are not governed by fate, idols, or human politics, but by the true God of Israel.

4. Theological Implications

The revelation of God's personal name teaches that God is not an abstract distant power, but a living Person who enters into relationship with human beings. Because God is Yahweh, He remains faithful to His character across generations.

The name also establishes God's absolute sovereignty. He is the maker of heaven and earth, the controller of nature, and the supreme ruler over human history. In the book of 1 Samuel, the name Yahweh reminds Israel that kings and leaders do not hold ultimate power; Yahweh alone establishes rulers, brings down the proud, and answers the humble.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:6, the narrator states, "And her rival also provoked her severely, to make her miserable, because the LORD had closed her womb." The use of Yahweh here carries immense weight for understanding Hannah's trial:

  • It identifies divine sovereignty over human life: The text does not attribute Hannah's condition to medical chance or nature alone, but directly to Yahweh.

  • It establishes the tension of the narrative: Peninnah mocks Hannah's misery, but Peninnah fails to realize that Hannah's situation is under God's direct control.

  • It sets up Hannah's refuge: Because Yahweh is the one who closed her womb, He is also the only One who can open it. This drives Hannah to pour out her heart in prayer before the LORD at Shiloh.

The verse shows that even painful and confusing circumstances fall under the watchful sovereign care of Israel's covenant God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 — "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens..."

  • Joshua 14:12 — "Now therefore, give me this mountain of which the LORD spoke in that day..."

  • 2 Samuel 22:2 — "And he said: 'The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer...'"

  • Psalms 25:1 — "To You, O LORD, I lift up my soul."

  • Proverbs 16:6 — "In mercy and truth atonement is provided for peace, and by the fear of the LORD one departs from evil."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the opening chapters of 1 Samuel, Hannah becomes the first person in Scripture to address God by the title "LORD of hosts" (Yahweh Tsebaoth). Her suffering drives her to appeal to the ultimate power of God over all earthly and heavenly armies.

Furthermore, God's action in closing and later opening Hannah's womb provides a vital pattern for the entire book of 1 Samuel: God routinely humbles the self-sufficient and exalts the humble, demonstrating that true strength and victory belong to Yahweh alone.
