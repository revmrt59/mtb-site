SEVERELY - (kaʻaç, Strong's H3708)

Summary:

The word ka'as refers to vexation, severe emotional distress, provocation, or grief. It describes inner turmoil caused by distressing circumstances or hostile treatment, as well as the intense grief or anger that results from such torment. In 1 Samuel 1:6, ka'as describes how Peninnah provoked Hannah "severely," highlighting the deep emotional anguish and distress inflicted on Hannah because of her childlessness.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The opening chapter of 1 Samuel introduces the story of Elkanah and his two wives, Hannah and Peninnah. Hannah was beloved by Elkanah, but she was childless, while Peninnah had children. In ancient Israelite society, childlessness brought intense social stigma and personal grief.

This family pain escalated during the annual pilgrimage to the sanctuary at Shiloh. Instead of being a time of joyful worship and feast, the trip became an annual trial for Hannah. Peninnah weaponized Hannah's barrenness, actively seeking to distress her. The biblical narrative uses ka'as to capture the severe emotional anguish caused by Peninnah's persistent rivalry and cruelty.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Ka'as refers to feelings of vexation, intense grief, provocation, or deep anger. It describes both an internal state of bitter emotional distress and the external provocation that causes such pain.

The word is used in Scripture in two main settings:

  • Human emotion and conduct: Ka'as describes deep sorrow, emotional pain, or internal grief resulting from hardship, loss, or harsh treatment. It can also describe foolish, uncontrolled anger or resentment in a person's heart.

  • Divine grief and indignation: When directed toward God, ka'as describes His righteous anger or provocation caused by Israel's rebellion, idolatry, and persistent unfaithfulness.

In every case, ka'as marks a strong, agitating emotional reaction to painful or offensive conditions.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Scripture, ka'as carries distinct shades of meaning based on the situation:

  • Vexation and sorrow: In Job and Psalms, ka'as often expresses the overwhelming sorrow or agony felt by someone enduring deep suffering, persecution, or physical distress. The word highlights the internal strain that wears down a person's spirit.

  • Provocation and anger: In historical books, ka'as frequently describes Israel's idolatry provoking God to anger. Human sin offends His holiness and breaks covenant fellowship, bringing about divine grief and indignation.

  • Frustration and resentment: In wisdom literature like Proverbs and Ecclesiastes, ka'as often warns against holding onto foolish anger, resentment, or inner turmoil, which robs a person of peace and wisdom.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of ka'as in Hannah's story highlights important biblical truths about human suffering and God's care:

  • God sees raw human pain: Scripture does not sanitize the bitter grief caused by human cruelty. Hannah's deep vexation was fully seen and understood by God.

  • Grief brought to God becomes prayer: Rather than retaliating against her rival or sinking into silent bitterness, Hannah brought her ka'as directly to the Lord in honest, desperate prayer.

  • Divine grace answers deep sorrow: God attends to the brokenhearted. Hannah's severe distress became the backdrop for God's merciful intervention, answering her prayer with the birth of Samuel.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:6, ka'as functions to show the severity of the provocation Hannah endured. The NKJV translates the term as "severely" within the phrase "provoked her severely."

Peninnah targeted Hannah's deepest vulnerability—her childlessness—to make her miserable and agitate her spirit. The word ka'as focuses on the deep emotional vexation and grief caused by this mocking. While the surrounding narrative explains that this cruelty happened year after year (1 Samuel 1:7), the word ka'as itself captures the intense weight of the emotional wound inflicted on Hannah.

Later in the same chapter, Hannah uses this same word when explaining her silent, weeping prayer to Eli the priest, telling him she had been praying out of the abundance of her complaint and "grief" (1 Samuel 1:16). The very vexation used by her rival to drive her to despair ultimately drove her to her knees before the Lord.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Samuel 1:16 – "Do not consider your maidservant a wicked woman, for out of the abundance of my complaint and grief I have spoken until now."

  • Job 6:2 – "Oh, that my grief were fully weighed, and my calamity laid with it on the scales!"

  • Psalms 31:9 – "Have mercy on me, O LORD, for I am in trouble; my eye wastes away with grief, yes, my soul and my body!"

  • Proverbs 17:25 – "A foolish son is a grief to his father, and bitterness to her who bore him."

  • Ecclesiastes 7:3 – "Sorrow is better than laughter, for by a sad countenance the heart is made better."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's story provides a powerful model for processing severe emotional pain. Peninnah used Hannah's distress to drive her toward misery, but Hannah poured out her soul before God.

This contrast shows that deep emotional vexation does not have to turn into sinful bitterness. When believers take their grief and provocation directly to God, He receives their honest prayers and offers true comfort in the midst of their trial.
