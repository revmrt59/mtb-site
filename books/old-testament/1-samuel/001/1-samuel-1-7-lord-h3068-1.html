LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

The word Yahweh (rendered "Lord" or "LORD" in English Bibles) is the personal divine name of the God of Israel. It distinguishes the living God from false deities and signifies His personal, covenant relationship with His people. In 1 Samuel 1:7, this name identifies the sanctuary at Shiloh as "the house of the Lord," showing that Hannah brought her deep personal grief directly to the God who hears and remembers His people.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the Old Testament, Yahweh is the sacred proper name of God. God revealed this personal name to Moses at the burning bush, establishing it as the primary name by which Israel would know Him throughout their history. Grammatically, the word is a proper noun, functioning as a personal name rather than a general title.

Because of ancient Jewish reverence for the sacred name, readers spoke the title Adonai ("Lord") whenever they encountered the name in the Hebrew text. English translations reflect this tradition by rendering the name as "LORD" or "Lord."

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word functions as the proper personal name of the God of Israel. While general terms like Elohim describe God as the Creator or supreme God over all creation, Yahweh specifies who God is in personal relationship with His chosen people.

The name identifies the living God who entered into covenant with Abraham, brought Israel out of Egypt, and established His worship in the land of promise. It distinguishes the true God of Israel from the idols and gods of the surrounding nations.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the divine name in contexts of covenant promises, worship, prayer, and sacred places. Sacred spaces where Israel gathered to worship—such as the tabernacle at Shiloh and later the temple in Jerusalem—were designated as "the house of the Lord."

When worshippers prayed or made vows, they invoked this personal name. Praying to Yahweh signified approaching a personal God who makes promises, listens to crying hearts, and acts in human history, rather than pleading before an indifferent or impersonal deity.

4. Theological Implications

The revelation of God's personal name demonstrates that God desires to be known personally by His people. Worship in Israel was not directed toward an unknown deity, but to the God who declared His name and invited His people into fellowship.

Because God places His name upon His sanctuary and His people, believers can approach Him in confident prayer. The personal divine name reassures worshippers that God is present to hear their sorrows and respond according to His faithful character.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:7, Hannah endures year after year of bitter provocation from Peninnah because of her childlessness. During the family's annual pilgrimage to Shiloh, Peninnah taunts Hannah to the point of tears and grief.

In the middle of this painful ordeal, the text notes that Hannah goes up to "the house of the Lord." The presence of the divine name here is crucial. The house at Shiloh was not merely a ceremonial building; it was the location where the personal God of Israel dwelt among His people. Even though her heart was broken, Hannah turned toward the sanctuary of Yahweh.

The passage highlights that Hannah did not allow her mistreatment to drive her away from God. Instead, she brought her grief straight to the house of the Lord, setting the stage for her heartfelt, silent prayer before Yahweh in the verses that follow.

  • Proverbs 16:6 — "In mercy and truth atonement is provided for iniquity; and by the fear of the Lord one departs from evil."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's story in 1 Samuel 1 presents a stark contrast between human cruelty and divine refuge. Peninnah's harassment happened repeatedly year by year, but Hannah repeatedly sought comfort in the house of the Lord.

Her experience shows that going to the house of the Lord does not instantly shield a person from painful circumstances or unkind people. However, placing one's burden before Yahweh brings the trial into the light of God's presence, where sorrow can be turned into quiet trust and honest prayer.
