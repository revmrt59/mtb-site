PROVOKED - (kaʻaç, Strong's H3707)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb ka'as means to provoke to anger, vex, irritate, or stir up deep grief and indignation. In 1 Samuel 1:7, it describes how Peninnah deliberately and repeatedly taunted Hannah during their family's annual pilgrimage to the sanctuary at Shiloh. Peninnah used Hannah's barrenness to wound her emotionally, driving Hannah to bitter tears and fasting before the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The opening chapter of 1 Samuel introduces the household of Elkanah, a Levite living in the hill country of Ephraim. Elkanah had two wives: Hannah, whom he loved deeply, and Peninnah. While Peninnah had children, Hannah was barren. In ancient Israelite culture, barrenness was viewed not merely as a medical tragedy, but often as a source of intense social shame and spiritual grief.

Each year, Elkanah brought his family to Shiloh to worship and offer sacrifices at the house of the Lord. These trips were intended to be times of gratitude, joy, and holy fellowship. However, Peninnah routinely converted these sacred occasions into seasons of torment, taking advantage of Hannah's vulnerability to cause her anguish.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Ka'as means to arouse anger, cause grief, vex, or provoke someone to deep agitation. The word describes actions that intentionally disturb another person's spirit, stirring up emotional pain, resentment, or sorrow.

When the subject of the verb is a person directed toward God, the word refers to acts of idolatry, rebellion, or sin that arouse God's righteous anger. When used between human beings, as in 1 Samuel 1:7, ka'as describes cruel taunting or deliberate harassment designed to upset, hurt, and distress the recipient.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, ka'as carries two closely connected sides: the active provocation that acts as the cause, and the emotional distress or anger produced as the result.

In narrative and prophetic texts, the word frequently describes Israel provoking the Lord to anger through false worship and stubborn disobedience. In personal relationships, ka'as highlights the deliberate intent to wound another person's feelings or provoke an emotional response.

Grammatically, the target occurrence in 1 Samuel 1:7 uses a feminine singular imperfect form. This grammatical form fits the ongoing, continuous nature of Peninnah's behavior. Peninnah did not merely make a single hurtful remark; she made a habit of taunting Hannah repeatedly over many years.

4. Theological Implications

Peninnah's treatment of Hannah reveals how human pride and envy can infect even holy settings. Peninnah possessed the social status of motherhood, yet she used her blessing as a weapon to diminish another. This shows how fallen human nature can turn times of spiritual worship into opportunities for self-exaltation and cruelty.

At the same time, the passage highlights God's attentiveness to those who suffer under human provocation. Peninnah intended to crush Hannah's spirit, but the relentless painful taunts ultimately pushed Hannah to pour out her heart before God in honest prayer. Human provocation, though deeply painful, became the catalyst that brought Hannah into intimate communion with the Lord, who answered her distress.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

The setting of 1 Samuel 1:7 amplifies the malice behind Peninnah's actions. The text notes that this harassment took place "year by year, when she went up to the house of the LORD."

The journey to Shiloh was supposed to be marked by gladness, worship, and shared sacrificial meals. Yet Peninnah chose these exact moments to mock Hannah's barrenness. Peninnah knew that Elkanah gave Hannah a double portion of the sacrificial meat because he loved her, which likely fueled Peninnah's jealousy.

As a result of this repeated provocation, Hannah "wept and did not eat." Peninnah succeeded in ruining Hannah's joy during the feast, but Peninnah's cruelty also drove Hannah directly to the altar, where she made her vow to the Lord.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Kings 21:22 — "...because of the provocation with which you have provoked Me to anger, and made Israel sin."

  • 2 Kings 23:26 — "...because of all the provocations with which Manasseh had provoked Him."

  • Nehemiah 4:5 — "...for they have provoked You to anger before the builders."

  • Psalms 106:29 — "Thus they provoked Him to anger with their deeds, and the plague broke out among them."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's response to being provoked provides a striking model of spiritual focus. She did not retaliate against Peninnah, nor did she abandon the worship of the Lord at Shiloh. Instead, she took her heavy grief and turned it into earnest prayer.

This setting serves as the backdrop for the birth of Samuel, demonstrating how God operates in history. While human beings inflict pain through selfish provocation, God hears the cries of the afflicted and works through their trials to accomplish His sovereign purposes.
