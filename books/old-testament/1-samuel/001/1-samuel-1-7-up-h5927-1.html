UP - (ʻâlâh, Strong's H5927)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'alah basic definition means to go up, ascend, climb, or rise. In 1 Samuel 1:7, it describes Hannah's regular journey up to the sanctuary at Shiloh for worship. In this passage, the word marks the annual cadence of pilgrimage, showing how a time set aside to draw near to God became the very setting where Hannah faced ongoing personal grief and provocation.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israel, geography and theology were closely connected. Central places of worship, such as Shiloh and later Jerusalem, were situated in the central hill country. As a result, traveling to the sanctuary always required physical elevation. Worshipers literally walked upward to reach the altar and the tabernacle.

Because of this terrain, traveling toward the place of God's presence was described as going "up." Over time, the physical climb became inseparable from the spiritual act of pilgrimage. The action of traveling to the sanctuary was both a bodily ascent up the hills and a deliberate spiritual approach to God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb 'alah means to go up, ascend, climb, or rise upward. It is a broad action word used across many settings in the Old Testament. Depending on the context, the verb can refer to:

  • Physical elevation, such as climbing a mountain, hill, or staircase.

  • Traveling to a prominent city, region, or holy site.

  • Objects or substances rising, such as smoke ascending from an altar or a plant springing up from the soil.

  • Military movement, as armies advance or "go up" against a city.

In 1 Samuel 1:7, the verb functions as an infinitive construct indicating time ("when she went up"). It describes Hannah's regular ascent to the tabernacle at Shiloh.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

The word 'alah ranges from ordinary daily movements to sacred acts of worship. When applied to people traveling toward a sanctuary, it carries a sense of intentional approach. One does not drift into God's presence; one intentionally goes up to meet Him.

The verb is also used for the burnt offering ('olah), a noun derived from this same root, which describes a sacrifice that "ascends" in fire and smoke toward heaven. Thus, going up to the house of the LORD involves both the movement of the worshiper and the rising of worship before God.

4. Theological Implications

In the life of Israel, going up to the sanctuary expressed devotion, obedience, and dependence on God. The physical effort of climbing to the tabernacle reinforced that worship requires deliberate action.

Scripture often connects physical ascension with spiritual drawing near to God. Going up to the house of the LORD was meant to be a time of joy, feast-keeping, and spiritual renewal. However, Scripture remains honest about human life: drawing near to God does not instantly remove earthly pain or difficult relationships.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:7, 'alah highlights a painful contrast in Hannah's life. The text notes that "year by year, when she went up to the house of the LORD," her rival Peninnah provoked her bitterly regarding her barrenness.

The holy season meant for rejoicing became an annual trial. Every time Elkanah's family made the trip up to Shiloh, Hannah was reminded of her emptiness. The rhythm of going up to worship was mirrored by the rhythm of her rival's cruelty.

Yet, the repeated use of this verb reveals Hannah's quiet perseverance. Despite the distress awaiting her at Shiloh, she did not abandon the worship of God. She kept going up year after year, eventually pouring out her soul before the LORD in the very place of her deepest pain.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The grammatical form of the verb in 1 Samuel 1:7 sets a recurring timeline. It points to a repeated habit rather than a single event.

This ongoing rhythm underscores Hannah's faith. It would have been easy for her to refuse the journey to avoid Peninnah's taunts. By continuing to go up to Shiloh, Hannah positioned herself where God would ultimately answer her prayer and turn her mourning into joy.
