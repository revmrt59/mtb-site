WEPT - (bâkâh, Strong's H1058)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb bakah means to weep, shed tears, or lament deeply in response to grief, distress, or intense emotion. In 1 Samuel 1:7, the word describes Hannah's visible grief and heartache caused by years of childlessness and the bitter, ongoing provocation of Peninnah, driving her to sorrowful weeping and an inability to eat as she stood before the LORD at Shiloh.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The events of 1 Samuel 1 take place during the closing period of the judges in Israel, prior to the establishment of the monarchy. Each year, Elkanah traveled with his family to the sanctuary at Shiloh to worship and offer sacrifices to the LORD of hosts.

While Elkanah loved his wife Hannah, she had no children because God had closed her womb. In contrast, Elkanah's other wife, Peninnah, had children. Year after year during these annual pilgrimages to Shiloh, Peninnah taunted and provoked Hannah severely regarding her childlessness. In 1 Samuel 1:7, as the family visited the house of the LORD, Peninnah's relentless provocation broke Hannah's heart once again, causing her to break down in tears and refuse food.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

refreshed

The verb bakah refers to the physical act of weeping or shedding tears. It communicates an outward expression of inner emotional pain, grief, sorrow, or distress.

Depending on who is weeping and why, the word can refer to:

  • Weeping out of deep personal sorrow or mourning.

  • Crying out in fear, helplessness, or physical distress.

  • Shedding tears in repentance, godly sorrow, or humble contrition.

  • Weeping in moments of profound relief, joy, or emotional reunion.

In 1 Samuel 1:7, the word describes Hannah's sorrowful weeping resulting from long-standing emotional distress and severe provocation.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, bakah depicts genuine human emotion brought out in the open. It is not restricted to one type of weeping, but captures a wide range of human experiences:

  • Grief and Loss: The word frequently appears when individuals mourn the loss or separation of loved ones, showing that sorrow is a normal human experience.

  • Helplessness and Pain: It describes the crying of vulnerable people in dire situations who have reached the end of their human strength.

  • Spiritual Contrition: The word describes the weeping of those whose hearts are broken over sin or who humble themselves before God upon hearing His word.

  • Joyful Emotion: It is also used when long-separated family members reunite and weep together in gladness.

In every case, bakah indicates an unfeigned, emotional response where inner sorrow or passion manifests in tears.

4. Theological Implications

Scripture does not present weeping as a lack of faith or a spiritual failure. Hannah's tears in 1 Samuel 1:7 reveal several important biblical truths:

  • God Cares for the Brokenhearted: God does not ignore human tears. The distress Hannah expressed through her weeping was seen and remembered by the LORD.

  • Tears as an Gateway to Prayer: Hannah's weeping was not directed into self-pity or resentment toward God. Instead, her tears became the emotional runway for deep, honest prayer as she poured out her soul before the LORD.

  • Grace in Weakness: God frequently works through individuals who have reached the end of their own self-reliance. Hannah's posture of helpless sorrow prepared the way for God's gracious intervention.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

The context of 1 Samuel 1:7 highlights the bitter tragedy of Hannah's circumstance. The sanctuary at Shiloh was intended to be a place of holy feasting, thanksgiving, and joy for Israelite families. Yet, it was precisely at the house of the LORD that Peninnah chose to provoke Hannah most severely.

The text links Hannah's weeping directly with her refusal to eat ("she wept and did not eat"). Her grief was so intense that it overwhelmed her physical appetite. Rather than enjoying the sacrificial feast, she was consumed by heartbreak.

Grammatically, the verb form used here (imperfect, wayyiqtol, feminine singular) carries the narrative forward, describing Hannah's ongoing, real-time response to Peninnah's mistreatment during this specific visit to Shiloh. Her weeping in verse 7 sets up her earnest prayer in verse 10, demonstrating how unbearable pain drove her directly to the throne of God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 21:16 – "Then she went and sat down opposite him a good way off, about a bowshot away; for she said to herself, 'Let me not see the death of the boy.' So she sat opposite him, and lifted up her voice and wept." Here the word describes Hagar's desperate distress in the wilderness as she feared for her son's life.

  • Exodus 2:6 – "And when she opened it, she saw the child, and behold, the baby wept. So she had compassion on him, and said, 'This is one of the Hebrews' children.'" Here the word describes the helpless crying of the infant Moses, which stirred compassion in Pharaoh's daughter.

  • 2 Chronicles 34:27 – "because your heart was tender, and you humbled yourself before God when you heard His words against this place and against its inhabitants, and you humbled yourself before Me, and you tore your clothes and wept before Me, I also have heard you, says the LORD." Here the word describes King Josiah's humble, contrite response to God's word of judgment.

  • Psalms 126:6 – "He who continually goes forth weeping, bearing seed for sowing, shall doubtless come again with rejoicing, bringing his sheaves with him." Here the word depicts the sorrowful toil of planting in difficult times, which God promises will eventually produce a joyful harvest.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's weeping in 1 Samuel 1:7 demonstrates the power of bringing unedited pain directly to God. In ancient Israel, childlessness carried heavy social stigma and emotional pain. Peninnah exploited this vulnerability, turning sacred family occasions into bitter reminders of Hannah's emptiness.

However, Hannah's weeping did not terminate in despair. It led directly to her silent, desperate prayer before the LORD in the tabernacle. God heard her cry, remembered her, and gave her a son, Samuel, who would become one of Israel's greatest prophets and judges. Hannah's tears at Shiloh were the seedbed for God's mighty deliverance, proving that God hears and values the sorrow of His people.
