YEAR - (shâneh, Strong's H8141)

Summary:

The Hebrew word shanah refers to a year, marking a full annual cycle of time and seasonal change. In 1 Samuel 1:7, it describes the painful annual cycle of Hannah's pilgrimage to Shiloh, where she faced ongoing provocation about her childlessness year after year while continuing to faithfully seek God.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew noun shanah is the standard word used in the Old Testament to designate a year, representing a complete annual cycle marked by changing seasons, agricultural harvests, and religious festivals. In ancient Israel, the passage of a year was observed both through astronomical signs in the sky and through the sacred calendar established by God.

In the opening chapter of 1 Samuel, the narrative introduces the household of Elkanah, a man from the mountains of Ephraim who traveled regularly to worship and sacrifice to the LORD of hosts at Shiloh. The text highlights a recurring pattern in the family's life: each year they made this religious journey, and each year Hannah faced intense personal heartache during what was meant to be a time of sacred celebration.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word shanah refers to the annual unit of time. It can describe several related ideas:

  • A literal calendar year, marking twelve months or a full cycle of seasons.

  • The measurement of a person's age or life span when combined with numbers.

  • A regular, recurring annual event or season when used in repetitive phrasing like "year by year."

In 1 Samuel 1:7, shanah is used as a feminine singular noun within a repeated Hebrew phrase meaning "year by year." Here the word stresses regular recurrence over a prolonged period of time.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses shanah across many contexts to organize time and describe human experience:

  • Creation and cosmic order: God appointed the lights in the heavens to serve as signs for seasons, days, and years.

  • Lifespan and memory: The word measures the length of human life, tracking the generations from the patriarchs down through Israel's kings.

  • Sacred rhythms: God commanded His people to observe annual feasts, sacred assemblies, and sabbatical years, embedding the regular count of years into Israel's spiritual calendar.

  • Recurring seasons of life: When repeated in phrases such as "year by year," the word conveys ongoing habits, long-standing burdens, or unyielding patterns across time.

4. Theological Implications

The ongoing passage of years in Scripture demonstrates God's sovereignty over time and human history. God established the annual cycles of nature and mandated annual feasts so that His people would regularly remember His covenant faithfulness.

At the same time, the passage of time can highlight the tension of human waiting. When a trial persists "year by year," the repetition tests faith and endurance. In 1 Samuel, the annual calendar brings Hannah face-to-face with her unmet longing for a child. Her persistent journey to Shiloh year after year shows that ongoing pain does not lessen God's worthiness to be worshipped, nor does it mean God has forgotten His servant.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:7, shanah emphasizes the heavy, relentless weight of Hannah's trial. Her husband's other wife, Peninnah, provoked her severely to make her miserable because the LORD had closed her womb. This harassment was not an isolated incident; it happened "year by year, when she went up to the house of the LORD."

The passage exposes a sharp contrast between the setting and Hannah's emotional reality. The annual trip to Shiloh was supposed to be a joyous occasion of fellowship and thanksgiving. Yet, for Hannah, the annual feast became a recurring trigger for deep grief, causing her to weep and refuse to eat. The word shanah draws attention to the agonizing regular frequency of her burden, making her quiet perseverance in worship all the more notable before God answered her prayer through the gift of Samuel.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 1:14 — God appointed the sun, moon, and stars to mark seasons, days, and "years," establishing the order of time.

  • Leviticus 23:18 — The law commanded the offering of a bull, rams, and seven lambs of the first "year" during annual sacred feasts.

  • Job 3:6 — In his deep grief, Job wished that the night of his birth would be removed from the count of the months of the "year."

  • Jeremiah 36:9 — A public fast was proclaimed before the LORD in the fifth "year" of King Jehoiakim.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The phrase "year by year" in 1 Samuel 1:7 connects Hannah's story to the theme of silent endurance before divine deliverance. Her situation did not resolve overnight; it dragged on over many years. The annual cycle highlights the slow work of divine providence. What appeared to be an unending cycle of misery was actually the backdrop against which God was preparing a major transition in Israel's history—the transition from the chaotic period of the Judges to the establishment of the monarchy through Samuel.
