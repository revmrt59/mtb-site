HANNAH - (Channâh, Strong's H2584)

Summary:

The proper name Channah means "favor," "grace," or "gracious one." In 1 Samuel 1:8, her husband Elkanah addresses her directly by name while attempting to comfort her in her deep grief over childlessness, highlighting her personal sorrow right before she turns to God in faith.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The historical setting of 1 Samuel 1 takes place during the closing years of the period of the judges in Israel, shortly before the establishment of the monarchy. Israel's central worship rested at Shiloh, where the ark of the covenant was housed and where families traveled annually for sacrifices. Within this setting, the text introduces Elkanah, a Levite living in the mountains of Ephraim, and his two wives, Peninnah and Hannah.

Peninnah had children, but Hannah was barren. In 1 Samuel 1:8, during one of their annual visits to Shiloh, Elkanah addresses Hannah directly after observing that her severe grief prevented her from taking part in the sacrificial meal.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The proper name Channah is a feminine noun derived from a Hebrew verbal root that means to show favor, to be gracious, or to bestow unmerited kindness. As a personal name, it translates directly to "favor," "grace," or "gracious one."

In ancient Israelite tradition, names frequently reflected divine attributes, parental prayers, or the individual's future role. Although her name signifies divine favor, Hannah’s story opens in deep personal suffering and social disgrace due to her childlessness. This creates a striking contrast between the meaning of her name and the reality of her immediate circumstances.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In the Old Testament, this proper name is used exclusively for this specific woman—the mother of the prophet Samuel. Her narrative spans the first two chapters of 1 Samuel, documenting her journey from silent weeping at the sanctuary in Shiloh to joyful thanksgiving after the birth and dedication of her son.

Scripture uses her name to trace a clear spiritual progression. She begins as a woman severely distressed by barrenness and provoked by her rival, but she becomes the recipient of God's redemptive answer to prayer. The name anchors her personal journey within the overarching story of God showing mercy to the afflicted.

4. Theological Implications

The narrative surrounding Hannah illustrates how divine favor operates in human history. Her barrenness was not an indication of God's final rejection, but rather the earthly setting through which God demonstrated His sovereign power and grace.

Through Hannah's experience, Scripture shows that God hears the prayers of the humble and needy. Her life demonstrates that divine grace is unearned, initiated entirely by God, who lifts up the distressed and accomplishes His broader redemptive purposes through unexpected means.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:8, Elkanah speaks directly to his wife, saying, "Hannah, why do you weep? Why do you not eat? And why is your heart grieved?" His use of her name underscores her personal identity and highlights the depth of her distress.

Elkanah attempts to console her by giving her a double portion of sacrificial meat and reminding her of his love. Yet his well-intentioned questions reveal the boundaries of human sympathy. Human love alone could not remove her sorrow or grant her desire for a child. By addressing her by name at her lowest point, the passage focuses on her individual burden, which leads her to pour out her soul directly to the Lord rather than relying on human comfort alone.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Samuel 1:2 — "And he had two wives; the name of one was Hannah, and the name of the other Peninnah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no children."

  • 1 Samuel 1:5 — "But to Hannah he would give a double portion, for he loved Hannah, although the Lord had closed her womb."

  • 1 Samuel 1:15 — "But Hannah answered and said, 'No, my lord, I am a woman of sorrowful spirit. I have drunk neither wine nor intoxicating drink, but have poured out my soul before the Lord.'"

  • 1 Samuel 1:20 — "So it came to pass in the process of time that Hannah conceived and bore a son, and called his name Samuel, saying, 'Because I have asked for him from the Lord.'"

  • 1 Samuel 2:1 — "And Hannah prayed and said: 'My heart rejoices in the Lord; my horn is exalted in the Lord. I smile at my enemies, because I rejoice in Your salvation.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's prayer of praise in 1 Samuel 2 serves as a prominent literary and theological prototype for later biblical prayers, most notably Mary's song of praise in the Gospel of Luke. Both prayers celebrate how God exalts the humble and provides for the needy. Furthermore, the Greek form of the name appears in the New Testament as Anna, the devout prophetess who recognized the infant Jesus in the temple.
