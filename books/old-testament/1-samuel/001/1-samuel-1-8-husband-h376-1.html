HUSBAND - (ʼîysh, Strong's H376)

'ish ( 'ish , Strong's H0376 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun 'ish is a general term for a male person, an individual, or a husband. While its broad standard meaning refers to a man, its specific meaning is determined by the surrounding context. When used in a marital context alongside a possessive relationship, it denotes a husband. In 1 Samuel 1:8, the word identifies Elkanah in his covenant relationship to Hannah, highlighting his tender, human attempt to offer comfort in her time of deep grief and barrenness.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word 'ish is one of the most fundamental nouns in Old Testament Hebrew. It is used across every genre of Scripture, including narrative, law, poetry, and prophecy. Rather than describing a specific office, rank, or formal title, the word basic identifying sense refers to a male human being or an individual.

In historical narratives like 1 Samuel, the word often distinguishes specific individuals within a family or community. In 1 Samuel 1, the narrative opens by introducing Elkanah and his household. Although Elkanah is a devout worshiper who faithfully travels to Shiloh, his household is marked by grief due to Hannah's childlessness and the distress caused by his second wife, Peninnah. Within this setting, 'ish identifies Elkanah in his specific marital role toward Hannah.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meaning of 'ish depends heavily on how it is used in a sentence:

  • An adult male human being (as opposed to a woman or a child).

  • An individual or person ("anyone," "each one," or "everyone").

  • A husband, when linked relationally to a woman.

  • A person characterized by a particular quality, such as a "man of war" or a "man of standing."

The word itself does not carry intrinsic theological meaning or inherent marital quality. Instead, context establishes its exact meaning. When 'ish appears with a pronoun indicating a personal relationship—such as "her husband"—it specifically designates a married man in relation to his wife.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament narrative and poetry, 'ish spans a wide range of usage. At its most basic level, it distinguishes a man from a woman ('ishshah). In legal and historical settings, it can mean "each person" or "everyone," pointing to individual participation within a group.

When applied to marriage, 'ish describes the relational head and companion of a wife. Hebrew has another word sometimes used for a husband (ba'al), which carries the sense of master or owner. In contrast, 'ish emphasizes personal presence and companionship. In family narratives, 'ish highlights the human side of marital care, responsibility, and emotional connection.

4. Theological Implications

While 'ish is a standard vocabulary word for a man, its use in marital settings connects back to the creation order established in Genesis. God created humanity male and female, establishing marriage as a union where the man and woman belong together in covenant care.

In biblical narrative, human husbands—even godly ones—have real limitations. Elkanah exhibits genuine affection and concern for Hannah, yet his comfort cannot cure her barrenness or fully heal her broken spirit. This dynamic underscores a recurring biblical theme: human marital devotion is good and ordained by God, but it cannot substitute for the Lord's divine intervention and sovereign grace.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:8, Elkanah sees Hannah weeping and refusing to eat during the sacrificial feast. The text identifies him explicitly as "her husband" before recording his gentle inquiry: "Hannah, why do you weep? Why do you not eat? And why is your heart grieved? Am I not better to you than ten sons?"

The term "husband" here establishes the intimacy and legitimacy of Elkanah's affection. Grammatically, the noun appears in the singular number and common gender. His words express a sincere desire to soothe her sorrow, offering his own love as a substitute for the blessing of children. However, the scene reveals the boundary of his ability as her husband. While Elkanah can offer love, understanding, and double portions of sacrificial meat, he cannot open her womb or relieve her inner agony. The mention of Elkanah as her husband sets up the transition to the next scene, where Hannah turns past all human consolation to pour out her soul directly before the Lord.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The interaction between Elkanah and Hannah in 1 Samuel 1 provides a striking contrast between human limitations and divine power. Elkanah's question, "Am I not better to you than ten sons?", shows that he misunderstands the depth of Hannah's pain. He assumes that his devotion as her husband should satisfy her heart.

Hannah's silent departure to pray at the tabernacle shows that she recognizes what her husband cannot provide. By honoring Elkanah as her husband while taking her grief directly to God, Hannah demonstrates that ultimate hope rests not in human relationships, but in the Lord who hears prayer.
