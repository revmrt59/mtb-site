AFTER - (ʼachar, Strong's H310)

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'achar basicly refers to spatial position ("behind" or "following") or temporal sequence ("after" or "afterward"). In 1 Samuel 1:9, the word functions temporally to mark a pivotal moment in Hannah's life. Following the annual family sacrificial feast in Shiloh, Hannah waited until the public meal was over before slipping away to pour out her heart in private, silent prayer to the Lord at the tabernacle.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root idea of 'achar relates to the back or rear position of an object, person, or location. In ancient Hebrew thinking, descriptions of time and space were deeply connected. Just as a person can stand physical space behind someone else, an event can take place in time following another event. Over time, the word naturally served to describe both physical location ("behind") and chronological sequence ("after").

In the narrative setting of 1 Samuel 1, the family of Elkanah traveled annually to the sanctuary at Shiloh to offer sacrifices and feast before the Lord. Despite her husband's favor, Hannah was deeply distressed because of her barrenness and the persistent harassment of Elkanah's second wife, Peninnah. The temporal transition marked by "after" indicates the precise moment Hannah moved from participating in the communal meal to approaching God directly in earnest personal prayer.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

bound temporally or spatially, the word can mean:

  • Spatial position: "behind," "rear," or "following after."

  • Temporal sequence: "after," "afterward," or "following an event."

  • Relational or generational continuity: "descendants," "posterity," or "those coming after."

In 1 Samuel 1:9, the target occurrence is grammatically a masculine plural form functioning as a preposition of time. Here the word means "after" or "following." It specifies that Hannah's rising to pray occurred immediately after the sacrificial eating and drinking had concluded in Shiloh.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Scripture, 'achar expresses several distinct shades of physical and conceptual movement:

  • Spatial direction: It describes trailing behind a leader, an army, or a flock.

  • Temporal sequence: It anchors historical events relative to one another, pointing to what takes place after a specific milestone, decree, or period of time.

  • Spiritual allegiance: It is frequently used for following after the Lord in faithful obedience, or conversely, following after false gods in apostasy.

  • Generational succession: It refers to family lines, inheritance, and future generations who come after their ancestors.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's decision to act "after" the meal carries important spiritual lessons:

  • Human comfort vs. divine recourse: Elkanah attempted to comfort Hannah with extra portions of meat and kind words, but human efforts could not relieve her spiritual grief. After the family meal ended, Hannah turned away from human remedies to lay her request before the Sovereign Lord.

  • The place of quiet lamentation: Hannah respected the family feast and refrained from disrupting the fellowship. Once the formal gathering was over, she sought the Lord in private, showing that quiet, persistent prayer has a distinct place alongside public religious life.

  • Divine intervention in human extremities: The moment after human resources were exhausted became the exact moment God prepared to act. Hannah's private plea set in motion the birth of Samuel, showing how God works through the quiet prayers of His afflicted people.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:9, "after" creates a sharp contrast between the outward activities of the feast and Hannah's inward spiritual burden. The rest of the family had finished eating and drinking, but Hannah's appetite for spiritual deliverance remained unfulfilled.

This setting also highlights a striking irony later in the scene. Eli the priest sat near the doorpost of the tabernacle watching Hannah. Because she prayed silently in her heart—her lips moving without a sound—Eli assumed she was drunk from the meal that had just concluded. Thus, the temporal note "after they had finished eating and drinking" sets the stage for Eli's mistaken rebuke and Hannah's humble defense, proving that her condition was not born of wine, but of a deeply troubled spirit seeking God.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The transition marked by "after" in 1 Samuel 1:9 marks a decisive turning point in Israel's national history. Up to this moment, Israel was living through the chaotic era of the Judges, characterized by spiritual decline and weak leadership, as illustrated by Eli's spiritually dull perception.

When Hannah arose "after" the meal to pray, her earnest supplication resulted in the birth of Samuel. Samuel would go on to serve as prophet, priest, and judge, eventually anointing King David and establishing the monarchy in Israel. What seemed like an ordinary personal moment following a family dinner was actually the dawn of a new era for God's covenant people.
