AROSE - (qûwm, Strong's H6965)

Summary:

The word means to stand up, get up, or rise from a sitting or reclining posture. It often describes transitioning from rest or passivity into active movement and purpose. In 1 Samuel 1:9, the word describes Hannah physically getting up from the family's sacrificial meal at Shiloh to take her sorrow directly to the Lord in earnest prayer.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the Old Testament, social life and worship often centered on communal meals. Each year, Elkanah brought his family to Shiloh to sacrifice to the Lord. After the sacrifice, the family shared a ceremonial feast. For Hannah, this annual event was a time of intense heartbreak. Peninnah, her husband's other wife, provoked her deeply because Hannah had no children. Hannah was so distressed that she wept and could not eat.

When the meal ended, Hannah changed her posture. Rather than remaining trapped in despair at the dinner table, she stood up to go to the tabernacle. Her movement marks the dividing line in the story between bearing grief in silence and laying it before the Lord.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word basically refers to bodily movement—rising from a bed, a chair, or the ground. It can describe standard daily actions, such as getting up in the morning, or standing up out of respect.

Beyond physical posture, the word frequently carries the idea of preparing to act. When a person rises in this sense, they are shaking off inactivity and stepping forward to carry out a decision, start a journey, or face a challenge. In other settings, the word can also mean to stand firm, to endure, or to be established and fulfilled. Here in 1 Samuel 1:9, it refers to the literal physical act of getting up from the table, driven by a clear decision to seek God.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses this word across a wide range of situations:

  • Physical Movement: Getting up from sitting, sleeping, or crouching.

  • Preparation for Action: Setting out on a mission, preparing for battle, or beginning a journey.

  • Elevation or Authority: A king rising to power, an army rising against an enemy, or a leader taking charge.

  • Confirmation or Fulfillment: A vow being established, a promise being fulfilled, or a testimony standing as valid.

The word regularly highlights a change in state. A person does not merely change location; they shift from passive waiting to active response.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's act of rising reflects an important biblical pattern regarding prayer and faith. Sorrow and hardship can easily paralyze a person, leaving them stuck in grief. Hannah did not allow her distress to end in hopeless resignation. By getting up from the meal, she brought her inner anguish into the presence of God.

This movement shows that true worship involves actively turning toward God with one's burdens. Hannah's action preceded one of the most intense personal prayers recorded in the Old Testament, showing that seeking God requires an intentional turning away from present circumstances to appeal to the Lord.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:9, the word marks a vital turning point in the opening chapter of the book. Up to this verse, Hannah is presented as the passive victim of Peninnah's cruel teasing and Elkanah's well-meaning but unhelpful comfort. She sits, weeps, and refuses to eat.

When the text states that Hannah "arose," the narrative momentum changes completely. She steps away from the feast and moves toward the doorpost of the tabernacle where Eli the priest is sitting. The contrast between Eli sitting by the doorpost and Hannah standing up to pray highlights her spiritual urgency. Her rising is the decisive action that leads directly to her famous vow, the birth of Samuel, and the changing of Israel's history.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 27:43 — "Now therefore, my son, obey my voice: arise, flee to my brother Laban in Haran."

  • Joshua 8:3 — "So Joshua arose, and all the people of war, to go up against Ai."

  • Esther 8:4 — "So Esther arose and stood before the king."

  • Proverbs 31:28 — "Her children rise up and call her blessed; Her husband also, and he praises her."

  • Psalms 78:5 — "For He established a testimony in Jacob, and appointed a law in Israel."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The grammar of the word in 1 Samuel 1:9 highlights the smooth flow of the story. It appears in a feminine singular imperfect form with a wayyiqtol structure. This grammatical pattern connects Hannah's rising directly to the completion of the meal. The moment the feast ended, she got up without delay. The feminine singular form keeps the focus entirely on Hannah as an individual taking deliberate, personal action.
