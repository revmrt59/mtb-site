HANNAH - (Channâh, Strong's H2584)

Summary:

Channah is a feminine proper name derived from a Hebrew root meaning "grace" or "favor." In 1 Samuel 1:9, the name identifies the grieving wife of Elkanah who steps forward at the sanctuary in Shiloh to lay her deep pain directly before the Lord in prayer.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

During the closing years of the period of the Judges, Israel experienced widespread political turmoil and moral decline. The central sanctuary of the nation stood at Shiloh, where the priesthood under Eli had grown spiritually weak. Within this difficult era, the Bible introduces the household of Elkanah, a Levite living in the hill country of Ephraim.

Elkanah had two wives: Hannah and Peninnah. Peninnah had children and mocked Hannah continuously because Hannah was childless. Year after year, during the annual feast at Shiloh, Hannah suffered intense emotional distress. First Samuel 1:9 marks the critical moment when, after the communal meal, Hannah stood up at the tabernacle to pray directly to God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Channah is a feminine proper noun derived from the Hebrew root chanan, which means "to show favor," "be gracious," or "pity." The name itself is understood as "grace," "favor," or "gracious one." In ancient Israelite culture, personal names were frequently drawn from ordinary words and verbs that reflected family hope or divine attributes.

While the etymology of Channah points to "grace" or "favor," the biblical text itself does not explicitly interpret or pun on her name. The narrative presents Channah simply as her historical personal name. While readers naturally notice a fitting thematic link between her name's root meaning and the unmerited favor God eventually shows her, this connection is a narrative harmony rather than a stated literary design in the passage.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In the Hebrew Scriptures, Channah is used exclusively as a personal proper name for this specific woman in the opening narrative of 1 Samuel. Her story traces several distinct experiences in her life:

  • Her enduring sorrow and rivalry during years of barrenness.

  • Her silent, tearful prayer at the sanctuary in Shiloh.

  • God answering her request through the birth and dedication of Samuel.

  • Her magnificent hymn celebrating God's power to lift the needy and humble the proud.

4. Theological Implications

Although Scripture does not build a direct argument around the etymology of Hannah's name, her life story reveals significant truths about God's care for the humble. When human comfort failed—as Elkanah's affection could not remove her grief and Peninnah's harassment worsened it—Hannah brought her affliction directly to God.

Her narrative demonstrates that God hears the desperate prayers of the crushed in spirit. Furthermore, her song in 1 Samuel 2 establishes a key theological framework for the rest of the book: God brings down the arrogant and raises up the lowly.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:9, the explicit name Hannah signals a major shift in the narrative. Before this verse, Hannah is described primarily in terms of her circumstances—suffering from Peninnah's taunts and receiving a double portion of meat from Elkanah. In verse 9, after the sacrificial meal concludes, "Hannah arose."

The use of her personal name highlights her individual faith and initiative. She ceases to endure her pain passively and takes her burden directly to the Lord. Placing her name right at the moment she stands near the doorpost of the tabernacle sets her deep, heart-poured devotion in quiet contrast to the insensitive oversight of Eli the high priest, who initially mistakes her silent prayer for drunkenness.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Samuel 1:2 — "And he had two wives; the name of one was Hannah, and the name of the other Peninnah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no children."

  • 1 Samuel 1:5 — "But to Hannah he would give a double portion, for he loved Hannah, although the LORD had closed her womb."

  • 1 Samuel 1:19 — "Then they rose early in the morning and worshiped before the LORD, and returned and entered their house at Ramah. And Elkanah knew Hannah his wife, and the LORD remembered her."

  • 1 Samuel 2:1 — "And Hannah prayed and said: 'My heart rejoices in the LORD, my horn is exalted in the LORD. I smile at my enemies, because I rejoice in Your salvation.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's prayer at Shiloh provides one of the clearest biblical examples of silent, inward intercession. Her lips moved while her voice was silent, leading Eli to misjudge her condition. Her gentle yet firm response to Eli corrected the priest's misapprehension and moved him to pronounce a blessing over her. Her quiet trust in God stands as a remarkable testimony during a period marked by widespread spiritual darkness in Israel.
