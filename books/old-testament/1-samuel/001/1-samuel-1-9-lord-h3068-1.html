LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

The Hebrew word transliterated as Yahweh (rendered as "Lord" in English Bibles) is the personal proper name of the God of Israel. Unlike general titles for deity, this name identifies God as the personal, self-existent Lord who enters into covenant with His people and remains faithful to His promises. In 1 Samuel 1:9, the phrase "tabernacle of the LORD" identifies the sanctuary at Shiloh as the place where God met with His covenant people, showing that Hannah brought her deep personal grief directly into the presence of Israel's faithful God.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the Old Testament, God is spoken of using both general titles (such as Elohim, meaning "God") and His personal covenant name, Yahweh. Hebrew scribes held this divine name in profound reverence. In English translations, it is traditionally printed in small capital letters as "LORD" to distinguish it from the common Hebrew title Adonai ("Lord" or "master").

In 1 Samuel 1:9, Hannah travels with her family to Shiloh, where the sacred tent and the ark of the covenant resided during the era of the judges. By identifying the sanctuary at Shiloh as the sanctuary of the Lord, the passage establishes that Hannah brought her heavy burden into the direct presence of the God who makes Himself known to His people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word functions as the personal proper noun for the living God of Israel. It is closely connected in divine revelation to the Hebrew verb meaning "to be," highlighting God's self-existence, dynamic presence, and enduring faithfulness. While general titles emphasize divine authority or creative power over the universe, this personal name identifies God in His ongoing relationship with human beings.

The name serves as a proper name rather than a descriptive adjective or title. It sets the God of Israel apart from all false gods and identifies Him specifically as the God who rescued Israel from bondage, established a covenant with them, and hears their prayers.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout Scripture, this divine name appears in every genre, including narrative, legal texts, poetry, and prophecy. It is used when God addresses His people directly, when believers approach Him in worship, and when covenant promises are declared.

The use of this divine name emphasizes accessibility and relationship. God is not distant or unapproachable; He has made His personal name known. Calling on this name is an act of worship, trust, and covenantal prayer. When God acts for the sake of His name, He acts in accordance with His revealed character.

4. Theological Implications

The revelation of God's personal name teaches that God desires to be known by His people. Scripture shows that God reveals Himself by name so that His people may place their trust in Him and seek Him in prayer. The name guarantees that God is present with His people and attentive to their cries.

Furthermore, the personal name highlights God's unique identity. While other nations worshipped idols made by hands, Israel was called to serve the living God who speaks, saves, and keeps covenant. Religious worship and personal petition derive their confidence from the character of the God whose name is invoked.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:9, the title appears in the description of Eli the priest, who sat by the doorpost of the tabernacle of the Lord. This setting frames Hannah's grief and prayer. Hannah was deeply distressed because of her childlessness and the bitter taunting she endured. Rather than turning away in despair, she took her anguish directly to the sanctuary of the Lord at Shiloh.

The passage places Eli sitting casually by the doorpost while Hannah prays silently in overwhelming distress nearby. While human leaders like Eli might misjudge her sorrow, the sanctuary belongs to the Lord, the God who sees the humbled and hears the prayers of the brokenhearted. The presence of the Lord's name in this verse prepares the reader for God's personal response to Hannah's petition.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 – "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens."

  • Exodus 13:5 – "And it shall be, when the LORD brings you into the land of the Canaanites..."

  • Joshua 14:12 – "Now therefore, give me this mountain of which the LORD spoke in that day."

  • 2 Samuel 22:2 – "The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer."

  • Psalms 25:1 – "To You, O LORD, I lift up my soul."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The proper noun form confirms that the word functions as a personal name rather than a simple title. In the narrative of 1 Samuel, the spiritual revival of Israel begins not with political power or military strength, but with a sorrowful woman praying at the sanctuary of the Lord. The divine name anchored to the sanctuary at Shiloh reminds readers that God remains accessible to individuals in their personal suffering.
