SEAT - (kiççêʼ, Strong's H3678)

Summary:

A seat of dignity, official standing, or royal authority, such as a throne, official chair, or seat of honor.

In 1 Samuel 1:9, the word refers to Eli's official chair situated by the doorpost of the tabernacle in Shiloh. It highlights his position as Israel's high priest and leader, serving as the official setting against which Hannah comes in deep sorrow to pray before the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word kisse' refers to a chair or seat reserved for someone in authority or honor. In ancient Israel and surrounding cultures, ordinary people usually sat on mats, cushions, or the ground. A raised seat with a back or armrests was a distinct sign of honor, official status, and elevated responsibility.

In 1 Samuel 1:9, the word appears at the beginning of the book of 1 Samuel. The tabernacle was stationed at Shiloh, which served as Israel's primary sanctuary during the time of the judges. Eli was both high priest and a judge in Israel. His chair was located right by the doorpost of the house of the Lord, placing him in an official position to watch over sanctuary activities and observe those who came to worship.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to a royal throne occupied by a monarch, an official chair used by a judge or leader, or a distinguished seat at a banquet or formal gathering.

When used for God, the word refers to His royal throne in heaven, expressing His supreme reign over the universe.

When used for human leaders, it describes the seat from which a ruler or magistrate exercises authority, renders decisions, and maintains order.

In 1 Samuel 1:9, the word refers to Eli's designated chair of office at Shiloh. Grammatically, it is a common noun in the singular form, describing the physical seat that symbolized Eli's religious and leadership authority in Israel.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, the word carries a consistent focus on authority, official duty, and ruling presence:

1. Royal Throne: The word frequently refers to the throne of kings such as Solomon or David, representing monarchical rule, political power, and royal succession.

2. Divine Throne: Scripture uses the word to describe God's heavenly throne, showing His absolute sovereignty over all nations and creation.

3. Chair of Official Office: In narrative settings like 1 Samuel, the word describes an official seat used by a priest or judge. Sitting on this seat indicates being on duty in a public, responsible capacity before the community and God.

4. Theological Implications

Eli's seat in 1 Samuel 1:9 carries meaningful theological weight within the larger narrative of 1 Samuel:

First, Eli's official chair stands in quiet contrast to his declining spiritual insight. Although seated in the chair of high-priestly authority, Eli initially fails to understand Hannah's silent, heartfelt prayer, misinterpreting her grief as drunkenness.

Second, the seat represents human leadership under an old order that is approaching divine judgment. Eli's family had dishonored the priestly office, and his seated position reflects a fragile human authority that will soon give way.

Third, the passage sets human official seats against God's true throne. While Eli sits on his earthly seat of office, Hannah brings her silent agony directly to the God of Israel, who sits enthroned in heaven and responds to the humble.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:9, Hannah rises after eating in Shiloh to pray to the Lord. The text notes that "Eli the priest was sitting on the seat by the doorpost of the tabernacle of the LORD."

The mention of the seat establishes Eli's formal presence as the high priest observing the entrance to the sanctuary. His position on the chair emphasizes his duty as the guardian of Shiloh. Yet as the scene unfolds, Eli's posture on his seat contrasts sharply with Hannah's posture of desperate prayer.

Eli sits in passive human leadership, while Hannah pours out her soul directly to God. The narrative uses Eli's official seat to show that formal religious office alone cannot resolve human heartbreak or deliver Israel; God sovereignly acts through the genuine faith of the brokenhearted.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 41:40 – Pharaoh elevates Joseph over Egypt, stating that only in regard to the "throne" would Pharaoh remain greater than Joseph.

  • 1 Samuel 4:13 – Eli sits on his "seat" by the wayside, anxiously waiting for news of the Ark of God during Israel's battle with the Philistines.

  • 1 Kings 2:12 – Solomon sits on the "throne" of David his father, marking the official establishment of his royal reign.

  • Psalms 47:8 – God reigns over the nations, seated upon His holy "throne" as the ruler of all the earth.

  • Proverbs 29:14 – A king who faithfully judges the poor is promised that his "throne" will be established forever.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Eli's seat serves as a literary boundary for his life and ministry in 1 Samuel. In 1 Samuel 1:9, he is introduced sitting on his seat at the sanctuary doorpost, representing the established leadership at Shiloh. In 1 Samuel 4:18, his story ends when he falls backward off his seat upon hearing that the Ark of God was captured by the Philistines and that his sons were dead. The sudden fall from his official seat physically illustrates the collapse of his priestly line and the transition toward a new era under Samuel.
