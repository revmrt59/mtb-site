SHILOH - (Shîylôh, Strong's H7887)

Summary:

Shiloh is the name of a prominent city in the territory of Ephraim where Israel set up the Tabernacle during the period of the judges. In 1 Samuel 1:9, Shiloh identifies the location of the central sanctuary where Hannah took her broken heart and desperate request directly to the Lord in prayer. In this verse, the name marks the place where God turned private grief into divine purpose, beginning a major turning point in Israel's history.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Shiloh was an ancient city located in the central hill country of Canaan, in the territory allotted to the tribe of Ephraim. It lay north of Bethel and south of Lebonah. After the conquest of Canaan under Joshua, Israel assembled at Shiloh and set up the Tabernacle of meeting.

Because the Ark of the Covenant rested there, Shiloh became the central gathering place for the nation. Israelite families traveled to Shiloh for annual religious feasts, sacrifices, and communal worship. For generations before Jerusalem became the capital, Shiloh stood as the primary earthly center of Israel's worship and covenant life.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Shiloh is a proper place name identifying this specific town and worship site in central Israel. The root of the name is connected with concepts of rest, quiet, or tranquility, but as a proper noun in Scripture, it functions directly as a geographical location.

In biblical history, the name designates the specific city where the Tabernacle stood and where Eli and his sons carried out their duties as priests before the Lord.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament history, Shiloh serves as the setting for pivotal moments in Israel's life as a nation:

  • A Center for Organization: Under Joshua, Shiloh served as the headquarters where the remaining land of Canaan was surveyed and divided among the tribes by lot.

  • A Hub for Worship: During the time of the judges, Shiloh was the site where the house of God remained, drawing worshippers from across the land for national festivals.

  • A Place of Divine Revelation: In early 1 Samuel, Shiloh represents the sanctuary where the Lord met His people, answered prayer, and raised up new leadership when old spiritual structures were failing.

4. Theological Implications

Shiloh demonstrates that God condescends to dwell among His people in a tangible location, yet His presence is never captive to a site or structure. The city was meant to be a place of rest and communion between God and Israel.

The history of Shiloh shows that true worship requires faith and obedience. While priests and ritual feasting were present at Shiloh, God looked past formal religious activity to respond to sincere, humble faith. God met Hannah at Shiloh not because of the merit of the religious establishment, but because He hears the cries of the brokenhearted who seek Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 1:9, the mention of Shiloh anchors Hannah's private pain within the central sanctuary of Israel. After eating and drinking during the family's annual visit, Hannah arose and went to pray before the Lord.

The text places Eli the priest sitting on a seat by the doorpost of the tabernacle in Shiloh. This sets up a striking contrast. Eli observed her silent, desperate moving of her lips and mistakenly thought she was drunk. Meanwhile, Hannah was opening her soul directly to the Lord.

In this passage, Shiloh is not merely a town on a map. It is the holy place where human misunderstanding met divine compassion. By recording that this happened at Shiloh, the text shows how God used a quiet, personal prayer at the national sanctuary to begin renewing the spiritual life of the entire nation through the birth of Samuel.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Joshua 18:1 — "Now the whole congregation of the children of Israel assembled together at Shiloh, and set up the tabernacle of meeting there."

  • Judges 18:31 — "So they set up for themselves Micah’s carved image which he made, all the time that the house of God was in Shiloh."

  • 1 Samuel 1:3 — "This man went up from his city yearly to worship and sacrifice to the Lord of hosts in Shiloh."

  • 1 Samuel 3:21 — "Then the Lord appeared again in Shiloh. For the Lord revealed Himself to Samuel in Shiloh by the word of the Lord."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The transition seen at Shiloh in 1 Samuel 1 highlights how God operates in history. Even during times when formal religious leaders lack spiritual discernment, God remains fully attentive to the prayers of ordinary believers. Hannah's experience at Shiloh reassures believers that no matter how distracted or dull the surrounding environment may be, God hears the silent, sincere prayers of the heart.
