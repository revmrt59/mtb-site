Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 1
The Core:

Hannah asks the LORD for a son, and God answers by raising Samuel—a faithful servant and prophet who will prepare Israel for a king.

The Context:

At Shiloh, during the days of Eli the priest, Hannah carries the sorrow of childlessness and her rival’s relentless provocation.

The Structure:

  • 1 Samuel 1:1–8 — Hannah’s Barrenness and Grief

  • 1 Samuel 1:9–18 — Hannah Prays for a Son

  • 1 Samuel 1:19–28 — Samuel’s Birth and Dedication

The Key Verse:

“For this child I prayed, and the LORD has granted me my petition which I asked of Him.” (1 Samuel 1:27, NKJV)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Hannah comes to Shiloh with a longing no one around her can satisfy. Peninnah provokes her, Elkanah loves her, and Eli initially misunderstands her. But Hannah brings her sorrow to the LORD. She pours out her soul, asks for a son, and vows that the child will belong to Him all his life.

Hannah comes before the LORD, pours out her troubles, and asks Him for a son. As she prays, God reveals His desire to raise up someone who will serve Him. Hannah responds by partnering with God's purpose, promising that if He gives her a son, she will give him back to the LORD for his entire life. God answers her prayer, and Hannah fulfills her promise.

Chapter Sections

1 Samuel 1:1–8 — Hannah’s Barrenness and Grief

Hannah has no children, and Peninnah repeatedly provokes her. Even Elkanah’s love cannot take away her grief.

1 Samuel 1:9–18 — Hannah Prays for a Son

At Shiloh, Hannah pours out her soul to the LORD and vows to dedicate a son to Him for life. Eli misunderstands her at first, then sends her away with a blessing.

1 Samuel 1:19–28 — Samuel’s Birth and Dedication

The LORD remembers Hannah and answers her prayer. After Samuel is weaned, she brings him to Shiloh and fulfills her vow in worship.

Why This Chapter Matters

Hannah brings her deepest desire to the LORD, and He remembers her and answers her request. She receives Samuel with gratitude and gives him back in worship.

God’s answer reaches beyond Hannah’s household. He raises a prophet to speak His word, restores faithful service before Him, and prepares the way for Israel’s kings. A personal prayer becomes part of God’s purpose for a nation.

Key Themes to Watch For

Prayer from the heart. Hannah brings her anguish and desire openly before the LORD (vv. 10–16).

The LORD answers. God remembers Hannah and gives her the son she requested (vv. 19–20, 27).

A life given to God. Hannah dedicates Samuel to the LORD before he is born and fulfills her vow afterward (vv. 11, 24–28).

God’s preparation of faithful leadership. Samuel’s birth begins the raising of a servant who will minister before God and lead Israel (vv. 20, 27–28).

Worship through the whole journey. Hannah comes to Shiloh in sorrow and returns with Samuel in thanksgiving and surrender (vv. 7, 10, 24–28).

Key Words & Concepts

Shiloh. Israel’s central place of worship in this period, where the tabernacle stood and Eli served as priest.

Vow. A solemn promise to God. Hannah pledges to give her son to the LORD for his entire life (v. 11).

“Poured out my soul.” Hannah’s description of bringing her deep grief and longing before God in prayer (v. 15).

“No razor shall come upon his head.” A sign associated with Nazirite dedication, expressing Samuel’s lifelong consecration (v. 11; Numbers 6:5).

“The LORD remembered her.” The LORD turns toward Hannah in mercy and answers her prayer (v. 19).

Samuel / “lent to the LORD.” Hannah connects Samuel’s name with asking the LORD for him. She now gives him to God for life (vv. 20, 27–28).
