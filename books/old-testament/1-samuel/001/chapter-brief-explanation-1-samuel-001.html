1 Samuel 1:1–8 — Elkanah’s Household and Hannah’s Grief

1 Samuel 1:1
Brief Explanation: The narrative opens by introducing Elkanah, a man of Ramathaim Zophim in the mountains of Ephraim, whose ancestry is traced back four generations to Zuph. God’s work in this book begins within a particular Israelite household rooted in a real place and family.

1 Samuel 1:2
Brief Explanation: Elkanah has two wives, and the verse sets them in painful contrast: Peninnah has children, but Hannah has none. This difference creates the tension that the rest of the chapter will resolve.

1 Samuel 1:3
Brief Explanation: Elkanah faithfully goes up from his city each year to worship and sacrifice to the LORD of hosts at Shiloh, where the tabernacle stood. The note that Eli’s sons Hophni and Phinehas serve there as priests introduces the men whose conduct will soon come under the LORD’s judgment.

1 Samuel 1:4
Brief Explanation: At the sacrificial meal Elkanah gives portions to Peninnah and to all her sons and daughters. The ordinary custom of sharing the offering makes the size of Peninnah’s family plain to everyone at the table.

1 Samuel 1:5
Brief Explanation: Elkanah gives Hannah a double portion because he loves her, even though the LORD had closed her womb. The verse sets a husband’s real love beside a sorrow that only the LORD can address.

1 Samuel 1:6
Brief Explanation: Peninnah, called Hannah’s rival, provokes her severely to make her miserable, using her barrenness against her. For the second time the narrator states that the LORD had closed Hannah’s womb, keeping her situation under His hand.

1 Samuel 1:7
Brief Explanation: The provocation is not a single incident but happens year after year whenever Hannah goes up to the house of the LORD. The very occasion of worship becomes the time of her deepest grief, so that she weeps and will not eat.

1 Samuel 1:8
Brief Explanation: Elkanah asks why Hannah weeps, why she does not eat, and why her heart is grieved, and whether he is not better to her than ten sons. His love is sincere, but his words show that he cannot meet the deepest longing of her heart.

1 Samuel 1:9–18 — Hannah’s Prayer and Vow at Shiloh

1 Samuel 1:9
Brief Explanation: After the meal at Shiloh, Hannah rises to go and pray, while Eli the priest sits by the doorpost of the tabernacle of the LORD. The scene moves from the family’s yearly pattern to this particular visit and places Eli where he will observe her.

1 Samuel 1:10
Brief Explanation: In bitterness of soul Hannah prays to the LORD and weeps in anguish. She brings her grief directly to God rather than hiding it or turning away from Him.

1 Samuel 1:11
Brief Explanation: Hannah calls on the LORD of hosts to look on her affliction, remember her, and give her a son, and she vows to give that son to the LORD all the days of his life, with no razor coming upon his head. Her vow is understood as a response to God’s leading concerning this child rather than a bargain, although the passage does not record a message from God to her.

1 Samuel 1:12
Brief Explanation: Hannah keeps on praying before the LORD, and Eli watches her mouth. His attention to what he can outwardly see sets up the misunderstanding that follows.

1 Samuel 1:13
Brief Explanation: Hannah is praying in her heart, with her lips moving but her voice unheard. Seeing only these outward signs, Eli concludes that she is drunk.

1 Samuel 1:14
Brief Explanation: Eli rebukes Hannah as though she were drunk and tells her to put away her wine. The priest at Shiloh misjudges a woman in sincere prayer, showing that holding religious office does not guarantee discernment.

1 Samuel 1:15
Brief Explanation: Hannah respectfully denies drinking wine or strong drink and explains that she is a woman of sorrowful spirit who has poured out her soul before the LORD. Her answer describes her prayer as the honest emptying of her heart before God.

1 Samuel 1:16
Brief Explanation: Hannah asks Eli not to regard her as a wicked woman, because she has been speaking out of the abundance of her complaint and grief. She defends her character while openly acknowledging the depth of her distress.

1 Samuel 1:17
Brief Explanation: Eli now sends Hannah away in peace and asks that the God of Israel grant the petition she has made of Him. The priest who misjudged her becomes the one who blesses her request.

1 Samuel 1:18
Brief Explanation: Hannah asks to find favor in Eli’s sight, then goes her way, eats, and her face is no longer sad. Her countenance changes before any child is conceived, which supports the understanding that she had received assurance from the LORD in prayer.

1 Samuel 1:19–23 — The LORD Remembers Hannah, and Samuel Is Born

1 Samuel 1:19
Brief Explanation: The family rises early, worships before the LORD, and returns home to Ramah, where Elkanah knows Hannah his wife and the LORD remembers her. The LORD answers the very request she made in verse 11, that He would remember His maidservant.

1 Samuel 1:20
Brief Explanation: In the process of time Hannah conceives and bears a son, and she names him Samuel, saying, “Because I have asked for him from the LORD.” The child’s name becomes a lasting testimony that he is God’s answer to her prayer.

1 Samuel 1:21
Brief Explanation: Elkanah goes up with all his household to offer the LORD the yearly sacrifice and his vow. The family’s faithful worship continues, now with the promised son in the household.

1 Samuel 1:22
Brief Explanation: Hannah does not go up this time, telling her husband that once the child is weaned, likely around age three, she will bring him to appear before the LORD and remain there forever. Her plan shows that she intends to keep her vow completely, giving Samuel to the LORD’s presence for life.

1 Samuel 1:23
Brief Explanation: Elkanah approves Hannah’s plan and adds, “Only let the LORD establish His word,” and she nurses her son until he is weaned. His words ask the LORD to bring His purpose to completion and leave room for a word from God connected with Hannah’s prayer, though the passage does not say precisely what that word was.

1 Samuel 1:24–28 — Samuel Lent to the LORD

1 Samuel 1:24
Brief Explanation: When Samuel is weaned, Hannah brings him to the house of the LORD in Shiloh with three bulls, an ephah of flour, and a skin of wine. The narrator’s note that the child was young underscores the cost of what she is giving.

1 Samuel 1:25
Brief Explanation: They slaughter a bull and bring the child to Eli. Sacrifice and the presentation of Samuel stand side by side, so that handing over her son takes place as an act of worship.

1 Samuel 1:26
Brief Explanation: Hannah addresses Eli with a respectful oath and reminds him that she is the woman who stood beside him there, praying to the LORD. She connects this moment directly to the prayer he once misjudged.

1 Samuel 1:27
Brief Explanation: Hannah testifies, “For this child I prayed,” and declares that the LORD has granted the petition she asked of Him. She gives the LORD full credit for Samuel’s birth.

1 Samuel 1:28
Brief Explanation: Hannah declares that she has lent Samuel to the LORD for as long as he lives, fulfilling her vow, and they worship the LORD there. The child received through asking is now given back to the LORD for life.
