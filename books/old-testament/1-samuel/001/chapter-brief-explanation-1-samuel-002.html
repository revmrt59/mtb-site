1 Samuel 2:1–10 — Hannah’s Song of the LORD Who Brings Low and Lifts Up

1 Samuel 2:1
Brief Explanation: Hannah prays in a song of rejoicing, declaring that her heart rejoices in the LORD and that her horn, a picture of strength and dignity, is exalted in Him. She can smile at her enemies because her joy rests in the LORD’s salvation, not merely in her changed circumstances.

1 Samuel 2:2
Brief Explanation: Hannah confesses that no one is holy like the LORD, that there is no God besides Him, and that there is no rock like our God. Her praise moves from what God has done for her to who He is.

1 Samuel 2:3
Brief Explanation: Hannah warns the proud to stop their arrogant talk, because the LORD is the God of knowledge and He weighs every action. Boasting is foolish before a God who sees and evaluates all that people do.

1 Samuel 2:4
Brief Explanation: The bows of mighty warriors are broken, while those who stumbled are clothed with strength. The first of the song’s reversals shows that human strength does not decide who stands.

1 Samuel 2:5
Brief Explanation: Those who were full must hire themselves out for bread while the hungry are satisfied, and the barren woman bears seven while the mother of many grows feeble. Hannah sets her own story within a wider pattern of God reversing human conditions, with seven expressing complete blessing.

1 Samuel 2:6
Brief Explanation: The LORD kills and makes alive; He brings down to the grave and brings up. Life and death themselves lie under His authority.

1 Samuel 2:7
Brief Explanation: The LORD makes poor and makes rich; He brings low and lifts up. Wealth and status are not finally in human hands but under His rule.

1 Samuel 2:8
Brief Explanation: God raises the poor from the dust and the beggar from the ash heap to seat them among princes and give them a throne of glory. He has every right to do this, because the pillars of the earth belong to Him and He has set the world upon them.

1 Samuel 2:9
Brief Explanation: The LORD guards the feet of His saints, while the wicked are silenced in darkness. The verse states the song’s conclusion plainly: no one prevails by his own strength.

1 Samuel 2:10
Brief Explanation: The LORD will break His adversaries, thunder against them from heaven, and judge the ends of the earth, and He will give strength to His king and exalt the horn of His anointed. Spoken before Israel had a king, these closing words are prophetic, anticipating the kings Samuel will anoint and, in the wider canon, the Messiah.

1 Samuel 2:11–17 — The Contempt of Eli’s Sons

1 Samuel 2:11
Brief Explanation: Elkanah returns home to Ramah, but the child remains and ministers to the LORD before Eli the priest. The song gives way to the narrative, and Samuel’s service is introduced before the account of Eli’s sons begins.

1 Samuel 2:12
Brief Explanation: The narrator states plainly that Eli’s sons were corrupt and did not know the LORD. Though they held priestly office, they had no true knowledge of the God they were appointed to serve.

1 Samuel 2:13
Brief Explanation: The priests had made their own custom: while a worshiper’s sacrifice was boiling, the priest’s servant came with a three-pronged fleshhook. Their practice replaced the portion God had prescribed with a system that served their own appetite.

1 Samuel 2:14
Brief Explanation: The servant thrust the fleshhook into the pot, and the priest kept whatever it brought up, and this was done to every Israelite who came to Shiloh. The abuse was not occasional but the standard treatment of all who came to worship.

1 Samuel 2:15
Brief Explanation: Even before the fat was burned, the servant demanded raw meat for the priest to roast. The fat belonged to the LORD and was to be burned first, so the priests were taking their share ahead of God’s.

1 Samuel 2:16
Brief Explanation: When a worshiper urged that the fat be burned first, the servant refused and threatened to take the meat by force. Worshipers who wanted to honor the LORD were overruled and intimidated by the very men who should have led them.

1 Samuel 2:17
Brief Explanation: The sin of the young men was very great before the LORD, because the LORD’s offering was treated with contempt. The priests themselves despised God’s offering, and their conduct also damaged the people’s regard for worship.

1 Samuel 2:18–21 — Samuel Ministers and Hannah Is Blessed

1 Samuel 2:18
Brief Explanation: In contrast, Samuel ministers before the LORD even as a child, wearing a linen ephod. The boy serves faithfully in the same sanctuary where the priests are corrupt.

1 Samuel 2:19
Brief Explanation: Each year Hannah makes Samuel a little robe and brings it to him when she comes up with her husband for the yearly sacrifice. Her care for her son continues even after she has given him to the LORD.

1 Samuel 2:20
Brief Explanation: Eli blesses Elkanah and his wife, asking the LORD to give them descendants in place of the son given to the LORD, and they return home. The priest’s blessing honors the gift Hannah has made.

1 Samuel 2:21
Brief Explanation: The LORD visits Hannah, and she bears three sons and two daughters, while the child Samuel grows before the LORD. God answers with generous fruitfulness, and Samuel’s growth continues in His presence.

1 Samuel 2:22–26 — Eli’s Rebuke Goes Unheeded

1 Samuel 2:22
Brief Explanation: Eli, now very old, hears everything his sons are doing to all Israel, including lying with the women who assembled at the door of the tabernacle of meeting. Their corruption extends beyond seizing the offerings to sexual immorality at the place of worship.

1 Samuel 2:23
Brief Explanation: Eli confronts his sons, asking why they do such things, since he hears of their evil dealings from all the people. Their wickedness has become public knowledge throughout Israel.

1 Samuel 2:24
Brief Explanation: Eli tells his sons that the report he hears is not good and that they are making the LORD’s people transgress. Their sin harms not only themselves but leads God’s people into wrongdoing.

1 Samuel 2:25
Brief Explanation: Eli warns that God may judge when one person wrongs another, but asks who will intercede for a man who sins against the LORD, exposing how grave his sons’ offense has become. They do not heed their father, because the LORD desired to kill them, a judgment on their persistent rebellion that does not remove their responsibility.

1 Samuel 2:26
Brief Explanation: Meanwhile the child Samuel grows in stature and in favor with both the LORD and men. His steady growth stands in sharp contrast to the hardening of Eli’s sons, and Luke describes the boy Jesus in nearly the same words (Luke 2:52), pointing ahead to the Son who would please His Father fully.

1 Samuel 2:27–36 — A Man of God Announces Judgment on Eli’s House

1 Samuel 2:27
Brief Explanation: A man of God comes to Eli with the word of the LORD, who reminds Eli that He clearly revealed Himself to his father’s house when they were in Egypt under Pharaoh. The message of judgment begins by recalling the LORD’s gracious initiative toward Eli’s family.

1 Samuel 2:28
Brief Explanation: The LORD recalls that He chose Eli’s ancestral house out of all the tribes of Israel to be His priests, to offer at His altar, burn incense, and wear the ephod before Him, and that He gave them the offerings made by fire. The priesthood was a privilege God had granted, together with His provision for them.

1 Samuel 2:29
Brief Explanation: The LORD asks why Eli’s house kicks at His sacrifice and offering, and He charges Eli with honoring his sons more than Him and with sharing in the best of the offerings they took from Israel. Eli has given his sons the honor that belongs to God and has grown fat on what they seized.

1 Samuel 2:30
Brief Explanation: The LORD had said that Eli’s house would walk before Him forever, but now He declares, “Far be it from Me.” His principle governs the outcome, “those who honor Me I will honor, and those who despise Me shall be lightly esteemed,” so the earlier word does not secure an unfaithful family’s privileged place.

1 Samuel 2:31
Brief Explanation: The LORD announces that days are coming when He will cut off the arm, the strength, of Eli and his father’s house, so that no man in his house will reach old age. The family that fattened itself on the offerings will be weakened and cut short.

1 Samuel 2:32
Brief Explanation: Eli will see an enemy in the LORD’s dwelling place even while God does good for Israel, and there will never be an old man in his house. Judgment on Eli’s house does not mean the LORD has abandoned His people.

1 Samuel 2:33
Brief Explanation: Those of Eli’s men who are not cut off from the altar will remain only to consume his eyes and grieve his heart, and all his descendants will die in the flower of their age. The judgment will bring continuing sorrow, not a single blow.

1 Samuel 2:34
Brief Explanation: The LORD gives Eli a sign: his two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, will both die in one day. This sign will confirm the certainty of the whole word spoken against his house.

1 Samuel 2:35
Brief Explanation: The LORD promises to raise up for Himself a faithful priest who will act according to what is in His heart and mind, and to build him a sure house that will serve before His anointed king forever. Historically the promise is fulfilled through Zadok’s line (1 Kings 2:27, 35), and in the wider canon it reaches its fullest realization in Christ, the faithful High Priest.

1 Samuel 2:36
Brief Explanation: Everyone left in Eli’s house will come and bow before this priest for a piece of silver and a morsel of bread, pleading for any priestly position just to eat. The house that seized the best of the offerings will be reduced to begging for bread.
