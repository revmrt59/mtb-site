1 Samuel 3:1–10 — The LORD Calls Samuel

1 Samuel 3:1
Brief Explanation: The boy Samuel ministers to the LORD before Eli at a time when the word of the LORD was rare and there was no widespread revelation. This scarcity of God’s word sets the scene for His call to Samuel.

1 Samuel 3:2
Brief Explanation: At that time Eli is lying down in his place, his eyes having grown so dim that he cannot see. His failing sight marks Eli’s age and pictures the spiritual dimness of his leadership, since he had not stopped the corruption in his own household.

1 Samuel 3:3
Brief Explanation: Before the lamp of God goes out in the tabernacle of the LORD, where the ark of God is, Samuel is lying down. The scene is set late in the night within the sanctuary, though the text does not place Samuel in the Most Holy Place or beside the ark.

1 Samuel 3:4
Brief Explanation: The LORD calls Samuel, and the boy answers at once, “Here I am!” God breaks the silence of those days by addressing a young servant by name.

1 Samuel 3:5
Brief Explanation: Samuel runs to Eli, thinking the old priest has called him, but Eli says he did not call and sends him back to lie down. Samuel’s readiness is clear, though he does not yet recognize whose voice he has heard.

1 Samuel 3:6
Brief Explanation: The LORD calls Samuel a second time, and again Samuel goes to Eli, who calls him my son and sends him back. The repeated call shows the LORD’s patience while Samuel still misunderstands.

1 Samuel 3:7
Brief Explanation: The narrator explains that Samuel did not yet know the LORD, because the word of the LORD had not yet been revealed to him. This does not mean Samuel was ungodly like Eli’s sons, who “did not know the LORD” (2:12), but that he had not yet experienced God speaking to him personally.

1 Samuel 3:8
Brief Explanation: When the LORD calls the third time and Samuel again comes to him, Eli perceives that the LORD has been calling the boy. Despite his failures, Eli recognizes God’s work and is able to guide Samuel.

1 Samuel 3:9
Brief Explanation: Eli tells Samuel to lie down and, if he is called again, to answer, “Speak, LORD, for Your servant hears.” His instruction prepares Samuel to respond as a servant ready to listen and obey.

1 Samuel 3:10
Brief Explanation: The LORD comes and stands and calls as at the other times, “Samuel! Samuel!” and Samuel answers, “Speak, for Your servant hears.” The LORD’s coming is a real divine manifestation, though the text does not describe what Samuel saw, and Samuel now answers Him directly.

1 Samuel 3:11–14 — The Word Against Eli’s House

1 Samuel 3:11
Brief Explanation: The LORD tells Samuel that He is about to do something in Israel that will make both ears of everyone who hears it tingle. Samuel’s first revelation is a word of coming judgment.

1 Samuel 3:12
Brief Explanation: In that day the LORD will carry out against Eli everything He has spoken concerning his house, from beginning to end. The word Samuel receives confirms the warning already delivered by the man of God (2:27–36).

1 Samuel 3:13
Brief Explanation: The LORD has told Eli that He will judge his house forever for the iniquity Eli knew about: his sons made themselves vile, and he did not restrain them. Eli had rebuked them, but he failed to stop their wickedness or remove them from their priestly service.

1 Samuel 3:14
Brief Explanation: The LORD has sworn to the house of Eli that its iniquity will not be atoned for by sacrifice or offering forever. This oath makes the judgment on Eli’s house irrevocable; it does not teach that forgiveness is closed to everyone who has sinned grievously.

1 Samuel 3:15–18 — Samuel Tells Eli Everything

1 Samuel 3:15
Brief Explanation: Samuel lies down until morning and then opens the doors of the house of the LORD, continuing his ordinary duties. He is afraid to tell Eli the vision he has received.

1 Samuel 3:16
Brief Explanation: Eli calls him, “Samuel, my son!” and Samuel answers, “Here I am.” The same readiness Samuel showed when he was called in the night now meets the hard task of facing Eli.

1 Samuel 3:17
Brief Explanation: Eli asks what the LORD has spoken and binds Samuel with a solemn oath not to hide anything from him. Eli insists on hearing the full word, however painful it may be.

1 Samuel 3:18
Brief Explanation: Samuel tells Eli everything and hides nothing, and Eli responds, “It is the LORD. Let Him do what seems good to Him.” Samuel proves a faithful messenger, and Eli submits to the LORD’s judgment, though his submission does not undo his earlier failure.

1 Samuel 3:19–21 — Samuel Established as a Prophet of the LORD

1 Samuel 3:19
Brief Explanation: Samuel grows, and the LORD is with him and lets none of his words fall to the ground. God Himself confirms Samuel’s words by bringing them to pass.

1 Samuel 3:20
Brief Explanation: All Israel, from Dan in the north to Beersheba in the south, knows that Samuel has been established as a prophet of the LORD. His recognition as God’s prophet becomes national.

1 Samuel 3:21
Brief Explanation: The LORD appears again in Shiloh and reveals Himself to Samuel there by the word of the LORD. The chapter that began with God’s word rare now ends with His word being revealed again.
