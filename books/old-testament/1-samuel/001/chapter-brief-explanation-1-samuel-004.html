1 Samuel 4:1–4 — Israel Defeated and the Ark Brought from Shiloh

1 Samuel 4:1
Brief Explanation: The word of Samuel comes to all Israel, and Israel goes out to battle against the Philistines, camping beside Ebenezer while the Philistines camp at Aphek. The opening clause closes the account of Samuel’s establishment as prophet, and the rest of the verse sets the scene for a new crisis.

1 Samuel 4:2
Brief Explanation: The Philistines draw up in battle array, and Israel is defeated, losing about four thousand men in the field. This first defeat raises the question of why the LORD has allowed it.

1 Samuel 4:3
Brief Explanation: The elders rightly ask why the LORD has defeated them, but instead of seeking Him they decide to bring the ark of the covenant from Shiloh so that it may save them from their enemies. They treat the ark as a means of securing victory, and the text records no command from the LORD to bring it.

1 Samuel 4:4
Brief Explanation: The people bring from Shiloh the ark of the covenant of the LORD of hosts, who dwells between the cherubim, and Eli’s sons Hophni and Phinehas come with it. The ark was truly bound up with God’s holy presence among His people, yet it arrives in the care of the very priests under His judgment.

1 Samuel 4:5–11 — The Ark Captured and Eli’s Sons Killed

1 Samuel 4:5
Brief Explanation: When the ark of the covenant of the LORD comes into the camp, all Israel shouts so loudly that the earth shakes. Their celebration expresses confidence that the ark will secure victory, a confidence the coming defeat will expose as misplaced.

1 Samuel 4:6
Brief Explanation: The Philistines hear the great shout from the camp of the Hebrews and learn that the ark of the LORD has arrived. Israel’s celebration carries across to the enemy camp.

1 Samuel 4:7
Brief Explanation: The Philistines are afraid, crying that God has come into the camp and that nothing like this has ever happened before. They sense that they now face something more than an army.

1 Samuel 4:8
Brief Explanation: In their alarm the Philistines ask who will deliver them from “these mighty gods,” who struck the Egyptians with plagues in the wilderness. Their words recall Israel’s deliverance from Egypt in confused terms shaped by their own belief in many gods, and Scripture records their speech without endorsing it.

1 Samuel 4:9
Brief Explanation: Instead of surrendering, the Philistines urge one another to be strong and fight like men, so that they will not become servants of the Hebrews as the Hebrews had been to them. Their fear drives them to fight harder.

1 Samuel 4:10
Brief Explanation: The Philistines fight, Israel is defeated, and every man flees to his tent, with thirty thousand foot soldiers falling. The ark’s presence does not prevent a far greater slaughter than the first defeat.

1 Samuel 4:11
Brief Explanation: The ark of God is captured, and Eli’s two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, die, just as the LORD had announced (2:34). The capture of the ark is the unfolding of God’s judgment on Eli’s house, not a defeat of God Himself.

1 Samuel 4:12–18 — Eli Hears the News and Dies

1 Samuel 4:12
Brief Explanation: A man of Benjamin runs from the battle line that same day and comes to Shiloh with his clothes torn and dirt on his head. His appearance announces disaster before he says a word.

1 Samuel 4:13
Brief Explanation: Eli sits on a seat by the wayside watching, because his heart trembles for the ark of God, and when the man tells the news in the city, the whole city cries out. Eli’s deepest concern is for the ark.

1 Samuel 4:14
Brief Explanation: Eli hears the outcry and asks what the tumult means, and the man hurries to tell him. The news Eli has anxiously awaited now comes directly to him.

1 Samuel 4:15
Brief Explanation: Eli is ninety-eight years old, and his eyes are so dim that he cannot see. The note explains why he must ask about the noise and depend entirely on the messenger’s report.

1 Samuel 4:16
Brief Explanation: The man tells Eli that he has come from the battle and fled that very day from the battle line, and Eli asks, “What happened, my son?” The aged priest presses for the full report.

1 Samuel 4:17
Brief Explanation: The messenger reports the disaster in rising order: Israel has fled, there has been a great slaughter, Eli’s two sons are dead, and the ark of God has been captured. The last and heaviest news is the loss of the ark.

1 Samuel 4:18
Brief Explanation: At the mention of the ark of God, Eli falls backward off his seat beside the gate, breaks his neck, and dies, for he was old and heavy. His forty years of judging Israel end on the day the ark is lost.

1 Samuel 4:19–22 — Ichabod: The Glory Has Departed

1 Samuel 4:19
Brief Explanation: Phinehas’s wife, who is near childbirth, hears that the ark of God is captured and that her father-in-law and husband are dead, and her labor suddenly comes upon her. The shock of the news brings on the birth.

1 Samuel 4:20
Brief Explanation: As she is dying, the women standing by her try to encourage her with the news that she has borne a son, but she does not answer or take it to heart. Even the birth of a son cannot lighten the weight of the catastrophe.

1 Samuel 4:21
Brief Explanation: She names the child Ichabod, saying, “The glory has departed from Israel!” because the ark of God has been captured and because of the deaths of her father-in-law and husband. The name mourns the loss of the ark together with the deaths in Eli’s house.

1 Samuel 4:22
Brief Explanation: She repeats her lament, “The glory has departed from Israel, for the ark of God has been captured,” now naming the ark alone. Her words truly express the catastrophe of the glory lost with the ark, without meaning that the LORD has been defeated or has permanently abandoned His people.
