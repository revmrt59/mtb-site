1 Samuel 5:1–5 — Dagon Falls Before the Ark

1 Samuel 5:1
Brief Explanation: The Philistines take the captured ark of God and bring it from Ebenezer to Ashdod. The ark now enters Philistine territory as a prize of war.

1 Samuel 5:2
Brief Explanation: They bring the ark of God into the house of Dagon and set it beside their god. Placing it there presents Israel’s God as though He were a captive trophy in Dagon’s house.

1 Samuel 5:3
Brief Explanation: Early the next morning the people of Ashdod find Dagon fallen on its face to the earth before the ark of the LORD, and they must set it back in its place. The idol lies prostrate before the LORD and depends on its worshipers to stand again.

1 Samuel 5:4
Brief Explanation: The following morning Dagon has fallen again before the ark, and its head and both palms lie broken off on the threshold, leaving only its torso. The second fall is a decisive humiliation, and the idol’s severed hands stand in contrast to the powerful hand of the LORD that follows.

1 Samuel 5:5
Brief Explanation: For this reason the priests of Dagon and all who enter his house do not tread on the threshold of Dagon in Ashdod to the writer’s day. The lasting custom preserves the memory of Dagon’s humiliation before the LORD.

1 Samuel 5:6–12 — The Hand of the LORD Heavy on the Philistine Cities

1 Samuel 5:6
Brief Explanation: The hand of the LORD is heavy on the people of Ashdod; He ravages them and strikes them with tumors, both the city and its territory. The LORD Himself is the agent of this judgment, acting without any Israelite army.

1 Samuel 5:7
Brief Explanation: The men of Ashdod recognize that the hand of the God of Israel is harsh against them and against Dagon their god, and they insist that His ark must not remain with them. Their answer is to send the ark away, even as they go on calling Dagon their god.

1 Samuel 5:8
Brief Explanation: They gather all the lords of the Philistines to ask what to do with the ark of the God of Israel, and the council decides to carry it to Gath. Relocating the ark carries the crisis into another Philistine city.

1 Samuel 5:9
Brief Explanation: After the ark is carried to Gath, the hand of the LORD comes against that city with very great destruction, striking its men, small and great, with tumors. Moving the ark does not escape the LORD’s judgment but only extends it.

1 Samuel 5:10
Brief Explanation: The ark of God is sent on to Ekron, and as it arrives the Ekronites cry out that it has been brought to kill them and their people. Their immediate alarm suggests they already knew what the ark had brought on Ashdod and Gath.

1 Samuel 5:11
Brief Explanation: The lords of the Philistines are gathered again, and the people plead that the ark of the God of Israel be sent back to its own place so that it does not kill them, for deadly destruction fills the city and the hand of God is very heavy there. The repeated judgment forces the Philistines to give up the trophy they had taken.

1 Samuel 5:12
Brief Explanation: The men who do not die are stricken with tumors, and the cry of the city goes up to heaven. The chapter ends with the Philistines overwhelmed under the LORD’s hand.
