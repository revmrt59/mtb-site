We are a House of Prayer

Culture Statement

Jesus identified His Father’s house as a House of Prayer. We assemble to seek the heart of the Father and partner with Him in faith, inquiry, intercession, supplication, petition, lament, and thanksgiving. We contend for mercy, righteousness, healing, and justice, believing that God transforms lives, cities, and nations through a praying Church.

Living It in 1 Samuel 1

Hannah brings her deepest sorrow directly before the Lord. At Shiloh, “she was in bitterness of soul, and prayed to the LORD and wept in anguish” (1 Samuel 1:10). Her prayer includes lament, petition, surrender, and ultimately thanksgiving and worship. She does not hide her anguish or allow it to drive her away from God. She pours out her soul before Him. Even before Samuel is conceived, something changes in Hannah: after praying and receiving Eli’s blessing, “her face was no longer sad” (1 Samuel 1:18). Prayer has brought her burden into the presence and care of God before the answer has appeared.

This gives real substance to being a House of Prayer. Prayer is not merely something the church schedules. It is the life of a people who bring their need, grief, desire, and future before the Lord because they believe He hears. Hannah’s prayer also reaches beyond personal relief. The son she asks for is given back to God for His purposes. God answers one woman’s anguished prayer in a way that will eventually affect an entire nation. A praying church learns to bring everything before God while remaining open to the larger purposes He may accomplish through the answer.

Vision

Culture Statement

East Gate exists to teach you how to hear God’s voice, connect with Him daily and walk with Him in obedience.

Living It in 1 Samuel 1

Hear. Connect. Obey. Hannah heard God as she prayed, connected with His heart and purpose for the son He would give her, and obeyed by giving Samuel wholly back to Him. Her obedience reached far beyond her own life. The child she surrendered became the prophet through whom God spoke to Israel and anointed David as king. One woman heard God, came into agreement with His heart, and obeyed, and through her obedience God helped change the course of a nation.

At East Gate, that is not a one-time moment; it is a rhythm we return to daily, the way Hannah’s answer did not arrive the day she prayed. It looks like keeping a daily Secret Place where we actually listen, not just bring requests. It looks like testing what we sense against Scripture and the people who know us well, the way both Elkanah and Eli had a part in confirming what Hannah had heard. And it looks like obeying even when it costs us something over years, not only when it is easy. Hannah’s obedience did not end when Samuel was born; it carried through the nursing years, the weaning, and the morning she walked him to Shiloh and left him there. Walking with God often looks the same in that it requires a long obedience and not just a single response.

HONOR || Through His Eyes

Culture Statement

Relationships are protected when we ascribe heaven’s value to the people around us. Like Jesus, we choose to interact with people, not as they deserve, but according to their God-given value.

Living It in 1 Samuel 1

Being wrongly judged does not give us permission to dishonor someone else. Hannah answered Eli firmly but respectfully (1:15–16). She could have exposed the failures in his own household or answered his accusation with contempt. Instead, she told him the truth without dishonoring him. Our response to someone else’s failure says as much about our own heart as it does about theirs.

Communication

Culture Statement

We “real talk” because we desire real relationships built on trust. We have 100% of our conversations, choosing to be authentic, honest, and open with one another. Awkward today, stronger tomorrow.

Living It in 1 Samuel 1

Hannah had every reason to stay quiet, or to answer Eli with an edge. He had just accused her, in the middle of her grief, of being drunk. Instead she told him the plain truth — “I have not been drinking... I was pouring out my soul before the LORD” — clearly, but without contempt (1:15–16). That is what real talk looks like when it is done well. Honest communication does not have to come out sharp, and grace does not require us to stay silent. At East Gate, when someone in leadership misjudges us or gets the wrong idea about our heart, we choose to handle it maturely, having 100% of the conversation with that person. We do not stay silent and let it fester in our heart. We do not complain to someone else. We communicate, plainly, and humbly the way Hannah did with a man who held real authority over her. Awkward conversations handled with humility instead of silence or sharpness are what actually build trust. That is what makes us stronger tomorrow.

We Empower Women

Culture Statement

We actively endorse and promote godly women in ministry, creating opportunities for them to step into their God-given calling. We empower women to fulfill the biblical purpose and destiny God has placed on their lives, equipping them to lead, serve, and advance His kingdom.

Living It in 1 Samuel 1

Hannah stands at the center of this chapter’s movement. She seeks God, pours out her soul before Him, hears God’s heart, makes a solemn vow, receives His answer by faith, bears Samuel, and faithfully presents him to the Lord. The future Samuel is not introduced first through a king, priest, or national leader, but through the prayer, faith, and obedience of a woman who takes God seriously. Her husband recognizes the importance of what she has heard from the Lord as he tells her, “Do what seems best to you” (1 Samuel 1:23). When the time comes, Hannah herself brings Samuel to the house of the Lord and testifies concerning the child for whom she prayed.

That gives biblical substance to a culture that makes room for godly women to follow the calling God places on their lives. At East Gate, that means watching for the Hannahs among us — women marked by depth in prayer, discernment, and obedience — and inviting them into real ministry, not just supporting roles behind the scenes: teaching, leading intercession, shepherding, and carrying spiritual authority as God has gifted them. It also asks something of the Elkanahs among us. When a woman senses God calling her to something costly, the leader who says, “Do what seems best to you,” and actually makes room for it, is living this out too. Empowering women recognizes that God works through women whose faith, prayer, obedience, and ability to hear His voice, become instruments of His purposes.
