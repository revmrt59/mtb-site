Central Message

The Lord hears Hannah in her barrenness, gives her Samuel, and receives the child she willingly gives back to Him. Through Hannah’s prayer, faith, and surrender, God begins His provision of a faithful servant through whom He will speak to Israel.

What This Chapter Is About

1 Samuel opens with Elkanah’s household and its yearly worship at Shiloh. Peninnah has children, while Hannah has none. Peninnah repeatedly provokes Hannah over her childlessness. Elkanah loves Hannah, but he cannot remove her grief.

During one visit to Shiloh, Hannah brings her sorrow directly before the LORD. She weeps, prays earnestly, and vows that if God gives her a son, she will give him to the LORD for his entire life. Eli the priest initially mistakes her silent prayer for drunkenness, but after hearing her explanation, he blesses her and sends her away in peace.

The LORD remembers Hannah, and in time she bears Samuel, the son she asked for. She remains home to nurse him until he is weaned, then brings him with offerings to Shiloh. Before Eli she identifies Samuel as the child for whom she prayed and gives him to the LORD for lifelong service. The chapter ends in worship.

The Heart of the Chapter

The heart of 1 Samuel 1 is the meeting of Hannah’s anguish over her barrenness with the larger purpose of God for Israel. The LORD had closed her womb, and her rival Peninnah continued to provoke her and deepen her sorrow.

Despite years of deep sorrow and relentless provocation, Hannah brings her pain directly to the LORD rather than allowing it to embitter her. At Shiloh, she pours out her soul to the Lord, begging for a son and promising that the child will belong to the LORD all his life.

The LORD answers Hannah's prayer with the birth of Samuel. Once he is weaned, she faithfully returns to Shiloh to surrender her child to the LORD for lifelong service.

Ultimately, what begins as one woman’s desperate cry becomes the catalyst for God’s provision for His nation. Samuel is not just the answer to Hannah's barrenness; he is the future prophet through whom God will restore His voice to Israel. The chapter beautifully transitions from personal grief to national redemption, and from the joy of receiving to the ultimate act of surrender.

Why This Chapter Matters

1 Samuel 1 establishes the circumstances surrounding Samuel’s birth and consecration to God. Samuel will serve at a crucial time in Israel’s history, but his story begins not with political power or public prominence but with the prayer of a distressed woman before the LORD.

The Lord had closed Hannah’s womb, and later He remembered her. The latter does not mean He previously forgot her; He acts on her behalf and grants her particular petition. The chapter does not give a single explanation for every experience of infertility or promise that every request will receive the same answer.

Hannah’s response is equally important. She understands Samuel as received from God and therefore willingly entrusts him back to God. Her faith is expressed not only in asking but also in keeping her word after the answer comes. The chapter presents worship as more than receiving from God. It includes recognizing that His gifts ultimately belong to Him and can be placed wholly at His disposal.

Samuel’s presentation at Shiloh begins his life in service to the LORD. In a setting whose priestly failures become plain in the following chapter, the birth and consecration of this child anticipate God’s provision of a faithful voice for Israel.

Key Themes to Watch For

Prayer in distress - Hannah brings her anguish directly before the LORD rather than allowing her pain to remain only inward. Her prayer is deeply personal, earnest, and specific.

The LORD remembers - God responds to Hannah’s affliction and prayer. His remembering means that He acts faithfully and graciously toward her.

Barrenness and divine provision - The LORD had closed Hannah’s womb, and the LORD remembered her. Her story displays how God uses barrenness to bring forth His purposes in our lives.

Surrender to God - Hannah asks for a son but vows to give him back to the LORD. Her answered prayer leads to consecration rather than possession.

Faithful vows - Hannah fulfills what she promised. Her devotion is demonstrated by obedience after her prayer has been answered.

Worship through sorrow and surrender - Hannah continued going to Shiloh despite her grief, worshiped before conceiving, and returned with Samuel in fulfillment of her vow.

A private prayer within Israel’s history - Samuel will later serve as prophet and judge, but the beginning of his story is Hannah’s earnest prayer and faithful vow.

Mistaken judgment and discernment - Eli mistakes Hannah’s silent prayer for drunkenness, then listens and blesses her. The next chapter reveals serious disorder in his priestly household.

Key Words & Concepts

Shiloh - The principal place of Israelite worship at this time, where the tabernacle and priestly ministry were located. Elkanah’s family regularly went there to worship and sacrifice.

Barrenness - The inability to bear children. In the social world of ancient Israel, barrenness could bring profound grief and humiliation, especially when contrasted with another woman’s fertility.

Vow - A solemn promise made to God. Hannah promises that if God gives her a son, she will give him to the LORD for his entire life.

“No razor shall come upon his head” - The language recalls a sign of Nazirite dedication (Numbers 6:5). The chapter mentions this sign of Samuel’s lifelong devotion, though it does not explicitly call him a Nazirite or list every provision of a Nazirite vow.

“Poured out my soul before the LORD” - Hannah’s description of her prayer. She is openly laying her grief, longing, and distress before God.

“The LORD remembered her” - Biblical language describing God acting on behalf of someone according to His faithful care. It does not mean that God had previously forgotten Hannah.

Samuel - Hannah connects his name with her asking him from the LORD. The narrative emphasizes that his life began as the answer to her prayer and was then devoted to God.

“Lent to the LORD” - Hannah’s wording echoes her asking for the child. “As long as he lives” clarifies that his dedication is lifelong, not a temporary loan.
