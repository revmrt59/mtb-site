ENEMIES - (ʼôyêb, Strong's H341)

OYEB - ( oyeb, Strong’s [H0341] )

Summary:

The word refers to one who manifests hostility, opposition, or enmity toward another, functioning as an adversary or foe. In 1 Samuel 2:1, Hannah rejoices because the Lord has turned the tables on her adversaries, bringing vindication where she once faced mockery and opposition.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew term denotes an active adversary or personal foe. In ancient biblical culture, enmity often involved tangible social conflict, military opposition, or targeted hostility from rivals. The word paints a picture of active opposition, whether from foreign nations on the battlefield or local adversaries within the community. In Hannah's setting, her adversary was Peninnah, who provoked her bitterly regarding her barrenness, alongside the broader cultural stigma associated with childlessness in ancient Israel.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to anyone who acts with hostility or opposition toward another person or group. When used in a personal or national sense, it identifies an active opponent, rival, or enemy. It describes both individual adversaries within a community and military foes arrayed against Israel. In 1 Samuel 2:1, the masculine plural participle form describes those who actively oppose or gloat over another, functioning here as opponents whose mouths were ultimately silenced by God's intervention.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the term across historical narratives, legal texts, and poetry to describe those who seek harm, promote division, or oppose God's people. It frequently appears in the Psalms, where the writers cry out for deliverance from those who surround them with malice. The usage spans personal conflicts, royal rivalries, and national warfare. The focus remains on the active stance of hostility taken by one party against another.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of adversaries in the biblical text highlights the fallen, fractured nature of human relationships and the opposition God's people frequently face. Throughout Scripture, the ultimate resolution of enmity belongs to God. When human adversaries rise up in pride, God remains the defender of the afflicted. Hannah's song anchors deliverance not in human retaliation or strength, but in the sovereign action of the Lord who humbles the proud and raises the lowly.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:1, Hannah prays after delivering Samuel to the tabernacle in fulfillment of her vow. Her heart rejoices in the Lord because her horn is exalted, and her mouth is enlarged over her enemies. The immediate context contrasts her previous humiliation with her present vindication. The adversaries who once mocked her barrenness now stand answered by God's gracious gift of a son and the establishment of His prophetic servant. Her joy is not mean-spirited gloating, but a profound recognition that God has decisively intervened to overturn the reproach cast upon her by her opponents.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 22:17 — God promises Abraham that his descendants shall possess the gate of their enemies, pointing to future national security and victory.

  • Deuteronomy 28:7 — The Lord promises to defeat the enemies who rise up against His obedient people, causing them to flee in different directions.

  • Psalms 3:7 — David cries out to God to strike all his enemies on the cheek-bone, expressing trust in divine deliverance amid desperate personal danger.

  • Lamentations 1:9 — The writer laments Jerusalem’s deep desolation, noting how the enemy has magnified himself over the stricken city.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The posture of the worshipper in 1 Samuel 2:1 shifts away from the pressure of human opposition toward the triumph of God's character. By opening her song with a declaration concerning her adversaries, Hannah highlights the stark contrast between human hostility and divine salvation. The defeat of the foe clears the way for pure praise directed solely toward the Lord.
