EXALTED - (rûwm, Strong's H7311)

WORD - ( rum, Strong’s [H7311] )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb rum generally means to rise, be high, be lifted up, or be exalted. In 1 Samuel 2:1, Hannah declares that her horn is exalted in the Lord, using the word to describe how God has lifted her up from humiliation and distress, restoring her dignity and strength through His sovereign deliverance.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew root behind the translation "exalted" paints a vivid physical picture of rising, climbing, or being raised high above surrounding obstacles. In the Old Testament, this movement can describe physical structures rising, waters swelling, or living beings lifting themselves up. When applied to a person’s status or standing, it describes a transition from a lowly or oppressed condition to a position of honor, strength, and security.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to literal elevation, such as lifting an object or building a structure high. It can also describe a figurative elevation, where a person, nation, or voice is lifted up in victory, praise, or status. When used in contexts of human experience, it points to the restoration of honor, the granting of strength, or the public vindication of someone who was previously brought low.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses this verb in various ways depending on the subject. Sometimes people lift up their hands in prayer or raise their voices in praise. At other times, God lifts up the needy from the dust or exalts a faithful servant. The nuance often depends on the contrast between human pride—which God brings down—and divine rescue, where God alone lifts the humble out of despair.

4. Theological Implications

The Bible consistently teaches that true honor and exaltation do not come from human achievement or self-promotion. When individuals are lifted up in a positive sense before God, it is always the result of divine grace and intervention. God delights in reversing human fortunes, casting down the arrogant, and raising up the lowly so that all glory remains His.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:1, Hannah bursts forth in a song of profound gratitude after the birth of Samuel. For years, she endured barrenness and the bitter taunts of her rival, feeling utterly humiliated and helpless. When God answers her desperate prayer, her entire status changes. Her declaration that her "horn"—a traditional biblical symbol of strength, dignity, and victory—is exalted means that God has rescued her from disgrace and given her renewed strength. She does not boast in her own ability or perseverance; she rejoices directly in the Lord, whose salvation turned her mourning into triumph.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 15:2 — Moses and the Israelites sing of the Lord, declaring, "He is my God, and I will prepare Him an habitation; my father's God, and I will exalt Him," lifting high the praises of God for His mighty deliverance at the Red Sea.

  • Nehemiah 9:5 — The Levites lead the assembly in praise, calling the people to stand up and bless the Lord their God, whose glorious name is "exalted above all blessing and praise."

  • Psalms 89:17 — The psalmist acknowledges that God is the glory of His people's strength, noting that "in Your favor our horn is exalted," linking human dignity and triumph entirely to God's gracious pleasure.

  • Psalms 140:8 — The psalmist prays that the wicked may not have their way, asking that their wicked plots not be "exalted," lest they become proud or successful in their opposition to the righteous.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's song serves as a foundational theological template in Scripture, anticipating later passages like Mary's Magnificat in the New Testament. Both women recognize that God actively notices the lowly, breaks the power of the proud, and lifts up those who depend entirely upon Him. The verb reminds readers that the vertical movement of life—from sorrow to joy, from weakness to strength—is directed by the hand of God.
