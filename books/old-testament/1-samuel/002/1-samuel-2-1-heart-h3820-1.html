HEART - (lêb, Strong's H3820)

HEB - ( leb, Strong’s [H3820] )

Summary:

The word refers to the inner person, encompassing the mind, emotions, and will. In 1 Samuel 2:1, when Hannah says her heart rejoices in the Lord, she is describing her entire inner being overflowing with deep gladness and triumphant joy because of God's personal rescue and steadfast salvation.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In biblical Hebrew thought, the heart represents the control center of human existence. It is not merely the seat of passing emotions, but the core of a person where thoughts are formed, decisions are made, and inner life is experienced. When biblical writers use this term, they are looking past external appearances straight to the private, authentic reality of an individual's thoughts, motives, and emotional responses before God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to the physical organ in ancient anatomical terms, but in biblical usage, it almost always points to the immaterial inner self. It is where a person thinks, feels, and chooses. When the word is used of human beings, it describes the whole inner life—the place where human consciousness interacts with God. In Hannah's song, the heart is the deep reservoir of her personal identity that has moved from bitter grief to overflowing praise.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses this term across a wide range of human experiences. Sometimes the heart is described as troubled, fearful, or hardened in rebellion. At other times, as in Hannah's song, it is lifted, strengthened, and made glad. The word frequently stands in contrast to outward appearance; while people look at the face or the actions, God looks directly at the heart. This makes the inner person the true arena of genuine worship, repentance, and trust.

4. Theological Implications

The inner person is the primary place where God works and where humans respond to Him. Scripture teaches that the inner life of every person is fully open and known to God. Because the heart holds the faculties of thought, emotion, and will, true faith cannot remain merely external or ritualistic. God claims the innermost thoughts and affections of His people, transforming human grief and weakness into a source of enduring praise directed solely toward Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:1, Hannah is standing before the Lord in profound relief and joy after years of barrenness, heartache, and public taunting. Her prayer is not a detached or formal poem; it is the immediate eruption of a redeemed inner life. By declaring that her heart rejoices in the Lord, she shows that her entire emotional and mental landscape has been utterly transformed. Her joy is anchored not in her circumstances, but in the character and salvation of God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 6:5 — "Then the Lord saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every intent of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually."

  • Proverbs 23:12 — "Apply your heart to instruction, and your ears to words of knowledge."

  • Jeremiah 13:10 — "This evil people, who refuse to hear my words, who follow the dictates of their hearts, and walk after other gods to serve them and worship them, shall just be like this sash which is profitable for nothing."

  • Psalms 36:10 — "Oh, continue Your lovingkindness to those who know You, and Your righteousness to the upright in heart."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The transition from sorrow to joy in Hannah’s life highlights how deeply God engages with human emotion and experience. Her prayer demonstrates that biblical praise often rises from the ashes of personal pain when God intervenes. The inner person, once broken by despair, becomes the instrument through which God's strength and holiness are proclaimed to others.
