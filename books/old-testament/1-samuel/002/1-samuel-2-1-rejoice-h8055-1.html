REJOICE - (sâmach, Strong's H8055)

WORD - ( samach, Strong’s [H8055] )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb can mean to rejoice, be glad, or be joyful, expressing a deep inner gladness that often overflows into outward celebration or thanksgiving. In 1 Samuel 2:1, Hannah uses this word to declare that her heart rejoices in the Lord, shifting her focus away from personal sorrow and human enemies to find her ultimate delight in God's salvation and sovereign character.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew language uses several terms related to joy and gladness, and this particular verb captures the emotional and physical response of gladness. In ancient Israelite life, such joy was rarely a private, silent emotion; it frequently accompanied communal festivals, answered prayers, and decisive acts of divine deliverance. When individuals or the nation experienced God's rescue, this verb described the sudden lifting of heaviness and the resulting expression of gladness.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to experiencing gladness, joy, or rejoicing. When a person is the subject, it describes an inward feeling of delight that wells up in response to favorable circumstances, restored relationships, or, most importantly, divine intervention. The term does not inherently specify a trivial happiness or a fleeting mood; rather, it often points to a settled, exuberant gladness rooted in external realities, especially the goodness and deliverance of God.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, the word appears in varied settings ranging from daily domestic life to grand national celebrations. It often describes the emotional posture of people who have received unexpected grace, provision, or victory. While humans often rejoice over physical blessings or agricultural harvests, the highest expressions of this gladness occur when worshippers recognize the active hand of God in their lives. The joy described by the term is visible, heartfelt, and directed either toward gracious human relationships or toward God Himself.

4. Theological Implications

True joy, as portrayed by the use of this term in Scripture, is intimately connected to who God is and what He has done. When believers rejoice, their gladness is not generated merely by favorable life circumstances, but by the character, faithfulness, and saving acts of God. This joy serves as a spiritual anchor, reminding the faithful that ultimate security rests in the Lord rather than in human strength, military might, or material abundance.

5. Contextual Nuance in Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:1, Hannah stands before the tabernacle offering a profound prayer of thanksgiving after the birth of Samuel. Having previously lived under the deep social stigma of barrenness and the bitter taunts of her rival, Hannah's life is utterly transformed. By declaring that her heart rejoices in the Lord, she explicitly refuses to locate her joy merely in the gift of a son, but rather in the Giver. Her gladness is framed by the reality of God's attentiveness to the lowly, demonstrating that true spiritual triumph looks past immediate circumstances to praise the Lord for His incomparable holiness and salvation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Deuteronomy 16:14 — And you shall rejoice in your feast, you and your son and your daughter, your male servant and your female servant, the Levite, the stranger and the fatherless and the widow who are within your gates.

  • Psalms 34:2 — My soul shall make its boast in the Lord; the humble shall hear of it and be glad.

  • Proverbs 23:15 — My son, if your heart is wise, my heart will rejoice—yes, mine!

  • Isaiah 25:9 — And it will be said in that day: “Behold, this is our God; We have waited for Him, and He will save us. This is the Lord; We have waited for Him; We will be glad and rejoice in His salvation.”

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's prayer serves as a foundational theological template for later biblical songs, including the Magnificat of Mary in the New Testament. The transition from silent, bitter weeping in chapter one to exuberant rejoicing in chapter two illustrates how God routinely reverses human fortunes. The joy expressed here is robust enough to withstand past suffering because it is anchored in the unchanging character of God.
