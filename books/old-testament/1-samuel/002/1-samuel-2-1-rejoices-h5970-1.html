WORD - ( `alaz` , Strong’s `H5970` )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb alaz expresses exuberant gladness, exultation, or triumphant rejoicing. In 1 Samuel 2:1, Hannah uses this verb at the climax of her prayer of thanksgiving, declaring that her heart exults in the Lord. Here, the word describes a profound, unrestrained outburst of joy that arises not from changing circumstances, but from the personal character and salvation of God.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

  • The Hebrew root alaz points to a vibrant, physical, and emotional expression of joy. In biblical Hebrew, expressions of gladness often involve vocal praise, dancing, or leaping. This background helps explain why the term is frequently linked to public celebration, victory, and corporate worship in ancient Israel. The word describes a gladness that cannot be contained quietly within the heart, but breaks forth in visible triumph and praise.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The word can refer to shouting for joy, exulting, or triumphing.

  • When a person is the subject, it describes a state of exuberant delight or cheerful confidence.

  • When a nation or city is the subject, it often points to public celebration or victory.

  • In poetic and prophetic literature, the term frequently captures the emotional height of deliverance where sorrow is completely swallowed up by God's triumphant intervention.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

  • Scripture uses this verb in contexts of profound rescue, divine victory, and restored fortunes. It appears when God turns a situation of shame, oppression, or defeat into an occasion of unashamed praise. The nuance is rarely a quiet, passive contentment; instead, it carries the active posture of someone standing on the winning side of a battle, lifting their voice in victory because the Lord has acted.

4. Theological Implications

  • The theological weight of the word rests on its object. True exultation in Scripture is directed toward God Himself rather than human strength, military might, or material wealth. When Hannah and the psalmists exult, their joy is anchored entirely in the holiness, righteousness, and saving acts of God. The term reminds readers that genuine spiritual triumph is a response to divine grace and sovereign intervention.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In 1 Samuel 2:1, Hannah stands before the tabernacle offering a song of praise after years of barrenness, social humiliation, and deep personal grief. When she sings that her heart exults in the Lord, the word captures the sudden, overwhelming reversal of her sorrow. Her barrenness has ended through the birth of Samuel, but her joy looks past the child to the Giver. The verse sets the tone for the entire book of Samuel, establishing that God humbles the proud and raises the lowly, giving His people ample reason to triumph in Him.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Chronicles 16:32 — "Let the field be joyful, and all that is in it. Then all the trees of the woods will rejoice before the Lord"

  • Psalms 9:2 — "I will be glad and rejoice in You; I will sing praise to Your name, O Most High."

  • Psalms 68:3 — "But let the righteous be glad; Let them rejoice before God; Yes, let them rejoice exceedingly."

  • Proverbs 11:10 — "When it goes well with the righteous, the city rejoices; And when the wicked perish, there is jubilation."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The verb often appears in poetic parallelism alongside terms for rejoicing, shouting, and being glad, reinforcing the sense of unrestrained praise. In prophetic passages, the same root is sometimes used ironically when earthly powers rejoice prematurely, highlighting the contrast between arrogant human boasting and the lasting triumph of God's righteous kingdom.
