ENDS - (ʼepheç, Strong's H657)

  • ( , Strong’s [H0657] )

Summary:

The word primarily points to an extremity, boundary, or the farthest limit of something, such as the distant parts of the earth. In 1 Samuel 2:10, translated as "ends," it refers to the utmost borders of the inhabited world. The word contributes to Hannah’s prophetic declaration by emphasizing the universal scope of the Lord's sovereign rule, reaching to the very edges of creation.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The term stems from a root meaning to cease, come to an end, or stop, which naturally leads to the idea of a boundary or extremity where something terminates. In ancient Hebrew geography and poetry, the "ends" of the earth represented the farthest frontiers known to humanity. When applied to the earth, the word does not imply a flat surface with physical edges in a modern scientific sense, but rather pictures the absolute limits of the created order, stretching as far as human habitation and creation extend.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to an extremity, a limit, or the final boundary of a surface or region. It frequently appears in poetic and prophetic literature to designate the farthest geographic boundaries, such as the ends of the earth. When used in this spatial sense, it encompasses everything up to the very margin of the world, leaving no place outside its reach.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture often employs the word to stress universality. When linked with the earth, it highlights that God's authority, judgment, or salvation is not localized or restricted to a single nation. Instead, the term sweeps across the entire horizon, indicating that the farthest peoples and most distant lands fall within the sphere of divine concern and governance.

4. Theological Implications

The usage of the word in poetic and prophetic contexts underscores the absolute sovereignty of God over all creation. Because God's power extends to the utmost boundaries of the earth, no territory, ruler, or individual is hidden from His dominion or exempt from His righteous rule.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:10, Hannah prays at the tabernacle after delivering Samuel to the Lord's service. Her song celebrates God's reversal of human fortunes—lifting the humble and breaking His adversaries. By declaring that the Lord shall judge the "ends" of the earth, Hannah anchors her prophetic vision in the universal extent of God's kingdom. The verse moves from local vindication in Israel to a sweeping declaration that Yahweh's judicial authority spans the entire globe.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Psalms 22:27 — "All the ends of the world shall remember and turn to the Lord, and all the families of the nations shall worship before You."

  • Psalms 98:3 — "All the ends of the earth have seen the salvation of our God."

  • Isaiah 52:10 — "The Lord has made bare His holy arm in the eyes of all the nations; and all the ends of the earth shall see the salvation of our God."

  • Jeremiah 16:19 — "O Lord, my strength and my fortress, my refuge in the day of affliction, the Gentiles shall come to You from the ends of the earth and say, 'Surely our fathers have inherited lies, worthlessness and things wherein there is no profit.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The word frequently pairs with nations, families, or the earth itself to create a comprehensive picture of humanity. It bridges the gap between local devotion and cosmic reality, reminding readers that the God of Israel is simultaneously the Creator and Judge of all mankind.
