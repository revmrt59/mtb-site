LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

LORD - ( YHWH, Strong’s [H3068] )

Summary:

The name refers to the proper, covenantal personal name of the God of Israel, most frequently represented in English Bibles as "Lord." In 1 Samuel 2:10, Hannah uses this sacred name at the climax of her song to declare that the ultimate governance, judgment, and vindication of the earth belong exclusively to the living God, who shatters the pride of human adversaries and establishes the strength of His anointed King.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The name consists of four Hebrew consonants, often called the Tetragrammaton, which identify the personal and self-existent God who revealed Himself to Moses at the burning bush. Throughout the Old Testament, English translations render this proper name in capital letters to distinguish it from common Hebrew titles for master or ruler. In ancient Israelite tradition, this name carried the weight of God's unchangeable character, steadfast faithfulness, and covenant relationship with His people. When Hannah uses the name in her prayer, she invokes the absolute reality of God’s personal involvement in human history, contrasting fickle human strength with the unchanging sovereignty of Israel's Maker.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term functions as the personal and covenant name of God. In biblical usage, the name identifies the One who is eternal, self-existent, and active in fulfilling His promises. Unlike generic titles that describe God's power or status, this proper name emphasizes His intimate relationship with His covenant people combined with His absolute rule over all nations. When Scripture uses this name, it points directly to the God who acts, speaks, remembers, and redeems.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses this name whenever God reveals Himself as the faithful Savior, Judge, and Covenant-maker. It appears in foundational historical narratives, prophetic announcements, and personal prayers of lament or praise. The name carries no abstract philosophical definition in the biblical text; rather, its meaning unfolds through what God does. He is known by this name because He rescues His people from bondage, hears their cries, establishes His law, and holds the nations accountable to His moral standard.

4. Theological Implications

The use of the covenant name anchors all theological truth in the personal character of God rather than in human religious effort. Because He is the unchanging One, His promises regarding justice, mercy, and redemption remain entirely secure. In 1 Samuel 2, Hannah’s song theological dismantles the illusion of human self-reliance. Nations and rulers who pit themselves against His purposes discover that the covenant God holds ultimate dominion over the ends of the earth.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:10, Hannah concludes her prophetic song of triumph with a striking declaration regarding the adversaries of the covenant God. She states that those who contend against Him will be broken to pieces, for He will thunder against them from the heavens. Here, the personal name of God highlights the direct, personal intervention of the Almighty in human affairs. Hannah is not speaking of blind fate or political fortune, but of the deliberate, sovereign action of the covenant Lord who protects His king and exalts the horn of His anointed.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 — This verse uses the name to introduce the account of creation, linking the cosmic Maker directly to His ongoing relationship with the earth and humanity.

  • Psalms 25:1 — The psalm uses the name to anchor a personal cry for rescue and guidance, showing that the believer's trust rests in God's personal covenant character.

  • Isaiah 43:16 — The prophetic text invokes the name to remind wandering people of the God who previously made a way through the sea, grounding present hope in past deliverance.

  • Jeremiah 33:1 — The prophetic word employs the name to assure a displaced and discouraged people of future restoration, tying their hope to the unchanging character of the One who speaks.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The placement of this divine name at the end of Hannah’s song serves as a profound hinge for the entire book of Samuel. It redirects the reader's attention away from the failures of earthly priesthood and the chaos of the Judges period toward the sovereign rule of God. The song anticipates the establishment of the monarchy and ultimately points forward to the righteous rule of God's anointed King.
