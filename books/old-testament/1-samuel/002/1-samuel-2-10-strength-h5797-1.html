STRENGTH - (ʻôz, Strong's H5797)

oz - ( oz , H5797 )

Summary:

The word oz refers to power, strength, might, or a strong refuge. Throughout Scripture, it describes both the overwhelming power that belongs to God and the strength He bestows on His people. In 1 Samuel 2:10, Hannah declares that the LORD will grant divine strength and royal authority to His king, looking forward to God's appointed ruler who will triumph under divine power rather than human self-reliance.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The noun oz derives from a root associated with firmness, power, and protection. It often carries the idea of tangible force or a secure stronghold where one finds safety. In ancient Israel, true power was not measured merely by physical size or human military force, but by the ultimate authority and sustaining power of God.

The setting of 1 Samuel 2:10 comes at a pivotal transition in Israel's history. Hannah, once barren and grieved by her rival, had prayed fervently for a child. When God answered her prayer with the birth of Samuel, she brought the child to Shiloh to dedicate him to the LORD's service. Her prayer in 1 Samuel 2 begins with personal gratitude for her deliverance, but it quickly expands into a prophetic hymn about God's universal sovereignty. She sings of a God who brings down the proud, exalts the humble, controls life and death, and weighs human actions. The passage climaxes in verse 10 with a vision of God judging the earth and granting victorious strength to His appointed king.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Oz can refer to physical power, royal or military might, bold confidence, or a safe fortress. When used of God, it denotes His intrinsic omnipotence and majesty—the unstoppable power by which He governs creation, defeats His enemies, and redeems His people.

When used of human beings, oz describes strength that is received from God rather than generated by human effort. It can describe an individual's inner fortitude, the defense of a fortified city, or the public authority given to a ruler. In 1 Samuel 2:10, oz identifies the royal might and divine backing that the LORD will bestow upon His chosen king, enabling that ruler to execute justice and establish righteousness on earth.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses oz across several major contexts:

  • Divine Praise: Israel frequently celebrates God as the ultimate source of power. God does not merely exercise strength; He is Himself the strength and refuge of His people.

  • Sanctuary and Presence: The word is linked with God's sanctuary and the ark of the covenant, representing the visible manifestation of His holy power dwelling among His people.

  • Character and Fortitude: In wisdom literature, oz describes moral resilience and dignity, showing that true strength manifests as godly character and quiet confidence under trial.

  • Royal and Prophetic Hope: The word appears in royal settings where God promises to equip His anointed leader with victorious power over all adversaries.

Throughout the Old Testament, a vital distinction remains: human strength without God is frail and deceptive, but strength received from the LORD provides absolute security.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's use of oz highlights several major biblical truths:

First, power belongs ultimately to God alone. Human beings often place their confidence in personal talent, wealth, or political leverage, but Hannah warns that no person prevails by human strength. True security comes only from the LORD.

Second, God delights in lifting up the weak and humbling the proud. Hannah's own experience of moving from barrenness to blessing mirrors God's larger way of working in redemption. God displaces the self-reliant and equips those who depend on Him.

Third, the passage introduces a foundational messianic hope. When Hannah speaks of God giving strength to His king and exalting the horn of His anointed, she introduces the expectation of a ruler empowered directly by God. This theme runs through the books of Samuel, establishes the Davidic covenant, and ultimately points to Jesus Christ, the true Anointed King who reigns in divine power.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:10, oz appears in the final lines of Hannah's song. The verse builds to a grand conclusion: the adversaries of the LORD will be broken in pieces, He will thunder from heaven against them, and He will execute justice across the entire earth. Immediately following these declarations of judgment, Hannah declares that the LORD will give strength to His king.

This declaration is striking because Israel had no human king at the time Hannah prayed. Judges and priests led the nation, and the monarchy had not yet been established. Under divine inspiration, Hannah looks beyond her immediate circumstances to a future day when God would set a king over His people. The parallel line—"And exalt the horn of His anointed"—clarifies what giving strength means. The horn is a common biblical symbol for dignity, authority, and victorious power. Bestowing strength on the king means granting him the divine authority and triumph needed to carry out God's righteous rule.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 15:2 — "The LORD is my strength and song, and He has become my salvation..." In the Song of the Sea, Moses and Israel praise God for delivering them from Pharaoh, acknowledging that their victory came entirely from the LORD's power.

  • Psalms 28:7 — "The LORD is my strength and my shield; my heart trusted in Him, and I am helped..." David expresses personal trust in God, identifying the LORD as his defender and source of endurance.

  • Psalms 132:8 — "Arise, O LORD, to Your resting place, You and the ark of Your strength." Here the word is associated with the ark of the covenant, representing God's holy presence and power dwelling among His people.

  • Proverbs 31:25 — "Strength and honor are her clothing; she shall rejoice in time to come." Wisdom literature uses the word to describe the moral fortitude and dignified confidence of a godly person.

  • Isaiah 45:24 — "Surely in the LORD I have righteousness and strength." The prophet emphasizes that spiritual victory and righteousness are found in God alone, not in idol worship or human effort.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's song in 1 Samuel 2 provides the thematic blueprint for the entire book of 1 Samuel. The theme of God humbling the proud (such as Peninnah, Eli's sons, and Saul) and exalting the humble (such as Hannah, Samuel, and David) recurs throughout the narrative. Furthermore, Mary's song in Luke 1 consciously echoes Hannah's song, celebrating how God brings down the mighty and exalts the lowly through the coming of the Messiah. The strength prophesied in 1 Samuel 2:10 finds its ultimate expression in Christ, who defeated sin and death not through human weapons, but through the resurrection power of God.
