LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

LORD - ( YHWH, Strong’s [H3068] )

Summary:

The proper personal and covenant name of the God of Israel, used throughout Scripture to designate the living and true God. In 1 Samuel 2:11, the name identifies the sovereign God to whom young Samuel is committed to minister, highlighting that human service in the sanctuary is directed entirely to the Lord Himself rather than to mere human leadership.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew name represented in English Bibles as "Lord" (printed in capital letters) is the personal and covenant name of God revealed to Israel. In ancient Hebrew tradition, this four-consonant name came to be spoken with deep reverence, leading English translations to render it as "LORD." It sets the God of Israel apart from all manufactured idols and false deities, identifying Him as the self-existent Creator who enters into personal relationship with His people and redeems them.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term functions as a proper noun identifying the God of Scripture. It points directly to the living God who acts in history, hears the prayers of His people, establishes His covenant, and fulfills His promises. In this passage, the name names the specific deity who receives Hannah's dedication of her son and who oversees the ongoing service at the sanctuary in Shiloh.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, the name appears whenever Scripture emphasizes God's personal presence, His faithfulness to His covenant promises, and His supreme rule over human affairs. It is associated with His moral law, His judgment against sin, and His steadfast love toward those who trust Him. When the biblical text uses this name, it directs attention away from abstract theological concepts and toward the living God who speaks, acts, and interacts directly with human beings.

4. Theological Implications

The use of the divine name underscores that human devotion and institutional service find their ultimate focus in God alone. In 1 Samuel, the narrative contrasts the corrupt and selfish ministry of Eli's biological sons with the faithful service of Samuel. The presence of the divine name at this juncture reminds the reader that worship, obedience, and ministry are owed exclusively to the Lord. Human failure within the priesthood does not diminish His authority or nullify His purposes.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:11, the text records that Elkanah went to his home in Ramah while the child Samuel remained behind to minister to the Lord before Eli the priest. The mention of the divine name here anchors Samuel's early life and duties directly in the presence of God. Although Eli holds the office of high priest, Samuel's ultimate master and recipient of service is the Lord. This establishes the proper order of loyalty that shapes the remainder of Samuel's prophetic career.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 — "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the Lord God made the earth and the heavens."

  • Deuteronomy 18:16 — "according to all you desired of the Lord your God in Horeb in the day of the assembly, saying, 'Let me not hear again the voice of the Lord my God, nor let me see this great fire anymore, lest I die.'"

  • Psalms 25:1 — "To You, O Lord, I lift up my soul."

  • Jeremiah 33:1 — "Moreover the word of the Lord came to Jeremiah a second time, while he was still shut up in the court of the prison, saying,"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The transition in the narrative from Hannah's song of praise to Samuel's quiet service emphasizes that God works through humble obedience. By linking Samuel's daily duties directly to the divine name, the author shows that even the most routine tasks performed in the sanctuary carry profound spiritual significance when directed to the Lord.
