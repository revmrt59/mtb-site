MINISTERED - (shârath, Strong's H8334)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb sharat refers to rendering personal, honored service to a superior, ruler, or God. Unlike general labor, it denotes an official role of attendance, assistance, or worship in a royal court or sanctuary setting. In 1 Samuel 2:11, the word describes young Samuel beginning his life of dedicated service to God at Shiloh under the direction of Eli the priest. This marks Samuel's commitment to God's work from his youth, standing in sharp contrast to the wickedness of Eli's sons.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israel, two main verbs described serving others: 'abad, which usually referred to work, labor, or physical servitude, and sharat, which denoted personal, personal-assistant, or sanctuary service. Sharat carried an elevated tone, used for royal attendants, personal aides to leaders, and priests performing duties before God.

When Hannah presented Samuel to God at Shiloh after weaning him, she fulfilled her vow to dedicate him to the LORD for his entire life. The background of 1 Samuel 2 is set at the central place of Israelite worship, where the ark of the covenant rested. The phrase "ministered to the LORD before Eli the priest" places Samuel within the priestly circle at the sanctuary, showing that his service was both directed toward God and supervised by the high priest.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb sharat means to serve, wait upon, attend to, or perform official duties for someone of higher status.

When used of human relationships, it describes an assistant or trusted attendant caring for the needs of a master, ruler, or leader. When used in a religious setting, it describes priests, Levites, or consecrated servants carrying out sacred duties in God's presence, such as tending the sanctuary, assisting with sacrifices, and helping conduct divine worship.

In 1 Samuel 2:11, the word identifies Samuel as actively engaged in sanctuary service. The word is a singular masculine participle, describing Samuel as one engaged in the ongoing role of ministering to God at Shiloh.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses sharat in two primary settings:

  • Civil and Personal Service: The word describes trusted personal assistants serving key figures. Joseph served Potiphar in Egypt, Joshua served Moses as his trusted assistant throughout the wilderness journey, and Elisha served Elijah as his disciple and helper.

  • Sanctuary and Worship Service: The dominant biblical usage involves priests and Levites ministering before God at the tabernacle and temple. They handled holy instruments, assisted with sacrifices, and stood in God's presence on behalf of the nation.

Unlike generic service, sharat emphasizes proximity to the one being served. To minister in this sense means to stand near, wait upon, and perform specific, designated tasks assigned by a leader or required by God's holy law.

4. Theological Implications

The use of sharat for Samuel's actions highlights several important biblical truths:

  • Service is Directed to God: Though Samuel worked under Eli's human supervision, the text explicitly states that he "ministered to the LORD." True biblical ministry recognizes God as the primary recipient of all service.

  • Youth is No Barrier to Godly Service: Samuel was a young child when his service began. God accepts and values the devotion of the young, entrusting them with meaningful places in His house.

  • Ministry Requires Purity and Reverence: Because sharat involves standing in the presence of a holy God, those who minister must maintain reverence. This sets the foundation for the divine judgment that soon falls on Eli's house for abusing their sacred duties.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:11, Elkanah and Hannah return to their home in Ramah after leaving Samuel at Shiloh. The statement that "the child ministered to the LORD before Eli the priest" provides a key transition in the narrative.

This description sits immediately before the introducing of Eli's sons, Hophni and Phinehas, in 1 Samuel 2:12. The author places Samuel's humble, faithful service in direct contrast to the corrupt behavior of Eli's sons, who dishonored the sacrifices and treated God's worship with contempt. While Eli's own sons profaned the sanctuary, the young boy Samuel quietly and faithfully ministered before the LORD under Eli's care.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 39:4 — "So Joseph found favor in his sight, and served him. Then he made him overseer of his house, and all that he had he put under his authority."

  • Deuteronomy 18:5 — "For the LORD your God has chosen him out of all your tribes to stand to minister in the name of the LORD, him and his sons forever."

  • 1 Samuel 2:18 — "But Samuel ministered before the LORD, even as a child, wearing a linen ephod."

  • 1 Kings 19:21 — "Then he arose and followed Elijah, and became his servant."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Samuel's early service established a foundation for his future leadership as prophet, priest, and judge in Israel. His life demonstrates that faithful leadership often begins with simple, daily tasks of service performed faithfully under God's oversight.

Furthermore, Samuel ministering "before Eli" shows the importance of submission to established godly oversight. Although Eli failed to discipline his wicked sons, Samuel maintained a respectful and submissive posture under Eli's training, learning how to hear and obey the voice of God.
