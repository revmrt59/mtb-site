LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

WORD - ( YHWH / Yahweh, Strong’s [H3068] )

Summary:

The Hebrew name translated as "Lord" in English Bibles is the personal, covenant name of the God of Israel. In 1 Samuel 2:12, this name anchors the narrative against the dark backdrop of Eli's wicked sons, setting the moral and spiritual standard by which the actions of men are judged.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew name represented by this term is the personal and proper name for the God of Israel, most commonly transliterated as Yahweh or Jehovah. In English translations, it is typically rendered in small capital letters ("Lord") to distinguish it from the common Hebrew title of master or lord. This name was revealed to Moses at the burning bush and serves as the primary identifier of Israel’s covenant God, emphasizing His self-existence, eternal nature, and steadfast commitment to His people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term functions as a proper noun identifying the God of Israel. Historically and etymologically, the name is closely linked to the Hebrew verb meaning "to be" or "to exist," pointing to the God who lives independently of all creation, who has always been, and who remains forever unchanging. Unlike generic titles for deity that describe power or status, this specific name carries deeply personal and relational weight. It identifies the sovereign Creator who enters into covenant relationship with human beings, binding Himself to them by His own promise and character.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses this name to distinguish the true God from the lifeless idols of the surrounding nations. When the text employs this specific designation, it draws attention to God's holy character, His righteous demands, and His faithful execution of both blessing and judgment. The name frequently appears in contexts where God's personal involvement in human history, covenant loyalty, and moral authority are brought into sharp focus.

4. Theological Implications

The use of this proper name underscores that God is not an abstract philosophical concept or a distant force, but a personal Sovereign who speaks, acts, and holds His creatures accountable. Because He is the covenant God, His actions toward His people flow from His unchanging character. His holiness requires purity from those who bear His name, and His sovereignty ensures that human rebellion will ultimately be addressed.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:12, the mention of the Lord provides the ultimate standard of evaluation for the corruption taking place in Israel. The text introduces the sons of Eli as corrupt men who did not know the Lord. Their failure was not merely a lack of formal religious participation, but a profound estrangement from the character, authority, and presence of God Himself. By invoking the divine name here, the narrator highlights the gravity of their sin: they were operating in contempt of the living God within His own sanctuary.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 — This passage uses the name to describe the creative origin of the heavens and the earth, linking the personal God directly to the formation of the physical universe.

  • Exodus 13:5 — The name appears in the context of God's promise to bring Israel into a land flowing with milk and honey, connecting the divine identity directly to the fulfillment of ancestral covenants.

  • Psalms 25:1 — The psalmist directs his soul upward, using the divine name to express personal trust, dependence, and expectation toward God in times of trouble.

  • Jeremiah 33:1 — The name comes to the prophet during a time of national siege, introducing a divine message of future restoration and steadfast loyalty amid impending judgment.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Hebrew tradition, this proper name was treated with profound reverence, leading readers to substitute other titles when reading aloud. However, its frequent appearance in historical narratives, prophetic warnings, and personal prayers demonstrates that God desires His people to know Him by His personal name, recognizing both His transcendent holiness and His intimate nearness.
