FLESHHOOK - (mazlêg, Strong's H4207)

mizlaq — ( miz·lāq , H4207 )

Summary:

The word refers to a metal fork or hook used in sacrificial service at the tabernacle and temple. In 1 Samuel 2:13, it highlights the specific kitchen tool corrupt priests used to exploit worshippers by taking whatever the instrument pierced from the sacrificial pot.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israel, the tabernacle and temple service required specialized bronze and iron implements for handling sacrifices. The word describes a three-pronged fork or fleshhook designed to retrieve portions of meat from large boiling pots. These instruments were part of the standard inventory given for maintaining the altar and sanctuary, meant to be used with reverence according to God's prescribed order for fellowship offerings.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word refers to a fork or hook used for handling meat in sacrificial pots. It points to a physical utensil crafted for sanctuary service. In the context of the sanctuary, it served a practical function in managing the portions of sacrifices boiled during worship.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the term across descriptions of tabernacle and temple furnishings. The word appears when detailing the bronze equipment made for the altar of burnt offering, such as pans, shovels, basins, and firepans. It also appears in lists of the gold utensils prepared for the temple service during the reign of Solomon and David's architectural plans. Across these passages, the word consistently identifies a utilitarian instrument of priestly service connected to the offerings brought by the people.

4. Theological Implications

The use of sanctuary utensils underlines that God's worship involved orderly, tangible acts. The proper use of these tools reflected holiness and obedience in handling what belonged to the Lord and to the priests. Conversely, when sacred tools were misused for personal greed, it exposed a deeper spiritual corruption at the heart of the priesthood.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:13, the fleshhook exposes the blatant greed and corruption of Eli's sons, Hophni and Phinehas. Instead of waiting for the fat to be burned according to God's law, their servant would thrust a three-pronged fleshhook into the boiling pot and take whatever the hook brought up for the priests. This domestic abuse of a sanctuary utensil illustrates how far the priesthood had fallen, turning an instrument designed for holy service into a tool of robbery against both worshippers and God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 27:3 — The word describes the bronze forks made for the altar of burnt offering as part of the initial tabernacle instructions.

  • Numbers 4:14 — The word denotes the sacred forks wrapped and transported by the Kohathites during the wilderness journeys.

  • 1 Samuel 2:14 — The word appears again in the immediate narrative to show the full extent of the corrupt priests' demands on the people of Israel.

  • 1 Chronicles 28:17 — The word refers to the pure gold forks designated by David for the future temple service.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The narrative in 1 Samuel uses mundane sanctuary objects to highlight spiritual decay. A tool meant to assist in peaceful fellowship offerings became a symbol of tyrannical greed. This contrast warns that holy forms and sacred tools mean nothing when the hearts of the leaders are bent on self-indulgence.
