SERVANT - (naʻar, Strong's H5288)

NAAR ( na'ar , Strong’s [H5288] )

Summary:

The word na'ar refers broadly to a young person, ranging from an infant or child to a youth, attendant, or servant. In 1 Samuel 2:13, it describes the young assistants or attendants serving at the tabernacle under Eli's wicked sons, Hophni and Phinehas.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew term na'ar appears frequently across the Old Testament to describe individuals in the early stages of life or those occupying subordinate support roles. In ancient Israelite culture, a na'ar often filled a position of service, assisting priests, warriors, kings, or elders. The word carries no inherent moral judgment by itself; it simply identifies a person's age, status, or role as a junior attendant or young man.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to a newborn infant, a growing child, a young man in his youth, or an attendant who performs practical duties for a superior. Depending on the context, English translations render the term as child, lad, youth, man, or servant. In service contexts, it highlights a subordinate status where the individual acts under the direction of an authority figure.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses na'ar across diverse historical settings to describe both familial relationships and official duties. It frequently designates a young helper, armor-bearer, or household attendant who carries out practical tasks. While often used literally for chronological youth, it also describes a trusted helper who attends to the daily needs of a master, prophet, or priest.

4. Theological Implications

The tasks entrusted to a na'ar often highlight the practical realities of temple service, warfare, or daily life. When young people serve in sacred settings, their actions reflect the spiritual environment provided by their leaders. The behavior of attendants in places of worship exposes the broader spiritual health of the leadership supervising them.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:13, the text describes the corrupt custom practiced by the priests' attendants regarding the sacrificial offerings. These young men acted on behalf of Hophni and Phinehas, abusing their position of service to take the best portions of the meat by force before the Lord's sacrifice was properly burned. The use of the word here underscores the tragic irony that those stationed near the holy tabernacle to assist in worship had become instruments of greed and blatant disrespect for God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Ruth 2:5 — "Then Boaz said to his servant who was in charge of the reapers, 'Whose young man is this?'"

  • 1 Samuel 9:8 — "And the servant answered Saul again and said, 'Look, I have here at hand one fourth of a shekel of silver.'"

  • 2 Kings 6:15 — "And when the servant of the man of God arose early and went out, there was an army, surrounding the city with horses and chariots."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The actions of an attendant or junior worker in Scripture often mirror the character of the one in authority. When leaders tolerate corruption, those who assist them quickly learn to imitate that same lawlessness. This dynamic emphasizes the heavy responsibility placed on spiritual mentors and leaders to model integrity before those who watch and assist them.
