FLESHHOOK - (mazlêg, Strong's H4207)

mizla — ( miz·lāh , H4207 )

Summary:

The Hebrew term mizla refers to a multi-pronged fork or hook used in ancient temple and sacrificial service to handle meat at the altar. In 1 Samuel 2:14, the word describes the brazen instrument abused by the corrupt sons of Eli to demand raw portions of sacrifices for themselves before the fat could be burned to God.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The instrument designated by mizla was a standard piece of bronze equipment crafted for the tabernacle and temple services. In ancient Israelite worship, animal sacrifices involved specific procedures where portions were burned on the altar for God while other parts were designated for the officiating priests. The mizla was designed to manage these sacrificial offerings practically and efficiently within the courtyard of the sanctuary.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word refers to a fork, fleshhook, or pronged instrument. In biblical Hebrew usage, it identifies a utilitarian metal tool equipped with multiple prongs or teeth. It belongs to the vocabulary of sanctuary equipment, specifically associated with the bronze implements used around the altar of burnt offering and the pots holding the sacred meat.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Scripture, the word appears in contexts detailing the construction, organization, and inventory of sanctuary utensils. It is listed alongside basins, pans, and firepans as part of the official bronze equipment ordered for worship. These references show that the implement was an ordinary, necessary tool for managing offerings in strict accordance with Levitical law.

4. Theological Implications

The use of sanctuary implements highlights the holiness and order required in approaching God. Every physical item in the tabernacle or temple, down to the cooking forks and basins, was consecrated for holy use. When these tools were handled outside of God's appointed instructions, as seen in the priesthood of Eli's sons, the sacred order of worship was severely violated, turning holy service into an instrument of greed.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:14, the mizla exposes the brazen corruption eating away at the heart of Israel's worship. Instead of waiting for the fat to be offered to God as the law required, the wicked sons of Eli sent their servant with a three-pronged fleshhook while the meat was boiling. Whatever the fork brought up, the priests took for themselves. The tool designed for holy service became the instrument of violent extortion against worshippers and open contempt for God.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The historical setting of 1 Samuel reveals that the moral decay of a nation often begins at the altar. When spiritual leaders treat the holy things of God with casual indifference or greedy self-interest, the entire religious and social fabric of the community suffers. The three-pronged fork stands in the narrative as a vivid witness to the abuse of sacred authority.
