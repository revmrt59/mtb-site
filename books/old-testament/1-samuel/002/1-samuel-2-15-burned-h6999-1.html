BURNED - (qâṭar, Strong's H6999)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb qatar means to turn a sacrificial offering into smoke upon an altar, whether burning fat portions or offering incense so that the aroma ascends to God. In 1 Samuel 2:15, "burned" refers to the sacred act of burning the fat of a sacrifice on the altar before any portion could be given to the worshippers or priests. The passage highlights the blatant corruption of Eli's sons, who demanded their share of raw meat before God received His prescribed offering, treating the Lord's sacrifice with open contempt.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israel's sacrificial worship, fire played a central role in converting gifts into smoke that ascended toward God. The verb qatar describes this specific process of burning sacrificial portions or incense on an altar so that the offering yielded a soothing aroma to the Lord.

According to the Law of Moses, when a worshipper brought a peace offering, specific parts of the animal—especially the fat covering the internal organs—were set aside exclusively for God. This fat was brought to the altar of burnt offering, where the priest placed it on the fire. Only after this portion was burned could the priest receive his legal share of the meat and the worshipper consume the rest. Burning the fat first demonstrated that God held absolute priority in every act of worship.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Qatar means to make an offering go up in smoke, to burn sacrificial fat, or to burn incense on an altar. The term focuses on the transformation of the physical gift into smoke rising heavenward as an act of devotion.

When used in legitimate worship at the Tabernacle or Temple, qatar refers to priests burning incense on the golden altar or burning the fat of animal sacrifices on the bronze altar. When used in contexts of apostasy, the word describes the illegitimate practice of burning incense or sacrifices to pagan deities or at unauthorized high places.

In 1 Samuel 2:15, the word refers specifically to the requirement that the worshippers burn the fat of the sacrifice on the Lord's altar before anyone took meat for themselves.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses qatar across three main settings:

  • Tabernacle and Temple ritual: Priests used the word for the daily burning of incense and the routine burning of fat portions from animal sacrifices.

  • Unfaithful or idolatrous worship: Later Israelite history frequently uses qatar to describe burning incense to idols such as Baal, or burning offerings on the high places contrary to God's command.

  • Distinct priestly duty: Burning sacrifices on the altar was reserved specifically for the ordained Levitical priests. Unauthorized individuals who attempted to burn incense or sacrifices committed a grave violation.

The core idea of the word is not destruction by fire, but the presentation of an offering to God through fire, where the smoke represents the gift ascending to Him.

4. Theological Implications

The sacrificial system assigned the fat of the animal to God alone because the fat represented the richest, choice portion of the sacrifice. Burning the fat on the altar before distributing the remaining meat taught Israel several critical truths:

  • God's portion takes absolute priority over human desire.

  • Worship must be conducted on God's terms, not according to human preference.

  • Human beings are permitted to enjoy the blessings of God's provision only after God has received His rightful honor.

When people bypassed or rushed past the burning of the fat, they were not merely breaking a ritual rule; they were attempting to place their physical appetites above the honor of God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

1 Samuel 2 records the deep moral and spiritual decay of the priesthood under Eli's sons, Hophni and Phinehas. Instead of serving as faithful shepherds, they used their priestly position for personal enrichment and greed.

In 1 Samuel 2:15, the verb "burned" appears in a masculine plural imperfect form, describing the expected ritual procedure that was supposed to take place whenever a worshipper offered a sacrifice. The customary practice was for the worshippers and priests to burn the fat on the altar first.

However, Eli's servants would interrupt this process before the fat was burned. They demanded raw meat from the worshipper so they could roast it according to their own taste, refusing the boiled meat normally allocated to priests after the sacrificial fire had received its portion. When god-fearing worshippers protested and urged them to let the fat be burned first, the servants threatened to take the meat by force (1 Samuel 2:16).

This misuse of authority exposed a wicked heart. By taking their share before "they burned the fat," Eli's sons treated God's holy altar as secondary to their own luxury, bringing severe divine judgment upon their household.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Leviticus 4:19 – In the instructions for the sin offering, the priest was commanded to take all the fat from the sacrifice and "burn" it on the altar, showing that the fat belonged exclusively to God.

  • Leviticus 17:6 – God commanded the priest to sprinkle blood on the altar and "burn" the fat for a sweet aroma to the Lord, establishing the ritual priority of God's portion.

  • 1 Samuel 2:28 – The Lord reminded Eli that He chose his father's house out of all Israel to be His priests, to offer sacrifices, and to "burn" incense before Him—a sacred trust his sons violated.

  • Jeremiah 7:9 – The prophet rebuked the nation for moral corruption and hypocrisy, pointing out that they would break God's commandments and burn "incense" to Baal while claiming the temple's protection.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The sin of Eli's sons highlights how easily religious service can degenerate into self-serving ritual. The sacrificial laws were designed so that God received His portion first, ensuring that worship remained focused on God's glory rather than human gratification.

When Eli's sons refused to wait until the fat was burned, they demonstrated that they cared nothing for God's holiness or the spiritual meaning of the sacrifice. Later in the chapter, God summarizes the principle behind His judgment: "Those who honor Me I will honor, and those who despise Me shall be lightly esteemed" (1 Samuel 2:30). True worship always puts God's honor first.
