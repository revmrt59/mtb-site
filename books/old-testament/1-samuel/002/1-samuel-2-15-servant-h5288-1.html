SERVANT - (naʻar, Strong's H5288)

SERVANT - ( na'ar , Strong’s [H5288] )

Summary:

The word refers primarily to a young person, ranging from an infant or child to a youth, young man, or attendant. In 1 Samuel 2:15, it describes the personal attendant who serves the wicked sons of Eli at the tabernacle. The term highlights his dependent or subordinate standing as he carries out the demands of the corrupt priesthood.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew term pictures youth, early growth, or dependent status. In ancient Israelite society, young people often held roles of service, assisting elders, family members, or officials. Depending on the setting, the word can describe a newborn baby, a young boy, a growing lad, or a personal attendant performing daily duties. In communal and household life, an attendant of this sort worked under authority, carrying out instructions and running errands for those of higher rank.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to a child, boy, or youth in terms of age and stage of life. It also extends to describe an attendant, helper, or servant who ministers to another person. While it often points to youthfulness, its practical use frequently emphasizes a subordinate relationship where someone acts under the direction of a master, leader, or employer.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the term across various stages of early life and subordinate service. It frequently appears when describing a young boy growing up, a youth assisting a prophet or priest, or a young man serving in a military or domestic capacity. The nuance often shifts between age and function: sometimes the focus rests purely on youth, while other times it highlights a helper who attends to practical tasks for someone else.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of young attendants in places of worship or leadership underscores how early training and service shape the community. While the term itself simply denotes age or subordinate service, the surrounding narratives often use these figures to highlight contrasts between faithful obedience and corrupt leadership. In 1 Samuel, the actions of those serving at the tabernacle reveal the deep moral crisis within Israel's religious leadership.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In this passage, the attendant arrives before the sacrifice is even burned to demand raw meat for the priests, directly violating God's prescribed procedures for the offerings. The use of this term reminds the reader that this young worker acts under the direct oversight of Eli's wicked sons, Hophni and Phinehas. Rather than honoring the Lord, the attendant applies pressure to worshippers, illustrating how widespread the corruption has become within the very center of Israel's worship.

The term frequently appears in narratives involving mentors and their younger assistants, such as Elisha serving Elijah or young men aiding military commanders. These interactions often portray the practical, everyday reality of ancient service, where assistants managed logistics, delivered messages, and stood ready to carry out their master's bidding.
