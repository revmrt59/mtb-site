GIVE - (nâthan, Strong's H5414)

give - ( nathan, Strong’s [H5414] )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb nathan is a common and versatile term that simply means to give, put, set, or place. In 1 Samuel 2:16, it describes the greedy demand of Eli’s wicked sons, who insist that a worshipper "give" them meat immediately before it is offered to God. Instead of waiting for the Lord's portion to be burned on the altar, the corrupt priests demand immediate provision for themselves, demonstrating their contempt for proper worship and their greed for selfish gain.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew language uses this verb across thousands of Old Testament passages to describe basic physical actions involving transfer or placement. It is not a technical religious term by itself, but rather a flexible word grounded in everyday life. Whether a person hands an object to another, sets a boundary marker in the earth, appoints a leader to an office, or delivers an enemy into the hand of a conqueror, this same root supplies the action. In ancient Israelite society, the word naturally fit contexts of commerce, hospitality, legal transactions, and religious offerings.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb can refer to handing something over to someone else, placing an object in a specific location, or bestowing a gift or charge upon a person. When an individual is the subject, the word often describes offering a sacrifice, paying tribute, or handing over property. When God is the subject, it frequently describes bestowing blessing, granting wisdom, handing over nations in judgment, or making a covenant. The exact shade of meaning depends entirely on the surrounding objects and the context of the sentence.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the term across a wide range of literal and figurative settings. It describes physical placement, such as putting a garment on a person or setting a lamp upon a stand. It also describes relational and administrative actions, such as appointing a person to a position of authority or entrusting responsibility into capable hands. In sacrificial contexts, the word frequently tracks the transfer of offerings from human hands toward the altar or the sanctuary, marking what is dedicated to the Lord or claimed by His servants.

4. Theological Implications

While the verb itself is neutral, its use in religious contexts highlights the moral quality of human actions. When God gives, His action flows from sovereign grace, covenant faithfulness, and generosity toward His people. When human beings give, their actions reveal the condition of their hearts toward God. Righteous giving honors the Lord by respecting His commands and placing His portion first, whereas unrighteous giving or selfish demanding exposes rebellion against divine authority and a preference for personal appetite over holy worship.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2, the narrative exposes the severe corruption of Hophni and Phinehas, the sons of Eli. The prescribed law of sacrifices carefully guarded the order of worship, ensuring that the Lord received His designated portion of the animal fat before the priests took their share. Hophni and Phinehas reject this order entirely. When a worshipper attempts to follow the proper procedure, the priest's servant arrives with a three-pronged fork and demands that the worshipper "give" meat to the priest on the spot. The use of the verb here highlights their brazen entitlement and impatience. They refuse to wait for God to be honored first, seizing by force what belongs to the sanctuary and turning the holy service of tabernacle worship into a mechanism for satisfying their own greed.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 1:17 — God used this same Hebrew verb when He placed the lights in the firmament of the heavens to give light on the earth.

  • Genesis 39:4 — Potiphar placed Joseph in charge of his entire household, entrusting all his possessions into Joseph's care.

  • Deuteronomy 26:2 — The Israelites were instructed to bring the firstfruits of their harvest, placing them in a basket as an act of worship and gratitude to God.

  • Ecclesiastes 2:26 — God grants wisdom, knowledge, and joy to the person who pleases Him, while leaving the sinner with the task of gathering and hoarding.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast in 1 Samuel 2 between proper reverence and priestly greed underscores the tragedy of the period before Samuel's rise. While God's law established clear boundaries for how offerings were to be handled, Eli's sons treated the holy offerings with contempt. Their demand for immediate self-gratification violated the sanctuary and ultimately brought severe judgment upon Eli's house. The word "give" thus stands at the center of a profound moral failure, marking the exact moment sinful men attempted to take for themselves what belonged exclusively to God.
