TAKE - (lâqach, Strong's H3947)

LAQAH - ( laqah, Strong’s [H3947] )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb laqah broadly means to take, seize, fetch, or receive. It is a common term used for grasping or acquiring something, whether by physical force, legal purchase, rightful receiving, or personal selection. In 1 Samuel 2:16, the word describes the corrupt demand made by Eli’s wicked sons, who insisted on taking whatever meat they wanted from the sacrifices by force before the fat was offered to God.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Laqah is a flexible Old Testament verb used across a wide range of everyday and religious contexts. It describes simple physical actions, such as picking up an object or gathering materials, as well as significant relational and legal transactions, such as marriage or purchasing property. The word often carries a sense of seizing or claiming control over something. In ancient Israelite worship, taking portions of a sacrifice was strictly regulated by divine law, making unauthorized or forceful grabbing a direct violation of sacred boundaries.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to taking an object into one's hand, receiving a gift or payment, capturing someone in battle, or selecting something for personal use. When used in neutral contexts, it simply means to fetch or accept. However, the action can also take on an aggressive or unauthorized tone depending on the context, denoting seizure, robbery, or taking by force. In sacrificial settings, laqah describes the physical removal or appropriation of offerings.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses laqah in many different scenarios, ranging from domestic life to solemn religious rituals. It describes God taking Enoch or Elijah up to heaven, individuals taking wives, and soldiers capturing spoils of war. The nuance of the word depends heavily on who is acting and what is being taken. When greedy individuals seize what belongs to God or to others by force, the word highlights human selfishness and disregard for divine authority.

4. Theological Implications

The way things are taken in Scripture often reveals the state of a person's heart before God. While God graciously gives and receives offerings, sinful human nature often attempts to seize by force what should be given in reverence. In 1 Samuel, the unauthorized grabbing of sacred offerings exposed a deep spiritual corruption among the priesthood, showing how casual disrespect for holy things invites divine judgment.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:16, the offending worshipper would suggest burning the fat first, according to proper custom, and then the priests' servant would demand whatever meat he wanted immediately. If the worshipper objected, the servant would threaten to take it by force. Here, laqah exposes the blatant greed and lawlessness of Eli's sons. Instead of waiting to receive their proper portion with humility, they used intimidation to seize the best parts of the offering for themselves, treating the sacrifices of God with contempt.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:15 — "Then the Lord God took the man and put him in the garden of Eden to tend and keep it."

  • Exodus 25:2 — "Speak to the children of Israel, that they take an offering for Me."

  • Leviticus 9:2 — "Take a young bull for the sin offering and a ram for a burnt offering, without blemish, and offer them before the Lord."

  • 1 Samuel 25:35 — "So David received from her hand what she had brought him, and said to her, 'Go up in peace to your house.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The abuses described in 1 Samuel 2:16 highlight the danger of corrupted leadership within the house of God. When those appointed to serve spiritual roles begin to demand and seize for themselves what belongs to the Lord, it destroys public trust in worship and provokes God's righteous discipline. The contrast between humble receiving and arrogant seizing runs throughout Scripture as a mark of true versus false devotion.
