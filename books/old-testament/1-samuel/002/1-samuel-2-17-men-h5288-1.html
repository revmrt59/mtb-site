MEN - (naʻar, Strong's H5288)

NAAR ( na'ar , Strong’s [H5288] )

Summary:

The Hebrew term typically refers to a young male in the earlier stages of life, ranging from an infant or small child to a youth, servant, or attendant. In 1 Samuel 2:17, the word describes the young priests, Hophni and Phinehas, who served at the tabernacle in Shiloh. Here the term underscores their youth and subordinate station, contrasting sharply with the grave corruption and abuse of office they displayed in their priestly duties.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word is common in biblical Hebrew and describes the developmental stages of youth and young manhood. In ancient Israelite society, a person in this stage of life was often under authority, serving as a helper, attendant, or junior worker in a household, royal court, or religious sanctuary. The term does not carry a rigid age bracket but is defined by social role and dependence, frequently pointing to someone who assists an older leader or master.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to a newborn infant, a young child, a growing youth, or a servant. When used in a domestic or official setting, it often designates an attendant or junior assistant who performs practical duties under the direction of another. The term highlights a status of transition, subordination, or apprenticeship rather than a specific title of office. In 1 Samuel 2:17, the plural form identifies the young men who assisted in the physical operations of the sanctuary sacrifices.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the word across various settings to describe sons, royal attendants, messengers, and junior servants. Sometimes it highlights vulnerability and youth, while other times it points to the active service of a helper. In narrative contexts, the word frequently bridges the gap between childhood and full adult responsibility, capturing individuals who are learning a trade, assisting a superior, or carrying out delegated tasks.

4. Theological Implications

While youth and inexperience can invite temptation, Scripture does not excuse spiritual rebellion on the grounds of youth. In 1 Samuel, the failures of the younger generation of leaders demonstrate that position and privilege cannot replace genuine reverence for God. The passage highlights that sacred service requires holy fear, regardless of age or status within the religious community.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:17, the term points directly to Hophni and Phinehas, the sons of Eli, who functioned as junior priests in the tabernacle. The narrative uses the word to emphasize their youth and the gravity of their sin against the sacrifices of the Lord. Instead of honoring God, these young men treated the holy offerings with contempt. The text contrasts their elevated sacred office with their reckless disregard for divine law, showing that their sin was exceptionally grievous in the eyes of God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 14:24 — The word describes young men or allies who accompanied Abraham in his pursuit to rescue Lot, illustrating companions assisting in a vital mission.

  • Joshua 6:23 — The term refers to young attendants or survivors rescued from Jericho along with Rahab's household.

  • 1 Samuel 21:4 — The word denotes the young companions traveling with David who shared in the provisions of the sanctuary when fleeing from Saul.

  • 2 Kings 6:15 — The term describes the personal servant or attendant of the prophet Elisha who woke up to discover the city surrounded by an Aramean army.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The broader narrative of 1 Samuel 2 contrasts the spiritual failure of Eli's sons with the faithful ministry of young Samuel, who also served in the tabernacle. While Hophni and Phinehas abused their positions as young leaders, Samuel grew in favor with both God and man. This thematic parallel demonstrates that youthful service can either rebel against God's standards or honor Him faithfully.
