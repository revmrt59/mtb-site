LINEN - (bad, Strong's H906)

Baddad - ( bad-dad', Strong’s [H0906] )

Summary:

The Hebrew term refers to a specific type of linen cloth or garment, most often associated with priestly service, sacred duties, and holy ministry in the presence of God. In 1 Samuel 2:18, the word describes the sacred garment worn by young Samuel as he ministered before the Lord at the tabernacle in Shiloh, marking him out for holy service despite his youth.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient Near East, linen was a valued and refined textile produced from the flax plant. In the Hebrew Bible, particular garments crafted from fine linen carried distinct religious and ceremonial associations. They were especially tied to the priesthood and the sacred precincts of the tabernacle and temple. Wearing linen conveyed purity, reverence, and fitness for approaching the holy presence of God. In the setting of 1 Samuel, where the official priesthood under Eli's house was corrupt and failing, the mention of this garment highlights the continuation of genuine priestly ministry through the young boy Samuel.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word refers to linen material or a garment made of linen. When used in biblical texts, it frequently designates the white linen garments worn by priests and attendants during sacred rituals. The material stood in contrast to common woolen or everyday clothing, representing ritual cleanliness and distinct separation for divine service. In this passage, the word identifies the specific sacred tunic or ephod-like garment worn by Samuel as he performed his duties in the house of the Lord.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the term primarily within contexts of worship, priestly service, and heavenly visions. Priests wore linen garments when performing daily sacrifices and entering the holy places. On the Day of Atonement, the high priest set aside his ornate golden garments to wear plain linen tunics for the most solemn rituals. In prophetic literature, heavenly messengers and angelic figures appear clothed in linen, symbolizing divine purity and heavenly authority. Across these various uses, the word consistently points to holiness, sacred function, and service rendered directly before God.

4. Theological Implications

The clothing worn by those who ministered before God carried deep symbolic weight under the old covenant. Linen represented the purity required to draw near to a holy God. While ordinary clothing belonged to the common spheres of daily life, linen garments set apart the wearer for sacred responsibilities. In 1 Samuel, the presence of this priestly garment on a young child who is not from the genealogical line of Aaron signals a fresh work of God. It points forward to a time when God would raise up a faithful servant to minister according to His own heart.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

The narrative of 1 Samuel 2 contrasts the wicked behavior of Eli’s biological sons with the growing faithfulness of young Samuel. While Hophni and Phinehas abused their priestly office for selfish greed and blatant sin, Samuel ministered before the Lord. The text notes that Samuel was clothed in linen even as a boy, serving under the guidance of Eli. This detail underscores that true ministry is defined by devotion and obedience to God rather than mere age or inherited privilege. The linen garment visually confirms Samuel’s active place in the sanctuary, serving as a sign of his dedication to the Lord's house.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The repetition of this term in connection with both Levitical priests and figures like David and Samuel shows that sacred service required proper preparation and reverent attire. For Samuel, wearing a garment associated with priestly ministry marked a pivotal role in Israel's history. As a prophet, judge, and priest-like figure, Samuel stood in the gap for the nation, bridging the turbulent transition from the period of the judges to the establishment of the monarchy.
