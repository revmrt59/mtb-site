MINISTERED - (shârath, Strong's H8334)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb sharat means to serve, attend, or minister. It often refers to personal attendance on a person of higher status or the ritual service rendered to God by priests and Levites in the sanctuary. In 1 Samuel 2:18, the word describes the young boy Samuel performing sanctuary service in the presence of the Lord at Shiloh, contrasting his pure devotion with the corrupt behavior of Eli’s sons.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The historical setting of 1 Samuel 2 takes place during the late period of the judges in Israel. The central place of worship was the tabernacle at Shiloh, where Eli served as high priest. Eli’s sons, Hophni and Phinehas, were also priests, but they abused their position by taking the best parts of the sacrifices for themselves and treating the sanctuary with contempt.

In contrast to this spiritual decline, Hannah brought her young son Samuel to Shiloh after weaning him, fulfilling her vow to dedicate him to the Lord for life. Despite his youth and the corrupt surroundings, Samuel began serving at the sanctuary under Eli's supervision. The verb sharat captures Samuel's assignment, showing that God was quietly preparing a faithful prophet and leader in the very shadow of a failing priesthood.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word sharat describes rendering personal assistance or honorable service to a superior. Unlike ordinary manual labor or forced servitude, this verb typically refers to higher-level personal service, royal court duty, or sanctuary worship.

When used of human relationships, the word means to wait on, assist, or serve an officer, king, or prophet. When used of religious service, it means to minister in the sanctuary, attend to sacred duties, and stand in the presence of God. In 1 Samuel 2:18, the word identifies Samuel's role as an assistant in sanctuary operations, performing duties assigned to him before the Lord.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, sharat appears in two main contexts: royal/domestic administrative service and priestly/tabernacle ministry.

In household and administrative settings, the word describes trusted subordinates who assist leaders. For example, Joseph served Potiphar in Egypt, and Elisha served Elijah as his minister and successor.

In tabernacle and temple settings, the word becomes a specialized term for priests and Levites. They were set apart to minister before the Lord, handling sacred vessels, assisting with sacrifices, and conducting public worship. The service described by sharat is elevated and honorable because the ultimate beneficiary of the service is God Himself.

4. Theological Implications

The use of sharat for young Samuel highlights several important truths about ministry and divine worship:

  • God values heart devotion over age or social status. A young child serving with integrity pleases God more than prominent religious officials living in willful disobedience.

  • True service is directed toward the Lord. Samuel ministered "before the LORD," showing that faithful service focuses on God's presence and approval rather than human recognition.

  • God preserves a remnant of true worship even in times of corruption. While the official priestly family dishonored the tabernacle, God raised up a young boy whose quiet service prepared the way for spiritual renewal in Israel.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

The placement of 1 Samuel 2:18 creates a dramatic contrast in the narrative. Verses 12–17 detail the grave sins of Eli’s sons, who dishonored the Lord's sacrifices and drove people away from worship.

Immediately following that dark account, verse 18 opens with a striking turn: "But Samuel ministered before the LORD, even as a child, wearing a linen ephod." The grammar uses a masculine singular participle, presenting Samuel as actively engaged in his role of sanctuary service. Even as a young boy, Samuel was clothed in a linen ephod—a garment associated with priestly service—showing that his youth did not prevent him from being set apart for holy work.

While the older priests treated holy things with contempt, the child Samuel served God with sincerity and reverent obedience.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 28:43 — "They shall be on Aaron and on his sons when they come into the tabernacle of meeting... to minister in the holy place..."

  • Deuteronomy 18:5 — "For the LORD your God has chosen him out of all your tribes to stand to minister in the name of the LORD..."

  • 1 Kings 19:21 — "Then he arose and followed Elijah, and became his servant."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Samuel’s early experience of ministering before the Lord laid the foundation for his future leadership. The habit of serving God in quiet daily duties formed his character before God called him to speak as a prophet to all Israel in 1 Samuel 3. Small acts of faithful service in the sanctuary prepared Samuel for greater responsibilities in God's kingdom.
