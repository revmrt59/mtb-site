MOTHER - (ʼêm, Strong's H517)

EM — ( em, Strong’s [H0517] )

Summary:

The Hebrew word `em` means mother, maternal parent, or female ancestor. In 1 Samuel 2:19, the word identifies Hannah, who lovingly brings a small, yearly robe to her young son Samuel at the tabernacle in Shiloh, reflecting her enduring maternal devotion and faithfulness to the vow she made before the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word `em` is a foundational Hebrew term used throughout the Old Testament to designate a mother. In the ancient Israelite household, the mother held a position of profound respect, authority, and care alongside the father. Within the patriarchal setting of Scripture, the term extends beyond the immediate biological parent to include grandmothers, female ancestors, and sometimes a maternal clan or nurturing community. The word carries strong connotations of shelter, emotional refuge, and early instruction.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word refers primarily to a mother or female parent. It can also describe a nation's capital or a mother city in extended geographic contexts, and occasionally a point of origin or source. When used of a person, it simply denotes the woman who gave birth to or raised a child. In this passage, the word points directly to Hannah in her active, nurturing role as a parent.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the term to describe biological motherhood, familial bonds, and moral guidance. The word frequently appears in wisdom literature, where the instruction of a mother is placed on equal footing with the instruction of a father. In narrative texts, the word often highlights deep emotional ties, protective care, or generational continuity. It anchors the reader in the tangible realities of family life, covenant training, and domestic devotion.

4. Theological Implications

While the term itself designates a family relationship rather than a direct theological title, Scripture frequently uses maternal imagery to illustrate profound spiritual truths. The Bible often portrays God's comforting, protecting, and persistent care using parental metaphors that mirror the nurturing heart of a devoted parent. In historical narratives, faithful mothers often serve as crucial instruments in preserving the covenant line and raising leaders dedicated to God's service.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:19, the mention of Hannah acting as a mother highlights the tender personal connection that survived across geographical distance and the passing of time. Although Samuel ministered away from home at the sanctuary under Eli's care, Hannah did not abandon her maternal responsibilities. Every year, when she traveled with her husband to offer the yearly sacrifice, she brought a small robe she had made with her own hands. This detail reveals a quiet, practical expression of love that sustained a young boy serving the Lord far from home.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:24 — "Therefore a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and they shall become one flesh."

  • Proverbs 1:8 — "My son, hear the instruction of your father, and do not forsake the law of your mother."

  • Psalms 27:10 — "When my father and my mother forsake me, then the Lord will take care of me."

  • Song of Solomon 8:2 — "I would lead you and bring you into the house of my mother, who used to instruct me."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The simplicity of the word `em` grounds lofty theological events in ordinary human experience. God accomplishes His grand redemptive purposes—such as raising up Samuel to lead Israel through a pivotal transition—through the ordinary, faithful actions of everyday family life. Hannah's yearly visit reminds readers that spiritual devotion to God does not detach believers from familial love; rather, it deepens and purifies it.
