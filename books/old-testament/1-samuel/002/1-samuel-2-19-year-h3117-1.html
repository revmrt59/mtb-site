YEAR - (yôwm, Strong's H3117)

YOM - ( yom, Strong’s [H3117] )

Summary:

The Hebrew word primarily means "day," referring to the daylight hours, a full twenty-four-hour period, or a general span of time. In 1 Samuel 2:19, the plural form is translated as "year" in the English text, marking the recurring annual rhythm of a mother visiting her young son at the tabernacle. Here the word highlights the steady, faithful passage of time and the dependable devotion of Hannah as she returns year after year to bring Samuel a little robe.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew term fundamentally denotes a period of time, most commonly a single calendar day from morning until evening or through the night cycle. However, biblical Hebrew often uses this same basic word of time in broader ways to describe seasons, lifetimes, or specific yearly cycles. In ancient Israelite culture, the agricultural and festival calendar was measured by recurring divisions of time. When paired with specific markers or contextual clues, the term frequently measures the rhythm of family life and religious observance.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to the daylight portion of the daily cycle, a complete day and night rotation, or an extended duration of time such as an epoch, a lifetime, or a year. When used in plural constructions with specific modifiers, it often marks recurring periodic intervals. Here the term functions idiomatically within a temporal phrase to designate the annual cycle—the passing of twelve months—marking the regular interval at which Hannah fulfills her promise and makes her journey to Shiloh.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the term across a wide semantic range, moving easily from literal twenty-four-hour days to broader expressions of time. It can describe a specific point of decision, a historical era, or the fleeting brevity of human life. The nuances depend almost entirely on the surrounding modifiers and prepositional phrases rather than any rigid technical definition within the word itself.

4. Theological Implications

The regular passage of time reflected in this vocabulary serves as the backdrop for God's unfolding faithfulness. The faithful return of Hannah each year underscores a steadfast devotion that mirrors the steady, unchanging nature of God's covenant care. While human life is measured out in passing days and recurring seasons, the Lord remains constant over every span of time.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In the narrative of 1 Samuel, the mother's annual journey highlights the profound bond between Hannah and Samuel, as well as her commitment to the vow she made before the Lord. The specific mention of the yearly interval emphasizes patience, devotion, and the quiet faithfulness of ordinary life taking place outside the immediate spotlight of national history. While Samuel grows under the care of Eli the priest, his mother's steady yearly visits anchor his identity and maintain his connection to his family.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 1:5 — "God called the light Day, and the darkness He called Night. So the evening and the morning were the first day."

  • Deuteronomy 16:8 — "Six days you shall eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day there shall be a sacred assembly to the Lord your God..."

  • Job 12:12 — "Wisdom is with aged men, and with length of days understanding."

  • Isaiah 2:12 — "For the day of the Lord of hosts shall come upon everything proud and lofty..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The translation of the plural form as "year" in 1 Samuel 2:19 demonstrates how ancient Hebrew idiom could use standard units of time to describe recurring seasonal cycles. This linguistic flexibility allows the biblical text to capture natural human experiences of time without needing specialized technical vocabulary for every calendar variation.
