HOLY - (qâdôwsh, Strong's H6918)

Summary:

The Hebrew word qadosh describes that which is set apart, sacred, and distinct from common or worldly things. When applied to God, it refers to His supreme purity, absolute perfection, and unique majesty that separate Him from all created things and false gods. In 1 Samuel 2:2, Hannah uses the word to exalt the Lord as completely unique and without equal, declaring that no other being possesses His matchless perfection and unshakeable character.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The opening chapters of 1 Samuel record the story of Hannah, a woman who suffered years of barrenness and continuous provocation from her husband's second wife. In her deep sorrow, Hannah prayed fervently at the tabernacle in Shiloh, promising that if God gave her a son, she would dedicate him to the Lord's service for life. God answered her petition, and after weaning young Samuel, she brought him to the tabernacle to fulfill her vow.

1 Samuel 2 presents Hannah's song of praise. Rather than focusing merely on her personal vindication or relief, Hannah lifts her eyes to the character of God. She opens her song by praising the Lord's transcendent nature. In 1 Samuel 2:2, she declares that God stands entirely alone in His character, having no rivals or equals in the universe.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Qadosh is an adjective that primary means "holy," "sacred," or "set apart." At its core, the word indicates distinction and separation from that which is common, ordinary, or unclean.

When applied to objects, places, or human beings, the word means consecrated or set apart for God's exclusive ownership and service. When applied directly to God, qadosh describes His essential nature. It refers to His complete moral purity, flawless righteousness, and transcendent majesty. God is not merely morally upright; He is separate from all sin and corruption, elevated far above the finite human order.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In the Old Testament, qadosh carries distinct shades of meaning depending on what or who is being described:

  • Divine Nature: The word describes God Himself as the ultimate standard of holiness and moral purity. He is frequently called the "Holy One," highlighting His absolute uniqueness and distinction from all creation.

  • Consecrated People: The word describes people who belong to God and are called to live according to His holy standards, reflecting His character in daily life.

  • Sacred Places and Things: The word describes places, objects, and times that are set apart exclusively for divine worship, such as the tabernacle, sacred garments, and holy days.

Across all these usages, the word consistently highlights separation from the mundane and devotion to divine purpose.

4. Theological Implications

God's holiness is central to His character. It is not simply one attribute among many; it describes the sheer excellence and purity of who He is.

In Hannah's prayer, God's holiness is closely linked to His absolute uniqueness. She follows the statement "No one is holy like the LORD" with the declarations that "there is none besides You" and "nor is there any rock like our God." This demonstrates that God's holiness sets Him completely apart from human rulers, worldly powers, and lifeless idols. Because God is holy, He is completely trustworthy, just, and uncorrupted by the moral failings that characterize human strength and politics.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:2, Hannah uses qadosh to frame her personal experience within a broad vision of God's character. Having endured years of mistreatment in a broken family dynamic, Hannah knew firsthand the fragility and cruelty of human pride.

When Hannah proclaims, "No one is holy like the LORD," she is drawing a contrast between God and every human power. Earthly strength is temporary and prone to arrogance, but God is holy, stable, and sovereign. The parallel phrase calling God a "rock" reinforces this truth: His holy nature makes Him an unshakeable refuge for the weak and oppressed. Hannah rejoices not merely because her circumstances changed, but because the holy God actively brings down the proud and exalts the humble.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 19:6 — "And you shall be to Me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation."

  • Leviticus 19:2 — "Speak to all the congregation of the children of Israel, and say to them: 'You shall be holy, for I the LORD your God am holy.'"

  • Isaiah 1:4 — "Alas, sinful nation, A people laden with iniquity... They have provoked to anger The Holy One of Israel..."

  • Isaiah 6:3 — "And one cried to another and said: 'Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts; The whole earth is full of His glory!'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the culture of the ancient Near East, neighboring nations worshiped gods who were depicted as selfish, unpredictable, and morally flawed. Hannah’s declaration that Yahweh is qadosh sets Israel’s faith apart from ancient paganism. Israel's God is entirely righteous, faithful, and morally pure.

Furthermore, Hannah's celebration of God's holiness lays the foundation for the entire book of 1 Samuel. As the narrative unfolds—contrasting the corrupt priesthood of Eli's sons with the faithful service of Samuel, and later contrasting the pride of King Saul with the humility of David—Hannah's song reminds the reader that the holy God evaluates human hearts and executes true justice.
