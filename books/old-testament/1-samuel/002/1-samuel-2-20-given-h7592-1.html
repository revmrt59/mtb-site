GIVEN - (shâʼal, Strong's H7592)

sha'al ( H7592 )

Summary:

Sha'al is a primary Hebrew verb that means to ask, request, petition, or inquire. Depending on the setting, it can describe asking a basic question, making a solemn request, inquiring of God for direction, or requesting something that is subsequently granted or dedicated. In 1 Samuel 2:20, the word describes the child Samuel, whom Hannah asked from the LORD in prayer and then surrendered back to God's service. In this passage, it highlights that Samuel was both the answer to a desperate petition and a gift devoted back to the LORD, serving as the basis for Eli's prayer that God would bless Elkanah and Hannah with additional children.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The opening chapters of 1 Samuel record a period of transition in Israel's history. The nation suffered under weak spiritual leadership, marked by the corrupt priesthood of Eli's sons at Shiloh. Against this gloomy backdrop, God brought forward a godly family from the hill country of Ephraim. Elkanah and his wife Hannah traveled annually to the tabernacle at Shiloh to worship and offer sacrifices.

Hannah was childless and suffered deep anguish because of her barrenness and the taunts of her rival, Peninnah. In her distress, Hannah prayed silently before the LORD, asking for a male child and promising that if God granted her request, she would dedicate the boy to the LORD for all the days of his life. God answered her prayer, and she gave birth to Samuel. After weaning him, Hannah faithfully brought the boy to Shiloh to serve at the tabernacle. Each year when the family returned to offer the annual sacrifice, Eli blessed Elkanah and Hannah, asking God to give them more children in place of the one requested and handed over to the LORD.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Sha'al means to ask, petition, request, or inquire.

When used of human interactions, the verb describes asking a question or requesting a gift or favor. When used in worship or prayer, it describes seeking counsel from God or making a formal request before Him. In contexts involving vows or surrender, it refers to asking for something that is then returned, yielded, or dedicated to the one who granted it.

In 1 Samuel 2:20, the word describes the petition made to the LORD regarding Samuel, who was asked from God and subsequently given over to God's sanctuary.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, sha'al appears in several distinct settings:

  • Seeking Information or Counsel: People use the word when asking questions of others or inquiring of God to determine His divine guidance before major decisions.

  • Making Requests or Demands: The word describes bringing a formal request to a king, a neighbor, or a ruler for material help, property, or assistance.

  • Prayerful Petition: Believers use sha'al to describe calling upon God in prayer, bringing personal needs and requests to Him with the expectation that He hears and responds.

  • Dedication and Yielding: In the sanctuary setting of 1 Samuel, the word carries a special nuance. It links the act of asking God for a child with the act of yielding that same child back to God's ownership and service.

4. Theological Implications

The narrative use of sha'al in 1 Samuel highlights several foundational truths about God and prayer:

  • God Hears Prayer: The story of Hannah demonstrates that God pays attention to the quiet petitions of the brokenhearted. Her prayer was not a empty ritual, but a real request heard by the Living God.

  • Believers Belong to God: What God grants in response to prayer remains ultimately His. Hannah did not view the child merely as a personal possession to hold onto, but as a gift to be held with open hands and returned to God's service.

  • Divine Generosity Outpaces Human Surrender: Surrendering what God gives does not deplete a believer's life. When Hannah dedicated Samuel to the LORD, God responded by granting her five more children. God is never in debt to those who yield their blessings back to Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:20, Eli speaks a blessing over Elkanah and his wife: "The LORD give you offspring from this woman for the loan which was given to the LORD."

The verb form used here is a masculine singular perfect form, referring directly back to the petition and dedication of Samuel. Eli's blessing acknowledges that Samuel was requested from God and then surrendered to Him.

This creates a deliberate contrast within 1 Samuel 2. While Eli's wicked sons were taking meat dishonestly from the sanctuary for their own selfish desires, Hannah and Elkanah were freely surrendering their prized firstborn son to God. Eli recognizes this extraordinary faith and pronounces a priest's blessing over the couple, asking God to supply new offspring to fill their home.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Samuel 1:17 — "Then Eli answered and said, 'Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition which you have asked of Him.'"

  • 1 Kings 3:13 — "And I have also given you what you have not asked, both riches and honor, so that there shall not be anyone like you among the kings all your days."

  • Psalms 40:6 — "Sacrifice and offering You did not desire; My ears You have opened. Burnt offering and sin offering You did not require."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The verb sha'al is central to the literary structure of 1 Samuel 1–2. The entire narrative surrounding Samuel's birth and dedication centers on asking and giving:

  • Hannah asks (sha'al) God for a son (1 Samuel 1:17, 27).

  • She names him Samuel, explaining that she asked (sha'al) for him from the LORD (1 Samuel 1:20).

  • She presents him at Shiloh, stating that he is given or dedicated (sha'al) to the LORD as long as he lives (1 Samuel 1:28).

  • Eli pronounces a blessing over the parents for the child given (sha'al) to the LORD (1 Samuel 2:20).

This underlying theme reminds readers that the greatest blessings in life are received through prayerful dependence on God and experienced most fully when yielded back to Him.
