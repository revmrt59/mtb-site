GREW - (gâdal, Strong's H1431)

WORD - ( gadal , Strong’s [H1431] )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb gadal primarily means to grow, become great, or increase in stature, strength, or importance. In 1 Samuel 2:21, the verb describes young Samuel as he grew physically and matured in life while serving in the tabernacle at Shiloh. Here, the word highlights the steady, tangible progress of the child Samuel's life under the gracious provision of God, standing in sharp contrast to the moral decay and judgment surrounding Eli's house.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root behind the English word "grew" in 1 Samuel 2:21 is a common Hebrew verb used throughout the Old Testament to describe physical growth, rising to greatness, or gaining influence and honor. In ancient Israelite culture, watching a child grow was not merely a biological observation; it was a matter of community life and divine blessing. The setting of this verse places Samuel right in the center of the tabernacle, where he lived and ministered under the aging priest Eli. While the surrounding political and spiritual atmosphere was fractured by the sins of Eli’s biological sons, Samuel's ongoing development signaled a fresh work of God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb can refer to physical growth, such as a child maturing into adulthood or a seed sprouting into a plant. It can also describe becoming great in power, wealth, or status. When used of people, it frequently points to aging, increasing in strength, or receiving honor and distinction in the eyes of others. The word simply denotes an upward movement in size, capability, or significance.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses this verb in diverse ways depending on the context. Sometimes it describes literal physical development, while at other times it speaks of a person being exalted to a position of leadership or influence. It can also describe magnifying God through praise or magnifying a matter by making it prominent. In narrative passages, the verb often traces the passage of time and the unfolding of a person's life story, marking the transition from youth to maturity or obscurity to prominence.

4. Theological Implications

The ongoing development of Samuel carries profound theological meaning for the narrative of Israel. God was quietly preserving a faithful servant while the established religious leadership was crumbling from within. The growth of Samuel was not an accident of nature, but part of God's deliberate plan to provide a trustworthy prophet and judge for His people. This underscores the truth that the Lord sustains the humble and prepares leaders behind the scenes, often in quiet places, long before their public ministry begins.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:21, the verb is paired with the detail that Samuel "grew before the Lord." This placement emphasizes that his development took place in the immediate presence of God. While Hophni and Phinehas were growing in wickedness and bringing contempt upon the Lord's sacrifices, Samuel was maturing in a godly environment. The phrase contrasts the blessing resting on Hannah's dedicated son with the impending rejection of Eli's household. His daily maturation was tangible evidence of divine favor resting upon him.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 12:2 — God promises Abraham that He will make him a great nation, using the verb to point toward future national prominence and blessing.

  • Exodus 2:11 — The text uses the verb to describe Moses growing into manhood before witnessing the oppression of his Hebrew kinsmen.

  • 1 Samuel 20:41 — Jonathan and David mourn their parting, and the narrative notes that David grew in grief or exceeded previous sorrow during their emotional farewell.

  • Job 2:13 — Job's friends sit with him in silence because they see that his suffering has grown exceedingly great.

  • Psalms 34:3 — The psalmist invites others to magnify the Lord together, using the verb to call for the public exalting of God's character and greatness.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The repetition of this verb across different books of the Bible shows that growth can apply to nations, individuals, grief, or praise. In Samuel's case, physical maturation goes hand in hand with spiritual preparation. The narrative quietly prepares the reader for the day when Samuel will speak authoritatively for God, anchoring his future prophetic voice in his faithful, everyday upbringing in the tabernacle.
