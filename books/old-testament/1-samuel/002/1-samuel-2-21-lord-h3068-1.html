LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

The word is the personal proper name of the one true God of Israel, often rendered in English Bibles as "LORD" in small capital letters. It identifies God not merely as a distant Creator, but as the living, self-existent God who enters into covenant with His people and acts personally in human history. In 1 Samuel 2:21, this personal divine name highlights God's faithful personal action in visiting Hannah to bless her with additional children and overseeing the young boy Samuel as he grew in service before Him.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The personal divine name was revealed in a unique way to Moses at the burning bush when God identified Himself as "I AM WHO I AM" (Exodus 3:14). Unlike general titles for deity, this proper name set the God of Israel apart from all false gods. It identified Him as the faithful, self-sufficient God who remembers His promises, speaks to His people, and delivers them from trouble.

The book of 1 Samuel opens during a period of spiritual decline in Israel. The priesthood at Shiloh under Eli had become corrupt and compromise was widespread. In the middle of this dark era, a faithful woman named Hannah prayed in deep anguish for a child, promising to dedicate him to God's service. God answered her prayer with the birth of Samuel. In 1 Samuel 2:21, the personal name of God appears as the narrator records how God continued to care for Hannah and guide the boy Samuel.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

This proper noun is the personal name of the God of Israel. Etymologically, it connects to the Hebrew verb for "to be," carrying the fundamental sense of "the Existing One" or "He who is."

It is not a generic title like Elohim (which means "God" or "mighty ones"), but a distinct personal name. When an Israelite heard this name, they recognized the specific God who brought their ancestors out of Egypt and established a covenant with them.

Out of reverent fear of breaking the commandment against taking God's name in vain, Jewish tradition began substituting the title Adonai ("Lord") when reading the sacred name aloud. Following this ancient practice, most modern English Bibles translate the name using capital letters as "LORD."

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, this personal name appears whenever God interacts directly with humanity in covenant grace, redemption, and revelation. General titles like Elohim often emphasize God's power as Creator and Ruler of the universe, whereas this personal name highlights His closeness, truthfulness, and loyalty to His promises.

When Scripture connects this name with divine action—such as God "visiting" a person—it signifies His direct, gracious intervention in human lives. God does not remain a passive spectator. He hears prayers, notices individual suffering, and moves decisively to grant mercy and fulfill His word.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of God's personal name in 1 Samuel 2:21 carries significant theological truth:

  • God Cares for Individuals: The almighty God of the universe is attentive to individual lives. He cared for Hannah in her quiet distress just as surely as He directed the national destiny of Israel.

  • Life and Fruitfulness Come from God: Human effort and natural processes are subordinate to divine providence. Hannah's fertility and the birth of her children were direct expressions of God's favor and life-giving power.

  • God Preserves His Work: While human institutions, like Eli's priesthood, may falter through sin, God continues His sovereign purpose. He personally protected and nurtured young Samuel, preparing him to lead Israel into a new spiritual era.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:21, the narrator records that "the LORD visited Hannah, so that she conceived and bore three sons and two daughters." The term "visited" refers to a special action of divine favor and attention. Hannah had generously surrendered her firstborn son Samuel to serve at the tabernacle in fulfillment of her vow. God responded to her sacrifice not by leaving her empty-handed, but by overflowing her home with five more children.

The passage sets up a deliberate contrast. In the surrounding verses, the sons of Eli were abusing their priestly position and showing contempt for sacred offerings. Yet while corrupt leaders were failing in the sanctuary, God was quietly working in the life of a faithful family.

The verse concludes by stating that "the child Samuel grew before the LORD." This phrasing indicates that Samuel's growth took place in the immediate, personal presence of God. His physical, spiritual, and moral development was guarded by God Himself, shielding him from the corruption surrounding him at Shiloh.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 — "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens." Here the personal name is paired with God's creative title, introducing His intimate work in forming humanity and caring for creation.

  • Exodus 13:5 — "And it shall be, when the LORD brings you into the land of the Canaanites... which He swore to your fathers to give you..." The name highlights God as the faithful covenant keeper who brings His promises to fulfillment.

  • Joshua 14:12 — "Now therefore, give me this mountain of which the LORD spoke in that day..." Caleb recalls God's specific promise, trusting in His personal presence to conquer difficult terrain.

  • 2 Samuel 22:2 — "And he said: 'The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer...'" David uses the personal divine name to praise God for shielding and saving him throughout his trial-filled life.

  • Psalms 25:1 — "To You, O LORD, I lift up my soul." David speaks directly to God by His covenant name in earnest prayer and complete trust.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The statement that Samuel grew "before the LORD" offers an encouraging perspective on spiritual growth. True growth happens when a person lives conscious of God's presence. Even though Samuel was raised in an environment marked by poor leadership and moral compromise, living "before the LORD" kept him pure and prepared him for his prophetic calling.

Furthermore, Hannah's experience shows the generous nature of God's rewards. She gave her single son to God's service, and God blessed her with five additional children. God never remains in debt to those who place their trust and treasure in His hands.
