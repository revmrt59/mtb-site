HEARD - (shâmaʻ, Strong's H8085)

HEARD - ( shama, Strong’s [H8085] )

Summary:

The word primarily means to hear, listen, pay attention, or heed. In 1 Samuel 2:22, it describes Eli receiving reports concerning the corrupt and sinful behavior of his sons. The term highlights the transmission of alarming information that reaches an authority figure, setting the stage for a severe parental and priestly rebuke.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In biblical Hebrew, perception through sound involves more than mere physical hearing; it naturally extends to listening with intent, understanding, and responding to what is communicated. In the narrative of 1 Samuel, news travels through the community regarding the actions of Eli's sons at the tabernacle. The word captures the moment information crosses the barrier from private misconduct to public knowledge, drawing the attention of an aging father and high priest who was responsible for maintaining the holiness of worship.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to the physical sense of perceiving sound with the ear. Beyond that basic acoustic function, it frequently means to listen attentively, to understand a message, or to give heed to a report. When applied to instructions or warnings, it often carries the active sense of obeying or complying with what was said. In this context, it simply denotes receiving information or reports about an event or behavior.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the term across a wide semantic range depending on who is speaking and who is listening. When people hear one another, it often describes the receipt of news, gossip, or official reports. When superiors hear complaints or grievances, it implies an awareness that demands investigation or action. In other settings, when humanity hears God's voice, the word emphasizes moral responsibility and the necessity of obedience. The nuance is determined almost entirely by the immediate conversational or narrative setting rather than the verb alone.

4. Theological Implications

The narrative around this occurrence reveals that hidden sin does not remain concealed before God or His people. Reports surface because actions have public and spiritual consequences. Eli's position as a father and spiritual leader meant that hearing these specific reports carried immediate moral weight. The broader witness of Scripture shows that God holds leaders accountable for what they allow in their households and spheres of responsibility, making the reception of such warnings a critical moment for repentance or judgment.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:22, Eli is an old man who learns of the egregious transgressions committed by his sons against the people and the sacrifices at Shiloh. The text states that he "heard" all that they were doing. This reception of bad news exposes his previous failure to restrain them adequately. The word highlights the painful confrontation between an indulgent father and the widespread wickedness of his family, prompting his eventual warning about the gravity of sinning directly against God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 3:8 — And they "heard" the sound of the Lord God walking in the garden in the cool of the day, and Adam and his wife hid themselves from the presence of the Lord God among the trees of the garden.

  • Leviticus 26:14 — But if you do not "obey" Me, and do not observe all these commandments,

  • Ecclesiastes 9:16 — Then I said: "Wisdom is better than strength. Nevertheless the poor man’s wisdom is despised, and his words are not "heard"."

  • Isaiah 42:2 — He shall not cry out, nor raise His voice, nor cause His voice to be "heard" in the street.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The dual capacity of the word to mean both "hearing" sound and "obeying" instruction reflects an ancient Hebrew worldview where listening and doing were closely linked. To truly hear a message from a superior or from God was to act upon it. In Eli's case, merely hearing the reports was not enough; the tragedy of the passage is that while the news reached his ears, the necessary structural and spiritual correction came too late to avert judgment.
