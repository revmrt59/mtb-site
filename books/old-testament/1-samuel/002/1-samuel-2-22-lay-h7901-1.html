LAY - (shâkab, Strong's H7901)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb shakhab means to lie down, rest, sleep, or recline. Depending on the setting, it can refer to normal physical sleep, resting in death with one's ancestors, or engaging in sexual relations. In 1 Samuel 2:22, the word describes the wicked conduct of Eli's sons, Hophni and Phinehas, who engaged in sexual acts with women ministering at the entrance of the tabernacle. Their actions demonstrate how deeply Eli's sons had corrupted the priesthood and dishonored God's holy worship.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

During the period of the judges and early Samuel, Shiloh served as the central place of worship for Israel. The tabernacle housed the Ark of the Covenant, where the priests presented sacrifices and led the nation in honoring God. Eli served as the high priest, but his sons, Hophni and Phinehas, used their sacred positions for selfish gain.

In 1 Samuel 2, the narrative contrasts the righteous growth of the young child Samuel with the flagrant wickedness of Eli's sons. Not content with taking choice portions of the sacrificial meat by force, Hophni and Phinehas turned the sanctuary itself into a place of moral filth. Verse 22 records Eli hearing about everything his sons were doing to Israel, including their sexual misconduct right at the doorway of the tabernacle.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Shakhab fundamentally describes the physical act of reclining or lying down. The specific meaning of the word depends on who is lying down and what the surrounding context specifies.

The word is used in three main ways across Scripture:

  • Physical rest or sleep: Lying down on a bed, the ground, or a couch to sleep or recover strength.

  • Death and burial: Lying down with one's fathers or resting in the grave at the end of life.

  • Sexual relations: Engaging in physical intimacy, often expressed by the phrase "lie with."

In 1 Samuel 2:22, shakhab carries this third sense of sexual intimacy.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

The Hebrew language often uses simple, physical action words as idioms for human behaviors. Shakhab literally describes putting one's body in a horizontal position, but prepositions and surrounding words direct its specific shade of meaning.

When a person lies down alone or under a tree, the word describes physical sleep or exhaustion. When a ruler lies down with his ancestors, the word serves as an expression for a peaceful death after a full life. When used alongside prepositions like "with," the word frequently becomes a modest idiom for sexual intercourse. Scripture uses this expression both for lawful sexual union within marriage and for illegitimate sexual acts such as adultery or abuse of authority.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of shakhab in 1 Samuel 2:22 carries severe theological weight because of where the sin took place. God's tabernacle represented His dwelling place among His people, a holy environment where Israel came to be cleansed from sin and to offer pure worship.

By engaging in sexual immorality with women serving at the entrance of the tabernacle, Eli's sons treated God's holy presence with utter contempt. They turned a place designed for divine worship into a site of self-gratification. This passage teaches that leadership in God's house brings high responsibility. Those who represent God to others must treat His worship with holy reverence, recognizing that profaning sacred duties brings divine judgment.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:22, shakhab appears in a report brought to the aging high priest Eli. The plural verb form identifies Hophni and Phinehas as the subjects performing the action.

The context places their action "at the door of the tabernacle of meeting." This detail highlights the audacity of their sin. They did not attempt to hide their corruption in secret; they committed sexual acts at the very threshold where worshipers arrived to seek God. The contrast between Samuel, who served faithfully before the Lord in the tabernacle, and Eli's sons, who degraded the tabernacle through worldly lusts, sets up God's coming judgment on the house of Eli.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 28:13 – "The land on which you lie I will give to you and your descendants." Here the word refers to Jacob lying down upon the ground to sleep, where God granted him a comforting vision.

  • Numbers 5:13 – "And a man lies with her carnally, and it be hidden from the eyes of her husband..." The word is used here in a legal setting regarding marital faithfulness and sexual union.

  • 1 Samuel 3:2 – "And it came to pass at that time, while Eli was lying down in his place..." In the chapter following the description of his sons' sins, the word describes Eli reclining for sleep at night as his sight failed.

  • 2 Samuel 7:12 – "When your days are fulfilled and you rest with your fathers..." God uses the word in a peaceful sense to describe David's eventual death and burial alongside his ancestors.

  • 1 Kings 19:5 – "Then as he lay and slept under a broom tree, suddenly an angel touched him..." The term describes the prophet Elijah lying down in exhaustion after fleeing into the wilderness.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The women mentioned in 1 Samuel 2:22 served or assembled at the tabernacle entrance, performing practical duties for the sanctuary. Hophni and Phinehas exploited their religious authority to abuse these women sexually. This passage shows that God sees not only the outward performance of religious rituals, but also the moral conduct and heart motives of those who lead His people.
