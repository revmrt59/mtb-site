EVIL - (raʻ, Strong's H7451)

Summary:

The Hebrew word ra' is the primary Old Testament term for that which is bad, harmful, corrupt, or wrong, standing as the direct opposite of good (tov). It describes a wide range of negative concepts, from physical hardship and disaster to moral guilt and wicked behavior. In 1 Samuel 2:23, Eli uses ra' to describe the corrupt practices of his sons, Hophni and Phinehas, whose public abuse of the priestly office and blatant immorality had become a widespread scandal across Israel.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The book of 1 Samuel opens during a spiritually dark era in Israel's history, near the end of the period of the judges. The center of worship was located at Shiloh, where Eli served as high priest. Eli's sons, Hophni and Phinehas, also ministered as priests, but the biblical narrative describes them as corrupt men who did not know the Lord. They treated the offerings brought to God with contempt by taking cooked sacrificial meat by force and committing sexual immorality at the tabernacle doorway.

In 1 Samuel 2:23, the aging high priest Eli confronts his sons after receiving reports about their disgraceful behavior. He asks them why they commit such offenses, explaining that he hears of their bad deeds from all the people gathered at Shiloh. The term ra' appears here as an adjective describing their deeds, highlighting the offensive and ruinous character of their ministry.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The root ra' fundamentally refers to anything that falls short of an established standard, whether moral, physical, or functional. When applied to physical circumstances, it describes distress, injury, trouble, or disaster. When applied to human behavior, it refers to moral evil, wickedness, and actions that violate God's law.

In Scripture, ra' covers several distinct shades of meaning:

  • Moral wickedness or ethical wrong, describing thoughts, motives, and actions contrary to God's righteousness.

  • Harm, injury, or misfortune inflicted on someone.

  • Misery, trouble, or ruin resulting from calamity or divine judgment.

In 1 Samuel 2:23, the word functions morally and ethically. Paired with the word for "deeds" or "matters," it identifies the actions of Eli's sons not merely as foolish or administrative errors, but as morally vile conduct in the sight of God and the community.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, ra' forms a central moral category, frequently contrasted with tov (good). The distinction between good and bad is rooted in God's holy character. What God commands and approves is tov; what violates His character and word is ra'.

The context determines whether ra' means moral wickedness or non-moral trouble. For instance, when describing human hearts, plans, or idolatrous practices, the word denotes sin and moral rebellion. Conversely, when describing a severe famine, a devastating war, or an unpleasant situation, the word refers to hardship or calamity. In passages concerning God's law and sanctuary, ra' carries heavy ethical weight, identifying actions that corrupt worship and destroy covenant faithful life.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of ra' in the context of Shiloh highlights the gravity of corrupt worship. Spiritual leadership carries strict accountability before God. When priests who represent God to the people engage in moral evil, they corrupt the worship of the sanctuary and lead the community into dishonor.

Furthermore, the narrative surrounding 1 Samuel 2:23 exposes the inadequacy of passive rebuke. Eli acknowledged that his sons' deeds were evil, yet he failed to remove them from their holy office or restrain their wicked conduct. Recognizing evil is not the same as taking action against it. God holds leaders accountable not only for avoiding personal wickedness, but also for upholding righteousness within the spheres authority entrusted to them.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:23, Eli tells his sons, "Why do you do such things? For I hear of your evil deeds from all the people." The use of ra' here underscores the public nature of their sin. The report of their behavior had spread throughout the nation, causing God's people to transgress.

The word choice shows that Eli was not ignorant of his sons' guilt. He explicitly identified their conduct as ra' ("evil"). However, the contrast between Eli's soft verbal question and the severe nature of their evil actions reveals a tragedy in leadership. Eli gave a mild parental warning where divine justice required official priestly removal. As the narrative unfolds, God brings judgment upon the house of Eli because his sons made themselves vile and he did not restrain them.

  • Job 1:1 — "There was a man in the land of Uz, whose name was Job; and that man was blameless and upright, and one who feared God and shunned evil."

  • Ecclesiastes 5:1 — "Keep your foot when you go to the house of God; and draw near to hear rather than to give the sacrifice of fools, for they do not know that they do evil."

  • Isaiah 56:2 — "Blessed is the man who does this... Who keeps from defiling the Sabbath, and keeps his hand from doing any evil."

  • Jeremiah 29:11 — "'For I know the thoughts that I think toward you,' says the LORD, 'thoughts of peace and not of evil, to give you a future and a hope.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

Grammatically, ra' in 1 Samuel 2:23 is a masculine plural adjective. It modifies the plural noun "deeds" (or "matters"), agreeing with it in gender and number. This simple grammatical agreement points directly to the specific acts performed by Hophni and Phinehas.

The passage contrasts the corrupt priesthood of Eli's sons with the quiet growth of young Samuel. While Eli's sons were committing evil deeds at the tabernacle, Samuel was growing in favor both with the Lord and with men. Scripture sets the evil of the existing priests against the backdrop of God preparing a faithful servant to replace them.
