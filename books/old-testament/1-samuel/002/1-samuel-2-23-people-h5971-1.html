PEOPLE - (ʻam, Strong's H5971)

WORD - ( am, Strong’s [H5971] )

Summary:

The Hebrew word am generally refers to a people, nation, crowd, or assembled community of individuals joined by common ties. In 1 Samuel 2:23, Eli uses the word to describe the general public or community who have witnessed the wicked and corrupt behavior of his sons, Hophni and Phinehas.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The term appears frequently throughout the Hebrew Old Testament to designate a collective group, often specifically the nation of Israel as God's covenant people, but just as easily any gathered populace, tribe, or crowd of human beings. In the opening chapters of 1 Samuel, Israel is undergoing a painful transition from the era of the judges toward the establishment of the monarchy, a time marked by spiritual decay and leadership failure centered right at the tabernacle in Shiloh.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to a people, a nation, a tribe, or an assembly of persons. When used in a general sense, it describes a crowd or a populace without specific national or covenantal distinctions. When applied to Israel, it often highlights their corporate identity as a community belonging together. In 1 Samuel 2:23, the word designates the ordinary people of Israel—the community who observed the scandalous actions of Eli's sons at the tent of meeting.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the term across a wide semantic range. Sometimes it distinguishes ordinary citizens or soldiers from their rulers. At other times, it emphasizes the unity of a nation facing war, worship, or judgment. The word frequently points to the human social fabric, capturing groups of people interacting with leaders, prophets, and kings.

4. Theological Implications

While the term itself simply means a people or community, its backdrop in historical narratives like 1 Samuel highlights the serious accountability of religious leaders before the broader community. When leaders sin openly before the people, the moral and spiritual standing of the entire community is affected, bringing public reproach upon the name of God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:23, Eli confronts his morally bankrupt sons by asking why they do such wicked things, noting that he hears of their evil dealings from all the people. Here, the word highlights the wide public awareness of their corruption. Their sins were not hidden private failures; the common people were already talking about and stumbling over the blatant abuse of priestly authority at the sanctuary.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 12:27 — "Then the people bowed their heads and worshiped."

  • Joshua 6:5 — "All the people shall shout with a great shout..."

  • Judges 14:3 — "Then his father and mother said to him, 'Is there no woman among the daughters of your brethren, or among all my people, that you must go and get a wife from the uncircumcised Philistines?'"

  • Proverbs 11:14 — "Where there is no counsel, the people fall; but in the multitude of counselors there is safety."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The public nature of sin among leaders creates widespread stumbling. In 1 Samuel 2, Eli's failure to restrain his sons effectively placed a spiritual burden on the entire community of worshippers who came to Shiloh seeking the Lord.
