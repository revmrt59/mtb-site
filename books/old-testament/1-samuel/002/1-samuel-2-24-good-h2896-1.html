GOOD - (ṭôwb, Strong's H2896)

Summary:

Tovah refers to that which is beneficial, pleasant, morally right, favorable, or excellent. In 1 Samuel 2:24, the word describes the quality of the report or news circulating among the congregation of Israel. By stating that he hears "no good report," Eli acknowledges that the public scandal caused by his sons was dishonoring the LORD and leading God's people into sin.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In 1 Samuel 2, Israel stood at a critical spiritual transition point. The sanctuary at Shiloh served as the central place of worship, where priests represented God to the nation. However, Eli’s sons, Hophni and Phinehas, abused their priestly office. They treated the offerings of the LORD with contempt, forcibly took choice portions of sacrifices for themselves, and committed immorality at the tabernacle entrance.

Word of their corrupt behavior spread rapidly throughout the nation. In verse 24, Eli confronts his sons regarding these widespread accounts. He raises concern over the news reaching his ears, noting that the report circulating among God's assembly was far from commendable.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Tovah can refer to physical beauty, practical benefit, moral uprightness, high quality, or favorable news. Depending on the context, it describes anything that aligns with God's standards, promotes human well-being, or brings genuine satisfaction.

In this grammatical form, the word is feminine and singular, matching the Hebrew word for "report" or "rumor" in the verse. Here it refers to news or testimony that is favorable, clean, and encouraging. When combined with the negative particle ("no good report"), the expression describes news that is scandalous, morally ruinous, and deeply dishonorable.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament narratives, poetry, and wisdom literature, the root word covers a broad range of positive qualities:

  • Practical benefit and physical well-being.

  • Moral integrity and ethical behavior.

  • Favorable, encouraging, or joyful news.

  • Aesthetic appeal and beauty.

When applied to news or public testimony, a "good report" brings joy, strengthens community trust, and honors God. Conversely, when the word is negated—as in Eli's statement—it highlights news that causes grief, damages public righteousness, and spreads spiritual harm.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of this word in Eli's confrontation highlights the public nature of spiritual leadership. Priests were called to reflect God’s holiness and lead the nation in true worship. When leaders fall into blatant sin, the resulting report does not stay hidden; it dishonors God before the entire community.

The passage also illustrates the danger of passive leadership. Eli correctly diagnosed the news as "no good," yet his verbal rebuke lacked decisive corrective action. Recognizing that a situation is bad is not the same as taking active steps to restrain evil. Because Eli failed to discipline his sons, God brought divine judgment upon his family line.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:24, Eli tells his sons, "No, my sons! For it is no good report that I hear. You make the LORD's people transgress." Calling the scandal "no good report" functions as a soft phrasing for an appalling reality. The news circulating was not merely unpleasant gossip; it was evidence that the sanctuary leaders were actively leading the nation into rebellion against God.

The passage explicitly links the bad report to its outcome: the priests' immoral conduct caused the people of Israel to stumble. Rather than serving as a source of spiritual light and wholesome testimony, the sanctuary at Shiloh had become a place of public shame.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Grammatically, the singular feminine form of the word in 1 Samuel 2:24 directly modifies the singular feminine noun for "report" or "news." This grammatical agreement shows that Eli was addressing a specific, widespread report that had reached his ears.

The contrast in the chapter is striking: while Samuel was growing in favor with both God and men, Eli's sons were generating a shameful public report. The narrative contrasts the ruinous influence of corrupt leaders with the quiet, faithful growth of a young servant devoted to God.
