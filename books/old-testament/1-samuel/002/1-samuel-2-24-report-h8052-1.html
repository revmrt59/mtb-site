REPORT - (shᵉmûwʻâh, Strong's H8052)

HEBREW WORD - ( shemuah , Strong’s [H8052] )

Summary:

The word refers to something heard, such as news, a rumor, a message, or a public report. In 1 Samuel 2:24, Eli uses the term to describe the troubling accounts circulating about his sons' sinful behavior among the Lord's people.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew root behind this term relates to hearing or listening. Throughout the Old Testament, it describes information that travels from person to person or community to community. In ancient times, before modern communication, news and public perception depended entirely on travelers, messengers, and eyewitness accounts carried by word of mouth.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term can refer to news, tidings, a rumor, a message, or a public report. It simply designates information that is heard or received by others. When used in a negative sense, it points to discreditable news or damaging accounts about someone's actions. When used positively, it can describe encouraging tidings or a widespread, favorable reputation.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses this word across various contexts to capture the transmission of news, whether true or false, alarming or comforting. It often describes the public hearing of an event that carries weight and consequences for those involved. In prophetic literature, it refers to a divine message or warning that demands a response from the listeners. In historical narratives, it frequently marks the moment troubling news reaches a leader or household, setting off a chain of events.

4. Theological Implications

Information travels, and hidden actions eventually come to light. The presence of damaging public reports regarding those who serve in leadership highlights the destructive nature of sin within the covenant community. God sees all actions, and human sin does not remain hidden forever; it eventually becomes a matter of public knowledge that brings shame to the name of the Lord.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:24, Eli confronts his wicked sons, Hophni and Phinehas, regarding the evil reports circulating among the people. The news of their blatant disregard for the Lord's sacrifices and their moral corruption had reached the ears of the entire congregation of Israel. Eli uses the term to impress upon his sons the gravity of their public witness. Their actions were not private failures; they were generating widespread scandal that caused the people of God to stumble and transgress.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Psalms 112:7 — "He will not be afraid of evil tidings [shemuah]; His heart is steadfast, trusting in the Lord."

  • Proverbs 15:30 — "The light of the eyes rejoices the heart, and a good report [shemuah] makes the bones healthy."

  • Isaiah 53:1 — "Who has believed our report [shemuah]? And to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?"

  • Obadiah 1:1 — "The vision of Obadiah. Thus says the Lord God concerning Edom— We have heard a report [shemuah] from the Lord, and an ambassador has been sent among the nations..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The word underscores the reality of accountability within leadership and community life. What leaders do in private eventually surfaces in public. In Eli's situation, the failure to restrain his sons transformed their private lawlessness into a public stumbling block for the entire nation of Israel, demonstrating how personal compromise inevitably damages the wider testimony of God's people.
