HEED - (shâmaʻ, Strong's H8085)

HEED - ( shama, Strong’s [H8085] )

Summary:

The word primarily means to hear, listen, pay attention to, or obey. In 1 Samuel 2:25, it describes the rebellious sons of Eli refusing to listen to their father's correction, choosing instead to persist in their sin despite his warning.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In biblical Hebrew, the ear is closely linked to the heart and will. To hear is not merely to register sound waves, but to respond with action, compliance, or heed. In the narrative of 1 Samuel, Eli's sons, Hophni and Phinehas, occupy sacred office as priests yet live in open wickedness. When Eli rebukes their corrupt behavior, the text highlights their stubborn refusal to pay attention or submit to godly counsel.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb can refer to physical hearing, such as perceiving sound with the ears. Beyond mere audile perception, it frequently means to listen with discernment, pay careful attention, or give heed to instruction. When directed toward authority, a warning, or a command, it carries the clear sense of obeying or yielding to what is said.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses this word across many settings, ranging from God hearing the cry of the afflicted to people listening to a message or obeying a command. The context always determines whether the focus is simply taking in information or actively submitting to a directive. In contexts of moral correction, hearing involves a choice of the will either to respect the speaker and change course or to turn away in defiance.

4. Theological Implications

Listening to God and His appointed authorities is fundamental to covenant life. Throughout Scripture, spiritual stubbornness is consistently described as a failure to hear or pay attention to divine instruction. When individuals reject correction from those God has placed over them, they reveal a deeper rebellion against God Himself.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:25, Eli confronts his wicked sons with the gravity of sining against God, noting that if a person sins against another, an arbiter may intervene, but if a person sins against the Lord, who can help? Yet the sons stubbornly refuse to heed their father's warning. The narrative immediately adds a sobering reason: the Lord desired to put them to death. Their refusal to listen is thus bound up with their hardening under divine judgment.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 3:8 — And they heard the sound of the Lord God walking in the garden in the cool of the day, and Adam and his wife hid themselves from the presence of the Lord God among the trees of the garden.

  • Deuteronomy 30:8 — And you will return and obey the voice of the Lord and do all His commandments which I command you today.

  • 1 Kings 5:1 — Now Hiram king of Tyre sent his servants to Solomon, because he heard that they had anointed him king in place of his father, for Hiram was a lover of David all his days.

  • Psalms 84:8 — O Lord God of hosts, hear my prayer; Give ear, O God of Jacob! Selah

7. Other Relevant Insights

The refusal of Eli's sons to listen illustrates how persistent sin dulls the conscience. Warning becomes ineffective when a heart is fully set on doing evil. In biblical narrative, a closed ear is often the prelude to judgment, showing that the ability to receive correction is a gracious gift that must not be despised.
