FAVOR - (ṭôwb, Strong's H2896)

Summary:

The Hebrew word tob is a foundational term describing that which is good, pleasing, beneficial, or desirable. In 1 Samuel 2:26, it describes the child Samuel's growth into a state of moral goodness and acceptance, showing that he was pleasing and favorably regarded both by the LORD and by the people around him. In a chapter dominated by the corruption of Eli's sons, the word highlights Samuel's godly development and divine approval.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

1 Samuel 2 presents a sharp contrast between two generations of leadership at the sanctuary in Shiloh. The sons of the high priest Eli were corrupt men who abused their authority, treated God's offerings with contempt, and led Israel into spiritual decline. In the middle of this dark period, the young boy Samuel was serving in the tabernacle under Eli.

While judgment was being announced against the house of Eli, God was quietly preparing a faithful prophet. The narrative repeatedly pauses its description of the wicked priests to report on Samuel's progress. In 1 Samuel 2:26, the author uses the word tob to summarize how Samuel was growing up in the middle of a compromised environment.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word tob is the standard Hebrew term for "good." Its core meaning refers to anything that meets a high standard, fulfills its intended purpose, or brings delight and benefit. Depending on the context, the word can refer to physical beauty, practical usefulness, moral integrity, or relational approval.

When used to describe a person's standing before God or other people, the word means being acceptable, pleasing, or favorably regarded. In 1 Samuel 2:26, translated as "favor," the word indicates that young Samuel possessed a character and life that brought pleasure to God and earned the genuine respect of the community.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In the Hebrew Bible, tob is applied across many areas of life:

  • Divine Creation: God evaluates His creation as "good," meaning it is well-ordered, complete, and suited for its divine design.

  • Moral Character: The word describes actions and attitudes that line up with God's moral law.

  • Practical Benefit: It refers to news, gifts, or conditions that bring genuine comfort, refreshment, or help to people.

  • Relational Delight: It describes a person whose conduct and spirit make them pleasant and well-pleasing to those around them.

When applied to relationships, tob means more than mere popularity. It indicates that a person's life reflects a genuine quality of goodness that garners authentic approval.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of tob in 1 Samuel 2:26 carries several important theological truths:

  • Divine Sovereignty in Preserving Righteousness: Even when the highest spiritual offices in the land were corrupted, God preserved a faithful servant. God's work does not fail when human leaders fail.

  • Integrated Character: True godly character pleases God and commends itself to people. Samuel's life was centered on God, yet his daily conduct made a positive impression on those who observed him.

  • Prophetic Foreshadowing: The description of Samuel's holistic growth provided a pattern for describing the ideal leader in Israel. Luke later draws upon this exact passage in Luke 2:52 to describe the childhood growth of Jesus Christ, who grew in wisdom, stature, and favor with God and men.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:26, the phrase "in favor both with the LORD and men" translates tob alongside the prepositions indicating relationship. The word forms a deliberate contrast with the behavior of Eli's sons described in the preceding verses.

Eli had warned his sons that their sins were directly against the LORD, yet they refused to listen. Immediately following that description of stubborn rebellion, verse 26 opens with Samuel's progress. While Eli's sons were becoming increasingly vile and hated, Samuel was growing in "goodness" or "favor."

The passage emphasizes two dimensions of his growth: physical development ("in stature") and relational/moral character ("in favor"). Samuel was not merely growing older; he was growing better. His conduct pleased God, and his integrity was obvious to the people of Israel who visited the tabernacle.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The dual aspect of Samuel's favor—with God and with men—shows that true spiritual health produces visible fruit in everyday life. Favor with God is foundational, stemming from a heart oriented toward worship and obedience. Favor with men often follows as a natural outcome when a person lives with honesty, humility, and kindness.

Furthermore, the word tob highlights God's approval during a time when spiritual discernment in Israel was low. Even in a dark culture, true goodness stands out clearly and receives God's seal of approval.
