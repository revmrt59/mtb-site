GOD - (ʼĕlôhîym, Strong's H430)

Summary:

The word 'elohim is the standard Hebrew term for God, denoting supreme divine power, majesty, and ruler-ship. While the form carries a plural ending, when referring to the one true God of Israel it takes singular verbs, highlighting His unique glory and complete authority. In 1 Samuel 2:27, the word appears in the title "man of God," identifying an unnamed prophet sent to deliver a solemn divine message of rebuke and judgment to the high priest Eli.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

1 Samuel 2 describes a period of severe spiritual decay in Israel. Eli served as high priest at Shiloh, but his sons, Hophni and Phinehas, abused their sacred duties. They treated the offerings of the Lord with contempt and committed immoral acts at the sanctuary. Eli offered mild verbal reproof but failed to restrain his sons or honor God’s holy standards.

In the middle of this corruption, an unnamed prophet arrives on the scene. He is introduced simply as a "man of God." This unexpected appearance shows that God does not leave Himself without a witness. When formal religious leaders compromise their calling, God raises up His own spokesperson to declare His truth and hold leadership accountable.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word 'elohim can refer to the one true God, to false gods worshipped by foreign nations, or occasionally to heavenly beings. When used of the true God of Israel, the word functions as a title of supreme divine majesty.

In Hebrew grammar, the word carries a masculine plural form, yet it is regularly used with singular verbs and adjectives when referring to the Lord. This usage highlights God's preeminence, fullness, and sovereign power. In 1 Samuel 2:27, 'elohim refers specifically to the living Creator and Covenant God who redeemed Israel from Egypt and demands absolute holiness from His servants.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament historical narratives, poetry, and prophecy, 'elohim reveals God as Creator, Ruler, and righteous Judge of all the earth. The term emphasizes God's power over all created things and His supreme right to command obedience.

When 'elohim is combined with the Hebrew word for man ('ish), the compound phrase "man of God" creates a distinct title for a prophet. A "man of God" is someone set apart by God to carry His authority and deliver His precise words. The title guarantees that the messenger's authority does not come from human office or priestly heritage, but directly from God Himself.

4. Theological Implications

The arrival of the "man of God" in 1 Samuel 2 establishes several vital truths about God's character and government:

  • God's Holiness and Justice: Position and privilege do not shield anyone from God's righteous judgment. Even the high priest and his house are accountable to the holy God.

  • God's Sovereign Word: When human leadership strays, God's word remains active and uncompromised. God can raise up a messenger from outside the established hierarchy to speak His truth.

  • God's Grace in Warning: Before executing judgment, God sends His word to expose sin and reveal the consequences of disobedience, demonstrating His patience and righteous order.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:27, the phrase "man of God" creates a sharp contrast between Eli and the messenger. Eli held the highest sacred office in the land, yet he permitted sacred duties to be profaned. The messenger possessed no official title or lineage mentioned in the text, yet he carried full divine authority because he spoke the true word of 'elohim.

The message begins with a reminder of God's sovereign grace: God had revealed Himself to Eli's ancestors in Egypt and chosen them for the priesthood. By setting Eli's current spiritual neglect against God's past redemptive actions, the title "man of God" highlights that God alone sets the standards for priesthood and worship. Human leaders cannot treat God's holy requirements as optional.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Deuteronomy 7:21 — "You shall not be terrified of them; for the LORD your God is among you, the great and awesome God."

  • Psalms 42:1 — "As the deer pants for the water brooks, So pants my soul for You, O God."

  • Jeremiah 23:36 — "And the burden of the LORD you shall mention no more. For every man's word will be his burden, for you have perverted the words of the living God, the LORD of hosts, our God."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Immediately following the title "man of God," the prophet speaks the messenger formula: "Thus says the LORD." This connects the general divine title 'elohim (God as sovereign Creator) directly to God's personal covenant name, YHWH ("the LORD"). The prophet's authority rests on the fact that the almighty Creator ('elohim) is the very same Lord who made a covenant with Israel.

The anonymity of the "man of God" in 1 Samuel 2:27 is also significant. By keeping the messenger nameless, Scripture keeps the reader's focus entirely on the origin and content of the message rather than on the personality of the prophet.
