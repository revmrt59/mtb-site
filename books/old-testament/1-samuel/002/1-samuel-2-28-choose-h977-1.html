CHOOSE - (bâchar, Strong's H977)

Summary:

Bachar means to choose, select, or single out someone or something after deliberate consideration. In 1 Samuel 2:28, the word highlights God's sovereign initiative in selecting the priestly house of Aaron from among all the tribes of Israel to perform holy service at His altar.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In 1 Samuel 2, an unnamed "man of God" arrives to deliver a message of divine judgment against Eli, the high priest of Israel. Eli’s sons, Hophni and Phinehas, had shown profound disrespect for the sanctuary by abusing their office, exploiting worshipers, and treating the Lord’s sacrifices with contempt. Eli failed to restrain his sons, effectively honoring his family above God.

Before announcing the coming downfall of Eli’s lineage, the prophet delivers a solemn reminder of God's past mercy. God reminds Eli of how He revealed Himself to Aaron's ancestors in Egypt and chose them to be His consecrated priests. The verb bachar appears at the beginning of this remembrance to contrast God's generous calling with the corrupt behavior of Eli’s household.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Bachar refers to the act of selecting or choosing from among multiple options. The word implies a conscious decision where one option is preferred and set apart.

When people are the subject, bachar describes decisions such as choosing a king, selecting soldiers for battle, or choosing a path of wisdom over folly. When God is the subject, bachar describes His sovereign decision to appoint specific individuals, families, tribes, or locations for special roles in His redemptive plan. God's choice is not forced by human accomplishment; it originates in His own purpose and grace.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament historical books, poetry, and prophetic literature, bachar functions as a key term for God's act of election. Scripture speaks of God choosing:

  • A nation: Israel is chosen to be His special treasure out of all the peoples of the earth.

  • A place: Mount Zion and Jerusalem are chosen as the location for His sanctuary.

  • A tribe and family: The tribe of Levi and the family of Aaron are chosen for sacred priestly ministry.

  • A leader: David is chosen to shepherd God’s people as king.

In all these instances, God's choice carries a double implication. First, it bestows a position of distinct favor and sacred trust. Second, it demands faithfulness. Divine choice is never a license for pride or self-indulgence; it sets the chosen party apart for dedicated service and obedience.

4. Theological Implications

God’s statement in 1 Samuel 2:28 illustrates that spiritual office is a divine privilege, not a human right or personal property. Aaron and his descendants did not earn or capture the priesthood; God freely conferred it upon them.

The passage also demonstrates that divine election does not exempt leaders from moral accountability. Eli and his sons assumed that because their family was chosen for the priesthood, their standing was secure regardless of how they behaved. However, God’s election establishes a standard of holiness. When religious leaders despise the duties attached to their calling, they bring divine judgment upon themselves. Privilege without reverence leads to ruin.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:28, bachar forms part of an indictment against Eli's house. God asks a rhetorical question: "Did I not choose him out of all the tribes of Israel to be My priest...?"

God explicitly lists the duties assigned through this choice: offering sacrifices on the altar, burning incense, wearing the priestly ephod, and receiving a portion of the fire-offerings for support. The verb appears in an infinitive absolute form, highlighting the deliberate and clear nature of God's original election.

By reminding Eli of this divine choice, God emphasizes the sheer ingratitude of Eli's sons. God elevated Aaron’s house from obscurity to the highest spiritual office in Israel, yet Eli's sons treated the altar offerings with greed and contempt. The mention of God's choice exposes the depth of their rebellion.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Deuteronomy 4:37 — "And because He loved your fathers, therefore He chose their descendants after them; and He brought you out of Egypt with His Presence, with His mighty power..."

  • Deuteronomy 18:5 — "For the LORD your God has chosen him out of all your tribes to stand to minister in the name of the LORD, him and his sons forever."

  • Psalms 78:68 — "But chose the tribe of Judah, Mount Zion which He loved."

  • Isaiah 41:8 — "But you, Israel, are My servant, Jacob whom I have chosen, the descendants of Abraham My friend."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between divine election and human unfaithfulness sets up a major transition in the book of 1 Samuel. Because Eli's house rejected the responsibilities of God's choice, God declared that He would raise up a faithful priest who would do according to what was in His heart and mind.

This pattern recurs shortly after when God rejects Saul and chooses David. God's act of choosing remains tied to His sovereign purpose, exalting those who honor Him and humbling those who despise His holy calling.
