EPHOD - (ʼêphôwd, Strong's H646)

WORD - ( ephod, Strong’s [H0646] )

Summary:

The Hebrew term refers to a sacred priestly garment worn during Israel's early history, specifically associated with the high priest and the administration of divine guidance. In 1 Samuel 2:28, the word highlights the sacred privileges and responsibilities entrusted to the house of Eli as part of their priestly service before God.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The term identifies a distinctive priestly garment prescribed in the Old Testament. Historically, the garment served as a key vestment worn by Aaron and his successors, featuring an ornate design of blue, purple, scarlet, and fine twisted linen, adorned with gold thread. Attached to it was the breastplate containing the Urim and Thummim, the sacred means by which God communicated His will to His people. Over time, the term also came to describe simpler priestly garments worn by lesser priests or individuals engaged in sanctuary service.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word refers specifically to a sacred vestment or priestly garment. In its fullest form, it was an elaborate apron-like garment suspended from the shoulders, bound by a skillfully woven band, and fitted with shoulder pieces holding engraved stones representing the tribes of Israel. While primarily denoting the high priestly garment, the word can also describe related ephods worn in various religious contexts throughout Israel's history.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the term to denote both the official high priestly vestment prescribed by the covenant law and, occasionally, similar garments associated with worship. In the historical books, the ephod frequently intersects with moments of divine inquiry, serving as the physical instrument connected to the priestly office of seeking guidance from God.

4. Theological Implications

The garment pointed beyond physical craftsmanship to the vital mediatorial role required between a holy God and sinful humanity. The presence of the tribal names on the vestment emphasized that the high priest bore the people before the Lord continually. However, the misuse or corruption of these sacred privileges—as seen in the history of Eli's household—demonstrates that outward religious symbols and positions do not guarantee divine favor when obedience and reverence are lacking.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In this passage, the term forms part of a divine rebuke delivered by a man of God to Eli. God reminds Eli that out of all the tribes of Israel, his family was chosen to wear the ephod and offer sacrifices before the altar. By recalling this specific privilege, the text underscores the great height from which Eli's sons have fallen through their greedy and lawless abuse of the sacrificial service. The mention of the ephod grounds the indictment in concrete historical and covenantal responsibilities that Eli failed to uphold.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 28:4 — "And these are the garments which they shall make: a breastplate, an ephod, a robe, a skillfully woven tunic, a turban, and a sash."

  • Exodus 39:2 — "He made the ephod of gold, blue, purple, and scarlet, and of fine twisted linen."

  • Exodus 29:5 — "Then you shall take the garments, put the tunic on Aaron, and the robe of the ephod, the ephod, and the breastplate, and gird him with the intricately woven band of the ephod."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The historical narrative surrounding the ephod reveals a tension between legitimate sanctuary worship and the persistent human temptation to manipulate sacred objects. While God instituted the garment for proper administration and guidance, later biblical accounts show how religious artifacts could be misdirected, reminding readers that true worship requires the heart's submission rather than mere reliance on religious forms.
