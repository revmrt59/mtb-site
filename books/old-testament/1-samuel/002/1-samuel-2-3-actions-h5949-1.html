ACTIONS - (ʻălîylâh, Strong's H5949)

  • ( , Strong’s [H5949] )

Summary:

The Hebrew term behind the English rendering "actions" refers broadly to deeds, works, or the practical behavior and accomplishments of a person. In 1 Samuel 2:3, the word describes human conduct weighed by the Lord, emphasizing that God looks past mere words to evaluate the true character of what people do.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word comes from a root denoting making, doing, or accomplishing. In Old Testament usage, it frequently points to the visible product of a person's life—their practical deeds, whether righteous or wicked. In the setting of 1 Samuel 2, Hannah is offering a prophetic prayer of praise after the birth of Samuel. She exalts God’s absolute sovereignty and moral consistency, warning human beings against pride because God observes and weighs every practical expression of human life.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to a deed, a work, an action, or a habitual practice. When applied to human beings, it highlights concrete behavior and lifestyle choices. When applied to God, it often points to His mighty acts of judgment or salvation. In this context, it encompasses the whole scope of human deeds and undertakings that are laid open before the all-knowing God.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses this term to remind readers that human behavior is never hidden from God. It often appears in contexts where human pride, arrogance, or deceitful speech are contrasted with God’s perfect knowledge. While people may boast in words, God examines the actual weight and quality of their deeds. The word brings a strong sense of moral accountability, showing that actions carry real weight in the divine balance.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, this verse underscores the omniscience and absolute justice of God. Because the Lord is a God of knowledge, human actions cannot be disguised by clever speech or self-justification. God does not judge by outward appearances or human pretensions; He weighs the moral reality of what people do. This truth humbles the arrogant and reassures the oppressed that divine justice governs human history.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Hannah’s song contrasts human boasting with divine sovereignty. Immediately preceding this term, she warns people not to let pride come out of their mouths, because the Lord is a God of knowledge and by Him actions are weighed. Here, the word highlights the contrast between human talk and divine evaluation. No matter how lofty a person's words may be, God looks directly at the actual deeds of their life.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Chronicles 16:8 — "Oh, give thanks to the Lord! Call upon His name; make known His deeds among the peoples!"

  • Psalms 9:11 — "Sing praises to the Lord, who dwells in Zion; declare His deeds among the people."

  • Psalms 103:7 — "He made known His ways to Moses, His acts to the children of Israel."

  • Isaiah 12:4 — "Praise the Lord, call upon His name; declare His deeds among the peoples, make mention that His name is exalted."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The metaphor of weighing actions suggests a judicial standard. In the ancient world, weights and measures were used to ensure honesty in trade; here, God uses an infallible standard to assess human behavior. This reinforces the biblical theme that human life has moral weight and consequence before the Creator.
