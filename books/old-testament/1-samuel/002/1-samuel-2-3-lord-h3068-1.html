LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

Yahweh is the personal divine name of the God of Israel, derived from the Hebrew verb meaning "to be." In 1 Samuel 2:3, the name identifies the personal, living God who possesses perfect knowledge and evaluates human actions, confronting human pride with His divine oversight.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The divine name Yahweh (translated "LORD" in small capital letters in most English Bibles) is the sacred personal name by which God made Himself known to Israel. In the narrative setting of 1 Samuel 2, Hannah opens her prayer with joyful worship after giving her long-awaited son Samuel to the service of the tabernacle at Shiloh. Having experienced deliverance from years of barrenness and personal reproach, Hannah expands her song of praise from her individual story to a broader declaration of God's universal sovereignty. In verse 3, she addresses those who speak arrogantly, warning them that the LORD is the true judge who sees and evaluates everything.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Yahweh is a proper name identifying the one true God of Israel. Etymologically, the name is closely tied to the Hebrew verb hayah, meaning "to be" or "to exist." When God revealed His name at the burning bush, He explained it with the phrase "I AM WHO I AM," highlighting His self-existence, presence, and faithful reality.

As a proper noun, Yahweh distinguishes the personal God of Israel from generic terms for deity. It does not merely describe a general divine power or abstract ruler; it names the specific, personal God who makes promises, enters into relationship with His people, and acts in human history.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In English translations like the NKJV, the proper name Yahweh is rendered as "LORD" with small capital letters to distinguish it from Adonai, which means "master" or "lord."

Across the Old Testament, the divine name appears in diverse settings:

  • In covenant relationships, identifying the God who redeemed Israel from bondage.

  • In legal and ethical contexts, anchoring moral commands in God's holy character.

  • In historical narratives, showing God actively guiding the destiny of nations and individuals.

  • In worship and poetry, invoking the personal presence of God in prayer and praise.

The name emphasizes that God is not an impersonal force, but a living, active person who hears the cries of the afflicted and brings down the proud.

4. Theological Implications

The revelation of the divine name Yahweh grounds biblical faith in God's self-revelation. God is not defined by human imagination; He reveals Himself on His own terms. Because He is the self-existent God who acts in history, His people can rely on His presence and truthfulness.

In 1 Samuel 2:3, Hannah connects the personal name Yahweh with God's perfect knowledge. Calling Him the "God of knowledge" shows that His supreme authority is accompanied by absolute awareness. Human rulers and mockers may evaluate circumstances based on outward appearances, but Yahweh knows all things thoroughly and accurately.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:3, Hannah places Yahweh in direct opposition to human arrogance: "Talk no more so very proudly; let no arrogance come from your mouth, for the LORD is the God of knowledge; and by Him actions are weighed."

Hannah's rival, Peninnah, had provoked and mocked her for years because of her barrenness. Now that God had answered Hannah's prayer, Hannah warned against such boastful speech. The rationale for hushing proud talk is the character of Yahweh Himself. Because Yahweh is the God of knowledge, no hidden motive or secret contempt escapes His notice. He weighs actions with perfect fairness. The passage demonstrates that human pride is folly in the presence of the God who sees every heart.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 — "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens."

  • Exodus 13:5 — "And it shall be, when the LORD brings you into the land of the Canaanites... which He swore to your fathers to give you..."

  • 2 Samuel 22:2 — "And he said: 'The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer...'"

  • Psalms 25:1 — "To You, O LORD, I lift up my soul."

  • Proverbs 16:6 — "In mercy and truth atonement is provided for iniquity; and by the fear of the LORD one departs from evil."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah’s song in 1 Samuel 2 provides the thematic foundation for the entire book of Samuel. Her declaration that Yahweh exalts the humble and lowers the proud anticipates the rise of David and the fall of Saul. By invoking the LORD as the one who weighs human actions, the passage introduces a central biblical principle: God's evaluation of human life is the ultimate reality, rendering human self-exaltation completely empty.
