ESTEEMED - (qâlal, Strong's H7043)

Summary:

The word qalal fundamentally means to be light, swift, or of little weight. When applied to people or actions, it describes treating someone as insignificant, making light of them, or bringing them low. In 1 Samuel 2:30, God uses this word to declare that those who treat Him with contempt will themselves be held in low regard and brought to shame by Him.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root idea behind qalal involves lightness or lack of weight. In ancient Hebrew thought, weight carried the idea of importance, honor, and authority, while lightness represented insignificance, dishonor, or lack of worth.

In the historical setting of 1 Samuel 2, Eli the high priest allowed his sons, Hophni and Phinehas, to abuse their sacred office. They stole choice portions of sacrifices and brought corrupt practices into the tabernacle. By prioritizing his sons over God's worship, Eli treated God's sacred holiness as a light thing. God sent a prophet to deliver a severe judgment on Eli's family, declaring the spiritual principle that governs His interactions with humanity: God honors those who honor Him, but those who despise Him will be brought low.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of qalal is to be light or swift. It can describe a physical reduction in weight or intensity, such as floodwaters receding or a burden being lightened.

When used in a social, moral, or spiritual sense, the word means to consider or treat someone as light, trifling, or unworthy of respect. Depending on the context, it can refer to:

  • Being swift or fast in motion.

  • Making a burden lighter or lessening a heavy load.

  • Treating someone with contempt, disdaining them, or regarding them as insignificant.

  • Pronouncing a curse or reviling someone by declaring them to be of low value.

In 1 Samuel 2:30, the word describes the divine response to human contempt. Here it means to be held in low esteem, reduced to insignificance, or brought into humiliation by God.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses qalal in a variety of physical and relational settings. In physical contexts, it describes things that move quickly or lose their weight, such as swift runners or receding waters after a flood. It also describes easing a political or financial burden, such as a king lightening the taxes on his people.

In relational contexts, qalal operates as the opposite of honor. To honor someone in Hebrew thought is to recognize their "weight" or dignity. To practice qalal toward someone is to treat them as light, worthless, or easily dismissed.

When humans practice this toward God or His appointed authority, it manifests as reviling, cursing, or insolent disregard. When God is the one who causes a person to be qalal, it signifies a loss of honor, position, and divine favor. Instead of enjoying a position of lasting glory, those who disregard God are stripped of their dignity and brought into shame.

4. Theological Implications

The statement in 1 Samuel 2:30 lays down a foundational principle of divine justice: God's relationship with people involves real moral accountability. God cannot be mocked or treated as negligible.

When people treat God's holiness, worship, or commands as light matters, they act contrary to reality. God is the sovereign Creator whose weight of glory is absolute. Attempting to reduce God to a minor concern does not diminish God's actual glory, but it guarantees that the person who does so will lose spiritual weight and standing. God ensures that those who attempt to make light of Him will ultimately experience the lightness of their own temporary glory as it fades into disgrace.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:30, qalal forms a direct contrast with the word for honor (kabad). Kabad literally means to be heavy or weighty, representing rich honor and reverence. God states, "those who honor Me I will honor [kabad], and those who despise Me shall be lightly esteemed [qalal]."

Eli's sons treated the LORD's offerings with public contempt, taking what belonged to God and using it for their own pleasure. Eli rebuked them mildly but refused to remove them from office, showing that he valued his sons' approval more than God's honor. God answered this insult by reversing the promise of perpetual priestly leadership for Eli's lineage.

The contrast in this verse demonstrates an exact correspondence in divine judgment. Those who treat God as heavy and important receive enduring honor from Him. Those who treat God as light and trivial will find that God treats their status as light and trivial, removing their influence and bringing their priesthood to an end.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The word qalal highlights the foolishness of human pride. When King David danced before the LORD as the ark was brought into Jerusalem, his wife Michal despised him for setting aside royal pomp. David responded in 2 Samuel 6:22 that he was willing to become even more "undignified" (qalal) for the sake of honoring God.

This creates a powerful spiritual lesson when placed alongside Eli's family. Eli's sons refused to humble themselves before God and were consequently brought low (qalal) in judgment. David willingly humbled himself (qalal) to exalt God and was established in lasting honor. True honor comes not from seeking personal prestige, but from treating God with the absolute weight and reverence He deserves.
