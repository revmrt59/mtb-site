ARM - (zᵉrôwaʻ, Strong's H2220)

Summary:

The Hebrew word zeroa primarily refers to the physical arm or forearm. Metaphorically, it represents human or divine strength, capability, power, and authority. In 1 Samuel 2:31, the word is used figuratively to describe the complete breaking and removal of the strength, vitality, and influence of Eli's priestly household as a divine judgment for their unfaithfulness.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In 1 Samuel 2, Israel was going through a period of deep spiritual decline under the high priesthood of Eli. Eli’s sons, Hophni and Phinehas, treated the sacrifices of the Lord with contempt, committing severe moral and liturgical abuses at the tabernacle. Eli rebuked his sons mildly but failed to restrain their evil actions or remove them from office, effectively honoring his sons above God.

In response, God sent an unnamed prophet ("a man of God") to announce a severe divine decree against Eli’s priestly lineage. In 1 Samuel 2:31, God declares that He will "cut off your arm and the arm of your father’s house." This judgment marks a dramatic transition in Israel's leadership, paving the way for the eventual rise of Samuel and the removal of Eli’s house from prominent priestly service.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Zeroa can refer directly to the physical arm or shoulder of a human being or animal. Because the arm is the main instrument through which a person performs labor, wields weapons, and exerts physical influence, the word frequently carries a metaphorical meaning.

Depending on who is described, the word can mean:

  • Physical limb: the arm, forearm, or shoulder.

  • Human vigor: physical power, personal strength, or military force.

  • Divine power: God's supreme strength, authority, and saving action in history.

  • Support or authority: the power or standing by which a family or ruler governs and protects others.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, zeroa serves as a picture of active power. When applied to God, the word often appears alongside descriptive terms like "a mighty hand" or "an outstretched arm," picturing God intervening to deliver His people, conquer enemies, or sustain the weak.

When applied to human beings, zeroa can refer to ordinary human capability. However, Scripture frequently contrasts human power with divine power. Human strength is frail and temporary, whereas God's arm is invincible.

A specific idiom appears in judgment passages where God promises to "break" or "cut off" a person's arm. In ancient imagery, breaking or cutting off the arm does not merely describe a physical injury; it symbolizes removing a person's power, stripping away their authority, and rendering them incapable of defending themselves or exercising leadership.

4. Theological Implications

The usage of zeroa in divine judgment reveals several foundational biblical truths:

  • God is the ultimate source of human authority and vitality. Human strength and official positions are held under God's sovereignty.

  • God holds religious leaders to a high standard. Misusing sacred office to dishonor God results in the forfeiture of divine backing.

  • Relying on human strength rather than divine grace leads to weakness. When human authority exalts itself against God's commands, God can easily break that strength.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:31, God announces: "Behold, the days are coming that I will cut off your arm and the arm of your father’s house, so that there will not be an old man in your house."

In this verse, "arm" does not refer to a literal physical amputation. Instead, it functions as a metaphor for the vitality, influence, and strength of Eli’s family line. The arm represents the capability of Eli's house to exercise priestly authority and maintain family strength across generations.

The second half of the verse clarifies the meaning of the metaphor: "so that there will not be an old man in your house." God was going to strip away the long life, vigor, and institutional power of Eli’s lineage. Instead of enjoying elder status, honorable authority, and strength in the sanctuary, Eli's descendants would suffer premature death and diminished capacity. The word zeroa highlights that the very power Eli's family used unfaithfully would be completely severed by God's righteous judgment.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Deuteronomy 4:34 — "arm" (God's redeeming power delivering Israel from Egypt with an outstretched arm).

  • Psalms 10:15 — "arm" (A prayer asking God to break the power and strength of the wicked).

  • Isaiah 40:11 — "arm" (A gentle picture of God as a shepherd gathering young lambs in His arm).

  • Jeremiah 17:5 — "strength" (A warning against trusting in human power and making human flesh one's strength).

  • Ezekiel 30:22 — "arms" (A decree of judgment where God breaks the arms and power of Pharaoh).

7. Other Relevant Insights

Scripture presents a striking contrast between God's arm and the arm of man. While God's "outstretched arm" brings salvation, protection, and comfort to those who trust Him, He breaks or cuts off the "arm" of those who act in pride or corrupt leadership.

Eli thought he could protect his family position by tolerating his sons' evil acts. Yet the very strategy he used to preserve his family's standing resulted in God cutting off their strength entirely. True spiritual authority and endurance come from honoring God, not from guarding human power.
