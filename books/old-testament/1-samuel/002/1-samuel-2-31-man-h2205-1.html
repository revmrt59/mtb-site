MAN - (zâqên, Strong's H2205)

WORD - ( zaqen , Strong’s [H2205] )

Summary:

The Hebrew term typically describes someone advanced in years, an elder, an aged person, or a man of mature age who often held a position of leadership, respect, or counsel in the community. In 1 Samuel 2:31, the word is translated as "man" in the English rendering, pointing specifically to an adult male of mature years within Eli’s household whose strength, status, and vigor would be cut off by divine judgment.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The term is deeply rooted in the ancient Near Eastern and biblical concept of age being closely tied to wisdom, authority, and standing within the community. In early Israelite society, older men—often referred to as elders—formed the leadership backbone for local governance, legal decisions, and tribal representation. The word draws its primary imagery from the beard or the physical signs of aging, marking a transition from youthful strength to the gray hair of seasoned maturity. When applied to individuals or groups in Scripture, it frequently highlights the gravity associated with seniority, whether that seniority brings honor through righteousness or accountability through unfaithfulness.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word primarily refers to an old person, an elder, or someone advanced in years. When used as a noun or adjective, it can designate biological age—someone who is aged or elderly—or it can function as an official title for those who sit in council and govern the community. The core semantic range centers on the state of being advanced in time and experience rather than a mere abstraction of time passing. In 1 Samuel 2:31, the term denotes a mature, adult man within the household whose life and strength represent the sustaining power of that family line.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, the word appears in varied settings, ranging from quiet domestic scenes of aging family members to formal assemblies where leaders deliberate the affairs of the nation. It describes gray-headed patriarchs, village elders who judge at the city gate, and seasoned counselors whose advice guides kings. Sometimes the focus rests purely on the physical weakness and vulnerability that accompany advanced years, while at other times it emphasizes the dignity, wisdom, and authority expected of those who have lived long.

4. Theological Implications

Age and maturity do not automatically insulate anyone from the consequences of moral failure. While Scripture frequently honors gray hair found in the way of righteousness, the use of this term in contexts of judgment reminds readers that human status, family legacy, and established authority cannot withstand the holy displeasure of God. When God intervenes to humble a proud household, neither the vigor of youth nor the seasoned experience of mature men can preserve what God has determined to break.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:31, a man of God delivers a stern prophecy to Eli concerning the complete dismantling of his priestly house. The divine pronouncement declares that the time is coming when God will sever the arm of Eli's father's house, ensuring that no old man will remain in his household. Here, the absence of mature, aged men serves as a striking picture of generational truncation. The loss of elders means the loss of continuity, wisdom, strength, and future leadership for Eli's lineage, portraying a devastating judgment where the family line is cut off before its natural course can reach old age.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 3:16 — "Go and gather the elders of Israel together, and say to them, 'The Lord God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, of Isaac, and of Jacob, appeared to me...'"

  • Deuteronomy 21:2 — "Then your elders and your judges shall go out and measure the valleys surrounding the city which is slain."

  • 1 Samuel 8:4 — "Then all the elders of Israel gathered together and came to Samuel at Ramah..."

  • Isaiah 3:5 — "The people will be oppressed, every one by another and every one by his neighbor; the child will be insolent toward the elder, and the base toward the honorable."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In biblical narrative, the presence of respected elders normally signifies stability, divine blessing, and ordered community life under God's covenant. Conversely, the removal or corruption of such leadership signals deep societal fracture or divine discipline. The text in 1 Samuel uses this vocabulary to emphasize the absolute finality of the judgment pronounced against Eli’s house, where the usual blessing of living to a ripe old age is deliberately withheld as a consequence of persistent sin.
