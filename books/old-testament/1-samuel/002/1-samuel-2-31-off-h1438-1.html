OFF - (gâdaʻ, Strong's H1438)

WORD - ( gadsah, Strong’s [H1438] )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb translated as "off" in this passage carries the core meaning of cutting down, hewing, or severing something completely. In 1 Samuel 2:31, the word is used in a solemn divine warning delivered by a man of God to Eli, announcing that God is about to sever the strength of Eli's household so that no elderly descendant will remain in his family line.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient biblical culture, physical actions involving cutting, hewing, or chopping wood and stone provided vivid imagery for decisive termination. The verb underlying this English rendering describes the forceful act of cutting through an object to separate, fell, or destroy it. In the agricultural and architectural world of ancient Israel, such terminology was readily understood as the removal of a branch, a tree, or a structural support so that it could no longer stand or bear weight. When applied to human lineages and dynasties, this action conveys the sudden and complete removal of strength, authority, and future continuity.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb can refer to cutting down trees, hewing away materials, or severing physical objects so that they fall or break apart. When used in prophetic and historical texts, it frequently describes the decisive dismantling of power, security, or generational continuity. It denotes an irreversible act of removal or destruction, leaving the object incapable of standing or recovering its former position.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses this term both in literal contexts, such as felling trees or breaking structural objects, and in metaphorical contexts to describe the dismantling of human arrogance, national strength, or corrupt dynasties. The word carries a sense of finality and divine resolve. When God initiates the action, it marks the execution of a judgment where human structures of power are brought down because they have violated sacred trust.

4. Theological Implications

The use of this term in prophetic warnings highlights the seriousness of spiritual accountability, particularly for those entrusted with spiritual leadership. It demonstrates that human privilege and hereditary position do not shield individuals or families from divine judgment when holiness is abandoned. God alone establishes and removes authority, and His righteous standards demand that those who profane His service will face the loss of their standing and influence.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:31, the word describes the drastic curtailment of Eli's house. Eli failed to restrain his wicked sons, honoring them above God, and as a result, the Lord announced that the strength of Eli's family would be completely severed. The term emphasizes the abrupt and decisive nature of this judgment. The lineage that once held prominent priestly authority would be cut off from its power, fulfilling a solemn divine decree that actions have generational consequences before a holy God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Deuteronomy 7:5 — God commands Israel regarding the pagan altars of Canaan: "and cut down their wooden images"

  • Psalms 75:10 — The Lord declares regarding the uplifted horns of the wicked: "All the horns of the wicked I will also cut off"

  • Isaiah 14:12 — The prophetic lament over the fallen king of Babylon describes his descent: "How you are cut down to the ground"

  • Jeremiah 48:25 — The judgment announced upon Moab uses the term to describe national devastation: "The horn of Moab is cut off"

  • Amos 3:14 — The impending judgment upon the altars of Bethel is described with finality: "the horns of the altar shall be cut off and fall to the ground"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The imagery of cutting away or felling an object underscores that divine judgment often addresses the root of a problem rather than merely trimming its symptoms. In the narrative of Eli, the warning reaches to the very foundation of his household's strength, signaling that persistent disobedience results in the complete removal of divine favor and institutional stability.
