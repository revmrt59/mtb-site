DWELLING - (mâʻôwn, Strong's H4583)

dwelling - ( ma`on, Strong’s [H4583] )

Summary:

The Hebrew term rendered as "dwelling" refers to a home, habitation, or resting place. In 1 Samuel 2:32, it describes the earthly sanctuary or dwelling place of God in Shiloh, which would suffer distress and loss as a consequence of the sins of Eli’s household.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The term appears throughout the Old Testament to describe places of habitation, whether literal homes, animal dens, or sacred dwellings. In the historical and prophetic literature, it frequently carries deep emotional or spiritual weight, pointing to a place where someone—or God Himself—makes a settled home. In the context of early Israel, before the permanent establishment of the temple in Jerusalem, the central sanctuary at Shiloh served as the focal point of covenant worship, where God's presence dwelt among His people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word primarily means a dwelling place, habitation, or refuge. When used of human beings or animals, it denotes a physical home or lair. When applied to God, it refers to His heavenly residence or His chosen earthly sanctuary where His presence is specially manifested. The term carries a sense of settled residence, security, and belonging, though in 1 Samuel 2:32 it stands under the shadow of divine judgment.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the term across various contexts to illustrate both comfort and desolation. It describes the security found in God as a spiritual refuge, the beauty of His holy habitation, and the desolation of cities turned into dens for jackals because of divine judgment. The nuance shifts depending on the subject: when associated with the righteous, it speaks of divine protection and fellowship; when associated with rebellious nations or corrupt religious leaders, it depicts the stripping away of security and the ruin of once-honored places.

4. Theological Implications

The usage of the term in connection with the sanctuary highlights the sobering reality of divine holiness. God graciously condescends to establish a dwelling place among His people, but His presence cannot be presumed upon or treated with contempt. The judgment announced against Eli’s house demonstrates that sacred structures and religious privileges do not shield unrepentant sinners from the consequences of their rebellion. True dwelling with God requires reverence, faithful obedience, and moral integrity.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:32, an anonymous man of God delivers a severe oracle against Eli because Eli has honored his sons above God. The prophecy declares that disaster will come upon God’s dwelling—referring to the tabernacle or sanctuary at Shiloh—and that no old man will remain in Eli's house. The term "dwelling" here grounds the divine judgment in a very tangible place of worship that the corrupt priesthood had polluted by their greed and arrogance. The loss of the dwelling symbolizes the rupture of fellowship and the removal of divine favor from that lineage.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Deuteronomy 26:15: "Look down from Your holy habitation [H4583], from heaven, and bless Your people Israel..."

  • Here the word is used of God's heavenly dwelling, appealing to His mercy and attentiveness from above.

  • Psalms 26:8: "Lord, I have loved the habitation [H4583] of Your house, and the place where Your glory dwells."

  • The psalm uses the term to express deep affection for the earthly sanctuary and the manifest presence of God.

  • Psalms 91:9: "Because you have made the Lord, who is my refuge, even the Most High, your dwelling [H4583]..."

  • The term illustrates God Himself as the ultimate secure habitation and shelter for the believer.

  • Jeremiah 25:30: "The Lord will roar from on high, and utter His voice from His holy habitation [H4583]..."

  • The prophet employs the word to depict God issuing a majestic and terrifying decree of judgment against the nations.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The fate of the sanctuary at Shiloh serves as a pivotal warning in redemptive history. Long before the Babylonian exile of Jerusalem, the destruction or abandonment of the Shiloh tabernacle demonstrated that God's presence is not bound unconditionally to physical buildings where His covenant is flouted. This historical memory later echoed in the prophetic warnings of Jeremiah, reminding Israel that religious outward forms cannot replace authentic obedience.
