SONS - (bên, Strong's H1121)

B - ( ben, Strong’s [H1121] )

Summary: The word primarily refers to a male descendant, son, or child, and in plural form can describe male children, descendants, or members of a larger group or community. In 1 Samuel 2:34, the word refers to Hophni and Phinehas, Eli's wicked sons, whose sudden and simultaneous deaths serve as the sign and execution of God's judgment against Eli's house.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew word is one of the most common familial terms in the Old Testament, fundamentally denoting a male offspring or descendant. Beyond immediate parentage, it frequently describes members of a guild, tribe, nation, or spiritual lineage. In the ancient Near Eastern context, sons carried the family name, inherited the estate, and were expected to continue the father's work or office. When applied to the priesthood, the term carries deep generational and representative weight, connecting a man's legacy directly to his household's standing before God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term can refer to an immediate male child born to a father or mother. Beyond strict biological descent, it frequently describes grandsons, later descendants, or members of a broader collective group. When used in the plural, it often designates children or descendants collectively. In 1 Samuel 2:34, the plural construct form identifies the specific biological offspring of Eli who inherited priestly responsibilities but disgraced the sanctuary.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the term across a wide semantic range depending on the immediate context. It describes direct biological descendants within a family tree, young animals, and groups united by common characteristics or loyalties. In legal and covenantal settings, the status of being a son involves responsibility, training, and accountability to the head of the house. The word often highlights succession, inheritance, and the passing down of either blessing or judgment from one generation to the next.

4. Theological Implications

The use of the term in historical narratives often underscores the principle of generational accountability and the serious nature of spiritual leadership. A father's failure to discipline his household or honor God's sanctuary directly impacts his descendants. In the broader scope of Scripture, the concept of sonship moves from biological lineage to covenant relationship, ultimately finding its fulfillment in Christ as the unique Son of God who perfectly honors the Father.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:34, the word stands at the center of a severe prophetic pronouncement against the house of Eli. God is holding Eli responsible for honoring his sons above God. The immediate consequence announced is that both of Eli's sons will die on the exact same day. The word here anchors the divine judgment in concrete historical reality, showing that the corrupt legacy of Eli's household will be cut off decisively through the fate of his offspring.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The fate of Eli's sons illustrates how personal sin within a position of sacred trust brings ruin not only to the individual but to the family line. The precise fulfillment of this warning in subsequent chapters demonstrates the absolute reliability of divine speech when delivered through a true prophet.
