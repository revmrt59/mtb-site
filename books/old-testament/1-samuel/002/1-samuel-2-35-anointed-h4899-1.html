ANOINTED - (mâshîyach, Strong's H4899)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun mashiach refers to an "anointed one"—a person consecrated and set apart for a specific office through the pouring of oil. In the Old Testament, the word is used primarily for high priests and Israelite kings, indicating divine authorization and appointment. In 1 Samuel 2:35, "anointed" refers to the God-appointed king of Israel, before whom a promised faithful priest will serve. In this verse, the word establishes that God will align His true priesthood with the royal leadership He appoints to shepherd His people.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The book of 1 Samuel records a major transition in Israel's history, moving from the period of the judges to the establishment of the monarchy. The early chapters contrast the corrupt leadership of the high priest Eli and his sons with the faithful service of young Samuel. Because Eli's sons abused their priestly office and treated God's offerings with contempt, a man of God brought a message of judgment against Eli's family line.

In 1 Samuel 2:35, God announces a coming change in leadership: He will replace the corrupt priestly line with a faithful priest who will do what is in God's heart and mind. As part of this promise, God declares that this priest will serve before His "anointed." This statement comes at a time before Israel had an earthly king, pointing forward to the royal monarchy that God would establish beginning with Saul and David.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun mashiach comes from a verb meaning to smear or anoint with oil. The word literally means "anointed one" or "one who has been anointed."

In ancient Israel, physical anointing with oil was a sacred ritual used to set individuals apart for holy office. The word can refer to:

  • The high priest, who was anointed with sacred oil to perform sacrificial duties.

  • The king of Israel, who was anointed by a prophet or priest as a visible sign that God had chosen him to rule.

  • An authorized leader or ruler singled out by God for a special mission.

While the term later developed into the title "Messiah" in prophetic expectation, its basic meaning is a person who has received divine authorization for office through symbolic anointing.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In the Old Testament, mashiach functions primarily as a title of office and authority rather than a personal name. The pouring of oil represented the conferral of divine backing, calling, and responsibility.

When applied to the priest, the word highlights ceremonial holiness and fitness to mediate between God and the congregation. When applied to the king, the phrase "the LORD's anointed" emphasizes that Israel's monarch was not an absolute ruler, but a vassal leader subject to God's law. Because the king was God's anointed, striking or harming him was treated as a severe offense against God Himself.

4. Theological Implications

The idea of the anointed leader demonstrates that authority among God's people originates with God Himself. Human leaders do not self-appoint; they receive their standing and responsibility from the Lord.

The title also connects spiritual and civil leadership under God's supreme reign. By pairing the faithful priest and the anointed king, Scripture shows that both sacred worship and public governance are meant to operate in harmony under divine authority. Canonically, this double office of priest and king anticipates Jesus Christ, who perfectly fulfills both roles as the ultimate Anointed One.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:35, God promises, "I will build him a sure house, and he shall walk before My anointed forever." The word appears here in the singular, common gender form, referring directly to the future king of Israel.

The phrase "walk before" describes public service and loyal attendance. God promises that the faithful priest will carry out his duties in close proximity to, and in harmony with, God's chosen king. This promise served as a stark contrast to the corrupt house of Eli, which faced dishonor and removal. Instead of a self-serving priesthood, God promised an enduring priestly house that would honor the king whom God would raise up.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Leviticus 4:3 — "if the anointed priest sins, bringing guilt on the people, then let him offer to the LORD for his sin which he has committed a young bullock without blemish as a sin offering."

  • 1 Samuel 2:10 — "The adversaries of the LORD shall be broken in pieces; from heaven He will thunder against them. The LORD will judge the ends of the earth. He will give strength to His king, and exalt the horn of His anointed."

  • 1 Samuel 24:6 — "And he said to his men, 'The LORD forbid that I should do this thing to my master, the LORD's anointed, to stretch out my hand against him, seeing he is the anointed of the LORD.'"

  • 2 Samuel 22:51 — "He is the tower of salvation to His king, and shows mercy to His anointed, to David and his descendants forevermore."

  • 2 Samuel 23:1 — "Now these are the last words of David. Thus says David the son of Jesse; thus says the man raised up on high, the anointed of the God of Jacob, and the sweet psalmist of Israel."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's prayer in 1 Samuel 2:10 provides an important backdrop for 1 Samuel 2:35. In her song of praise, Hannah speaks of God giving strength to His king and exalting the horn of His "anointed," long before Israel had a king.

When God later promises in verse 35 that the faithful priest will walk before His "anointed," He reinforces Hannah's prophetic praise. The sanctuary ministry and the monarchy were designed by God to work together to preserve true worship and righteous judgment in Israel.
