BUILD - (bânâh, Strong's H1129)

BUILD - ( banah, Strong’s [H1129] )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb banah primarily means to build, construct, or rebuild physical structures such as houses, cities, walls, and altars. Beyond physical construction, it is widely used in Scripture to describe establishing, restoring, or perpetuating a family, dynasty, or community. In 1 Samuel 2:35, the word describes God's promise to raise up a faithful priest and establish a secure, enduring house for him. This divine action directly contrasts with the crumbling house of Eli, showing that God sovereignly dismantles corrupt leadership and establishes a lasting, faithful ministry for His own glory.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the Old Testament world, building was a fundamental labor necessary for survival, community life, and worship. The basic vocabulary of construction involved raising dwellings, fortifying cities, and erecting places of sacrifice. Beyond literal architecture, ancient cultures naturally viewed a family or lineage in architectural terms—a household was something established, extended, and secured across generations. When God promised to build a house for someone, listeners immediately understood the metaphor of establishing a lasting dynasty and securing a family line that would endure through time.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to literal, physical construction, such as putting together stones and timber to form a dwelling place, city wall, or temple. When applied to people rather than buildings, it means to build up, restore, or establish a family line, lineage, or community. In this metaphorical sense, to build a house is to give someone descendants, permanence, and honor. The term carries the sense of deliberate planning, structural stability, and long-term establishment.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture employs this verb in two major spheres: literal building and familial establishment. On one hand, people build houses, altars, and fortified cities. On the other hand, God is frequently the one who builds. He builds human families by granting children and descendants, and He builds spiritual communities by establishing His people in safety and righteousness. The word often highlights divine initiative and faithfulness, emphasizing that human security and lasting legacy ultimately depend on God's foundational work rather than human effort.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, the language of building points to the permanence and reliability of God's covenant purposes. When human leaders fail through corruption and disobedience, their house falls into ruin. However, God steps in to build a righteous and enduring order that cannot be overthrown. This points forward to the ultimate establishment of God's eternal kingdom and faithful priesthood through His promised redemptive work, reminding believers that true spiritual stability comes exclusively from what God establishes.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2, the corrupt house of Eli faces judgment because of unfaithfulness and abuse of sacred office. In stark contrast, God announces that He will raise up a faithful priest who shall do according to what is in His heart and mind. Regarding this faithful servant, God declares, "I will build him a sure house." Here the word emphasizes the secure, permanent establishment of a lasting priestly line that will honor God faithfully, replacing the crumbling and disgraced lineage of Eli.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:22 — Then the rib which the Lord God had taken from man He "made" into a woman, and brought her to the man.

  • Exodus 17:15 — And Moses "built" an altar and called its name, The-Lord-Is-My-Banner.

  • Joshua 24:13 — I have given you a land for which you did not labor, and cities which you did not "build," and you dwell in them; you eat of the vineyards and olive groves which you did not plant.

  • Proverbs 9:1 — Wisdom has "built" her house, she has hewn out her seven pillars.

  • Jeremiah 31:28 — And it shall come to pass, that as I have watched over them to pluck up, to break down, to throw down, to destroy, and to afflict, so I will watch over them to "build" and to plant, says the Lord.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The architectural metaphor in 1 Samuel 2:35 carries deep covenantal weight. While Eli's family lost their standing through persistent sin, God's promise to build a sure house demonstrates that divine judgment against corrupt leadership clears the way for a faithful and enduring ministry. The contrast between tearing down and building up underpins the entire narrative arc of Samuel, where old, failing structures give way to God's chosen instruments.
