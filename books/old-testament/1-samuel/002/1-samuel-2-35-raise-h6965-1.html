RAISE - (qûwm, Strong's H6965)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb qum broadly means to rise, stand up, establish, or cause someone or something to take a position of leadership or stability. In 1 Samuel 2:35, God uses this word to declare His direct intervention in appointing a faithful priest to replace the corrupt house of Eli, establishing a stable and enduring ministry that fulfills His divine purpose.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the early chapters of 1 Samuel, Israel faced a severe leadership crisis. Eli served as high priest, but his sons Hophni and Phinehas abused their duties, treating God's sacrifices with contempt and dishonoring the sanctuary. Because Eli failed to restrain them, God pronounced judgment on his family line. Against this backdrop of priestly failure and spiritual decline, God sent a messenger to announce judgment and hope. The word qum appears as the decisive turning point in this declaration. Where human leaders failed, God Himself steps forward to establish leadership that honors Him.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of qum is to arise, stand, or get up from a sitting or lying position. When used in a causative sense—where an agent causes something or someone to stand—it means to raise up, appoint, establish, or bring to pass. Depending on the context, the word can refer to a person standing up physically, a king or priest being installed into office, a covenant being confirmed, or a word of prophecy being fulfilled. In 1 Samuel 2:35, God is the subject who acts, meaning He will personally raise up and position a loyal servant to carry out priestly duties.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, qum appears in a wide variety of settings ranging from simple physical movement to major historical acts of God. In everyday narrative, people qum when they stand up to speak, depart on a journey, or rise in the morning. In military or political settings, God or human leaders qum to initiate action or raise up deliverers and rulers. In covenantal and prophetic contexts, God uses qum to indicate that He is establishing an enduring institution or fulfilling His spoken promise. The word highlights transition and purpose: a movement from inaction to action, or from instability to an established order.

4. Theological Implications

God's use of qum in 1 Samuel 2:35 demonstrates that true spiritual authority is appointed by God, not secured through human entitlement or self-interest. Eli’s house lost its standing because they honored themselves above the Lord, yet God’s purposes were not defeated by human sin. The word highlights divine sovereignty in leadership: God raises up leaders who act according to His heart and mind. Furthermore, this divine action points toward God's long-term care for His people. While the immediate historical fulfillment began with Samuel and later Zadok during Solomon's reign, the broader trajectory reflects God's goal of establishing a permanent, unshakeable priesthood centered on His will.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:35, qum provides a striking contrast to the downfall of Eli’s house described in the preceding verses. God had just pronounced that the strength of Eli's family would be cut short and their priestly role brought to an end. Immediately following that declaration of judgment, God announces, "Then I will raise up for Myself a faithful priest." The work of raising up belongs entirely to God. The promised priest will act according to what is in God's heart and mind, and God promises to build him an enduring house. Here, qum marks the shift from human corruption and divine judgment to divine initiative and holy renewal.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Psalms 78:5 — "established" — God established His testimony and law in Jacob, setting up His revealed truth as a lasting standard for future generations.

  • 1 Kings 11:14 — "up" — The Lord raised up an adversary against Solomon when Solomon turned his heart away from God's commands.

  • Jeremiah 28:6 — "perform" — Jeremiah responded to a prophet by expressing the desire that the Lord would perform or fulfill the words that had been prophesied.

  • Psalms 1:5 — "stand" — The psalm contrasts the righteous with the ungodly, noting that the wicked will not stand in the judgment or hold a place among the congregation of the righteous.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The expression "I will raise up for Myself" in 1 Samuel 2:35 highlights that true ministry exists primarily for God's honor and according to His initiative. In the ancient Near East, religious offices were frequently treated as family positions or political tools. By using qum, Scripture shows that God maintains the integrity of His sanctuary by intervening to replace self-serving leaders with faithful servants who carry out His divine purpose.
