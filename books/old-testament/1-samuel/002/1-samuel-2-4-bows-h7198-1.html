BOWS - (qesheth, Strong's H7198)

Qeshet ( qesheth, Strong’s [H7198] )

Summary:

The Hebrew word refers fundamentally to a bow, the ancient military and hunting weapon used for shooting arrows. In 1 Samuel 2:4, it appears in Hannah’s song of praise as a vivid image of martial strength and human power, used to declare that God humbles the mighty and breaks the weapons of those who rely on their own strength.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The term appears frequently in the Old Testament, denoting the physical bow crafted from wood, horn, or metal. In the ancient Near East, the bow was a primary instrument of warfare and self-defense, symbolizing military might, political dominance, and national security. To possess and wield a bow meant having the capacity to strike from a distance and assert power over others.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word means a bow, referring to the hand-held weapon designed to bend under tension and release arrows with force. Beyond its literal use in battle and hunting, the word often serves in poetic and prophetic literature as a representative symbol for military force, human boasting, and armed might.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the term both literally and figuratively. In historical narratives, it describes actual weapons carried by soldiers, archers, and kings. In poetic texts, prophets and psalmists frequently picture God breaking, snapping, or unstringing the bow to demonstrate that human military superiority cannot withstand divine intervention. When God breaks the bow, He strips away the false security of nations and individuals who trust in their own strength rather than in the Lord.

4. Theological Implications

The mention of broken bows confronts human pride and self-reliance. In Scripture, military weapons represent the ultimate earthly means of securing one's own fate or enforcing one's will. By depicting these instruments as shattered in the hands of the mighty, the text points away from human prowess and toward the sovereign rule of God, who alone determines who rises and who falls.

5. Contextual Nuance in Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:4, Hannah sings that the bows of the mighty are broken while those who stumbled are girded with strength. This forms the first line of a profound reversal motif running through her entire prayer. The verse contrasts those who feel secure in their own defense with the vulnerable who have no earthly armor. By focusing on the shattering of the weapon, Hannah emphasizes that human strength is fragile and easily dismantled by the Lord, preparing the way for a song that exalts God as the true source of rescue.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 49:24 — The patriarch Jacob describes how Joseph’s bow remained strong even when archers attacked him, pointing to the sustaining power of the Mighty God of Jacob.

  • Psalms 37:15 — The psalm declares that the wicked draw their bow to cast down the poor, but their swords shall enter their own heart and their bows shall be broken.

  • Isaiah 41:2 — The prophet speaks of God stirring up a conqueror from the east, whose sword makes them like dust and whose bow is driven like driven stubble before the wind.

  • Habakkuk 3:9 — The prophet declares that God's bow was made quite ready, framing divine judgment through the powerful imagery of a warrior's weapon.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The imagery of the broken bow recurs across the prophetic books as a sign of divine judgment against aggressive nations. When God removes the instrument of war, He accomplishes peace not through human treaties, but by disarming those who practice violence. This shifts the focus of security away from arsenals and onto the faithfulness of God.
