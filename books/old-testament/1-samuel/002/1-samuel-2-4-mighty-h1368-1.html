MIGHTY - (gibbôwr, Strong's H1368)

Summary:

The Hebrew word gibbor refers to a strong, powerful individual, a seasoned warrior, or a heroic champion possessing great strength or influence. In 1 Samuel 2:4, it describes elite military warriors whose bows are shattered by the Lord, demonstrating that human martial strength and self-reliance cannot withstand divine sovereignty.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Near Eastern culture, military might and physical strength were the ultimate measures of security and influence. A community relied on its trained warriors (gibborim) and their weaponry for protection against enemies. The bow was one of the primary long-range weapons of warfare, representing military offensive strength and readiness for battle.

Against this cultural backdrop, Hannah breaks into praise after bringing her young son Samuel to the house of the Lord at Shiloh. Once disgraced and barren, Hannah experienced God’s gracious intervention. Her song looks past her immediate personal joy to celebrate how God governs the whole world, humbling the self-reliant and lifting up the helpless.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to a strong man, a military hero, a champion, a noble ruler, or an elite warrior. When used of human beings, it highlights physical power, military skill, bravery, or high social standing.

When applied to God, the word describes His invincible power as a divine warrior who rescues His people and defeats evil. In 1 Samuel 2:4, the word refers to confident human warriors who rely on their own strength and weapons.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses gibbor across several distinct settings:

  • Military Champions and Troops: In narrative historical accounts, the word frequently identifies elite soldiers, famous champions, or seasoned battle troops known for their valor and skill.

  • Men of Prominence: The term can describe men of wealth, social standing, or regional influence whose resources give them power in their communities.

  • The Power of God: The word describes God Himself as the ultimate Warrior who commands supreme power over creation and nations.

Throughout the Old Testament, a recurring theme appears: human strength, no matter how great, remains limited and vulnerable. Human warriors can fall in battle, but God’s power remains absolute.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah’s song introduces the main theological theme of the books of Samuel: God opposes the proud and exalts the humble. Human society often measures security by visible power, weapons, and status. However, God regularly reverses these earthly expectations.

The breaking of the warriors' bows shows that physical power offers no absolute protection against God’s purpose. Salvation does not depend on human armaments or natural ability. God delights to disarm those who rely on their own strength, while granting unexpected power and endurance to those who are weak and helpless.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:4, Hannah declares, "The bows of the mighty are broken, and those who stumbled are girded with strength."

The word appears here as a plural description of elite, well-armed warriors. The image is striking: warriors step into battle with powerful bows, fully equipped to dominate. Yet their weapons are suddenly snapped in two, rendering them helpless. At the exact same time, those who were stumbling from weakness and exhaustion are supplied with supernatural strength.

This dramatic contrast highlights divine intervention. The defeat of the mighty does not occur because their human opponents were stronger, but because the Lord Himself disarms human pride.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 6:4 — Describes early legendary figures who were famous "men" of old, renowned for their power.

  • Joshua 10:2 — Describes the military reputation of Gibeon, noting that all its men were "mighty" warriors.

  • 2 Samuel 1:25 — David mourns the loss of Saul and Jonathan, lamenting how the "mighty" have fallen in battle.

  • Psalms 52:1 — Challenges the arrogant ruler who boasts in his wealth and power, asking why the "mighty" man boasts in evil.

  • Jeremiah 32:18 — Exalts the Lord as the Great and "Mighty" God, whose name is the Lord of hosts.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The image of a broken bow is a common ancient biblical picture of demilitarization and total defeat. When God breaks a warrior's bow, He strips away their ability to wage war and enforce their own will.

Hannah’s song serves as a direct prototype for Mary’s song of praise in the New Testament (the Magnificat in Luke 1). Both songs celebrate how God turns earthly hierarchies upside down, bringing down rulers from their thrones and lifting up the lowly.
