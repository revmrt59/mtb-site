CEASED - (châdal, Strong's H2308)

CHADAL - ( chadal, Strong’s [H2308] )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb chadal generally means to cease, stop, leave off, or desist from an activity or condition. In 1 Samuel 2:5, it describes how the hungry have ceased their labor or hunger because God has completely reversed their fortunes, lifting up the lowly and filling the needy.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew language uses this verb to describe a point where an action, movement, or state comes to an end. It often appears when people leave off working, stop speaking, refrain from a course of action, or find that a continuous condition has finally run its course. In the historical narrative and poetic literature of the Old Testament, the word paints a clear picture of an activity stopping or a persistent state coming to a halt.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb can refer to desisting from a task, failing to perform a duty, or letting an ongoing situation stop. When applied to people or physical conditions, it describes coming to an end, resting from toil, or leaving a matter alone. In this passage, the word points to the stopping of a long-standing condition of hardship, marking a decisive shift from lack and labor to provision and rest.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture employs this verb across various situations to signal when something stops happening or when people decide to refrain from doing something. It can describe physical actions coming to a halt, such as work stopping or building projects being abandoned. It also describes people holding back from a planned course, choosing to desist from speaking or acting. The nuance depends entirely on the context: sometimes it portrays a voluntary choice to stop, while other times, as in 1 Samuel 2:5, it describes a circumstance or condition that naturally or providentially comes to an end.

4. Theological Implications

When applied to human fortunes and divine reversals, the word highlights God's sovereign authority to alter human circumstances. The wider song of Hannah reveals that the Lord humbles and exalts, wounds and heals, brings low and lifts up. The stopping of hunger and hardship does not happen by mere chance; it flows directly from the Lord's intervention in the lives of the humble and afflicted.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Hannah’s song of praise celebrates a profound reversal of human fortunes. Those who were once full must now hire themselves out for bread, while the hungry have ceased their hunger because God has abundantly provided. The verb marks the exact point where the bitter experience of scarcity stops. The previous state of continuous want reaches its end, replaced by the reality of divine sustenance.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 11:8: "And so the Lord scattered them abroad from there over the face of all the earth, and they ceased building the city."

  • Exodus 9:33: "So Moses went out of the city from Pharaoh and spread out his hands to the Lord, then the thunder and the hail ceased, and the rain was not poured on the earth."

  • Judges 5:7: "The villagers ceased, they ceased in Israel, until I, Deborah, arose, arose a mother in Israel."

  • Psalms 36:3: "The words of his mouth are wickedness and deceit; he has ceased to be wise and to do good."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The structure of Hannah's prayer sets up a series of stark contrasts between the proud and the humble, the rich and the poor, the barren and the fruitful. The stopping of the hunger of the destitute stands as a focal point of this divine turnaround. What was once an ongoing, painful reality is brought to a complete halt by the Lord's gracious intervention.
