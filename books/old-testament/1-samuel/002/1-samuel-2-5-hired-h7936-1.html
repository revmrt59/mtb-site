HIRED - (sâkar, Strong's H7936)

hired - (sakar, H7936)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb sakar means to hire, engage for wages, or hire oneself out for labor. In 1 Samuel 2:5, the word describes people who were once wealthy and full, but who have now lost their abundance and must hire themselves out to work for a simple loaf of bread. Through this vivid picture, Hannah celebrates how God sovereignly reverses human circumstances, humbling the self-sufficient and providing for the needy.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Hannah speaks these words in a song of praise after bringing her young son Samuel to the tabernacle at Shiloh to serve the Lord. For years, Hannah suffered the pain of childlessness and the cruel taunts of Peninnah. When God graciously answered her prayer and gave her a son, Hannah responded with a song glorifying God's unique holiness, power, and sovereign rule over human life.

In this song, Hannah describes dramatic reversals in human society. Warriors lose their strength, the weak become strong, the barren bear children, and the wealthy lose their abundance. The mention of those who were once "full" having to hire themselves out for bread illustrates this sudden shift, showing that earthly prosperity and self-reliance offer no permanent security apart from God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

, The verb sakar means to hire someone or to work for wages. It describes a transaction or labor agreement where one person pays compensation in exchange for work, service, or influence. Depending on the context, it can refer to employing craftsmen for construction, engaging mercenaries for war, paying bribe money to influencers, or securing personal labor.

When used in a reflexive or passive sense, as in 1 Samuel 2:5, sakar means to hire oneself out or enter into paid labor for another. Here, it refers to individuals who formerly possessed an abundance of food ("those who were full") becoming so impoverished that they must sell their physical labor simply to earn basic sustenance.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, sakar appears in several practical contexts:

  • Labor and construction: Employing workers, builders, or masons to complete physical projects, such as temple repairs.

  • Military arrangements: Paying foreign nations, kings, or soldiers to fight as mercenaries in a conflict.

  • Corrupt hiring or bribery: Paying individuals to carry out harmful plans, such as hiring false prophets or advisors to oppose God's people.

  • Personal service: Entering into an agreement to labor for an employer in exchange for food or money.

In 1 Samuel 2:5, the word carries a tragic irony. It describes people who used to enjoy complete financial independence who are now brought down to the low status of day laborers working for food.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's song uses this picture of economic reversal to communicate key biblical truths:

  • God rules over all human circumstances: Human status, physical strength, and wealth are not permanent achievements. God has the authority to bring down the proud and lift up the humble.

  • Riches cannot provide ultimate security: Present wealth is no guarantee of future stability. Those who rely on their own abundance can lose everything when God turns the tables.

  • God remembers the needy: While those who trust in their self-sufficiency are humbled, God consistently cares for those who recognize their need for Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:5, sakar is paired with a series of sharp contrasts:

  • The full versus the hungry: Those who had plenty must now hire themselves out for bread, while those who suffered hunger now have enough.

  • The barren versus the mother: The woman without children gives birth to seven, while the mother with many children grows weak.

The word "hired" emphasizes how complete this downfall is. The prosperous were not merely reduced to modest living; they were forced into manual labor just to survive. The verb form presents this reversal as a completed reality in Hannah's song, highlighting the certainty of God's sovereign judgments.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 30:16 — Leah meets Jacob and says, "You must come in to me, for I have surely hired you with my son's mandrakes."

  • Deuteronomy 23:4 — Israel was commanded to exclude Ammonites and Moabites because "they hired against you Balaam the son of Beor... to curse you."

  • 2 Chronicles 24:12 — King Joash and Jehoiada gave money to overseers who " hired masons and carpenters to repair the house of the LORD."

  • Nehemiah 6:12 — Nehemiah recognized that God had not sent Shemaiah, "for Tobiah and Sanballat had hired him" to utter a false prophecy.

  • Haggai 1:6 — Haggai warns the people who neglected rebuilding the temple that "he who earns wages, earns wages to put into a bag with holes."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's song in 1 Samuel 2 provides the thematic foundation for Mary's song of praise in Luke 1:46–55 (the Magnificat). Mary echoes Hannah's message when she sings that God "has filled the hungry with good things, and the rich He has sent away empty." Both passages celebrate the God who humbles the proud and lifts up those who depend on Him.
