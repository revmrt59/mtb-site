LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

The word refers to the personal divine name of the God of Israel, often rendered in English Bibles as "LORD" in small capital letters. Derived from the Hebrew verb meaning "to be," the name identifies God as the living, active God who reveals Himself to His people. In 1 Samuel 2:7, the name identifies the personal God of Israel as the ultimate ruler who determines human circumstances, making poor or rich and bringing low or lifting up according to His sovereign power.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The opening chapters of 1 Samuel describe a critical transition in Israel's history, moving from the period of the judges toward the establishment of the monarchy. The spiritual leadership of Israel was weak and corrupt under the sons of Eli at Shiloh. In the midst of this national decline, a woman named Hannah, who was previously barren and disgraced, prayed to God for a child. God answered her prayer by giving her a son, Samuel.

After bringing the young child Samuel to the tabernacle to dedicate him to God's service, Hannah responded with a powerful song of praise in 1 Samuel 2. Her prayer is a hymn of thanksgiving that looks beyond her individual situation to declare God's character and rule over all human society. In verse 7, Hannah uses the personal divine name when describing how God reverses human conditions, elevating the humble and bringing down the proud.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word is a proper noun that serves as the personal name of the God of Israel. In Hebrew tradition, this name is connected to the verb root meaning "to be" (hayah or havah). When God revealed this name to Moses at the burning bush, He associated it with the declaration, "I AM WHO I AM" (Exodus 3:14). The name emphasizes that God exists as a living, active, personal Being.

Unlike general titles for God that can describe power or mastery, this proper name identifies the specific God who made a covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. In English Bible translations, it is traditionally printed in capital or small capital letters ("LORD") to distinguish it from the general title for "master" or "lord" (Adonai). In 1 Samuel 2:7, the name identifies the personal God of Israel as the direct agent behind the shifting fortunes of human life.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses this proper name to set the God of Israel apart from false idols and generic ideas of deity. While general terms for God focus on His power as Creator or Ruler, His personal name emphasizes His active presence, His covenant commitment, and His personal interaction with humanity.

Throughout Old Testament narrative and poetry, the name appears when God acts directly in human events. It marks His sovereign control over history, His faithfulness to His promises, and His direct leadership of His people. The name is constantly linked to God's personal actions—He speaks, delivers, judges, loves, and governs.

4. Theological Implications

The personal name of God teaches that the Creator of the universe is not an abstract force or a distant observer. God reveals Himself by name so that His people may know Him personally.

Because He is the living God, human destinies are not governed by blind fate, random luck, or human power alone. Hannah's use of the name highlights that the God who commands the universe is the very same personal God who hears individual prayers. His rule over wealth, poverty, lowliness, and honor means that all earthly status remains under His direct authority.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:7, Hannah asserts: "The LORD makes poor and makes rich; He brings low and lifts up." She places the personal God of Israel at the exact center of human socio-economic and earthly conditions.

This declaration arises directly out of Hannah's personal story. She knew what it was to be brought low by barrenness and social mockery, and she experienced being lifted up when God answered her prayer. Yet in verse 7, she expands her view to include all of human society. Material wealth and social prominence are not strictly the results of human cleverness, nor are poverty and low status purely the result of misfortune. The personal God actively directs the affairs of men, humbling those who trust in their own strength and exalting those who rely on Him.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 — "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens."

  • Joshua 14:12 — "Now therefore, give me this mountain of which the LORD spoke in that day; for you heard in that day how the Anakim were there, and that the cities were great and fortified. It may be that the LORD will be with me, and I shall be able to drive them out as the LORD said."

  • 2 Samuel 22:2 — "And he said: 'The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer.'"

  • Psalms 25:1 — "To You, O LORD, I lift up my soul."

  • Proverbs 16:6 — "In mercy and truth atonement is provided for iniquity; and by the fear of the LORD one departs from evil."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The distinction between God's general title and His personal name carries structural importance in the books of Samuel. While Israel's leaders and neighboring nations often spoke of God in general terms, Hannah addressed Him by His personal name in prayer.

Hannah's song in 1 Samuel 2 establishes a key thematic foundation for the entire book. The rise of David from an obscure shepherd boy to the king of Israel, alongside the downfall of King Saul, directly illustrates the truth of verse 7: God brings low and lifts up. This same theme reappears in the New Testament in Mary's song of praise, where she praises God for scattering the proud and exalting the humble.
