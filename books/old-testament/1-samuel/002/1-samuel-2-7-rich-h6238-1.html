RICH - (ʻâshar, Strong's H6238)

WORD - ( 'ashar , H6238 )

Summary:

'Ashar means to accumulate wealth, gain riches, or cause someone to become wealthy. In 1 Samuel 2:7, the word describes God's supreme power to grant material abundance and prosperity to whomever He chooses. Hannah uses it in her song of praise to declare that human economic status and earthly honor do not rest in human hands, but in the sovereign hand of the LORD.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Hannah speaks these words after God answered her desperate prayers for a child by giving her Samuel. Having dedicated Samuel to the LORD at Shiloh, Hannah offers a vibrant hymn of praise. Her personal deliverance from childlessness and social shame expands into a broad celebration of God's authority over all human life. In the ancient world, family size, social standing, and physical wealth were deeply connected. Those who possessed riches held influence and honor, while the poor faced severe vulnerability. Hannah turns this social order upside down, declaring that God alone distributes wealth, poverty, status, and power according to His wise purposes.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

'Ashar refers to gaining, accumulating, or bestowing material wealth and physical prosperity. Depending on how it is used in a sentence, the word can describe a person who grows wealthy through trade or effort, or it can describe a benefactor who enriches someone else. When used of human activity, it can refer to honest work or greedy gain. When God is the subject, the word signifies His divine ability to bestow physical success, earthly goods, and material abundance. In 1 Samuel 2:7, the word refers specifically to God actively causing someone to become rich.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, 'ashar appears in contexts ranging from commercial gain to divine blessing. Old Testament wisdom literature frequently uses the word to contrast the outcomes of righteousness and wickedness. Proverbs highlights that the blessing of the LORD brings true, peaceful enrichment, whereas greedy attempts to get rich quickly lead to trouble. Old Testament prophets use the word to describe nations or individuals who falsely credit their own trade or oppression for their wealth. However, when attributed to God, 'ashar reflects His generous care in providing physical sustenance, material security, and earthly honor.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah’s song establishes a foundational biblical truth: ultimate control over human conditions belongs to God alone. Wealth is neither an absolute sign of personal righteousness nor the product of ultimate self-reliance. By attributing both poverty and enrichment to the LORD, Scripture guards against two spiritual traps: pride in times of abundance and despair in times of need. God holds the absolute authority to exalt or humble, to impoverish or enrich. Security rests not in the temporary presence of riches, but in the God who distributes all good things according to His sovereign plan.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:7, 'ashar works alongside a series of poetic contrasts in Hannah’s song: life and death, poverty and wealth, lowliness and exaltation. Hannah contrasts the proud who boast in their own strength with the needy whom God raises up. In this verse, "The LORD makes poor and makes rich," Hannah asserts that economic conditions are subject to divine providence. For Hannah, who had suffered intense social humiliation as a barren woman before God gave her a son, this truth was deeply personal. God had reversed her circumstances, proving that He can transform the most helpless state into one of honor and abundance.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 14:23 — Abram refuses the king of Sodom's goods, saying he will take nothing lest the king say, "I have made Abram rich." Here the word describes human credit for wealth, which Abram rejects so that God alone receives the glory for his prosperity.

  • Proverbs 10:22 — "The blessing of the LORD makes one rich, and He adds no sorrow with it." This passage presents God as the true source of lasting abundance that comes without the painful burdens of sinful ambition.

  • Proverbs 23:4 — "Do not overwork to be rich; because of your own understanding, cease!" In this passage, the word warns against consuming oneself with the relentless pursuit of earthly wealth.

  • Hosea 12:8 — Hosea exposes Ephraim's arrogant boast, "Surely I have become rich, I have found wealth for myself," showing how self-satisfied pride conceals spiritual blindness and dishonest gain.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's statement sets the narrative framework for the rest of 1 Samuel. Throughout the book, God repeatedly brings low those who rely on earthly power and wealth while exalting those who are humble. He brings low the proud priestly family of Eli and raises up the faithful boy Samuel. Later, He strips royalty from King Saul and lifts up David, a humble shepherd boy, to sit on the throne.
