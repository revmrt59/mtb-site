BEGGAR - (ʼebyôwn, Strong's H34)

Summary:

The Hebrew word evyon refers to a person in deep need, lacking the basic necessities of life and depending on others for relief. In 1 Samuel 2:8, Hannah uses this word in her song of praise to describe someone in ultimate poverty and lowliness, whom God dramatically lifts from an ash heap to seat among rulers and honor.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

1 Samuel 2 recorded Hannah's prayer of thanksgiving after God answered her petition for a child. Barren and ridiculed by her rival, Hannah endured bitter distress until God remembered her and granted her a son, Samuel. When she presented Samuel at the tabernacle to serve the Lord, she broke into a song of worship.

Hannah's song does not focus merely on her personal joy; it proclaims the character and kingdom of God. She celebrates how God reverses human expectations by humbling the proud and exalting the lowly. Within this song, she introduces the word evyon to portray a person trapped in severe hardship, showing that God specializes in transforming the desperate condition of those who have no earthly help.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word evyon describes a needy or destitute person. It refers to someone who lacks essential provisions like food, shelter, or land, and is therefore dependent on charity, justice, or divine help.

While English Bibles often translate this word as "needy" or "poor," the NKJV translates it as "beggar" in 1 Samuel 2:8 to emphasize extreme poverty. The core idea of evyon is not merely low income, but vulnerability and active need. It marks someone who longs for relief because they cannot deliver themselves.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, God pays close attention to the evyon. Israelite law contains explicit commands protecting the evyon from economic exploitation and legal corruption. Judges were forbidden from denying justice to the needy, and wealthy landowners were required to leave gleanings in their fields for them.

In the Psalms, the evyon frequently appears as a figure who cries out to God in times of trouble. The word often carries a spiritual dimension alongside physical distress. The needy person recognizes their helplessness and looks to God as their sole refuge and defender.

4. Theological Implications

The usage of evyon reveals fundamental truths about God's character and kingdom:

  • God sees and defends the helpless. Human societies often ignore or exploit those without resources, but God stands as their guardian.

  • God exercises sovereign power to reverse human conditions. Human strength, pride, and status do not secure God's favor. He takes delight in lifting up those who own nothing.

  • Divine salvation rests on grace rather than human power. Just as God rescues the physically destitute, He saves spiritual beggars who recognize their need for His mercy.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:8, Hannah places evyon in parallelism with another word for the poor (dal): "He raises the poor from the dust / And lifts the beggar from the ash heap."

The imagery in this verse paints a vivid picture of restoration. The "ash heap" refers to the public dump outside a town where garbage and ashes were thrown, a place where outcast individuals often sat to beg or scavenge. To sit in the dust or on an ash heap signifies complete social collapse and humiliation.

God does not merely grant slight relief to the evyon; He lifts them completely out of degradation and sets them among princes, making them inherit a throne of glory. Through this poetic image, Hannah celebrates how God turned her own barrenness into blessing, establishing a principle that governs all of redemptive history: God exalts the humble and needy.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 23:6 — "You shall not pervert the judgment of your poor in his dispute."

  • Deuteronomy 15:11 — "For the poor will never cease from the land; therefore I command you, saying, 'You shall open your hand wide to your brother, to your poor and your needy, in your land.'"

  • Psalms 9:18 — "For the needy shall not always be forgotten; the expectation of the poor shall not perish forever."

  • Psalms 72:13 — "He will spare the poor and needy, and will save the souls of the needy."

  • Proverbs 14:31 — "He who oppresses the poor reproaches his Maker, but he who honors Him has mercy on the needy."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's song in 1 Samuel 2 serves as a foundational text for praise in Scripture. Centuries later, Mary, the mother of Jesus, echoed Hannah's words in her own song of praise (the Magnificat in Luke 1). Mary similarly praised God for scattering the proud, bringing down rulers, and exalting the humble.

This theme of divine reversal reaches its fullness in the gospel. Jesus Christ, though rich, became poor so that through His poverty believers might become rich. The transformation of the evyon from the ash heap to a seat among rulers foreshadows the spiritual elevation of every believer brought from spiritual poverty into the royal family of God.
