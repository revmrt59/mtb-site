INHERIT - (nâchal, Strong's H5157)

Summary:

The word nachal means to acquire, possess, or receive an inheritance, or to grant something as a lasting possession to another. In ancient Israel, an inheritance was not merely a temporary gift; it represented an enduring, God-given allotment that belonged permanently to a family or tribe. In 1 Samuel 2:8, Hannah uses the word to describe how God transforms the lives of the needy, taking those who are destitute and causing them to inherit a position of supreme honor and lasting dignity.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Israel, property and position were deeply tied to the land and the family line. God allotted the Promised Land to the tribes of Israel as a heritage passed down through generations. To receive an inheritance meant to gain a secure place, a lasting identity, and physical provision guaranteed by God's covenant.

When Hannah speaks her song of praise in 1 Samuel 2, Israel is undergoing a dark spiritual and political transition. The priesthood under Eli is corrupt, and the nation lacks godly leadership. Hannah, who had suffered years of social shame because of her barrenness, experienced God's miraculous favor when He answered her prayer and gave her a son, Samuel. In response, Hannah sings of God's absolute sovereignty over human destiny. She describes how the Lord reverses human expectations by lifting up the lowly and dethroning the proud. In this song, nachal highlights the divine act of giving helpless people an enduring place of honor that they could never achieve by human power.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Nachal refers to taking possession of property, receiving an inheritance, or dividing land among heirs. When used in forms where God or a human leader causes someone else to receive something, the word means to allot, grant, or bestow a lasting heritage.

Unlike a temporary reward or loan, an inheritance given through nachal carries the idea of permanent ownership and official standing.

In 1 Samuel 2:8, the word describes God's active role in conferring an inheritance. God does not merely offer temporary relief to the poor; He causes them to possess a royal seat of dignity as an enduring heritage.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, nachal appears in several related contexts:

  • Land and Tribal Allotments: The primary use of the word relates to Israel entering and possessing Canaan. Leaders like Joshua were commanded to divide the land so the tribes could possess their designated heritage.

  • Covenant Relations: God describes Israel as His own personal inheritance, while God Himself is declared to be the inheritance of the Levites, who received no physical land division.

  • Spiritual Heritage: Beyond physical property, believers take God's word, truth, and promises as a permanent treasure, treating God's testimonies as a heritage for the heart.

The word always implies a transfer of ownership that grants security and belonging to the receiver.

4

. Theological Implications

The concept of inheriting in 1 Samuel 2:8 carries profound theological truths about God's kingdom and His character:

First, true honor and exaltation originate with God alone. Human society measures worth by wealth, power, and social standing. Hannah's song insists that God routinely turns these worldly hierarchies upside down. The needy do not climb their way into power; God graciously lifts them up and grants them an inheritance of glory.

Second, God's gifts are secure and lasting. Because nachal refers to an inheritance rather than a temporary wage, the honor God bestows upon His people cannot be stripped away by worldly powers. This truth foreshadows the broader biblical promise that God's humble servants will inherit the kingdom prepared for them.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:8, Hannah paints a vivid picture of extreme contrast. She speaks of the poor being raised from the dust and the beggar lifted from the ash heap—the lowest, most degraded places in the ancient world. The ash heap was where garbage and waste accumulated outside the city gates, representing total social isolation and physical misery.

God takes people from this state of absolute destitution and grants them a seat among noble rulers. The phrase "make them inherit the throne of glory" brings this transformation to its highest point. The word nachal shows that this elevation is not a temporary honor; God establishes the humble in a permanent royal position.

The immediate context reinforces that this outcome happens entirely by divine power. Verse 9 explicitly states, "By strength no man shall prevail." The poor do not conquer the throne; God causes them to inherit it as a gift of sovereign grace.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Deuteronomy 1:38 — "Encourage him, for he shall cause Israel to inherit it."

  • Joshua 1:6 — "Be strong and of good courage, for to this people you shall divide as an inheritance the land which I swore to their fathers to give them."

  • Psalms 69:36 — "Also the descendants of His servants shall inherit it, and those who love His name shall dwell in it."

  • Psalms 119:111 — "Your testimonies I have taken as a heritage forever, for they are the rejoicing of my heart."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's song in 1 Samuel 2 forms the literary and theological foundation for the rest of 1 and 2 Samuel. It sets the stage for God rejecting proud leaders like King Saul and exalting a humble shepherd boy like David.

Furthermore, Hannah's praise strongly echoes in the New Testament through the Magnificat, Mary's song of praise in Luke 1:46–55. Like Hannah, Mary praises God for scattering the proud, bringing down rulers from their thrones, and exalting the humble. The idea of inheriting a glorious seat points directly to the gospel message, where God saves spiritual outcasts, adopts them into His family, and makes them co-heirs with Christ.
