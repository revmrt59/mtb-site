POOR - (dal, Strong's H1800)

Summary:

The Hebrew word dal describes a person who is low, weak, poor, or helpless, lacking both physical strength and earthly resources. It refers to someone in a reduced or vulnerable condition who cannot elevate or protect themselves. In 1 Samuel 2:8, dal describes those who are powerless and destitute in human terms, whom God sovereignly lifts out of the dust to grant them a place of honor and security.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In 1 Samuel 2, Hannah offers a song of praise after bringing her young son Samuel to the house of the Lord at Shiloh. For years, Hannah had suffered the pain of barrenness and the cruel taunts of her rival. When God remembered her and granted her a son, her response was not mere personal relief, but a prophetic song exalting the sovereign character of God.

Hannah’s song celebrates how God routinely turns human expectations upside down. The proud and powerful are brought low, while the hungry and weak are vindicated. Within this setting, the word dal introduces those who occupy the lowest tier of human society—those who are completely dependent on God because they possess no worldly clout or standing.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word dal describes a state of weakness, lowliness, or poverty. It derives from a root that conveys the idea of dangling, leaning, or becoming thin and reduced.

Depending on the context, the word can mean:

  • Materially poor, lacking basic wealth or property.

  • Socially weak, possessing little influence or defense against injustice.

  • Physically depleted or frail, lacking vigor.

Unlike words that focus strictly on spiritual humility, dal primarily highlights a person's tangible lack of resources, power, or status. A person described as dal is one who has been brought low by circumstances and lacks the capacity to restore their own situation.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, dal appears in legal, wisdom, prophetic, and poetic writings to highlight God's standard of justice and care for the vulnerable.

In the Mosaic Law, God establishes clear protections for the dal. Judges are commanded not to favor a person simply because they are poor, nor to allow the wealthy to crush them in court. God also makes special provisions in the sacrificial system so that a poor person who cannot afford a lamb can still bring an acceptable offering.

In Wisdom Literature and the Prophets, dal frequently describes those who suffer at the hands of greedy or oppressive rulers. Proverbs warns that oppressing the poor insults their Maker, while showing kindness to them is honored by God. The prophets repeatedly declare that a key mark of a righteous king is whether he defends the rights of the dal.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah’s song uses dal to expose the vanity of human self-reliance. In the vision of Scripture, earthly wealth and power do not guarantee permanent security, nor does low estate mean abandonment by God.

God’s character is revealed in His active concern for those who have no earthly defense. He is not indifferent to those trapped in lowliness. The elevation of the poor demonstrates that true honor, security, and position do not come from human effort or social status, but from the sovereign grace of God, who raises up the humble according to His good purpose.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:8, dal is set in poetic parallelism with the word for "needy." Hannah declares that God "raises the poor out of the dust / And lifts the needy from the ash heap."

The imagery of "dust" and the "ash heap" represents total degradation, poverty, and social displacement. Sitting in the dust or on a refuse pile was the reality for outcast beggars who had nowhere else to turn. By placing dal in this setting, the passage stresses the complete lowliness of the person's earthly condition.

The power of the passage lies in the dramatic reversal that follows: God does not merely grant minor relief to the poor; He lifts them from the lowest place and seats them with princes to inherit a throne of glory. The term dal establishes the starting point of helpless human lowliness so that God's lifting power stands out in full contrast.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Leviticus 14:21 — "But if he is poor and cannot afford it, then he shall take one male lamb..."

  • Job 5:16 — "So the poor have hope, and injustice shuts her mouth."

  • Psalms 41:1 — "Blessed is he who considers the poor; the Lord will deliver him in time of trouble."

  • Psalms 113:7 — "He raises the poor out of the dust and lifts the needy out of the ash heap..."

  • Proverbs 19:17 — "He who has pity on the poor lends to the Lord, and He will pay back what he has given."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The themes of Hannah’s song, centered on the elevation of the dal, shape the entire narrative of 1 and 2 Samuel. God repeatedly sets aside human standards of strength to exalt the lowly—choosing young David over his older, taller brothers and establishing an enduring kingdom through one who began as a humble shepherd boy.

Furthermore, Hannah's words directly anticipate the song of Mary in Luke 1. When Mary learns she will bear the Messiah, she echoes Hannah’s praise, rejoicing that God has put down the mighty from their thrones and exalted the lowly.
