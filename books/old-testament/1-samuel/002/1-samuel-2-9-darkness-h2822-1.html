DARKNESS - (chôshek, Strong's H2822)

CHOSHEK - ( choshek, Strong’s [H2822] )

Summary:

The word refers to literal darkness, night, obscurity, or the absence of light, and is also used figuratively for distress, ignorance, wickedness, or the realm of death. In 1 Samuel 2:9, the word describes the profound obscurity and peril that overtake the wicked, contrasting with the Lord's preservation of His saints.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The term is a common Old Testament noun used across historical, poetic, and prophetic literature to describe physical night, storm clouds, or deep gloom. In ancient Hebrew thought, light often symbolized life, divine favor, and order, while darkness represented danger, confusion, and places hidden from human sight. Scriptural writers frequently draw upon these concrete associations of the physical environment to picture moral realities, human helplessness, and divine judgment.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to the simple absence of physical illumination, such as the darkness of night or a heavy storm. Beyond literal night, it describes obscurity, hidden places, or the gloomy subterranean realm associated with death and the grave. It also serves as a vivid metaphor for moral blindness, spiritual distress, and the profound adversity that overwhelms those who oppose God.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses the word both literally and metaphorically. Physically, it marks the boundary of the day, the cover of night, or the dimness of deep caves and shadows. Figuratively, writers use it to portray times of severe trouble, national calamity, or spiritual confusion. It describes a condition where human strength and vision fail completely, leaving a person entirely dependent on outside help.

4. Theological Implications

The Bible teaches that God's sovereignty extends even over the darkest places. While darkness often represents judgment, distress, or moral evil, Scripture makes clear that nothing is hidden from God and that even the deepest gloom cannot thwart His purposes. The wicked stumble in obscurity because they reject the Lord, whereas God Himself can turn darkness into light for those who trust Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:9, Hannah prays prophetically during a time of personal and national transition, magnifying the justice and power of God. She declares that the Lord guards the feet of His saints, but the wicked shall be silent in darkness. Here, the word pictures the ultimate helplessness, ruin, and silencing of those who rely on their own strength rather than honoring God. Physical imagery of stumbling in the dark illustrates the moral and spiritual collapse of the proud.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 1:2 — "The earth was without form, and void; and darkness was on the face of the deep."

  • Job 3:4 — "Let that day be darkness; let not God seek it from above, nor let the light shine upon it."

  • Psalms 107:14 — "He brought them out of darkness and the shadow of death, and broke their chains in pieces."

  • Isaiah 42:7 — "To open blind eyes, to bring out prisoners from the prison, those who sit in darkness from the prison house."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between light and darkness runs throughout the biblical narrative as a picture of the fundamental division between human rebellion and divine grace. While people often wander in spiritual obscurity, God's ultimate rescue involves bringing His people out of the shadow of death and into His marvelous light.
