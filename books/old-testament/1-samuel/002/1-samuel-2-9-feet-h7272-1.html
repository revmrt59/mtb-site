FEET - (regel, Strong's H7272)

REGEL - ( regel, Strong’s [H7272] )

Summary:

The Hebrew word regel primarily refers to the physical foot or leg of a human or animal, and it is frequently used in biblical poetry and prose to picture human walking, standing, and stability. In 1 Samuel 2:9, within Hannah’s prayer, the word is used in the dual form to describe the feet of the Lord's saints, symbolizing their footing, security, and path of life. Hannah declares that God will guard the feet of His saints while the wicked shall be silent in darkness, showing that human stability and ultimate perseverance rest entirely upon the protective care of God rather than on human strength.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In biblical Hebrew, regel is the standard term for the lower limb, foot, or leg. Beyond the literal anatomical meaning, bodily members are frequently used in Scripture to represent a person's entire walk, conduct, and journey through life. In ancient Near Eastern cultures and throughout the Old Testament, the feet represent one's stance, progress, and stability. To have secure feet means to walk uprightly and safely, while stumbling feet represent ruin, defeat, or moral failure. In 1 Samuel 2:9, this physical imagery is anchored in Hannah’s song of praise, where she reflects on the reversal of human fortunes and the absolute sovereignty of God over human affairs.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word regel refers directly to the foot or leg. Depending on the context, it can describe the physical foot used for walking, standing, or running. It is also used in various idiomatic expressions, such as marching or stepping, and in architectural contexts for the base or support of an object. When applied to people, it regularly represents physical movement and the direction of one's life choices and actions. In 1 Samuel 2:9, the dual form specifies both feet, pointing to a person's complete stance and physical footing as they walk through life.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Scripture uses regel both literally and metaphorically. Literally, it describes the washing of feet, physical steps, and the body parts used for travel or labor. Metaphorically, the word often describes moral and spiritual progress. A person's "feet" can walk in the way of righteousness or wander into destruction. The posture of the feet also conveys submission, as when someone falls at another's feet, or victory, as when a conqueror places a foot upon an enemy. In poetic texts, God's protection of the feet emphasizes keeping a person from slipping into calamity or moral ruin.

4. Theological Implications

The theological significance of regel, especially in wisdom and poetic literature, centers on divine guidance and preservation. Human beings are prone to stumbling, both physically and morally, but Scripture repeatedly affirms that true stability comes from God. When Hannah declares that God will guard the feet of His saints, she points to the doctrine of divine preservation. Believers do not stand by their own inherent strength or cleverness; rather, God sustains their steps and prevents them from being overthrown by the wickedness of the world.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:9, Hannah contrasts the proud self-reliance of humanity with the faithful protection of God. She states that the adversaries of the Lord shall be broken in pieces and that God will judge the ends of the earth, adding that He will keep the feet of His saints. In this setting, the feet represent the daily walk and security of those who belong to God. While human strength fails and the wicked are silenced in darkness, the saints remain upright because God Himself watches over their steps and preserves them from stumbling.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 8:9 — "And the dove found no resting place for the sole of her foot, and she returned into the ark to him, for the waters were on the face of the whole earth."

  • Psalms 40:2 — "He also brought me up out of a horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, and established my steps."

  • Proverbs 3:23 — "Then you will walk safely in your way, and your foot will not stumble."

  • Isaiah 26:6 — "The foot shall tread it down—the feet of the poor and the steps of the needy."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The imagery of the foot is closely tied to the biblical theme of the path or the way. Throughout the Psalms and Proverbs, the believer's life is viewed as a journey where every step requires divine direction. The contrast between falling and standing runs throughout the historical and poetic books, reminding readers that human stability is entirely dependent upon God's upholding grace.
