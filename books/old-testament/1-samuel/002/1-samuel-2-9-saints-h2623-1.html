SAINTS - (châçîyd, Strong's H2623)

Summary:

The word chasid describes a person characterized by godliness, faithful devotion, and covenant loyalty toward God. It comes from a word root associated with steadfast love and mercy. In 1 Samuel 2:9, Hannah uses this word when declaring that God "will guard the feet of His saints." In this context, the term highlights those who rely faithfully on the Lord, contrasting them with the arrogant and self-reliant wicked. Hannah assures the listener that God personally protects the path and life of those devoted to Him.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

1 Samuel 2 contains the prayer of Hannah, spoken after the Lord fulfilled her plea for a child and she dedicated young Samuel to God's service at the tabernacle in Shiloh. Hannah's prayer is a prophetic song of praise that celebrates God's holiness, power, and sovereign rule over human history.

In this song, Hannah describes a complete reversal of earthly expectations: the hungry are filled, the weak are given strength, and the proud are brought low. In verse 9, she focuses on how God treats two distinct groups of people: those who are faithful to Him and those who oppose Him. The word translated "saints" identifies the faithful group whose steps God actively protects.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Chasid is closely related to the well-known Hebrew concept of chesed, which means steadfast love, mercy, or covenant loyalty. A chasid is someone who reflects or responds to God's faithful love through personal devotion and loyalty.

Depending on the context, English translations render chasid as "saints," "godly," "holy," or "merciful." The term does not imply sinless perfection. Rather, it identifies a person whose life is marked by faithful commitment to God and trust in His covenant promises. In 1 Samuel 2:9, the word identifies God's devoted people as the objects of His personal guard and protection.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, particularly in poetry and worship, chasid functions as a primary descriptor for God's faithful people. The word frequently appears in contrast with the wicked or arrogant.

While the wicked trust in their own strength, the chasid recognizes complete dependence on God. The term highlights both a relationship and a character:

  • Relationally, the chasid belongs to God and rests under His care.

  • Characteristically, the chasid seeks to walk in obedience and loyalty to the Lord.

When God is described with a related sense, it points to His unending mercy and faithfulness. When applied to people, it describes those who respond to God's mercy with faithful commitment.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah's use of chasid in 1 Samuel 2:9 sets forth important truths about divine providence and human weakness:

  • Protection is grounded in grace: God guards His saints not because they are self-sufficient, but because they belong to Him in covenant trust.

  • Human power is limited: As Hannah declares in the same verse, "by strength no man shall prevail." Salvation and ultimate security never come from human effort alone.

  • God distinguishes the righteous and the wicked: The Lord actively orders the outcomes of human lives, preserving His faithful ones while bringing judgment upon those who persist in pride.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:9, Hannah specifically speaks of God guarding the "feet" of His saints. In Hebrew thought, a person's feet represent their path, conduct, and journey through life.

Guarding their feet means that God keeps His faithful ones from stumbling into spiritual ruin or total destruction. While the wicked are eventually "silent in darkness," losing their footing and their boastful voice, the faithful walk safely under divine keeping. This contrast comforted Hannah during her own years of affliction and provided a prophetic framework for Israel's future leaders, showing that true success comes only through humble dependence on the Lord.

  • Psalms 116:15 — "Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of His saints."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's song serves as an essential bridge in the narrative of 1 Samuel. Shortly after Hannah offers this prayer, the corrupt priesthood of Eli's sons falls under divine judgment because of their arrogance and disregard for God. In contrast, Samuel grows up serving the Lord faithfully. Hannah's declaration that God guards the feet of His chasid explains why Eli's house collapses while God preserves young Samuel to guide the nation into a new era.
