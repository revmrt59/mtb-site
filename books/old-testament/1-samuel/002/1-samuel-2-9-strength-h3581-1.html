STRENGTH - (kôach, Strong's H3581)

Summary:

The word koach refers to physical vigor, natural capacity, human power, force, or capability. In 1 Samuel 2:9, Hannah declares that "by strength no man shall prevail," emphasizing that natural human effort, self-reliance, and physical force are entirely insufficient to secure ultimate victory, protection, or success apart from God's sovereign hand.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The book of 1 Samuel opens during a dark period of spiritual decline in Israel's history. The nation suffered under weak spiritual leadership, moral corruption, and external threats from neighboring enemies. Against this bleak backdrop, the narrative introduces Hannah, a woman burdened by barrenness and tormented by her rival, Peninnah.

After praying in deep distress at the tabernacle in Shiloh, God granted Hannah a son, whom she named Samuel. When Samuel was old enough, Hannah presented him to the LORD in dedicated service. 1 Samuel 2 contains Hannah's song of praise—a prophetic prayer celebrating God's holiness, justice, and sovereign power. Hannah recognizes that her personal deliverance is a picture of how God operates across human history: He humbles the proud and self-sufficient, while raising up the weak and needy. In verse 9, she uses the word koach ("strength") to contrast human power with divine sovereignty.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Koach denotes physical force, human vitality, natural ability, energy, power, or capacity. Depending on the setting, the word can refer to several forms of natural capability:

  • Physical strength or muscle force in human bodies or animals.

  • Natural vigor and vitality that sustains life and labor.

  • Capability, resources, or financial means to produce wealth or achieve results.

  • Military force, power, or human potential.

When used of human beings, the word highlights created, finite human capacity. When set in opposition to God, koach demonstrates the natural limits of human effort. Natural strength is a gift from God, but it cannot stand as a substitute for Him.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, koach appears in contexts where physical capacity, endurance, or resources are weighed.

In some passages, koach represents ordinary physical vigor or natural energy. It describes the physical power of a strong champion or the strength required for daily work and military duty. It is also used negatively to describe failing strength due to aging, distress, or exhaustion.

In other settings, koach refers to financial or practical ability—the capacity to generate resources or accomplish tasks. In every case, Scripture treats human koach as creaturely and limited. When individuals or nations place their trust in their own koach, they fall into spiritual pride. The central biblical dynamic surrounding koach is the reminder that human energy is derived from God, and true security comes from trusting Him rather than relying on mortal strength.

4. Theological Implications

Hannah’s declaration highlights a major biblical truth: human capacity is inherently insufficient to accomplish spiritual victory or divine purposes. Society routinely honors those with physical power, political dominance, financial resources, or high social status. However, God measures success by an entirely different standard.

Human strength (koach) cannot overcome sin, secure ultimate deliverance, or withstand God’s righteous judgment. Relying on self-generated strength breeds arrogance and blinds individuals to their absolute dependence on the LORD. God frequently allows human strength to fail so that His people will learn to rely entirely on His grace and power. True strength is not found in self-sufficiency, but in humble reliance on the Almighty, who guards and sustains those who trust in Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In 1 Samuel 2:9, Hannah contrasts two destinies: the LORD "will guard the feet of His saints," but "the wicked shall be silent in darkness." She grounds this assurance in a foundational truth: "For by strength no man shall prevail."

The word koach in this verse directly targets human self-reliance. Peninnah had children and social status, yet Hannah—weak, misunderstood, and childless—was vindicated by God. Hannah recognized that human status, physical capacity, and earthly advantages do not determine the final outcome of a person's life.

The verb "prevail" carries the idea of overcoming, gaining mastery, or securing victory. Hannah asserts that no human being can prevail before God or achieve true success merely through human exertion, scheming, or force. The wicked may boast in their current power, but their human strength will ultimately fail them. Conversely, those whom God guards do not stand by their own power, but by His divine protection.

  • Psalms 71:9 — "Do not cast me off in the time of old age; do not forsake me when my strength fails."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Hannah's song in 1 Samuel 2 sets the theological agenda for the entire narrative of 1 and 2 Samuel. Throughout these books, the contrast between human self-reliance (koach) and divine trust recurs repeatedly:

  • King Saul appeared tall, impressive, and strong in the eyes of the people, yet his reliance on his own wisdom and power led to his down-fall.

  • David was a young shepherd boy lacking worldly power, yet he defeated Goliath not with sword or spear, but in the name of the LORD.

Grammatically, koach appears here as a singular common noun, functioning as a general rule: no amount of human strength, in any form, enables a person to prevail independently of God. This principle points forward to the gospel message, where salvation is explicitly revealed as a work of divine grace rather than human effort or natural merit.
