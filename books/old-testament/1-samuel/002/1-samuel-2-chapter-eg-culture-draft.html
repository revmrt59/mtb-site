EG Culture - 1 Samuel 2

HONOR || Through His Eyes

Culture Statement

Relationships are protected when we ascribe heaven’s value to the people around us. Like Jesus, we choose to interact with people, not as they deserve, but according to their God given value.

Living It in 1 Samuel 2

Honor is a central issue in this chapter, and it appears in more than one way.

Giving God His rightful place. The chapter’s own language of honor begins with God. The Hebrew word for honor carries the sense of weight: we give God the weight His worth deserves when other loyalties compete. God told Eli, “you … honor your sons more than Me” (2:29), and declared, “those who honor Me I will honor” (2:30). Honoring God means valuing Him above every competing loyalty and acting accordingly.

Valuing people through heaven’s eyes means not treating people as if they existed for our benefit. Eli’s sons treated worshipers as a source of meat, demanded God’s portion for themselves, and threatened force when challenged (2:13–16). Their sexual misconduct further dishonored those serving at the tabernacle (2:22). These were “the LORD’S people” (2:24), not people who existed for the priests’ benefit. Honor recognizes their God-given value and treats them accordingly for their value to God, not their value to us.

Misplaced honor. Eli rebuked his sons, but he did not restrain them (2:22–25; 3:13). God’s verdict was that he honored his sons above the LORD (2:29). Honoring family cannot mean allowing them to dishonor God or harm His people. Heaven’s value must govern both our relationships and the decisions those relationships require.

Honor through God’s eyes. Hannah’s song shows what heaven’s value looks like. God “raises the poor from the dust and lifts the beggar from the ash heap, to set them among princes” (2:8). The world’s ranking of who matters is not His. The chapter therefore gives us a picture of Honor at East Gate: people are valued according to what God says about them, whether they sit on a refuse heap, hold a priestly office, or are a child in a linen ephod, and God Himself honors those who honor Him.

We are People of the Word

Culture Statement

The Holy Bible is the only infallible and final authority for Christian faith and life. The Holy Scriptures are timeless, inerrant and are to be read personally, discerned corporately, and walked out in community. We interpret all Scripture through the life and teachings of Jesus, trusting Holy Spirit to reveal truth and transform us as we submit ourselves fully to God’s revealed Word.

Living It in 1 Samuel 2

The chapter shows what happens when custom replaces commandment, and what happens when a person stays anchored in what God has said.

Custom against commandment. The priests’ “custom” (mishpat) with the people was to send a servant with a three-pronged fork to take whatever came up (2:13–14). God’s law had already said which portions belonged to the priests, and that the fat belonged to the LORD and must be burned first (Leviticus 3:16; 7:28–34). Eli’s sons replaced God’s instructions with a procedure that served themselves. “The way we have always done it” does not make something right. The standard remains what God has said.

A worshiper who held to the Word. When the servant demanded meat, an ordinary Israelite answered, “They should really burn the fat first” (2:16). He knew what God required and appealed to it when the priest’s servant demanded otherwise. As people of the Word, we all need to know Scripture so we can recognize when instruction or practice conflicts with what God has said and avoid being misled.

God’s word comes to pass. The man of God speaks, “Thus says the LORD” (2:27), and announces that Eli’s sons will die on the same day (2:34). Chapter 4 records the fulfillment. As people of the Word, we need to remember that God’s warnings are as sure as His promises.

We are a Tabernacle of David

Culture Statement

We were created to delight the Father and host His presence. As we embrace His ways, our lives become continual offerings of praise—both personally and together. We live with unhindered access to God, ministering to Him first and carrying His presence wherever we go. As His dwelling is restored among us, we become a people through whom the nations are drawn to seek the Lord.

Living It in 1 Samuel 2

Samuel’s service and Hannah’s praise show lives offered to the LORD.

Ministering to Him first. Samuel “ministered to the LORD” (2:11) and “ministered before the LORD” (2:18). His service was directed toward God. Eli’s sons, although serving at the sanctuary, “did not know the LORD” (2:12) and used their position to satisfy themselves. As a people called to host His presence, we minister to Him first above all else.

Lives as offerings of praise. Hannah’s song is a picture of worship offered from a whole life. Having entrusted Samuel and given Samuel back to the LORD, she rejoices, “My heart rejoices in the LORD” (2:1). Her praise and her surrender belong together. Our lives become continual offerings of praise as our actions, as well as our songs, express our devotion and worship to Him.
