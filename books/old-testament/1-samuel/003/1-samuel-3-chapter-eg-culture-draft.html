Presence Driven

Culture Statement

Culture Statement
Holy Spirit is God Himself dwelling within us. He directs our decisions, shapes our communities, and equips us to supernaturally impact our surroundings. We hear, connect, and obey as He leads. We are Spirit-led and unashamed.

Living It in 1 Samuel 3

Living It in 1 Samuel 3
“The LORD was with him” (1 Samuel 3:19). The defining reality behind Samuel’s growing ministry was not his position, ability, or reputation, but the presence of God with him. The Lord’s presence gave weight to his words and effectiveness to his calling.

We want the same reality to characterize us. We do not want merely to do things for God or depend upon programs, personalities, methods, or human ability. We want to live and serve with the Lord present among us, directing and empowering what we do.

Hear God and Obey

Culture Statement

Culture Statement
East Gate exists to teach people how to hear God’s voice, connect with Him daily, and walk with Him in obedience.

Living It in 1 Samuel 3

Living It in 1 Samuel 3
“Speak, for Your servant hears” (1 Samuel 3:10). Samuel positioned himself to hear God, but hearing was only the beginning. He had to receive what God said, connect it to the situation before him, and then obey even when obedience was difficult.

This is the pattern we want to live: hear, connect, and obey. We listen for what God is saying, connect His truth to our lives and circumstances, and respond to what He shows us. Hearing God is not merely about receiving revelation. The goal is a life that walks with Him in obedience.

Living It in 1 Samuel 3
“Then the LORD appeared again in Shiloh” (1 Samuel 3:21). The word “again” is significant. Samuel’s first encounter with God was not intended to be his last. As he continued before the Lord, God continued revealing Himself to him.

We can position ourselves to hear God with expectancy, but we must also “take heed how you hear” (Luke 8:18). Hearing God is a privilege that carries responsibility. Jesus said, “For everyone to whom much is given, from him much will be required” (Luke 12:48). The more God entrusts to us, the more carefully we must steward what we hear. We want to expect God to speak and be ready to obey when He does.

People of the Word

Culture Statement

Culture Statement
The Holy Bible is the only infallible and final authority for Christian faith and life. The Holy Scriptures are timeless, inerrant and are to be read personally, discerned corporately, and walked out in community. We interpret all Scripture through the life and teachings of Jesus, trusting Holy Spirit to reveal truth and transform us as we submit ourselves fully to God’s revealed Word.

Living It in 1 Samuel 3

Living It in 1 Samuel 3
“Then Samuel told him everything, and hid nothing from him” (1 Samuel 3:18). Samuel was afraid to tell Eli what God had said, but he did not soften the message, leave out the difficult parts, or change it to make it easier to receive.

As People of the Word, we do not water down Scripture because something is uncomfortable, unpopular, or difficult to communicate. We submit ourselves to what God has revealed rather than reshaping His Word around our preferences. Samuel also reminds us that faithfulness does not require harshness. He cared about Eli and felt the weight of the message, but he still told him everything. We want to handle God’s Word with the same courage, humility, accuracy, and integrity.
