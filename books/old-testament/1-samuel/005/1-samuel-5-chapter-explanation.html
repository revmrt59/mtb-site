Chapter Study

1 Samuel 5

Verse-by-Verse
1 Samuel 5:1
1 Samuel 5:1
NKJV

1 Samuel 5:1 Then the Philistines took the ark of God and brought it from Ebenezer to Ashdod.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 5:1 After the Philistines captured the Ark of God, they took it from the battleground at Ebenezer to the town of Ashdod. (NLT)

Main Idea

The Philistines take the captured ark of God and bring it from Ebenezer to Ashdod. The ark now enters Philistine territory as a prize of war.

Verse Explanation

The Philistines take the captured ark of God and bring it from Ebenezer to Ashdod. The ark now enters Philistine territory as a prize of war.

“Then the Philistines took the ark of God”

Chapter 4 ended with a dying mother's cry that the glory had departed from Israel. Chapter 5 follows the ark itself. “The Philistines took the ark of God” as plunder from the battlefield. To them it was a prize of war, the captured emblem of a defeated people's god. The narrator still calls it “the ark of God.” Its capture had not changed whose ark it was.

“and brought it from Ebenezer to Ashdod.”

The Philistines carried the ark from “Ebenezer,” the Israelite camp where it had been taken (4:1), to “Ashdod,” one of their five principal cities (6:17). Ashdod lay on the coastal plain, several miles inland from the Mediterranean, well inside Philistine territory. From a human point of view, the ark was now as far from Israel's help as it could be. No Israelite army, priest, or prophet went with it. Whatever happened next, Israel would have no part in it.

Study Notes

Ashdod is identified with Tel Ashdod (Esdud), about three to four miles (5–6 km) inland from the coast.

The five Philistine cities were Ashdod, Gaza, Ashkelon, Gath, and Ekron (Joshua 13:3; 1 Samuel 6:17).

Gaza also had a temple of Dagon, where the Philistine lords offered sacrifice after Samson's capture (Judges 16:23).

The account of the ark runs from 4:1 to 7:2. In chapter 5 no Israelite speaks or acts.

1 Samuel 5:2
1 Samuel 5:2
NKJV

1 Samuel 5:2 When the Philistines took the ark of God, they brought it into the house of Dagon and set it by Dagon.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 5:2 They carried the Ark of God into the temple of Dagon and placed it beside an idol of Dagon. (NLT)

Main Idea

They bring the ark of God into the house of Dagon and set it beside their god. Placing it there presents Israel’s God as though He were a captive trophy in Dagon’s house.

Verse Explanation

They bring the ark of God into the house of Dagon and set it beside their god. Placing it there presents Israel’s God as though He were a captive trophy in Dagon’s house.

“When the Philistines took the ark of God, they brought it into the house of Dagon”

“The house of Dagon” was Dagon's temple in Ashdod. “Dagon” was a major god of the Philistines, to whom they had offered a great sacrifice when Samson was captured, saying, “Our god has delivered into our hands Samson our enemy!” (Judges 16:23). Bringing the ark into Dagon's temple made the same claim: Dagon had defeated Israel's God.

“and set it by Dagon.”

The Philistines placed the ark “by Dagon,” beside the image of their god. Victorious peoples in the ancient world often placed captured gods or sacred objects in the temple of their own god as trophies, displaying their god's superiority. The ark was set there as a captive, a subordinate beside the conqueror. That is how the scene looked to the Philistines. The next morning would show who was subordinate to whom.

Study Notes

A god named Dagan was worshiped widely in the ancient Near East, including at Mari, Ebla, and Ugarit, long before the Philistines settled in Canaan. The Philistines apparently adopted him after arriving.

Two explanations of the name have been proposed: a connection with the Hebrew word dagan, ‘grain,’ suggesting a god of crops, and a connection with dag, ‘fish,’ which gave rise to the familiar picture of Dagon as half man, half fish. The fish-shaped image comes from later interpretation and is not established, and neither explanation of the name is settled.

After Saul's death the Philistines fastened his head “in the temple of Dagon” (1 Chronicles 10:10), another example of war trophies displayed in their god's house.

1 Samuel 5:3
1 Samuel 5:3
NKJV

1 Samuel 5:3 And when the people of Ashdod arose early in the morning, there was Dagon, fallen on its face to the earth before the ark of the LORD. So they took Dagon and set it in its place again.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 5:3 But when the citizens of Ashdod went to see it the next morning, Dagon had fallen with his face to the ground in front of the Ark of the LORD! So they took Dagon and put him in his place again. (NLT)

Main Idea

Early the next morning the people of Ashdod find Dagon fallen on its face to the earth before the ark of the LORD, and they must set it back in its place. The idol lies prostrate before the LORD and depends on its worshipers to stand again.

Verse Explanation

Early the next morning the people of Ashdod find Dagon fallen on its face to the earth before the ark of the LORD, and they must set it back in its place. The idol lies prostrate before the LORD and depends on its worshipers to stand again.

“And when the people of Ashdod arose early in the morning,”

The next day began like any other at the temple. The people of Ashdod, likely including Dagon's priests and worshipers, came early, perhaps to offer their morning worship.

“there was Dagon, fallen on its face to the earth before the ark of the LORD.”

What they found was humiliating. Dagon had “fallen on its face to the earth,” the posture of a worshiper bowing down in submission before a superior. The idol lay prostrate “before the ark of the LORD.” The narrator now uses the name “the LORD,” Israel's God by His own name, for the first time in this chapter. The scene reverses the Philistines' intention. They had set the ark beside Dagon as a captive; Dagon now lay facedown before the ark as a servant before his Master. No Israelite was present. The LORD needed no one to defend Him.

“So they took Dagon and set it in its place again.”

The worshipers lifted their god back onto his pedestal. The verse exposes the helplessness of an idol. Dagon could not raise himself; his own people had to set him up again. Isaiah later mocks idols that must be carried and set in place, “a carved image that will not totter” (Isaiah 40:20), and the Psalms describe idols that have hands but cannot handle and feet but cannot walk (Psalm 115:7). The LORD, by contrast, had acted alone in the night.

Study Notes

The Philistines “took” the ark (5:1–2), and now they must “take” Dagon to put him back; the same Hebrew verb is used.

Earlier in this account the Philistines had spoken of the plagues on Egypt (4:8). In 6:6 their priests again recall Egypt as they advise sending the ark back.

1 Samuel 5:4
1 Samuel 5:4
NKJV

1 Samuel 5:4 And when they arose early the next morning, there was Dagon, fallen on its face to the ground before the ark of the LORD. The head of Dagon and both the palms of its hands were broken off on the threshold; only Dagon's torso was left of it.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 5:4 But the next morning the same thing happened — Dagon had fallen face down before the Ark of the LORD again. This time his head and hands had broken off and were lying in the doorway. Only the trunk of his body was left intact. (NLT)

Main Idea

The following morning Dagon has fallen again before the ark, and its head and both palms lie broken off on the threshold, leaving only its torso. The second fall is a decisive humiliation, and the idol’s severed hands stand in contrast to the powerful hand of the LORD that follows.

Verse Explanation

The following morning Dagon has fallen again before the ark, and its head and both palms lie broken off on the threshold, leaving only its torso. The second fall is a decisive humiliation, and the idol’s severed hands stand in contrast to the powerful hand of the LORD that follows.

“And when they arose early the next morning, there was Dagon, fallen on its face to the ground before the ark of the LORD.”

The second morning repeated the first, but with a decisive difference. Dagon had again fallen facedown before the ark. The repetition ruled out accident. Whatever the Philistines thought on the first day, the second fall showed that a power greater than Dagon was at work.

“The head of Dagon and both the palms of its hands were broken off on the threshold;”

This time the idol was broken. Its “head” and “both the palms of its hands” lay severed “on the threshold,” the entrance to the inner shrine. In ancient warfare victors sometimes cut off the heads and hands of defeated enemies as proof of victory (compare 17:51; 31:9). Dagon now lay like a defeated warrior. His head, the seat of authority, and his hands, the instruments of power, were gone. The god credited with capturing the ark had no head to rule and no hands to act. The chapter's next section speaks repeatedly of the LORD's hand (5:6, 7, 9, 11), and the contrast is deliberate: Dagon's hands lay broken on the floor while the LORD's hand fell heavy on the city.

“only Dagon's torso was left of it.”

All that remained was the trunk of the image. The god who seemed to have triumphed over Israel's God was reduced to a stump.

Study Notes

The final clause reads literally ‘only Dagon was left to him,’ which is difficult. Translations supply a word such as ‘torso’ or ‘trunk’ to make sense of it (NKJV, NLT).

Some older interpreters connected the phrase with a supposed fish-shaped lower body, but that reading depends on an uncertain explanation of Dagon's name and is not required by the text.

The Septuagint supplies ‘the back’ or ‘spine’ of Dagon (NETS ‘only the spine of Dagon was left’).

1 Samuel 5:5
1 Samuel 5:5
NKJV

1 Samuel 5:5 Therefore neither the priests of Dagon nor any who come into Dagon's house tread on the threshold of Dagon in Ashdod to this day.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 5:5 That is why to this day neither the priests of Dagon nor anyone who enters the temple of Dagon in Ashdod will step on its threshold. (NLT)

Main Idea

For this reason the priests of Dagon and all who enter his house do not tread on the threshold of Dagon in Ashdod to the writer’s day. The lasting custom preserves the memory of Dagon’s humiliation before the LORD.

Verse Explanation

For this reason the priests of Dagon and all who enter his house do not tread on the threshold of Dagon in Ashdod to the writer’s day. The lasting custom preserves the memory of Dagon’s humiliation before the LORD.

“Therefore neither the priests of Dagon nor any who come into Dagon's house”

The narrator explains a custom that resulted from these events. “Therefore” links the practice directly to the breaking of Dagon on the threshold. It applied to “the priests of Dagon” and to all worshipers who entered his temple.

“tread on the threshold of Dagon in Ashdod to this day.”

Dagon's worshipers would not step on the threshold where their god's head and hands had fallen. Perhaps they regarded the spot as sacred or as touched by misfortune. Whatever their reason, they continued the custom “to this day,” meaning up to the time when the account was written. Every time a Philistine stepped over that threshold, the custom bore unwitting witness to the night Dagon fell before the LORD. The worshipers kept a reminder of their god's humiliation as part of his worship.

Study Notes

Zephaniah 1:9 speaks of punishing “those who leap over the threshold.” Some interpreters connect that verse with a pagan custom like the one at Ashdod, but the connection is uncertain.

1 Samuel 5:6
1 Samuel 5:6
NKJV

1 Samuel 5:6 But the hand of the LORD was heavy on the people of Ashdod, and He ravaged them and struck them with tumors, both Ashdod and its territory.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 5:6 Then the LORD’s heavy hand struck the people of Ashdod and the nearby villages with a plague of tumors. (NLT)

Main Idea

The hand of the LORD is heavy on the people of Ashdod; He ravages them and strikes them with tumors, both the city and its territory. The LORD Himself is the agent of this judgment, acting without any Israelite army.

Verse Explanation

The hand of the LORD is heavy on the people of Ashdod; He ravages them and strikes them with tumors, both the city and its territory. The LORD Himself is the agent of this judgment, acting without any Israelite army.

“But the hand of the LORD was heavy on the people of Ashdod,”

The LORD now turned from Dagon to Dagon's people. “The hand of the LORD” is a common biblical expression for God's active power, whether in deliverance or in judgment. It recalls the plagues on Egypt, where “the hand of the LORD” brought pestilence (Exodus 9:3). “Heavy” comes from the same Hebrew root as ‘glory’ and ‘honor,’ the root that runs through the previous chapter (2:29–30; 4:18, 21–22). Phinehas's wife had cried that the glory had departed from Israel. The LORD's glory had not been diminished; it now rested with crushing weight on the Philistines. Dagon's hands lay broken on the threshold, but the LORD's hand was heavy on Ashdod.

“and He ravaged them and struck them with tumors,”

The verse names the LORD as the agent. “He ravaged them” describes devastation, the kind of destruction that leaves a place desolate. “Struck them with tumors” describes a painful affliction of swellings. Verse 11 adds that many died. Chapter 6 also mentions rats that ravaged the land (6:4–5), which suggests a widespread plague. Scripture does not give a medical diagnosis. What it stresses is that the LORD Himself struck them.

“both Ashdod and its territory.”

The judgment was not limited to the temple or to the city. It reached the surrounding villages and countryside, the whole region around Ashdod. The Philistines had taken the ark as a trophy; it had brought the LORD's judgment into their land.

Study Notes

The word for “tumors” appears in the written Hebrew text as 'ophalim, a word meaning swellings or mounds, also used among the curses of Deuteronomy 28:27 (“the boils of Egypt, with tumors”).

Jewish scribal tradition directed readers to pronounce a different word, techorim, possibly regarded as a less offensive term.

The KJV's ‘emerods’ (hemorrhoids) reflects one older interpretation. Modern translations generally render “tumors,” leaving the exact nature open.

The Septuagint adds at this point that mice (or rats) sprang up in the land and that there was a great deathly confusion in the city. The Hebrew text followed by the NKJV does not mention the rats until 6:4–5.

Because of the tumors, the deaths, and the rats of 6:4–5, many interpreters have suggested bubonic plague. That is a possibility, not a proven conclusion.

1 Samuel 5:7
1 Samuel 5:7
NKJV

1 Samuel 5:7 And when the men of Ashdod saw how it was, they said, "The ark of the God of Israel must not remain with us, for His hand is harsh toward us and Dagon our god."(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 5:7 When the people realized what was happening, they cried out, “We can’t keep the Ark of the God of Israel here any longer! He is against us! We will all be destroyed along with Dagon, our god.” (NLT)

Main Idea

The men of Ashdod recognize that the hand of the God of Israel is harsh against them and against Dagon their god, and they insist that His ark must not remain with them. Their answer is to send the ark away, even as they go on calling Dagon their god.

Verse Explanation

The men of Ashdod recognize that the hand of the God of Israel is harsh against them and against Dagon their god, and they insist that His ark must not remain with them. Their answer is to send the ark away, even as they go on calling Dagon their god.

“And when the men of Ashdod saw how it was, they said,”

The people of Ashdod drew the obvious conclusion. They had seen their god fall twice and break, and now their city was struck with disease and death. They understood that these events were connected.

“The ark of the God of Israel must not remain with us,”

The trophy had become a terror. They called it “the ark of the God of Israel,” acknowledging whose ark it was. Their solution was to send it away. They did not ask what the God of Israel wanted, and they did not turn from their idol to Him. They wanted to be rid of Him.

“for His hand is harsh toward us and Dagon our god.”

They recognized that the God of Israel was behind both the plague on their city and the fall of Dagon. “His hand is harsh” describes severe, unrelenting pressure. Yet they still called Dagon “our god.” They had seen Dagon fall before the LORD, and they continued to claim the broken idol as their own. Their recognition of the LORD's power produced fear but not faith.

Study Notes

In 6:5 the Philistine priests and diviners still hope that the LORD “will lighten His hand” from them and from “your gods,” addressing their own people's gods.

“Harsh” translates a word meaning hard or severe. It is a different word from “heavy” in 5:6 and 5:11, though the meaning overlaps.

1 Samuel 5:8
1 Samuel 5:8
NKJV

1 Samuel 5:8 Therefore they sent and gathered to themselves all the lords of the Philistines, and said, "What shall we do with the ark of the God of Israel?" And they answered, "Let the ark of the God of Israel be carried away to Gath." So they carried the ark of the God of Israel away.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 5:8 So they called together the rulers of the Philistine towns and asked, “What should we do with the Ark of the God of Israel?” The rulers discussed it and replied, “Move it to the town of Gath.” So they moved the Ark of the God of Israel to Gath. (NLT)

Main Idea

They gather all the lords of the Philistines to ask what to do with the ark of the God of Israel, and the council decides to carry it to Gath. Relocating the ark carries the crisis into another Philistine city.

Verse Explanation

They gather all the lords of the Philistines to ask what to do with the ark of the God of Israel, and the council decides to carry it to Gath. Relocating the ark carries the crisis into another Philistine city.

“Therefore they sent and gathered to themselves all the lords of the Philistines,”

Ashdod could not decide the matter alone. The people summoned “all the lords of the Philistines,” the rulers of the five cities who together governed the Philistine confederation (Joshua 13:3). Their council made decisions affecting all Philistia.

“and said, ‘What shall we do with the ark of the God of Israel?’”

The question shows the depth of their problem. The ark was too dangerous to keep, yet returning it would mean admitting defeat. They still speak of it as “the ark of the God of Israel.”

“And they answered, ‘Let the ark of the God of Israel be carried away to Gath.’”

The lords decided to move the ark to “Gath,” another of the five cities, farther inland. Perhaps they thought the troubles in Ashdod were local, or that they could test whether the ark itself was the cause. Either way, they were trying to manage the God of Israel by moving His ark. Gath would later be the home of Goliath (17:4).

“So they carried the ark of the God of Israel away.”

The decision was carried out. The phrase “the ark of the God of Israel” appears three times in this verse. The Philistines could not stop speaking of whose ark it was.

Study Notes

“Lords” translates seranim, a term used in the Old Testament only for the Philistine rulers. It is not the ordinary Hebrew word for king or lord and may be a loanword from the Philistines' own language.

Some scholars connect it with the Greek word tyrannos, ‘ruler,’ but the derivation is uncertain.

Gath is commonly identified with Tell es-Safi, inland and east of Ashdod near the edge of the lowland hills facing Judah.

1 Samuel 5:9
1 Samuel 5:9
NKJV

1 Samuel 5:9 So it was, after they had carried it away, that the hand of the LORD was against the city with a very great destruction; and He struck the men of the city, both small and great, and tumors broke out on them.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 5:9 But when the Ark arrived at Gath, the LORD’s heavy hand fell on its men, young and old; he struck them with a plague of tumors, and there was a great panic. (NLT)

Main Idea

After the ark is carried to Gath, the hand of the LORD comes against that city with very great destruction, striking its men, small and great, with tumors. Moving the ark does not escape the LORD’s judgment but only extends it.

Verse Explanation

After the ark is carried to Gath, the hand of the LORD comes against that city with very great destruction, striking its men, small and great, with tumors. Moving the ark does not escape the LORD’s judgment but only extends it.

“So it was, after they had carried it away, that the hand of the LORD was against the city with a very great destruction;”

Moving the ark did not escape the LORD's judgment; it carried the judgment to Gath. “The hand of the LORD” appears again. The narrator states plainly that the LORD was “against the city.” “A very great destruction” describes panic and confusion as well as devastation. The Hebrew word suggests a city thrown into turmoil.

“and He struck the men of the city, both small and great,”

The LORD struck the people of Gath as He had struck Ashdod. “Both small and great” means everyone, young and old, common people and leaders alike. No one was exempt.

“and tumors broke out on them.”

The same affliction appeared in Gath. The repetition confirmed that the ark's presence, and the LORD's hand, not local conditions, was the cause.

Study Notes

The verb translated “broke out” is rare, and its exact meaning is uncertain. Some take it to mean that the tumors burst out on the body; others relate it to swelling.

A similar pattern runs through Ashdod, Gath, and Ekron: the ark arrives and the hand of the LORD weighs heavily on the city. The order is not identical.

At Ashdod the stroke comes first and the people then decide to send the ark away (5:6–8). At Gath only the stroke is recorded (5:9). At Ekron the people cry out as soon as the ark arrives (5:10), before the deadly affliction is described (5:11–12).

NLT

1 Samuel 5:10 So they sent the Ark of God to the town of Ekron, but when the people of Ekron saw it coming they cried out, “They are bringing the Ark of the God of Israel here to kill us, too!” (NLT)

Main Idea

The ark of God is sent on to Ekron, and as it arrives the Ekronites cry out that it has been brought to kill them and their people. Their immediate alarm suggests they already knew what the ark had brought on Ashdod and Gath.

Verse Explanation

The ark of God is sent on to Ekron, and as it arrives the Ekronites cry out that it has been brought to kill them and their people. Their immediate alarm suggests they already knew what the ark had brought on Ashdod and Gath.

“Therefore they sent the ark of God to Ekron.”

Gath also sent the ark away. “Ekron” was the northernmost of the five Philistine cities, closest to Israelite territory. The ark was moving, city by city, back toward the border.

“So it was, as the ark of God came to Ekron, that the Ekronites cried out, saying,”

The people of Ekron did not wait for the plague to begin. As soon as the ark arrived, they cried out in alarm. They knew what had happened in Ashdod and Gath.

“They have brought the ark of the God of Israel to us, to kill us and our people!”

The Ekronites accused their own leaders of sending death to them. “To kill us and our people” shows that they now understood the ark's presence as deadly. The trophy that the Philistines had carried home in triumph from Ebenezer had become a terror that no Philistine city wanted.

Study Notes

Ekron is identified with Tel Miqne, near the border with Judah. The ark later went from there to Beth Shemesh in Israelite territory (6:12–16).

NLT

1 Samuel 5:11 The people summoned the Philistine rulers again and begged them, “Please send the Ark of the God of Israel back to its own country, or it will kill us all.” For the deadly plague from God had already begun, and great fear was sweeping across the town. (NLT)

Main Idea

The lords of the Philistines are gathered again, and the people plead that the ark of the God of Israel be sent back to its own place so that it does not kill them, for deadly destruction fills the city and the hand of God is very heavy there. The repeated judgment forces the Philistines to give up the trophy they had taken.

Verse Explanation

The lords of the Philistines are gathered again, and the people plead that the ark of the God of Israel be sent back to its own place so that it does not kill them, for deadly destruction fills the city and the hand of God is very heavy there. The repeated judgment forces the Philistines to give up the trophy they had taken.

“So they sent and gathered together all the lords of the Philistines, and said,”

The council of lords met a second time. The people of Ekron now set the agenda.

“Send away the ark of the God of Israel, and let it go back to its own place,”

The demand was clear: return the ark to Israel. “Its own place” meant Israel's land and sanctuary. The Philistines had tried keeping it in Dagon's temple and moving it from city to city. Now they recognized that the ark of the God of Israel belonged with Israel. The trophy they had taken in triumph must be given up.

“so that it does not kill us and our people.”

The motive was survival. The Philistines wanted to be free of the LORD's judgment, not to know Him.

“For there was a deadly destruction throughout all the city;”

The narrator explains their desperation. “Deadly destruction” combines panic with death. People were dying throughout Ekron.

“the hand of God was very heavy there.”

The section ends where it began, with God's heavy hand (5:6). “Very heavy” intensifies the description. Once again the narrator uses the root associated with glory. The glory had departed from Israel (4:21–22), but God's glory had not departed from the world. It weighed upon those who had claimed victory over Him.

Study Notes

“Deadly destruction” is literally ‘a confusion (or panic) of death.’ The same word for confusion appears in 5:9 (“a very great destruction”).

The second council (5:11) parallels the first (5:8): the first moved the ark, and the second demanded its return.

1 Samuel 5:12 And the men who did not die were stricken with the tumors, and the cry of the city went up to heaven.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 5:12 Those who didn’t die were afflicted with tumors; and the cry from the town rose to heaven. (NLT)

Main Idea

The men who do not die are stricken with tumors, and the cry of the city goes up to heaven. The chapter ends with the Philistines overwhelmed under the LORD’s hand.

Verse Explanation

The men who do not die are stricken with tumors, and the cry of the city goes up to heaven. The chapter ends with the Philistines overwhelmed under the LORD’s hand.

“And the men who did not die were stricken with the tumors,”

Those who survived the plague still suffered. Death and disease spread throughout the city. No one in Ekron escaped the LORD's hand.

“and the cry of the city went up to heaven.”

The chapter ends with a cry rising from Ekron. “Went up to heaven” shows how great and desperate the outcry was. The language recalls Israel in Egypt, when “their cry came up to God because of the bondage” (Exodus 2:23). The irony is sharp: the Philistines, who had celebrated the capture of Israel's God, now raised a cry of anguish heavenward. Dagon could not help them. The chapter that began with the ark as a captive trophy ends with the captors overwhelmed and pleading to be rid of it. Chapter 6 will tell how they sent it home.

Study Notes

Chapter 4 ends with a cry in Shiloh (4:13–14) and a mother's lament over departed glory; chapter 5 ends with a cry rising from Ekron.
