Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 5
The Core:

With no Israelite army in the field, the LORD humbles Dagon in his own temple and lays His heavy hand on the Philistine cities until they plead to send the ark of the God of Israel away.

The Context:

The Philistines have just defeated Israel and captured the ark (4:10–11). They carry it to Ashdod, one of their five chief cities, and set it in the house of their god Dagon as a trophy of victory.

The Structure:

  • 1 Samuel 5:1–5 — Dagon Falls Before the Ark

  • 1 Samuel 5:6–12 — The Hand of the LORD Heavy on the Philistine Cities

The Key Verse:

“But the hand of the LORD was heavy on the people of Ashdod, and He ravaged them and struck them with tumors, both Ashdod and its territory.” (1 Samuel 5:6, NKJV)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Chapter 4 ended with the cry that the glory had departed from Israel. Chapter 5 shows that the LORD’s glory had not been defeated. The Philistines set the ark beside Dagon, as though Israel’s God now stood as a captive in their god’s house. The next morning Dagon is found “fallen on its face to the earth before the ark of the LORD” (5:3), and his worshipers have to lift him back into place. The following morning his head and both palms lie broken off on the threshold (5:4). A god who must be propped up by his worshipers cannot stand in the presence of the living God.

Then the scene widens from the idol to the people. “The hand of the LORD was heavy on the people of Ashdod” (5:6), and as the ark moves from Ashdod to Gath and on to Ekron, tumors, panic and death follow it. No priest carries it and no Israelite soldier fights for it. The LORD acts alone. Israel’s sin had brought defeat, but the LORD still defends His own name, and the Philistines learn that His hand is heavier than any power they trust. The chapter closes as “the cry of the city went up to heaven” (5:12).

Chapter Sections

1 Samuel 5:1–5 — Dagon Falls Before the Ark

The Philistines carry the ark to Ashdod and set it beside Dagon, but on two mornings the idol lies fallen on its face before the ark of the LORD, the second time with its head and hands broken off. The idol’s severed hands stand in contrast to the powerful hand of the LORD that follows, and the threshold of Dagon’s house becomes a lasting reminder of that morning.

1 Samuel 5:6–12 — The Hand of the LORD Heavy on the Philistine Cities

The LORD strikes the people of Ashdod with tumors, and each city that receives the ark, first Gath and then Ekron, suffers under the same heavy hand. The Philistine lords finally plead for the ark of the God of Israel to be sent back to its own place, while the cry of the stricken city rises to heaven.

Why This Chapter Matters

This chapter shows that God’s honor does not depend on the strength of His people. Israel had failed and the ark sat in enemy hands, yet the LORD needed no army to defend His name. That truth humbles us, because God cannot be used, and it comforts us, because our failures do not overthrow Him.

The chapter also exposes idolatry for what it is. Whatever people set beside the LORD will finally fall before Him, whether it is an image of stone or anything else we trust in His place. Isaiah later mocks idols that must be carried by those who worship them (Isaiah 46:1–7), and Dagon lying broken on his own threshold gives that truth a vivid picture.

Key Themes to Watch For

The LORD acts alone. God works in Philistine territory without priest, army or Israelite help (5:1–12).

The helpless idol. Dagon falls, is set back in place, and falls again broken (5:3–4).

The heavy hand of the LORD. The image of God’s hand recurs as His judgment spreads from city to city (5:6–7, 9, 11).

Fear without repentance. The Philistines recognize God’s hand against them but seek only to be rid of the ark (5:7–8, 10–11).

Key Words & Concepts

Dagon. The chief god worshiped at Ashdod. The popular picture of Dagon as a fish-shaped god and the proposed meanings of his name are uncertain; the chapter’s point is that he cannot stand before the LORD (5:2–4).

Ashdod, Gath and Ekron. Three of the five main Philistine cities, each struck in turn as the ark is moved (5:1, 8, 10).

The threshold. The doorway of Dagon’s house where the idol’s head and hands lay. The priests’ custom of not stepping on it kept the memory of his fall alive (5:4–5).

Tumors. Swellings that broke out on the people of each city (5:6, 9, 12). Together with the golden rats of 6:4–5, they suggest some kind of plague, but the exact disease cannot be identified with certainty. The text’s emphasis is that the LORD struck them.

Lords of the Philistines. The rulers of the Philistine cities, who meet twice to decide what to do with the ark (5:8, 11).
