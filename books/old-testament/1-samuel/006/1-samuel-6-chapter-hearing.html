1 Samuel 6:1
NKJV

1 Samuel 6:1 Now the ark of the LORD was in the country of the Philistines seven months.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 6:1 “Now the ark of the LORD was in the country of the Philistines seven months.” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

The ark of the LORD remains in Philistine territory for seven months. The long stay shows how much the Philistines suffered under the LORD’s hand before they were ready to give up their prize.

Observe: Something to notice


Seven months is a long time to keep something that is bringing God’s hand down on you. Ashdod had said, “The ark of the God of Israel must not remain with us” (5:7), and later the lords were told, “Send away the ark of the God of Israel” (5:11). Yet the whole episode stretched to seven months before the Philistines asked how to return it. The text does not tell us how long they waited after any one meeting, so we should not guess. But the length of the stay shows a people who knew what was crushing them and were slow to let it go.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


I can know that something in my life is under God’s hand and still hold on to it. I may tell myself I will deal with it, and months go by. The delay does not make it lighter. When the Lord shows me what I need to release, I want to yield early rather than carry for months a weight He never meant me to carry.

1 Samuel 6:2
NKJV

1 Samuel 6:2 And the Philistines called for the priests and the diviners, saying, "What shall we do with the ark of the LORD? Tell us how we should send it to its place."(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 6:2 “And the Philistines called for the priests and the diviners, saying, ‘What shall we do with the ark of the LORD? Tell us how we should send it to its place.’” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

The Philistines call their priests and diviners and ask what to do with the ark of the LORD and how to send it back to its place. The nation that captured the ark now wants only to be rid of it, and its own religious experts must find the way.

Observe: Something to notice


The Philistines called for “the priests and the diviners.” Diviners tried to read hidden things through omens and signs. Moses had told Israel, “these nations which you will dispossess listened to soothsayers and diviners; but as for you, the LORD your God has not appointed such for you” (Deuteronomy 18:14). In the next breath he promised, “The LORD your God will raise up for you a Prophet like me from your midst, from your brethren. Him you shall hear” (Deuteronomy 18:15). People who do not know the Lord are left to guess at Him. They read signs, consult experts and hope they get it right. The Philistines knew Israel’s God was dealing with them, but they had no word from Him, so they turned to divination.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


I do not have to guess at God. He has spoken, and He “has in these last days spoken to us by His Son” (Hebrews 1:2). When I am under pressure, it is tempting to look for signs, follow hunches or collect opinions, the way the Philistines called in their experts. Isaiah asked a people who were consulting mediums, “should not a people seek their God?” (Isaiah 8:19). Before I go looking for answers anywhere else, I want to open His word and ask Him.

NKJV

1 Samuel 6:3 So they said, "If you send away the ark of the God of Israel, do not send it empty; but by all means return it to Him with a trespass offering. Then you will be healed, and it will be known to you why His hand is not removed from you."(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 6:3 “So they said, ‘If you send away the ark of the God of Israel, do not send it empty; but by all means return it to Him with a trespass offering. Then you will be healed, and it will be known to you why His hand is not removed from you.’” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

The priests and diviners advise that the ark of the God of Israel must not be sent back empty but returned to Him with a trespass offering. They acknowledge that their suffering is tied to guilt before Israel’s God, and they promise healing and an explanation of why His hand has not lifted.

Observe: Something to notice


These pagan priests did not call the plague bad luck. They knew the Philistines had wronged Israel’s God, and they knew something had to be given: “do not send it empty.” Guilty people feel that they must give or do something to make things right. Moving the ark from city to city had not solved anything, because guilt does not go away when we move it somewhere else. Their instinct was right, but their remedy was their own, and the chapter never reports that their promise of healing came true.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


I know that instinct. When I have wronged God, I want to do something to make up for it: try harder, give more, stay busy for Him. But my guilt is not settled by what I bring. It is settled by the offering God has provided. Isaiah says of the Servant of the LORD, “by His stripes we are healed” (Isaiah 53:5). So I can stop trying to pay, confess my sin honestly, and rest in what Christ has done.

1 Samuel 6:4
NKJV

1 Samuel 6:4 Then they said, "What is the trespass offering which we shall return to Him?" They answered, "Five golden tumors and five golden rats, according to the number of the lords of the Philistines. For the same plague was on all of you and on your lords.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 6:4 “Then they said, ‘What is the trespass offering which we shall return to Him?’ They answered, ‘Five golden tumors and five golden rats, according to the number of the lords of the Philistines. For the same plague was on all of you and on your lords.” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

When asked what the trespass offering should be, they prescribe five golden tumors and five golden rats, one for each Philistine lord, since the same plague struck the people and their rulers alike. The gifts are the Philistine priests’ own remedy, not a pattern God gave to Israel.

Observe: Something to notice


The offering was a confession in gold. Golden images of the very tumors and rats that had struck them said, in effect, “This is what Your hand did to us.” The priests also built a humbling fact into the gift: one of each “according to the number of the lords of the Philistines. For the same plague was on all of you and on your lords.” The rulers who had decided where the ark would go were under the same hand as everyone else. Their rank did not exempt them, and the offering itself put that on display. Peter learned the same truth centuries later: “In truth I perceive that God shows no partiality” (Acts 10:34).

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


My position does not exempt me when God exposes something in my life. A parent, a pastor, a supervisor or a long-time church member can be tempted to think that responsibility puts him above correction. The lords of Philistia were counted with their people. When the Lord brings something to light in me, I need to face it as honestly as I would expect anyone under my care to face it, not hide behind my title.

1 Samuel 6:5
NKJV

1 Samuel 6:5 Therefore you shall make images of your tumors and images of your rats that ravage the land, and you shall give glory to the God of Israel; perhaps He will lighten His hand from you, from your gods, and from your land.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 6:5 “Therefore you shall make images of your tumors and images of your rats that ravage the land, and you shall give glory to the God of Israel; perhaps He will lighten His hand from you, from your gods, and from your land.” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

They tell the people to make images of their tumors and of the rats ravaging the land and so give glory to the God of Israel, hoping He will lighten His hand from them, their gods, and their land. Even pagan priests must admit that the God of Israel is the one pressing down on them, though they can only say “perhaps” He will relent.

Observation 1

Observe: Something to notice


In Hebrew the word for “glory” comes from a root that means “heavy” or “weighty.” For months the LORD’s hand had been “heavy” on Philistia (5:6, 11). Now the Philistines must give glory, give weight, to the God of Israel so that He will “lighten” His hand. In chapter 4 a dying mother cried, “The glory has departed from Israel” (4:22); here the people who captured the ark are told to give that glory back to Israel’s God. To give God glory is to tell the truth about Him and about ourselves. Joshua said to Achan, “give glory to the LORD God of Israel, and make confession to Him” (Joshua 7:19). The priests even admitted that God’s hand had been on “your gods,” remembering Dagon fallen before the ark.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


Giving God glory is more than singing words of praise. It means telling the truth: that He is the Lord and that I have needed Him to deal with me. I want to give Him that weight freely and gladly, not only when His hand is heavy on me.

Observation 2

Observe: Something to notice


Notice the little word “perhaps.” The priests had no promise of mercy to offer, only a hope. Their religion could not tell them whether God would forgive. God’s people stand on something firmer: “If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness” (1 John 1:9).

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


When I come to God confessing my sin, I am not standing on a “perhaps.” I am standing on His promise. I do not have to wonder whether He will receive me when I come honestly to Him through Christ. He has said He will.

1 Samuel 6:6
NKJV

1 Samuel 6:6 Why then do you harden your hearts as the Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened their hearts? When He did mighty things among them, did they not let the people go, that they might depart?(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 6:6 “Why then do you harden your hearts as the Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened their hearts? When He did mighty things among them, did they not let the people go, that they might depart?” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

The priests warn the Philistines not to harden their hearts as Pharaoh and the Egyptians did, recalling that Egypt finally let Israel go after the LORD did mighty things among them. The memory of the exodus becomes a warning against resisting the God whose hand they now feel.

Observe: Something to notice


“Why then do you harden your hearts as the Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened their hearts?” The priests asked a simple question, and it showed how pointless resistance is. Pharaoh “hardened his heart” (Exodus 8:15) again and again, and in the end he let Israel go anyway. All his hardness did was add one plague to another. The words “harden your hearts” are literally “make your hearts heavy,” the same Hebrew root as “glory” in verse 5. A person can give God His weight, or make his own heart heavy against Him, and the second way never wins.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


Why do I harden my heart? When God is dealing with me, resisting Him does not change where He is taking me. It only makes the road longer and harder. Hebrews says to God’s own people, “Do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion” (Hebrews 3:8). If He is pressing on something in me now, the wise thing, and the kind thing for my own soul, is to give way early.

1 Samuel 6:7
NKJV

1 Samuel 6:7 Now therefore, make a new cart, take two milk cows which have never been yoked, and hitch the cows to the cart; and take their calves home, away from them.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 6:7 “Now therefore, make a new cart, take two milk cows which have never been yoked, and hitch the cows to the cart; and take their calves home, away from them.” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

The priests direct them to make a new cart, hitch two milk cows that have never been yoked, and take the cows’ calves home away from them. The arrangement is set against nature, since untrained cows would not ordinarily pull a cart or leave their young.

Observe: Something to notice


The priests built the test so that only God could pass it. Cows “which have never been yoked” would not know how to pull a cart, and nursing cows with their calves taken “home, away from them” would want to turn back. If those cows carried the ark away from home toward Israel, nothing else would explain it. The arrangement was the Philistines’ idea, not God’s instruction. But God chose to answer their test in a way no one could explain away.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


Sometimes God answers in a way that leaves no other explanation. I do not need to set up tests for Him, but I can recognize when He has done what only He could do. When that happens, I want to give Him the credit plainly instead of looking for some ordinary reason to explain it away.

1 Samuel 6:8
NKJV

1 Samuel 6:8 Then take the ark of the LORD and set it on the cart; and put the articles of gold which you are returning to Him as a trespass offering in a chest by its side. Then send it away, and let it go.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 6:8 “Then take the ark of the LORD and set it on the cart; and put the articles of gold which you are returning to Him as a trespass offering in a chest by its side. Then send it away, and let it go.” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

The ark of the LORD is to be set on the cart, with the gold articles of the trespass offering in a chest beside it, and then the cart is to be sent off and left to go. No one will drive or guide it.

Observe: Something to notice


“Send it away, and let it go.” The Philistines did everything the plan required: they built the cart, hitched the cows, loaded the ark and the chest. Then they sent the cart away and let it go. The instructions named no driver. Whatever happened next would not be steered by them. In chapter 4 Israel had tried to direct God’s presence, carrying the ark into battle so that “it may save us” (4:3). Here the direction of the ark is left entirely to God.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


Some things I can do, and some things I cannot steer. I am responsible to do what God has plainly given me to do: work, pray, speak, prepare. But there comes a point where I have to take my hands off the reins and leave the outcome with Him. Peter tells me to cast “all your care upon Him, for He cares for you” (1 Peter 5:7). Entrusting a matter to God does not mean ignoring my duties. It means doing them and then letting Him decide where things go.

1 Samuel 6:9
NKJV

1 Samuel 6:9 And watch: if it goes up the road to its own territory, to Beth Shemesh, then He has done us this great evil. But if not, then we shall know that it is not His hand that struck us"”it happened to us by chance."(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 6:9 “And watch: if it goes up the road to its own territory, to Beth Shemesh, then He has done us this great evil. But if not, then we shall know that it is not His hand that struck us—it happened to us by chance.’” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

The Philistines are to watch the cart’s direction: if it goes up toward Beth Shemesh in Israel’s territory, then the God of Israel brought this great evil on them, but if not, the plague was mere chance. The test is meant to settle whether the LORD’s hand truly struck them.

Observation 1

Observe: Something to notice


After seven months of plague and a god fallen on his face, the Philistines still left a door open for “chance.” Unbelief always looks for another explanation. But Scripture says, “The lot is cast into the lap, But its every decision is from the LORD” (Proverbs 16:33). The Hebrew word for “chance” here is the same word behind Ruth 2:3, where Ruth “happened to come” to the field of Boaz. In Ruth’s story, what looked like chance was God’s quiet hand preparing redemption.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


When I look back over my life, I can see His hand in many things I once thought simply happened: a meeting, a delay, a door that opened or closed. What looked like chance was the Lord at work. That makes me more willing to trust Him with what I cannot understand yet.

Observation 2

Observe: Something to notice


The priests called Israel’s land the ark’s “own territory.” In verse 2 they had spoken of sending it “to its place.” Even the enemy knew that God’s ark did not belong in Dagon’s land. It had been carried into enemy territory and set beside an idol in Dagon’s house (5:2), and it could not stay there.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


Have I carried God’s presence into enemy territory? Paul writes to believers, “what agreement has the temple of God with idols? For you are the temple of the living God” (2 Corinthians 6:16). I bring the Lord with me wherever I go. So I need to ask whether I have taken Him into places, habits or relationships where He is set beside idols. The Philistines knew the ark did not belong there. I should know that too.

1 Samuel 6:10

NLT

1 Samuel 6:10 “Then the men did so; they took two milk cows and hitched them to the cart, and shut up their calves at home.” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

The men follow the priests’ instructions, hitching two milk cows to the cart and shutting their calves up at home. The conditions of the test are now fully in place.

Observe: Something to notice


“Then the men did so.” The Philistines carried out every step their priests had given them: two milk cows, hitched to the cart, their calves shut up at home. They did exactly what they were told, though the advice came from priests and diviners, not from God. Israel, by contrast, had God’s word through Samuel in chapter 4, and the account gives no sign that anyone sought it before going out to battle (4:1).

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


These men were careful to do what their advisers told them. Am I as careful to do what God tells me? It is possible to follow human counsel to the letter and treat God’s word as a suggestion. I want to take His instructions at least as seriously as these men took their priests’.

1 Samuel 6:11
NKJV

1 Samuel 6:11 And they set the ark of the LORD on the cart, and the chest with the gold rats and the images of their tumors.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 6:11 “And they set the ark of the LORD on the cart, and the chest with the gold rats and the images of their tumors.” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

They set the ark of the LORD on the cart along with the chest holding the gold rats and the images of their tumors. The ark leaves Philistia carrying the Philistines’ own confession of the plague the LORD sent.

Observe: Something to notice


The ark was the throne of “the LORD of hosts, who dwells between the cherubim” (4:4). Now it rides out of Philistia on a farm cart pulled by two cows, with a chest of gold beside it. The cart was the Philistines’ arrangement, not the way God had told Israel to carry the ark (Numbers 7:9), and the point here is not to approve it. The point is that God did not refuse to go home this way. The King whose throne the cherubim guard let His ark be carried on a farm cart. Centuries later Zechariah announced another King, “lowly and riding on a donkey” (Zechariah 9:9), and Jesus rode into Jerusalem that way (Matthew 21:5).

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


God is not too proud to come to me in humble ways. He does not wait for a grand setting before He shows up. Paul says Christ “made Himself of no reputation, taking the form of a bondservant” (Philippians 2:7). If the Lord of glory was willing to come humbly, I have no reason to insist on being treated as important. I want to learn humility from the One who rode a donkey into Jerusalem.

1 Samuel 6:12
NKJV

1 Samuel 6:12 Then the cows headed straight for the road to Beth Shemesh, and went along the highway, lowing as they went, and did not turn aside to the right hand or the left. And the lords of the Philistines went after them to the border of Beth Shemesh.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 6:12 “Then the cows headed straight for the road to Beth Shemesh, and went along the highway, lowing as they went, and did not turn aside to the right hand or the left. And the lords of the Philistines went after them to the border of Beth Shemesh.” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

The cows head straight for the road to Beth Shemesh, lowing as they go and never turning to the right or left, while the Philistine lords follow them to the border. Against their instinct to return to their calves, the cows carry the ark home, and the LORD’s hand is unmistakably shown.

Observation 1

Observe: Something to notice


No Israelite army went to retrieve the ark. No priest went to fetch it. No one drove the cart. Two untrained, nursing cows went “straight for the road to Beth Shemesh,” away from their calves. The LORD brought the sign of His presence back to His people by His own hand. This is the heart of the chapter. It is also a picture of grace: God does not wait for His people to find their way back to Him. He comes to them. Jesus said, “the Son of Man has come to seek and to save that which was lost” (Luke 19:10).

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


What a wonder that the Lord comes near by His own hand. He did not wait for His people to go and get Him, and He did not wait for me either. Before I knew to look for Him, He came looking for me. My walk with Him began with His initiative, not mine.

Observation 2

Observe: Something to notice


Notice how the cows went: “lowing as they went, and did not turn aside to the right hand or the left.” They lowed as they went away from their calves, which had been shut up at home (v. 10), and yet they kept to the road. If, as many readers take it, the lowing was for the calves they had left behind, they felt that pull and did not give in to it. Christians have long seen in these cows a picture of costly, single-minded devotion, love for God above even the dearest ties (Matthew 10:37). Their road also brings to mind the road Jesus walked. He “steadfastly set His face to go to Jerusalem” (Luke 9:51), and in the garden He prayed, “nevertheless, not as I will, but as You will” (Matthew 26:39). The cows were moved by God’s hand, not by faith, so the comparison is with the shape of the journey. But faithfulness is not the absence of feeling.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


There are times when obeying the Lord costs me something dear, and I feel it. Feeling the pull of what I am leaving does not mean I am failing. I can feel it, even with tears, and still keep walking His way without turning aside, as Jesus did on His road to the cross.

Observation 3

Observe: Something to notice


“Did not turn aside to the right hand or the left.” That is the language Moses used for faithful obedience: “you shall not turn aside to the right hand or to the left” (Deuteronomy 5:32). Two animals with no knowledge of God’s law walked the straight road that God’s own people had so often left. In chapters 2–4 Israel’s priests and people had turned aside. Here, under God’s hand, two cows did not.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


A straight path is possible when God’s hand is guiding. I am easily pulled off course by distractions, small compromises and what everyone else is doing. But the same Lord who kept two cows on the road can keep me on His path. I want to ask Him to direct my steps so that I do not turn aside.

1 Samuel 6:13

NLT

1 Samuel 6:13 “Now the people of Beth Shemesh were reaping their wheat harvest in the valley; and they lifted their eyes and saw the ark, and rejoiced to see it.” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

The people of Beth Shemesh are reaping their wheat harvest in the valley when they look up, see the ark, and rejoice. Their joy at its return is right and is not the offense that comes later.

Observation 1

Observe: Something to notice


The people of Beth Shemesh were not at worship or at war. They were reaping wheat on an ordinary workday. No one had gone to get the ark. They simply “lifted their eyes and saw the ark, and rejoiced to see it.” After the cry “The glory has departed from Israel” (4:22), this is a moment of pure gladness, and the narrator records it without a word of criticism.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


The Lord often meets His people in the middle of ordinary days. I do not have to save my gladness in Him for special occasions. Between one task and the next, I want to lift my eyes, notice His goodness and rejoice in Him.

Observation 2

Observe: Something to notice


The ark was the place where God had promised to meet His people: “there I will meet with you, and I will speak with you from above the mercy seat” (Exodus 25:22). Christians have long seen in its homecoming a picture of God coming to dwell among His people. The ark is not Christ, but it points to the same desire in God’s heart that was fulfilled when “the Word became flesh and dwelt among us” (John 1:14). His name is “Immanuel,” which is translated, “God with us” (Matthew 1:23).

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


God wants to be with His people. That is not a small thing to me. The God who is holy and needs nothing has chosen to come near, and in Jesus He has done it in the fullest way. I can receive that with wonder, not as a doctrine to file away but as the reason I am never alone.

1 Samuel 6:14
NKJV

1 Samuel 6:14 Then the cart came into the field of Joshua of Beth Shemesh, and stood there; a large stone was there. So they split the wood of the cart and offered the cows as a burnt offering to the LORD.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 6:14 “Then the cart came into the field of Joshua of Beth Shemesh, and stood there; a large stone was there. So they split the wood of the cart and offered the cows as a burnt offering to the LORD.” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

The cart enters the field of Joshua of Beth Shemesh and stops beside a large stone, and the people split the cart’s wood and offer the cows as a burnt offering to the LORD. The cart and cows that brought the ark home become an offering of thanks to Him.

Observation 1

Observe: Something to notice


The cart “came into the field of Joshua of Beth Shemesh, and stood there,” beside a large stone. The narrator does not tell us how it came to a stop, only where. The cows had set out with no driver named in the plan, and the journey ended in an Israelite field. The test the Philistines had set was answered from the first step out of Philistia to the last step in Joshua’s field. The ark came home the way God brought it.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


God finishes what He begins. Paul was “confident of this very thing, that He who has begun a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ” (Philippians 1:6). When I cannot see how the Lord will bring something to its end, I can remember the cart that stopped exactly where He meant it to stop.

Observation 2

Observe: Something to notice


The people gave God everything that had carried the ark. They split the cart for firewood and offered the cows as a burnt offering, the offering that was burned whole on the altar (Leviticus 1:9). Nothing that had carried the ark went back to ordinary use. Read as a picture, the cows that went straight up the road, lowing, ended on the altar. Devotion that follows God’s way becomes a whole offering of oneself, what Paul calls “a living sacrifice” (Romans 12:1). The narrator reports the offering without pronouncing on every detail of it, so the point here is the people’s whole-hearted thanks, not a verdict that everything was done exactly as the law required.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


When I see how near the Lord has come to me, I want to give Him all of myself, not a part held back for ordinary use. This is not a demand pressed on me. It is what love does when it sees how much it has been loved.

When the Lord shows me His goodness, I want my gratitude to turn quickly into worship, the same day, not someday. Thankfulness that never becomes worship fades. Thankfulness offered back to Him grows.

NLT

1 Samuel 6:16 “So when the five lords of the Philistines had seen it, they returned to Ekron the same day.” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

After the five lords of the Philistines see the ark received in Israel, they return to Ekron the same day. They have witnessed the outcome of their own test.

Observe: Something to notice


The five lords saw everything: the cows going straight up the road, the joy of the reapers, the offerings. They had their answer. It was the LORD’s hand. Then they went home to Ekron, and the text records no turning to Him. On the same day, in the same field, Israel worshiped and the Philistine lords walked away. Seeing God’s work, and even admitting it, is not the same as turning to Him.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


I do not want to be only a spectator of what God does. I can watch Him answer prayer, hear testimonies and even admit that He is real, and still go home unchanged. When He shows me His hand, I want to turn toward Him.

1 Samuel 6:17 “These are the golden tumors which the Philistines returned as a trespass offering to the LORD: one for Ashdod, one for Gaza, one for Ashkelon, one for Gath, one for Ekron;” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

The narrator lists the golden tumors returned as a trespass offering to the LORD, one each for Ashdod, Gaza, Ashkelon, Gath, and Ekron. Every major Philistine city that suffered under His hand is accounted for in the offering.

Observe: Something to notice


All five cities are named, one by one, even Gaza and Ashkelon, which chapter 5 never says received the ark. The list reads like a record of tribute paid by a defeated people, written down so that the LORD’s victory would be remembered. Each city is named for its own share in the offering. The list does not tell us that anyone in those cities turned to the LORD. It records that each city had its part in acknowledging Him.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


A group response can hide an individual heart. I can sing with the congregation, stand with my family or agree with my church, and never make the response my own. Each city was named. “So then each of us shall give account of himself to God” (Romans 14:12). I want my answer to the Lord to be mine, not only the one I am carried along in.

1 Samuel 6:18
NKJV

1 Samuel 6:18 and the golden rats, according to the number of all the cities of the Philistines belonging to the five lords, both fortified cities and country villages, even as far as the large stone of Abel on which they set the ark of the LORD, which stone remains to this day in the field of Joshua of Beth Shemesh.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 6:18 “and the golden rats, according to the number of all the cities of the Philistines belonging to the five lords, both fortified cities and country villages, even as far as the large stone of Abel on which they set the ark of the LORD, which stone remains to this day in the field of Joshua of Beth Shemesh.” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

The golden rats match the number of all the Philistine cities under the five lords, both fortified cities and country villages, and the large stone where the ark was set still remains in Joshua’s field to the writer’s day. The plague reached the whole land, and the stone stands as a lasting witness to the ark’s return.

Observe: Something to notice


The stone where the ark was set “remains to this day.” Scripture often marks God’s works with stones: Jacob’s pillar at Bethel (Genesis 28:18), the twelve stones from the Jordan set up “for a memorial” (Joshua 4:7), and soon Samuel’s stone called Ebenezer, “Thus far the LORD has helped us” (7:12). This stone marked the place where God brought the ark home, and it stood near the place where His holiness would be solemnly shown in 6:19. It called people to remember both His mercy and His holiness.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


I forget quickly. I want to keep reminders of what the Lord has done, both His kindness in coming near and His holiness that calls for reverence. Remembering keeps my heart grateful and humble at the same time.

1 Samuel 6:19
NKJV

1 Samuel 6:19 Then He struck the men of Beth Shemesh, because they had looked into the ark of the LORD. He struck fifty thousand and seventy men of the people, and the people lamented because the LORD had struck the people with a great slaughter.(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 6:19 “Then He struck the men of Beth Shemesh, because they had looked into the ark of the LORD. He struck fifty thousand and seventy men of the people, and the people lamented because the LORD had struck the people with a great slaughter.” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

The LORD strikes the men of Beth Shemesh because they looked into the ark of the LORD, and the NKJV reports fifty thousand and seventy men struck, while some translations read seventy. The people mourn the great slaughter, learning that the LORD’s holiness governs Israel just as it governed the Philistines.

Observation 1

Observe: Something to notice


The holiness that humbled Dagon and struck the Philistine cities was the same holiness among God’s own people. The Philistines suffered for treating the ark as a trophy. The men of Beth Shemesh suffered for treating it as a curiosity. God had said long before, “By those who come near Me I must be regarded as holy” (Leviticus 10:3). He was not being random or cruel. The reason is stated plainly, and His law had warned them.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


Belonging to God’s people never makes His presence something to handle casually. He is still God. I can grow so familiar with holy things that I stop treating them as holy. Paul warned believers about coming to the Lord’s Table carelessly: “he who eats and drinks in an unworthy manner eats and drinks judgment to himself, not discerning the Lord’s body” (1 Corinthians 11:29). I want to come to the Lord as one who knows Him and still fears Him.

Observation 2

Observe: Something to notice


The same day began with harvest joy (v. 13) and worship (vv. 14–15) and ended with “the people lamented.” Their joy was not the offense; their looking was. Scripture holds the two together: “Serve the LORD with fear, And rejoice with trembling” (Psalm 2:11). Real joy in God and real reverence for Him belong together.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


I do not have to choose between gladness and reverence. My worship can be full of joy and still careful, warm and still humble. If my joy ever makes me careless with God, it has become something less than joy in Him.

1 Samuel 6:20
NKJV

1 Samuel 6:20 And the men of Beth Shemesh said, "Who is able to stand before this holy LORD God? And to whom shall it go up from us?"(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 6:20 “And the men of Beth Shemesh said, ‘Who is able to stand before this holy LORD God? And to whom shall it go up from us?’” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

The men of Beth Shemesh ask, “Who is able to stand before this holy LORD God?” and wonder where the ark should go from them. Their question rightly recognizes His holiness, but their response is to send His presence away rather than seek how to receive it rightly.

Observation 1

Observe: Something to notice


The men of Beth Shemesh asked exactly the right question. Hannah had sung, “No one is holy like the LORD” (2:2), and now a town feels it. Scripture keeps asking this question: “who may stand in His holy place?” (Psalm 24:3); “O Lord, who could stand?” (Psalm 130:3); “who is able to stand?” (Revelation 6:17). And Scripture answers it. No one can stand in his own right, but through Christ “we have access by faith into this grace in which we stand” (Romans 5:2). John saw a great multitude before the throne in robes made “white in the blood of the Lamb” (Revelation 7:14).

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


Who can stand before this holy God? In myself, I cannot. But in Christ I stand, held in His grace and clothed in what the Lamb has done for me. That is the deepest security I know.

Observation 2

Observe: Something to notice


Their second question tells a sadder story: “to whom shall it go up from us?” Like the Philistines before them, they wanted the ark gone, even though Levites were among them (v. 15). They did not ask how to receive it rightly. Feeling God’s holiness can lead us to come to Him His way, or it can lead us to keep Him at a distance. David later asked almost the same thing, “How can the ark of the LORD come to me?” (2 Samuel 6:9), until he learned to bring it God’s way (1 Chronicles 15:13–15).

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


When I feel the holiness of God, I do not want to back away from Him. The gospel invites me to “draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith” (Hebrews 10:22). I want to come closer in the way He has made, not keep Him at arm’s length.

1 Samuel 6:21
NKJV

1 Samuel 6:21 So they sent messengers to the inhabitants of Kirjath Jearim, saying, "The Philistines have brought back the ark of the LORD; come down and take it up with you."(NKJV)

NLT

1 Samuel 6:21 “So they sent messengers to the inhabitants of Kirjath Jearim, saying, ‘The Philistines have brought back the ark of the LORD; come down and take it up with you.’” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says

They send messengers to the people of Kirjath Jearim, announcing that the Philistines have returned the ark of the LORD and asking them to come down and take it up. The ark that was welcomed with joy at Beth Shemesh is now passed on to another town.

Observe: Something to notice


The chapter ends unfinished. The ark is passed along to Kirjath Jearim, where it will be received with care (7:1) and will stay until David, the LORD’s anointed king, comes to bring it up (2 Samuel 6:2–3). The twenty years of 7:2 lead up to Israel’s lament; they are not the ark’s whole stay. Psalm 132 remembers that search for the ark: “We found it in the fields of the woods” (Psalm 132:6). Many interpreters take those words as a reference to Kirjath Jearim, a name that means “town of forests,” and the same psalm prays for the LORD’s “Anointed” (Psalm 132:10). Even when His people respond poorly, God keeps working out His purposes. The story of His presence among His people moves toward the anointed king and, beyond him, toward Christ, the Son of David.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me


God’s purposes do not stall when His people stumble. Even when we push Him away, He keeps moving His story forward toward His King. That gives me hope for my own failures, and for the church’s. The King has come, and He will finish what He started.
