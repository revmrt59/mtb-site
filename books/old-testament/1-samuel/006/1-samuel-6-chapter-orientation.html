Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 6
Main Idea:

The LORD brings His ark home by turning untrained cows straight to Beth Shemesh, then strikes men there who look into it, showing that His holiness governs Israel as surely as it humbled the Philistines.

Setting:

After seven months of plagues (5:6–12; 6:1), the Philistines ask their priests and diviners how to send the ark back. Beth Shemesh, an Israelite town on the border, was one of the towns given to the priests (Joshua 21:13–16).

Chapter Outline:

  • 1 Samuel 6:1–9 — The Philistines Plan the Ark’s Return

  • 1 Samuel 6:10–18 — The Ark Comes to Beth Shemesh

  • 1 Samuel 6:19–21 — Judgment at Beth Shemesh

Key Verse:

“And the men of Beth Shemesh said, ‘Who is able to stand before this holy LORD God? And to whom shall it go up from us?’ ” (1 Samuel 6:20, NKJV)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The Philistines have learned that the ark is not a trophy. Their own priests admit that the God of Israel has laid His hand on them and warn against hardening their hearts “as the Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened their hearts” (6:6). Their remedy is their own: golden tumors and rats, a new cart, and a test with two milk cows that have never been yoked (6:7–9). God does not endorse their methods, yet He answers their test. The cows head “straight for the road to Beth Shemesh,” lowing as they go, and do not turn “to the right hand or the left” (6:12). With no Israelite hand on the cart, the LORD brings His own presence home.

Reapers at Beth Shemesh lift their eyes from the wheat harvest and rejoice (6:13), and the day becomes a day of sacrifice. Then the scene turns. The LORD strikes men of Beth Shemesh “because they had looked into the ark of the LORD” (6:19). Their joy was right; their handling of His holy presence was not. The holiness that broke Dagon does not change at Israel’s border. The survivors ask the right question, “Who is able to stand before this holy LORD God?” (6:20), but their answer is to send the ark away to Kirjath Jearim. The chapter ends with God’s presence back in Israel and His people not yet knowing how to live near Him.

Chapter Sections

1 Samuel 6:1–9 — The Philistines Plan the Ark’s Return

After seven months the Philistine priests and diviners advise sending the ark back with a trespass offering of five golden tumors and five golden rats, and they devise a test with untrained milk cows to learn whether Israel’s God or mere chance had struck them. Their counsel acknowledges the LORD’s hand, but it remains their own religious remedy, not instruction from the LORD.

1 Samuel 6:10–18 — The Ark Comes to Beth Shemesh

Against their instinct, the cows carry the ark straight to Beth Shemesh, where the people rejoice, the Levites set the ark on the large stone, and burnt offerings are made to the LORD. The narrator closes the scene by listing the golden offerings of the five Philistine cities and the stone that remained as a witness.

1 Samuel 6:19–21 — Judgment at Beth Shemesh

The LORD strikes men of Beth Shemesh because they looked into the ark, and the people mourn the great slaughter. Asking who can stand before so holy a God, they send to Kirjath Jearim to have the ark taken away.

Why This Chapter Matters

This chapter shows that nearness to God does not make His holiness safer. The same presence that humbled the Philistines strikes Israelites who treat the ark as an object to inspect, because the Law had already said that holy things were not open to casual viewing (Numbers 4:20).

It also shows that God can bend even a pagan test to His purpose without approving it. The new cart worked for the Philistines because the LORD chose to answer them; it was never a pattern for Israel, and when David later moves the ark on a new cart, the LORD breaks out against them, and David recognizes that they had not sought Him “about the proper order” (2 Samuel 6:3–7; 1 Chronicles 15:13).

Key Themes to Watch For

The hand of the LORD acknowledged. Even Philistine priests concede that Israel’s God, not chance, has struck them (6:3, 5, 9).

God directs His own return. The cows go straight to Beth Shemesh without driver or Israelite help, so the outcome of the test is the LORD’s doing (6:7–12).

Joy and worship at the ark’s return. Harvesters rejoice, Levites receive the ark, and offerings are made the same day (6:13–15).

Holiness that governs Israel too. The God who humbled Dagon strikes those in Israel who look into His ark (6:19–20).

Sending God’s presence away. Like the Philistine cities (5:11), Beth Shemesh responds to judgment by passing the ark on (6:20–21).

Key Words & Concepts

Trespass offering. The Hebrew word used here is the word for a guilt offering, an offering that makes amends for an offense (6:3–4, 8, 17). The Philistines use it for their own gifts of golden tumors and rats, admitting guilt before Israel’s God without receiving His law.

Milk cows and a new cart. Two cows that had never been yoked, separated from their calves, would not naturally pull a cart in a straight line away from home (6:7, 10–12). Their straight course is the sign the Philistines asked for.

Beth Shemesh. An Israelite town on the border nearest the Philistines, given to the priests in Joshua’s day (Joshua 21:13–16), which may explain why Levites were on hand to receive the ark (6:15).

Looked into the ark. The stated reason for the judgment (6:19). The Law forbade even those who served at the sanctuary to look on the holy things while they were being covered (Numbers 4:20).

Fifty thousand and seventy. The NKJV follows the traditional Hebrew text in 6:19. A few Hebrew manuscripts read only seventy, as do some modern translations such as the NIV. The judgment and the people’s mourning are clear on either reading.

Kirjath Jearim. The town to which the ark is sent (6:21), where it will remain for a long time (7:1–2).
