Ecclesiastes Book Outline

Revision 5 November 6, 2026

Major Movements

I. The Preacher’s Question and the Endless Cycles (Ecclesiastes 1:1–11)

Solomon, introduced as “the Preacher, the son of David, king in Jerusalem” (1:1), announces his verdict on life and asks the question that drives the whole book: “What profit has a man from all his labor In which he toils under the sun?” (1:3). A poem about the sun, the wind, the rivers, and passing generations then pictures a world of constant motion that never arrives anywhere. In that world “there is nothing new under the sun” (1:9), and nothing is remembered for long.

II. Solomon’s Search Under the Sun (Ecclesiastes 1:12–2:26)

The king sets his heart “to seek and search out by wisdom” (1:13), tests wisdom, pleasure, great building projects, and wealth while holding nothing back, and concludes that “all was vanity and grasping for the wind,” with “no profit under the sun” (2:11). Death erases even the advantage the wise have over the fool (2:14–23). Yet the search closes with the book’s first glimpse of an answer: the ability to enjoy food, drink, and work comes “from the hand of God” (2:24).

III. God’s Times and Human Limits (Ecclesiastes 3:1–22)

“To everything there is a season” (3:1). The poem of paired times shows that God orders the changing circumstances of life, that “He has made everything beautiful in its time,” and that He has “put eternity in their hearts” (3:11), even though no one can find out His work from beginning to end. Corrupt justice and the death that humans share with animals raise hard questions (3:16–21), yet the chapter closes by commending joy in one’s work, “for that is his heritage” (3:22).

IV. Oppression, Toil, Worship, and Wealth (Ecclesiastes 4:1–6:12)

Solomon turns from his own experiments to life in society: oppressed people who “have no comforter” (4:1), work driven by envy, the loneliness of working only for oneself, the strength of companionship, since “a threefold cord is not quickly broken” (4:12), and how quickly popular favor fades. At the center he calls for reverence in the house of God, because “God is in heaven, and you on earth” (5:2). He then exposes the emptiness of loving money, since the lover of silver “will not be satisfied with silver” (5:10), contrasts it with riches received as “the gift of God” (5:19), and ends with the question, “who knows what is good for man in life” (6:12).

V. The Value and Limits of Wisdom (Ecclesiastes 7:1–8:17)

Practical sayings such as “A good name is better than precious ointment” (7:1) show that wisdom has real value. Then Solomon looks at the reasoning that follows when only earthly outcomes count, and at the failure of his own search, concluding that “God made man upright, But they have sought out many schemes” (7:29). Life under a king’s authority and justice that comes slowly lead to the confidence that “it will be well with those who fear God” (8:12) and to the admission that “a man cannot find out the work that is done under the sun” (8:17).

VI. One Event for All; Wisdom and Folly (Ecclesiastes 9:1–10:20)

Although “the righteous and the wise and their works are in the hand of God” (9:1), death comes to everyone, and that fact calls people to receive life as God’s gift, to “eat your bread with joy” (9:7), and to work with all their strength while they can (9:10). Success is never guaranteed, for “time and chance happen to them all” (9:11), and the forgotten poor wise man shows wisdom saving a city yet being despised (9:13–16). Sayings on folly, speech, rulers, and laziness then show both the worth of wisdom (“wisdom brings success,” 10:10) and how easily a little folly spoils it.

VII. Live Well Before the Days of Darkness (Ecclesiastes 11:1–12:8)

“Cast your bread upon the waters” (11:1) calls readers to give generously and work diligently even though the future is uncertain. The young are told to “Rejoice, O young man, in your youth” (11:9), knowing that God will judge, and to remember “your Creator in the days of your youth” (12:1) before old age and death, pictured in 12:2–7, when “the spirit will return to God who gave it” (12:7). The opening refrain returns in 12:8 and closes Solomon’s search.

VIII. The Conclusion of the Whole Matter (Ecclesiastes 12:9–14)

The closing voice commends the Preacher’s “words of truth” (12:10), wisdom “given by one Shepherd” (12:11). It then gives the book’s governing conclusion: “Fear God and keep His commandments, For this is man’s all” (12:13), because God will bring every work, even every secret thing, into judgment (12:14).
