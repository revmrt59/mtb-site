Ecclesiastes 1 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Ecclesiastes 1 opens the book with Solomon’s verdict that all is “vanity,” meaning that life pursued without God is meaningless, and with his question about what lasting profit a person gains from labor, then pictures a world of endless cycles that never arrive anywhere. In verse 12 Solomon begins speaking in his own voice and launches the royal search that continues through 2:26, starting with wisdom itself and finding that greater knowledge only deepens his grief.

Chapter Sections

1. Ecclesiastes 1:1–3 — The Preacher’s Verdict and Question

The opening voice introduces “the Preacher, the son of David, king in Jerusalem” and reports his verdict, “Vanity of vanities, all is vanity.” Solomon then asks the question the whole book will test: “What profit has a man from all his labor In which he toils under the sun?”

2. Ecclesiastes 1:4–11 — The Endless Cycles That Never Arrive

Generations pass while the earth remains, and the sun, the wind, and the rivers keep circling without ever completing anything. Human life follows the same pattern: the eye is never satisfied with seeing, “there is nothing new under the sun,” and each generation is forgotten by the ones that come after.

3. Ecclesiastes 1:12–15 — The King Begins His Search

Speaking now in his own voice, the king sets his heart to search out by wisdom “all that is done under heaven,” a “burdensome task God has given to the sons of man.” He finds it all “vanity and grasping for the wind” and sums up what he saw in a proverb: what is crooked cannot be made straight, and what is lacking cannot be numbered.

4. Ecclesiastes 1:16–18 — Great Wisdom That Increases Grief

Solomon reflects on his own unmatched wisdom and sets his heart to know both wisdom and “madness and folly,” only to find that this pursuit too is grasping for the wind. A second proverb closes the chapter: “For in much wisdom is much grief, And he who increases knowledge increases sorrow.”
