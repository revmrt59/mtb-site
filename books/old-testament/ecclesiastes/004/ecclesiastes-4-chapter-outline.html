Ecclesiastes 4 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Solomon turns from his search through God's times in chapter 3 to life among other people, looking in turn at the oppressed who have no comforter, work driven by envy or carried on in lonely accumulation, the strength of companionship, and the popular favor that even a wise ruler cannot keep. The chapter moves from the isolation of victims and solitary strivers to the shared strength of two or three, and it ends by showing that wisdom's real advantage still cannot secure lasting acclaim under the sun.

Chapter Sections

1. Ecclesiastes 4:1–3 — Tears of the Oppressed with No Comforter

Solomon sees the tears of the oppressed, notes that "On the side of their oppressors there is power," and says twice that "they have no comforter." That suffering drives him to praise the dead, and then the one "who has never existed," above the living, but these are anguished comparisons made from the earthly horizon, not God's final verdict on the worth of human life.

2. Ecclesiastes 4:4–6 — Envy, Idleness, and the Quiet Handful

Solomon finds that skillful work often comes from rivalry with one's neighbor, and beside this he sets the fool who "folds his hands And consumes his own flesh." Between restless competition and destructive laziness he commends a better measure: "a handful with quietness" rather than "both hands full, together with toil and grasping for the wind."

3. Ecclesiastes 4:7–8 — The Solitary Worker Whose Eye Is Never Satisfied

Solomon turns again to a man with neither son nor brother whose labor never ends and whose eye is never "satisfied with riches," yet who never asks, "For whom do I toil and deprive myself of good?" Solomon calls this lonely accumulation "vanity and a grave misfortune."

4. Ecclesiastes 4:9–12 — Two Are Better Than One

In contrast to the man who works alone, Solomon commends companionship: companions share the reward of their labor, lift each other up when one falls, keep each other warm, and stand together against attack. The closing image, "a threefold cord is not quickly broken," heightens the picture of strength through union.

5. Ecclesiastes 4:13–16 — The Wise Youth, the Foolish King, and Fading Acclaim

A poor and wise youth is better than "an old and foolish king who will be admonished no more," yet the one who rises from prison and poverty to the throne and gathers countless followers cannot keep their favor, for "those who come afterward will not rejoice in him." The chapter ends with Solomon's verdict that this also is "vanity and grasping for the wind."
