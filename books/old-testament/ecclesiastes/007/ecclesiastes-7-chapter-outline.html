Ecclesiastes 7 Chapter Outline

Revision 2 · November 26, 2026

Chapter Focus

Ecclesiastes 7 opens the movement on wisdom’s value and limits (7:1–8:17) with a run of “better” sayings that answer the closing question of 6:12, “who knows what is good for man in life.” The chapter then moves from wisdom’s real benefits to its limits: earthly calculation in the face of unjust outcomes, the universality of sin, wisdom that remains “far from me,” and Solomon’s own failed search, which ends with the diagnosis that “God made man upright, But they have sought out many schemes.”

Chapter Sections

1. Ecclesiastes 7:1–6 — Better the House of Mourning Than the House of Feasting

A good name and the day of death are better than precious ointment and the day of birth, because the house of mourning teaches the living to “take it to heart.” The rebuke of the wise is better than “the song of fools,” whose laughter is like “the crackling of thorns under a pot.”

2. Ecclesiastes 7:7–10 — Patience Instead of Anger and Longing for the Past

Oppression and bribes can corrupt even a wise man’s judgment, so “The patient in spirit is better than the proud in spirit.” Solomon warns against quick anger and against asking, “Why were the former days better than these?”

3. Ecclesiastes 7:11–14 — Wisdom’s Protection and the Work of God

Wisdom is good with an inheritance and “gives life to those who have it,” yet no one can “make straight what He has made crooked.” In prosperity a person should be joyful, and in adversity he should consider that God has appointed both, “So that man can find out nothing that will come after him.”

4. Ecclesiastes 7:15–18 — Earthly Expediency and the Fear of God

Solomon has seen “a just man who perishes in his righteousness” and a wicked man who lives long, and from that earthly horizon comes the calculated counsel not to be “overly righteous” or “overly wicked.” Verse 18 brings the fear of God into view: “he who fears God will escape them all.”

5. Ecclesiastes 7:19–22 — Wisdom’s Strength and the Sin of Every Person

Wisdom strengthens the wise “More than ten rulers of the city,” yet “there is not a just man on earth who does good And does not sin.” Remembering his own careless words, a person should not take to heart every curse he overhears.

6. Ecclesiastes 7:23–25 — Wisdom Beyond Reach

Solomon tested everything by wisdom and resolved, “I will be wise”; “But it was far from me.” What is “exceedingly deep” lies beyond finding out, yet he still set his heart to search out wisdom and to know the wickedness of folly.

7. Ecclesiastes 7:26–29 — The Snare Solomon Found and Humanity’s Many Schemes

Solomon finds “more bitter than death” the woman whose heart is snares and nets, from whom “He who pleases God shall escape.” Searching one thing after another, he found one man among a thousand but no woman, and he concludes that “God made man upright, But they have sought out many schemes.”
