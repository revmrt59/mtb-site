Ecclesiastes 8 Chapter Outline

Revision 1 · November 26, 2026

Chapter Focus

Ecclesiastes 8 completes the movement on wisdom’s value and limits (7:1–8:17). It moves from wise conduct under a king’s power, through human helplessness before time and death, to the problem of delayed justice, and it ends by commending God-given enjoyment while admitting that no one, however wise, can find out the work of God.

Chapter Sections

1. Ecclesiastes 8:1–5 — Wisdom Before the King

“A man’s wisdom makes his face shine,” and the wise man keeps “the king’s commandment for the sake of your oath to God.” Because “Where the word of a king is, there is power,” he avoids rash defiance and “discerns both time and judgment.”

2. Ecclesiastes 8:6–9 — The Limits of Human Power

There is a time and judgment for every matter, yet no one knows what will happen, and “no one has power in the day of death.” Solomon has also seen that one man may rule over another “to his own hurt.”

3. Ecclesiastes 8:10–14 — Delayed Justice and the Good of Fearing God

Solomon saw the wicked buried who “had come and gone from the place of holiness” and were forgotten, and he observes that when sentence against evil is not executed speedily, people set their hearts to do evil. Even so, “it will be well with those who fear God,” though the just and the wicked sometimes receive each other’s outcomes.

4. Ecclesiastes 8:15–17 — Enjoyment Commended and the Unsearchable Work of God

Solomon “commended enjoyment,” for eating, drinking, and being merry will remain with a man through “all the days of his life which God gives him under the sun.” Yet though a wise man labors to know it, “a man cannot find out the work that is done under the sun.”
