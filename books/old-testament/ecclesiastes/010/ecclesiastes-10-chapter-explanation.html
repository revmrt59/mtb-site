Ecclesiastes 10:1
Ecclesiastes 10:1
NKJV

Ecclesiastes 10:1 Dead flies putrefy the perfumer's ointment, And cause it to give off a foul odor; So does a little folly to one respected for wisdom and honor.(NKJV)

NLT

Ecclesiastes 10:1 As dead flies cause even a bottle of perfume to stink,so a little foolishness spoils great wisdom and honor. (NLT)

Main Idea

Just as dead flies make perfume stink, “So does a little folly to one respected for wisdom and honor.” A small act of foolishness can spoil a reputation built over a long time.

Verse Explanation

Just as dead flies make perfume stink, “So does a little folly to one respected for wisdom and honor.” A small act of foolishness can spoil a reputation built over a long time.

Dead flies putrefy the perfumer’s ointment, gives a picture from everyday life. A few dead flies in a jar of perfumed ointment can ruin the whole batch.

And cause it to give off a foul odor; describes the result. Something precious becomes offensive.

So does a little folly to one respected for wisdom and honor. applies the picture. A small act of foolishness can spoil the reputation of a person long respected for wisdom and honor. This continues the warning of 9:18 that “one sinner destroys much good.”

Ecclesiastes 10:2
NKJV

Ecclesiastes 10:2 A wise man's heart is at his right hand, But a fool's heart at his left.(NKJV)

NLT

Ecclesiastes 10:2 A wise person chooses the right road;a fool takes the wrong one. (NLT)

Main Idea

“A wise man’s heart is at his right hand, But a fool’s heart at his left.” Using the common picture of the right hand as the side of skill and favor, Solomon contrasts the wise and foolish direction of the heart.

Verse Explanation

“A wise man’s heart is at his right hand, But a fool’s heart at his left.” Using the common picture of the right hand as the side of skill and favor, Solomon contrasts the wise and foolish direction of the heart.

A wise man’s heart is at his right hand, uses a common picture. In the ancient world the right hand was associated with skill, strength, and favor. The wise person’s heart is directed toward what is good and skillful.

But a fool’s heart at his left. contrasts the fool, whose heart is directed toward what is weak or wrong. The saying uses the conventional imagery of right and left to contrast wise and foolish orientation; it is not a comment on bodily handedness.

Even as a fool walks along the road, his lack of sense shows, and he makes it plain to everyone that he is a fool. Folly cannot stay hidden; it reveals itself in ordinary conduct.

If a ruler becomes angry with you, do not abandon your post, “For conciliation pacifies great offenses.” A calm and steady response can quiet even serious anger.

Ecclesiastes 10:8
NKJV

NLT

Ecclesiastes 10:8 When you dig a well,you might fall in.When you demolish an old wall,you could be bitten by a snake. (NLT)

Main Idea

One who digs a pit may fall into it, and one who breaks through a wall may be bitten by a serpent. Ordinary work carries risks, and harmful plans can rebound on the one who makes them.

Verse Explanation

One who digs a pit may fall into it, and one who breaks through a wall may be bitten by a serpent. Ordinary work carries risks, and harmful plans can rebound on the one who makes them.

He who digs a pit will fall into it, warns that ordinary work carries risk, and that a pit dug to trap others can trap its maker.

And whoever breaks through a wall will be bitten by a serpent. adds another danger. Snakes often hid in the cracks of stone walls, so someone breaking through a wall might be bitten. These are observations about risk, not guarantees that every worker will be harmed.

Ecclesiastes 10:10

NLT

Ecclesiastes 10:10 Using a dull ax requires great strength,so sharpen the blade.That’s the value of wisdom;it helps you succeed. (NLT)

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

If an ax is dull and its edge is not sharpened, more strength is needed, “But wisdom brings success.” Preparation and skill make work more effective than mere effort.

If the ax is dull, continues the picture of woodcutting.

But wisdom brings success. draws the lesson. Wisdom, like a sharpened ax, makes work more effective. Preparation and skill achieve more than mere strength.

Study Notes

The Hebrew of this verse is difficult, and translations vary in detail. The general sense, that wisdom gives an advantage over brute effort, is clear.

Ecclesiastes 10:11
Ecclesiastes 10:11
NKJV

Ecclesiastes 10:11 A serpent may bite when it is not charmed; The babbler is no different.(NKJV)

NLT

Ecclesiastes 10:11 If a snake bites before you charm it,what’s the use of being a snake charmer? (NLT)

Main Idea

A serpent may bite when it has not been charmed, and “The babbler is no different.” In the NKJV’s comparison, a person who talks without control can do harm as surely as an uncharmed snake.

Verse Explanation

A serpent may bite when it has not been charmed, and “The babbler is no different.” In the NKJV’s comparison, a person who talks without control can do harm as surely as an uncharmed snake.

A serpent may bite when it is not charmed; refers to snake charmers, who claimed to control serpents. A snake that has not been charmed may bite.

The babbler is no different. compares the uncontrolled talker to such a snake. In the NKJV, a person whose tongue runs freely can do as much harm as an uncharmed serpent.

Study Notes

Several modern translations read the verse differently. The ESV says that if the serpent bites before it is charmed, “there is no advantage to the charmer,” and the NIV and NASB read similarly. On that reading the verse teaches that skill is useless if applied too late. The Hebrew phrase behind “the babbler” is literally “master of the tongue,” a literal gloss noted in the KJV margin, and it can describe either a charmer or a talker. This study follows the NKJV comparison with the babbler.

Ecclesiastes 10:17 Happy is the land whose king is a noble leaderand whose leaders feast at the proper timeto gain strength for their work, not to get drunk. (NLT)

Main Idea

Blessed is the land whose king is “the son of nobles” and whose princes eat at the proper time, “For strength and not for drunkenness.” In contrast to the child king and morning feasting of Ecclesiastes 10:16, such leaders show honorable, disciplined rule that serves the land rather than their own appetites.

Verse Explanation

Blessed is the land whose king is “the son of nobles” and whose princes eat at the proper time, “For strength and not for drunkenness.” In contrast to the child king and morning feasting of Ecclesiastes 10:16, such leaders show honorable, disciplined rule that serves the land rather than their own appetites.

Blessed are you, O land, when your king is the son of nobles, pronounces blessing on a land with a true king. The image speaks in terms of noble birth, setting such a king against the child king of verse 16, but the force of the contrast lies in how the king and his officials rule, as the next lines show.

And your princes feast at the proper time adds that its officials eat at the right time.

For strength and not for drunkenness! gives the purpose of their eating: to be strengthened for their work, not to indulge themselves. Disciplined rule serves the good of the land rather than the rulers’ appetites.

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

Because of laziness the building decays, says neglect causes a building to deteriorate.

Study Notes

Ecclesiastes 10:19
Ecclesiastes 10:19
NKJV

Ecclesiastes 10:19 A feast is made for laughter, And wine makes merry; But money answers everything.(NKJV)

NLT

Ecclesiastes 10:19 A party gives laughter,wine gives happiness,and money gives everything! (NLT)

Main Idea

“A feast is made for laughter, And wine makes merry; But money answers everything.” Solomon observes money’s practical usefulness: it provides for ordinary needs such as food and drink, though the book elsewhere shows that money cannot satisfy or save.

Verse Explanation

“A feast is made for laughter, And wine makes merry; But money answers everything.” Solomon observes money’s practical usefulness: it provides for ordinary needs such as food and drink, though the book elsewhere shows that money cannot satisfy or save.

A feast is made for laughter, says feasts are prepared for enjoyment.

And wine makes merry; adds that wine brings gladness.

But money answers everything. completes the saying. Solomon observes money’s practical usefulness: it provides the food and drink just mentioned and meets ordinary needs. Verses 16–17 have already distinguished proper feasting from indulgence, and the wider book shows that money cannot satisfy its lover or save its owner from death (5:10–16).

Study Notes

Some interpreters read verse 19 as the outlook of the undisciplined rulers of verse 16, who feast for pleasure and think money solves every problem. The NKJV wording allows that reading, but this study takes money’s practical usefulness as the primary sense.
