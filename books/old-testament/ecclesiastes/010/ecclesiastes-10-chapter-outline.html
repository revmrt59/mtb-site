Ecclesiastes 10 Chapter Outline

Revision 1 · November 26, 2026

Chapter Focus

Ecclesiastes 10 is a collection of wisdom sayings that closes the movement begun in 9:1. Picking up the warning that “one sinner destroys much good” (9:18), it shows how folly damages a person’s honor, public order, work, speech, and leadership, while wisdom brings real though limited advantage.

Chapter Sections

1. Ecclesiastes 10:1–3 — A Little Folly Spoils Wisdom

As dead flies make perfume give off a foul odor, “So does a little folly to one respected for wisdom and honor.” The wise and the foolish differ in the direction of their hearts, and the fool shows his folly even as he walks along the way.

2. Ecclesiastes 10:4–7 — Calm Before a Ruler and Honor Misplaced

When a ruler’s anger rises, “Do not leave your post; For conciliation pacifies great offenses.” Solomon has also seen an error that proceeds from rulers: “Folly is set in great dignity,” while servants ride on horses and princes walk.

3. Ecclesiastes 10:8–11 — Everyday Risks and the Advantage of Wisdom

Digging a pit, breaking through a wall, quarrying stones, and splitting wood all carry danger, and a dull ax demands more strength, “But wisdom brings success.” A serpent may bite when it is not charmed, and “The babbler is no different.”

4. Ecclesiastes 10:12–15 — The Words and Labor of Fools

“The words of a wise man’s mouth are gracious,” but a fool’s talk begins in foolishness and ends in “raving madness.” He multiplies words about a future no one knows, and his labor wears him out.

5. Ecclesiastes 10:16–20 — Rulers, Laziness, Provision, and Careful Speech

A land suffers when its princes “feast in the morning” and is blessed when they feast at the proper time, “For strength and not for drunkenness.” Laziness lets the house decay, “money answers everything,” and even a curse spoken in private may be carried by “a bird of the air.”
