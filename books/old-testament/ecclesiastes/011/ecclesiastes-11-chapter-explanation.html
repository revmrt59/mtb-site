Chapter Study

Ecclesiastes 11

Verse-by-Verse
Ecclesiastes 11:1
Ecclesiastes 11:1
NKJV

Ecclesiastes 11:1 Cast your bread upon the waters, For you will find it after many days.(NKJV)

NLT

Ecclesiastes 11:1 Send your grain across the seas,and in time, profits will flow back to you. (NLT)

Main Idea

“Cast your bread upon the waters, For you will find it after many days.” Solomon calls for generous giving and doing good in trust toward God, encouraged that such kindness will be found again in time, while the form and timing of any return are left in God’s hands.

Verse Explanation

“Cast your bread upon the waters, For you will find it after many days.” Solomon calls for generous giving and doing good in trust toward God, encouraged that such kindness will be found again in time, while the form and timing of any return are left in God’s hands.

Cast your bread upon the waters, begins a call to action. Bread was the basic food, and casting it on the waters pictures giving it away freely without knowing where it will go. This is best read as a call to generosity and to doing good.

For you will find it after many days. gives the encouragement. Generosity is not wasted. What is given in trust toward God will be found again in time, though the timing and form of any return rest in God’s hands. The verse is not a promise of financial profit or a guaranteed return date.

Study Notes

Some interpreters read verse 1 as advice about overseas trade, sending grain by ship and waiting for a return, and the NLT displayed above follows that reading. Others see the spreading of investments. These are secondary possibilities; the context of giving a portion to many in verse 2 supports generosity as the primary reading.

Ecclesiastes 11:2
Ecclesiastes 11:2
NKJV

Ecclesiastes 11:2 Give a serving to seven, and also to eight, For you do not know what evil will be on the earth.(NKJV)

NLT

Ecclesiastes 11:2 But divide your investments among many places,for you do not know what risks might lie ahead. (NLT)

Main Idea

Give a portion to seven, and even to eight, because you do not know what trouble may come on the earth. The uncertainty of the future is a reason to be generous broadly now, not a reason to hold back.

Verse Explanation

Give a portion to seven, and even to eight, because you do not know what trouble may come on the earth. The uncertainty of the future is a reason to be generous broadly now, not a reason to hold back.

Give a serving to seven, and also to eight, urges sharing broadly. Seven was a number of fullness, and adding eight pictures going beyond what seems enough. The point is open-handed breadth, not an exact count of recipients.

For you do not know what evil will be on the earth. gives the reason. Since no one knows what trouble may come, the time to be generous is now. Uncertainty is a reason to give, not a reason to hold back.

Study Notes

The pattern “seven, and also to eight” is a Hebrew numerical saying in which one number is followed by the next. Micah 5:5 uses the same pairing: “Seven shepherds and eight princely men.”

Ecclesiastes 11:3–6 — Working Without Knowing the Outcome

Ecclesiastes 11:3
Ecclesiastes 11:3
NKJV

Ecclesiastes 11:3 If the clouds are full of rain, They empty themselves upon the earth; And if a tree falls to the south or the north, In the place where the tree falls, there it shall lie.(NKJV)

NLT

Ecclesiastes 11:3 When clouds are heavy, the rains come down.Whether a tree falls north or south, it stays where it falls. (NLT)

Main Idea

When clouds are full of rain they empty on the earth, and wherever a tree falls, there it lies. Some things follow their course regardless of human wishes, and people must act within conditions they cannot control.

Verse Explanation

When clouds are full of rain they empty on the earth, and wherever a tree falls, there it lies. Some things follow their course regardless of human wishes, and people must act within conditions they cannot control.

If the clouds are full of rain, gives a picture from nature.

They empty themselves upon the earth; says rain falls when the clouds are full. Some things follow their natural course regardless of human wishes.

And if a tree falls to the south or the north, gives a second picture.

In the place where the tree falls, there it shall lie. says a fallen tree stays where it falls. Like the rain, its course is beyond human control. People must act within conditions they cannot change, which verse 4 applies to the farmer who waits for perfect weather.

None.

Ecclesiastes 11:5
Ecclesiastes 11:5
NKJV

Ecclesiastes 11:5 As you do not know what is the way of the wind, Or how the bones grow in the womb of her who is with child, So you do not know the works of God who makes everything.(NKJV)

NLT

Ecclesiastes 11:5 Just as you cannot understand the path of the wind or the mystery of a tiny baby growing in its mother’s womb, so you cannot understand the activity of God, who does all things. (NLT)

Main Idea

As no one knows the way of the wind or how the bones form in a mother’s womb, so no one knows “the works of God who makes everything.” Human ignorance before God’s creative work is a reason for humility, not for idleness.

Verse Explanation

As no one knows the way of the wind or how the bones form in a mother’s womb, so no one knows “the works of God who makes everything.” Human ignorance before God’s creative work is a reason for humility, not for idleness.

As you do not know what is the way of the wind, points to a limit of human knowledge. No one fully understands the movement of the wind.

Or how the bones grow in the womb of her who is with child, gives a second example. The formation of a child in the womb was a mystery beyond human understanding.

So you do not know the works of God who makes everything. applies the examples. If people cannot understand the wind or the forming of a child, they cannot understand all that God does. God is the Maker of everything, and human ignorance before His work calls for humility.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word translated “wind” (ruach) can also mean spirit or breath. The ESV connects it with the next line and reads, “the way the spirit comes to the bones in the womb.” The NKJV keeps two separate examples, and this study follows it.

Ecclesiastes 11:6
NKJV

Ecclesiastes 11:6 In the morning sow your seed, And in the evening do not withhold your hand; For you do not know which will prosper, Either this or that, Or whether both alike will be good.(NKJV)

NLT

Ecclesiastes 11:6 Plant your seed in the morning and keep busy all afternoon, for you don’t know if profit will come from one activity or another — or maybe both. (NLT)

Main Idea

Sow your seed in the morning and do not hold back your hand in the evening, because you do not know which will succeed, or whether both will be good. Since the outcome is unknown, diligent work should continue.

Verse Explanation

Sow your seed in the morning and do not hold back your hand in the evening, because you do not know which will succeed, or whether both will be good. Since the outcome is unknown, diligent work should continue.

In the morning sow your seed, urges work at the start of the day.

And in the evening do not withhold your hand; urges continuing to work until day’s end.

For you do not know which will prosper, gives the reason. No one knows which effort will succeed.

Either this or that, Or whether both alike will be good. adds that perhaps both will succeed. Since the outcome is unknown, diligent work should continue.

Main Idea

Even if a person lives many years and rejoices in them all, he should remember that the days of darkness will be many. Joy in life is real, but it should be held with awareness that life is brief and darker days will come.

Verse Explanation

Even if a person lives many years and rejoices in them all, he should remember that the days of darkness will be many. Joy in life is real, but it should be held with awareness that life is brief and darker days will come.

But if a man lives many years considers a long life.

And rejoices in them all, urges rejoicing throughout those years.

Yet let him remember the days of darkness, adds a sober reminder. Darker days are coming, such as old age and death.

For they will be many. says those days will be many.

All that is coming is vanity. says what is to come is fleeting, like a breath. Joy in life is real, but it should be held with awareness that life is brief.

Ecclesiastes 11:9
Ecclesiastes 11:9
NKJV

Ecclesiastes 11:9 Rejoice, O young man, in your youth, And let your heart cheer you in the days of your youth; Walk in the ways of your heart, And in the sight of your eyes; But know that for all these God will bring you into judgment.(NKJV)

NLT

Ecclesiastes 11:9 Young people, it’s wonderful to be young! Enjoy every minute of it. Do everything you want to do; take it all in. But remember that you must give an account to God for everything you do. (NLT)

Main Idea

Solomon tells the young man to rejoice in his youth and to walk in the ways of his heart and the sight of his eyes, “But know that for all these God will bring you into judgment.” Youthful joy is commended, but it must be lived in accountability to God.

Verse Explanation

Solomon tells the young man to rejoice in his youth and to walk in the ways of his heart and the sight of his eyes, “But know that for all these God will bring you into judgment.” Youthful joy is commended, but it must be lived in accountability to God.

Rejoice, O young man, in your youth, addresses the young directly with a call to rejoice.

And let your heart cheer you in the days of your youth; urges enjoyment of the years of youth.

Walk in the ways of your heart, And in the sight of your eyes; encourages the young to pursue the good desires of their hearts and the delights they see.

But know that for all these God will bring you into judgment. adds the essential qualification. God will judge how they live. This does not cancel the call to joy; it keeps joy within accountability to God and prevents the command from becoming permission to sin.

Study Notes

None.

Ecclesiastes 11:10
Ecclesiastes 11:10
NKJV

Ecclesiastes 11:10 Therefore remove sorrow from your heart, And put away evil from your flesh, For childhood and youth are vanity.(NKJV)

NLT

Ecclesiastes 11:10 So refuse to worry, and keep your body healthy. But remember that youth, with a whole life before you, is meaningless. (NLT)

Main Idea

Therefore remove sorrow from your heart and put away evil from your body, “For childhood and youth are vanity,” fleeting like a breath. Because youth passes quickly, it should be lived free of needless grief and of sin.

Verse Explanation

Therefore remove sorrow from your heart and put away evil from your body, “For childhood and youth are vanity,” fleeting like a breath. Because youth passes quickly, it should be lived free of needless grief and of sin.

Therefore remove sorrow from your heart, urges putting away needless grief and anxiety.

And put away evil from your flesh, urges turning away from sin and from what harms the body.

For childhood and youth are vanity. gives the reason. Youth is fleeting, like a breath, so it should be lived well, free of needless sorrow and of sin. The address continues directly into 12:1, “Remember now your Creator in the days of your youth.”

Study Notes

The word translated “youth” at the end of the verse is rare. The ESV renders it “the dawn of life,” and the NASB “the prime of life.”
