Exodus Book Outline

Revision 3 November 23, 2026

Major Movements

I. Israel Enslaved and Moses Preserved (Exodus 1:1–2:25)

Jacob’s family grows in Egypt until “the land was filled with them” (1:7), and a new king “who did not know Joseph” (1:8) answers God’s blessing with forced labor and a decree against Israel’s newborn sons. The midwives fear God, and Moses’ mother, his sister, and Pharaoh’s own daughter preserve the child through whom God will deliver His people. Moses’ first attempt to help his people ends in flight to Midian, yet the movement closes with Israel’s cry rising to God, who hears their groaning and remembers “His covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob” (2:24).

II. God Calls Moses and Reveals His Name (Exodus 3:1–7:7)

At a bush that burns but “was not consumed” (3:2), the Angel of the LORD appears, and the LORD declares that He has seen His people’s oppression and has “come down to deliver them” (3:8). He answers Moses’ “Who am I” (3:11) with “I will certainly be with you” (3:12) and reveals His name, “I AM WHO I AM” (3:14). When Moses and Aaron tell Pharaoh to let God’s people go, Pharaoh refuses, “Who is the LORD, that I should obey His voice” (5:2), and increases Israel’s workload (5:6–9); God answers Moses’ honest complaint by renewing His promise, “I will take you as My people, and I will be your God” (6:7), and sends Moses and Aaron back to speak to Pharaoh (7:1–7).

III. The Plagues on Egypt (Exodus 7:8–11:10)

Through a series of signs and judgments, from water turned to blood to three days of darkness, the LORD makes Himself known to both peoples: the Egyptians are to know “that I am the LORD” (7:5), and Israel is to tell its children what He has done in Egypt, “that you may know that I am the LORD” (10:1–2). Pharaoh bargains, confesses, and hardens his heart again, and the LORD hardens it as He had announced (4:21; 9:12), so that His name may be declared “in all the earth” (9:16). The movement ends with the last plague announced but not yet carried out and Pharaoh still refusing to let Israel go (11:10).

IV. Passover and the Crossing of the Sea (Exodus 12:1–15:21)

God gives the Passover, and the blood of the lamb on the doorposts becomes the sign by which He passes over Israel’s houses when He strikes Egypt’s firstborn and executes judgment “against all the gods of Egypt” (12:12–13). Israel goes out on a night to be remembered throughout their generations, the LORD leads them by the pillar of cloud and fire, and at the sea He saves His people and overthrows Pharaoh’s army (14:30–31). Moses and Israel answer with a song to the God who has redeemed and guided His people and will bring them in and plant them in the place He has made for His own dwelling (15:13, 17), and Miriam leads the women in reply (15:20–21).

V. From the Sea to Sinai (Exodus 15:22–18:27)

Three days past the sea, Israel finds only bitter water, and the road to Sinai becomes a school of trust: the LORD makes the water sweet and reveals Himself as “the LORD who heals you” (15:26), rains bread from heaven, teaches Sabbath rest through the manna, and brings water from the rock at Horeb. The people’s complaints are complaints against the LORD (16:8), and at Massah they ask, “Is the LORD among us or not?” (17:7), yet He keeps providing and gives victory over Amalek while Moses’ hands are held up. Jethro hears what God has done, confesses that the LORD is “greater than all the gods” (18:11), and counsels Moses to share the work of judging the people.

VI. The Covenant at Sinai (Exodus 19:1–24:18)

At Sinai God tells Israel, “I bore you on eagles’ wings and brought you to Myself” (19:4), and calls them to be “a kingdom of priests and a holy nation” (19:6). He descends on the mountain in fire, speaks the Ten Commandments to a people He has already brought “out of the house of bondage” (20:2), and gives the laws of the Book of the Covenant for their life together before Him. The covenant is sealed with “the blood of the covenant” (24:8), Israel’s leaders see the God of Israel and eat and drink before Him, and Moses enters the cloud on the mountain for forty days and forty nights (24:18).

VII. The Pattern of the Tabernacle (Exodus 25:1–31:18)

On the mountain the LORD says, “let them make Me a sanctuary, that I may dwell among them” (25:8), and shows Moses the pattern of the ark and mercy seat, the table, the lampstand, the tabernacle, and the altar and courtyard. He appoints Aaron and his sons as priests, describes their garments and consecration, and states the purpose of it all: “I will dwell among the children of Israel and will be their God” (29:45). The instructions close with Bezalel, whom God fills with His Spirit, and Aholiab, whom He appoints to work with him (31:2–6), then the Sabbath as the sign “that I am the LORD who sanctifies you” (31:13), and the two tablets written “with the finger of God” (31:18).

VIII. The Golden Calf and the Covenant Renewed (Exodus 32:1–34:35)

While Moses is still on the mountain, the people make a golden calf, and Aaron proclaims a feast to the LORD before it (32:1–7); Moses comes down and breaks the tablets, and judgment falls on the camp. Moses intercedes, asks to be blotted out of God’s book if the people are not forgiven (32:32), and refuses to go on unless God’s Presence goes with them (33:15). The LORD makes His goodness pass before Moses, proclaims Himself “merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abounding in goodness and truth” (34:6); He renews the covenant and its words are written on new tablets (34:1, 10, 27–28), and Moses comes down with his face shining from speaking with Him (34:29).

IX. The Tabernacle Built and Filled with Glory (Exodus 35:1–40:38)

The people bring their offerings with willing hearts until they must be told to stop, and Bezalel, Aholiab, and the skilled workers carry out the pattern “according to all that the LORD had commanded Moses” (39:42). Moses sets up the tabernacle, and “the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle” (40:34). The book ends with the cloud over God’s dwelling by day and fire by night, leading Israel “throughout all their journeys” (40:38).
