Exodus 1 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Exodus 1 shows Jacob’s household of seventy becoming a people that fills the land, just as God had promised, and then shows a new king of Egypt trying by every means to stop that growth. The chapter moves from blessing to forced labor, from forced labor to a secret order against Israel’s newborn sons, and finally to a public decree that every son be cast into the river.

Chapter Sections

1. Exodus 1:1–7 — Jacob’s Family Becomes a People in Egypt

Exodus names the sons of Israel who came to Egypt with Jacob, seventy persons in all, and records the death of Joseph and his generation. Yet the children of Israel “were fruitful and increased abundantly” until “the land was filled with them” (Exodus 1:7), the growth God had promised to Abraham.

2. Exodus 1:8–14 — A New King Turns Israel’s Growth into Hard Bondage

A king “who did not know Joseph” (Exodus 1:8) fears Israel’s numbers and sets taskmasters over them to build Pithom and Raamses. The more Egypt afflicts them, the more they multiply, so the Egyptians make Israel’s lives bitter with hard bondage and make them serve “with rigor” (Exodus 1:14).

3. Exodus 1:15–21 — The Midwives Fear God and Save the Sons

Pharaoh secretly orders the Hebrew midwives Shiphrah and Puah to kill every newborn son, but “the midwives feared God” (Exodus 1:17) and let the boys live. God deals well with them, the people keep multiplying, and because the midwives feared God, He provides households for them.

4. Exodus 1:22 — Pharaoh Commands Every Son to Be Cast into the River

When his secret order fails, Pharaoh commands all his people to throw every Hebrew son into the river. The chapter ends with this danger unresolved, and the next chapter begins with the birth of a son in the house of Levi.
