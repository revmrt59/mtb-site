Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 1
Main Idea:

God keeps His promise by turning Jacob’s family into a great people in Egypt, and when a new Pharaoh answers that blessing with slavery and a decree of death, he finds he cannot stop what God is doing.

Setting:

Exodus 1 picks up where Genesis ends. Jacob’s seventy descendants have settled in Egypt, Joseph and his generation have died, and a new king rises “who did not know Joseph” (1:8).

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 1:1–7 — Jacob’s Family Becomes a People in Egypt

  • Exodus 1:8–14 — A New King Turns Israel’s Growth into Hard Bondage

  • Exodus 1:15–21 — The Midwives Fear God and Save the Sons

  • Exodus 1:22 — Pharaoh Commands Every Son to Be Cast into the River

Key Verse:

“But the more they afflicted them, the more they multiplied and grew. And they were in dread of the children of Israel.” (Exodus 1:12)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Exodus begins with a list of names, the sons of Jacob who came down to Egypt with their households, seventy persons in all. Then, in one crowded sentence, they become a nation: they “were fruitful and increased abundantly, multiplied and grew exceedingly mighty; and the land was filled with them” (1:7). Those words echo God’s blessing on Abraham’s family. The promise is being kept far from the land God had promised.

A new king sees the same growth and calls it a threat. Pharaoh tries forced labor, and then secret killing at the hands of the midwives, and both attempts fail. “The more they afflicted them, the more they multiplied and grew” (1:12). So he issues a public decree that every Hebrew son be thrown into the river (1:22). The chapter quietly sets two powers against each other. Pharaoh holds the whip and the river, yet two midwives who “feared God” (1:17) defy him, and God deals well with them. The chapter ends with that decree in force, and chapter 2 begins to show how God preserves the deliverer under it.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 1:1–7 — Jacob’s Family Becomes a People in Egypt

Exodus names the sons of Israel who came to Egypt and notes the death of Joseph and his generation. The small family of seventy then grows until it fills the land, the fruitfulness God had promised to Abraham.

Exodus 1:8–14 — A New King Turns Israel’s Growth into Hard Bondage

A king who no longer honors Joseph’s legacy fears Israel’s numbers and sets taskmasters over them to build Pithom and Raamses. Oppression only multiplies them, so Egypt makes their lives bitter with hard bondage and makes them serve “with rigor” (1:14).

Exodus 1:15–21 — The Midwives Fear God and Save the Sons

Pharaoh secretly orders the midwives Shiphrah and Puah to kill every Hebrew son at birth, but they fear God and let the boys live. God deals well with them and gives them households of their own, while Israel keeps growing.

Exodus 1:22 — Pharaoh Commands Every Son to Be Cast into the River

When the secret plan fails, Pharaoh commands all his people to throw every Hebrew son into the river. The chapter ends with that decree hanging over the next generation, waiting for the son born in chapter 2.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 1 shows that God’s promises keep working in places that look hopeless. Israel is a foreign minority under a powerful king, yet the blessing spoken to Abraham keeps unfolding, and Pharaoh’s efforts only display it. The suffering is real and bitter, and Scripture does not soften it, but it is not the last word.

The chapter also honors ordinary faithfulness. The king of Egypt is never named, while two midwives are remembered by name. Their fear of God outweighed their fear of Pharaoh, and they protected the lives he ordered them to take. God noticed, and He built households for the women who saved Israel’s households.

Key Themes to Watch For

God’s promise of fruitfulness. Israel’s growth fulfills God’s word to Abraham and continues even under oppression (1:7, 12, 20).

Pharaoh against God’s purpose. Forced labor and secret killing both fail to stop what God is doing, and the chapter ends with Pharaoh’s public decree still in force (1:10–14, 16, 22).

The fear of God. The midwives’ reverence for God leads them to refuse the king’s command, and God rewards them (1:17, 21).

Bondage and bitter service. Israel is forced to serve Pharaoh “with rigor,” the first chapter in the book’s movement from slavery to the service of God (1:13–14).

Key Words & Concepts

“Who did not know Joseph” (1:8). The new king did not acknowledge or honor what Joseph had done for Egypt, so Israel lost the protection his memory had given them.

Pithom and Raamses (1:11). Supply cities Israel was forced to build for Pharaoh, places for storing grain and goods.

Shiphrah and Puah (1:15). The Hebrew midwives who refused Pharaoh’s order. Exodus records their answer to Pharaoh without commending it; what God honors is their fear of Him and their protection of life.

Households (1:21). God gave the midwives families and a lasting legacy of their own because they protected Israel’s families.
