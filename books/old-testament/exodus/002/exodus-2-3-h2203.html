PITCH - ( chemar , Strong's H2203 )

Summary:

In Exodus 2:3, the word "pitch" translates a Hebrew term referring to asphalt, bitumen, or mineral pitch. This sticky, petroleum-based substance was utilized in the ancient world as a reliable waterproofing agent, perfectly serving Jochebed's desperate and faithful act of sealing the papyrus basket to preserve the infant Moses upon the waters of the Nile.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The Hebrew term chemar evokes the bubbling, tar-like petroleum deposits found naturally in the Middle East, particularly around the Dead Sea region.

• Ancient peoples immediately recognized this dark, viscous substance for its impermeable qualities, making it the premier material for sealing boats, water vessels, and roofing materials against moisture.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The noun chemar specifically denotes bitumen, asphalt, or mineral pitch.

• It is etymologically related to a root meaning to boil, foam, or ferment, likely describing the appearance or processing of the bubbling, tar-like substance as it seeps from the earth or is heated for application.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The application of pitch creates an absolute barrier against the encroaching waters of the Nile, transforming a fragile ark of bulrushes into a seaworthy vessel.

• This practical use mirrors divine preservation; the same substance used to waterproof massive engineering feats in antiquity is here enlisted in a mother's humble, courageous act of faith.

4. Theological Implications

• The sealing of the ark with pitch quietly echoes the deliverance of Noah's ark, which was similarly coated inside and out with pitch (Hebrew kopher, Genesis 6:14) to shield it from destructive waters.

• Water in Scripture frequently symbolizes chaos, judgment, and death; the pitch-sealed vessel represents divine rescue, carrying the future deliverer safely through the deadly floods of tyranny.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Exodus 2:3, Jochebed uses both tar/pitch (chemar) and tree resin/slime (hochemar or related adhesive terms, paired with asphalt) to ensure the papyrus basket is thoroughly watertight.

• The physical measure taken by the mother—applying sticky, impenetrable pitch—highlights the blend of human diligence and absolute trust in God as she commits her child to the river.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 11:3 - "Then they said to one another, 'Come, let us make bricks and bake them thoroughly.' They had brick for stone, and they had asphalt [chemar] for mortar."

• Genesis 14:10 - "Now the Valley of Siddim was full of asphalt [chemar] pits; and the kings of Sodom and Gomorrah fled..."

• Isaiah 34:9 - "Its streams shall be turned into pitch [chemar], and its dust into sulfur; its land shall become burning pitch."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• Bitumen was widely traded and valued in the ancient Near East not only as an adhesive and sealant, but also in mummification processes and medicine.

• The contrast between the deadly waters ordered by Pharaoh to drown Hebrew boys and the waterproofed basket that saves Moses emphasizes the futility of human opposition against God's sovereign purposes.
