Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 2
Main Idea:

God preserves the deliverer His people will need, drawing Moses out of the very river meant for his death, and when Israel cries out under bondage, He hears, remembers His covenant, and sees them.

Setting:

Exodus 2 answers Pharaoh’s decree that every Hebrew son be thrown into the river (1:22). It follows Moses from his birth in a Levite home to Pharaoh’s palace and into exile in Midian.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 2:1–10 — Moses Is Hidden, Drawn from the River, and Raised as Pharaoh’s Daughter’s Son

  • Exodus 2:11–15 — Moses Kills an Egyptian and Flees from Pharaoh

  • Exodus 2:16–22 — Moses Finds a Home in Midian

  • Exodus 2:23–25 — God Hears Israel’s Groaning and Remembers His Covenant

Key Verse:

“So God heard their groaning, and God remembered His covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob. And God looked upon the children of Israel, and God acknowledged them.” (Exodus 2:24–25)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Pharaoh had ordered every Hebrew son thrown into the river, and Exodus 2 begins at that river. A Levite mother hides her baby, then places him in a small ark of bulrushes among the reeds. His sister watches, and Pharaoh’s own daughter opens the basket, sees the child weeping, and has compassion on him. She even pays his mother to nurse him. God works through a mother’s faith, a sister’s courage, and a foreign princess’s pity. The child is named Moses, “Because I drew him out of the water” (2:10), and one day God will use him to draw Israel out through the sea.

The grown Moses tries to rescue his people on his own. He kills an Egyptian, his own people reject him, and he flees to Midian, where he names his son Gershom because he has become “a stranger in a foreign land” (2:22). Then the story turns from Moses to God. Four short statements close the chapter: God heard, God remembered, God looked, and God acknowledged. Israel’s deliverance begins with what God hears and remembers, not with what Moses attempts.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 2:1–10 — Moses Is Hidden, Drawn from the River, and Raised as Pharaoh’s Daughter’s Son

A Levite mother hides her son, then lays him in an ark of bulrushes among the reeds by the river’s bank while his sister watches from a distance. Pharaoh’s daughter has compassion on the child, pays his own mother to nurse him, and names him Moses, “Because I drew him out of the water” (2:10).

Exodus 2:11–15 — Moses Kills an Egyptian and Flees from Pharaoh

Grown to manhood, Moses sides with his oppressed brethren, kills an Egyptian, and hides the body. When a Hebrew rejects him with “Who made you a prince and a judge over us?” (2:14) and Pharaoh seeks his life, Moses flees to Midian.

Exodus 2:16–22 — Moses Finds a Home in Midian

At a well in Midian Moses defends seven sisters from shepherds and waters their flock. Their father Reuel welcomes him, Moses marries Zipporah, and he names his son Gershom as a stranger in a foreign land.

Exodus 2:23–25 — God Hears Israel’s Groaning and Remembers His Covenant

The king of Egypt dies, but Israel’s bondage goes on and their cry rises to God. God hears their groaning, remembers His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, looks upon His people, and acknowledges them.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 2 shows God at work before anyone can see Him. His name is not mentioned until the last three verses, yet His care runs through every scene: the hiding, the basket, the princess’s pity, and the nursing wages. Hebrews 11:23 credits Moses’ parents with faith, and the chapter shows how God answers faith through unexpected people.

The chapter also separates zeal from calling. Moses cared about his people, but his own attempt to deliver them ended in killing, rejection, and exile. The commission to confront Pharaoh comes from the LORD at Horeb (3:10). Before Israel sees any rescue, God has already heard their cry and turned to act on His promises.

Key Themes to Watch For

God’s hidden providence. God preserves the future deliverer through faith, watchfulness, and compassion in the very place Pharaoh meant for death (2:2–10).

Identifying with God’s people. Moses, raised in Pharaoh’s house, goes out to his brethren and sees their burdens (2:11; compare Hebrews 11:24–26).

Exile and waiting. Moses becomes a stranger in a foreign land, and the long years in Midian prepare for God’s call (2:15–22).

God hears and remembers. God turns to act on His covenant when His people cry out (2:23–25).

Key Words & Concepts

Ark of bulrushes (2:3). A small basket made of papyrus reeds and sealed with asphalt and pitch. The Hebrew word translated “ark” is the same word used for Noah’s ark, a vessel of rescue.

Moses (2:10). Pharaoh’s daughter names him because she “drew him out of the water.” The child drawn from the Nile will later lead Israel out through the sea.

Reuel (2:18). Moses’ father-in-law, the priest of Midian. He is called Jethro in chapter 3, and the two names refer to the same man.

Remembered His covenant (2:24). For God to remember does not mean He had forgotten. It means He now turns to act on the promises He made to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.
