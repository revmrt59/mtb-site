FLAME - ( lababah , Strong's H3827 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word lababah describes a blazing flame, specifically used in Exodus 3:2 to depict the miraculous, non-consuming fire of God within the burning bush that arrested Moses' attention.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• Rooted in a primitive root associated with turning, twisting, or whirling, lababah vividly captures the dynamic, flickering movement of a tongue of fire leaping upward into the air.

• In ancient Near Eastern pastoral culture, fire in the wilderness was both a vital tool for warmth and a terrifying hazard that could instantly devastate grazing lands, making a fire that burned brightly without consuming the bush an immediate and arresting anomaly.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The noun refers strictly to a flame, a blaze, or the bright tongue of fire produced by burning fuel.

• It denotes visible, radiant combustion rather than invisible heat or smoke, focusing the reader's attention on the bright, luminous manifestation of fire.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The term paints a striking visual picture of leaping, twisting tongues of fire licking upward from a source of combustion.

• While fire in Scripture often symbolizes judgment, testing, or purification, the application here emphasizes divine presence and holiness that sustains rather than destroys.

4. Theological Implications

• The appearance of the flame in the bush marks a profound moment of divine self-disclosure, where God meets humanity on holy ground.

• The unconsumed bush illustrates that the consuming holiness of God can dwell in the midst of a frail, fallen creation without destroying it, prefiguring the incarnation and the indwelling Holy Spirit.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Exodus 3:2, the lababah breaks forth out of the midst of a common thornbush in the desert wilderness of Horeb.

• The focus is on the supernatural character of the flame: the bush was burning with fire, yet the bush was not consumed, compelling Moses to turn aside and witness the great sight that initiates his prophetic and pastoral calling.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Judges 13:20 - "For it happened when the flame went up toward heaven from the altar—that the Angel of the Lord ascended in the flame of the altar!"

• Isaiah 29:6 - "You will be punished by the Lord of hosts with thunder and earthquake and great noise, with storm and tempest and the flame of devouring fire."

• Hosea 7:6 - "For they prepare their heart like an oven, while they lie in wait; their baker sleeps all night; in the morning it burns like a blazing flame."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The surrounding context of Exodus 3 emphasizes sight and hearing; God calls to Moses out of the midst of the burning lababah, demanding reverence (removing sandals) because the place is holy.

• Ancient readers would immediately connect the imagery of fire with the manifest presence of God, looking ahead to the pillar of fire in the wilderness.
