GOOD - ( tob , Strong's H2896 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word tob in Exodus 3:8 describes a land characterized by beauty, abundance, fruitfulness, and divine favor, presenting a stark contrast to the harsh slavery of Egypt by offering a place where God's people can truly flourish.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root idea of tob stretches across a wide semantic range in the Old Testament, fundamentally denoting anything that is pleasant, agreeable, beneficial, or morally excellent.

• In ancient agrarian contexts, a "good" land was not merely a moral concept, but a deeply practical reality of water, soil, pasture, and harvest.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• As an adjective, tob means good, pleasant, agreeable, happy, prosperous, or best.

• It frequently describes physical objects that are pleasing to the sight or functionally excellent, as well as situations that bring well-being and favor.

• In relation to land or geography, it signifies high quality, fertility, and the capacity to sustain abundant life.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Genesis creation account repeatedly uses tob to describe God's work, where each part of creation is declared "good" because it fulfills its divine purpose and reflects God's design.

• When applied to the Promised Land in Exodus 3:8, the word paints a vivid picture of a rich, flowing landscape that stands ready to nourish, shelter, and satisfy.

4. Theological Implications

• The goodness of the land in Exodus 3:8 is directly tied to the character and promise of God; He is not only rescuing His people out of oppressive bondage, but bringing them into a provision that mirrors His own generous nature.

• Physical provision in Scripture often serves as a foretaste of spiritual redemption, showing that God cares comprehensively for the bodily and spiritual needs of His people.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Exodus 3:8, God tells Moses He has come down to deliver Israel and bring them "to a good and large land, to a land flowing with milk and honey."

• Here, tob contrasts sharply with the bitter toil of Egypt, emphasizing the qualitative difference between Pharaoh's slave labor camps and the freedom and abundance of God's inheritance.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 1:4 - "And God saw the light, that it was good..."

• Deuteronomy 8:7 - "For the Lord your God is bringing you into a good land, a land of brooks of water, of fountains and springs, that flow out of valleys and hills..."

• Psalm 34:8 - "Oh, taste and see that the Lord is good; blessed is the man who trusts in Him!"

• Psalm 119:68 - "You are good, and do good; teach me Your statutes."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The pairing of "good" with "flowing with milk and honey" is a stock biblical idiom for pastoral and agricultural prosperity, combining the wealth of livestock (milk) with the sweetness of gathered harvest (honey).

• The transition from Egypt to the "good land" highlights the redemptive pattern of the Exodus: rescue from death and misery into life and flourishing.
