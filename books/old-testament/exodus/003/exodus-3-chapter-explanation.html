Exodus 3

Verse-by-Verse
Exodus 3:1
Exodus 3:1
NKJV

Exodus 3:1 Now Moses was tending the flock of Jethro his father-in-law, the priest of Midian. And he led the flock to the back of the desert, and came to Horeb, the mountain of God.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 3:1 One day Moses was tending the flock of his father-in-law, Jethro, the priest of Midian. He led the flock far into the wilderness and came to Sinai, the mountain of God. (NLT)

Main Idea

Moses is tending the flock of Jethro his father-in-law, the priest of Midian, when he leads it to the back of the desert and comes to Horeb, “the mountain of God.” In the middle of ordinary shepherd’s work, Moses arrives at the place where God will meet him.

Verse Explanation

Moses is tending the flock of Jethro his father-in-law, the priest of Midian, when he leads it to the back of the desert and comes to Horeb, “the mountain of God.” In the middle of ordinary shepherd’s work, Moses arrives at the place where God will meet him.

“Now Moses was tending the flock of Jethro his father-in-law, the priest of Midian.”

Moses, once a prince of Egypt, is now a shepherd. He “was tending the flock of Jethro his father-in-law.” The flock was not even his own. Jethro is the same man called Reuel in 2:18. Forty years have passed since Moses fled Egypt (Acts 7:30).

“And he led the flock to the back of the desert,”

The back of the desert means the far side of the wilderness, beyond the usual grazing areas. Shepherds moved their flocks in search of grass and water.

“and came to Horeb, the mountain of God.”

Horeb is another name for Mount Sinai, where God would later give the law (19:11; Deuteronomy 4:10, 15). Moses calls it “the mountain of God” because of what happened there. In the middle of ordinary shepherd’s work, Moses arrives at the place where God will meet him.

Study Notes

The location of Mount Sinai is debated. The traditional site is Jebel Musa in the southern Sinai Peninsula. Other proposed locations include sites in northwestern Arabia.

Exodus 3:2
Exodus 3:2
NKJV

Exodus 3:2 And the Angel of the LORD appeared to him in a flame of fire from the midst of a bush. So he looked, and behold, the bush was burning with fire, but the bush was not consumed.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 3:2 There the angel of the LORD appeared to him in a blazing fire from the middle of a bush. Moses stared in amazement. Though the bush was engulfed in flames, it didn’t burn up. (NLT)

Main Idea

The Angel of the LORD appears to Moses in a flame of fire from the midst of a bush, and the bush burns but “was not consumed.” The next verses identify the Angel as the LORD Himself, and His appearance here is best read as Christ before His incarnation.

Verse Explanation

The Angel of the LORD appears to Moses in a flame of fire from the midst of a bush, and the bush burns but “was not consumed.” The next verses identify the Angel as the LORD Himself, and His appearance here is best read as Christ before His incarnation.

“And the Angel of the LORD appeared to him in a flame of fire from the midst of a bush.”

“The Angel of the LORD appeared to him.” The word “Angel” means messenger, one sent. Yet as the scene unfolds, the One who appears is identified as the LORD Himself. In 3:4 “God called to him from the midst of the bush,” and in 3:6 He says, “I am the God of your father.” The Angel of the LORD speaks as God and is God. This appearance is best read as Christ before His incarnation, the Son making the Father known (John 1:18).

He appeared “in a flame of fire from the midst of a bush.” Fire often signals God’s presence in Scripture. It speaks of His holiness, His power, and His purifying judgment. Later God would lead Israel by a pillar of fire (13:21) and descend on Sinai in fire (19:18).

“So he looked, and behold, the bush was burning with fire, but the bush was not consumed.”

Moses “looked,” and what he saw was strange: “the bush was burning with fire, but the bush was not consumed.” A dry desert bush should have burned up quickly. This one kept burning without being destroyed. The fire did not depend on the bush for fuel. The holy God was present, drawing near without consuming the one He calls.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word for “bush” sounds similar to the name Sinai. The bush was most likely a thorny desert shrub.

The Angel of the LORD appears elsewhere in the Old Testament as One who speaks as God (for example, Genesis 16:7–13; 22:11–18; Judges 13:18–22).

Exodus 3:4
Exodus 3:4
NKJV

Exodus 3:4 So when the LORD saw that he turned aside to look, God called to him from the midst of the bush and said, "Moses, Moses!" And he said, "Here I am."(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 3:4 When the LORD saw Moses coming to take a closer look, God called to him from the middle of the bush, “Moses! Moses!” “Here I am!” Moses replied. (NLT)

Main Idea

When the LORD sees Moses turn aside, God calls to him from the midst of the bush, “Moses, Moses!” and Moses answers, “Here I am.” The God who drew Moses’ attention now calls him personally by name.

Verse Explanation

When the LORD sees Moses turn aside, God calls to him from the midst of the bush, “Moses, Moses!” and Moses answers, “Here I am.” The God who drew Moses’ attention now calls him personally by name.

“So when the LORD saw that he turned aside to look,”

“When the LORD saw that he turned aside.” God watched Moses’ response. He waited until Moses turned aside before He spoke.

“God called to him from the midst of the bush and said, ‘Moses, Moses!”

“God called to him from the midst of the bush.” The speaker is now named “the LORD” and “God.” He called Moses by name, twice: “Moses, Moses!” Calling a name twice expressed urgency and closeness. God called Abraham the same way (Genesis 22:11), and later Samuel (1 Samuel 3:10).

“And he said, ‘Here I am.”

Moses answered, “Here I am.” It is the response of a servant ready to listen. The God who drew Moses’ attention now calls him personally by name.

Study Notes

The verse uses both “the LORD” (the divine name YHWH) and “God,” showing that the Angel of 3:2 is the LORD Himself.

Exodus 3:5
NKJV

Exodus 3:5 Then He said, "Do not draw near this place. Take your sandals off your feet, for the place where you stand is holy ground."(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 3:5 “Do not come any closer,” the LORD warned. “Take off your sandals, for you are standing on holy ground. (NLT)

Main Idea

God tells Moses not to come near and to take off his sandals, “for the place where you stand is holy ground.” The ground is holy because the holy God is present, and Moses must approach Him with reverence.

Verse Explanation

God tells Moses not to come near and to take off his sandals, “for the place where you stand is holy ground.” The ground is holy because the holy God is present, and Moses must approach Him with reverence.

“Then He said, ‘Do not draw near this place.”

God’s first words set a boundary. “Do not draw near this place.” Moses had approached out of curiosity. Now he learns that he stands before the holy God. He cannot treat this as an interesting sight to examine.

“Take your sandals off your feet, for the place where you stand is holy ground.”

“Take your sandals off your feet.” Removing sandals was a sign of reverence and humility. The reason is “the place where you stand is holy ground.” The ground was not holy in itself. It was holy because the holy God was present. Moses must approach Him with reverence.

Study Notes

Joshua received the same command from “the Commander of the LORD’s army” (Joshua 5:13–15).

This is the first use of the word “holy” in Exodus. Holiness will become a major theme in the book (for example, 15:11; 19:6; 28:36).

Verse Explanation

God identifies Himself as the God of Moses’ father and the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and Moses hides his face, afraid to look upon God. The One speaking from the bush is the same God who made promises to the patriarchs.

“Moreover He said, ‘I am the God of your father—the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.”

God identified Himself: “I am the God of your father.” He was the God Moses’ own father had worshiped. He is also “the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.” The One speaking from the bush is the same God who made promises to the patriarchs. He has not forgotten them.

Jesus later quoted this verse to show that the dead live before God: He is “not the God of the dead, but the God of the living” (Mark 12:26–27).

“And Moses hid his face, for he was afraid to look upon God.”

He understood that he stood in the presence of the holy God, and he was overwhelmed. Sinful people cannot look on God’s holiness casually.

Exodus 3:7
NKJV

Exodus 3:7 And the LORD said: "I have surely seen the oppression of My people who are in Egypt, and have heard their cry because of their taskmasters, for I know their sorrows.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 3:7 Then the LORD told him, “I have certainly seen the oppression of my people in Egypt. I have heard their cries of distress because of their harsh slave drivers. Yes, I am aware of their suffering. (NLT)

Main Idea

The LORD says, “I have surely seen the oppression of My people,” heard their cry, and knows their sorrows. He calls Israel “My people” and shows that their suffering has not escaped His notice.

Verse Explanation

The LORD says, “I have surely seen the oppression of My people,” heard their cry, and knows their sorrows. He calls Israel “My people” and shows that their suffering has not escaped His notice.

“And the LORD said: ‘I have surely seen the oppression of My people who are in Egypt,”

“I have surely seen.” The Hebrew repeats the verb for emphasis: God has truly seen. He has seen “the oppression of My people.” God calls Israel “My people.” They belong to Him. Their suffering has not escaped His notice.

“and have heard their cry because of their taskmasters,”

God has heard what 2:23–24 described.

“for I know their sorrows.”

God does not merely observe from a distance. The word “know” speaks of personal, intimate knowledge. He knows their sorrows fully, and He cares about them as the grief of His own people.

Study Notes

The verbs in 3:7 echo 2:24–25, where God heard, remembered, looked, and acknowledged. The LORD now tells Moses directly what the narrator described.

Exodus 3:8
Exodus 3:8
NKJV

Exodus 3:8 So I have come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians, and to bring them up from that land to a good and large land, to a land flowing with milk and honey, to the place of the Canaanites and the Hittites and the Amorites and the Perizzites and the Hivites and the Jebusites.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 3:8 So I have come down to rescue them from the power of the Egyptians and lead them out of Egypt into their own fertile and spacious land. It is a land flowing with milk and honey — the land where the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites, and Jebusites now live. (NLT)

Main Idea

The LORD says, “I have come down to deliver them” from the Egyptians and to bring them to a good and large land flowing with milk and honey. His purpose includes both rescue from Egypt and the gift of the land promised to the fathers.

Verse Explanation

The LORD says, “I have come down to deliver them” from the Egyptians and to bring them to a good and large land flowing with milk and honey. His purpose includes both rescue from Egypt and the gift of the land promised to the fathers.

“So I have come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians,”

“I have come down.” God does not stay distant. He comes down to act. His purpose is “to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians.” The God who sees and hears also rescues.

“and to bring them up from that land to a good and large land, to a land flowing with milk and honey,”

God’s purpose goes further than rescue. He will “bring them up from that land to a good and large land.” He will give them a home. The land is described as “flowing with milk and honey.” Milk came from flocks and herds grazing on rich pasture, and honey from bees among abundant flowers. The phrase pictures a fertile, abundant land.

“to the place of the Canaanites and the Hittites and the Amorites and the Perizzites and the Hivites and the Jebusites.”

The land is “the place of the Canaanites” and other peoples. God names the nations then living there. The land had been promised to Abraham’s descendants (Genesis 15:18–21). His purpose includes both rescue from Egypt and the gift of the land promised to the fathers.

Study Notes

“A land flowing with milk and honey” appears here for the first time. It becomes the standard description of the promised land in the Pentateuch (for example, 3:17; 13:5; 33:3).

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

God answers, “I will certainly be with you,” and gives a sign: when Moses has brought the people out, they will serve God on this mountain. The answer to Moses’ weakness is God’s own presence, and the goal of the rescue is worship.

“So He said, ‘I will certainly be with you.”

God did not answer Moses’ question by telling him who Moses was. He told Moses who would be with him: “I will certainly be with you.” The answer to Moses’ weakness is God’s own presence. Moses did not need to be adequate. God would be with him.

“And this shall be a sign to you that I have sent you:”

God gave “a sign” to confirm that He had sent Moses.

“When you have brought the people out of Egypt, you shall serve God on this mountain.”

The sign would come after Moses obeyed. Moses would have to trust God first, and the confirmation would follow. The goal of the rescue is worship. God would bring Israel to this same mountain to serve Him, which He did at Sinai (19:1–6).

Study Notes

The Hebrew words behind “I will certainly be with you” use the same verb “to be” that appears in God’s name in 3:14, “I AM.”

The verb translated “serve” is the same verb used for Israel’s forced labor in 1:13–14. Israel will move from serving Pharaoh to serving God.

Exodus 3:14
Exodus 3:14
NKJV

Exodus 3:14 And God said to Moses, "I AM WHO I AM." And He said, "Thus you shall say to the children of Israel, "˜I AM has sent me to you.' "(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 3:14 God replied to Moses, “I AM WHO I AM. Say this to the people of Israel: I AM has sent me to you.” (NLT)

Main Idea

God answers, “I AM WHO I AM,” and tells Moses to say, “I AM has sent me to you.” The name declares the God who exists in Himself and who is truly present with His people to deliver them.

Verse Explanation

God answers, “I AM WHO I AM,” and tells Moses to say, “I AM has sent me to you.” The name declares the God who exists in Himself and who is truly present with His people to deliver them.

“And God said to Moses, ‘I AM WHO I AM.”

God answered, “I AM WHO I AM.” This is God’s self-declaration. He is the God who exists in Himself. He depends on no one and nothing. He always has been and always will be. He is not defined by anything outside Himself.

The name also speaks of His faithful presence. God has just said, “I will certainly be with you” (3:12). The God who exists in Himself is also the God who is truly present with His people to deliver them.

“And He said, ‘Thus you shall say to the children of Israel, ‘I AM has sent me to you.”

Moses is to tell Israel, “I AM has sent me to you.” The self-existent God is the One sending Moses.

The name also answers a question: I am what? As Exodus unfolds, God shows what He is for His people. He is the LORD who heals (15:26), who provides (16:4), and who is Israel’s banner (17:15). Jesus took up this name when He said, “before Abraham was, I AM” (John 8:58). His “I am” sayings fill out the same name: “I am the bread of life” (John 6:35), “I am the light of the world” (John 8:12), “I am the good shepherd” (John 10:11).

Study Notes

The Hebrew phrase translated “I AM WHO I AM” can also be translated I will be what I will be. The verb form allows both present and future meanings. The exact derivation of the divine name is debated.

God adds that Moses is to call Him the LORD God of their fathers, the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and declares, “This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations.” The God of the patriarchs gives His name as a lasting way for His people to know and remember Him.

“Moreover God said to Moses, ‘Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: ‘The LORD God of your fathers,”

God continued. Moses is to call Him “The LORD God of your fathers.” The name translated “LORD” (printed in small capitals in English Bibles) is the personal name of God, often written as YHWH. It is closely related to the verb “I AM” in 3:14. The God who said “I AM” is the LORD.

“the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you.”

“This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations.”

Study Notes

The divine name YHWH is printed “LORD” in the NKJV. Out of reverence, Jewish readers came to say “Lord” (Adonai) instead of pronouncing the name. The pronunciation “Yahweh” is a modern reconstruction.

NLT

Exodus 3:18 “The elders of Israel will accept your message. Then you and the elders must go to the king of Egypt and tell him, ‘The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, has met with us. So please let us take a three-day journey into the wilderness to offer sacrifices to the LORD, our God.’ (NLT)

Main Idea

God says the elders will heed Moses, and together they are to ask the king of Egypt to let Israel go three days’ journey into the wilderness to sacrifice to the LORD their God. The request centers on Israel’s freedom to worship their God.

Verse Explanation

God says the elders will heed Moses, and together they are to ask the king of Egypt to let Israel go three days’ journey into the wilderness to sacrifice to the LORD their God. The request centers on Israel’s freedom to worship their God.

“Then they will heed your voice;”

God assures Moses that the elders will listen. Moses’ fear in 4:1 that they will not believe him has already been answered here.

“and you shall come, you and the elders of Israel, to the king of Egypt;”

Moses and the elders together will go “to the king of Egypt.”

“and you shall say to him, ‘The LORD God of the Hebrews has met with us;”

They will tell Pharaoh, “The LORD God of the Hebrews has met with us.” They introduce their God by His relationship with the Hebrews.

“and now, please, let us go three days’ journey into the wilderness, that we may sacrifice to the LORD our God.”

The request is for a short journey to offer sacrifice. The request centers on Israel’s freedom to worship their God. It is a modest request that would test Pharaoh’s heart. If he would not allow even a short journey for worship, his opposition to God would be plain.

Study Notes

Interpreters discuss whether the request for three days was meant as the full demand or as a first step. God’s later words make clear that His purpose was Israel’s complete release (3:8; 6:6–8). The modest request exposed Pharaoh’s refusal to let Israel worship God at all.

Verse Explanation

God tells Moses that He knows the king of Egypt will not let them go, “no, not even by a mighty hand.” God prepares Moses in advance for Pharaoh’s stubborn refusal.

“But I am sure that the king of Egypt will not let you go, no, not even by a mighty hand.”

God prepared Moses for resistance. “I am sure that the king of Egypt will not let you go.” God knew Pharaoh would refuse. He would not yield “not even by a mighty hand,” unless he were forced by overwhelming power. God prepared Moses in advance for Pharaoh’s stubborn refusal.

Study Notes

The phrase “not even by a mighty hand” can also be translated unless by a mighty hand, and the Greek translation reads that way. Either way, Pharaoh will yield only to God’s power.

Exodus 3:22 But every woman shall ask of her neighbor, namely, of her who dwells near her house, articles of silver, articles of gold, and clothing; and you shall put them on your sons and on your daughters. So you shall plunder the Egyptians."(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 3:22 Every Israelite woman will ask for articles of silver and gold and fine clothing from her Egyptian neighbors and from the foreign women in their houses. You will dress your sons and daughters with these, stripping the Egyptians of their wealth.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Every woman is to ask her neighbor for articles of silver, gold, and clothing to put on their sons and daughters, and so Israel will plunder the Egyptians. The Egyptians will give these things in response to Israel’s request, as God promised Abraham that his descendants would come out with great possessions.

Verse Explanation

Every woman is to ask her neighbor for articles of silver, gold, and clothing to put on their sons and daughters, and so Israel will plunder the Egyptians. The Egyptians will give these things in response to Israel’s request, as God promised Abraham that his descendants would come out with great possessions.

“But every woman shall ask of her neighbor, namely, of her who dwells near her house, articles of silver, articles of gold, and clothing;”

God gave instructions. “Every woman shall ask of her neighbor.” The Israelite women would ask the Egyptian women living near them for “articles of silver, articles of gold, and clothing.” The word “ask” means to request.

“and you shall put them on your sons and on your daughters.”

These gifts would be put “on your sons and on your daughters.” Israel’s children would wear what Egypt gave.

“So you shall plunder the Egyptians.”

The word “plunder” usually describes taking spoil after a victory. Here the Egyptians will give these things willingly in response to Israel’s request (12:35–36). God will give Israel the spoil of a victory won by His hand alone. This also fulfilled God’s promise to Abraham that his descendants would come out “with great possessions” (Genesis 15:14), and it served as partial recompense for generations of unpaid labor. It gives no precedent for taking from others.

Study Notes

Exodus 12:35–36 records the fulfillment: “the LORD had given the people favor in the sight of the Egyptians, so that they granted them what they requested. Thus they plundered the Egyptians.”

Exodus 35:22 records men and women bringing earrings, nose rings, rings, necklaces, and jewelry of gold for the tabernacle. That verse does not say where the individual articles came from. Some may have been among the things Israel received from the Egyptians, but Exodus gives no such inventory.
