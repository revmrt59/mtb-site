Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 3
Main Idea:

At the burning bush the LORD reveals that He has seen His people’s suffering and come down to deliver them, sends a reluctant Moses with the promise “I will certainly be with you,” and makes Himself known by His name, “I AM WHO I AM.”

Setting:

After long years as a shepherd in Midian, Moses comes to Horeb, “the mountain of God” (3:1), just after God has heard Israel’s groaning and remembered His covenant (2:23–25).

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 3:1–6 — The Angel of the LORD Appears in the Burning Bush

  • Exodus 3:7–12 — The LORD Sends Moses with the Promise of His Presence

  • Exodus 3:13–15 — God Reveals His Name

  • Exodus 3:16–22 — God Foretells Israel’s Belief, Pharaoh’s Refusal, and the Departure

Key Verse:

“And God said to Moses, ‘I AM WHO I AM.’ And He said, ‘Thus you shall say to the children of Israel, “I AM has sent me to you.” ’” (Exodus 3:14)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Moses is doing ordinary work, leading Jethro’s sheep at the back of the desert, when he sees a bush that burns “but the bush was not consumed” (3:2). He turns aside to look, and God calls him by name. The Angel of the LORD appears in the flame (3:2), and then the One who calls from the bush is the LORD Himself, the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob (3:4–6). The Angel who appears is the LORD who speaks: this is an appearance of Christ before His incarnation. The ground is holy because God is there, and Moses hides his face.

Then the LORD tells Moses what His remembering means: “I have surely seen the oppression of My people” (3:7), and “I have come down to deliver them” (3:8). He sends Moses, and when Moses protests, “Who am I,” God does not list Moses’ qualifications. He promises His presence: “I will certainly be with you” (3:12). When Moses asks God’s name, God answers, “I AM WHO I AM” (3:14). He is the God who exists in Himself, depends on nothing, and is truly present with His people to save them. The goal of the rescue is already named: the people will serve God on this very mountain.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 3:1–6 — The Angel of the LORD Appears in the Burning Bush

While tending Jethro’s flock at Horeb, Moses turns aside to see a bush that burns without being consumed. God calls him by name, tells him to remove his sandals on holy ground, and identifies Himself as the God of his fathers, and Moses hides his face in fear.

Exodus 3:7–12 — The LORD Sends Moses with the Promise of His Presence

The LORD has seen His people’s oppression and come down to deliver them and bring them into a good land, and He sends Moses to Pharaoh. To Moses’ “Who am I,” God answers with His presence and the sign that Israel will worship Him on this mountain.

Exodus 3:13–15 — God Reveals His Name

Moses asks what name to give the people, and God answers, “I AM WHO I AM.” He sends Moses in the name of the LORD God of their fathers and declares this to be His name forever.

Exodus 3:16–22 — God Foretells Israel’s Belief, Pharaoh’s Refusal, and the Departure

God tells Moses that the elders will listen and that Pharaoh will refuse until God strikes Egypt with His wonders. He promises Israel favor, so that the Egyptians will give them silver, gold, and clothing and they will not leave empty-handed.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 3 shows that God’s answer to human weakness is Himself. Moses felt inadequate, and he was, yet God never argued him into self-confidence. He promised to go with him. The same pattern runs through the whole book, until Moses himself prays that God’s Presence will go with Israel (33:15).

The chapter also gives Israel the name by which they will know and call on their God. “I AM” speaks of the God who needs nothing and yet chooses to be present for His people. As Exodus unfolds He fills out that name by what He does for them: He heals, provides, and fights for them. Jesus takes up the same words when He says, “before Abraham was, I AM” (John 8:58).

Key Themes to Watch For

God’s holy presence. The bush burns without being consumed, and the ground is holy because God is there (3:2–5).

God who sees and comes down. The LORD sees, hears, and knows His people’s suffering and comes down to deliver them (3:7–9).

Presence as the answer to weakness. God meets Moses’ “Who am I” with “I will certainly be with you” (3:11–12).

The name of God. God reveals Himself as “I AM WHO I AM” and as the LORD God of the fathers, His name forever (3:14–15).

Rescue leading to worship. The people are brought out so that they may serve God on His mountain and sacrifice to Him (3:12, 18).

Key Words & Concepts

Horeb (3:1). Another name for Mount Sinai, “the mountain of God,” where Israel will later receive the covenant.

The Angel of the LORD (3:2). The divine messenger who appears in the flame and speaks as the LORD Himself (3:4–6). Because the Angel who appears is the LORD who speaks, the bush appearance is Christ before His incarnation. Jesus later takes the name “I AM” for Himself (John 8:58).

Holy ground (3:5). Ground set apart by God’s presence. Moses removes his sandals as a sign of reverence.

I AM WHO I AM (3:14). God’s self-revealed name, closely tied to the name translated “the LORD” in verse 15. It declares that God exists in Himself and is faithfully present with His people. The exact derivation of the name is debated.

Plunder the Egyptians (3:22). Israel will ask their neighbors for silver, gold, and clothing, and the Egyptians will give them, as God gives Israel favor (12:35–36). It fulfills God’s promise that Abraham’s descendants would come out with great possessions (Genesis 15:14).
