“suppose they say, ‘The LORD has not appeared to you.”

“He said, ‘A rod.”

Moses answered, “A rod.” It was a shepherd’s staff, an ordinary tool for guiding sheep and leaning on while walking. God begins with the ordinary shepherd’s staff Moses already holds. Through it God would bring plagues on Egypt, part the sea, and bring water from the rock.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word for “rod” refers to a staff or stick. Shepherds used such staffs to guide and protect their flocks.

Moses raised a fourth objection. “O my Lord, I am not eloquent.” He did not consider himself a skilled speaker. The phrase reads literally not a man of words.

Then God warned Moses. God prepared Moses for Pharaoh’s refusal and declared that He would be at work even in it.

Study Notes

Several different Hebrew verbs are used for the hardening of Pharaoh’s heart in Exodus. The verb here means to make strong or firm.

Paul quotes 9:16 in Romans 9:17–18 in discussing God’s sovereign purposes.

“And it came to pass on the way, at the encampment,”

A strange and difficult event follows. “On the way,” during the journey to Egypt, “at the encampment,” the place where the family stopped for the night.

“that the LORD met him and sought to kill him.”

The man sent to lead God’s people faced God’s deadly confrontation. The reason becomes clear in the next verse: Moses had not circumcised his son as God commanded Abraham’s descendants (Genesis 17:10–14). The form the threat took is not described, but it is best understood as a sudden, life-threatening illness.

God had just declared Israel His firstborn and warned of death for Pharaoh’s firstborn. Now God confronts Moses, whose own son lacked the sign of belonging to God’s people. The man sent to lead Israel under God’s covenant could not neglect it in his own home.

Study Notes

The Hebrew text says “him” without naming Moses, and interpreters have debated whether the LORD sought to kill Moses or his son. The setting and the ending (“So He let him go,” 4:26) are most naturally read as referring to Moses.

The reason for the delay is not recorded. Possibilities include a recent birth (perhaps of Eliezer, 18:4) with the eighth day falling on the journey, neglect while Moses was busy with the mission, or Moses giving in to Zipporah, who seems from her words in 4:25 to have resisted the practice. Scripture does not say which.

Study Notes

The phrase “husband of blood” is difficult. It may reflect a connection between circumcision and marriage, or it may mean that Moses had been restored to her through blood. Interpreters have proposed several explanations, and none is certain.
