Exodus 4 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Exodus 4 completes the conversation at the bush as the LORD answers Moses’ remaining objections with signs, with His promise to be with his mouth, and with Aaron as his spokesman, then sends him back to Egypt. On the journey the LORD gives Moses His word for Pharaoh, confronts Moses over the circumcision of his son, and brings him to Aaron, and the chapter ends with Israel believing and worshiping.

Chapter Sections

1. Exodus 4:1–9 — The LORD Gives Moses Signs for the People

When Moses fears that the people will not believe him, the LORD gives him three signs: the rod that becomes a serpent, the hand made leprous and restored, and water from the river that will become blood. These signs are given so “that they may believe that the LORD God of their fathers” (Exodus 4:5) has appeared to Moses.

2. Exodus 4:10–17 — The Maker of Man’s Mouth Appoints Aaron as Spokesman

Moses pleads that he is slow of speech, and the LORD answers, “Who has made man’s mouth?” (Exodus 4:11), promising to be with his mouth. When Moses asks Him to send someone else, the LORD’s anger is kindled, yet He appoints Aaron as Moses’ spokesman and tells Moses to take the rod with which he will do the signs.

3. Exodus 4:18–23 — Moses Returns to Egypt with God’s Word for Pharaoh

Jethro sends Moses away in peace, and Moses sets out for Egypt with his wife, his sons, and “the rod of God” (Exodus 4:20). The LORD tells him that He will harden Pharaoh’s heart and gives him the message, “Israel is My son, My firstborn” (Exodus 4:22), with the warning that Pharaoh’s own firstborn will die if he refuses to let God’s son go.

4. Exodus 4:24–26 — The LORD Confronts Moses over His Son’s Circumcision

At the encampment on the way, “the LORD met him and sought to kill him” (Exodus 4:24). Zipporah circumcises their son, and the LORD lets Moses go.

5. Exodus 4:27–31 — Aaron Meets Moses and the People Believe

The LORD sends Aaron to meet Moses at the mountain of God, and Moses tells him all the LORD’s words and signs. In Egypt Aaron speaks to the elders and the signs are done before the people, who believe and bow their heads in worship when they hear that the LORD has visited them.
