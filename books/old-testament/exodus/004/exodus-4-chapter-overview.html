Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 4
Main Idea:

The LORD answers every one of Moses’ objections with His own power and presence, sends him back to Egypt with Aaron and with His claim on Israel as His firstborn son, and brings His people to believe and worship.

Setting:

Exodus 4 continues the conversation at the burning bush (3:1–22). Moses then leaves Midian for Egypt with his family, and the chapter ends among Israel’s elders.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 4:1–9 — The LORD Gives Moses Signs for the People

  • Exodus 4:10–17 — The Maker of Man’s Mouth Appoints Aaron as Spokesman

  • Exodus 4:18–23 — Moses Returns to Egypt with God’s Word for Pharaoh

  • Exodus 4:24–26 — The LORD Confronts Moses over His Son’s Circumcision

  • Exodus 4:27–31 — Aaron Meets Moses and the People Believe

Key Verse:

“So the people believed; and when they heard that the LORD had visited the children of Israel and that He had looked on their affliction, then they bowed their heads and worshiped.” (Exodus 4:31)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Moses keeps finding reasons not to go. What if the people will not believe him? God gives him three signs, beginning with the shepherd’s rod already in his hand. What if he cannot speak? “Who has made man’s mouth?” (4:11), the LORD answers, and He promises, “I will be with your mouth and teach you what you shall say” (4:12). God is patient with honest weakness. His anger comes only when Moses asks Him to send someone else, and even then He gives Moses Aaron as his spokesman.

On the road God gives Moses the heart of the message: “Israel is My son, My firstborn” (4:22), and “let My son go that he may serve Me” (4:23). Pharaoh’s oppression is an assault on God’s own child, and refusing to release him will cost Pharaoh his firstborn. Then, on the way, the LORD confronts Moses himself because Moses had not circumcised his son, and the danger ends only when Zipporah does it. The man sent to call Israel to obedience could not neglect God’s command in his own home. The chapter closes as God intended: the people hear that the LORD has visited them, and they believe and worship.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 4:1–9 — The LORD Gives Moses Signs for the People

When Moses fears that Israel will not believe him, the LORD gives him three signs: the rod that becomes a serpent, the hand made leprous and healed, and river water that will turn to blood. The signs are meant to show that the God of their fathers has truly appeared to him.

Exodus 4:10–17 — The Maker of Man’s Mouth Appoints Aaron as Spokesman

Moses pleads that he is slow of speech, and the LORD reminds him that He made the human mouth and will teach him what to say. When Moses asks Him to send someone else, the LORD is angry, yet He appoints Aaron to speak for Moses.

Exodus 4:18–23 — Moses Returns to Egypt with God’s Word for Pharaoh

Jethro sends Moses away in peace, and Moses sets out for Egypt with his family and “the rod of God” (4:20). The LORD warns that He will harden Pharaoh’s heart and gives Moses His claim: Israel is His firstborn son, and refusing to release him will cost Pharaoh his own firstborn.

Exodus 4:24–26 — The LORD Confronts Moses over His Son’s Circumcision

At a lodging place on the journey, the LORD confronts Moses with deadly danger, most likely a sudden, life-threatening illness, because Moses had failed to circumcise his son as God commanded. Zipporah circumcises the boy, and the LORD releases Moses.

Exodus 4:27–31 — Aaron Meets Moses and the People Believe

God brings Aaron to meet Moses at the mountain of God, and together they gather Israel’s elders. Aaron speaks the LORD’s words, the signs are done, and the people believe and bow in worship because the LORD has seen their affliction.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 4 shows how God deals with a reluctant servant. He does not wait for Moses to feel ready. He answers fear with signs, weakness with promises, and refusal with firm anger mixed with provision. What God asks of Moses rests on what God Himself will do.

The chapter also reveals why the exodus happens. Israel is God’s firstborn son, and He wants His son free to serve Him. That claim frames every plague that follows and looks ahead to Jesus, God’s Son, called out of Egypt (Hosea 11:1; Matthew 2:15). The night on the road adds a sober note: God takes obedience seriously even in what looks small, and public calling does not excuse disobedience at home.

Key Themes to Watch For

Signs that confirm God’s word. The LORD gives signs so that the people will believe He has appeared to Moses (4:1–9, 30–31).

God’s sufficiency for human weakness. The Maker of the mouth promises to teach Moses what to say (4:10–12).

Israel as God’s firstborn son. God claims Israel as His son and demands his release for worship (4:22–23).

Obedience at home. God holds His servant accountable for the covenant sign in his own household (4:24–26).

Faith and worship. When the people hear that the LORD has visited them, they believe and worship (4:31).

Key Words & Concepts

The rod (4:2, 17, 20). Moses’ ordinary shepherd’s staff, which God uses for His signs and which is now called “the rod of God.”

Spokesman (4:16). Aaron speaks for Moses, and Moses is “as God” to him. The phrase describes their roles: Moses receives God’s word, and Aaron delivers it.

Harden his heart (4:21). God announces that He will harden Pharaoh’s heart. The coming chapters show both Pharaoh hardening himself and God hardening him, and Pharaoh remains responsible for his refusal.

Firstborn (4:22–23). The son with the first place in a family. Israel is God’s firstborn, so Pharaoh’s refusal to release him will be answered by the death of Pharaoh’s firstborn.

Husband of blood (4:25). Zipporah’s words after the circumcision. She recognizes that her husband’s life was spared and given back to her through the blood of their son’s circumcision.
