Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 5
Main Idea:

Pharaoh answers the LORD’s demand with “Who is the LORD, that I should obey His voice?” and crushes the call to worship under heavier labor, leaving Israel’s officers bitter and Moses pouring out his distress to the God who sent him.

Setting:

Exodus 5 follows directly on Israel’s worship in 4:31. Moses and Aaron now stand before Pharaoh for the first time, as the LORD had warned that Pharaoh would refuse (3:19; 4:21).

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 5:1–5 — Pharaoh Refuses to Know the LORD

  • Exodus 5:6–14 — Pharaoh Withholds the Straw and Increases the Burden

  • Exodus 5:15–21 — The Officers Appeal to Pharaoh and Blame Moses and Aaron

  • Exodus 5:22–23 — Moses Brings His Distress to the LORD

Key Verse:

“And Pharaoh said, ‘Who is the LORD, that I should obey His voice to let Israel go? I do not know the LORD, nor will I let Israel go.’” (Exodus 5:2)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Moses and Aaron bring the LORD’s word: “Let My people go, that they may hold a feast to Me in the wilderness” (5:1). The LORD claims Israel as His own and calls them to worship. Pharaoh’s reply sets the question the next ten chapters will answer: “Who is the LORD, that I should obey His voice to let Israel go? I do not know the LORD” (5:2). The real dispute is over who has the right to Israel’s service.

Pharaoh answers with policy. He withholds the straw but keeps the brick quota, calls the people “idle,” and dismisses God’s call as “false words” (5:9). Harder labor is meant to silence the desire to worship. The people scatter across Egypt for stubble, their officers are beaten, and the hope of chapter 4 turns to blame. The officers accuse Moses and Aaron of putting a sword in Pharaoh’s hand. Moses has nowhere to take that accusation but to the LORD, and he takes it honestly: “Why is it You have sent me?” (5:22). The chapter ends with no relief yet. God’s answer begins in the next verse of the story.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 5:1–5 — Pharaoh Refuses to Know the LORD

Moses and Aaron deliver the LORD’s command to let His people go and worship Him. Pharaoh refuses to acknowledge the LORD at all, sends them back to work, and complains that they are giving the people rest.

Exodus 5:6–14 — Pharaoh Withholds the Straw and Increases the Burden

That same day Pharaoh orders that the people gather their own straw while making the same number of bricks, calling their desire to sacrifice laziness. Israel scatters through Egypt for stubble, and their officers are beaten when the quota is not met.

Exodus 5:15–21 — The Officers Appeal to Pharaoh and Blame Moses and Aaron

The officers appeal to Pharaoh, but he only repeats, “You are idle! Idle!” (5:17), and holds them to the full quota. Leaving the palace, they turn on Moses and Aaron and blame them for making Israel hateful to Pharaoh.

Exodus 5:22–23 — Moses Brings His Distress to the LORD

Moses returns to the LORD and asks why He has brought trouble on His people and why He sent Moses at all. Since Moses spoke in God’s name, things have only grown worse, and the people have not yet been delivered.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 5 exposes what Pharaoh’s power is for. He calls the people idle and increases their work so that they will not regard what he calls false words (5:8–9). The oppression is aimed at keeping God’s people too exhausted to answer God’s call. Pharaoh’s question, “Who is the LORD?” is answered as the plagues unfold, when Egypt learns “that I am the LORD” (7:5).

The chapter is honest about what obedience can cost. Moses obeyed, and things got worse. Scripture does not hide the gap between God’s promise and the people’s circumstances, and it shows Moses bringing his confusion straight to God. Obedience does not guarantee quick relief, but God can be trusted with the hardest questions about His timing.

Key Themes to Watch For

Who commands Israel’s service. The LORD claims Israel as His people for worship, while Pharaoh claims them for his labor (5:1–5).

Knowing the LORD. Pharaoh says, “I do not know the LORD” (5:2), the challenge the rest of the plague story answers.

Oppression that silences worship. Heavier labor is meant to crush the people’s cry to sacrifice to God (5:8–9, 17).

Honest prayer under pressure. Moses brings the officers’ accusation and his own distress to the LORD (5:22–23).

Key Words & Concepts

Feast to Me in the wilderness (5:1). A festival of worship to the LORD away from Egypt. From the start, the goal of Israel’s release is worship.

Three days’ journey (5:3). The distance Israel asks to travel to sacrifice. The request tested whether Pharaoh would honor the LORD’s basic claim on His people.

Straw and stubble (5:7, 12). Straw was mixed into mud bricks. Forcing the people to gather their own stubble made the same quota nearly impossible.

Officers (5:14). Israelite foremen set over their own people by Pharaoh’s taskmasters. They are beaten when the quota is not met.
