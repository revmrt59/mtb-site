Exodus 6 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Exodus 6 gives the LORD’s answer to Moses’ distress: He renews His promise to bring Israel out, redeem them, take them as His people, and bring them into the land, so that they will know that He is the LORD. When the crushed people cannot listen and Moses doubts that Pharaoh will hear him, a genealogy identifies Moses and Aaron as the men God has sent, and the chapter returns to Moses’ objection.

Chapter Sections

1. Exodus 6:1–8 — The LORD Renews His Promise of Redemption

The LORD tells Moses, “Now you shall see what I will do to Pharaoh” (Exodus 6:1), and declares that He who appeared to the fathers as God Almighty will now make His name LORD known by bringing Israel out of bondage. He promises, “I will take you as My people, and I will be your God” (Exodus 6:7), and swears to bring them into the land He promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

2. Exodus 6:9–13 — The People Cannot Listen, and Moses Is Sent Again

Israel does not heed Moses “because of anguish of spirit and cruel bondage” (Exodus 6:9). When the LORD sends him to Pharaoh, Moses protests that he is “of uncircumcised lips” (Exodus 6:12), and the LORD gives Moses and Aaron a command to bring the children of Israel out of Egypt.

3. Exodus 6:14–27 — The Family Record of Moses and Aaron

A list of the heads of fathers’ houses moves from Reuben and Simeon to Levi and traces the line of Kohath and Amram to Aaron and Moses and to Aaron’s descendants. It ends by declaring, “These are the same Moses and Aaron” (Exodus 6:27), the men the LORD commanded to bring Israel out and who spoke to Pharaoh.

4. Exodus 6:28–30 — The Narrative Returns to Moses’ Objection

The account resumes on the day the LORD spoke to Moses in Egypt and commanded him to speak all His words to Pharaoh. Moses again protests that he is of uncircumcised lips and asks, “how shall Pharaoh heed me?” (Exodus 6:30).
