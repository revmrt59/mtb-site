Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 6
Main Idea:

The LORD answers Moses’ distress by renewing His promise in His own name: He will bring Israel out, redeem them, take them as His people, and bring them into the land, so that they will know that He is the LORD.

Setting:

Exodus 6 answers Moses’ prayer at the end of chapter 5, after Pharaoh has made Israel’s labor heavier. A family record of Levi then identifies Moses and Aaron.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 6:1–8 — The LORD Renews His Promise of Redemption

  • Exodus 6:9–13 — The People Cannot Listen, and Moses Is Sent Again

  • Exodus 6:14–27 — The Family Record of Moses and Aaron

Key Verse:

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Moses had asked why God sent him, and God’s answer begins with His name: “I am the LORD” (6:2). He appeared to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob as God Almighty, but they had not experienced the full meaning of His name LORD. Now He will show what that name means by what He does. The promises tumble out: He will bring them out, rescue them, and “redeem you with an outstretched arm and with great judgments” (6:6). Then comes the heart of it all: “I will take you as My people, and I will be your God” (6:7). The rescue is for the sake of belonging. God frames the whole promise with His name, beginning and ending with “I am the LORD.”

The people cannot take it in. They do not listen “because of anguish of spirit and cruel bondage” (6:9), and Scripture treats their weariness with compassion. Moses, discouraged, protests again that he has “uncircumcised lips” (6:12). God does not withdraw His word. A family record interrupts the scene to locate Moses and Aaron within Levi’s line, then states plainly, “These are the same Moses and Aaron” (6:27). God’s promise does not depend on how strong His people or His servants feel.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 6:1–8 — The LORD Renews His Promise of Redemption

The LORD tells Moses that he will now see what God will do to Pharaoh, and He renews His promise under His name LORD. He pledges to bring Israel out of bondage, redeem them with great judgments, take them as His own people, and bring them into the land sworn to their fathers.

Exodus 6:9–13 — The People Cannot Listen, and Moses Is Sent Again

Israel does not listen to Moses because their spirit is crushed and their slavery is cruel. When the LORD sends Moses to Pharaoh again, Moses protests that his lips are unfit, and the LORD gives both Moses and Aaron His command to bring Israel out.

Exodus 6:14–27 — The Family Record of Moses and Aaron

A record of the heads of Israel’s families moves from Reuben and Simeon to Levi and traces the line through Kohath and Amram to Aaron and Moses and Aaron’s sons. It closes by identifying these as the very men the LORD sent to bring Israel out and to speak to Pharaoh.

Exodus 6:28–30 — The Narrative Returns to Moses’ Objection

The account returns to the day the LORD commanded Moses to speak all His words to Pharaoh. Moses again protests that his lips are unfit and asks how Pharaoh will listen, setting up God’s answer in chapter 7.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 6 shows that God’s answer to discouragement is a fresh word about who He is and what He will do. Moses’ circumstances had not changed, but God restated His purpose in the strongest terms. The goal of redemption is relationship: God takes a people as His own and gives Himself to them as their God, the same promise that reaches its fullness in the new covenant (Revelation 21:3).

The chapter is also gentle with people too crushed to listen. Israel’s failure to listen is explained, not condemned, by their anguish and their bondage. God keeps working on behalf of people who cannot yet hear His promise. The genealogy reminds readers that God works through real families and real history.

Key Themes to Watch For

The name of the LORD. God opens and closes His promise with “I am the LORD” and will make His name known by redeeming Israel (6:2–8).

Redemption and belonging. God promises to bring out, rescue, redeem, take as His people, and bring into the land (6:6–8).

God remembers His covenant. The LORD has heard Israel’s groaning and remembered His promise to the fathers (6:4–5).

Crushed spirits and renewed commission. The people cannot listen, and Moses feels unfit, yet God sends him again (6:9–13, 28–30).

Key Words & Concepts

God Almighty (6:3). The name under which God appeared to the patriarchs (compare Genesis 17:1). The fathers knew the name LORD, but they had not yet experienced its full meaning as God now displays it in redemption.

Redeem (6:6). To free someone from bondage by paying a price or exercising power. Here God redeems Israel “with an outstretched arm and with great judgments.”

Uncircumcised lips (6:12, 30). A Hebrew way of describing lips that feel unqualified or unfit to speak for God (compare Isaiah 6:5).

The genealogy (6:14–27). A family record that narrows from Reuben and Simeon to Levi to place Moses and Aaron within Israel. It names Korah and Aaron’s sons, who appear later in the story.
