COMMAND - ( tsavah , Strong's H6680 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb tsavah means to command, charge, order, or commission. In Exodus 7:2, it highlights the absolute, authoritative delegation of divine instruction from Yahweh to Moses, who must then pass it on to Aaron to declare to Pharaoh. It is not a mere request, suggestion, or advice, but a royal and divine decree backed by the supreme authority of God, establishing a strict chain of command for the deliverance of Israel.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• tsavah is a primary root word that carries the fundamental idea of laying a charge upon someone, setting up an order, or establishing a boundary that must be maintained. In the ancient Near East, a sovereign king or tribal leader would issue a tsavah to delegates, ambassadors, or vassals to establish binding covenantal obligations. The historical background of the word pictures a master builder laying down a precise line that must be followed, or a military commander delivering an unalterable dispatch to a subordinate.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• tsavah occurs over 500 times in the Hebrew Bible, primarily in the Pi'el stem. It denotes the act of giving a binding instruction, charge, or commission. The semantic range of the word includes:

• To order or command a subordinate with absolute authority.

• To commission a messenger or deputy to act on behalf of a superior.

• To give final instructions, decree a will, or set an inheritance in order.

• To charge, appoint, or inaugurate a leader into an office.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

In the Pentateuch, tsavah is the standard term for God's legislative actions. It forms the basis of the noun mitzvah (commandment).

• The word is used as a military metaphor where Yahweh acts as the Commander of the hosts of Israel, issuing orders that require immediate and exact obedience.

• It is also used in a domestic sense when a father charges his household to maintain his legacy, showing that the word carries a weight of relational authority and covenantal responsibility.

4. Theological Implications

The use of tsavah establishes God’s absolute sovereignty. When Yahweh commands, His words are non-negotiable.

• It defines the prophetic office: a true prophet does not speak from his own imagination, but only speaks what he is commanded.

• It frames the covenant relationship: God’s tsavah is the standard of righteousness. Faith is demonstrated through obedience to these divine charges.

• It highlights God's sovereignty over creation; when God commands, even inanimate nature, history, and spiritual forces must obey.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Exodus 7:2, God tells Moses, "You shall speak all that I command (tsavah) you."

• This occurs directly after Moses expresses insecurity about his speech ("uncircumcised lips" in Exodus 6:30). God addresses this insecurity by removing the burden of invention from Moses. Moses does not need to craft his own speeches or negotiate with Pharaoh; he only needs to transmit what God has commanded.

• The term establishes a precise chain of authority: Yahweh commands Moses, Moses instructs Aaron, and Aaron speaks to Pharaoh. Moses acts as the direct ambassador of the King of kings, carrying a royal tsavah that Pharaoh is obligated to obey, making Pharaoh's resistance an act of direct rebellion against the cosmic Sovereign.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:16 - "And the LORD God commanded the man, saying, 'Of every tree of the garden you may freely eat...'"

• Deuteronomy 6:6 - "And these words which I command you today shall be in your heart."

• Joshua 1:9 - "Have I not commanded you? Be strong and of good courage..."

• Psalm 33:9 - "For He spoke, and it was done; He commanded, and it stood fast."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The noun form mitzvah (commandment) is derived directly from tsavah.

• In the Septuagint (LXX), the Greek verb entellomai is used to translate tsavah in Exodus 7:2. This Greek term similarly denotes an official, authoritative order given by a high official, reinforcing the binding and legal nature of the divine instruction.
