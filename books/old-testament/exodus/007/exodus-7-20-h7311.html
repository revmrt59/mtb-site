LIFTED - ( rum , Strong's H7311 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb rum primarily communicates the physical motion of rising, being high, or being exalted, but in Exodus 7:20 it describes Moses and Aaron actively raising the staff to strike the Nile, transforming its waters into blood as an unmistakable sign of divine judgment against Egypt's false gods.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root rum conveys a very literal sense of elevation—something moving upward from a lower position to a higher one.

- In ancient Near Eastern contexts, physical gestures of lifting hands, staffs, or weapons often signified divine authority, invocation of power, or the exertion of sovereign will.

- The action stands in sharp contrast to the posture of humility, showing humanity acting as the visible agent of an invisible, higher power.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- To be high, rise, raise, lift up, exalt, or set on high.

- The verb can function transitively (to lift or raise an object) or intransitively (to be high or exalted).

- It describes both literal physical elevation and metaphorical exaltation, such as lifting up a voice, a standard, or a heart in prayer.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- In Exodus 7:20, the action is intensely practical and dramatic: Aaron physically lifts the staff.

- The imagery bridges the human action below with the divine mandate above; the uplifted staff becomes the focal point of God's intervention in history.

- Throughout the Old Testament, the word frequently paints pictures of triumph, pride (when lifted up wrongly), or holy reverence.

4. Theological Implications

- The lifting of the staff highlights God's delegation of authority to His human servants to execute His judgment.

- It marks the initiation of the plagues, demonstrating that the God of Israel reigns supreme over the supposed deities of the Nile.

- Human participation (lifting) meets divine power, initiating a sovereign act that humbles the proud nation of Egypt.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- Here, rum specifies the deliberate, authorized gesture that triggers the plague.

- It is not a casual movement, but a public, authoritative demonstration before Pharaoh and his court that Yahweh's word through Moses and Aaron is immediately backed by supernatural reality.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Genesis 7:17 - "The water increased and lifted up the ark, and it rose high above the earth."

- Exodus 14:16 - "But lift up your rod, and stretch out your hand over the sea and divide it."

- Numbers 24:7 - "His king shall be higher than Agag, and his kingdom shall be exalted."

- Psalm 89:13 - "You have a mighty arm; strong is your hand, and high is your right hand."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The gesture of raising the staff echoes throughout the early chapters of Exodus as the visible sign of God's power working through weakness.

- Every time the staff is lifted, it acts as a punctuation mark of divine intervention in the affairs of nations.
