Exodus 7:1
Exodus 7:1
NKJV

Exodus 7:1 So the LORD said to Moses: "See, I have made you as God to Pharaoh, and Aaron your brother shall be your prophet.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 7:1 Then the LORD said to Moses, “Pay close attention to this. I will make you seem like God to Pharaoh, and your brother, Aaron, will be your prophet. (NLT)

Main Idea

The LORD tells Moses, “See, I have made you as God to Pharaoh,” with Aaron as his prophet. Moses will stand before Pharaoh with God’s authority, and Aaron will speak for him.

Verse Explanation

The LORD tells Moses, “See, I have made you as God to Pharaoh,” with Aaron as his prophet. Moses will stand before Pharaoh with God’s authority, and Aaron will speak for him.

“So the LORD said to Moses: ‘See, I have made you as God to Pharaoh,”

God answers Moses’ objection about his speech (6:30). Moses will stand before Pharaoh with God’s authority. To Pharaoh, Moses will be “as God,” the one who speaks God’s word and through whom God’s power comes. The comparison describes Moses’ role, not his nature. He remains God’s commissioned servant. Pharaoh claimed divine status in Egypt, but he will face a shepherd carrying the authority of the true God.

“and Aaron your brother shall be your prophet.”

Aaron will be Moses’ “prophet.” A prophet speaks for someone else. As a prophet speaks God’s words, Aaron will speak Moses’ words. This echoes 4:16.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word translated “prophet” refers to a spokesman who speaks on behalf of another. This verse gives one of the clearest pictures in Scripture of what a prophet is.

NLT

Exodus 7:3 But I will make Pharaoh’s heart stubborn so I can multiply my miraculous signs and wonders in the land of Egypt. (NLT)

Main Idea

God declares, “I will harden Pharaoh’s heart,” and multiply His signs and wonders in Egypt. God announces in advance that Pharaoh’s resistance will become the occasion for many more signs.

Verse Explanation

God declares, “I will harden Pharaoh’s heart,” and multiply His signs and wonders in Egypt. God announces in advance that Pharaoh’s resistance will become the occasion for many more signs.

“And I will harden Pharaoh’s heart,”

God declares in advance that He will “harden Pharaoh’s heart.” This was announced first in 4:21. Pharaoh has already shown that he will not acknowledge the LORD (5:2). God will confirm Pharaoh in the stubbornness he has chosen. Pharaoh remains responsible for his refusal, and God carries out His purpose through it.

“and multiply My signs and My wonders in the land of Egypt.”

Pharaoh’s resistance will become the occasion for many more “signs” and “wonders.” Each refusal will bring another display of God’s power. God announces in advance that Pharaoh’s resistance will lead to many more signs.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb translated “harden” in this verse means to make hard or stubborn. A different verb is used in 4:21. Exodus uses several verbs for hardening, and their exact differences should not be pressed.

Study Notes

The speaker shifts within the verse. God speaks, but “the rod that is in my hand” refers to Moses’ rod. Moses speaks God’s word, and God acts through Moses’ hand.

Interpreters have suggested that some plagues confronted specific Egyptian gods, such as gods associated with the Nile. Exodus 12:12 says God would execute judgment on “all the gods of Egypt,” but the text does not link each plague to a particular god.
