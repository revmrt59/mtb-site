Exodus 8:1
NKJV

Exodus 8:1 And the LORD spoke to Moses, "Go to Pharaoh and say to him, "˜Thus says the LORD: "Let My people go, that they may serve Me.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 8:1 Then the LORD said to Moses, “Go back to Pharaoh and announce to him, ‘This is what the LORD says: Let my people go, so they can worship me. (NLT)

Main Idea

The LORD sends Moses to Pharaoh with the word, “Let My people go, that they may serve Me.” The demand is renewed before the second plague.

Verse Explanation

The LORD sends Moses to Pharaoh with the word, “Let My people go, that they may serve Me.” The demand is renewed before the second plague.

“And the LORD spoke to Moses, ‘Go to Pharaoh and say to him, ‘Thus says the LORD:”

After the plague of blood, God sends Moses back to Pharaoh with His word.

“Let My people go, that they may serve Me.”

The demand is renewed before the second plague. God’s claim has not changed. Israel belongs to Him and must be free to “serve” Him.

Study Notes

In Hebrew Bibles, the first four verses of this chapter are counted as the last four verses of chapter 7, so the rest of chapter 8 is numbered four verses lower. The NKJV follows the English numbering.

Exodus 8:3
Exodus 8:3
NKJV

Exodus 8:3 So the river shall bring forth frogs abundantly, which shall go up and come into your house, into your bedroom, on your bed, into the houses of your servants, on your people, into your ovens, and into your kneading bowls.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 8:3 The Nile River will swarm with frogs. They will come up out of the river and into your palace, even into your bedroom and onto your bed! They will enter the houses of your officials and your people. They will even jump into your ovens and your kneading bowls. (NLT)

Main Idea

The river will bring forth frogs that will come into Pharaoh’s house, bedroom, and bed, and into his servants’ houses, ovens, and kneading bowls. The plague will invade every part of Egyptian life, from the palace to the kitchen.

Verse Explanation

The river will bring forth frogs that will come into Pharaoh’s house, bedroom, and bed, and into his servants’ houses, ovens, and kneading bowls. The plague will invade every part of Egyptian life, from the palace to the kitchen.

“So the river shall bring forth frogs abundantly,”

The river that had just turned to blood will now “bring forth frogs abundantly.” The Hebrew word suggests swarming, the same word used for Israel’s growth in 1:7.

“which shall go up and come into your house, into your bedroom, on your bed,”

The frogs will invade Pharaoh’s private spaces: “your house,” “your bedroom,” even “your bed.” The king who thought himself untouchable will find no place to escape.

“into the houses of your servants, on your people, into your ovens, and into your kneading bowls.”

The plague will reach everyone, from the palace to the kitchen. Frogs will be in the “ovens,” where bread was baked, and in the “kneading bowls,” where dough was prepared. The plague will invade every part of Egyptian life.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb behind “shall bring forth frogs abundantly” is the verb for swarming used in Genesis 1:20–21 and Exodus 1:7.

“But when Pharaoh saw that there was relief, he hardened his heart”

“But” marks the turn. As soon as Pharaoh saw “relief,” he “hardened his heart.” Here the text says plainly that Pharaoh hardened his own heart. Pressure brought a promise. Relief brought a broken promise.

“and did not heed them, as the LORD had said.”

Pharaoh did not keep his word. This happened “as the LORD had said.” Pharaoh broke his promise as soon as the pressure lifted. His actions after relief showed what his earlier promise was worth.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb translated “hardened” here means to make heavy, the same root as in 7:14. Pharaoh made his own heart heavy and unresponsive.

The Hebrew word translated “relief” means breathing room or respite.

The magicians admitted defeat. “This is the finger of God.” They recognized that this plague came from a power beyond anything they could match. “Finger of God” describes God’s direct action. Study Notes
