Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 8
Main Idea:

Through frogs, lice, and swarms of flies the LORD presses Pharaoh to let His people go and serve Him, while Pharaoh breaks his promises whenever relief comes, his magicians confess “the finger of God,” and God begins to set His people apart from Egypt.

Setting:

Exodus 8 continues the plagues that began when the Nile was turned to blood (7:14–25). Each plague opens with the LORD’s demand and ends with Pharaoh’s hardened heart.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 8:1–15 — Frogs Cover the Land and Pharaoh Breaks His Word

  • Exodus 8:16–19 — The Dust Becomes Lice and the Magicians Confess God’s Finger

  • Exodus 8:20–32 — Swarms of Flies Strike Egypt but Spare Goshen

Key Verse:

“And in that day I will set apart the land of Goshen, in which My people dwell, that no swarms of flies shall be there, in order that you may know that I am the LORD in the midst of the land.” (Exodus 8:22)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The same demand opens the chapter: “Let My people go, that they may serve Me” (8:1). Frogs pour out of the river into Pharaoh’s bedroom and the people’s kneading bowls, and for the first time Pharaoh asks Moses to pray to the LORD. Moses lets him choose the time so that the relief will prove “that there is no one like the LORD our God” (8:10). The frogs die on schedule, and Pharaoh, seeing relief, hardens his heart. His promise lasted only as long as his misery.

With the lice, Egypt’s power reaches its limit. The magicians cannot copy the sign and tell Pharaoh, “This is the finger of God” (8:19). With the flies, something new appears: God sets apart Goshen, where Israel lives, “that you may know that I am the LORD in the midst of the land” (8:22). The LORD is present in Egypt, and He knows His own. Pharaoh starts to bargain. Israel may sacrifice, but in Egypt, or at least not too far away. Moses refuses, because God sets the terms of worship. When the flies are gone, Pharaoh hardens his heart again.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 8:1–15 — Frogs Cover the Land and Pharaoh Breaks His Word

Frogs come up from the waters and fill Egypt, and Pharaoh begs Moses to pray, promising to let Israel go. Moses lets Pharaoh choose the time so he will know there is no one like the LORD, but when relief comes, Pharaoh hardens his heart.

Exodus 8:16–19 — The Dust Becomes Lice and the Magicians Confess God’s Finger

Aaron strikes the dust, and it becomes lice on people and animals throughout Egypt. The magicians cannot copy this sign and confess, “This is the finger of God” (8:19), yet Pharaoh will not listen.

Exodus 8:20–32 — Swarms of Flies Strike Egypt but Spare Goshen

The LORD sends swarms of flies on Egypt but spares Goshen, making a difference between His people and Pharaoh’s. Pharaoh offers worship in the land, then nearby, but Moses insists on the three days’ journey God commands, and once the flies are gone Pharaoh breaks his word again.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 8 exposes the difference between wanting relief and turning to God. Pharaoh asked for prayer and made promises, but as soon as the pressure lifted, he went back to his old refusal. A confession that disappears with the trouble was never surrender.

The chapter also shows that God will not accept worship on terms set by the world. Pharaoh’s offers sound reasonable: worship here, or just not too far away. Each offer would have kept Israel within Pharaoh’s reach. Moses holds to the LORD’s command, and God marks out His people as belonging to Him even in the middle of Egypt.

Key Themes to Watch For

No one like the LORD. The plagues and their removal reveal the LORD’s unique power (8:10, 19).

Relief without repentance. Pharaoh hardens his heart each time the pressure lifts (8:15, 32).

The limits of counterfeit power. The magicians can imitate the frogs but not the lice, and they confess God’s finger (8:7, 18–19).

God distinguishes His people. The LORD sets Goshen apart and makes a difference between His people and Egypt (8:22–23).

Worship on God’s terms. Moses refuses to sacrifice in Egypt or stay close at Pharaoh’s command (8:25–28).

Key Words & Concepts

Entreat the LORD (8:8). Pharaoh asks Moses to pray for him. Moses intercedes for the king who oppresses Israel, and God answers each time.

The finger of God (8:19). The magicians’ admission that this plague comes from God’s power and is beyond anything they can do.

Goshen (8:22). The region of Egypt where Israel lived. God spares it from the flies to show that He is present and knows His people.

The abomination of the Egyptians (8:26). Sacrifices of animals the Egyptians held sacred. Offering them in Egypt would provoke the Egyptians and put Israel in danger.
