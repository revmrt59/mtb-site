EXALT - ( salal, Strong's H5549 )

Summary:

In Exodus 9:17, the Hebrew verb salal (specifically in the Hitpael stem as mistolel) is translated as "exalt yourself." While the root meaning of salal is "to cast up" or "to build up a highway or rampart," in this intensive, reflexive grammatical form, it paints a vivid picture of Pharaoh building up a defensive barrier of pride against God's people to obstruct their departure. It describes a stubborn self-exaltation that sets oneself up as an obstacle to God's sovereign will, highlighting the futility and arrogance of human rebellion against the Creator.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root meaning of salal is to heap up, cast up, or pile up earth to construct a raised way or rampart. It is the term used for building highways (mesillah) or military siege ramps.

• In the ancient Near East, building a highway or throwing up a rampart required deliberate, heavy labor to pile up stones and dirt to overcome natural obstacles or fortify a position.

• When applied metaphorically to human behavior in the Hitpael stem (which is reflexive), the word depicts Pharaoh actively piling up his own pride, stubbornness, and self-will like a massive defensive dirt wall or siege ramp against God's people, blocking their path to freedom.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The verb salal has a primary semantic range of "to lift up," "cast up a highway," "build a ramp," or "pave a way."

• In the active Qal stem, it means to heap up or build a road.

• In the passive Pilpel stem, it can mean to highly esteem or exalt wisdom.

• In the reflexive Hitpael stem used in Exodus 9:17, it carries the unique meaning of "to behave insolently," "to obstruct," or "to set oneself as a barrier against." It means to lift oneself up in pride as an obstacle to others, actively opposing God’s command.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Salal is most frequently used in the Old Testament in a literal sense for building up highways or casting up siege mounds.

• The transition from a literal road-building term to a term of arrogant self-exaltation relies on the visual of a "barrier." Pharaoh is not merely feeling proud in his heart; he is actively throwing up a rampart of resistance to block Israel from leaving. His pride is an active, structural opposition to the purposes of God.

4. Theological Implications

• Pharaoh’s self-exaltation is the ultimate expression of human rebellion against divine authority. By setting himself up as a high barrier, Pharaoh attempts to match his sovereignty against the sovereignty of Yahweh.

• This highlights the biblical truth that pride is not passive; it is an active construction of self-deification. Humans attempt to build their own elevated platforms to declare independence from God.

• The theological irony is that while Pharaoh is busy building up his wall of resistance, God is the one who ultimately casts down the proud and makes a highway for His people through the wilderness and the sea.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• Exodus 9:17 occurs during the plague of hail, the seventh plague, which marks a transition to greater severity. God addresses Pharaoh's persistent refusal to let Israel go.

• The grammatical form mistolel (Hitpael participle) denotes ongoing, self-willed action: "As yet you are continually exalting yourself..." Pharaoh is actively and repeatedly piling up obstacles.

• The preposition used after the verb is be (against), indicating that Pharaoh’s self-exaltation is directed "against My people." To oppose God's people is to oppose God Himself. Pharaoh's pride acts as a human dam trying to hold back the unstoppable flow of God's redemptive plan.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Job 19:12: "His troops come together and build up (salal) their road against me."

• Psalm 68:4: "Sing to God, sing praises to His name; extol (salal) Him who rides on the clouds."

• Proverbs 4:8: "Exalt (salal) her, and she will promote you."

• Isaiah 57:14: "Heap up, heap up (salal), prepare the way, take the stumbling block out of the way of My people."

• Isaiah 62:10: "Go through, go through the gates! Prepare the way for the people; build up, build up (salal) the highway!"

• Jeremiah 50:26: "Come against her from the farthest border; open her storehouses; cast her up (salal) as heaps of ruins, and destroy her utterly."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The contrast between salal used for God and salal used for Pharaoh is stark. When God commands His people to "build up" (salal) a highway, it is to remove stumbling blocks so that the redeemed can walk in freedom (Isaiah 57:14). When Pharaoh "builds himself up" (salal), he is creating a stumbling block to prevent God's people from worshiping.

• The Hitpael participle indicates that Pharaoh is trapped in a self-reinforcing loop of pride. The more he resists God, the more he has to pile up his self-assertion to justify his past resistance.
