Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 9
Main Idea:

As the plagues strike Egypt’s livestock, bodies, and fields, the LORD declares that He has raised up Pharaoh to show His power and make His name known in all the earth, warns Egypt in mercy, and exposes Pharaoh’s confession as words without the fear of God.

Setting:

Exodus 9 brings the fifth, sixth, and seventh plagues. The LORD continues to set Israel apart, as He began to do with the swarms of flies (8:22–23).

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 9:1–7 — The LORD Strikes Egypt’s Livestock and Spares Israel’s

  • Exodus 9:8–12 — Boils Break Out on Man and Beast

  • Exodus 9:13–21 — The LORD Declares His Purpose and Warns of Hail

  • Exodus 9:22–26 — Hail and Fire Fall on Egypt but Not on Goshen

  • Exodus 9:27–35 — Pharaoh Confesses Sin and Hardens His Heart Again

Key Verse:

“But indeed for this purpose I have raised you up, that I may show My power in you, and that My name may be declared in all the earth.” (Exodus 9:16)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The judgments grow heavier. A pestilence strikes Egypt’s livestock in the field while not one of Israel’s animals dies, and Pharaoh sends to confirm it and still refuses. Boils break out on people and animals, and the magicians who once copied Moses cannot even stand before him. Then, for the first time, the narrator says, “the LORD hardened the heart of Pharaoh” (9:12), as God had announced.

Before the hail, God explains His purpose: “for this purpose I have raised you up, that I may show My power in you, and that My name may be declared in all the earth” (9:16). Yet even this judgment comes with mercy. God warns Egypt to bring people and animals inside, and Pharaoh’s own household divides. Those who “feared the word of the LORD” make their servants and livestock flee into the houses, and those who ignore it leave theirs in the field. The storm strikes all Egypt, but not Goshen.

Pharaoh finally says the right words: “I have sinned this time. The LORD is righteous” (9:27). Moses is not deceived: “I know that you will not yet fear the LORD God” (9:30). When the storm stops, Pharaoh sins yet more and hardens his heart. Admitting guilt is not the same as yielding to God.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 9:1–7 — The LORD Strikes Egypt’s Livestock and Spares Israel’s

The LORD sends a severe pestilence on Egypt’s livestock in the field at a set time, but not one of Israel’s animals dies. Pharaoh confirms the difference, yet his heart becomes hard.

Exodus 9:8–12 — Boils Break Out on Man and Beast

Moses scatters furnace ashes toward heaven, and boils break out on people and animals throughout Egypt. The magicians cannot stand before Moses, and the LORD hardens Pharaoh’s heart as He had said.

Exodus 9:13–21 — The LORD Declares His Purpose and Warns of Hail

The LORD tells Pharaoh that He has raised him up to show His power and make His name known in all the earth, and He warns of hail unlike any Egypt has seen. Pharaoh’s servants who fear the LORD’s word make their servants and livestock flee into the houses, while others leave theirs in the field.

Exodus 9:22–26 — Hail and Fire Fall on Egypt but Not on Goshen

Moses stretches out his rod, and the LORD sends thunder, hail, and fire that strike people, animals, plants, and trees throughout Egypt. Only in Goshen, where Israel lives, is there no hail.

Exodus 9:27–35 — Pharaoh Confesses Sin and Hardens His Heart Again

Pharaoh confesses that he has sinned and that the LORD is righteous, and Moses prays so that Pharaoh will know the earth belongs to the LORD. Moses knows Pharaoh still does not fear God, and when the storm stops, Pharaoh and his servants harden their hearts again.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 9 holds together two truths that people often pull apart. The LORD hardens Pharaoh and declares a purpose for doing so, and Pharaoh hardens himself and remains responsible. Paul quotes 9:16 to show God’s freedom in judgment (Romans 9:17). The same sun that softens wax hardens clay, and the same pressure that should have humbled Pharaoh only set him harder against God.

The chapter also shows how people treat God’s word. In the same household, some feared His warning and brought their servants and livestock inside, and others ignored it and lost what they had in the field. Pharaoh’s confession is a sober lesson too: it is possible to say “I have sinned” under pressure without ever bowing to God.

Key Themes to Watch For

God’s name in all the earth. The LORD acts so that His power is seen and His name is declared everywhere (9:14, 16, 29).

Divine hardening and human responsibility. The LORD hardens Pharaoh, and Pharaoh hardens his own heart (9:12, 34–35).

Mercy within judgment. God warns Egypt so that people and animals can take shelter (9:19–21).

God distinguishes His people. Israel’s livestock and Goshen are spared (9:4, 6, 26).

Confession without repentance. Pharaoh admits sin but does not fear the LORD (9:27–30, 34).

Key Words & Concepts

All the livestock (9:6). “All” here most likely means all within the scope announced, the livestock “in the field” (9:3). Egyptians still had animals to shelter from the hail (9:19–21).

The LORD hardened the heart of Pharaoh (9:12). The first time the narrator says that the LORD hardened Pharaoh, fulfilling His word in 4:21. It stands beside Pharaoh’s own hardening (9:34).

Raised you up (9:16). God kept Pharaoh standing and in power so that His power and name would be displayed through the confrontation.

Feared the word of the LORD (9:20). Taking God’s warning seriously enough to act on it. Some of Pharaoh’s own servants did.
