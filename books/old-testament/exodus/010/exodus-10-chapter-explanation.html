Main Idea

Verse Explanation

“for I have hardened his heart and the hearts of his servants, that I may show these signs of Mine before him,”

Exodus 10:2
Exodus 10:2
NKJV

Exodus 10:2 and that you may tell in the hearing of your son and your son's son the mighty things I have done in Egypt, and My signs which I have done among them, that you may know that I am the LORD."(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 10:2 I’ve also done it so you can tell your children and grandchildren about how I made a mockery of the Egyptians and about the signs I displayed among them — and so you will know that I am the LORD.” (NLT)

Main Idea

God gives a further purpose: that Israel may tell their sons and grandsons the mighty things He did in Egypt, “that you may know that I am the LORD.” The plagues are meant to be remembered and retold in Israel’s homes for generations.

Verse Explanation

God gives a further purpose: that Israel may tell their sons and grandsons the mighty things He did in Egypt, “that you may know that I am the LORD.” The plagues are meant to be remembered and retold in Israel’s homes for generations.

“and that you may tell in the hearing of your son and your son’s son the mighty things I have done in Egypt,”

God gives a further purpose for the plagues. They are not only for Pharaoh. They are for Israel’s children and grandchildren. Parents are to “tell” what God did, so that the story passes from one generation to the next. The command to teach children reappears in the Passover instructions (12:26–27; 13:8, 14).

“and My signs which I have done among them, that you may know that I am the LORD.”

The goal of the telling is knowledge of God: “that you may know that I am the LORD.” Earlier the plagues were meant to teach Pharaoh and Egypt (7:5, 17; 8:22). Here God says they will also teach Israel, as each generation hears what He did.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb translated “done” in “the mighty things I have done” can carry the sense of dealing harshly with someone or making sport of them. It pictures God’s complete mastery over Egypt.

Psalm 78:4–5 and Psalm 105:26–36 show Israel doing what this verse commands, retelling the signs in Egypt to later generations.

Verse Explanation

The locusts will cover the face of the earth and eat whatever the hail left, including every tree in the field. The plague will finish what the hail began.

“And they shall cover the face of the earth, so that no one will be able to see the earth;”

The swarm will be so dense that the ground itself will be hidden. The phrase “the face of the earth” reads literally the eye of the land, as though the land itself could no longer see.

“and they shall eat the residue of what is left, which remains to you from the hail,”

The hail had destroyed the flax and barley but spared the wheat and spelt (9:31–32). The locusts will eat what the hail left. One judgment builds on another.

“and they shall eat every tree which grows up for you out of the field.”

Even the trees will be stripped. Egypt’s remaining food supply is about to disappear.

Study Notes

Locust swarms were a known disaster in the ancient Near East and still occur in the region today. A large swarm can strip crops and leaves over a wide area in a short time. Joel 1–2 describes such a plague in Judah.

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

Pharaoh’s servants ask, “How long shall this man be a snare to us?” and urge him to let the men go, asking, “Do you not yet know that Egypt is destroyed?” Pharaoh’s own officials now see that Egypt is ruined and press him to yield.

“Then Pharaoh’s servants said to him, ‘How long shall this man be a snare to us?”

For the first time Pharaoh’s own officials press him to yield. They use the same words “How long” that God used in 10:3. They call Moses “a snare,” a trap that keeps catching them. They see that resisting is ruining them.

“Let the men go, that they may serve the LORD their God.”

The officials propose a compromise. “Let the men go.” They may mean only the men, or they may simply be urging Pharaoh to release the people. Either way, they want Pharaoh to give in before worse comes.

“Do you not yet know that Egypt is destroyed?”

The officials see what Pharaoh refuses to admit. “Egypt is destroyed.” The country’s crops, livestock, and health have been struck. The king’s own advisors now recognize the ruin his stubbornness has brought.

Study Notes

Some of Pharaoh’s servants had already feared the word of the LORD before the hail (9:20). Their counsel here shows the pressure growing within Pharaoh’s own court.

NLT

Exodus 10:21 Then the LORD said to Moses, “Lift your hand toward heaven, and the land of Egypt will be covered with a darkness so thick you can feel it.” (NLT)

Main Idea

The LORD tells Moses to stretch out his hand toward heaven so that darkness will cover Egypt, “darkness which may even be felt.” The ninth plague comes without warning and is described as darkness thick enough to feel.

Verse Explanation

The LORD tells Moses to stretch out his hand toward heaven so that darkness will cover Egypt, “darkness which may even be felt.” The ninth plague comes without warning and is described as darkness thick enough to feel.

“Then the LORD said to Moses, ‘Stretch out your hand toward heaven, that there may be darkness over the land of Egypt,”

The ninth plague, like the third and sixth, comes without warning. Moses is to stretch out his hand “toward heaven,” and darkness will fall over Egypt.

“darkness which may even be felt.”

The darkness will be so thick that it “may even be felt.” This is not an ordinary night. It is a darkness heavy enough to seem tangible.

Study Notes

The sun god was among Egypt’s most important deities, and Pharaoh was regarded as the son of the sun god Ra. A plague of darkness would show the LORD’s power over the source of light that Egypt worshiped. The text itself does not name the sun god, so this connection is drawn from Egypt’s background.

Some interpreters connect the darkness with a severe sandstorm (the khamsin) known in Egypt. The text presents it as the direct act of God.

Exodus 10:22 So Moses stretched out his hand toward heaven, and there was thick darkness in all the land of Egypt three days.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 10:22 So Moses lifted his hand to the sky, and a deep darkness covered the entire land of Egypt for three days. (NLT)

Main Idea

Moses stretches out his hand, and there is thick darkness in all Egypt for three days. The land that refused the LORD is plunged into darkness.

Verse Explanation

Moses stretches out his hand, and there is thick darkness in all Egypt for three days. The land that refused the LORD is plunged into darkness.

“So Moses stretched out his hand toward heaven, and there was thick darkness in all the land of Egypt three days.”

The darkness falls for “three days.” It covers “all the land of Egypt.” The land that refused the LORD is plunged into darkness.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word translated “thick darkness” is the word for gloom or deep darkness, also used for the darkness of the Day of the LORD (Joel 2:2; Zephaniah 1:15).

Exodus 10:23 During all that time the people could not see each other, and no one moved. But there was light as usual where the people of Israel lived. (NLT)

Main Idea

The Egyptians cannot see one another or move from their places for three days, “But all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings.” God’s distinction between His people and Egypt is now as plain as light and darkness.

Verse Explanation

The Egyptians cannot see one another or move from their places for three days, “But all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings.” God’s distinction between His people and Egypt is now as plain as light and darkness.

“They did not see one another; nor did anyone rise from his place for three days.”

The darkness paralyzed Egypt. People could not see each other and did not move from where they were for three days. All work and movement stopped.

“But all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings.”

The contrast is striking. Every Israelite home had light. God’s distinction between His people and Egypt is now as plain as light and darkness. The text does not explain how the light came. It simply records that God’s people had light while Egypt sat in darkness.

Exodus 10:24
Exodus 10:24
NKJV

Exodus 10:24 Then Pharaoh called to Moses and said, "Go, serve the LORD; only let your flocks and your herds be kept back. Let your little ones also go with you."(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 10:24 Finally, Pharaoh called for Moses. “Go and worship the LORD,” he said. “But leave your flocks and herds here. You may even take your little ones with you.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Pharaoh calls Moses and offers to let the people and their little ones go if the flocks and herds stay behind. Pharaoh offers new terms that would keep Israel’s animals in Egypt.

Verse Explanation

Pharaoh calls Moses and offers to let the people and their little ones go if the flocks and herds stay behind. Pharaoh offers new terms that would keep Israel’s animals in Egypt.

“Then Pharaoh called to Moses and said, ‘Go, serve the LORD;”

Pharaoh calls Moses again. “Go, serve the LORD.” He seems to yield further.

“only let your flocks and your herds be kept back. Let your little ones also go with you.”

Now he will allow the children to go, but “only” if the animals stay. This is Pharaoh’s fourth attempt at compromise (8:25, 28; 10:11). By holding the livestock, he would keep Israel’s wealth and the means of their worship, and he would ensure they came back.

None.

Main Idea

Moses answers that Pharaoh must also give them sacrifices and burnt offerings to offer to the LORD their God. Worship requires the animals Pharaoh wants to keep.

Verse Explanation

Moses answers that Pharaoh must also give them sacrifices and burnt offerings to offer to the LORD their God. Worship requires the animals Pharaoh wants to keep.

“But Moses said, ‘You must also give us sacrifices and burnt offerings, that we may sacrifice to the LORD our God.”

Moses not only refuses the condition but adds to the demand. “You must also give us sacrifices and burnt offerings.” Pharaoh will not only let the animals go. He will contribute animals for the sacrifices. Worship requires the animals Pharaoh wants to keep.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word for “burnt offerings” describes an offering that goes up, wholly burned on the altar. Leviticus 1 later gives the law for this offering.

Exodus 10:26
NKJV

Exodus 10:26 Our livestock also shall go with us; not a hoof shall be left behind. For we must take some of them to serve the LORD our God, and even we do not know with what we must serve the LORD until we arrive there."(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 10:26 All our livestock must go with us, too; not a hoof can be left behind. We must choose our sacrifices for the LORD our God from among these animals. And we won’t know how we are to worship the LORD until we get there.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Moses insists, “not a hoof shall be left behind,” because they do not know what they must offer to the LORD until they arrive. God alone will tell them what He requires, so Israel cannot leave anything behind.

Verse Explanation

Moses insists, “not a hoof shall be left behind,” because they do not know what they must offer to the LORD until they arrive. God alone will tell them what He requires, so Israel cannot leave anything behind.

“Our livestock also shall go with us; not a hoof shall be left behind.”

Moses insists on complete release. “Not a hoof shall be left behind.” Not one animal, not even one hoof, will remain in Egypt.

“For we must take some of them to serve the LORD our God,”

The reason is worship. The animals are needed to “serve the LORD.” Leaving them behind would hinder Israel from obeying God.

“and even we do not know with what we must serve the LORD until we arrive there.”

Moses adds that even Israel does not yet know what God will require. God alone sets the terms of worship. Israel must bring everything, because God will tell them what He requires when they arrive.

Exodus 10:29
Exodus 10:29
NKJV

Exodus 10:29 So Moses said, "You have spoken well. I will never see your face again."(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 10:29 “Very well,” Moses replied. “I will never see your face again.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Moses answers, “You have spoken well. I will never see your face again.” Moses announces the final break, though the same audience continues in Exodus 11:4–8 before he leaves.

Verse Explanation

Moses answers, “You have spoken well. I will never see your face again.” Moses announces the final break, though the same audience continues in Exodus 11:4–8 before he leaves.

“So Moses said, ‘You have spoken well. I will never see your face again.”

Moses accepts Pharaoh’s words. “You have spoken well.” He agrees that he will “never see your face again.” In Moses’ words the break is final. The natural reading is that this same audience continues in 11:4–8, where Moses announces the last plague. That continuation is an inference from the flow of the text. What the text states outright is that Moses then went out from Pharaoh in great anger (11:8), and that Pharaoh later summoned Moses and Aaron by night (12:31).

Study Notes

Exodus 11:1–3 records what God had told Moses. It stands between Moses’ words in 10:29 and his announcement to Pharaoh in 11:4–8.
