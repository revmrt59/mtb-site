Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 10
Main Idea:

Through locusts and darkness the LORD shows His signs so that Israel will tell their children what He has done, while Pharaoh keeps bargaining over who may go and what may be left behind until he drives Moses away with a threat of death.

Setting:

Exodus 10 brings the eighth and ninth plagues. Egypt has already lost its livestock in the field and much of its harvest to the hail (9:25, 31).

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 10:1–6 — The LORD Warns of Locusts and Gives the Signs a Purpose

  • Exodus 10:21–23 — Thick Darkness Covers Egypt While Israel Has Light

Key Verse:

“and that you may tell in the hearing of your son and your son’s son the mighty things I have done in Egypt, and My signs which I have done among them, that you may know that I am the LORD.’” (Exodus 10:2)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

God gives the plagues a purpose that reaches beyond Pharaoh: “that you may tell in the hearing of your son and your son’s son the mighty things I have done in Egypt” (10:2). The signs are meant to be retold in Israel’s homes, so that each generation will know the LORD. Moses and Aaron then name the root of Pharaoh’s refusal: “How long will you refuse to humble yourself before Me?” (10:3).

Pharaoh’s own officials now tell him that Egypt is destroyed, and he starts to bargain. Only the men may go. Moses answers that everyone goes, young and old, sons and daughters, flocks and herds, “for we must hold a feast to the LORD” (10:9). Locusts devour every green thing the hail had left, and Pharaoh asks forgiveness “only this once” (10:17), but only to be rid of the plague. Darkness thick enough to be felt covers Egypt for three days, “But all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings” (10:23). Pharaoh’s last offer would let the people go and keep their animals. Moses refuses: “not a hoof shall be left behind” (10:26). God, not Pharaoh, will decide what His worship requires.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 10:1–6 — The LORD Warns of Locusts and Gives the Signs a Purpose

The LORD sends Moses to Pharaoh so that Israel may tell its children and grandchildren what He has done in Egypt. Moses and Aaron ask how long Pharaoh will refuse to humble himself and warn that locusts will eat what the hail left.

Exodus 10:7–11 — Pharaoh Offers to Let Only the Men Go

Pharaoh’s servants urge him to let the men go, since Egypt is ruined. Moses insists that young and old, sons and daughters, and all the flocks and herds must go to the LORD’s feast, but Pharaoh will release only the men and drives Moses and Aaron out.

Exodus 10:12–20 — Locusts Devour Egypt and Pharaoh Confesses Again

An east wind brings locusts that cover the land and eat every plant the hail had left. Pharaoh confesses his sin and asks forgiveness “only this once” (10:17), but after a west wind drives the locusts into the Red Sea, the LORD hardens his heart.

Exodus 10:21–23 — Thick Darkness Covers Egypt While Israel Has Light

At Moses’ signal, darkness “which may even be felt” (10:21) covers Egypt for three days. The Egyptians cannot see one another, and no one rises from his place. Yet all the children of Israel have light where they live.

Exodus 10:24–29 — Pharaoh’s Last Offer and Moses’ Announcement of the Final Break

Pharaoh offers to let the little ones go if the livestock stays behind, but Moses refuses because Israel will need them to worship the LORD as He directs. The LORD hardens Pharaoh’s heart, Pharaoh threatens Moses with death, and Moses answers that he will never see Pharaoh’s face again.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 10 shows that God’s mighty acts are meant to be passed on. The plagues were not only for Pharaoh; they were for Israel’s children and grandchildren, so that every generation would know the LORD. Faith is handed down by telling what God has done.

The chapter also exposes the quiet power of compromise. Pharaoh’s offers moved step by step: only the men, then everyone but the animals. Each one would have left part of Israel in his hands. Moses would not leave behind the children or a single hoof, because God had called the whole people to worship Him and He alone would set the terms. Divided allegiance always keeps something back from God.

Key Themes to Watch For

Telling the next generation. God’s signs are to be told to children and grandchildren so that they will know the LORD (10:1–2).

Pride against God. The LORD asks how long Pharaoh will refuse to humble himself (10:3).

Worship without compromise. Moses refuses every partial release, keeping the children and the livestock with the people (10:9–11, 24–26).

Light and darkness. Egypt sits in thick darkness while Israel has light in its dwellings (10:21–23).

Key Words & Concepts

Locusts (10:4–15). Swarming insects that devour crops. They finish off what the hail had spared, leaving nothing green in Egypt.

Only this once (10:17). Pharaoh’s plea for forgiveness, aimed at ending the plague rather than obeying God.

Darkness which may even be felt (10:21). Darkness so thick that the Egyptians could not see one another, and no one rose from his place for three days. It came without warning.

Not a hoof (10:26). Moses’ refusal to leave any animal behind, because Israel would not know what the LORD required for their worship until they arrived.
