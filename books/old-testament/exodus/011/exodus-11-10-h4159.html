WONDERS - ( mopeth , Strong's H4159 )

Summary:

The Hebrew term mopeth designates extraordinary events or signs sent by God that evoke awe, disrupt normal expectations, and communicate divine authority or impending judgment. In Exodus 11:10, it describes the culmination of plagues sent upon Egypt, functioning as tangible proofs of God's sovereign power over Pharaoh and the Egyptian gods to secure the deliverance of Israel.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• Rooted in ideas of miraculous signs, portents, and omens that arrest human attention.

• Historically used in the ancient Near Eastern context to describe royal decrees backed by supernatural displays, though here strictly governed by the unilateral will of Yahweh.

• Conveys a visual and visceral jolt; these are events designed to make observers stop, look, and recognize a divine hand at work.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Refers to a wonder, miracle, sign, or portent.

• Denotes an extraordinary happening that falls outside the ordinary course of nature and carries profound symbolic and theological weight.

• Serves as a visible credential or proof of divine intervention and authority.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Often paired with the Hebrew word for signs (oth), forming a common biblical formula (othoth umophethim, "signs and wonders") that emphasizes both the revelatory message and the miraculous power behind it.

• Functions as a dramatic object lesson, acting as a flashing neon sign pointing directly to the character, power, and holiness of God.

4. Theological Implications

• Demonstrates that God acts decisively within human history to judge oppression and redeem His people.

• Highlights the contest between the Creator and earthly powers; the "wonders" prove that human rulers are utterly powerless when confronted by divine judgment.

• Anticipates ultimate redemptive acts, pointing forward to the greatest wonders accomplished in the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Exodus 11:10, Moses and Aaron have performed all these wonders before Pharaoh, yet Pharaoh's heart remains hardened because the Lord has ordained the final stage of deliverance through judgment.

• The term summarizes the entire sweeping campaign of plagues, framing them not merely as disasters, but as purposeful divine portents demanding submission.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 7:3 - "And I will harden Pharaoh's heart, and multiply My signs and My wonders in the land of Egypt."

• Deuteronomy 4:34 - "Or did God ever try to go and take for Himself a nation from the midst of another nation, by trials, by signs, by wonders, by war..."

• Nehemiah 9:10 - "You showed signs and wonders against Pharaoh, against all his servants, and against all the people of his land."

• Psalm 105:27 - "They performed His signs among them, and wonders in the land of Ham."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The persistence of Pharaoh in the face of such overwhelming wonders illustrates the tragic capacity of the human heart to resist clear evidence of God's power until judgment falls.

• Throughout Scripture, wonders are never performed merely for spectacle or entertainment; they invariably serve a revelatory and redemptive purpose.
