Verse-by-Verse
Exodus 11:1
Exodus 11:1
NKJV

Exodus 11:1 And the LORD said to Moses, "I will bring one more plague on Pharaoh and on Egypt. Afterward he will let you go from here. When he lets you go, he will surely drive you out of here altogether.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 11:1 Then the LORD said to Moses, “I will strike Pharaoh and the land of Egypt with one more blow. After that, Pharaoh will let you leave this country. In fact, he will be so eager to get rid of you that he will force you all to leave. (NLT)

Main Idea

The LORD tells Moses, “I will bring one more plague on Pharaoh and on Egypt,” after which Pharaoh will let them go and “surely drive you out of here altogether.” God announces that the long confrontation is about to end with Israel’s release.

Verse Explanation

The LORD tells Moses, “I will bring one more plague on Pharaoh and on Egypt,” after which Pharaoh will let them go and “surely drive you out of here altogether.” God announces that the long confrontation is about to end with Israel’s release.

“And the LORD said to Moses, ‘I will bring one more plague on Pharaoh and on Egypt.”

God tells Moses that the end is near. “One more plague” remains. After nine judgments, the next will be the last.

“Afterward he will let you go from here. When he lets you go, he will surely drive you out of here altogether.”

God foretells the outcome. Pharaoh will not merely permit Israel to leave. He will “drive you out of here altogether.” The king who refused to let them go will push them out. God had said this from the beginning (6:1).

Study Notes

Exodus 11:1–3 appears to record what God had already told Moses before or during the audience of 10:24–29. The narrative pauses to give this word before Moses’ final speech to Pharaoh in 11:4–8.

Exodus 11:2
Exodus 11:2
NKJV

Exodus 11:2 Speak now in the hearing of the people, and let every man ask from his neighbor and every woman from her neighbor, articles of silver and articles of gold."(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 11:2 Tell all the Israelite men and women to ask their Egyptian neighbors for articles of silver and gold.” (NLT)

Main Idea

The LORD tells Moses to have every man and woman ask their neighbors for articles of silver and gold. God prepares His people to leave Egypt with provision, as He promised in Exodus 3:21–22.

Verse Explanation

The LORD tells Moses to have every man and woman ask their neighbors for articles of silver and gold. God prepares His people to leave Egypt with provision, as He promised in Exodus 3:21–22.

“Speak now in the hearing of the people,”

Moses is to address all the people publicly.

“and let every man ask from his neighbor and every woman from her neighbor, articles of silver and articles of gold.”

Every man and woman is to “ask” their Egyptian neighbors for silver and gold. God had promised this at the burning bush (3:21–22). Israel will not leave Egypt empty-handed. God prepares His people to leave Egypt with provision, as He promised.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb translated “ask” is the ordinary word for asking or requesting. It does not imply deception or borrowing with a promise to return. The KJV rendering “borrow” has sometimes led readers to think so, but the word simply means to ask.

God had told Abraham that his descendants would come out “with great possessions” (Genesis 15:14).

The LORD gives the people favor with the Egyptians, and Moses is very great in Egypt in the sight of Pharaoh’s servants and the people. After all the plagues, God has given Israel and Moses honor in Egypt’s eyes.

Verse Explanation

The LORD gives the people favor with the Egyptians, and Moses is very great in Egypt in the sight of Pharaoh’s servants and the people. After all the plagues, God has given Israel and Moses honor in Egypt’s eyes.

“And the LORD gave the people favor in the sight of the Egyptians.”

The Egyptians respond with goodwill because “the LORD gave the people favor.” Their willingness to give is not merely fear or politeness. God has moved their hearts.

“Moreover the man Moses was very great in the land of Egypt, in the sight of Pharaoh’s servants and in the sight of the people.”

Moses himself is held in high regard. “The man Moses was very great.” Pharaoh’s officials and ordinary Egyptians alike respect him. After all the plagues, God has given Israel and Moses honor in Egypt’s eyes.

Moses declares, “Thus says the LORD: ‘About midnight I will go out into the midst of Egypt.’” The LORD Himself will act in the final judgment.

“Then Moses said, ‘Thus says the LORD:”

Moses now speaks to Pharaoh in the same audience that began in 10:24. He delivers God’s final warning.

“About midnight I will go out into the midst of Egypt;”

God Himself will act. “I will go out into the midst of Egypt.” No rod is raised and no sign is given. The LORD will move through the land personally. The time is “about midnight,” in the darkest hour of the night.

Exodus 11:5
Exodus 11:5
NKJV

Exodus 11:5 and all the firstborn in the land of Egypt shall die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sits on his throne, even to the firstborn of the female servant who is behind the handmill, and all the firstborn of the animals.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 11:5 All the firstborn sons will die in every family in Egypt, from the oldest son of Pharaoh, who sits on his throne, to the oldest son of his lowliest servant girl who grinds the flour. Even the firstborn of all the livestock will die. (NLT)

Main Idea

All the firstborn in Egypt will die, from Pharaoh’s firstborn on the throne to the firstborn of the servant at the handmill, along with the firstborn of animals. The judgment will reach every level of Egyptian society, answering Pharaoh’s refusal to release God’s firstborn son, Israel.

Verse Explanation

All the firstborn in Egypt will die, from Pharaoh’s firstborn on the throne to the firstborn of the servant at the handmill, along with the firstborn of animals. The judgment will reach every level of Egyptian society, answering Pharaoh’s refusal to release God’s firstborn son, Israel.

“and all the firstborn in the land of Egypt shall die,”

The last plague will strike “all the firstborn.” God had warned Pharaoh of this at the start. He called Israel “My son, My firstborn” and said, “if you refuse to let him go, indeed I will kill your son, your firstborn” (Exodus 4:22–23). Pharaoh refused to release God’s firstborn son, Israel, and now judgment will fall on Egypt’s firstborn.

“from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sits on his throne, even to the firstborn of the female servant who is behind the handmill,”

The judgment will reach from the top of society to the bottom. The firstborn of Pharaoh, the king seated on the throne, and the firstborn of the lowliest slave grinding grain will both die. The servant “behind the handmill” did the hardest, humblest work in the household.

“and all the firstborn of the animals.”

Even the firstborn of the animals will die. No part of Egypt will be untouched.

Study Notes

The judgment on Egypt’s firstborn answers Pharaoh’s earlier decree to kill Israel’s baby boys (1:16, 22). God had warned of this judgment from the beginning (4:23).

The firstborn son held a special place as heir and the strength of the family (Genesis 49:3; Psalm 78:51).

Exodus 11:6

Exodus 11:6 Then there shall be a great cry throughout all the land of Egypt, such as was not like it before, nor shall be like it again.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 11:6 Then a loud wail will rise throughout the land of Egypt, a wail like no one has heard before or will ever hear again. (NLT)

Main Idea

A great cry will rise throughout Egypt, such as has never been and never will be again. The grief that Israel knew under Pharaoh’s decree will now fill every Egyptian home.

Verse Explanation

A great cry will rise throughout Egypt, such as has never been and never will be again. The grief that Israel knew under Pharaoh’s decree will now fill every Egyptian home.

“Then there shall be a great cry throughout all the land of Egypt,”

The death of the firstborn will bring “a great cry” across Egypt, a wail of grief from every home.

“such as was not like it before, nor shall be like it again.”

The mourning will be unmatched. Israel had cried out under Egypt’s oppression (3:7, 9), and God heard them. Now the cry of grief will rise from Egypt.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word translated “cry” is the same word used of Israel’s cry under bondage in 3:7, 9.

Exodus 11:7
Exodus 11:7
NKJV

Exodus 11:7 But against none of the children of Israel shall a dog move its tongue, against man or beast, that you may know that the LORD does make a difference between the Egyptians and Israel.'(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 11:7 But among the Israelites it will be so peaceful that not even a dog will bark. Then you will know that the LORD makes a distinction between the Egyptians and the Israelites. (NLT)

Main Idea

Against Israel not even a dog will move its tongue, “that you may know that the LORD does make a difference between the Egyptians and Israel.” The quiet safety of Israel will show that the LORD protects His own.

Verse Explanation

Against Israel not even a dog will move its tongue, “that you may know that the LORD does make a difference between the Egyptians and Israel.” The quiet safety of Israel will show that the LORD protects His own.

“But against none of the children of Israel shall a dog move its tongue, against man or beast,”

In contrast to Egypt’s anguish, Israel will be so safe that not even “a dog” will growl or bark at them. The phrase pictures complete calm. Not even the least threat will touch them.

“that you may know that the LORD does make a difference between the Egyptians and Israel.”

The purpose is that Pharaoh will “know” that God distinguishes between the two peoples. The difference seen in earlier plagues (8:22–23; 9:4, 26; 10:23) will be shown most clearly on this night. The quiet safety of Israel will show that the LORD protects His own.

Study Notes

The Hebrew phrase “move its tongue” reads literally sharpen its tongue. It describes a dog snarling or barking.

Exodus 11:8
Exodus 11:8
NKJV

Exodus 11:8 And all these your servants shall come down to me and bow down to me, saying, "˜Get out, and all the people who follow you!' After that I will go out." Then he went out from Pharaoh in great anger.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 11:8 All the officials of Egypt will run to me and fall to the ground before me. ‘Please leave!’ they will beg. ‘Hurry! And take all your followers with you.’ Only then will I go!” Then, burning with anger, Moses left Pharaoh. (NLT)

Main Idea

Moses says Pharaoh’s servants will come and bow to him, saying, “Get out, and all the people who follow you!” and then he goes out from Pharaoh in great anger. The final audience ends with Moses leaving Pharaoh, as he had said he would.

Verse Explanation

Moses says Pharaoh’s servants will come and bow to him, saying, “Get out, and all the people who follow you!” and then he goes out from Pharaoh in great anger. The final audience ends with Moses leaving Pharaoh, as he had said he would.

“And all these your servants shall come down to me and bow down to me, saying,”

Moses foretells a reversal. Pharaoh’s own officials, the men around him now, will come and “bow down” to Moses.

“Get out, and all the people who follow you!”

They will beg Israel to leave. Pharaoh had driven Moses out of his presence (10:11, 28). Now his servants will urge Moses and all Israel to go.

“After that I will go out.”

Moses will leave only when Egypt begs him to go. The departure will be on God’s terms, not Pharaoh’s.

“Then he went out from Pharaoh in great anger.”

Moses leaves “in great anger.” His anger follows Pharaoh’s refusal and threat in the same audience (10:27–28) and his own announcement of the coming judgment. The text does not explain his feelings more precisely. This is the last time Moses stands before Pharaoh, as he said in 10:29.

The LORD tells Moses that Pharaoh will not heed him, “so that My wonders may be multiplied in the land of Egypt.” God’s purpose stands behind Pharaoh’s continued refusal.

“But the LORD said to Moses, ‘Pharaoh will not heed you,”

God reminds Moses of what He had said from the start (7:3–4). Pharaoh “will not heed you.”

“so that My wonders may be multiplied in the land of Egypt.”

God’s purpose stands behind Pharaoh’s refusal. Pharaoh’s stubbornness gives occasion for God’s “wonders” to be “multiplied.” The final judgment will show God’s power more fully than any before.

Exodus 11:10
Exodus 11:10
NKJV

Exodus 11:10 So Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before Pharaoh; and the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he did not let the children of Israel go out of his land.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 11:10 Moses and Aaron performed these miracles in Pharaoh’s presence, but the LORD hardened Pharaoh’s heart, and he wouldn’t let the Israelites leave the country. (NLT)

Main Idea

Moses and Aaron have done all these wonders before Pharaoh, and the LORD hardened Pharaoh’s heart, so that he did not let Israel go. The verse sums up the whole plague narrative and leaves the final judgment still to come.

Verse Explanation

Moses and Aaron have done all these wonders before Pharaoh, and the LORD hardened Pharaoh’s heart, so that he did not let Israel go. The verse sums up the whole plague narrative and leaves the final judgment still to come.

“So Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before Pharaoh;”

The verse looks back over the whole series of plagues. Moses and Aaron did “all these wonders” in Pharaoh’s sight.

“and the LORD hardened Pharaoh’s heart, and he did not let the children of Israel go out of his land.”

The summary matches what God foretold: “the LORD hardened Pharaoh’s heart.” Pharaoh refused to let Israel go. The plague narrative closes here, and the final judgment is still to come.

Study Notes

Exodus 11:9–10 echoes the language of 7:3–4 and forms a frame around the account of the first nine plagues (7:14–10:29).
