Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 11
Main Idea:

The LORD announces the last plague, the death of every firstborn in Egypt, as His answer to Pharaoh’s refusal to release Israel, His firstborn son, and promises that Pharaoh will finally drive His people out.

Setting:

Exodus 11 continues Moses’ final audience with Pharaoh from 10:28–29. It closes the plague narrative, and the judgment it announces falls in 12:29–30, after God gives the Passover.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 11:1–3 — The LORD Announces One More Plague and Gives Israel Favor

  • Exodus 11:4–8 — Moses Warns Pharaoh of the Death of the Firstborn

  • Exodus 11:9–10 — Pharaoh’s Refusal Closes the Plague Narrative

Key Verse:

“But against none of the children of Israel shall a dog move its tongue, against man or beast, that you may know that the LORD does make a difference between the Egyptians and Israel.’” (Exodus 11:7)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The long contest has reached its end. “I will bring one more plague on Pharaoh and on Egypt,” the LORD tells Moses (11:1), and afterward Pharaoh will not merely let Israel go; he will drive them out. God has already turned the Egyptians’ hearts toward His people, and Moses, once a fugitive, is now “very great in the land of Egypt” (11:3).

Then Moses gives Pharaoh the final warning. About midnight the LORD Himself will go out into Egypt, and every firstborn will die, from Pharaoh’s heir on the throne to the son of the slave woman at the handmill. The judgment answers what God said at the beginning: “Israel is My son, My firstborn” (4:22). Egypt had held God’s firstborn in bondage, and an earlier Pharaoh had ordered Israel’s sons thrown into the river (1:22). A great cry will rise in Egypt, “But against none of the children of Israel shall a dog move its tongue” (11:7). Moses walks out in great anger. The chapter closes with a summary of all the plagues: wonders done, a heart hardened, and Israel still not released. The final act belongs to the LORD.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 11:1–3 — The LORD Announces One More Plague and Gives Israel Favor

The LORD tells Moses that after one more plague Pharaoh will drive Israel out altogether, and He tells the people to ask their neighbors for silver and gold. God gives Israel favor with the Egyptians, and Moses is held in high honor in the land.

Exodus 11:4–8 — Moses Warns Pharaoh of the Death of the Firstborn

Moses announces that about midnight the LORD will go through Egypt and every firstborn will die, from Pharaoh’s son to the slave’s son and the firstborn of the animals. Not even a dog will growl at Israel, so that Pharaoh will know the LORD makes a difference between Egypt and Israel, and Moses leaves Pharaoh in great anger.

Exodus 11:9–10 — Pharaoh’s Refusal Closes the Plague Narrative

The LORD tells Moses that Pharaoh will not listen, so that His wonders may be multiplied. Moses and Aaron have done all these wonders, yet the LORD hardened Pharaoh’s heart, and Pharaoh did not let Israel go.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 11 shows that God’s judgment is neither sudden nor arbitrary. Pharaoh was warned from the start that refusing to release God’s firstborn would cost him his own (4:22–23), and he refused through nine plagues. The coming deaths are terrible, and Scripture does not make them light, but they are the just answer to a long and deliberate refusal.

The chapter also shows the LORD’s care for His people in the same moment. While Egypt faces its greatest grief, Israel will not even hear a dog growl. God knows the difference between those who are His and those who are not, and that distinction prepares for the Passover, where He provides the way of shelter.

Key Themes to Watch For

The firstborn. The final judgment answers Pharaoh’s refusal to release God’s firstborn son (11:5; compare 4:22–23).

The LORD makes a difference. God distinguishes between Egypt and Israel even in the night of judgment (11:7).

Favor and provision. God gives Israel favor with the Egyptians and prepares them to leave with silver and gold (11:2–3).

Hardening and multiplied wonders. Pharaoh’s refusal becomes the occasion for God’s wonders to be multiplied (11:9–10).

Key Words & Concepts

One more plague (11:1). The tenth and final plague, announced here and carried out in 12:29–30.

About midnight (11:4). The hour when the LORD Himself will go out through Egypt in judgment.

The handmill (11:5). A stone mill for grinding grain, worked by the lowest servants. The judgment will reach from the throne to the humblest household.

A dog move its tongue (11:7). An expression for complete safety. Not even a dog will growl at Israel or their animals.
