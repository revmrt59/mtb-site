Exodus 12:1
Exodus 12:1
NKJV

Exodus 12:1 Now the LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt, saying,(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 12:1 While the Israelites were still in the land of Egypt, the LORD gave the following instructions to Moses and Aaron: (NLT)

Main Idea

The LORD speaks to Moses and Aaron while they are still in Egypt. The instructions that follow are given before the deliverance, so Israel must act on God’s word in advance.

Verse Explanation

The LORD speaks to Moses and Aaron while they are still in Egypt. The instructions that follow are given before the deliverance, so Israel must act on God’s word in advance.

“Now the LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt, saying,”

The scene shifts from the palace to instructions for Israel. The narrator notes that God spoke “in the land of Egypt.” The Passover was given before the deliverance, while Israel was still in bondage. They had to act on God’s word before they saw it fulfilled.

Study Notes

Most of Israel’s laws were given at Sinai. The Passover is the exception, given in Egypt itself, because it is bound up with the night of the exodus.

NKJV

NLT

Exodus 12:2 “From now on, this month will be the first month of the year for you. (NLT)

Main Idea

“This month shall be your beginning of months.” God resets Israel’s calendar around the night He will redeem them, so their year begins with His act of deliverance.

Verse Explanation

“This month shall be your beginning of months.” God resets Israel’s calendar around the night He will redeem them, so their year begins with His act of deliverance.

“This month shall be your beginning of months; it shall be the first month of the year to you.”

God resets Israel’s calendar. The month of their deliverance becomes “the first month of the year.” Their year will now begin with God’s act of redemption. Every year would start by remembering how God brought them out.

Study Notes

This month is later called Abib (13:4; 23:15; 34:18), meaning young ears of grain, and after the exile it was called Nisan (Nehemiah 2:1; Esther 3:7). It falls in March–April. Israel also kept a civil year beginning in the autumn, which later Judaism observed as the new year.

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

On the tenth day of the month every man is to take a lamb for his father’s house, “a lamb for a household.” The Passover is centered in Israel’s homes, and verse 4 explains how small households will share a lamb with their neighbors.

“Speak to all the congregation of Israel, saying:”

The instructions go to “all the congregation of Israel.” This is the first time Exodus calls Israel a “congregation,” an assembled people under God.

“On the tenth of this month every man shall take for himself a lamb, according to the house of his father, a lamb for a household.”

On “the tenth” day, each family is to choose its lamb. The Passover centers in the home: “a lamb for a household.” Each family would have its own lamb, its own meal, and its own blood on the door.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word translated “lamb” can refer to a young sheep or a young goat, as 12:5 makes clear.

NLT

Exodus 12:5 The animal you select must be a one-year-old male, either a sheep or a goat, with no defects. (NLT)

Main Idea

The lamb must be “without blemish, a male of the first year,” taken from the sheep or the goats. Only a whole, healthy animal is fit for this purpose, and the New Testament later sees Christ as the Lamb without blemish.

Verse Explanation

The lamb must be “without blemish, a male of the first year,” taken from the sheep or the goats. Only a whole, healthy animal is fit for this purpose, and the New Testament later sees Christ as the Lamb without blemish.

“Your lamb shall be without blemish, a male of the first year.”

The lamb must be “without blemish,” whole and healthy, with no defect. It must be “a male of the first year,” a young animal in its prime. Only the best was fit for this purpose.

“You may take it from the sheep or from the goats.”

Either a sheep or a goat was acceptable. The requirement concerned the animal’s soundness, not its kind.

Study Notes

The requirement that sacrificial animals be without blemish runs through the law (Leviticus 22:19–22).

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

Israel is to keep the lamb until the fourteenth day, and then the whole assembly will kill it at twilight. The entire congregation acts together at the same appointed time.

“Now you shall keep it until the fourteenth day of the same month.”

The family was to keep the lamb for four days, from the tenth to “the fourteenth.” During that time the lamb could be watched to confirm it had no defect.

“Then the whole assembly of the congregation of Israel shall kill it at twilight.”

All Israel acts together. “The whole assembly” kills the lambs at the same appointed time, “at twilight.” Though each household kills its own lamb, the whole people acts as one.

Study Notes

The phrase translated “at twilight” reads literally between the two evenings. It most likely refers to the time between sunset and dark. Later Jewish practice understood it as the afternoon, from about three o’clock to sunset.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word for “Passover” is “pesach,” from a verb meaning to pass over (12:13, 23, 27). Some interpreters understand the verb as protecting or hovering over, since Isaiah 31:5 uses it alongside “defending” and “preserving.”

Main Idea

God declares that He will pass through Egypt that night, strike every firstborn of man and beast, and execute judgment “against all the gods of Egypt.” The final plague is God’s judgment on Egypt and its false gods.

Verse Explanation

God declares that He will pass through Egypt that night, strike every firstborn of man and beast, and execute judgment “against all the gods of Egypt.” The final plague is God’s judgment on Egypt and its false gods.

“For I will pass through the land of Egypt on that night,”

God will “pass through” Egypt Himself.

“and will strike all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, both man and beast;”

The judgment announced in 11:5 will fall on “all the firstborn,” human and animal.

“and against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgment: I am the LORD.”

The final plague is also God’s “judgment” on “all the gods of Egypt.” The plagues have shown that Egypt’s gods cannot protect it. This last blow strikes the land they were thought to guard. “I am the LORD.” He alone is God.

Study Notes

Numbers 33:4 says, “Also on their gods the LORD had executed judgments.” Egypt worshiped many gods connected with the Nile, the land, the sky, and Pharaoh himself. Interpreters have linked individual plagues with particular gods. The text speaks of God’s judgment on Egypt’s gods in general, so specific links are illustrative rather than stated.

Exodus 12:15

NLT

Exodus 12:15 For seven days the bread you eat must be made without yeast. On the first day of the festival, remove every trace of yeast from your homes. Anyone who eats bread made with yeast during the seven days of the festival will be cut off from the community of Israel. (NLT)

Main Idea

For seven days they are to eat unleavened bread and remove leaven from their houses on the first day, and anyone who eats leavened bread during that time will be cut off from Israel. The Passover is joined to a week-long feast that God takes with great seriousness.

Verse Explanation

For seven days they are to eat unleavened bread and remove leaven from their houses on the first day, and anyone who eats leavened bread during that time will be cut off from Israel. The Passover is joined to a week-long feast that God takes with great seriousness.

“Seven days you shall eat unleavened bread.”

The Passover is joined to a week-long feast of “unleavened bread.”

“On the first day you shall remove leaven from your houses.”

Every house must be cleared of leaven on the first day. Leaven was a portion of fermented dough kept from a previous batch to make new bread rise.

“For whoever eats leavened bread from the first day until the seventh day, that person shall be cut off from Israel.”

The penalty is severe. Anyone who eats leavened bread during the feast “shall be cut off from Israel.” God takes the feast with great seriousness. Paul later draws on this feast when he urges believers to purge out the old leaven and keep the feast “with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth” (1 Corinthians 5:7–8).

Study Notes

The phrase “cut off” means to be removed from the community of God’s people. Interpreters differ on whether it referred to death, exclusion, or judgment left in God’s hands.

The Passover is to be eaten in one house, none of the meat carried outside, “nor shall you break one of its bones.” The lamb is kept whole, and John 19:36 sees this fulfilled in Christ’s death.

Verse Explanation

The Passover is to be eaten in one house, none of the meat carried outside, “nor shall you break one of its bones.” The lamb is kept whole, and John 19:36 sees this fulfilled in Christ’s death.

“In one house it shall be eaten; you shall not carry any of the flesh outside the house,”

The lamb is eaten “in one house.” None of the meat may be taken outside. The household that shelters under the blood eats the lamb together.

“nor shall you break one of its bones.”

The lamb’s bones must not be broken. The lamb is kept whole.

Study Notes

John records that the soldiers did not break Jesus’ legs on the cross, and he sees this as fulfilling the Scripture, “Not one of His bones shall be broken” (John 19:36; compare Numbers 9:12; Psalm 34:20).

NLT

Exodus 12:48 “If there are foreigners living among you who want to celebrate the LORD’s Passover, let all their males be circumcised. Only then may they celebrate the Passover with you like any native-born Israelite. But no uncircumcised male may ever eat the Passover meal. (NLT)

Main Idea

A stranger who wants to keep the Passover must have all his males circumcised, and then he may keep it as a native of the land, for no uncircumcised person may eat it. The door is open to outsiders who join themselves to the LORD through the covenant sign.

Verse Explanation

A stranger who wants to keep the Passover must have all his males circumcised, and then he may keep it as a native of the land, for no uncircumcised person may eat it. The door is open to outsiders who join themselves to the LORD through the covenant sign.

“And when a stranger dwells with you and wants to keep the Passover to the LORD,”

God makes provision for “a stranger” who lives among Israel and “wants to keep the Passover.” An outsider may come in by choice.

“let all his males be circumcised, and then let him come near and keep it;”

The way in is circumcision. All the males of his household must be “circumcised.” Then he may “come near and keep it.”

“and he shall be as a native of the land. For no uncircumcised person shall eat it.”

Once circumcised, he is “as a native of the land.” He stands with Israel as one of them. The door is open to outsiders who join themselves to the LORD through the covenant sign, but “no uncircumcised person shall eat it.”

None.
