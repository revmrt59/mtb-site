Exodus 12 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Exodus 12 records the night the LORD brought Israel out of Egypt: He gives the Passover lamb and its blood as the sign that shelters Israel’s houses, strikes Egypt’s firstborn at midnight, and leads His people out after 430 years. Instructions, events, and lasting ordinances are woven together, so that the night of rescue becomes a memorial to be kept throughout Israel’s generations.

Chapter Sections

1. Exodus 12:1–13 — The LORD Gives the Passover Lamb and the Sign of the Blood

The LORD makes this month the first of Israel’s year and commands each household, sharing with its neighbor if it is too small, to take a lamb “without blemish” (Exodus 12:5), kill it at twilight, put its blood on the doorposts and lintel, and eat it in haste, ready to leave. He will strike Egypt’s firstborn and judge all its gods, but “when I see the blood, I will pass over you” (Exodus 12:13).

2. Exodus 12:14–20 — The Day Becomes a Memorial and the Feast of Unleavened Bread

The day is to be a memorial kept “as a feast to the LORD throughout your generations” (Exodus 12:14). For seven days Israel is to remove leaven and eat unleavened bread, holding holy convocations on the first and seventh days, because on that day the LORD brought their armies out of Egypt.

3. Exodus 12:21–28 — Moses Instructs the Elders and Israel Worships and Obeys

Moses tells the elders to kill the Passover lamb, apply the blood with hyssop, and stay inside until morning, for the LORD will not allow the destroyer to enter. He commands them to keep this service in the land and to answer their children’s question, and the people bow their heads, worship, and do as the LORD commanded.

4. Exodus 12:29–36 — The LORD Strikes Egypt’s Firstborn and Pharaoh Sends Israel Out

At midnight the LORD strikes every firstborn in Egypt, and a great cry rises, “for there was not a house where there was not one dead” (Exodus 12:30). Pharaoh summons Moses and Aaron and tells them to go and serve the LORD, the Egyptians urge them to leave in haste, and the LORD gives Israel favor so that the Egyptians grant the silver, gold, and clothing they ask for.

5. Exodus 12:37–42 — Israel Departs After 430 Years on a Night to Be Observed

About six hundred thousand men on foot, besides children, journey from Rameses to Succoth with a mixed multitude and much livestock, baking unleavened cakes because they were driven out in haste. At the end of 430 years all the armies of the LORD go out of Egypt, and the night becomes “a solemn observance for all the children of Israel throughout their generations” (Exodus 12:42).

6. Exodus 12:43–51 — Who May Keep the Passover

The ordinance of the Passover sets who may eat it: no foreigner, sojourner, or hired servant may eat it, a bought servant may eat it once he is circumcised, and a stranger may keep it once all his males are circumcised, for “One law shall be for the native-born and for the stranger” (Exodus 12:49). It also sets how it is eaten, in one house with none of its flesh carried outside and not one of its bones broken, and Israel does as the LORD commanded, and on that same day the LORD brings them out of Egypt.
