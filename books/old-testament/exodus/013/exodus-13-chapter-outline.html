Exodus 13 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Exodus 13 gathers the lasting meaning of the exodus into the consecration of the firstborn and the Feast of Unleavened Bread, so that every generation will tell how the LORD brought them out by strength of hand. The chapter then follows Israel’s first steps on the road, as God leads them the long way through the wilderness, Moses carries Joseph’s bones, and the LORD goes before them in the pillar of cloud and fire.

Chapter Sections

1. Exodus 13:1–2 — The LORD Claims Every Firstborn

The LORD commands Moses, “Consecrate to Me all the firstborn” (Exodus 13:2), whether of man or beast. Every firstborn among the children of Israel belongs to Him.

2. Exodus 13:3–10 — Remember This Day with Unleavened Bread

Moses tells the people to remember the day they came out of the house of bondage and to keep the Feast of Unleavened Bread in the land the LORD gives them. Each father is to tell his son, “This is done because of what the LORD did for me when I came up from Egypt” (Exodus 13:8), and the practice will be a sign on the hand and a memorial between the eyes.

3. Exodus 13:11–16 — Redeeming the Firstborn as a Testimony

In the land Israel is to set apart every firstborn male animal to the LORD, redeem the firstborn of a donkey with a lamb, and redeem every firstborn son. When a son asks, “What is this?” (Exodus 13:14), the father will explain that the LORD killed Egypt’s firstborn when Pharaoh refused, and that by strength of hand He brought Israel out.

4. Exodus 13:17–22 — God Leads Israel by the Wilderness Way in Cloud and Fire

God leads the people by the wilderness of the Red Sea rather than the near road through Philistine land, lest they see war and return to Egypt, and Moses takes Joseph’s bones as Joseph had asked. The LORD goes before them in a pillar of cloud by day and a pillar of fire by night, and He does not take either pillar away from before the people.
