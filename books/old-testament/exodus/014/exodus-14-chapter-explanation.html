Exodus 14:1

NLT

Main Idea

Exodus 14:2
Exodus 14:2
NKJV

Exodus 14:2 "Speak to the children of Israel, that they turn and camp before Pi Hahiroth, between Migdol and the sea, opposite Baal Zephon; you shall camp before it by the sea.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 14:2 “Order the Israelites to turn back and camp by Pi-hahiroth between Migdol and the sea. Camp there along the shore, across from Baal-zephon. (NLT)

Main Idea

Israel is to turn back and camp before Pi Hahiroth, between Migdol and the sea, opposite Baal Zephon. God deliberately places His people where they appear to be trapped.

Verse Explanation

Israel is to turn back and camp before Pi Hahiroth, between Migdol and the sea, opposite Baal Zephon. God deliberately places His people where they appear to be trapped.

“Speak to the children of Israel, that they turn and camp before Pi Hahiroth,”

God tells Israel to “turn” back. Instead of pressing on into the wilderness from Etham (13:20), they are to change direction and camp near “Pi Hahiroth.”

“between Migdol and the sea, opposite Baal Zephon; you shall camp before it by the sea.”

The camp will be hemmed in: “Migdol” on one side, “the sea” on another, “Baal Zephon” across from them. God deliberately places His people where they appear to be trapped.

Study Notes

The exact locations of Pi Hahiroth, Migdol, and Baal Zephon are uncertain. Migdol means tower and may have been an Egyptian fortress. Baal Zephon was the name of a Canaanite god, and the place probably had a shrine to him.

Exodus 14:4
Exodus 14:4
NKJV

Exodus 14:4 Then I will harden Pharaoh's heart, so that he will pursue them; and I will gain honor over Pharaoh and over all his army, that the Egyptians may know that I am the LORD." And they did so.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 14:4 And once again I will harden Pharaoh’s heart, and he will chase after you. I have planned this in order to display my glory through Pharaoh and his whole army. After this the Egyptians will know that I am the LORD!” So the Israelites camped there as they were told. (NLT)

Main Idea

God will harden Pharaoh’s heart to pursue, and He will gain honor over Pharaoh and his army, “that the Egyptians may know that I am the LORD,” and Israel obeys. The coming confrontation will reveal the LORD to Egypt.

Verse Explanation

God will harden Pharaoh’s heart to pursue, and He will gain honor over Pharaoh and his army, “that the Egyptians may know that I am the LORD,” and Israel obeys. The coming confrontation will reveal the LORD to Egypt.

“Then I will harden Pharaoh’s heart, so that he will pursue them;”

God will “harden Pharaoh’s heart” once more. Pharaoh had let Israel go only under the crushing weight of the last plague. Now God will confirm him in his true desire to get them back.

“and I will gain honor over Pharaoh and over all his army, that the Egyptians may know that I am the LORD.”

God’s purpose is to “gain honor” over Pharaoh and his army. The Hebrew word means to be glorified, to show His weight and greatness. The Egyptians will learn “that I am the LORD.” The coming confrontation will reveal the LORD to Egypt.

“And they did so.”

Israel obeyed. They turned and camped where God told them, though it made no sense by human reasoning.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb translated “gain honor” comes from the root meaning to be heavy, the same root used for Pharaoh’s heart becoming “hard” (heavy) in 7:14; 8:15, 32; 9:7, 34. Pharaoh made his heart heavy against God, and now God will make Himself heavy, that is, glorious, over Pharaoh.

When the king of Egypt hears that the people have fled, he and his servants turn against them and ask, “Why have we done this, that we have let Israel go from serving us?” Egypt regrets losing its slaves almost as soon as Israel is gone.

“Now it was told the king of Egypt that the people had fled,”

Word reaches Pharaoh that “the people had fled.” Pharaoh may have expected Israel to return after a three days’ journey to worship (5:3). Their turn toward the sea now looks like flight.

“and the heart of Pharaoh and his servants was turned against the people;”

Pharaoh and his officials change their minds. Their hearts are “turned against the people.” The grief of the last plague gives way to regret.

“and they said, ‘Why have we done this, that we have let Israel go from serving us?”

They ask “Why have we done this?” Their concern is the loss of their labor force. Israel had been serving Egypt. Egypt regrets losing its slaves almost as soon as Israel is gone.

The phrase “with boldness” reads literally with a high hand. Numbers 33:3 uses the same expression of Israel leaving Egypt “in the sight of all the Egyptians.”

Verse Explanation

The people ask Moses, “Because there were no graves in Egypt, have you taken us away to die in the wilderness?” Their fear turns into bitter accusation against Moses.

“Then they said to Moses, ‘Because there were no graves in Egypt, have you taken us away to die in the wilderness?”

The people turn on Moses with bitter sarcasm. Egypt was famous for its tombs and burial places. “Because there were no graves in Egypt” mocks the idea that Moses had to bring them out to die. Their fear turns into accusation.

“Why have you so dealt with us, to bring us up out of Egypt?”

They blame Moses for the exodus itself. They speak as though Moses, not God, had brought them out.

Their conclusion is that slavery would have been “better” than dying in the wilderness. In their panic they prefer slavery to trusting God. This complaint will return again and again in the wilderness (16:3; 17:3; Numbers 14:2–4).

Exodus 14:15–18 — The LORD Commands Israel to Go Forward

Exodus 14:15
Exodus 14:15
NKJV

Exodus 14:15 And the LORD said to Moses, "Why do you cry to Me? Tell the children of Israel to go forward.(NKJV)

NLT

Main Idea

The LORD says to Moses, “Why do you cry to Me? Tell the children of Israel to go forward.” The call to stand and see is followed by the command to move.

Verse Explanation

The LORD says to Moses, “Why do you cry to Me? Tell the children of Israel to go forward.” The call to stand and see is followed by the command to move.

“And the LORD said to Moses, ‘Why do you cry to Me?”

God speaks to Moses. “Why do you cry to Me?” The text has not recorded Moses crying out, but he may have been praying along with the people (14:10). Now is not the time to keep calling out. It is time to act.

“Tell the children of Israel to go forward.”

God commands Israel to “go forward.” Moses had told them to stand still and see God’s salvation (14:13). Now God tells them to move. Standing still meant not giving in to panic. Going forward meant obeying when God opened the way. Both belong together. They stand firm in trust, and they move when God speaks.

Israel “saw the great work,” literally the great hand, of the LORD. The phrase recalls the strong hand by which God brought them out (13:3, 9, 14, 16).

The sight of God’s salvation changes Israel’s response. The people who were very afraid of the Egyptians (14:10) now “feared the LORD.” Their fear turns to reverence. They “believed the LORD and His servant Moses.” The people who accused Moses (14:11–12) now trust him as God’s servant. The sight of God’s salvation leads His people to reverence and faith. Paul later describes Israel as having been “baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea” (1 Corinthians 10:2).
