Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 14
Main Idea:

The LORD leads Israel to the edge of the sea, draws Pharaoh into pursuit, and saves His people by dividing the waters and overthrowing Egypt’s army, so that Egypt knows He is the LORD and Israel fears Him and believes.

Setting:

Exodus 14 follows the first days of the journey under the pillar of cloud and fire (13:17–22). The exact crossing point is debated, but the crossing itself is God’s miracle.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 14:1–4 — The LORD Directs Israel to Camp by the Sea

  • Exodus 14:5–9 — Pharaoh Pursues Israel with His Chariots

  • Exodus 14:10–14 — Israel Cries Out and Moses Calls Them to See the LORD’s Salvation

  • Exodus 14:15–18 — The LORD Commands Israel to Go Forward

  • Exodus 14:19–25 — The Angel and the Cloud Protect Israel as the Sea Is Divided

  • Exodus 14:26–31 — The Sea Returns and Israel Fears and Believes the LORD

Key Verse:

“And Moses said to the people, ‘Do not be afraid. Stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD, which He will accomplish for you today. For the Egyptians whom you see today, you shall see again no more forever.” (Exodus 14:13)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

God deliberately leads Israel to a place that looks like a trap, with the sea in front and the wilderness around them. He tells Moses why: Pharaoh will pursue, and the LORD will “gain honor over Pharaoh and over all his army, that the Egyptians may know that I am the LORD” (14:4). When the chariots appear, Israel’s boldness collapses into panic and bitter accusation. Moses answers with one of the great words of Scripture: “Stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD” (14:13), and “The LORD will fight for you” (14:14).

Then the LORD adds, “Tell the children of Israel to go forward” (14:15). Trust waits for God and then obeys His next word. The Angel of God and the cloud move behind Israel, bringing darkness to Egypt and light to Israel. The LORD drives the sea back all night, and Israel walks through on dry ground with walls of water on either side. When Egypt follows, the LORD throws their army into confusion until they cry, “the LORD fights for them” (14:25). The waters return, and “So the LORD saved Israel that day” (14:30). Seeing His great work, the people fear the LORD and believe Him.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 14:1–4 — The LORD Directs Israel to Camp by the Sea

The LORD tells Israel to turn back and camp by the sea, so that Pharaoh will think they are trapped. He will harden Pharaoh’s heart to pursue them and gain honor over him, so that Egypt will know He is the LORD.

Exodus 14:5–9 — Pharaoh Pursues Israel with His Chariots

Pharaoh and his servants regret losing their slaves, and Pharaoh sets out with six hundred choice chariots and all the chariots of Egypt. The LORD hardens his heart, and the army overtakes Israel camped by the sea.

Exodus 14:10–14 — Israel Cries Out and Moses Calls Them to See the LORD’s Salvation

Seeing the Egyptians, the people cry out to the LORD and accuse Moses of bringing them out to die. Moses tells them not to fear, to stand still and see the LORD’s salvation, because the LORD will fight for them.

Exodus 14:15–18 — The LORD Commands Israel to Go Forward

The LORD tells Moses to command the people to go forward and to stretch his rod over the sea to divide it. He will harden the Egyptians to follow, and they will know He is the LORD when He gains honor over Pharaoh’s army.

Exodus 14:19–25 — The Angel and the Cloud Protect Israel as the Sea Is Divided

The Angel of God and the pillar of cloud move behind Israel, darkness to Egypt and light to Israel, while the LORD drives back the sea all night and Israel crosses on dry ground. When the Egyptians follow, the LORD troubles their army and takes off their chariot wheels until they confess that He fights for Israel.

Exodus 14:26–31 — The Sea Returns and Israel Fears and Believes the LORD

At the LORD’s command Moses stretches out his hand, the sea returns, and not one of the pursuing army survives. The LORD saves Israel that day, and the people fear the LORD and believe Him and His servant Moses.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 14 is the great picture of salvation that only God can accomplish. Israel faced Pharaoh’s chariots with no way out, and the LORD fought for them and opened a road no one could have made. Paul says Israel was “baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea” (1 Corinthians 10:2), and he uses their story to warn believers that being rescued must lead to continued trust (1 Corinthians 10:1–12).

The chapter also holds together two parts of faith: standing still to see what God will do, and stepping forward when He says go. Israel’s fear did not stop God from saving them, but His salvation changed them. They feared the LORD and believed Him.

Key Themes to Watch For

The LORD gains honor. God acts at the sea so that Egypt will know He is the LORD (14:4, 17–18).

Stand still and go forward. Israel is called to trust God’s salvation and to obey His command to move (14:13–16).

The LORD fights for His people. Moses promises it, and the Egyptians confess it (14:14, 25).

God’s protecting presence. The Angel of God and the cloud stand between Israel and their enemies (14:19–20).

Fear and faith. Israel’s terror gives way to reverence and belief after seeing God’s work (14:10–12, 31).

Key Words & Concepts

Pi Hahiroth (14:2). The place by the sea where God told Israel to camp. Its exact location, like the crossing point, is debated.

The Angel of God (14:19). The divine messenger who moves with the cloud to stand between Israel and Egypt. He is likely an appearance of Christ, like the Angel of the LORD at the bush (3:2–6).

A strong east wind (14:21). The means God used to drive back the sea. The waters standing “a wall to them on their right hand and on their left” (14:22) show it was a miracle, not a natural event.

The morning watch (14:24). The last part of the night, just before dawn, when the LORD threw the Egyptian army into confusion.
