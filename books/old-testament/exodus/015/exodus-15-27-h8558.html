PALM - ( tamar , Strong's H8558 )

Summary:

The Hebrew term tamar refers specifically to the date palm tree (Phoenix dactylifera), a majestic and vital symbol of life, endurance, and fruitfulness in the arid environments of the ancient Near East. In Exodus 15:27, the mention of seventy palm trees at Elim highlights God's unexpected provision of rest, shade, and sustenance for the weary Israelites immediately following their bitter trials at Marah.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root word evokes the towering, upright posture of the date palm, which can grow up to eighty feet high with a crown of sweeping, evergreen fronds.

• In the ancient Near East, the date palm was often called the "tree of life" because every part of it—fruit, leaves, bark, and sap—was utilized for food, shelter, and fuel.

• The presence of palm trees naturally indicated the availability of underground water sources, making them an immediate and welcome visual signal of an oasis to desert travelers.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The substantive noun tamar literally means a palm tree or date palm.

• It describes both the physical tree itself and, by extension, objects or architectural features carved in the shape of palm trees due to their pleasing symmetry and beauty.

• The term carries a strong association with sweetness and nourishment because of the abundant, energy-rich dates produced by the tree.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The palm tree frequently serves as a biblical metaphor for righteousness and spiritual vitality, as seen in Psalm 92:12: "The righteous shall flourish like a palm tree."

• Because the palm remains green and fruitful even in desert conditions with deep root systems accessing hidden water, it pictures steadfast faith under pressure.

• In architectural contexts, carved palm trees adorned Solomon's temple (1 Kings 6:29), symbolizing Edenic beauty, paradise, and the dwelling place of God.

4. Theological Implications

• Elim's seventy palm trees and twelve springs (Exodus 15:27) serve as a vivid sacramental sign of divine grace, transforming a place of potential despair into a sanctuary of rest.

• The juxtaposition of Marah (bitterness) and Elim (palms and springs) teaches that God frequently pairs testing with restorative grace, providing sufficient physical and spiritual sustenance for the journey.

• The evergreen nature of the palm points toward eternal life and the ultimate victory of God's people, a motif that echoes forward to the palm branches waved by the multitude in Revelation 7:9.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Exodus 15:27, the word functions geographically and historically to ground the narrative in a real, life-sustaining oasis encountered right after the bitter waters of Marah.

• The specific number of seventy palm trees matches the seventy elders of Israel (Exodus 24:1), subtly emphasizing complete provision for the entire assembly of God's people.

• The palms provided essential shade and comfort, addressing the immediate bodily exhaustion of the Israelites as they transitioned from slavery to freedom.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Leviticus 23:40 - "And you shall take for yourselves on the first day the fruit of beautiful trees, branches of palm trees, the boughs of the leafy trees, and willows of the brook..."

• Judges 4:5 - "And she [Deborah] would sit under the palm tree of Deborah between Ramah and Bethel in the mountains of Ephraim..."

• Psalm 92:12 - "The righteous shall flourish like a palm tree, He shall grow like a cedar in Lebanon."

• Song of Solomon 7:7 - "This stature of yours is like a palm tree, and your breasts like its clusters."

• John 12:13 - "...took branches of palm trees and went out to meet Him, and cried out: 'Hosanna! "Blessed is He who comes in the name of the LORD!"'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The ancient city of Jericho was famously known in antiquity as "the city of palm trees" (Deuteronomy 34:3; Judges 3:13) due to the immense groves fed by Elisha's spring.

• In ancient coinage and victory monuments, the palm branch was universally recognized as a symbol of triumph, peace, and jubilation.
