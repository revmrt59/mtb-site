Exodus 15:1
Exodus 15:1
NKJV

Exodus 15:1 Then Moses and the children of Israel sang this song to the LORD, and spoke, saying: "I will sing to the LORD, For He has triumphed gloriously! The horse and its rider He has thrown into the sea!(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 15:1 Then Moses and the people of Israel sang this song to the LORD: “I will sing to the LORD,for he has triumphed gloriously;he has hurled both horse and riderinto the sea. (NLT)

Main Idea

Moses and the children of Israel sing to the LORD, “I will sing to the LORD, For He has triumphed gloriously! The horse and its rider He has thrown into the sea!” Israel’s first response on the far shore is worship that gives all the credit to God.

Verse Explanation

Moses and the children of Israel sing to the LORD, “I will sing to the LORD, For He has triumphed gloriously! The horse and its rider He has thrown into the sea!” Israel’s first response on the far shore is worship that gives all the credit to God.

“Then Moses and the children of Israel sang this song to the LORD, and spoke, saying:”

Israel’s first response on the far shore is song. Moses leads, and the whole people join. The song is sung “to the LORD,” addressed to Him and in His honor. This is the first song recorded in the Bible.

“I will sing to the LORD, For He has triumphed gloriously!”

The singer gives the reason for singing. The word translated triumphed gloriously reads literally He is highly exalted or He has risen up gloriously. God has shown Himself supreme.

“The horse and its rider He has thrown into the sea!”

The opening verse sums up what happened. “He has thrown” Egypt’s chariot force “into the sea.” The song gives all the credit to God. Israel did not fight. The LORD won the battle. Revelation 15:3 later pictures the redeemed singing “the song of Moses, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb,” joining this deliverance at the sea to God’s final victory over His enemies.

None.

Exodus 15:2

Exodus 15:2 The LORD is my strength and song, And He has become my salvation; He is my God, and I will praise Him; My father's God, and I will exalt Him.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 15:2 The LORD is my strength and my song;he has given me victory.This is my God, and I will praise him —my father’s God, and I will exalt him! (NLT)

Main Idea

“The LORD is my strength and song, And He has become my salvation,” and the singers promise to praise and exalt Him as their God and their fathers’ God. The LORD is not only the One who saved them but the One they now sing about.

Verse Explanation

“The LORD is my strength and song, And He has become my salvation,” and the singers promise to praise and exalt Him as their God and their fathers’ God. The LORD is not only the One who saved them but the One they now sing about.

“The LORD is my strength and song, And He has become my salvation;”

The singer moves from what God did to who He is. “The LORD is my strength,” the source of power. He is also the singer’s “song,” the theme of praise. He “has become my salvation.” The word salvation echoes Moses’ promise at the sea, “see the salvation of the LORD” (14:13). What God promised, He has now done.

“He is my God, and I will praise Him; My father’s God, and I will exalt Him.”

The singer claims God personally, “my God,” and also as “My father’s God,” the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. The God who made promises to the fathers has kept them. The singer responds by praising and exalting Him.

Study Notes

The words of the first line appear again in Psalm 118:14 and Isaiah 12:2. Later generations used the song of the sea to praise God for new deliverances.

Verse Explanation

The song turns from judgment to love. God led His people “in Your mercy,” His steadfast covenant love. They are “the people whom You have redeemed,” bought back from slavery. God had promised, “I will redeem you with an outstretched arm” (6:6).

The Hebrew word translated “mercy” is chesed, God’s loyal, steadfast love to His covenant people.

The “holy habitation” may refer to Sinai, to the promised land, or to the place where God would later set His sanctuary. The song looks toward all of these as God brings His people to Himself (15:17).

“Now when they came to Marah, they could not drink the waters of Marah, for they were bitter.”

At last they find water at “Marah,” but it is “bitter,” undrinkable. Their hope turns to disappointment.

“Therefore the name of it was called Marah.”

The place is named for its water. “Marah” means bitter. The name records the bitterness of the disappointment.

Study Notes

Naomi later says, “Call me Mara, for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me” (Ruth 1:20). The name is from the same root.

Main Idea

Moses cries out to the LORD, who shows him a tree, and when Moses casts it into the water, the water becomes sweet; there God makes a statute for them and tests them. God meets the need and uses it to test His people.

Verse Explanation

Moses cries out to the LORD, who shows him a tree, and when Moses casts it into the water, the water becomes sweet; there God makes a statute for them and tests them. God meets the need and uses it to test His people.

“So he cried out to the LORD, and the LORD showed him a tree.”

Moses does what the people did not. He “cried out to the LORD.” God answers by showing him “a tree,” a piece of wood.

“When he cast it into the waters, the waters were made sweet.”

Moses throws it into the water, and “the waters were made sweet.” The text does not say the wood had any natural power to sweeten water. God made the water drinkable when Moses obeyed.

“There He made a statute and an ordinance for them, and there He tested them,”

At Marah God “made a statute and an ordinance,” a standing rule for Israel. He also “tested them.” God meets the need and uses it to test His people. The test is whether they will trust and listen to Him.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb translated “showed” is related to the word torah, meaning instruction. God taught Moses what to do.

Exodus 15:26 and said, "If you diligently heed the voice of the LORD your God and do what is right in His sight, give ear to His commandments and keep all His statutes, I will put none of the diseases on you which I have brought on the Egyptians. For I am the LORD who heals you."(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 15:26 He said, “If you will listen carefully to the voice of the LORD your God and do what is right in his sight, obeying his commands and keeping all his decrees, then I will not make you suffer any of the diseases I sent on the Egyptians; for I am the LORD who heals you.” (NLT)

Main Idea

God promises that if they diligently heed His voice and keep His commands, He will put none of Egypt’s diseases on them, “For I am the LORD who heals you.” The LORD reveals Himself as Israel’s healer and links His promise to their obedience.

Verse Explanation

God promises that if they diligently heed His voice and keep His commands, He will put none of Egypt’s diseases on them, “For I am the LORD who heals you.” The LORD reveals Himself as Israel’s healer and links His promise to their obedience.

“and said, ‘If you diligently heed the voice of the LORD your God and do what is right in His sight,”

God gives the terms of His promise. Israel is to “diligently heed the voice of the LORD,” listening carefully, and to “do what is right in His sight.”

“give ear to His commandments and keep all His statutes,”

They are to listen to and keep His “commandments” and “statutes.” The call to obey comes before Sinai and prepares Israel for the covenant there.

“I will put none of the diseases on you which I have brought on the Egyptians.”

The promise looks back to Egypt. God had brought plagues and “diseases” on the Egyptians, such as the boils (9:8–11). If Israel listens to Him, He will not bring such diseases on them.

“For I am the LORD who heals you.”

God reveals Himself by a new title: “the LORD who heals you.” He healed the bitter water, and He is the healer of His people. The LORD reveals Himself as Israel’s healer and links His promise to their obedience.

Study Notes

The Hebrew phrase is often transliterated “Yahweh Rapha.” The verb means to heal or restore. Psalm 103:3 praises the LORD “who heals all your diseases.” Deuteronomy 28:27, 60 warns that disobedience would bring the diseases of Egypt on Israel.
