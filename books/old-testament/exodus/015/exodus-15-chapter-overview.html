Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 15
Main Idea:

Israel answers the LORD’s victory at the sea with a song that praises the incomparable God who has redeemed His people and will plant them in His own dwelling, and then, at the bitter water of Marah, the LORD reveals Himself as the One who heals them.

Setting:

Exodus 15 stands on the far shore of the sea (14:30–31). Verse 22 begins the journey toward Sinai, the fifth major movement of the book.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 15:1–10 — The LORD Has Triumphed over Pharaoh at the Sea

  • Exodus 15:11–18 — The Incomparable LORD Leads His Redeemed to His Dwelling

  • Exodus 15:19–21 — Miriam Leads the Women in Answering Song

  • Exodus 15:22–27 — The LORD Heals the Bitter Water at Marah and Brings Israel to Elim

Key Verse:

“You in Your mercy have led forth The people whom You have redeemed; You have guided them in Your strength To Your holy habitation.” (Exodus 15:13)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The first thing Israel does on the far shore is sing. “I will sing to the LORD, For He has triumphed gloriously!” (15:1). Every line gives the credit to God. Pharaoh’s army sank like a stone, the waters stood up at the blast of His nostrils, and one breath of His wind undid all the enemy’s boasting. Then the song asks, “Who is like You, O LORD, among the gods?” (15:11), and it looks ahead. The God who redeemed His people is leading them “To Your holy habitation” (15:13) and will plant them in the place He has made “For Your own dwelling” (15:17). The rescue is only the beginning; God is bringing His people to Himself. The song ends, “The LORD shall reign forever and ever” (15:18), and Miriam leads the women in answering song.

Three days later the water is bitter, and the songs turn to complaint. Moses cries to the LORD, who shows him a tree that makes the water sweet. There God tests Israel and promises that if they listen to Him, He will not put on them the diseases of Egypt: “For I am the LORD who heals you” (15:26). Then He brings them to Elim, with twelve springs and seventy palms.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 15:1–10 — The LORD Has Triumphed over Pharaoh at the Sea

Moses and Israel sing to the LORD, who has thrown horse and rider into the sea and has become their strength, song, and salvation. The song celebrates how His right hand shattered the enemy, how the waters stood up at His breath, and how Pharaoh’s army sank like lead.

Exodus 15:11–18 — The Incomparable LORD Leads His Redeemed to His Dwelling

The song declares that no god is like the LORD in holiness and wonders, and that in His faithful love He has led His redeemed people toward His holy dwelling while the nations tremble. He will plant them in the place He has made for Himself, and He will reign forever.

Exodus 15:19–21 — Miriam Leads the Women in Answering Song

The narrator recalls Egypt’s army going down into the sea while Israel walked on dry land. Miriam the prophetess leads the women with timbrels and dancing, answering with the song’s opening words.

Exodus 15:22–27 — The LORD Heals the Bitter Water at Marah and Brings Israel to Elim

After three days without water, Israel finds only bitter water at Marah and complains, and the LORD shows Moses a tree that makes the water sweet. There God tests His people, promises to spare them Egypt’s diseases if they listen to Him, and reveals Himself as the LORD who heals them before bringing them to the springs of Elim.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 15 shows that worship is the right response to redemption. After the LORD saves Israel at the sea, Moses and the people answer with a song about God, not about themselves. The song also reveals where the exodus is going: to God’s own dwelling, which the rest of the book will build toward. Revelation joins “the song of Moses” with “the song of the Lamb” (Revelation 15:3), as the redeemed of every age praise the God who saves.

Marah shows how quickly praise can turn to complaint when need arises, and how patiently God answers. He meets the need, uses it to teach His people to listen, and makes Himself known by a new name, the LORD who heals. Elim follows Marah, and God’s refreshment follows His testing.

Key Themes to Watch For

The LORD as warrior and Savior. God fights for His people and becomes their strength and salvation (15:2–3, 6–10).

The incomparable God. No god compares with the LORD in holiness and wonders (15:11).

Redemption leading to God’s dwelling. The LORD leads His redeemed people toward the place He has made for His own dwelling (15:13, 17).

The LORD’s everlasting reign. The song ends with the LORD as King forever (15:18).

Testing and healing. At Marah God tests Israel and reveals Himself as the LORD who heals (15:25–26).

Key Words & Concepts

A man of war (15:3). The LORD fights as the righteous Defender of oppressed captives against tyranny and evil, never from bloodlust.

Redeemed (15:13). Freed by God’s power. The song calls Israel “the people whom You have redeemed” and “purchased” (15:16).

Miriam the prophetess (15:20). Aaron’s sister, who leads the women in praise. She is most likely the sister who watched over the infant Moses (2:4).

Marah (15:23). A name meaning “bitter,” given to the place where Israel found bitter water.

The LORD who heals you (15:26). God’s self-revelation at Marah, tied to His promise to spare Israel the diseases He brought on Egypt if they listen to Him.
