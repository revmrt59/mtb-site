FLESH - (basar, Strong's H1320)

Summary:

The Hebrew word basar primarily refers to the physical body or tissue of humans and animals, but in contexts like Exodus 16:3, it vividly extends to represent human physical appetite, bodily life, and mortal vulnerability when contrasted with divine provision.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root concept tied to basar involves skin, meat, or the visible, tangible exterior of a living creature.

• In ancient agrarian and sacrificial cultures, flesh was closely associated with sustenance, weakness, and the temporary nature of earthly existence.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Refers fundamentally to the physical body of a human or animal.

• Denotes kinship or blood relation (as in "flesh of my flesh").

• Extends metonymically to mankind in general or human nature in its earthly frailty.

• Signifies physical meat consumed as food, particularly in narratives of hunger and craving.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The term often captures the raw, physical reality of human existence, subject to hunger, pain, and death.

• When used in connection with craving food (as in the wilderness narratives), it highlights physical appetite and the immediate sensory desires of the body.

4. Theological Implications

• Human basar immediately underscores creaturely dependence; mortal flesh requires daily sustenance from the Creator to survive.

• Physical hunger often exposes spiritual discontent, revealing how quickly human nature distrusts divine provision in times of trial.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Exodus 16:3, the Israelites lament in the wilderness, remembering the pots of meat (basar) they sat by in Egypt.

• Here, the word carries a sharp edge of irony and rebellion: the people romanticize their enslavement because their physical bellies were full, prioritizing immediate physical satisfaction over freedom and covenant relationship with God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:23 - "This is now bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh..."

• Genesis 6:3 - "And the Lord said, 'My Spirit shall not strive with man forever, for he is indeed flesh...'"

• Psalm 78:20 - "Indeed, He struck the rock, so that the waters gushed out, and the streams overflowed. Can He give bread also? Can He provide meat (basar) for His people?"

• Isaiah 40:6 - "All flesh is grass, and all its loveliness is like the flower of the field."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The craving for flesh in the wilderness highlights the constant tension between spiritual testing and bodily temptation.

• God responds to this cry for physical meat with quail in the evening, yet the narrative warns against the spiritual danger of letting physical appetites dictate one's trust in God.
