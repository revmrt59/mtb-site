WAFERS - ( ratsiphar , Strong's H6838 )

Summary:

The NKJV word "wafers" in Exodus 16:31 translates a Hebrew term referring to thin, flat cakes or biscuits prepared for offerings or sustenance, specifically describing the texture and form of the miraculous manna provided in the wilderness.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The Hebrew root points to something flat, pressed, or laid down, evoking the image of dough rolled or pressed thin before baking.

• In the ancient Near East, bread baked quickly on hot stones or metal plates was typically flat and unleavened, allowing for rapid preparation during travel or nomadic life.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The noun denotes a thin cake, flatbread, or wafer.

• It describes baked goods made without yeast, characterized by their thin profile and crisp or delicate texture.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The term frequently appears in the Pentateuch in connection with grain offerings and consecration rituals, where unleavened cakes smeared or mixed with oil were presented to the Lord.

• In Exodus 16:31, the word paints a vivid sensory picture of the manna's physical form, noting that alongside its honey-like taste, its consistency resembled thin, flat bread prepared with oil.

4. Theological Implications

• The use of a term associated with sacred offerings to describe the daily bread of the wilderness subtly underscores the sacredness of God's provision.

• Daily sustenance in the desert was not merely survival rations, but a gracious, holy gift from God that sustained His covenant people day by day.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Exodus 16:31, the word specifically defines the culinary texture of the manna, distinguishing it from ordinary coarse bread and highlighting its pleasant, honey-like profile.

• It grounds the miraculous provision in tangible, recognizable human experience while retaining its divine wonder.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 29:2: "and unleavened bread, unleavened cakes mixed with oil, and unleavened wafers anointed with oil (you shall make them of wheat flour)."

• Exodus 29:23: "one loaf of bread, one cake of oiled bread, and one wafer from the basket of the unleavened bread that is before the Lord."

• Leviticus 7:12: "If he offers it for a thanksgiving, then he shall offer, with the sacrifice of thanksgiving, unleavened cakes mixed with oil, unleavened wafers anointed with oil, or cakes of blended flour mixed with oil."

• Numbers 6:15: "a basket of unleavened bread, cakes of fine flour mixed with oil, unleavened wafers anointed with oil, and their grain offering with their drink offerings."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The association of manna with wafers used in sacrificial offerings reminds believers that everyday provision and worship are deeply intertwined in the life of faith.

• The simple, unleavened nature of the wafer highlights the purity and unadorned grace of God's daily gift to Israel.
