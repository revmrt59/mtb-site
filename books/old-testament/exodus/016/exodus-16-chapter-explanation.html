NKJV

Exodus 16:1 And they journeyed from Elim, and all the congregation of the children of Israel came to the Wilderness of Sin, which is between Elim and Sinai, on the fifteenth day of the second month after they departed from the land of Egypt.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 16:1 Then the whole community of Israel set out from Elim and journeyed into the wilderness of Sin, between Elim and Mount Sinai. They arrived there on the fifteenth day of the second month, one month after leaving the land of Egypt. (NLT)

Main Idea

The whole congregation journeys from Elim to the Wilderness of Sin, between Elim and Sinai, a month after leaving Egypt. The next test comes as Israel moves deeper into the wilderness.

Verse Explanation

The whole congregation journeys from Elim to the Wilderness of Sin, between Elim and Sinai, a month after leaving Egypt. The next test comes as Israel moves deeper into the wilderness.

“And they journeyed from Elim, and all the congregation of the children of Israel came to the Wilderness of Sin, which is between Elim and Sinai,”

Israel leaves the oasis of Elim and comes to “the Wilderness of Sin,” a desert region on the way to Mount Sinai.

“on the fifteenth day of the second month after they departed from the land of Egypt.”

The date is precise: “the fifteenth day of the second month,” one month after they left Egypt (12:6, 18; Numbers 33:3). Whatever food they brought from Egypt would likely be running low.

Study Notes

The name Sin has nothing to do with the English word “sin.” It is probably related to the name Sinai. The region is usually placed in the southwestern Sinai Peninsula, though its exact location is uncertain.

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

The LORD tells Moses, “I will rain bread from heaven for you,” and the people are to gather a daily portion so that He may test whether they will walk in His law. God provides food in a way that teaches daily trust and obedience.

“Then the LORD said to Moses, ‘Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you.”

God answers the complaint not with judgment but with provision. “I will rain bread from heaven.” The food will come down from above, as rain falls, and God Himself will give it.

“And the people shall go out and gather a certain quota every day,”

The bread will come “every day.” Each day the people must go out and gather “a certain quota,” a day’s portion. They cannot gather once and stock up.

“that I may test them, whether they will walk in My law or not.”

The daily gathering is a test. Will they “walk in My law,” following God’s instructions about how to gather? God provides food in a way that teaches daily trust and obedience.

Study Notes

Moses later explains the lesson: God fed Israel with manna “that He might make you know that man shall not live by bread alone; but man lives by every word that proceeds from the mouth of the LORD” (Deuteronomy 8:3). Jesus quotes these words when tempted in the wilderness (Matthew 4:4).

Study Notes

Numbers 11:7–9 adds that the manna fell with the dew at night, was ground in mills or beaten in mortars, and was cooked in pans or made into cakes.

Some have connected the manna with natural substances found in Sinai, such as a sweet secretion left by insects on tamarisk trees. Such substances appear only in small amounts, at certain seasons, and do not match the daily supply, the double portion on the sixth day, or the absence on the Sabbath. The text presents the manna as God’s provision from heaven.

Main Idea

Moses explains, “Tomorrow is a Sabbath rest, a holy Sabbath to the LORD,” and tells them to prepare their food and keep the rest until morning. God’s provision teaches Israel the Sabbath before the law at Sinai.

Verse Explanation

Moses explains, “Tomorrow is a Sabbath rest, a holy Sabbath to the LORD,” and tells them to prepare their food and keep the rest until morning. God’s provision teaches Israel the Sabbath before the law at Sinai.

“Then he said to them, ‘This is what the LORD has said:”

Moses explains with God’s word.

“Tomorrow is a Sabbath rest, a holy Sabbath to the LORD.”

This is the first time the word “Sabbath” appears in Scripture. The day is “holy,” set apart “to the LORD.” God rested on the seventh day of creation (Genesis 2:2–3). Now He gives His people a day of rest.

“Bake what you will bake today, and boil what you will boil;”

The people are to cook what they need “today,” on the sixth day, so they need not work on the Sabbath.

“and lay up for yourselves all that remains, to be kept until morning.”

What is left over is to be kept “until morning.” On other days God forbade this (16:19). For the Sabbath He commands it. God’s provision teaches Israel the Sabbath before the law at Sinai.
