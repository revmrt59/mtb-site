Exodus 16 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Exodus 16 shows the LORD answering Israel’s hungry complaints in the Wilderness of Sin by giving quail and bread from heaven, and through that bread teaching His people to trust Him day by day. The chapter moves from complaint to promise, from daily gathering to the gift of the Sabbath, and finally to a jar of manna kept before the LORD as a testimony for later generations.

Chapter Sections

1. Exodus 16:1–3 — Israel Complains of Hunger in the Wilderness of Sin

A month after leaving Egypt, the whole congregation reaches the Wilderness of Sin and complains against Moses and Aaron. They wish they had died in Egypt “when we sat by the pots of meat” (Exodus 16:3) rather than be brought out to die of hunger.

2. Exodus 16:4–12 — The LORD Promises Bread from Heaven and Shows His Glory

The LORD promises, “I will rain bread from heaven for you” (Exodus 16:4), to be gathered daily as a test, with twice as much on the sixth day. Moses tells the people that their complaints are against the LORD, the glory of the LORD appears in the cloud, and the LORD promises meat at twilight and bread in the morning so that they will know that He is the LORD their God.

3. Exodus 16:13–21 — Quail and Manna Are Given and Gathered Daily

Quail cover the camp in the evening, and in the morning a fine substance lies on the ground, which Moses calls “the bread which the LORD has given you to eat” (Exodus 16:15). Each gathers according to need, so that no one has too much or too little, but the manna some keep until morning breeds worms and stinks.

4. Exodus 16:22–30 — The LORD Gives Israel the Sabbath Rest

On the sixth day the people gather twice as much, and Moses explains, “Tomorrow is a Sabbath rest, a holy Sabbath to the LORD” (Exodus 16:23), and what is kept does not spoil. When some go out on the seventh day and find nothing, the LORD rebukes their refusal to keep His commands, and “the people rested on the seventh day” (Exodus 16:30).

5. Exodus 16:31–36 — Manna Is Kept Before the LORD for the Generations

Israel names the bread manna, and Moses commands that an omer of it be kept so that later generations may see the bread the LORD gave in the wilderness. Aaron lays it up before the Testimony, and Israel eats manna forty years, until they come to the border of Canaan.
