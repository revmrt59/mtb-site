Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 16
Main Idea:

The LORD answers Israel’s hungry complaints with quail and bread from heaven, and through the daily manna and the double portion before the Sabbath He teaches His people to trust Him one day at a time and to rest in His provision.

Setting:

Exodus 16 takes place in the Wilderness of Sin, a month after Israel left Egypt and soon after the springs of Elim (15:27). Israel has not yet reached Sinai.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 16:1–3 — Israel Complains of Hunger in the Wilderness of Sin

  • Exodus 16:4–12 — The LORD Promises Bread from Heaven and Shows His Glory

  • Exodus 16:13–21 — Quail and Manna Are Given and Gathered Daily

  • Exodus 16:22–30 — The LORD Gives Israel the Sabbath Rest

  • Exodus 16:31–36 — Manna Is Kept Before the LORD for the Generations

Key Verse:

“Then the LORD said to Moses, ‘Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you. And the people shall go out and gather a certain quota every day, that I may test them, whether they will walk in My law or not.” (Exodus 16:4)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

A month into the wilderness, hunger makes Egypt look good again. The people remember the “pots of meat” (16:3) and forget the whip. Moses tells them the truth about their grumbling: “Your complaints are not against us but against the LORD” (16:8). Yet the LORD answers before He rebukes. His glory appears in the cloud, and He promises meat at twilight and bread in the morning, “And you shall know that I am the LORD your God” (16:12).

The bread comes with a lesson built in. Each person gathers what he needs, and no one has too much or too little. On ordinary days, manna kept overnight breeds worms, so it must be gathered every morning, trusted rather than stored. On the sixth day the LORD commands them to gather twice as much and keep the extra overnight, and it does not spoil, because the seventh day is “a holy Sabbath to the LORD” (16:23). God teaches rest before He gives it as a commandment, and He makes rest possible by His own provision: “See! For the LORD has given you the Sabbath” (16:29). A jar of manna is kept before the LORD so later generations can see how He fed His people for forty years.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 16:1–3 — Israel Complains of Hunger in the Wilderness of Sin

In the Wilderness of Sin the whole congregation complains against Moses and Aaron. They wish they had died in Egypt beside the pots of meat instead of starving in the wilderness.

Exodus 16:4–12 — The LORD Promises Bread from Heaven and Shows His Glory

The LORD promises to rain bread from heaven, gathered daily as a test, with a double portion on the sixth day. Moses tells the people their complaints are against the LORD, the glory of the LORD appears in the cloud, and He promises meat and bread so they will know He is their God.

Exodus 16:13–21 — Quail and Manna Are Given and Gathered Daily

Quail cover the camp in the evening, and in the morning a fine flaky substance lies on the ground, the bread the LORD has given. Each gathers according to need, but on ordinary days manna kept until morning breeds worms and stinks.

Exodus 16:22–30 — The LORD Gives Israel the Sabbath Rest

On the sixth day the people gather twice as much, and Moses explains that tomorrow is a holy Sabbath to the LORD, and the stored manna does not spoil. When some go out on the seventh day and find nothing, the LORD rebukes them, and the people rest.

Exodus 16:31–36 — Manna Is Kept Before the LORD for the Generations

Israel calls the bread manna, and the LORD commands that a jar of it be kept for future generations to see. Aaron lays it up before the Testimony, and Israel eats manna for forty years, until they reach the border of Canaan.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 16 teaches dependence on God one day at a time. The manna could not be hoarded, so Israel had to trust that He would provide again tomorrow. Jesus takes up this chapter when He calls Himself “the bread of life” (John 6:35), the true bread from heaven that gives life to the world.

The chapter also shows that rest is God’s gift before it is His command. He gave a double portion so His people could stop working without fear. The Sabbath is woven into creation and into God’s provision, and believers are called to a deliberate weekly rhythm of ceasing and resting with the Lord.

Key Themes to Watch For

Complaint and God’s patience. Israel grumbles against the LORD, and He answers by showing His glory and feeding them (16:2–3, 7–12).

Daily bread. On ordinary days the manna is gathered by need and cannot be kept overnight, while the sixth-day portion kept for the Sabbath does not spoil (16:4, 16–24).

Testing and obedience. The daily measure tests whether Israel will follow God’s instruction (16:4, 19–20, 27–28).

The gift of the Sabbath. God provides a double portion so His people can rest on the seventh day (16:22–30).

Remembering God’s provision. A jar of manna is kept before the LORD for future generations (16:32–35).

Key Words & Concepts

Wilderness of Sin (16:1). A desert region between Elim and Sinai. The name is a place name, unrelated to the English word “sin.”

Manna (16:31). The name Israel gave the bread from heaven. It looked like white coriander seed and tasted like wafers made with honey.

Omer (16:16, 36). A dry measure, one-tenth of an ephah, the daily portion for each person.

Sabbath (16:23). The seventh day, set apart as holy to the LORD for rest. Here Israel learns it through the manna, before the commandment at Sinai (20:8–11).

Before the Testimony (16:34). In front of the tablets of the covenant, later kept in the ark. The note looks ahead to the time after the tabernacle was built.
