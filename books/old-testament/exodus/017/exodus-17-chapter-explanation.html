Exodus 17

Verse-by-Verse
Exodus 17:1
Exodus 17:1
NKJV

Exodus 17:1 Then all the congregation of the children of Israel set out on their journey from the Wilderness of Sin, according to the commandment of the LORD, and camped in Rephidim; but there was no water for the people to drink.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 17:1 At the LORD’s command, the whole community of Israel left the wilderness of Sin and moved from place to place. Eventually they camped at Rephidim, but there was no water there for the people to drink. (NLT)

Main Idea

The congregation travels from the Wilderness of Sin by the LORD’s command and camps at Rephidim, but there is no water to drink. Israel arrives where God led them and finds a new and real need.

Verse Explanation

The congregation travels from the Wilderness of Sin by the LORD’s command and camps at Rephidim, but there is no water to drink. Israel arrives where God led them and finds a new and real need.

“Then all the congregation of the children of Israel set out on their journey from the Wilderness of Sin, according to the commandment of the LORD,”

Israel moves on from the place where God gave the manna. They travel “according to the commandment of the LORD,” literally according to the mouth of the LORD. They are going where God leads them, probably by the movement of the cloud (13:21–22).

“and camped in Rephidim; but there was no water for the people to drink.”

They camp at “Rephidim,” and there is “no water.” The need is real. In the desert, a large company with children and animals could not survive long without water. Israel arrives where God led them and finds a new need.

Study Notes

Numbers 33:12–14 lists two stops, Dophkah and Alush, between the Wilderness of Sin and Rephidim. The location of Rephidim is uncertain. It is often placed in the southwestern Sinai Peninsula, near the approach to Mount Sinai.

Exodus 17:2
Exodus 17:2
NKJV

Exodus 17:2 Therefore the people contended with Moses, and said, "Give us water, that we may drink." So Moses said to them, "Why do you contend with me? Why do you tempt the LORD?"(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 17:2 So once more the people complained against Moses. “Give us water to drink!” they demanded. “Quiet!” Moses replied. “Why are you complaining against me? And why are you testing the LORD?” (NLT)

Main Idea

The people contend with Moses and demand water, and Moses asks, “Why do you contend with me? Why do you tempt the LORD?” Their quarrel with Moses is really a testing of God.

Verse Explanation

The people contend with Moses and demand water, and Moses asks, “Why do you contend with me? Why do you tempt the LORD?” Their quarrel with Moses is really a testing of God.

“Therefore the people contended with Moses, and said, ‘Give us water, that we may drink.”

The people “contended with Moses.” The word describes a quarrel or a legal dispute. They do not ask God for water. They demand it from Moses: “Give us water.”

“So Moses said to them, ‘Why do you contend with me? Why do you tempt the LORD?”

Moses answers with two questions. Their quarrel with him is really a quarrel with God. To “tempt the LORD” means to put Him to the test, demanding that He prove Himself. Their quarrel with Moses is really a testing of God.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb translated “tempt” here means to test or try. Deuteronomy 6:16 warns, “You shall not tempt the LORD your God as you tempted Him in Massah.” Jesus quoted that verse when the devil tempted Him (Matthew 4:7).

Exodus 17:3 But tormented by thirst, they continued to argue with Moses. “Why did you bring us out of Egypt? Are you trying to kill us, our children, and our livestock with thirst?” (NLT)

Main Idea

The thirsty people complain against Moses and ask why he brought them out of Egypt to kill them, their children, and their livestock with thirst. Their real distress turns into accusation.

Verse Explanation

The thirsty people complain against Moses and ask why he brought them out of Egypt to kill them, their children, and their livestock with thirst. Their real distress turns into accusation.

“And the people thirsted there for water, and the people complained against Moses,”

The thirst was real, and “the people thirsted.” But instead of crying out to God, they “complained against Moses.”

“and said, ‘Why is it you have brought us up out of Egypt, to kill us and our children and our livestock with thirst?”

They accuse Moses of bringing them out “to kill us.” Their fear for their “children” and “livestock” is understandable, but the accusation is not. God had given water at Marah and bread in the wilderness. Their real distress turns into accusation against the one God sent.

Exodus 17:6
Exodus 17:6
NKJV

Exodus 17:6 Behold, I will stand before you there on the rock in Horeb; and you shall strike the rock, and water will come out of it, that the people may drink." And Moses did so in the sight of the elders of Israel.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 17:6 I will stand before you on the rock at Mount Sinai. Strike the rock, and water will come gushing out. Then the people will be able to drink.” So Moses struck the rock as he was told, and water gushed out as the elders looked on. (NLT)

Main Idea

The LORD says, “I will stand before you there on the rock in Horeb,” and when Moses strikes the rock, water comes out for the people, and Moses does so before the elders. God’s presence at the rock brings water, and 1 Corinthians 10:4 later says that Rock was Christ.

Verse Explanation

The LORD says, “I will stand before you there on the rock in Horeb,” and when Moses strikes the rock, water comes out for the people, and Moses does so before the elders. God’s presence at the rock brings water, and 1 Corinthians 10:4 later says that Rock was Christ.

“Behold, I will stand before you there on the rock in Horeb;”

God promises His presence: “I will stand before you there on the rock.” The people asked whether the LORD was among them (17:7). God answers by standing at the very place where He will give water. “Horeb” is another name for Sinai, the mountain of God (3:1).

“and you shall strike the rock, and water will come out of it, that the people may drink.”

Moses is to “strike the rock.” Water will come from it, enough for the whole people. God provides from the most unlikely source, solid rock in a dry land. Paul later writes that Israel “drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them, and that Rock was Christ” (1 Corinthians 10:4). The water was real provision for real thirst, and Paul teaches that Christ was the true source of Israel’s provision in the wilderness. Many Christians have also seen in the struck rock a picture of Christ, struck for His people and giving living water (John 7:37–39). That picture is a devotional reading, distinct from Paul’s own statement.

“And Moses did so in the sight of the elders of Israel.”

Moses obeys with the elders watching. They witnessed that the water came from God.

Study Notes

Psalm 78:15–16 and Psalm 105:41 recall the water from the rock among God’s great works.

Exodus 17:7
Exodus 17:7
NKJV

Exodus 17:7 So he called the name of the place Massah and Meribah, because of the contention of the children of Israel, and because they tempted the LORD, saying, "Is the LORD among us or not?"(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 17:7 Moses named the place Massah (which means “test”) and Meribah (which means “arguing”) because the people of Israel argued with Moses and tested the LORD by saying, “Is the LORD here with us or not?” (NLT)

Main Idea

Moses names the place Massah and Meribah because Israel quarreled and tested the LORD, saying, “Is the LORD among us or not?” The names preserve the memory of the people doubting the God who had just provided for them.

Verse Explanation

Moses names the place Massah and Meribah because Israel quarreled and tested the LORD, saying, “Is the LORD among us or not?” The names preserve the memory of the people doubting the God who had just provided for them.

“So he called the name of the place Massah and Meribah,”

Moses names the place twice. “Massah” means testing, and “Meribah” means quarreling.

“because of the contention of the children of Israel, and because they tempted the LORD,”

The names record what the people did. They quarreled with Moses, and they put the LORD to the test.

“saying, ‘Is the LORD among us or not?”

The narrator gives the heart of their testing: “Is the LORD among us or not?” The question doubts God’s presence. They had seen the plagues, the sea, the manna, and the pillar of cloud. Still they doubted. The names preserve the memory of the people doubting the God who had just provided for them.

Study Notes

Psalm 95:8–9 warns, “Do not harden your hearts, as in the rebellion,” recalling the day the fathers tested God in the wilderness.

A similar event occurred near the end of the wilderness period, also named Meribah (Numbers 20:1–13). There God told Moses to speak to the rock. Moses spoke rashly, called the people rebels, and struck the rock twice. God said that Moses and Aaron did not believe Him or honor Him as holy before Israel (Numbers 20:12; Psalm 106:32–33).

Exodus 17:8–13 — Israel Prevails over Amalek While Moses’ Hands Are Held Up

Exodus 17:8
Exodus 17:8
NKJV

Exodus 17:8 Now Amalek came and fought with Israel in Rephidim.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 17:8 While the people of Israel were still at Rephidim, the warriors of Amalek attacked them. (NLT)

Main Idea

Amalek comes and fights with Israel at Rephidim. A new threat arises from outside the camp.

Verse Explanation

Amalek comes and fights with Israel at Rephidim. A new threat arises from outside the camp.

“Now Amalek came and fought with Israel in Rephidim.”

A new threat comes from outside. “Amalek,” a desert people descended from Esau (Genesis 36:12), attacks Israel at Rephidim. Deuteronomy 25:17–18 adds that Amalek attacked the weak and weary stragglers at the rear of Israel’s column.

Study Notes

Deuteronomy 25:18 says of Amalek that “he did not fear God.” This helps explain the severity of God’s judgment on Amalek in 17:14–16.

Exodus 17:9
Exodus 17:9
NKJV

Exodus 17:9 And Moses said to Joshua, "Choose us some men and go out, fight with Amalek. Tomorrow I will stand on the top of the hill with the rod of God in my hand."(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 17:9 Moses commanded Joshua, “Choose some men to go out and fight the army of Amalek for us. Tomorrow, I will stand at the top of the hill, holding the staff of God in my hand.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Moses tells Joshua to choose men and fight Amalek, while he will stand on the hilltop “with the rod of God in my hand.” Joshua leads the battle below while Moses holds the sign of God’s power above.

Verse Explanation

Moses tells Joshua to choose men and fight Amalek, while he will stand on the hilltop “with the rod of God in my hand.” Joshua leads the battle below while Moses holds the sign of God’s power above.

“And Moses said to Joshua, ‘Choose us some men and go out, fight with Amalek.”

“Joshua” appears here for the first time in Scripture. Moses gives him the task of choosing fighting men and leading the battle. Israel has no trained army, but they must defend themselves.

“Tomorrow I will stand on the top of the hill with the rod of God in my hand.”

Moses will take his place on the hill “with the rod of God in my hand.” The rod is called “the rod of God” (4:20) because it is the sign of God’s power. Joshua leads the battle below while Moses holds the sign of God’s power above.

Study Notes

Joshua’s name was originally Hoshea (Numbers 13:16). Moses changed it to Joshua, meaning the LORD is salvation. The Greek form of the name is Jesus.

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

Joshua does as Moses said and fights Amalek, and Moses, Aaron, and Hur go up to the top of the hill. The battle and the hilltop scene happen together.

“So Joshua did as Moses said to him, and fought with Amalek.”

Joshua obeys Moses and leads the fight.

“And Moses, Aaron, and Hur went up to the top of the hill.”

Moses goes up with “Aaron” and “Hur.” This is the first mention of Hur. He appears again with Aaron as a leader during Moses’ absence on the mountain (24:14). The battle below and the scene on the hill happen together.

Study Notes

Jewish tradition identifies Hur as Miriam’s husband, but Scripture does not say so. A Hur of the tribe of Judah was grandfather of Bezalel, the tabernacle craftsman (31:2; 1 Chronicles 2:19–20). Whether he is the same man is uncertain.

Exodus 17:14–16 — A Memorial Written and an Altar Named The-LORD-Is-My-Banner

None.

Exodus 17:14
Exodus 17:14
NKJV

Exodus 17:14 Then the LORD said to Moses, "Write this for a memorial in the book and recount it in the hearing of Joshua, that I will utterly blot out the remembrance of Amalek from under heaven."(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 17:14 After the victory, the LORD instructed Moses, “Write this down on a scroll as a permanent reminder, and read it aloud to Joshua: I will erase the memory of Amalek from under heaven.” (NLT)

Main Idea

The LORD tells Moses, “Write this for a memorial in the book,” to be recounted to Joshua, for He will utterly blot out the remembrance of Amalek. God wants this victory and His judgment on Amalek remembered.

Verse Explanation

The LORD tells Moses, “Write this for a memorial in the book,” to be recounted to Joshua, for He will utterly blot out the remembrance of Amalek. God wants this victory and His judgment on Amalek remembered.

“Then the LORD said to Moses, ‘Write this for a memorial in the book”

God commands Moses to “write this.” This is the first mention in Scripture of Moses writing. The record is to be a “memorial,” a lasting reminder.

“and recount it in the hearing of Joshua,”

Moses is to tell it to “Joshua.” Joshua would lead Israel into Canaan, and he needed to remember God’s word about Amalek.

“that I will utterly blot out the remembrance of Amalek from under heaven.”

Amalek’s very memory is to be erased. God wants this victory and His judgment on Amalek remembered.

Study Notes

Deuteronomy 25:17–19 repeats this command. Saul was later told to destroy Amalek and failed to obey fully (1 Samuel 15:2–3, 9). David also fought the Amalekites (1 Samuel 30), and a remnant was destroyed in Hezekiah’s time (1 Chronicles 4:42–43).

Exodus 17:15
Exodus 17:15
NKJV

Exodus 17:15 And Moses built an altar and called its name, The-LORD-Is-My-Banner;(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 17:15 Moses built an altar there and named it Yahweh-Nissi (which means “the LORD is my banner”). (NLT)

Main Idea

Moses builds an altar and names it The-LORD-Is-My-Banner. The name credits the victory to the LORD, who led Israel in the battle.

Verse Explanation

Moses builds an altar and names it The-LORD-Is-My-Banner. The name credits the victory to the LORD, who led Israel in the battle.

“And Moses built an altar and called its name, The-LORD-Is-My-Banner;”

Moses builds “an altar” to mark the victory. He names it “The-LORD-Is-My-Banner.” A banner was a standard or flag raised on a pole, around which an army rallied. Moses held up the rod of God on the hill. Now he declares that the LORD Himself is the banner under which Israel fights. The name credits the victory to the LORD.

Study Notes

The Hebrew name is often transliterated Yahweh Nissi. The word for banner is also used in Isaiah 11:10, where the Root of Jesse will stand as a banner to the peoples.

Exodus 17:16
Exodus 17:16
NKJV

Exodus 17:16 for he said, "Because the LORD has sworn: the LORD will have war with Amalek from generation to generation."(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 17:16 He said, “They have raised their fist against the LORD’s throne, so now the LORD will be at war with Amalek generation after generation.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Moses says that the LORD has sworn to have war with Amalek “from generation to generation.” The conflict with Amalek will continue beyond this day.

Verse Explanation

Moses says that the LORD has sworn to have war with Amalek “from generation to generation.” The conflict with Amalek will continue beyond this day.

“for he said, ‘Because the LORD has sworn:”

Moses explains the altar’s name with God’s oath. The Hebrew here is difficult. It may read a hand upon the throne of the LORD, referring to an oath, or a hand upon the banner of the LORD.

“the LORD will have war with Amalek from generation to generation.”

The conflict with Amalek will continue beyond this day.

Study Notes

The Hebrew of the first part of this verse is uncertain. The Hebrew text has a word meaning “throne” (kes). Some interpreters propose reading instead the Hebrew word for “banner” (nes), a different word, which would connect the line with the altar’s name in 17:15. That is a proposed reading, not the wording of the Hebrew text. The NKJV takes the line as an oath.
