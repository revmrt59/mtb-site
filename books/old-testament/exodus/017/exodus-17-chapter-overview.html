Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 17
Main Idea:

At Rephidim the LORD answers His people’s doubting question, “Is the LORD among us or not?” by standing at the rock to give them water and by giving them victory over Amalek, which Moses honors with an altar named The-LORD-Is-My-Banner.

Setting:

Exodus 17 continues the journey from the Wilderness of Sin toward Sinai (16:1). Israel camps at Rephidim, near Horeb, the mountain of God.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 17:1–7 — Water from the Rock at Massah and Meribah

  • Exodus 17:8–13 — Israel Prevails over Amalek While Moses’ Hands Are Held Up

  • Exodus 17:14–16 — A Memorial Written and an Altar Named The-LORD-Is-My-Banner

Key Verse:

“Behold, I will stand before you there on the rock in Horeb; and you shall strike the rock, and water will come out of it, that the people may drink.’ And Moses did so in the sight of the elders of Israel.” (Exodus 17:6)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Israel camps where the LORD led them, and there is no water. The thirst is real, but the people turn it into a quarrel, ready to stone Moses and demanding that God prove Himself. Moses names the problem: “Why do you tempt the LORD?” (17:2). God does not answer their doubt with anger. He tells Moses to take the same rod that struck the Nile, and He promises, “I will stand before you there on the rock in Horeb” (17:6). Moses strikes the rock, and water flows. Paul later says, “that Rock was Christ” (1 Corinthians 10:4). Yet the place keeps names that remember the quarrel, Massah and Meribah, because the people asked, “Is the LORD among us or not?” (17:7).

Then Amalek attacks. Joshua leads the fight in the valley while Moses stands on the hill with the rod of God. When his hands are raised, Israel prevails, and when they drop, Amalek prevails. Aaron and Hur hold up Moses’ weary hands until sunset. The victory is shared work, but Moses builds an altar that gives the credit to God alone: The-LORD-Is-My-Banner.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 17:1–7 — Water from the Rock at Massah and Meribah

At Rephidim there is no water, and the people quarrel with Moses until he fears they will stone him. The LORD stands before Moses at the rock in Horeb, Moses strikes it, and water flows, but the place is named Massah and Meribah because Israel tested the LORD by asking whether He was among them.

Exodus 17:8–13 — Israel Prevails over Amalek While Moses’ Hands Are Held Up

When Amalek attacks, Joshua leads the fight while Moses holds up the rod of God on the hill, and Israel prevails while his hands are raised. Aaron and Hur hold up Moses’ tired hands until sunset, and Joshua defeats Amalek.

Exodus 17:14–16 — A Memorial Written and an Altar Named The-LORD-Is-My-Banner

The LORD tells Moses to write the victory in a book as a memorial and declares that He will blot out the remembrance of Amalek. Moses builds an altar named The-LORD-Is-My-Banner, for the LORD will war with Amalek in every generation.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 17 answers the question that troubled Israel then and troubles believers now: is the LORD really among us? God answered by standing at the rock and by fighting their battle. His presence is shown by what He does for His people, even when they doubt Him.

The battle with Amalek also pictures the life of God’s people. Joshua fought, Moses held up his hands, and Aaron and Hur supported his hands when they grew heavy. The text does not describe a spoken prayer, but the scene is a fitting picture of prayer that depends on God. No one won alone, and the victory belonged to the LORD. Strong leaders still need others to hold up their hands.

Key Themes to Watch For

Testing the LORD. Israel’s real thirst becomes a demand that God prove His presence (17:2–3, 7).

God’s provision at the rock. The LORD stands at the rock and gives water through Moses’ rod (17:5–6).

Shared burden in battle. Joshua fights, and Aaron and Hur hold up Moses’ hands until victory (17:9–13).

Victory belongs to the LORD. Moses names the altar The-LORD-Is-My-Banner (17:15).

Remembrance. The LORD commands a written memorial of the victory and His judgment on Amalek (17:14).

Key Words & Concepts

Massah and Meribah (17:7). Names meaning “testing” and “quarreling,” which preserve the memory of Israel’s demand that God prove Himself.

Amalek (17:8). A desert people who attacked Israel on the way. Deuteronomy recalls that they struck the weary stragglers at the rear (Deuteronomy 25:17–18).

Joshua (17:9). Moses’ young assistant, appearing here for the first time as the commander of Israel’s fighting men.

The-LORD-Is-My-Banner (17:15). The name of Moses’ altar. A banner was a standard raised to rally troops, and the name declares that the LORD is the One under whom Israel fights and wins.
