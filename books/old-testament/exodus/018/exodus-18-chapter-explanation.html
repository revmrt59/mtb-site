Exodus 18:1
Exodus 18:1
NKJV

Exodus 18:1 And Jethro, the priest of Midian, Moses' father-in-law, heard of all that God had done for Moses and for Israel His people"”that the LORD had brought Israel out of Egypt.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 18:1 Moses’ father-in-law, Jethro, the priest of Midian, heard about everything God had done for Moses and his people, the Israelites. He heard especially about how the LORD had rescued them from Egypt. (NLT)

Main Idea

Jethro, the priest of Midian and Moses’ father-in-law, hears all that God has done for Moses and Israel and how the LORD brought them out of Egypt. News of God’s deliverance reaches beyond Israel.

Verse Explanation

Jethro, the priest of Midian and Moses’ father-in-law, hears all that God has done for Moses and Israel and how the LORD brought them out of Egypt. News of God’s deliverance reaches beyond Israel.

“And Jethro, the priest of Midian, Moses’ father-in-law,”

“Jethro” is introduced again as “the priest of Midian” and Moses’ father-in-law. Moses lived with him for forty years after fleeing Egypt (2:15–22; 3:1). In 2:18 he is called Reuel.

“heard of all that God had done for Moses and for Israel His people—that the LORD had brought Israel out of Egypt.”

Jethro “heard” what God had done. The news of the exodus has spread beyond Israel, as God intended (9:16; 15:14). News of God’s deliverance reaches beyond Israel.

Study Notes

Jethro is also called Reuel (2:18) and possibly Hobab (Numbers 10:29; Judges 4:11). Interpreters differ on whether these are different names for the same man or whether Hobab was his son.

Exodus 18:11
Exodus 18:11
NKJV

Exodus 18:11 Now I know that the LORD is greater than all the gods; for in the very thing in which they behaved proudly, He was above them."(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 18:11 I know now that the LORD is greater than all other gods, because he rescued his people from the oppression of the proud Egyptians.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Jethro confesses, “Now I know that the LORD is greater than all the gods,” because He was above them in the very thing in which they acted proudly. Hearing what the LORD has done brings Jethro to acknowledge His supremacy.

Verse Explanation

Jethro confesses, “Now I know that the LORD is greater than all the gods,” because He was above them in the very thing in which they acted proudly. Hearing what the LORD has done brings Jethro to acknowledge His supremacy.

“Now I know that the LORD is greater than all the gods;”

Jethro makes a confession: “Now I know.” Hearing what the LORD has done brings him to acknowledge that “the LORD is greater than all the gods.” The plagues were meant to teach Egypt to know the LORD (7:5). They have taught a Midianite priest as well.

“for in the very thing in which they behaved proudly, He was above them.”

The second half of the verse is difficult in Hebrew. It means that where Egypt and its gods acted arrogantly against Israel, the LORD showed Himself superior. Many see an example in the sea: Egypt had tried to drown Israel’s sons in the Nile (1:22), and Egypt’s army was drowned in the sea.

Study Notes

Interpreters differ on how far Jethro’s faith went. His words and actions should be the measure. He blesses the LORD, confesses Him greater than all gods, offers sacrifice, and eats before God with Israel’s leaders. Naaman later made a similar confession (2 Kings 5:15), as did Rahab (Joshua 2:11).

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

Moses is to choose able men who fear God, men of truth who hate covetousness, and set them over thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens. The new leaders must combine ability with godly character.

“Moreover you shall select from all the people able men,”

Moses is to choose “able men.” The word means capable, strong, of good quality.

“such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness;”

Jethro lists their character. They must “fear God,” revering Him above all. They must be “men of truth,” honest and reliable. They must hate “covetousness,” refusing bribes and dishonest gain. The new leaders must combine ability with godly character.

“and place such over them to be rulers of thousands, rulers of hundreds, rulers of fifties, and rulers of tens.”

These men are to be placed over groups of different sizes, from “thousands” down to “tens.” Each level handles what it can. The apostles later followed a similar principle when they appointed men to serve tables so that they could give themselves to prayer and to the ministry of the word (Acts 6:1–7). Sharing the work protects the leaders’ essential work and serves the people better.

Study Notes

Deuteronomy 1:9–18 recalls this appointment, adding that the people chose wise and understanding men and Moses set them over the tribes.

Main Idea

Moses heeds his father-in-law and does all that he said. Moses humbly accepts wise counsel.

Verse Explanation

Moses heeds his father-in-law and does all that he said. Moses humbly accepts wise counsel.

“So Moses heeded the voice of his father-in-law and did all that he had said.”

Moses listens and acts. He “heeded” Jethro’s counsel. Moses humbly accepts wise counsel, even from someone outside Israel.

Main Idea

Moses lets his father-in-law depart, and Jethro returns to his own land. The chapter closes as Israel prepares for the events at Sinai.

Verse Explanation

Moses lets his father-in-law depart, and Jethro returns to his own land. The chapter closes as Israel prepares for the events at Sinai.

“Then Moses let his father-in-law depart, and he went his way to his own land.”

Jethro returns “to his own land.” His visit ends in peace. The chapter closes as Israel prepares for the events at Sinai.

Study Notes

Numbers 10:29–32 records Moses inviting Hobab, a relative of Jethro, to travel with Israel as a guide. Judges 1:16 mentions descendants of Moses’ father-in-law living among the people of Judah.
