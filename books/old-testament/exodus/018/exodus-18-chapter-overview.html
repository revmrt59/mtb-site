Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 18
Main Idea:

Jethro hears what the LORD has done for Israel, confesses that the LORD is greater than all the gods, and worships with Israel’s leaders, then counsels Moses to share the work of judging with able, God-fearing men while Moses stands before God for the people.

Setting:

Exodus 18 closes the journey from the sea to Sinai. Moses is camped at the mountain of God when Jethro, priest of Midian, arrives with Moses’ wife and sons.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 18:1–6 — Jethro Brings Moses’ Wife and Sons to the Mountain of God

  • Exodus 18:7–12 — Jethro Blesses the LORD and Worships with Israel’s Leaders

  • Exodus 18:13–23 — Jethro Counsels Moses to Share the Work of Judging

  • Exodus 18:24–27 — Moses Appoints Judges and Jethro Returns Home

Key Verse:

“Now I know that the LORD is greater than all the gods; for in the very thing in which they behaved proudly, He was above them.’” (Exodus 18:11)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

News of the exodus has reached Midian, and Jethro comes to see for himself, bringing Zipporah and Moses’ two sons. Their names tell Moses’ story: Gershom, because he had been a stranger, and Eliezer, because “The God of my father was my help” (18:4). Moses tells Jethro everything the LORD has done, the hardships along the way, and how the LORD delivered them. Jethro rejoices and confesses, “Now I know that the LORD is greater than all the gods” (18:11). A priest of Midian blesses the LORD by name, offers sacrifices, and eats bread with Aaron and Israel’s elders before God. The LORD is making Himself known beyond Israel.

The next day Jethro watches Moses judge the people from morning until evening, alone. “The thing that you do is not good” (18:17), he says. The load will wear out both Moses and the people. His counsel keeps Moses at the center of what only he can do: standing before God for the people and teaching them His ways. Ordinary cases are to go to able men “such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness” (18:21). Jethro makes his advice subject to God’s command, and Moses humbly takes it.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 18:1–6 — Jethro Brings Moses’ Wife and Sons to the Mountain of God

Jethro hears what God has done for Israel and comes to the wilderness with Zipporah and her two sons, whose names recall Moses’ exile and God’s help. He sends word to Moses, who is camped at the mountain of God.

Exodus 18:7–12 — Jethro Blesses the LORD and Worships with Israel’s Leaders

Moses welcomes Jethro and tells him everything the LORD has done to Pharaoh and how He delivered Israel through every hardship. Jethro blesses the LORD, confesses that He is greater than all the gods, offers sacrifices, and shares a meal before God with Aaron and the elders.

Exodus 18:13–23 — Jethro Counsels Moses to Share the Work of Judging

Seeing Moses judge the people alone from morning to evening, Jethro warns that the burden will wear out both Moses and the people. He advises Moses to represent the people before God and teach them His ways, and to appoint capable, God-fearing, honest men who hate dishonest gain to judge ordinary cases, if God so commands.

Exodus 18:24–27 — Moses Appoints Judges and Jethro Returns Home

Moses listens to his father-in-law and appoints able men as leaders over thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens. They judge the small cases and bring the hard ones to Moses, and Jethro returns home.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 18 shows that testimony about God’s works draws others to Him. Moses simply told what the LORD had done, and a foreign priest came to know that the LORD is greater than all gods. God’s deliverance of Israel was always meant to make Him known to the nations.

The chapter also shows the wisdom of shared leadership. Even a man as faithful as Moses could not carry the whole load alone, and Jethro warned that trying to do so would wear out both him and the people. Leaders for God’s people need both competence and character: reverence for God, honesty, and freedom from greed. The apostles later followed a similar pattern, sharing the work so they could give themselves to prayer and the word (Acts 6:1–7).

Key Themes to Watch For

Testimony to God’s works. Moses tells all that the LORD has done, and Jethro rejoices (18:8–9).

The LORD above all gods. Jethro confesses the LORD’s supremacy and worships Him (18:10–12).

A burden too heavy for one. Jethro sees that Moses cannot carry the work alone (18:13–18).

Character and competence. Leaders must be able, God-fearing, truthful, and hate covetousness (18:21).

Humility to receive counsel. Moses heeds his father-in-law’s advice (18:24).

Key Words & Concepts

Jethro (18:1). The priest of Midian and Moses’ father-in-law, called Reuel in 2:18.

Eliezer (18:4). Moses’ second son, whose name declares, “The God of my father was my help.”

Before God (18:12). In God’s presence. Jethro’s meal with Aaron and the elders is an act of worship shared with Israel’s leaders.

Hating covetousness (18:21). Refusing dishonest gain. Judges who love money can be bought, so Israel’s judges must hate greed.
