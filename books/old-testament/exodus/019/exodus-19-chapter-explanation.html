Exodus 19

Verse-by-Verse
Exodus 19:1
Exodus 19:1
NKJV

Exodus 19:1 In the third month after the children of Israel had gone out of the land of Egypt, on the same day, they came to the Wilderness of Sinai.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 19:1 Exactly two months after the Israelites left Egypt, they arrived in the wilderness of Sinai. (NLT)

Main Idea

In the third month after leaving Egypt, on the same day, Israel comes to the Wilderness of Sinai. The people arrive at the mountain where God promised Moses they would serve Him.

Verse Explanation

In the third month after leaving Egypt, on the same day, Israel comes to the Wilderness of Sinai. The people arrive at the mountain where God promised Moses they would serve Him.

“In the third month after the children of Israel had gone out of the land of Egypt,”

Israel arrives at Sinai “in the third month” after leaving Egypt. About seven weeks have passed since the Passover.

“on the same day, they came to the Wilderness of Sinai.”

The phrase “on the same day” probably means on the first day of that month, the new moon. The people have reached the place God promised Moses at the burning bush: “you shall serve God on this mountain” (3:12).

Study Notes

Later Jewish tradition linked the giving of the law with the Feast of Weeks (Pentecost), fifty days after Passover. Scripture itself does not make that link.

The exact location of Mount Sinai is debated. The traditional site is Jebel Musa in the southern Sinai Peninsula. Other locations have been proposed in northern Sinai and in Arabia (compare Galatians 4:25).

Exodus 19:4

NLT

Exodus 19:4 ‘You have seen what I did to the Egyptians. You know how I carried you on eagles’ wings and brought you to myself. (NLT)

Main Idea

God says, “You have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I bore you on eagles’ wings and brought you to Myself.” The goal of the exodus is not only freedom but a people brought to God Himself.

Verse Explanation

God says, “You have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I bore you on eagles’ wings and brought you to Myself.” The goal of the exodus is not only freedom but a people brought to God Himself.

“You have seen what I did to the Egyptians,”

God begins with what He has done. Israel had “seen” the plagues and the sea with their own eyes. God’s call to obedience rests on His completed rescue.

“and how I bore you on eagles’ wings and brought you to Myself.”

God pictures the journey as being carried “on eagles’ wings.” He carried them as a great bird carries its young, strong, swift, and safe. The goal was not just escape. God “brought you to Myself.” The exodus led Israel into relationship with God.

Study Notes

Deuteronomy 32:11–12 develops the picture of an eagle stirring up its nest, spreading its wings, and carrying its young. The bird may be an eagle or a vulture, since the Hebrew word covers both.

Exodus 19:5
Exodus 19:5
NKJV

Exodus 19:5 Now therefore, if you will indeed obey My voice and keep My covenant, then you shall be a special treasure to Me above all people; for all the earth is Mine.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 19:5 Now if you will obey me and keep my covenant, you will be my own special treasure from among all the peoples on earth; for all the earth belongs to me. (NLT)

Main Idea

If they obey His voice and keep His covenant, they will be “a special treasure to Me above all people; for all the earth is Mine.” The God who owns all the earth chooses Israel as His own treasured people.

Verse Explanation

If they obey His voice and keep His covenant, they will be “a special treasure to Me above all people; for all the earth is Mine.” The God who owns all the earth chooses Israel as His own treasured people.

“Now therefore, if you will indeed obey My voice and keep My covenant,”

“Now therefore” links the call to obey with what God has done. Israel is to “obey My voice and keep My covenant.” Obedience is the response of a rescued people, not the price of their rescue.

“then you shall be a special treasure to Me above all people;”

Israel will be God’s “special treasure.” The word was used for a king’s personal treasure, his own valued possession. Among all nations, Israel will belong to God in a special way.

“for all the earth is Mine.”

God owns “all the earth.” He did not choose Israel because He lacked other peoples. The God who owns all the earth chooses Israel as His own treasured people.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word for “special treasure” is segullah. It appears also in Deuteronomy 7:6; 14:2; 26:18, and in Malachi 3:17.

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

Israel will be to Him “a kingdom of priests and a holy nation,” and Moses is to speak these words to the people. Israel is called to a priestly and holy role among the nations, a calling 1 Peter 2:9 applies to the church.

“And you shall be to Me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation.”

Israel will be “a kingdom of priests.” A priest stood between God and others, bringing them near to God. Israel was to serve that role among the nations. They would also be “a holy nation,” set apart for God and different from the peoples around them. Israel is called to a priestly and holy role among the nations.

“These are the words which you shall speak to the children of Israel.”

Moses is to carry these words to the people exactly. Peter applies these titles to the people who belong to Christ: “But you are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, His own special people” (1 Peter 2:9). He adds that those who once were not a people are now the people of God (1 Peter 2:10).

For our God is a consuming fire” (Hebrews 12:28–29).
