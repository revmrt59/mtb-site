Exodus 20

Verse-by-Verse
Exodus 20:1
Exodus 20:1
NKJV

Exodus 20:1 And God spoke all these words, saying:(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 20:1 Then God gave the people all these instructions : (NLT)

Main Idea

“And God spoke all these words.” The commandments come directly from God Himself, spoken to the whole people.

Verse Explanation

“And God spoke all these words.” The commandments come directly from God Himself, spoken to the whole people.

“And God spoke all these words, saying:”

The commandments come directly from God. “God spoke.” Moses did not invent them, and no human council drew them up. The whole people heard God’s voice from the mountain (20:18–19; Deuteronomy 4:12–13; 5:22). Later God wrote them on tablets of stone with His own finger (31:18).

Study Notes

Deuteronomy 4:13 calls them “the Ten Commandments,” literally the ten words. Deuteronomy 5:6–21 repeats them with small differences, especially in the Sabbath command.

This study follows the Reformed numbering. Exodus 20:1–2 introduces the commandments. The first forbids other gods (20:3), the second forbids images (20:4–6), and coveting is one commandment (20:17). Other traditions number them differently.

Exodus 20:2
Exodus 20:2
NKJV

Exodus 20:2 "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 20:2 “I am the LORD your God, who rescued you from the land of Egypt, the place of your slavery. (NLT)

Main Idea

God introduces Himself: “I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.” The commandments are given to a people God has already redeemed, so obedience answers His saving love rather than earning it.

Verse Explanation

God introduces Himself: “I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.” The commandments are given to a people God has already redeemed, so obedience answers His saving love rather than earning it.

“I am the LORD your God,”

God begins by naming Himself. “I am the LORD,” the covenant name He revealed to Moses (3:14–15; 6:2–3). He is “your God,” bound to Israel by His own choice and promise.

“who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.”

Before giving one command, God reminds Israel what He has done. He “brought you out” of slavery. The commandments are given to a people already redeemed. Israel does not obey in order to be rescued. They obey because they have been rescued. The law is the shape of life for the people God set free. The Reformed tradition, which this study follows, speaks of three uses of the law: it shows sin and leads to Christ (Romans 3:20; Galatians 3:24), it restrains evil in society (1 Timothy 1:9–10), and it guides believers in a life that pleases God (Romans 8:4; 13:8–10). It also distinguishes the moral law, summed up in the Ten Commandments, from Israel’s civil and ceremonial laws, which applied to Israel as a nation and pointed ahead to Christ. The Ten Commandments remain the moral law that still binds believers. Some Christians understand the law differently, teaching that believers are under “the law of Christ” (1 Corinthians 9:21; Galatians 6:2) rather than the law of Moses as a whole, while still upholding the moral commands repeated in the New Testament.

Exodus 20:3–11 — The Commandments Concerning Israel’s Worship of the LORD

Study Notes

Exodus 20:3
Exodus 20:3
NKJV

Exodus 20:3 "You shall have no other gods before Me.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 20:3 “You must not have any other god but me. (NLT)

Main Idea

“You shall have no other gods before Me.” The first commandment calls for exclusive loyalty to the God who rescued them.

Verse Explanation

“You shall have no other gods before Me.” The first commandment calls for exclusive loyalty to the God who rescued them.

“You shall have no other gods before Me.”

The first commandment calls for complete loyalty to the God who rescued them. “No other gods.” The phrase “before Me” can mean in My presence or in addition to Me. Israel must not set any god beside the LORD, much less above Him. Egypt had many gods, and the nations of Canaan would have many more. Israel was to worship the LORD alone. Jesus calls this the first and great commandment in its positive form: “You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your mind” (Matthew 22:37; Deuteronomy 6:4–5). Anything a person loves, trusts, or serves above God becomes another god. Paul calls covetousness idolatry (Colossians 3:5).

None.

Exodus 20:4
Exodus 20:4
NKJV

Exodus 20:4 "You shall not make for yourself a carved image"”any likeness of anything that is in heaven above, or that is in the earth beneath, or that is in the water under the earth;(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 20:4 “You must not make for yourself an idol of any kind or an image of anything in the heavens or on the earth or in the sea. (NLT)

Main Idea

Israel must not make a carved image or likeness of anything in heaven, on earth, or in the water. The second commandment forbids representing God with images or worshiping created things.

Verse Explanation

Israel must not make a carved image or likeness of anything in heaven, on earth, or in the water. The second commandment forbids representing God with images or worshiping created things.

“You shall not make for yourself a carved image—”

The second commandment (20:4–6) forbids making “a carved image” for worship. The first commandment forbids worshiping false gods. The second forbids worshiping the true God in a false way.

“any likeness of anything that is in heaven above, or that is in the earth beneath, or that is in the water under the earth;”

No “likeness” of anything in creation may be made as an object of worship. The three realms, sky, land, and water, cover everything God made. Moses later explains the reason: Israel saw no form when God spoke from the fire (Deuteronomy 4:15–19). No created thing can represent the Creator.

Study Notes

The commandment does not forbid all art. God Himself commanded carved cherubim for the ark and woven figures in the tabernacle curtains (25:18–20; 26:1). What is forbidden is making images to worship or to represent God. The golden calf shows the danger: Israel called a calf the god who brought them out of Egypt (32:4–5).

Exodus 20:5
Exodus 20:5
NKJV

Exodus 20:5 you shall not bow down to them nor serve them. For I, the LORD your God, am a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children to the third and fourth generations of those who hate Me,(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 20:5 You must not bow down to them or worship them, for I, the LORD your God, am a jealous God who will not tolerate your affection for any other gods. I lay the sins of the parents upon their children; the entire family is affected — even children in the third and fourth generations of those who reject me. (NLT)

Main Idea

Israel must not bow down to or serve images, for the LORD is “a jealous God,” visiting the iniquity of the fathers on the children to the third and fourth generations “of those who hate Me.” God’s jealousy is His rightful claim on His people’s worship, and the consequences of idolatry continue in families that keep hating Him.

Verse Explanation

Israel must not bow down to or serve images, for the LORD is “a jealous God,” visiting the iniquity of the fathers on the children to the third and fourth generations “of those who hate Me.” God’s jealousy is His rightful claim on His people’s worship, and the consequences of idolatry continue in families that keep hating Him.

“you shall not bow down to them nor serve them.”

Israel must not “bow down” to images or “serve them.” Bowing and serving describe worship in body and in practice.

“For I, the LORD your God, am a jealous God,”

God gives the reason. He is “a jealous God.” This is not petty envy. It is the rightful zeal of a husband for his wife’s faithfulness. God has redeemed Israel, and He will not share His people’s worship with another. His jealousy is His rightful claim on the people He redeemed.

“visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children to the third and fourth generations of those who hate Me,”

God will “visit,” bring judgment on, sin “to the third and fourth generations.” The phrase “of those who hate Me” is important. The judgment falls on families who continue in their fathers’ hatred of God. Idolatry spreads through households, and its consequences continue in families that keep hating Him. Each person still answers for his own sin (Deuteronomy 24:16; Ezekiel 18:20).

Study Notes

Three or four generations could be alive in one household at the same time. The phrase may picture the whole family living under one roof sharing in the consequences of the head’s idolatry.

Main Idea

God shows mercy “to thousands, to those who love Me and keep My commandments.” His mercy far outweighs His judgment, reaching countless generations of those who love Him.

Verse Explanation

God shows mercy “to thousands, to those who love Me and keep My commandments.” His mercy far outweighs His judgment, reaching countless generations of those who love Him.

“but showing mercy to thousands, to those who love Me and keep My commandments.”

God’s “mercy” reaches “to thousands.” This may mean thousands of people or thousands of generations (Deuteronomy 7:9). Either way, the contrast with the third and fourth generations is striking. God’s mercy far outweighs His judgment. It rests on those “who love Me and keep My commandments.” Love for God shows itself in obedience (John 14:15).

Study Notes

The Hebrew word translated “mercy” is chesed, God’s steadfast covenant love.

Exodus 20:7
Exodus 20:7
NKJV

Exodus 20:7 "You shall not take the name of the LORD your God in vain, for the LORD will not hold him guiltless who takes His name in vain.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 20:7 “You must not misuse the name of the LORD your God. The LORD will not let you go unpunished if you misuse his name. (NLT)

Main Idea

Israel must not take the LORD’s name in vain, “for the LORD will not hold him guiltless who takes His name in vain.” The third commandment guards the honor of the name God revealed to Moses.

Verse Explanation

Israel must not take the LORD’s name in vain, “for the LORD will not hold him guiltless who takes His name in vain.” The third commandment guards the honor of the name God revealed to Moses.

“You shall not take the name of the LORD your God in vain,”

The third commandment guards God’s “name.” To take it “in vain” means to use it emptily, falsely, or carelessly. This includes swearing falsely by His name, using it lightly, or claiming to belong to Him while living as though He did not matter. The name God revealed to Moses (3:14–15) carries His character and must be treated as holy.

“for the LORD will not hold him guiltless who takes His name in vain.”

God warns that He “will not hold him guiltless.” Misusing His name is a serious offense, even if people treat it lightly. Jesus taught His disciples to pray, “Hallowed be Your name” (Matthew 6:9). He also warned against careless oaths and taught that a person’s plain word should be trustworthy (Matthew 5:33–37).

Main Idea

In six days the LORD made the heavens and the earth and rested the seventh day, “Therefore the LORD blessed the Sabbath day and hallowed it.” The Sabbath is grounded in God’s own pattern at creation.

Verse Explanation

In six days the LORD made the heavens and the earth and rested the seventh day, “Therefore the LORD blessed the Sabbath day and hallowed it.” The Sabbath is grounded in God’s own pattern at creation.

“For in six days the LORD made the heavens and the earth, the sea, and all that is in them, and rested the seventh day.”

God gives the reason for the Sabbath. He made everything “in six days” and “rested the seventh day” (Genesis 2:2–3). Israel’s week follows God’s own pattern at creation.

“Therefore the LORD blessed the Sabbath day and hallowed it.”

God “blessed” the day and “hallowed” it, set it apart as holy. The Sabbath is grounded in God’s own pattern at creation. This command belongs to the moral law. Jesus called Himself “Lord of the Sabbath” and used the day for mercy and healing (Mark 2:27–28; 3:1–5), and He calls the weary to come to Him for rest (Matthew 11:28–30). Believers are called to a serious, deliberate weekly rhythm of ceasing from ordinary work and setting time apart to be with Jesus, to remember His deliverance, worship, and receive His rest. Paul leaves believers free about which day they set apart and warns against judging others over observance (Romans 14:5–6; Colossians 2:16–17).

Study Notes

Deuteronomy 5:12–15 gives the same commandment with a different reason attached: Israel is to remember that it was a slave in Egypt and that the LORD brought it out.

Verse Explanation

“Honor your father and your mother,” so that your days may be long in the land God is giving. The fifth commandment begins the commands about life with others, starting in the home, and carries a promise.

“Honor your father and your mother,”

The fifth commandment begins the commands about life with others, starting in the home. To “honor” means to give weight to, to respect and value. Children are to treat their parents with respect, obey them while young, and care for them when old.

“that your days may be long upon the land which the LORD your God is giving you.”

The command comes with a promise: “that your days may be long.” A people who honor their parents will be stable and blessed in the land God gives. Paul calls this “the first commandment with promise” and applies it to children in the church (Ephesians 6:1–3). Jesus rebuked those who used religious excuses to avoid supporting their parents (Mark 7:9–13).

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

“You shall not murder.” The sixth commandment protects human life.

“You shall not murder.”

The sixth commandment protects human life. The Hebrew verb refers to the unlawful killing of a person. People are made in the image of God, and taking a life unjustly is an attack on Him (Genesis 9:6). Jesus taught that anger and contempt toward a brother violate the heart of this commandment (Matthew 5:21–22). John writes that hatred of a brother is murder in the heart (1 John 3:15).

Study Notes

The same law that forbids murder also commands the death penalty for certain crimes (21:12) and permits war in some circumstances. The commandment forbids murder, not every taking of life.

Verse Explanation

“You shall not bear false witness against your neighbor.” The ninth commandment guards the truth, especially when a neighbor’s life or good name is at stake.

“You shall not bear false witness against your neighbor.”

The ninth commandment guards the truth. To “bear false witness” first meant giving false testimony in court, where a lie could cost a neighbor his property, reputation, or life. Study Notes

Main Idea

Israel must not covet a neighbor’s house, wife, servants, animals, or “anything that is your neighbor’s.” The tenth commandment reaches beyond outward actions to the desires of the heart.

Verse Explanation

Israel must not covet a neighbor’s house, wife, servants, animals, or “anything that is your neighbor’s.” The tenth commandment reaches beyond outward actions to the desires of the heart.

“You shall not covet your neighbor’s house;”

The tenth commandment reaches beyond outward actions to the desires of the heart. To “covet” means to desire what belongs to someone else, wanting it for yourself.

“you shall not covet your neighbor’s wife, nor his male servant, nor his female servant, nor his ox, nor his donkey, nor anything that is your neighbor’s.”

The list covers family, workers, animals, and “anything.” Coveting is often where theft, adultery, lying, and even murder begin. This command shows that God’s law addresses not only what people do but what they want. Paul writes that this commandment showed him his own sin. Without it he “would not have known covetousness” (Romans 7:7). It is a clear example of the law acting as a mirror. Jesus fulfills the law and draws its demands into the heart (Matthew 5:17–28). He sums up the second table in the command to love your neighbor as yourself (Matthew 22:39; Romans 13:9–10).

Israel must not make gods of silver or gold to be with the LORD. The first instruction of the Book of the Covenant applies the commandments about worship.

“You shall not make anything to be with Me—gods of silver or gods of gold you shall not make for yourselves.”

The first instruction applies the commandments about worship. Israel must not make “gods of silver or gods of gold” to stand beside the LORD. Having heard the living God speak, they must not turn to idols made by human hands.

NLT

Exodus 20:24 “Build for me an altar made of earth, and offer your sacrifices to me — your burnt offerings and peace offerings, your sheep and goats, and your cattle. Build my altar wherever I cause my name to be remembered, and I will come to you and bless you. (NLT)

Main Idea

Israel is to make an altar of earth for burnt offerings and peace offerings, and God promises, “In every place where I record My name I will come to you, and I will bless you.” God, not the worshiper, chooses where He will meet and bless His people.

Verse Explanation

Israel is to make an altar of earth for burnt offerings and peace offerings, and God promises, “In every place where I record My name I will come to you, and I will bless you.” God, not the worshiper, chooses where He will meet and bless His people.

“An altar of earth you shall make for Me,”

God tells Israel how to build “an altar.” It is to be made “of earth,” simple and natural, not elaborate or costly.

“and you shall sacrifice on it your burnt offerings and your peace offerings, your sheep and your oxen.”

On it they will offer “burnt offerings,” wholly burned to God, and “peace offerings,” shared in fellowship with Him.

“In every place where I record My name I will come to you, and I will bless you.”

God chooses where He will be worshiped. “Where I record My name” means where God causes His name to be remembered. In such places He promises, “I will come to you, and I will bless you.” God, not the worshiper, chooses where He will meet and bless His people. The altar is not a means of controlling God’s presence.

Study Notes

Deuteronomy 12:5–14 later commands Israel to bring their offerings to the place God would choose to put His name.

Verse Explanation

If Israel builds an altar of stone, they must not build it of hewn stone, for using a tool on it profanes it. Even the way an altar is built must follow God’s instruction.

“And if you make Me an altar of stone, you shall not build it of hewn stone;”

If they use stones, the stones must be uncut.

“for if you use your tool on it, you have profaned it.”

Using “your tool” on the stones would “profane” the altar, make it common. The altar must be as God gave the materials, not shaped by human skill. Even the way an altar is built must follow God’s instruction.

Study Notes

Joshua built an altar of uncut stones on Mount Ebal “as Moses the servant of the LORD had commanded” (Joshua 8:30–31).

Exodus 20:26 And do not approach my altar by going up steps. If you do, someone might look up under your clothing and see your nakedness. (NLT)

Main Idea

Israel must not go up to the altar by steps, so that their nakedness is not exposed on it. Even the way they approach God’s altar must preserve modesty and reverence.

Verse Explanation

Israel must not go up to the altar by steps, so that their nakedness is not exposed on it. Even the way they approach God’s altar must preserve modesty and reverence.

“Nor shall you go up by steps to My altar, that your nakedness may not be exposed on it.”

Israel must not build “steps” up to the altar. Climbing steps in a long robe could expose the body. God’s worship must be marked by modesty and reverence. Later the priests were given linen undergarments for this purpose (28:42–43).
