EIGHTH - ( shmini , Strong's H8066 )

Summary:

The word shmini carries the foundational meaning of "the eighth," representing the number eight, which in biblical numerology frequently signifies new beginnings, resurrection, and the culmination of a full cycle followed by a fresh start. In Exodus 22:30, it governs the specific timing for presenting a firstborn animal to God, anchoring sacred obedience in deliberate, appointed rhythms of life and stewardship.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• Derived from a root meaning "to be fat," "superabundant," or reaching an excess beyond a standard measure.

• Historically associated with the completion of a standard seven-day perfection cycle (such as the days of creation or the consecration of priests), making the eighth day a threshold into a new realm or order.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• An ordinal number signifying the position following the seventh.

• Used specifically in biblical Hebrew to denote the eighth day, the eighth year, or an eighth part.

• In cultic and legal contexts, it marks a precise temporal boundary established by divine instruction.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• In the Levitical context, newborn animals remained with their mothers for seven days and were given to the Lord on the eighth day (Exodus 22:30; Leviticus 22:27).

• The delay until the eighth day ensures the animal is robust enough to survive while emphasizing that the very first fruits of the flock belong unreservedly to God.

• Connects the concept of time to holiness; ordinary time is marked off until a designated moment breaks in to claim the object for sacred use.

4. Theological Implications

• Points symbolically to the doctrine of firstfruits, teaching that God claims the initial and best portion of all increase.

• Anticipates the theological pattern of the "eighth day" seen throughout Scripture—such as circumcision on the eighth day (Genesis 17:12) and the resurrection of Christ on the first day of the week, which is also chronologically the eighth day of a cumulative weekly cycle, marking new creation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Exodus 22:30, "Likewise you shall do with your oxen and your sheep. Seven days it shall be with its mother; on the eighth day you shall give it to Me," the word functions as a strict legal and ritual boundary.

• It prevents premature offering while strictly forbidding the permanent withholding of what is due to God, maintaining the rhythm of covenant faithfulness.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 22:30: "Likewise you shall do with your oxen and your sheep. Seven days it shall be with its mother; on the eighth day you shall give it to Me."

• Leviticus 9:1: "And it came to pass on the eighth day that Moses called Aaron and his sons and the elders of Israel."

• Leviticus 14:23: "He shall bring them on the eighth day for his cleansing to the priest, to the door of the tabernacle of meeting, before the Lord."

• Numbers 7:54: "On the eighth day Gamaliel the son of Pedahzur, leader of the children of Manasseh, presented..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The recurring requirement of the eighth day across Exodus and Leviticus demonstrates that holiness in Israel was woven into the very calendar of daily agricultural and pastoral life.

• Pastoral application: God asks His people to trust Him with the first and best, knowing that honoring Him at the appointed threshold sanctifies all that follows.
