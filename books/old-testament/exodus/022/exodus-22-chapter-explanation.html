Exodus 22:1
Exodus 22:1
NKJV

Exodus 22:1 "If a man steals an ox or a sheep, and slaughters it or sells it, he shall restore five oxen for an ox and four sheep for a sheep.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 22:1 “If someone steals an ox or sheep and then kills or sells it, the thief must pay back five oxen for each ox stolen, and four sheep for each sheep stolen. (NLT)

Main Idea

A thief who steals an ox or sheep and slaughters or sells it must restore five oxen for an ox and four sheep for a sheep. Restitution is larger than the loss, making theft costly.

Verse Explanation

A thief who steals an ox or sheep and slaughters or sells it must restore five oxen for an ox and four sheep for a sheep. Restitution is larger than the loss, making theft costly.

“If a man steals an ox or a sheep, and slaughters it or sells it,”

A thief who takes an animal and then “slaughters it or sells it” has disposed of it and cannot simply return it.

“he shall restore five oxen for an ox and four sheep for a sheep.”

He must repay several times over. Restitution is larger than the loss, making theft costly. The higher rate for an ox may reflect its value as a working animal.

“there shall be no guilt for his bloodshed.”

The householder is not guilty. The next verse shows this applies at night, when he cannot see who has broken in or what the intruder intends. A householder defending his home in the dark is not held guilty.

Study Notes

The bride-price was a payment from the groom to the bride’s family. Deuteronomy 22:28–29 deals with a related but different case, in which a man seizes a virgin who is not betrothed and they are found out. There the law sets a payment of fifty shekels of silver and forbids the man ever to divorce her. Those terms belong to that case. This verse requires the bride-price, and 22:17 preserves the father’s right to refuse the marriage.

Exodus 22:18
NKJV

Exodus 22:18 "You shall not permit a sorceress to live.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 22:18 “You must not allow a sorceress to live. (NLT)

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

“You shall not permit a sorceress to live.” Sorcery is treated as a capital crime against Israel’s covenant with the LORD.

“You shall not permit a sorceress to live.”

Sorcery was a capital crime in Israel. It sought power and knowledge from spirits rather than from God (Deuteronomy 18:10–12). It was a crime against Israel’s covenant with the LORD. These penalties belong to Israel’s covenant order as a nation under God. No believer or church carries them out (John 18:36; 2 Corinthians 10:4). The principle of capital punishment itself runs through Scripture (Genesis 9:6; Romans 13:4), and a government may justly apply it to serious crime with safeguards against error such as the requirement of two or three witnesses (Deuteronomy 17:6).

NKJV

Exodus 22:27 For that is his only covering, it is his garment for his skin. What will he sleep in? And it will be that when he cries to Me, I will hear, for I am gracious.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 22:27 This coat may be the only blanket your neighbor has. How can a person sleep without it? If you do not return it and your neighbor cries out to me for help, then I will hear, for I am merciful. (NLT)

Main Idea

The garment is his only covering to sleep in, and when he cries to God, “I will hear, for I am gracious.” God’s own gracious character stands behind the protection of the poor.

Verse Explanation

The garment is his only covering to sleep in, and when he cries to God, “I will hear, for I am gracious.” God’s own gracious character stands behind the protection of the poor.

“For that is his only covering, it is his garment for his skin. What will he sleep in?”

The outer cloak was also a poor man’s blanket. God asks, “What will he sleep in?” He sees the cold night the poor man will face.

“And it will be that when he cries to Me, I will hear, for I am gracious.”

God ties the law to His own character. “I am gracious.” God’s own gracious character stands behind the protection of the poor. Mercy is to shape how His people use power and property.

Study Notes

Deuteronomy 24:10–13 adds that the lender must not enter the borrower’s house to take the pledge, and that returning it will be counted as righteousness before the LORD.

Exodus 22:28–31 — A People Holy to the LORD

Exodus 22:28
Exodus 22:28
NKJV

Exodus 22:28 "You shall not revile God, nor curse a ruler of your people.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 22:28 “You must not dishonor God or curse any of your rulers. (NLT)

Main Idea

Israel must not revile God nor curse a ruler of the people. Respect for God and for those in authority belong together.

Verse Explanation

Israel must not revile God nor curse a ruler of the people. Respect for God and for those in authority belong together.

“You shall not revile God, nor curse a ruler of your people.”

Israel must not “revile God,” speaking of Him with contempt. Nor may they “curse a ruler,” one God has placed in authority. Respect for God and for those in authority belong together.

Study Notes

Paul quoted this verse when he realized he had spoken harshly to the high priest: “You shall not speak evil of a ruler of your people” (Acts 23:5).

Exodus 22:29
Exodus 22:29
NKJV

Exodus 22:29 "You shall not delay to offer the first of your ripe produce and your juices. The firstborn of your sons you shall give to Me.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 22:29 “You must not hold anything back when you give me offerings from your crops and your wine. “You must give me your firstborn sons. (NLT)

Main Idea

Israel must not delay offering the first of its produce, and “The firstborn of your sons you shall give to Me.” The firstborn sons belong to God and are given to Him by redemption, as Exodus 13:13, 15 directs.

Verse Explanation

Israel must not delay offering the first of its produce, and “The firstborn of your sons you shall give to Me.” The firstborn sons belong to God and are given to Him by redemption, as Exodus 13:13, 15 directs.

“You shall not delay to offer the first of your ripe produce and your juices.”

Israel must not hold back “the first” of the harvest, the first grain and the first of the wine and oil. The first belongs to God and acknowledges that the whole harvest comes from Him.

“The firstborn of your sons you shall give to Me.”

The firstborn sons belong to God. They are given to Him by redemption, as 13:13, 15 directs, never by sacrifice.

Study Notes

Proverbs 3:9 says, “Honor the LORD with your possessions,” giving Him the firstfruits of all your increase.

Exodus 22:30
Exodus 22:30
NKJV

Exodus 22:30 Likewise you shall do with your oxen and your sheep. It shall be with its mother seven days; on the eighth day you shall give it to Me.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 22:30 “You must also give me the firstborn of your cattle, sheep, and goats. But leave the newborn animal with its mother for seven days; then give it to me on the eighth day. (NLT)

Main Idea

The firstborn of oxen and sheep stay with the mother seven days and are given to God on the eighth day. God’s claim on the firstborn extends to the animals, with care for the young.

Verse Explanation

The firstborn of oxen and sheep stay with the mother seven days and are given to God on the eighth day. God’s claim on the firstborn extends to the animals, with care for the young.

“Likewise you shall do with your oxen and your sheep.”

The same claim covers the firstborn of the animals.

“It shall be with its mother seven days; on the eighth day you shall give it to Me.”

The young animal stays with its mother for “seven days” before being offered on “the eighth day.” God’s claim on the firstborn extends to the animals, with care for the young.

Study Notes

Leviticus 22:27 gives the same rule for all sacrificial animals.

Exodus 22:31
Exodus 22:31
NKJV

Exodus 22:31 "And you shall be holy men to Me: you shall not eat meat torn by beasts in the field; you shall throw it to the dogs.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 22:31 “You must be my holy people. Therefore, do not eat any animal that has been torn up and killed by wild animals. Throw it to the dogs. (NLT)

Main Idea

“And you shall be holy men to Me,” so they must not eat meat torn by beasts but throw it to the dogs. Israel’s holiness reaches even into what they eat.

Verse Explanation

“And you shall be holy men to Me,” so they must not eat meat torn by beasts but throw it to the dogs. Israel’s holiness reaches even into what they eat.

“And you shall be holy men to Me:”

God calls Israel to be “holy” to Him, set apart in every part of life.

“you shall not eat meat torn by beasts in the field; you shall throw it to the dogs.”

Meat from an animal killed by wild beasts was unclean (Leviticus 17:15), probably because its blood had not been drained. It was to be given “to the dogs.” Israel’s holiness reaches even into what they eat.
