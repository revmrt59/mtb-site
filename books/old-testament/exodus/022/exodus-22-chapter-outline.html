Exodus 22 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Exodus 22 continues the judgments of the Book of the Covenant with laws of restitution for theft, damage, and property held in trust, then laws that guard sexual responsibility and Israel’s exclusive worship of the LORD. The chapter turns to God’s protection of strangers, widows, orphans, and the poor, whose cry He promises to hear, and ends by calling Israel to live as a people holy to Him.

Chapter Sections

1. Exodus 22:1–6 — Restitution for Theft and Damage

A thief must restore five oxen for an ox and four sheep for a sheep, or double if the stolen animal is found alive, and the law distinguishes a thief struck at night from one struck after sunrise. A man whose animal grazes in another’s field or whose fire spreads to another’s grain must make restitution.

2. Exodus 22:7–15 — Goods Held in Trust and Borrowed

When goods or animals left in a neighbor’s keeping are stolen or lost, the judges decide disputed cases, and in some cases “an oath of the LORD shall be between them both” (Exodus 22:11). Responsibility depends on what happened, whether the owner was present, and whether the animal was borrowed or hired.

3. Exodus 22:16–20 — Sexual Responsibility and Faithfulness to the LORD

A man who seduces an unbetrothed virgin must pay the bride-price and marry her, or pay it if her father refuses. Sorcery, lying with an animal, and sacrificing to any god “except to the LORD only” (Exodus 22:20) are capital offenses.

4. Exodus 22:21–27 — The LORD Hears the Stranger, Widow, Orphan, and Poor

Israel must not oppress the stranger, for they were strangers in Egypt, and must not afflict the widow or fatherless, for the LORD will hear their cry. They must not charge interest to the poor and must return a pledged garment by sunset, for “when he cries to Me, I will hear, for I am gracious” (Exodus 22:27).

5. Exodus 22:28–31 — A People Holy to the LORD

Israel must not revile God or curse a ruler, must not delay to offer the first of its produce, and must give its firstborn sons and animals to the LORD. “And you shall be holy men to Me” (Exodus 22:31), so they must not eat meat torn by beasts.
