Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 22
Main Idea:

The judgments of the covenant teach Israel to make full restitution for wrongs, to keep sexual responsibility and worship of the LORD alone, and to protect strangers, widows, orphans, and the poor, whose cry the gracious LORD promises to hear.

Setting:

Exodus 22 continues the Book of the Covenant that began at 20:22. Like chapter 21, it applies God’s commands to the common life of Israel as His people.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 22:1–6 — Restitution for Theft and Damage

  • Exodus 22:7–15 — Goods Held in Trust and Borrowed

  • Exodus 22:16–20 — Sexual Responsibility and Faithfulness to the LORD

  • Exodus 22:21–27 — The LORD Hears the Stranger, Widow, Orphan, and Poor

  • Exodus 22:28–31 — A People Holy to the LORD

Key Verse:

“For that is his only covering, it is his garment for his skin. What will he sleep in? And it will be that when he cries to Me, I will hear, for I am gracious.” (Exodus 22:27)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The chapter begins with property, and its principle is restoration. A thief repays more than he took, a careless farmer pays from the best of his own field, and the person who starts a fire that spreads is responsible for the damage. Where goods are left in trust, the law distinguishes theft from honest loss. A keeper whose animal was torn by a wild beast does not have to pay, and a dispute without witnesses is settled by “an oath of the LORD” (22:11). Every loss is not proof of guilt.

Then the laws turn to the heart of the covenant. A man who seduces a young woman must take responsibility for her, and sorcery, bestiality, and sacrifice to any god “except to the LORD only” (22:20) strike at Israel’s life with God. Most striking of all, God places Himself beside the people with no one to defend them. Israel must not oppress the stranger, “for you were strangers in the land of Egypt” (22:21). If a widow or orphan cries out, God says, “I will surely hear their cry” (22:23). A poor man’s cloak taken as a pledge must be returned before nightfall, for “when he cries to Me, I will hear, for I am gracious” (22:27). Israel is to be a people holy to God in ordinary life.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 22:1–6 — Restitution for Theft and Damage

A thief repays five oxen for an ox and four sheep for a sheep, or double if the animal is found alive, and the law treats a burglar killed at night differently from one killed in daylight. Anyone whose animal grazes in another’s field or whose fire spreads to another’s grain must make it good.

Exodus 22:7–15 — Goods Held in Trust and Borrowed

When goods or animals left in a neighbor’s care are stolen or lost, the judges decide disputed cases, and sometimes an oath before the LORD settles the matter. Responsibility depends on what happened, whether the owner was present, and whether the animal was borrowed or hired.

Exodus 22:16–20 — Sexual Responsibility and Faithfulness to the LORD

A man who seduces an unbetrothed young woman must pay the bride-price and marry her, or pay it if her father refuses. Sorcery, lying with an animal, and sacrificing to any god but the LORD are capital offenses.

Exodus 22:21–27 — The LORD Hears the Stranger, Widow, Orphan, and Poor

Israel must not oppress the stranger, remembering their own life as strangers in Egypt, and must not afflict widows or orphans, because the LORD will hear their cry. They must not charge interest to the poor and must return a pledged cloak by sunset, because the LORD is gracious and hears the poor.

Exodus 22:28–31 — A People Holy to the LORD

Israel must not revile God or curse a ruler, must bring the first of its harvest without delay, and must give its firstborn to the LORD. They are to be holy to Him, so they must not eat meat torn by wild animals.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 22 shows that God’s justice includes restoring what was lost. It distinguishes theft from damage caused by negligence, such as a spreading fire or a straying animal, and from loss that comes without anyone’s fault, and it guards against presuming guilt. The specific penalties of verses 18–20 belonged to Israel’s covenant order, and no believer or church carries them out (John 18:36). Yet the principles of honesty, restitution, and faithfulness to God reflect His unchanging character.

Above all, the chapter reveals God’s heart for the powerless. He grounds Israel’s kindness to strangers in their own memory of oppression, and He promises to hear the widow, the orphan, and the poor man shivering without his cloak. The God who heard Israel’s groaning in Egypt (2:24) still hears the cry of the helpless and expects His people to treat them as He does.

Key Themes to Watch For

Restitution. Wrongs against a neighbor’s property must be repaid, often more than was lost (22:1–6, 9).

Fair judgment. Liability depends on what actually happened, and disputes are settled before judges or by oath (22:7–15).

Faithfulness to the LORD alone. Sorcery and sacrifice to other gods strike at Israel’s covenant (22:18–20).

God hears the vulnerable. The LORD promises to hear strangers, widows, orphans, and the poor (22:21–27).

A holy people. Israel honors God and authority, gives Him the first and the firstborn, and stays holy even in what they eat (22:28–31).

Key Words & Concepts

Restitution (22:3). Repaying the owner for what was taken or damaged, often with an added amount.

An oath of the LORD (22:11). A solemn statement before God used to settle a dispute when there was no evidence.

Bride-price (22:16). A payment given to a bride’s family at marriage. A man who seduced a young woman had to pay it.

Stranger (22:21). A foreigner living among Israel without the protection of family or land. Israel’s own history as strangers in Egypt was to make them kind.

Pledge (22:26). Something given as security for a loan. A poor man’s cloak had to be returned each evening because it was his only covering at night.
