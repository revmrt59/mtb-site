Exodus 23:1
Exodus 23:1
NKJV

Exodus 23:1 "You shall not circulate a false report. Do not put your hand with the wicked to be an unrighteous witness.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 23:1 “You must not pass along false rumors. You must not cooperate with evil people by lying on the witness stand. (NLT)

Main Idea

Israel must not spread a false report or join with the wicked as an unrighteous witness. Truthful testimony protects justice.

Verse Explanation

Israel must not spread a false report or join with the wicked as an unrighteous witness. Truthful testimony protects justice.

“You shall not circulate a false report.”

Israel must not “circulate a false report,” spreading rumors or lies about another, especially in a legal matter. The ninth commandment (20:16) is applied to everyday speech and testimony.

“Do not put your hand with the wicked to be an unrighteous witness.”

To “put your hand with the wicked” means to join with them, perhaps by agreement. An “unrighteous witness” gives testimony that helps the guilty or harms the innocent. Truthful testimony protects justice.

Exodus 23:19
Exodus 23:19
NKJV

Exodus 23:19 The first of the firstfruits of your land you shall bring into the house of the LORD your God. You shall not boil a young goat in its mother's milk.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 23:19 “As you harvest your crops, bring the very best of the first harvest to the house of the LORD your God. “You must not cook a young goat in its mother’s milk. (NLT)

Main Idea

Israel must bring the first of the firstfruits to the house of the LORD and must not boil a young goat in its mother’s milk. Following its placement after the firstfruits, the command most likely separates Israel’s harvest worship from pagan practice.

Verse Explanation

Israel must bring the first of the firstfruits to the house of the LORD and must not boil a young goat in its mother’s milk. Following its placement after the firstfruits, the command most likely separates Israel’s harvest worship from pagan practice.

“The first of the firstfruits of your land you shall bring into the house of the LORD your God.”

Israel is to bring “the first of the firstfruits,” the very best of the earliest crops, to God’s house. God receives the first and best.

“You shall not boil a young goat in its mother’s milk.”

This command follows the firstfruits law. This study gives three reasons for it. First, its placement suggests it separates Israel’s harvest worship from pagan practice. Second, it reflects compassion and reverence for the order God created, like other laws protecting the bond between mother and young (Deuteronomy 22:6–7; Leviticus 22:28). Third, as a spiritual implication, it pictures the pure milk of the word, which must never be twisted to harm young believers (1 Peter 2:2).

Study Notes

The command appears three times (23:19; 34:26; Deuteronomy 14:21). A damaged text from ancient Ugarit is often cited as evidence of a Canaanite ritual of cooking a kid in milk, but its reading is debated. It can be cited as support, not proof. Later Jewish practice developed the separation of meat and dairy from this command.

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

Israel must beware of Him, obey His voice, and not provoke Him, for He will not pardon their transgressions, “for My name is in Him.” The Angel carries God’s own authority, so rebelling against Him is rebelling against God.

“Beware of Him and obey His voice;”

Israel must “beware of Him,” take Him seriously, and “obey His voice.” The journey depends on hearing and following Him.

“do not provoke Him, for He will not pardon your transgressions;”

The Angel “will not pardon” rebellion. He is not a mere messenger who can be ignored.

“for My name is in Him.”

The reason is decisive. The Angel carries God’s own identity and authority. To rebel against Him is to rebel against God. This Angel, like the Angel of the LORD at the burning bush (3:2–6), is probably an appearance of Christ before His incarnation. He bears God’s name and exercises God’s prerogative over sin.

No one will miscarry or be barren in the land, and God will fulfill the number of their days. God’s blessing extends to families and to long life.

Verse Explanation

No one will miscarry or be barren in the land, and God will fulfill the number of their days. God’s blessing extends to families and to long life.

“No one shall suffer miscarriage or be barren in your land;”

God promises fruitfulness in families. The people of Canaan sought children through their gods. Israel will receive them from the LORD.

“I will fulfill the number of your days.”

God will give full length of life. His blessing extends to families and to long life. These promises belong to Israel’s covenant in the land (Deuteronomy 7:12–15; 28:1–14). They show God’s goodness to an obedient people. They are not a guarantee that faithful believers today will never face sickness, barrenness, or early death.

God will send hornets before Israel to drive out the Hivite, the Canaanite, and the Hittite. God uses His own means to clear the way.

“And I will send hornets before you,”

God will send “hornets.” This may mean literal stinging insects or may describe the panic God sends on the nations.

They will drive out the peoples of the land. God uses His own means to clear the way.

Study Notes

Joshua 24:12 says God sent the hornet before Israel, “but not with your sword or with your bow.” The Hebrew word occurs only here, in Deuteronomy 7:20, and in Joshua 24:12. Its exact meaning is debated.

“And I will set your bounds from the Red Sea to the sea, Philistia, and from the desert to the River.”

God sets wide borders: from “the Red Sea” to “the sea” of the Philistines (the Mediterranean), and from “the desert” to “the River,” the Euphrates.

God will give the land, and Israel will take possession. The promised land has broad boundaries set by God.

Study Notes

The borders echo the promise to Abraham (Genesis 15:18). Israel reached something close to these limits under David and Solomon (1 Kings 4:21).

Verse Explanation

The nations must not dwell in Israel’s land, lest they lead Israel to sin, for serving their gods “will surely be a snare to you.” The warning closes the Book of the Covenant by guarding Israel’s exclusive loyalty to the LORD.

“They shall not dwell in your land, lest they make you sin against Me.”

The nations must not remain, because they would lead Israel into sin.

“For if you serve their gods, it will surely be a snare to you.”

Their gods would be “a snare,” a trap. The warning closes the Book of the Covenant by guarding Israel’s exclusive loyalty to the LORD.

Study Notes

Israel’s later history proved the warning true. Judges 2:1–3 records the Angel of the LORD rebuking Israel for making covenants with the people of the land, saying their gods would be a snare.
