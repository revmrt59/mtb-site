Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 23
Main Idea:

The Book of the Covenant closes with laws for honest justice and kindness even toward enemies, rhythms of rest that share God’s provision with the poor, three yearly feasts before the LORD, and His promise to send His Angel before Israel and give them the land little by little.

Setting:

Exodus 23 completes the Book of the Covenant that began at 20:22. In chapter 24 Israel accepts these words and the covenant is sealed.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 23:1–9 — Honest Justice and Kindness Even to an Enemy

  • Exodus 23:10–13 — Rest for the Land, Workers, and Animals

  • Exodus 23:14–19 — Three Feasts and the Firstfruits of the Land

  • Exodus 23:20–26 — The Angel Who Bears the LORD’s Name Goes Before Israel

  • Exodus 23:27–33 — The LORD Will Drive Out the Nations Little by Little

Key Verse:

“‘Behold, I send an Angel before you to keep you in the way and to bring you into the place which I have prepared.” (Exodus 23:20)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Justice in Israel must be honest all the way down. No false report, no following the crowd to do evil, no tilting a case for the rich or even for the poor, and no bribes, “for a bribe blinds the discerning” (23:8). Justice comes with kindness. If an enemy’s donkey strays or collapses under its load, Israel must help. The stranger must not be oppressed, “for you know the heart of a stranger” (23:9).

God then orders Israel’s time. Every seventh year the land rests so the poor can eat, and every seventh day the animals, servants, and strangers are refreshed. Three times a year Israel gathers before the LORD for the Feast of Unleavened Bread, the Feast of Harvest, and the Feast of Ingathering, and no one comes empty-handed. Worship shapes the calendar and confesses who gives every harvest.

Finally God looks ahead to the journey: “Behold, I send an Angel before you to keep you in the way and to bring you into the place which I have prepared” (23:20). This Angel bears God’s own name and authority, and He is probably an appearance of Christ. God will drive out the nations “little by little” (23:30), at the pace His people can hold the land, and Israel must make no covenant with their gods.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 23:1–9 — Honest Justice and Kindness Even to an Enemy

Israel must not spread false reports, follow the crowd into wrongdoing, twist justice for rich or poor, or take bribes. They must return an enemy’s straying animal, help the donkey of someone who hates them, and not oppress the stranger, since they know what it is to be strangers.

Exodus 23:10–13 — Rest for the Land, Workers, and Animals

Every seventh year the land lies fallow so the poor and the animals may eat, and every seventh day Israel rests so that animals, servants, and strangers are refreshed. Israel must heed all God has said and not even speak the names of other gods.

Exodus 23:14–19 — Three Feasts and the Firstfruits of the Land

Three times a year Israel keeps a feast to the LORD: Unleavened Bread, Harvest, and Ingathering, and no one is to appear before Him empty-handed. They are to bring the first of their harvest to the LORD’s house and not boil a young goat in its mother’s milk.

Exodus 23:20–26 — The Angel Who Bears the LORD’s Name Goes Before Israel

The LORD sends an Angel ahead of Israel to guard them and bring them to the place He has prepared, and they must obey Him because God’s name is in Him. If Israel obeys and rejects the gods of the land, the LORD will oppose their enemies and bless their food, water, and families.

Exodus 23:27–33 — The LORD Will Drive Out the Nations Little by Little

The LORD will send His terror and hornets ahead of Israel and drive out the land’s inhabitants little by little, until Israel grows enough to possess it within the borders He sets. Israel must make no covenant with these peoples or their gods, because serving them would be a trap.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 23 shows that justice and mercy belong together. The law forbids favoritism in court, even toward the poor, and in the same breath requires practical kindness even toward an enemy. God’s people are to be fair to everyone and kind to anyone.

The chapter also shows God’s care in the timing of His gifts. The seventh year and seventh day share His provision with those who have the least. The land would come gradually so Israel could hold it. The command about a young goat likely kept Israel’s harvest worship separate from pagan practice, reflected care for the order God made, and suggests that what God gives to nourish must never be turned to harm, like “the pure milk of the word” given to young believers (1 Peter 2:2).

Key Themes to Watch For

Honest justice. Israel must refuse false witness, crowd pressure, partiality, and bribes (23:1–3, 6–8).

Kindness to enemies and strangers. God’s people help an enemy’s animal and remember their own life as strangers (23:4–5, 9).

Rest that shares provision. The seventh year and seventh day refresh the poor, servants, strangers, and animals (23:10–12).

Worship that orders the year. Three feasts gather Israel before the LORD with thanksgiving for the harvest (23:14–17).

Guidance into the land. The name-bearing Angel goes before Israel, and God gives the land little by little (23:20–30).

Key Words & Concepts

Fallow (23:11). Land left unplanted for a year. Whatever grew on its own was left for the poor and the animals.

The three feasts (23:14–16). Unleavened Bread remembers the exodus. Harvest, later called Weeks or Pentecost, celebrates the first wheat. Ingathering, also called Tabernacles, celebrates the gathering of the year’s fruit.

A young goat in its mother’s milk (23:19). A command placed right after the firstfruits. It most likely separated Israel’s harvest worship from pagan practice and showed respect for the order God created.

My name is in Him (23:21). The Angel carries God’s own authority. In light of the bush (3:2–6), He is probably an appearance of Christ.

Hornets (23:28). Stinging insects, or possibly a picture of terror, that God would send ahead of Israel to drive out the nations.
