Verse-by-Verse
Exodus 24:1
Exodus 24:1
NKJV

Exodus 24:1 Now He said to Moses, "Come up to the LORD, you and Aaron, Nadab and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel, and worship from afar.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 24:1 Then the LORD instructed Moses: “Come up here to me, and bring along Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and seventy of Israel’s elders. All of you must worship from a distance. (NLT)

Main Idea

God tells Moses to come up with Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and seventy elders “and worship from afar.” Israel’s leaders are invited into God’s presence, but at a distance.

Verse Explanation

God tells Moses to come up with Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and seventy elders “and worship from afar.” Israel’s leaders are invited into God’s presence, but at a distance.

“Now He said to Moses, ‘Come up to the LORD, you and Aaron, Nadab and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel,”

God invites Israel’s leaders up the mountain. “Nadab and Abihu” were Aaron’s two oldest sons (6:23). The “seventy of the elders” represent the whole people.

“and worship from afar.”

They are to bow down at a distance. Israel’s leaders are invited into God’s presence, but at a distance.

Study Notes

Nadab and Abihu later died for offering “profane fire” before the LORD (Leviticus 10:1–2). Having been near God did not protect them from judgment when they disregarded His holiness.

Moses alone may come near the LORD, while the others stay back and the people do not go up at all. The levels of approach show both God’s welcome and His holiness.

“And Moses alone shall come near the LORD, but they shall not come near;”

Only Moses may come close. The leaders must stay back.

“nor shall the people go up with him.”

The people remain below. There are three levels of approach: the people at the foot, the leaders partway up, and Moses near God. The levels of approach show both God’s welcome and His holiness.

“And all the people answered with one voice and said, ‘All the words which the LORD has said we will do.”

The people answer “with one voice.” They commit themselves to obey (compare 19:8). The people commit themselves to the covenant God has spoken.

Exodus 24:4
Exodus 24:4
NKJV

Exodus 24:4 And Moses wrote all the words of the LORD. And he rose early in the morning, and built an altar at the foot of the mountain, and twelve pillars according to the twelve tribes of Israel.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 24:4 Then Moses carefully wrote down all the LORD’s instructions. Early the next morning Moses got up and built an altar at the foot of the mountain. He also set up twelve pillars, one for each of the twelve tribes of Israel. (NLT)

Main Idea

Moses writes all the words of the LORD, then builds an altar at the foot of the mountain with twelve pillars for the twelve tribes. The covenant is put in writing and set before God with every tribe represented.

Verse Explanation

Moses writes all the words of the LORD, then builds an altar at the foot of the mountain with twelve pillars for the twelve tribes. The covenant is put in writing and set before God with every tribe represented.

“And Moses wrote all the words of the LORD.”

Moses puts the covenant in writing. This written record is the Book of the Covenant (24:7).

“And he rose early in the morning, and built an altar at the foot of the mountain,”

Early the next morning Moses builds “an altar” below the mountain.

“and twelve pillars according to the twelve tribes of Israel.”

He also sets up “twelve pillars,” one for each tribe. The covenant is put in writing and set before God with every tribe represented.

Study Notes

These pillars were memorial stones, not idols. They differ from the pagan sacred pillars Israel was told to destroy (23:24). Joshua later set up twelve stones at the Jordan as a memorial (Joshua 4:1–9).

“Then he took the Book of the Covenant and read in the hearing of the people.”

Moses reads “the Book of the Covenant” aloud. The people hear exactly what they are agreeing to.

“And they said, ‘All that the LORD has said we will do, and be obedient.”

They respond a third time (19:8; 24:3), now adding “and be obedient,” literally and we will hear. The people hear the covenant read and pledge their obedience again.

Exodus 24:8
Exodus 24:8
NKJV

Exodus 24:8 And Moses took the blood, sprinkled it on the people, and said, "This is the blood of the covenant which the LORD has made with you according to all these words."(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 24:8 Then Moses took the blood from the basins and splattered it over the people, declaring, “Look, this blood confirms the covenant the LORD has made with you in giving you these instructions.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Moses sprinkles the blood on the people and says, “This is the blood of the covenant which the LORD has made with you.” The blood seals the covenant between God and Israel, words Jesus later echoes when He speaks of His own blood of the new covenant.

Verse Explanation

Moses sprinkles the blood on the people and says, “This is the blood of the covenant which the LORD has made with you.” The blood seals the covenant between God and Israel, words Jesus later echoes when He speaks of His own blood of the new covenant.

“And Moses took the blood, sprinkled it on the people, and said,”

Moses now “sprinkled” the other half of the blood “on the people.” The same sacrifice touches both the altar and the people, binding them together in covenant.

“This is the blood of the covenant which the LORD has made with you according to all these words.”

Moses names what the blood means. The covenant is ratified by blood, sealing the people’s pledge and God’s commitment. The blood seals the covenant between God and Israel. Hebrews 9:18–22 recalls this ceremony to explain that the first covenant was put into effect with blood. At the Last Supper Jesus said, “For this is My blood of the new covenant, which is shed for many for the remission of sins” (Matthew 26:28). His words echo Moses’ words here and point to the new covenant promised in Jeremiah 31:31–34.

Exodus 24:10
Exodus 24:10
NKJV

Exodus 24:10 and they saw the God of Israel. And there was under His feet as it were a paved work of sapphire stone, and it was like the very heavens in its clarity.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 24:10 There they saw the God of Israel. Under his feet there seemed to be a surface of brilliant blue lapis lazuli, as clear as the sky itself. (NLT)

Main Idea

“And they saw the God of Israel,” with something like a pavement of sapphire under His feet, as clear as the heavens. The leaders are given a real but limited vision of God’s glory.

Verse Explanation

“And they saw the God of Israel,” with something like a pavement of sapphire under His feet, as clear as the heavens. The leaders are given a real but limited vision of God’s glory.

“and they saw the God of Israel.”

The narrator states it without qualification. This was a real encounter with God, though limited. Later God tells Moses, “You cannot see My face; for no man shall see Me, and live” (33:20). The leaders saw a real manifestation of God’s glory, not the fullness of His being.

“And there was under His feet as it were a paved work of sapphire stone, and it was like the very heavens in its clarity.”

The account describes only what was “under His feet.” It looked “as it were a paved work of sapphire stone,” clear and brilliant “like the very heavens.” The text gives no description of God Himself. The leaders were given a real but limited vision of God’s glory.

Study Notes

Ezekiel also saw something “like a sapphire stone” in his vision of the throne of God (Ezekiel 1:26).

The Hebrew word translated “sapphire” may refer to lapis lazuli, a deep blue stone.

Exodus 24:11
Exodus 24:11
NKJV

Exodus 24:11 But on the nobles of the children of Israel He did not lay His hand. So they saw God, and they ate and drank.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 24:11 And though these nobles of Israel gazed upon God, he did not destroy them. In fact, they ate a covenant meal, eating and drinking in his presence! (NLT)

Main Idea

God does not lay His hand on the nobles of Israel, “So they saw God, and they ate and drank.” The covenant people’s leaders share a meal in God’s presence and live.

Verse Explanation

God does not lay His hand on the nobles of Israel, “So they saw God, and they ate and drank.” The covenant people’s leaders share a meal in God’s presence and live.

“But on the nobles of the children of Israel He did not lay His hand.”

God “did not lay His hand” on them. The text does not report what the nobles expected. Elsewhere the danger of approaching God is plain: the people at the mountain asked that God not speak with them, “lest we die” (20:19). Here God spared the leaders who saw Him.

“So they saw God, and they ate and drank.”

They “saw God,” and in His presence “they ate and drank.” The meal may have come from the peace offerings. The leaders of the covenant people shared a meal in God’s presence and lived. The covenant brought fellowship with God.

“and the cloud covered it six days. And on the seventh day He called to Moses out of the midst of the cloud.”

For “six days” the cloud covered the mountain while Moses waited. On “the seventh day” God called him. God’s glory remained on the mountain, and Moses waited for His call.
