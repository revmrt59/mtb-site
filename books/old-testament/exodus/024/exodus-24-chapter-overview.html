Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 24
Main Idea:

The covenant at Sinai is sealed: Israel pledges to do all the LORD has spoken, Moses confirms the covenant with blood, Israel’s leaders see the God of Israel and eat and drink before Him, and Moses enters the cloud of glory for forty days and nights.

Setting:

Exodus 24 follows the Ten Commandments and the Book of the Covenant (20:1–23:33) and prepares for the tabernacle instructions of chapter 25.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 24:1–2 — The LORD Summons Moses and the Leaders to Worship

  • Exodus 24:3–8 — The Covenant Is Confirmed with Blood

  • Exodus 24:9–11 — Israel’s Leaders See God and Eat Before Him

  • Exodus 24:12–18 — Moses Enters the Cloud of Glory on the Mountain

Key Verse:

“And Moses took the blood, sprinkled it on the people, and said, ‘This is the blood of the covenant which the LORD has made with you according to all these words.’” (Exodus 24:8)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The covenant God offered at Sinai now becomes binding. Moses tells the people all the LORD’s words, and they answer with one voice, “All the words which the LORD has said we will do” (24:3). Moses writes the words down and builds an altar with twelve pillars for the twelve tribes, and at his direction young men offer burnt offerings and peace offerings (24:5). Moses puts half the blood on the altar. Then Moses reads the Book of the Covenant, the people pledge obedience again, and he sprinkles the rest of the blood on them: “This is the blood of the covenant which the LORD has made with you” (24:8). God and His people are bound together by blood.

What follows is astonishing. Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and seventy elders go up the mountain, “and they saw the God of Israel” (24:10), with something like a sapphire pavement beneath His feet. They do not die. They eat and drink in His presence. The covenant leads to fellowship at God’s table. Then the glory of the LORD settles on Sinai like a consuming fire, and Moses walks into the cloud to stay forty days and nights, where God will show him the pattern for a dwelling among His people.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 24:1–2 — The LORD Summons Moses and the Leaders to Worship

The LORD calls Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and seventy elders to come up and worship at a distance. Only Moses may come near the LORD, and the people must not go up at all.

Exodus 24:3–8 — The Covenant Is Confirmed with Blood

Moses tells the people the LORD’s words, writes them down, builds an altar with twelve pillars, and sprinkles half the blood of the offerings on the altar. After the people hear the Book of the Covenant and pledge obedience, he sprinkles the blood on them as the blood of the covenant.

Exodus 24:9–11 — Israel’s Leaders See God and Eat Before Him

Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and the seventy elders go up and see the God of Israel, with a pavement like sapphire beneath His feet. God does not strike them, and they eat and drink in His presence.

Exodus 24:12–18 — Moses Enters the Cloud of Glory on the Mountain

The LORD calls Moses up to receive the stone tablets, and he goes with Joshua, leaving Aaron and Hur in charge. The glory of the LORD rests on Sinai like a consuming fire, and on the seventh day Moses enters the cloud and stays forty days and forty nights.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 24 shows that God’s covenant is sealed by blood and leads to fellowship. Jesus took up these very words at the Last Supper: “this is My blood of the new covenant” (Matthew 26:28). Hebrews explains that the first covenant was put into effect with blood, pointing to the blood of Christ (Hebrews 9:18–22).

The chapter also holds God’s holiness and nearness together. The leaders worship at a distance, yet they see Him and eat before Him. Their vision was real but limited, for no one can see God’s full face and live (33:20). The cloud, the glory on the mountain, and Moses’ forty days look ahead to the Transfiguration, where Moses appears in glory speaking of the departure Jesus would accomplish at Jerusalem (Luke 9:31).

Key Themes to Watch For

Covenant commitment. Israel twice pledges to do all the LORD has spoken (24:3, 7).

The blood of the covenant. Blood on the altar and on the people seals the covenant (24:6–8).

Fellowship before God. Israel’s leaders see God and eat and drink in His presence (24:9–11).

Glory on the mountain. The LORD’s glory rests on Sinai like a consuming fire (24:15–17).

Moses as mediator. Moses alone comes near and enters the cloud for forty days (24:2, 18).

Key Words & Concepts

Worship from afar (24:1). The leaders are invited into God’s presence, but at a distance that respects His holiness.

Book of the Covenant (24:7). The written record of God’s covenant words, which Moses reads to the people.

Blood of the covenant (24:8). The sacrificial blood that seals the covenant between God and Israel.

A paved work of sapphire stone (24:10). The only detail given of what the leaders saw, describing what lay beneath God’s feet, as clear as the sky.

The glory of the LORD (24:16–17). The visible radiance of God’s presence, which looked like a consuming fire to the people below.
