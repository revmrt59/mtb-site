DWELL - ( shakan , Strong's H7931 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb shakan means to settle down, abide, or take up residence. While other Hebrew words denote temporary lodging or general sitting, shakan specifically evokes the image of pitching a tent and establishing a persistent, relational presence. In Exodus 25:8, it marks a monumental shift in redemptive history: the transcendent Creator of the universe requests a physical sanctuary so that He may intimately tent in the very center of His people's camp.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near East, shakan was deeply rooted in the nomadic lifestyle of tent-dwelling. It carries the physical imagery of driving tent pegs into the ground, unfolding the skins, and settling down to rest after a journey. The related noun mishkan (the Tabernacle) literally translates to "dwelling place" or "tent." When God uses shakan, He adopts the language of His wandering people. He is not a distant deity bound to a fixed, celestial temple; He is a relational God who pitches His tent alongside His people, traveling with them through the dust and trials of the wilderness.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Shakan means to settle down, inhabit, abide, or reside. It is distinct from other common Hebrew verbs for dwelling:

• Gur (H1481) means to sojourn or dwell temporarily as a stranger or resident alien without permanent rights.

• Yashab (H3427) means to sit down, remain, or inhabit, and is frequently used of kings sitting on thrones or citizens settling permanently in a fortified city.

• By contrast, shakan emphasizes the localized, intentional act of establishing a residence in close proximity to others. It denotes a neighborly dwelling, a persistent presence that makes community possible.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The verb is used in several vivid ways throughout the Old Testament:

• The Settling of the Cloud: In Exodus 24:16, the glory of the Lord settled (shakan) on Mount Sinai, enveloped in a cloud. Later, when the Tabernacle was completed, the cloud settled upon it (Numbers 9:17), signaling God's guidance and presence.

• Animal Habitats: It describes wildlife making a home or nesting in a specific location (Ezekiel 17:23), illustrating a natural, peaceful resting place.

• The Neighborly Presence: It is used of tribes or people groups settling down in geographic proximity to one another (Genesis 16:12).

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of shakan cannot be overstated. It represents the ultimate goal of the covenant: God living in the midst of His redeemed people.

• The Shekinah: Although the word Shekinah (the visible, radiant manifestation of God's presence) does not appear in the Hebrew Bible, it is a post-biblical rabbinic noun directly derived from the root shakan.

• Holiness and Proximity: Because a holy God chooses to shakan among sinful people, a sanctuary (miqdash) and a system of sacrifice are required. God's desire for proximity drives the entire levitical system; clean and unclean laws exist precisely because His dwelling place is in the center of the camp.

• The Incarnation: This concept reaches its climax in John 1:14, where the Greek New Testament states that the Word became flesh and "tabernacled" (eskenosen) among us. The Gospel writers chose a Greek word that directly mirrors the sound and theology of the Hebrew shakan. Jesus is the ultimate shakan of God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Exodus 25:8, God commands, "And let them make Me a sanctuary, that I may dwell among them." The word order in the Hebrew text is highly significant. It does not say, "that I may dwell in it [the building]," but "that I may dwell among them [the people]." The physical Tabernacle was never meant to be a house that contained or localized the infinite God (1 Kings 8:27). Instead, it served as a visible, physical focal point of His covenantal presence. God did not command the building of the Tabernacle because He needed shelter, but because His people needed a visible assurance of His promise to be near them.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 29:45 - "I will dwell among the children of Israel and will be their God."

• Numbers 5:3 - "You shall send both male and female away; you shall send them outside the camp, that they may not defile their camps in the midst of which I dwell."

• Psalm 68:18 - "You have ascended on high, You have led captivity captive; You have received gifts among men, Even from the rebellious, That the Lord God might dwell there."

• Psalm 135:21 - "Blessed be the Lord out of Zion, Who dwells in Jerusalem! Praise the Lord!"

7. Other Relevant Insights

A beautiful contrast exists between God's transcendent dwelling place and His earthly dwelling place. While God "dwells (shakan) on high" in unapproachable holiness (Isaiah 33:5), He simultaneously promises to "dwell (shakan) with him who has a contrite and humble spirit" (Isaiah 57:15). The high and lofty Sovereign of the universe actively seeks to pitch His tent in the hearts of those who recognize their need for Him.
