Exodus 25:2
Exodus 25:2
NKJV

Exodus 25:2 "Speak to the children of Israel, that they bring Me an offering. From everyone who gives it willingly with his heart you shall take My offering.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 25:2 “Tell the people of Israel to bring me their sacred offerings. Accept the contributions from all whose hearts are moved to offer them. (NLT)

Main Idea

Israel is to bring God an offering, taken “From everyone who gives it willingly with his heart.” God’s dwelling will be built from gifts freely given.

Verse Explanation

Israel is to bring God an offering, taken “From everyone who gives it willingly with his heart.” God’s dwelling will be built from gifts freely given.

“Speak to the children of Israel, that they bring Me an offering.”

God asks His people for “an offering.” The Hebrew word describes something lifted up or set apart as a contribution to God.

“From everyone who gives it willingly with his heart you shall take My offering.”

The gift is to come only from those who give “willingly with his heart.” The materials listed in this chapter were to come from these freely given gifts. The census ransom in 30:11–16 was different: a required half shekel from each counted man, which later supplied the silver sockets and fittings of the sanctuary (38:25–28). When the time came, the people gave so much that Moses had to stop them (36:5–7).

Exodus 25:8
Exodus 25:8
NKJV

Exodus 25:8 And let them make Me a sanctuary, that I may dwell among them.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 25:8 “Have the people of Israel build me a holy sanctuary so I can live among them. (NLT)

Main Idea

“And let them make Me a sanctuary, that I may dwell among them.” God’s purpose in the whole project is to live among His people.

Verse Explanation

“And let them make Me a sanctuary, that I may dwell among them.” God’s purpose in the whole project is to live among His people.

“And let them make Me a sanctuary,”

A “sanctuary” is a holy place, set apart for God.

“that I may dwell among them.”

This is the purpose of everything that follows. God wants to “dwell among them.” The God who brought Israel out of Egypt to Himself (19:4) now commands a dwelling so that He can live in their midst. John 1:14 says, “the Word became flesh and dwelt among us,” using a Greek verb that means to pitch a tent. In Christ, God came to dwell with His people, and Revelation 21:3 looks to the day when “the tabernacle of God is with men, and He will dwell with them.”

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb for “dwell” (shakan) is related to the word for the tabernacle (mishkan), the dwelling place.

“You shall make a mercy seat of pure gold;”

It is the same size as the ark. The mercy seat will cover the ark and become the place of meeting.

Study Notes

The Greek translation uses the word hilasterion for the mercy seat. Paul uses the same word of Christ, “whom God set forth as a propitiation by His blood” (Romans 3:25). Hebrews 9:5 refers to the cherubim “overshadowing the mercy seat.”

“The showbread,” literally bread of the presence, is to be on the table “before Me always.” Leviticus 24:5–9 describes twelve loaves, one for each tribe, replaced every Sabbath and eaten by the priests. Bread is kept continually in the LORD’s presence. The table and its bread picture Christ, who nourishes His people. John 6 first speaks of the manna, and then Jesus says, “I am the bread of life” (John 6:35). The showbread keeps its own setting in the sanctuary, where the bread stood before God for all twelve tribes.

Study Notes

David and his men ate the showbread when they were hungry (1 Samuel 21:1–6). Jesus cited that event when He defended His disciples (Matthew 12:3–4).
