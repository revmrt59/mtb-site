TABLE - ( shulchan , Strong's H7979 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word shulchan primarily refers to a table, most prominently the golden Table of Showbread set within the Holy Place of the Tabernacle. Far from a mere piece of furniture, it served as a rich symbol of God's covenantal fellowship, hospitality, and daily provision for His people in the very presence of His glory.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- Derived from a root meaning to spread out or extend, a shulchan was historically a skin spread out on the ground for eating, which later evolved into a raised wooden frame or table.

- In ancient Near Eastern culture, sharing a table was an act of deep covenant, peace, friendship, and shared life.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Shulchan (H7979) means a table, tray, or counter used for eating, serving food, or holding ritual vessels.

- In the context of the sanctuary, it specifically designates the Table of Showbread overlaid with pure gold, commissioned for the Tabernacle and later the Temple.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The word is used over 70 times in the Old Testament, ranging from ordinary household dining tables (2 Samuel 9:7) to royal banquet tables (1 Kings 10:5) and sacred liturgical furnishings (Exodus 25:23).

- In the Tabernacle, the table carried the "bread of the presence," presented continuously before God as a fragrant offering and a visual acknowledgment that God sustains Israel's twelve tribes.

4. Theological Implications

- The Table in the Holy Place visually declared that God desires fellowship with His people, hosting them spiritually in His house.

- It prefigures the ultimate communion believers share with God, culminating in the table of the Lord in the New Testament and the Messianic banquet in the kingdom of heaven.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Exodus 26:35, Moses is instructed regarding the architectural arrangement of the Tabernacle: the table is to be placed on the north side of the Holy Place, opposite the golden lampstand.

- Its precise placement emphasizes order, holiness, and the ongoing maintenance of covenant fellowship before entering the inner veil.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Exodus 25:23: "You shall also make a table of acacia wood..."

- Leviticus 24:6: "And you shall set them in two rows, six in a row, on the pure gold table before the Lord."

- Psalm 23:5: "You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies..."

- Malachi 1:7: "You offer defiled food on My altar, but say, 'In what way have we defiled You?' By saying, 'The table of the Lord is contemptible.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The table was required to have a rim a handbreadth wide with a molding around the edge, ensuring that the sacred bread would not slide off, picturing divine security and care.

- The perpetual presence of bread upon the table underscored that human life depends entirely on divine sustenance.
