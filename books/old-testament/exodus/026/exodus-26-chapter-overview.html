Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 26
Main Idea:

The LORD gives the pattern for the tabernacle itself, its curtains, coverings, and gold-covered frame joined into one dwelling, and for the veil that divides the holy place from the Most Holy, where the ark and mercy seat will stand.

Setting:

Exodus 26 continues the instructions Moses receives on Sinai. Chapter 25 described the furnishings, and this chapter describes the tent that will house them.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 26:1–6 — The Inner Curtains Joined into One Tabernacle

  • Exodus 26:7–14 — The Tent of Goats’ Hair and Its Coverings

  • Exodus 26:15–30 — The Gold-Covered Frame of Boards, Sockets, and Bars

  • Exodus 26:31–35 — The Veil Divides the Holy Place from the Most Holy

  • Exodus 26:36–37 — The Screen for the Door of the Tabernacle

Key Verse:

“And you shall hang the veil from the clasps. Then you shall bring the ark of the Testimony in there, behind the veil. The veil shall be a divider for you between the holy place and the Most Holy.” (Exodus 26:33)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

After the furnishings, God describes the dwelling. Ten linen curtains in blue, purple, and scarlet, woven with cherubim, are joined by gold clasps “so that it may be one tabernacle” (26:6). Over them goes a tent of goats’ hair, then coverings of red-dyed ram skins and badger skins. Underneath stands a frame of upright boards covered with gold and set in sockets of silver, held together by bars. The tabernacle is portable, made to be taken down and carried as Israel travels, yet built with beauty and care. Everything is to be raised “according to its pattern which you were shown on the mountain” (26:30).

Then comes the veil. Woven like the inner curtains and hung on four gold-covered pillars, it marks the boundary between the holy place and the Most Holy. Behind it stand the ark and the mercy seat, God’s place of meeting. In front of it stand the table and the lampstand. Only the high priest would pass through, once a year, with blood. A second, separate screen hangs at the entrance of the tent. The veil shows both God’s nearness and the distance sin creates. Hebrews says that Jesus opened “a new and living way which He consecrated for us, through the veil, that is, His flesh” (Hebrews 10:20).

Chapter Sections

Exodus 26:1–6 — The Inner Curtains Joined into One Tabernacle

Ten curtains of fine linen in blue, purple, and scarlet, woven with cherubim, are joined in two sets of five. Fifty gold clasps join the sets so that the dwelling is one tabernacle.

Exodus 26:7–14 — The Tent of Goats’ Hair and Its Coverings

Eleven curtains of goats’ hair, joined with bronze clasps, form a tent over the tabernacle, with the extra length hanging over the back and sides. Over them go coverings of red-dyed ram skins and badger skins.

Exodus 26:15–30 — The Gold-Covered Frame of Boards, Sockets, and Bars

Upright acacia boards set in silver sockets form the south, north, and west walls, with strengthened corners and bars running through them, including a middle bar from end to end. Boards and bars are covered with gold, and Moses is to raise the tabernacle according to the pattern shown on the mountain.

Exodus 26:31–35 — The Veil Divides the Holy Place from the Most Holy

A veil woven with cherubim hangs on four gold-covered pillars, and the ark of the Testimony is placed behind it in the Most Holy. The mercy seat sits on the ark there, while the table and lampstand stand outside the veil.

Exodus 26:36–37 — The Screen for the Door of the Tabernacle

A woven screen of blue, purple, and scarlet thread and fine linen is made for the door of the tent. It hangs on five gold-covered pillars set in bronze sockets.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 26 shows that God wanted to live among His people in a dwelling that could travel with them. The tabernacle went where they went, a picture of the God who does not stay distant but pitches His tent among His people. John uses this very picture when he says that the Word became flesh and “dwelt,” literally tabernacled, among us (John 1:14).

The veil helps readers feel the weight of what Christ has done. Under the old arrangement the way into the Most Holy was closed to all but the high priest, once a year. When Jesus died, the temple veil was torn in two (Matthew 27:51), and now believers may draw near to God with confidence through His blood (Hebrews 10:19–22).

Key Themes to Watch For

One dwelling. The curtains and the tent are joined so that the tabernacle is one whole (26:6, 11).

A portable house for God. The tabernacle is built to be taken down and carried with Israel (26:15–30).

Following the pattern. Moses must raise the tabernacle exactly as shown on the mountain (26:30).

The veil and the Most Holy. The veil separates God’s innermost dwelling, where the ark and mercy seat stand (26:31–34).

Key Words & Concepts

Tabernacle (26:1). The tent God commanded as His dwelling among Israel. The Hebrew word is related to the verb for “dwell.”

Acacia wood (26:15). A hard, durable wood that grows in the desert, used for the frame and furnishings.

Sockets of silver (26:19). Bases that held the boards upright, giving the frame a foundation.

The veil (26:31). The woven curtain that divided the holy place from the Most Holy. Hebrews identifies it with Christ’s flesh, through which the way to God is opened (Hebrews 10:19–20).

The Most Holy (26:33). The inner room of the tabernacle, where the ark and mercy seat stood.
