Exodus 27:1
NKJV

Exodus 27:1 "You shall make an altar of acacia wood, five cubits long and five cubits wide"”the altar shall be square"”and its height shall be three cubits.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 27:1 “Using acacia wood, construct a square altar 7 1⁄2 feet wide, 7 1⁄2 feet long, and 4 1⁄2 feet high. (NLT)

Main Idea

Israel is to make an altar of acacia wood, five cubits square and three cubits high. The altar of burnt offering will stand in the courtyard.

Verse Explanation

Israel is to make an altar of acacia wood, five cubits square and three cubits high. The altar of burnt offering will stand in the courtyard.

“You shall make an altar of acacia wood, five cubits long and five cubits wide—the altar shall be square—”

The instructions move outside the tent to the courtyard. “An altar” of acacia wood is made, about seven and a half feet square.

“and its height shall be three cubits.”

It stood about four and a half feet high. This was the altar of burnt offering, where the daily sacrifices were made (29:38–42). It stood just inside the courtyard gate, in front of the tent (40:29).

None.

Verse Explanation

“You shall make its horns on its four corners; its horns shall be of one piece with it.”

“Horns” project from the four corners, made “of one piece” with the altar. Blood from sin offerings was put on the horns (29:12; Leviticus 4:7).

“And you shall overlay it with bronze.”

The altar is covered with “bronze,” which could withstand the fire. The horned altar is prepared for sacrifice.

Study Notes

People seeking mercy sometimes took hold of the horns of the altar (1 Kings 1:50–51; 2:28). The law did not protect a deliberate murderer there (21:14).

Exodus 27:8
Exodus 27:8

Exodus 27:8 You shall make it hollow with boards; as it was shown you on the mountain, so shall they make it.(NKJV)

NLT

Main Idea

The altar is to be hollow, made with boards, “as it was shown you on the mountain.” Even the altar’s construction follows God’s pattern.

Verse Explanation

The altar is to be hollow, made with boards, “as it was shown you on the mountain.” Even the altar’s construction follows God’s pattern.

“You shall make it hollow with boards;”

The altar is “hollow,” a box frame without top or bottom. When set up, it may have been filled with earth or stones (compare 20:24–25).

“as it was shown you on the mountain, so shall they make it.”

The altar too follows God’s pattern. Even the altar’s construction follows God’s design. The bronze altar points to Christ’s once-for-all offering. Hebrews says believers “have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all,” and that “by one offering He has perfected forever those who are being sanctified” (Hebrews 10:10, 14). The altar stood between the courtyard gate and the tent, so no one approached the dwelling without passing the place of sacrifice.

Exodus 27:20–21 — Pure Oil for the Lamp That Burns Before the LORD

Study Notes

None.

Exodus 27:20
Exodus 27:20
NKJV

Exodus 27:20 "And you shall command the children of Israel that they bring you pure oil of pressed olives for the light, to cause the lamp to burn continually.(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 27:20 “Command the people of Israel to bring you pure oil of pressed olives for the light, to keep the lamps burning continually. (NLT)

Main Idea

Israel is to bring pure oil of pressed olives for the light, “to cause the lamp to burn continually.” The people supply the oil that keeps the light burning before God.

Verse Explanation

Israel is to bring pure oil of pressed olives for the light, “to cause the lamp to burn continually.” The people supply the oil that keeps the light burning before God.

“And you shall command the children of Israel that they bring you pure oil of pressed olives for the light,”

The people must supply “pure oil of pressed olives.” The finest oil came from olives crushed by hand. It was clear and burned with little smoke.

“to cause the lamp to burn continually.”

The oil keeps “the lamp” burning “continually,” that is, every night without fail (27:21). The people supply the oil that keeps the light burning before God. The oil pictures dependence on the Holy Spirit. In Zechariah 4:2–6 a lampstand fed by olive oil is explained with the words, “Not by might nor by power, but by My Spirit,” says the LORD of hosts. The light in the sanctuary also points to Christ, the light of the world (John 8:12).

None.
