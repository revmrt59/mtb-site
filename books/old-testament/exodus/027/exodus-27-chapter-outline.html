Exodus 27 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Exodus 27 moves outward from the tent to the bronze altar of burnt offering and the courtyard that surrounds the tabernacle, with its single gate. The chapter ends by turning back inside the tent to command pure olive oil for the lamp, which Aaron and his sons are to keep burning before the LORD.

Chapter Sections

1. Exodus 27:1–8 — The Bronze Altar

Israel is to make a square altar of acacia wood overlaid with bronze, with horns of one piece with it, a bronze grate, and bronze utensils. It is to be hollow, carried by poles, and made “as it was shown you on the mountain” (Exodus 27:8).

2. Exodus 27:9–19 — The Courtyard and Its Gate

A courtyard one hundred cubits long and fifty wide is enclosed with hangings of fine linen on pillars set in bronze sockets with silver hooks and bands. On the east side a woven screen twenty cubits long forms the gate of the court, and all the pegs and utensils for the service are bronze.

3. Exodus 27:20–21 — Pure Oil for the Lamp That Burns Before the LORD

Israel is to bring pure oil of pressed olives “to cause the lamp to burn continually” (Exodus 27:20). Aaron and his sons are to tend it from evening until morning before the LORD, outside the veil, as a statute forever.
