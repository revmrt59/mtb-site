Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 27
Main Idea:

The LORD’s pattern moves outward to the bronze altar of burnt offering and the courtyard with its single entrance, then returns inside to command pure olive oil so that the lamp may burn continually before Him.

Setting:

Exodus 27 continues the instructions given to Moses on Sinai (chapters 25–31). After the tent and its veil (chapter 26), God describes what stands outside it.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 27:1–8 — The Bronze Altar

  • Exodus 27:9–19 — The Courtyard and Its Gate

  • Exodus 27:20–21 — Pure Oil for the Lamp That Burns Before the LORD

Key Verse:

“‘And you shall command the children of Israel that they bring you pure oil of pressed olives for the light, to cause the lamp to burn continually.” (Exodus 27:20)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Outside the tent, in the courtyard, stands the bronze altar, a large square box of acacia wood overlaid with bronze, with a horn at each corner. It is the first thing a worshiper would meet after entering the court, and it is the place of sacrifice. No one approached the tent of God’s dwelling except by way of the altar. It is hollow and carried by poles, made “as it was shown you on the mountain” (27:8).

Around everything runs the courtyard, a rectangle of white linen hangings on pillars set in bronze sockets, with silver hooks and bands, one hundred cubits long and fifty wide. It has only one way in, a wide, beautiful gate on the east side, woven in blue, purple, and scarlet. God provides the entrance, and there is only one. The chapter closes back inside the tent, where Israel is to bring pure oil of pressed olives “to cause the lamp to burn continually” (27:20), and Aaron and his sons keep it burning through the night before the LORD. Read in Christ, the single gate pictures Jesus, the way to the Father (John 14:6), and the bronze altar points to His once-for-all sacrifice (Hebrews 10:10–14).

Chapter Sections

Exodus 27:1–8 — The Bronze Altar

Israel is to make a square altar of acacia wood covered with bronze, with horns at its corners, a bronze grate, and bronze tools. It is hollow, carried on poles, and made as God showed Moses on the mountain.

Exodus 27:9–19 — The Courtyard and Its Gate

A courtyard one hundred cubits long and fifty wide is enclosed by linen hangings on pillars with bronze bases and silver hooks. On the east side a woven screen twenty cubits wide forms the only gate, and all the pegs and tools for the service are bronze.

Exodus 27:20–21 — Pure Oil for the Lamp That Burns Before the LORD

Israel is to bring pure oil from pressed olives so that the lamp burns continually. Aaron and his sons are to tend it from evening until morning before the LORD as a lasting statute.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 27 shows that the way to God’s presence ran through sacrifice. Every Israelite who came to worship passed the bronze altar first. Hebrews teaches that Christ “offered one sacrifice for sins forever” (Hebrews 10:12), and on the strength of His offering believers come to God and offer themselves to Him in response (Romans 12:1).

The chapter also shows that God provides the way in. The courtyard had one gate, and God designed it. Jesus said, “I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through Me” (John 14:6). The lamp, fed by oil the people supplied and tended by the priests, was to burn continually before the LORD. Jesus is the light of the world, and His people shine as they depend on the Holy Spirit.

Key Themes to Watch For

The altar of sacrifice. The bronze altar stands in the court as the place of burnt offering (27:1–8).

The courtyard and its single gate. God encloses His dwelling and provides one entrance (27:9–18).

Light that never goes out. Pure oil keeps the lamp burning continually before the LORD (27:20–21).

Following the pattern. The altar is made as God showed Moses on the mountain (27:8).

Key Words & Concepts

Bronze altar (27:1–2). The altar of burnt offering in the courtyard, made of acacia wood overlaid with bronze. It is distinct from the bronze washbasin and the golden incense altar of chapter 30.

Horns (27:2). Projections on the four corners of the altar, made of one piece with it.

The court (27:9). The fenced courtyard around the tabernacle, enclosed by linen hangings.

Pure oil of pressed olives (27:20). The finest olive oil, brought by the people for the lamp in the holy place.

Tabernacle of meeting (27:21). The NKJV name for the tent where God would meet with His people.
