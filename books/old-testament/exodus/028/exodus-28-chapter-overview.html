Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 28
Main Idea:

The LORD appoints Aaron and his sons as priests and gives them holy garments “for glory and for beauty,” so that Aaron carries the names of Israel’s tribes on his shoulders and over his heart before the LORD and bears the plate engraved HOLINESS TO THE LORD.

Setting:

Exodus 28 continues the instructions given on Sinai (chapters 25–31). After the tabernacle and its court, God turns to the priests who will serve there.

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

God now appoints the people who will serve in His dwelling. Aaron and his sons are set apart as priests, and they are given holy garments “for glory and for beauty” (28:2), made by artisans whom God has filled with wisdom. Each garment has a purpose, and the most moving purposes are personal.

On the shoulders of the ephod sit two onyx stones engraved with the names of Israel’s sons: “So Aaron shall bear their names before the LORD on his two shoulders as a memorial” (28:12). Over his heart hangs the breastplate with twelve precious stones, each bearing a tribe’s name, so that Aaron carries them “over his heart” whenever he goes before the LORD (28:29). The people are carried on his strength and held close in his love. The breastplate also holds the Urim and Thummim, by which Aaron bears Israel’s need for God’s judgment. His blue robe rings with golden bells as he goes in and out, and on his forehead a gold plate declares HOLINESS TO THE LORD, so that the people’s offerings may be accepted before God.

All of this looks ahead to Jesus, our great High Priest, who bears His people before the Father and always lives to intercede for them (Hebrews 4:14–16; 7:25).

Chapter Sections

Exodus 28:1–5 — Aaron and His Sons Are Set Apart, and Holy Garments Are Commanded

The LORD tells Moses to set apart Aaron and his sons as priests and to make holy garments for Aaron for glory and beauty. Skilled artisans whom God has filled with wisdom are to make the breastplate, ephod, robe, tunic, turban, and sash.

Exodus 28:6–14 — The Ephod and the Names on Aaron’s Shoulders

The ephod is woven of gold, blue, purple, scarlet, and fine linen, with two onyx stones on its shoulder straps, each engraved with six names of Israel’s sons. Aaron carries their names on his shoulders before the LORD as a memorial.

Exodus 28:15–30 — The Breastplate of Judgment over Aaron’s Heart

The breastplate holds twelve precious stones in four rows, each engraved with a tribe’s name, and is fastened to the ephod with gold chains, rings, and a blue cord. Aaron carries Israel’s names over his heart in the holy place, along with the Urim and Thummim for bearing Israel’s judgment before the LORD.

Exodus 28:31–35 — The Blue Robe with Bells and Pomegranates

The robe of the ephod is all blue, with a strong opening for the head and a hem of pomegranates and golden bells. The bells sound whenever Aaron goes into the holy place and comes out, so that he will not die.

Exodus 28:36–38 — The Gold Plate Engraved HOLINESS TO THE LORD

A plate of pure gold engraved HOLINESS TO THE LORD is fastened to the front of Aaron’s turban. Aaron bears the guilt connected with Israel’s holy gifts so that the people may be accepted before the LORD.

Exodus 28:39–43 — Garments for the Priests’ Ministry

Aaron receives his tunic, turban, and sash, and his sons receive tunics, sashes, and caps for glory and beauty, to be anointed and set apart as priests. Linen undergarments are made so that they will not incur guilt and die when they serve.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 28 shows that God provides someone to stand before Him on His people’s behalf. Aaron bore the names of Israel’s sons on his shoulders and over his heart when he went into the holy place, as a memorial before the LORD (28:12, 29). Jesus is the High Priest of whom Aaron was only a shadow. He carries His people in His strength and holds them on His heart, and He always lives to intercede for them (Hebrews 7:25).

The chapter also gives a pattern for how believers serve one another in Christ. Assured that Jesus bears us, we can bear others before the Father in prayer, help carry their burdens, and keep them close to our hearts (Galatians 6:2). Both the shoulders and the heart matter, strength that carries and love that keeps.

Key Themes to Watch For

A priesthood appointed by God. God chooses Aaron and his sons to minister to Him (28:1, 41).

Glory and beauty. The holy garments honor the priestly calling (28:2, 40).

Names on shoulders and heart. Aaron carries Israel before the LORD in strength and love (28:12, 29).

Bearing Israel’s judgment. The Urim and Thummim and the gold plate show Aaron representing the people before God (28:30, 38).

Holiness to the LORD. The priest is set apart to God, and approaching Him requires obedience (28:35–38, 43).

Key Words & Concepts

Ephod (28:6). The high priest’s richly woven outer garment, with shoulder straps holding two onyx stones.

Breastplate of judgment (28:15). A square pouch worn over the high priest’s chest, set with twelve stones bearing the tribes’ names.

Urim and Thummim (28:30). Sacred lots kept in the breastplate for seeking God’s judgment (Numbers 27:21). Scripture does not describe their shape or exactly how they were used.

HOLINESS TO THE LORD (28:36). The words engraved on the gold plate on Aaron’s turban, declaring that he belonged to God.

Memorial (28:12, 29). Something that keeps a person or event before God. Aaron carries Israel’s names as a memorial before the LORD.
