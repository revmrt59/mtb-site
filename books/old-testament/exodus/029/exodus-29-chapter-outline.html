Exodus 29 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Exodus 29 sets out the seven-day ceremony by which Aaron and his sons are washed, clothed, anointed, and consecrated as priests, and the altar is made most holy. The chapter closes with the continual morning and evening offering at the door of the tabernacle of meeting, where the LORD will meet His people, and with the LORD’s own statement of why He brought Israel out of Egypt: “that I may dwell among them” (Exodus 29:46).

Chapter Sections

1. Exodus 29:1–9 — Aaron and His Sons Are Washed, Clothed, and Anointed

Moses is to bring a young bull, two rams, and unleavened bread, then wash Aaron and his sons at the door of the tabernacle of meeting and clothe them in their holy garments. He is to pour the anointing oil on Aaron’s head, and “The priesthood shall be theirs for a perpetual statute” (Exodus 29:9).

2. Exodus 29:10–18 — The Sin Offering and the Burnt Offering

Aaron and his sons lay their hands on the bull, whose blood is put on the horns of the altar and whose flesh is burned outside the camp as a sin offering. They lay their hands on the first ram, which is burned whole on the altar as a burnt offering, “a sweet aroma, an offering made by fire to the LORD” (Exodus 29:18).

3. Exodus 29:19–28 — The Ram of Consecration and the Priests’ Portion

The blood of the second ram is put on the right ear, thumb, and big toe of Aaron and his sons, and blood and anointing oil are sprinkled on them and their garments so that they are hallowed. Portions of the ram and the bread are waved before the LORD and burned, and the breast and thigh become the priests’ portion from Israel’s peace offerings by a statute forever.

4. Exodus 29:29–37 — The Consecration Meal and Seven Days of Atonement for the Altar

Aaron’s holy garments are to pass to his sons, and Aaron and his sons eat the ram and bread “with which the atonement was made” (Exodus 29:33), which no outsider may eat. For seven days they are consecrated, and a bull is offered each day to atone for the altar until “the altar shall be most holy” (Exodus 29:37).

5. Exodus 29:38–46 — The Continual Offering and the LORD’s Dwelling Among Israel

Two lambs are offered every day, one in the morning and one at twilight, as a continual burnt offering at the door of the tabernacle of meeting, where the LORD will meet with Israel and sanctify the tabernacle by His glory. He declares, “I will dwell among the children of Israel and will be their God” (Exodus 29:45), for He brought them out of Egypt “that I may dwell among them” (Exodus 29:46).
