Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 29
Main Idea:

Through washing, clothing, anointing, sacrifice, and a sacred meal, Aaron and his sons are consecrated as priests and the altar is made most holy, and the LORD declares the purpose behind it all: He brought Israel out of Egypt so that He might dwell among them.

Setting:

Exodus 29 follows the description of the priestly garments in chapter 28. Moses receives the instructions on Sinai, and they are carried out later, in Leviticus 8–9.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 29:1–9 — Aaron and His Sons Are Washed, Clothed, and Anointed

  • Exodus 29:10–18 — The Sin Offering and the Burnt Offering

  • Exodus 29:19–28 — The Ram of Consecration and the Priests’ Portion

  • Exodus 29:29–37 — The Consecration Meal and Seven Days of Atonement for the Altar

  • Exodus 29:38–46 — The Continual Offering and the LORD’s Dwelling Among Israel

Key Verse:

“And they shall know that I am the LORD their God, who brought them up out of the land of Egypt, that I may dwell among them. I am the LORD their God.” (Exodus 29:46)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Priests who will stand between a holy God and His people must themselves be made fit to serve. The ceremony moves step by step. Aaron and his sons are washed at the door of the tabernacle, clothed in their holy garments, and Aaron is anointed with oil. Then come the sacrifices. They lay their hands on a bull as a sin offering, because the priests themselves need atonement before they can serve. A ram is burned whole as an offering of complete dedication. With the second ram, blood is put on the priests’ right ear, right thumb, and right big toe, setting apart what they hear, what they do, and where they walk. For seven days the consecration continues, and the altar itself is made most holy.

The chapter ends by lifting its eyes from details to purpose. Every morning and every evening a lamb is offered at the door of the tent, “where I will meet you to speak with you” (29:42). Then God says why He did everything: “I will dwell among the children of Israel and will be their God” (29:45). He brought them out of Egypt “that I may dwell among them” (29:46). The exodus, the covenant, the tabernacle, and the priesthood all serve that one goal.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 29:1–9 — Aaron and His Sons Are Washed, Clothed, and Anointed

Moses is to bring a young bull, two rams, and unleavened bread, then wash Aaron and his sons at the door of the tabernacle and dress them in their holy garments. He anoints Aaron with oil, and the priesthood becomes theirs by a lasting statute.

Exodus 29:10–18 — The Sin Offering and the Burnt Offering

Aaron and his sons lay their hands on a bull, whose blood is put on the altar’s horns and whose body is burned outside the camp as a sin offering. They lay their hands on the first ram, which is burned whole on the altar as a pleasing burnt offering to the LORD.

Exodus 29:19–28 — The Ram of Consecration and the Priests’ Portion

Blood from the second ram is put on the right ear, thumb, and big toe of Aaron and his sons, and blood and anointing oil are sprinkled on them and their garments to set them apart. Portions of the ram and bread are waved before the LORD and burned, and the breast and thigh become the priests’ lasting share of Israel’s peace offerings.

Exodus 29:29–37 — The Consecration Meal and Seven Days of Atonement for the Altar

Aaron’s garments are to pass to his sons, and the priests eat the ram and bread by which their atonement was made, food no outsider may eat. The consecration lasts seven days, with a bull offered daily to atone for the altar until it becomes most holy.

Exodus 29:38–46 — The Continual Offering and the LORD’s Dwelling Among Israel

Two lambs are offered every day, one in the morning and one at twilight, at the door of the tabernacle, where the LORD will meet with Israel and make the place holy by His glory. He declares that He will dwell among them and be their God, for this is why He brought them out of Egypt.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 29 states the goal of the whole book in God’s own words: He redeemed Israel so that He could live among them. That purpose runs from the garden to the Word who “dwelt among us” (John 1:14) and on to the new creation, where God dwells with His people forever (Revelation 21:3).

The chapter also shows how much it took for sinners to serve a holy God. Aaron needed a sin offering for himself, and the sacrifices had to be repeated morning and evening. Hebrews points to the difference in Jesus, a High Priest who needed no offering for His own sins and offered Himself once for all (Hebrews 7:26–28). In union with Him, His people are set apart to offer themselves to God.

Key Themes to Watch For

Consecration for service. Washing, clothing, anointing, and sacrifice set the priests apart (29:1–9, 19–21).

Atonement before ministry. The priests and the altar need atonement before they can serve (29:10–14, 33, 36–37).

The whole person set apart. Blood on ear, thumb, and toe dedicates what the priest hears, does, and where he walks (29:20).

Continual worship. A lamb is offered every morning and evening (29:38–42).

God dwelling among His people. The LORD brought Israel out of Egypt so that He might dwell among them (29:45–46).

Key Words & Concepts

Consecrate (29:1, 9). To set apart as holy for God’s service. The ceremony consecrates the priests and the altar.

Sin offering (29:14). A sacrifice that dealt with sin. Its body was burned outside the camp.

Ram of consecration (29:22). The second ram, whose blood was applied to the priests and whose meat they ate as part of their setting apart.

Wave offering (29:24). A portion lifted and moved before the LORD to present it to Him.

Continual burnt offering (29:42). The lamb offered every morning and evening at the door of the tabernacle.
