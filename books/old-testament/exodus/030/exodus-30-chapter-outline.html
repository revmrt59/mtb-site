Exodus 30 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Exodus 30 completes the sanctuary’s furnishings and supplies with five separate instructions from the LORD: the golden altar of incense, the ransom money paid at a census, the bronze laver for the priests’ washing, the holy anointing oil, and the holy incense. Each provision has its own purpose in keeping Israel’s approach to the holy God ordered and set apart.

Chapter Sections

1. Exodus 30:1–10 — The Altar of Incense Before the Veil

A small square altar of acacia wood overlaid with gold is placed before the veil, near the mercy seat “where I will meet with you” (Exodus 30:6). Aaron burns incense on it every morning and evening, no other offering is made on it, and once a year he makes atonement on its horns with blood.

2. Exodus 30:11–16 — The Ransom Money at the Census

When Israel is numbered, every man twenty years old and above gives half a shekel as a ransom to the LORD, the rich no more and the poor no less, so that no plague will come among them. The atonement money is used for the service of the tabernacle of meeting as a memorial before the LORD.

3. Exodus 30:17–21 — The Bronze Laver for the Priests’ Washing

A bronze laver with a bronze base stands between the tabernacle of meeting and the altar. Aaron and his sons must wash their hands and feet in it before they enter the tent or serve at the altar, “lest they die” (Exodus 30:20).

4. Exodus 30:22–33 — The Holy Anointing Oil

Myrrh, cinnamon, fragrant cane, cassia, and olive oil are blended into a holy anointing oil, which is used to consecrate the tabernacle, its furnishings, and Aaron and his sons as priests. It must never be copied or used on ordinary people, for “It is holy, and it shall be holy to you” (Exodus 30:32).

5. Exodus 30:34–38 — The Holy Incense

Stacte, onycha, galbanum, and pure frankincense are blended into an incense that is “salted, pure, and holy” (Exodus 30:35), and some is placed before the Testimony. No one may make it for personal use.
