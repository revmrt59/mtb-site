Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 30
Main Idea:

The LORD completes the sanctuary’s furnishings with the golden altar of incense, the equal ransom paid at a census, the bronze laver for the priests’ washing, and the holy anointing oil and incense, each set apart so that Israel may approach the holy God in the way He appoints.

Setting:

Exodus 30 continues the tabernacle instructions given on Sinai (chapters 25–31), following the consecration of the priests in chapter 29.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 30:1–10 — The Altar of Incense Before the Veil

“And when Aaron lights the lamps at twilight, he shall burn incense on it, a perpetual incense before the LORD throughout your generations.” (Exodus 30:8)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Five instructions fill out how Israel will approach God. A small golden altar stands just before the veil, near the mercy seat, “where I will meet with you” (30:6). Every morning and every evening Aaron burns incense on it, “a perpetual incense before the LORD” (30:8). Nothing else may be offered there, and once a year even this altar is cleansed with blood. Scripture later pictures the prayers of God’s people rising like incense (Psalm 141:2; Revelation 8:3–4), and Jesus always lives to intercede for us (Hebrews 7:25).

When Israel is counted, every man pays the same half shekel as a ransom: “The rich shall not give more and the poor shall not give less” (30:15). Every life counts the same before God. A bronze laver stands between the altar and the tent, and the priests must wash their hands and feet before they serve, “lest they die” (30:20). Finally God gives recipes for a holy anointing oil, which consecrates the tabernacle, its furnishings, and the priests, and for a holy incense. Both belong to God alone and must never be copied for ordinary use. What God sets apart must stay holy.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 30:1–10 — The Altar of Incense Before the Veil

A small square altar of acacia wood covered with gold is placed before the veil, near the mercy seat where God meets with Moses. Aaron burns incense on it every morning and evening, nothing else may be offered on it, and once a year he makes atonement on its horns with blood.

Exodus 30:11–16 — The Ransom Money at the Census

Whenever Israel is counted, every man twenty years and older gives half a shekel as a ransom to the LORD, the rich no more and the poor no less, so that no plague comes on them. The money supports the service of the tabernacle as a reminder before the LORD.

Exodus 30:17–21 — The Bronze Laver for the Priests’ Washing

A bronze laver on a bronze stand is placed between the tent and the altar. Aaron and his sons must wash their hands and feet in it before entering the tent or serving at the altar, so that they will not die.

Exodus 30:22–33 — The Holy Anointing Oil

Myrrh, cinnamon, fragrant cane, cassia, and olive oil are blended into a holy anointing oil, used to consecrate the tabernacle, its furnishings, and the priests. It must never be copied or poured on ordinary people, because it is holy.

Exodus 30:34–38 — The Holy Incense

Stacte, onycha, galbanum, and pure frankincense are blended into a salted, pure, and holy incense, and some is placed before the Testimony. No one may make it for personal use.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 30 shows that drawing near to God involves prayer, cleansing, and being set apart by Him. The incense altar stood closest to the mercy seat, a fitting picture of prayer rising before God, and of Christ who intercedes for His people. The laver pictures the cleansing Christ gives His people “with the washing of water by the word” (Ephesians 5:26), so that they can serve Him freely.

As the LORD directed, the anointing oil consecrated the tabernacle, its furnishings, and the priests for His service, and it was never to be used for ordinary purposes (30:26–33). Jesus is the Spirit-anointed One (Luke 4:18), and God anoints and equips His people by the same Spirit (2 Corinthians 1:21–22). The equal ransom at the census reminded Israel that no one could buy a better standing with God. Rich and poor alike owed the same price for their lives.

Key Themes to Watch For

Incense and prayer. Incense rises morning and evening before the veil, near God’s meeting place (30:1–8).

Atonement in the holy place. Even the incense altar is cleansed once a year with blood (30:10).

Equal worth before God. Rich and poor pay the same ransom at the census (30:12–15).

Cleansing for service. The priests wash hands and feet before they serve (30:17–21).

What is holy stays holy. The anointing oil and incense are reserved for God alone (30:31–38).

Key Words & Concepts

Altar of incense (30:1). A small gold-covered altar in the holy place before the veil, distinct from the bronze altar of burnt offering in the courtyard.

Ransom (30:12). A payment for a life. Each man counted in a census paid half a shekel to the LORD.

Shekel of the sanctuary (30:13). The standard weight of silver used for offerings.

Laver (30:18). A bronze basin of water for the priests’ washing, placed between the tent and the altar.

Anointing oil (30:25). A fragrant oil made by a set recipe and used only to consecrate the tabernacle, its furnishings, and the priests.
