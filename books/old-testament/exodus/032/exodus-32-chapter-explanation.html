NKJV

Exodus 32:1 Now when the people saw that Moses delayed coming down from the mountain, the people gathered together to Aaron, and said to him, "Come, make us gods that shall go before us; for as for this Moses, the man who brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we do not know what has become of him."(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 32:1 When the people saw how long it was taking Moses to come back down the mountain, they gathered around Aaron. “Come on,” they said, “make us some gods who can lead us. We don’t know what happened to this fellow Moses, who brought us here from the land of Egypt.” (NLT)

Main Idea

When the people see that Moses delays coming down, they gather to Aaron and demand, “Come, make us gods that shall go before us,” since they do not know what has become of Moses. While Moses is still on the mountain, the people turn from waiting on God to demanding a visible god.

Verse Explanation

When the people see that Moses delays coming down, they gather to Aaron and demand, “Come, make us gods that shall go before us,” since they do not know what has become of Moses. While Moses is still on the mountain, the people turn from waiting on God to demanding a visible god.

“Now when the people saw that Moses delayed coming down from the mountain,”

Moses has been on the mountain forty days (24:18). To the people, he seems to be delaying.

“the people gathered together to Aaron, and said to him, ‘Come, make us gods that shall go before us;”

They crowd around “Aaron” with a demand: “make us gods that shall go before us.” The LORD had been going before them in the pillar (13:21). Now they want a god they can see and control.

“for as for this Moses, the man who brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we do not know what has become of him.”

They speak of “this Moses” with contempt. They credit him, not God, with bringing them out, and they give up on him. While Moses is still on the mountain, the people turn from waiting on God to demanding a visible god.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word translated “gods” (elohim) can also mean God. The people may have meant a god or an image to represent God. The following verses suggest they intended to worship the LORD through the image (32:4–5).

Exodus 32:4
Exodus 32:4
NKJV

Exodus 32:4 And he received the gold from their hand, and he fashioned it with an engraving tool, and made a molded calf. Then they said, "This is your god, O Israel, that brought you out of the land of Egypt!"(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 32:4 Then Aaron took the gold, melted it down, and molded it into the shape of a calf. When the people saw it, they exclaimed, “O Israel, these are the gods who brought you out of the land of Egypt!” (NLT)

Main Idea

Aaron fashions the gold into a molded calf, and the people say, “This is your god, O Israel, that brought you out of the land of Egypt!” They credit an idol with the deliverance the LORD Himself accomplished.

Verse Explanation

Aaron fashions the gold into a molded calf, and the people say, “This is your god, O Israel, that brought you out of the land of Egypt!” They credit an idol with the deliverance the LORD Himself accomplished.

“And he received the gold from their hand, and he fashioned it with an engraving tool, and made a molded calf.”

The narrator is clear about who made it. Aaron “received the gold,” “fashioned it with an engraving tool,” and “made a molded calf.” A calf or young bull was a common symbol of strength and fertility in the ancient Near East.

“Then they said, ‘This is your god, O Israel, that brought you out of the land of Egypt!”

The people credit the calf with their deliverance. They give an idol the credit for what the LORD Himself accomplished. Only weeks earlier God had said, “I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt” (20:2).

Study Notes

Jeroboam later set up golden calves at Bethel and Dan with nearly the same words (1 Kings 12:28). Psalm 106:19–20 says Israel exchanged their glory for “the image of an ox that eats grass.”
