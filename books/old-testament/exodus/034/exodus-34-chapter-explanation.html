Exodus 34:6
Exodus 34:6
NKJV

Exodus 34:6 And the LORD passed before him and proclaimed, "The LORD, the LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abounding in goodness and truth,(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 34:6 The LORD passed in front of Moses, calling out, “Yahweh! The LORD!The God of compassion and mercy!I am slow to angerand filled with unfailing love and faithfulness. (NLT)

Main Idea

The LORD passes before him and proclaims, “The LORD, the LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abounding in goodness and truth.” God reveals His character, beginning with His mercy and faithfulness.

Verse Explanation

The LORD passes before him and proclaims, “The LORD, the LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abounding in goodness and truth.” God reveals His character, beginning with His mercy and faithfulness.

“And the LORD passed before him and proclaimed,”

As God passes by, He proclaims His name and character. This is the glory Moses asked to see (33:18).

“The LORD, the LORD God,”

God repeats His covenant name twice.

“merciful and gracious, longsuffering,”

He is “merciful,” tender like a mother’s love. He is “gracious,” giving undeserved favor. He is “longsuffering,” slow to anger, patient with sinners.

“and abounding in goodness and truth,”

The Hebrew words are chesed and emet, steadfast covenant love and faithfulness. God reveals His character, beginning with His mercy and faithfulness, right after Israel’s great sin.

Study Notes

This self-description is echoed again and again in Scripture (Numbers 14:18; Nehemiah 9:17; Psalm 86:15; 103:8; Joel 2:13; Jonah 4:2).

Exodus 34:7
Exodus 34:7
NKJV

Exodus 34:7 keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin, by no means clearing the guilty, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children and the children's children to the third and the fourth generation."(NKJV)

NLT

Exodus 34:7 I lavish unfailing love to a thousand generations.I forgive iniquity, rebellion, and sin.But I do not excuse the guilty.I lay the sins of the parents upon their children and grandchildren;the entire family is affected —even children in the third and fourth generations.” (NLT)

Main Idea

God keeps mercy for thousands and forgives iniquity, transgression, and sin, yet is “by no means clearing the guilty,” visiting iniquity to the third and fourth generation. God’s mercy and justice stand together in His own description of Himself.

Verse Explanation

God keeps mercy for thousands and forgives iniquity, transgression, and sin, yet is “by no means clearing the guilty,” visiting iniquity to the third and fourth generation. God’s mercy and justice stand together in His own description of Himself.

“keeping mercy for thousands,”

God keeps “mercy for thousands,” reaching thousands of people or generations.

“forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin,”

He forgives every kind of sin: “iniquity,” crookedness; “transgression,” rebellion; and “sin,” missing the mark.

“by no means clearing the guilty,”

Yet mercy is not the whole picture. God does not pretend sin is not sin. His mercy does not cancel His justice. Romans 3:25–26 shows how both meet at the cross, where God is just and the justifier of the one who believes in Jesus.

“visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children and the children’s children to the third and the fourth generation.”

The consequences of sin can reach “the third and the fourth generation.” Children often see, absorb, and practice their parents’ sins and values. The consequences continue where children continue in those ways (compare 20:5, “those who hate Me”). Each person still answers for his own sin (Ezekiel 18:20). God’s mercy and justice stand together in His own description of Himself.

Verse Explanation

Whenever the people see Moses’ shining face, he puts the veil back on until he goes in to speak with God again. The pattern continues, and 2 Corinthians 3:7–18 later develops the meaning of the veil.

“And whenever the children of Israel saw the face of Moses, that the skin of Moses’ face shone,”

The people saw that his face shone each time.

“then Moses would put the veil on his face again, until he went in to speak with Him.”

Moses would cover his face again until his next meeting with God. Exodus describes the pattern but does not explain all of Moses’ reasons for the veil. Exodus reports the shining and the veil. Paul later uses them in 2 Corinthians 3:7–18 to describe new covenant life in Christ. He says Moses veiled his face so that Israel could not look steadily at the end of the fading glory (2 Corinthians 3:13). He says a veil still lies on the heart when Moses is read, but “when one turns to the Lord, the veil is taken away” (2 Corinthians 3:16). Then believers, “with unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from glory to glory, just as by the Spirit of the Lord” (2 Corinthians 3:18).
