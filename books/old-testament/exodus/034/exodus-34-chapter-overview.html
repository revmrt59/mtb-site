Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 34
Main Idea:

The LORD answers Moses’ plea by proclaiming His name, merciful and gracious yet by no means clearing the guilty, and He renews the broken covenant with its call to undivided worship, while Moses comes down with his face shining from speaking with Him.

Setting:

Exodus 34 follows Moses’ request to see God’s glory and God’s promise to proclaim His name (33:18–23). The first tablets lie broken at the foot of the mountain (32:19).

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Israel had broken the covenant, and God offers to write it again. Moses cuts two new tablets and climbs Sinai alone. Then the LORD keeps His promise from chapter 33. He descends in the cloud, passes before Moses, and proclaims His own name: “The LORD, the LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abounding in goodness and truth” (34:6). He keeps mercy for thousands and forgives iniquity, transgression, and sin, yet He will “by no means” clear the guilty (34:7). Mercy and justice stand together in God’s own words about Himself, and this is the glory Moses had asked to see: the goodness of God revealed in His name. Moses bows to the ground and asks the LORD to go among His stiff-necked people, pardon them, and take them as His own.

God answers, “Behold, I make a covenant” (34:10). He renews His commands, above all the call to worship no other god, “for the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God” (34:14). His jealousy is His faithful love claiming His people for Himself. After another forty days the words are written again. Moses comes down not knowing that his face is shining from speaking with the LORD, and he veils it after he has given the people God’s words.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 34:1–4 — Moses Cuts New Tablets and Goes Up Sinai

The LORD tells Moses to cut two stone tablets like the first ones so that He can write on them again. Moses rises early and climbs Mount Sinai alone, carrying the tablets.

Exodus 34:5–9 — The LORD Proclaims His Name

The LORD comes down in the cloud and proclaims His name: merciful and gracious, patient, abounding in goodness and truth, forgiving sin yet not clearing the guilty. Moses quickly bows and worships and asks the Lord to go among His stubborn people, pardon them, and take them as His inheritance.

Exodus 34:10–17 — The LORD Renews the Covenant and Forbids Rival Worship

The LORD renews His covenant and promises wonders before all the people. Israel must make no covenant with the land’s inhabitants, must tear down their altars and images, and must make no molded gods, because the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God.

Exodus 34:18–26 — The Feasts, the Firstborn, and the Sabbath Renewed

The LORD renews the Feast of Unleavened Bread, the redemption of the firstborn, weekly rest even in plowing and harvest, and the Feasts of Weeks and Ingathering. All Israel’s men are to appear before Him three times a year, bringing the first of their harvest to His house.

Exodus 34:27–28 — The Words of the Covenant Are Written Again

The LORD tells Moses to write down these words, because by them He has made a covenant with Moses and Israel. Moses stays with the LORD forty days and nights without food or water, and the words of the covenant, the Ten Commandments, are written on the tablets.

Exodus 34:29–35 — Moses’ Face Shines and He Veils It

Moses comes down with the tablets, not knowing that his face shines from talking with the LORD, and the people are afraid to come near. He gives them all the LORD’s commands and then veils his face, removing the veil whenever he goes in to speak with the LORD.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 34:6–7 became Israel’s confession of who God is, repeated in psalms and prophets (Psalm 103:8; Jonah 4:2). After Israel broke the covenant, the LORD answered Moses’ request by proclaiming His name and His goodness, and the God who forgives is also the God who does not pretend sin is nothing. Each person answers for his own guilt (Ezekiel 18:20), yet the consequences of sin can pass down through generations where children continue in their parents’ ways, while His mercy reaches to thousands. The cross shows how God can be both just and the One who justifies (Romans 3:25–26).

Moses’ shining face showed what time in God’s presence does. Paul takes up the veil to describe believers who behold the glory of the Lord with unveiled faces and “are being transformed into the same image” (2 Corinthians 3:18).

Key Themes to Watch For

The name of the LORD. God proclaims His character: merciful, gracious, patient, faithful, forgiving, and just (34:5–7).

Covenant renewed after failure. God writes the broken covenant again (34:1, 10, 27–28).

Undivided worship. The jealous God forbids rival worship and molded gods (34:12–17).

Appointed times restored. The feasts, the firstborn, and the Sabbath are renewed (34:18–26).

Glory reflected. Moses’ face shines after speaking with the LORD (34:29–35).

Key Words & Concepts

Longsuffering (34:6). Patient and slow to anger, bearing long with His people.

Goodness and truth (34:6). God’s steadfast, faithful love and His reliability. He keeps His word.

By no means clearing the guilty (34:7). God does not pretend sin is innocent. His forgiveness never ignores justice.

Jealous (34:14). A name for God that expresses His rightful, loving claim on His people’s whole devotion. Because He is wholly good, He wants what is best for them, and nothing is better than Himself.

The veil (34:33). A covering Moses put over his shining face after speaking to the people. Paul develops its meaning in 2 Corinthians 3:7–18.
