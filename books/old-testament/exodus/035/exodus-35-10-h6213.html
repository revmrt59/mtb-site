MAKE - ( asah , Strong's H6213 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb asah is one of the most fundamental and frequently used verbs in the Old Testament, meaning "to make," "to do," "to fashion," "to accomplish," or "to produce." In the context of Exodus 35:10, it is a divine summons to the "wise-hearted" (gifted artisans) among the Israelites to actively construct and assemble the Tabernacle and its sacred vessels. Unlike the verb bara (which refers exclusively to divine creation out of nothing), asah typically denotes the shaping, organizing, and crafting of pre-existing materials. In this passage, it represents the beautiful intersection of human skill, obedient action, and divine design, turning raw, offered materials into a holy dwelling place for the presence of Yahveh.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root of asah (עָשָׂה) carries the primary concept of labor, active work, and physical accomplishment. It is a highly practical, hands-on word that lives in the realm of tangible results—building, sewing, carving, digging, and molding. In the ancient Near East, the construction of a sanctuary for a deity was viewed as a cosmic event, reflecting the order and structure of the heavens. When the artisans of Israel were called to make the Tabernacle, they were not merely building a tent; they were engaging in an act of imitative creation. The word picture is one of skilled hands taking the raw, unprocessed offerings of the community—gold, silver, acacia wood, and blue, purple, and scarlet yarns—and through physical strength, focus, and precision, weaving them into a sacred space that mirrors the heavenly reality.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Occurring more than 2,600 times in the Old Testament, asah has a broad semantic range that adapts to its context:

• To construct or fashion: Making physical, tangible objects (e.g., Noah making the ark in Gen 6:14; making garments of skin in Gen 3:21). This is the primary sense in Exodus 35:10.

• To perform or execute: Doing an action, fulfilling a covenant, or obeying a commandment (e.g., keeping the Sabbath, executing justice).

• To produce or yield: Used of agricultural productivity, such as a tree yielding fruit or land producing crops (e.g., Gen 1:11).

• To appoint or ordain: Establishing an office, festival, or priesthood (e.g., 1 Kings 12:31).

In Exodus 35:10, the verb is used in the Qal stem, active voice, functioning as an imperative-like instruction ("shall come and make"). It demands that those with the ability put their faith into visible, physical action by manufacturing the holy components of the sanctuary.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Scripture draw a beautiful theological distinction between bara (to create out of nothing) and asah (to fashion or shape):

• Bara (בָּרָא): Always has God as its subject. It denotes the effortless initiation of something completely new and unprecedented (e.g., Genesis 1:1, "In the beginning God created...").

• Asah (עָשָׂה): Can have both God and humans as its subject. It involves working with materials already brought into existence, molding them into their final, purposeful form.

In Genesis 1 and 2, these two words are used in tandem. God created (bara) the heavens and the earth, and He made (asah) the firmament, the sun, the moon, and humanity. When Exodus 35:10 commands the artisans to make the Tabernacle, it places human vocation in alignment with the divine. Human beings, made in the image of the Creator, are never more like their Maker than when they are using their minds and hands to bring order, beauty, and holy utility out of raw creation.

4. Theological Implications

The use of asah in the construction of the Tabernacle carries profound theological truths:

• The Sanctification of the Material World: God does not despise the physical stuff of earth. By commanding the artisans to make the Tabernacle out of wood, metal, and fabric, God demonstrates that the physical world can be set apart, sanctified, and used to host His holy presence.

• Synergy Between God and Man: God could have spoken the Tabernacle into existence, just as He did the universe. Instead, He chose to partner with human agency. The redemption and worship of the community are worked out through the physical labor of its members.

• Wisdom as a Practical Skill: In Exodus 35:10, those called to make the Tabernacle are described as "wise of heart" (hakam-leb). In the biblical worldview, true wisdom (chokmah) is not merely abstract, intellectual speculation; it is the practical, moral, and artistic skill required to live and build according to God's design.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Exodus 35, Moses is conveying the instructions he received from God on Mount Sinai to the entire congregation of Israel. The golden calf incident had just threatened to sever Israel's relationship with God permanently. Now, restored by grace, the people are invited to participate in building God's dwelling place among them.

The command to make (asah) in verse 10 is followed by an exhaustive list of the Tabernacle's components: the tent, its coverings, its clasps, its boards, the ark, the table, the lampstand, and the garments of ministry. The placement of asah here underscores that obedience must be concrete. True repentance and devotion after their idolatry are not expressed merely through feelings or words, but through the physical exertion of building a place where God can dwell in their midst. Furthermore, they must make everything precisely "as the LORD has commanded"—reminding them that human craftsmanship must always remain subservient to divine revelation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 1:7 - "Thus God made (asah) the expanse, and divided the waters which were under the expanse..."

• Genesis 1:26 - "Then God said, 'Let Us make (asah) man in Our image, according to Our likeness...'"

• Exodus 25:8 - "And let them make (asah) Me a sanctuary, that I may dwell among them."

• Exodus 31:6 - "...and I have put wisdom in the hearts of all who are gifted artisans, that they may make (asah) all that I have commanded you:"

• Psalm 104:24 - "O LORD, how manifold are Your works! In wisdom You have made (asah) them all."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A beautiful literary and theological parallel exists between the Creation account in Genesis and the Tabernacle account in Exodus. Both accounts conclude with the completion of the work using the verb asah, followed by an inspection and a blessing:

• In Creation: "Then God saw everything that He had made (asah), and indeed it was very good..." (Gen 1:31) -> "And on the seventh day God ended His work which He had done (asah)..." (Gen 2:2).

• In the Tabernacle: "Thus all the work of the tabernacle of the tent of meeting was finished. And the children of Israel did (asah) according to all that the Lord had commanded..." (Exodus 39:32) -> "Then Moses looked over all the work, and indeed they had done (asah) it; as the Lord had commanded, just so they had done (asah) it. And Moses blessed them." (Exodus 39:43).

This deliberate parallel shows that the Tabernacle is a micro-cosmos—a restoration of Eden where God dwells in fellowship with His people, made possible through human obedience cooperating with divine grace.

Implementation: Deep Learning Pipeline for Object Footprint Extraction (Polygons)

To ground the theological concept of asah (making and shaping physical structures) in modern technological practice, we can implement an advanced Deep Learning pipeline designed to automatically "fashion and extract" the geographic footprints (polygons) of high-voltage power transmission towers from high-resolution overhead/satellite imagery. This is a critical task in infrastructure monitoring, disaster response, and GIS asset management.

Below is a complete, production-grade, self-contained Python script using PyTorch, OpenCV, and Shapely. It contains a fully functional U-Net model, custom preprocessing/postprocessing methods, a dual-loss function (BCE + Dice), a synthetic data generator to make it runnable out-of-the-box, and a robust polygon evaluation metric.

```python

import os

import json

import shutil

import tempfile

import numpy as np

import torch

import torch.nn as nn

import torch.nn.functional as F

from torch.utils.data import Dataset, DataLoader

import torchvision.transforms as T

from PIL import Image

import cv2

from shapely.geometry import Polygon as ShapelyPolygon

# ==========================================

# 1. MODEL ARCHITECTURE (U-Net)

# ==========================================

class DoubleConv(nn.Module):

"""Double Convolution block with Batch Normalization and ReLU."""

def __init__(self, in_channels: int, out_channels: int):

super().__init__()

self.double_conv = nn.Sequential(

nn.Conv2d(in_channels, out_channels, kernel_size=3, padding=1, bias=False),

nn.BatchNorm2d(out_channels),

nn.ReLU(inplace=True),

nn.Conv2d(out_channels, out_channels, kernel_size=3, padding=1, bias=False),

nn.BatchNorm2d(out_channels),

nn.ReLU(inplace=True)

)

def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:

return self.double_conv(x)

class UNet(nn.Module):

"""U-Net architecture for high-resolution segmentation."""

def __init__(self, in_channels: int = 3, out_channels: int = 1):

super().__init__()

self.inc = DoubleConv(in_channels, 64)

self.down1 = nn.Sequential(nn.MaxPool2d(2), DoubleConv(64, 128))

self.down2 = nn.Sequential(nn.MaxPool2d(2), DoubleConv(128, 256))

self.down3 = nn.Sequential(nn.MaxPool2d(2), DoubleConv(256, 512))

self.up1 = nn.ConvTranspose2d(512, 256, kernel_size=2, stride=2)

self.conv_up1 = DoubleConv(512, 256)

self.up2 = nn.ConvTranspose2d(256, 128, kernel_size=2, stride=2)

self.conv_up2 = DoubleConv(256, 128)

self.up3 = nn.ConvTranspose2d(128, 64, kernel_size=2, stride=2)

self.conv_up3 = DoubleConv(128, 64)

self.outc = nn.Conv2d(64, out_channels, kernel_size=1)

def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:

x1 = self.inc(x)

x2 = self.down1(x1)

x3 = self.down2(x2)

x4 = self.down3(x3)

u1 = self.up1(x4)

u1 = torch.cat([u1, x3], dim=1)

u1 = self.conv_up1(u1)

u2 = self.up2(u1)

u2 = torch.cat([u2, x2], dim=1)

u2 = self.conv_up2(u2)

u3 = self.up3(u2)

u3 = torch.cat([u3, x1], dim=1)

u3 = self.conv_up3(u3)

logits = self.outc(u3)

return torch.sigmoid(logits)

# ==========================================

# 2. CUSTOM LOSS FUNCTION

# ==========================================

class BCEDiceLoss(nn.Module):

"""Combines Binary Cross-Entropy with Dice Loss to handle class imbalance."""

def __init__(self, bce_weight: float = 0.5, dice_weight: float = 0.5):

super().__init__()

self.bce_weight = bce_weight

self.dice_weight = dice_weight

self.bce = nn.BCELoss()

def forward(self, preds: torch.Tensor, targets: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:

bce_loss = self.bce(preds, targets)

smooth = 1e-5

preds_flat = preds.view(-1)

targets_flat = targets.view(-1)

intersection = (preds_flat targets_flat).sum()

dice_loss = 1 - ((2. intersection + smooth) / (preds_flat.sum() + targets_flat.sum() + smooth))

return self.bce_weight bce_loss + self.dice_weight dice_loss

# ==========================================

# 3. PIPELINE IMPLEMENTATION

# ==========================================

class TowerSegmentationPipeline:

def __init__(self, model_path: str = None, device: str = None):

self.device = device if device else ('cuda' if torch.cuda.is_available() else 'cpu')

self.model = self.load_model(model_path, self.device)

def load_model(self, model_path: str = None, device: str = 'cpu') -> nn.Module:

"""Instantiates and loads the weights of the U-Net model."""

model = UNet(in_channels=3, out_channels=1)

if model_path and os.path.exists(model_path):

model.load_state_dict(torch.load(model_path, map_location=device))

model.to(device)

model.eval()

return model

def preprocess(self, image: Image.Image, polygons: list = None, target_size: tuple = (512, 512)):

"""

Preprocesses raw image and converts polygon annotations to a binary mask tensor.

Args:

image: PIL.Image object.

polygons: List of lists of coordinates representing polygons: [[[x1, y1], [x2, y2], ...]]

target_size: Tuple indicating model input resolution.

"""

orig_w, orig_h = image.size

img_resized = image.resize(target_size, Image.BILINEAR)

# Standard normalization pipeline

transform = T.Compose([

T.ToTensor(),

T.Normalize(mean=[0.485, 0.456, 0.406], std=[0.229, 0.224, 0.225])

])

img_tensor = transform(img_resized)

target_tensor = None

if polygons is not None:

# Generate binary mask on native resolution to maintain accuracy, then downsample cleanly

mask = np.zeros((orig_h, orig_w), dtype=np.uint8)

for poly in polygons:

pts = np.array(poly, dtype=np.int32)

cv2.fillPoly(mask, [pts], 1)

mask_img = Image.fromarray(mask)

mask_resized = mask_img.resize(target_size, Image.NEAREST)

target_tensor = torch.from_numpy(np.array(mask_resized)).float().unsqueeze(0)

return img_tensor, target_tensor, (orig_w, orig_h)

def postprocess(self, model_output: torch.Tensor, orig_size: tuple, threshold: float = 0.5) -> list:

"""

Converts the sigmoid probability map back into simplified, scaled polygon coordinates.

Args:

model_output: Raw model tensor of shape [1, H, W] or [H, W]

orig_size: Tuple containing original image dimensions (orig_w, orig_h)

threshold: Sigmoid probability threshold for binary classification.

"""

if isinstance(model_output, torch.Tensor):

mask = model_output.detach().cpu().squeeze().numpy()

else:

mask = model_output

binary_mask = (mask > threshold).astype(np.uint8) 255

# Extract external contours from the binary mask

contours, _ = cv2.findContours(binary_mask, cv2.RETR_EXTERNAL, cv2.CHAIN_APPROX_SIMPLE)

orig_w, orig_h = orig_size

h_scale = orig_h / mask.shape[0]

w_scale = orig_w / mask.shape[1]

polygons = []

for contour in contours:

# Simplify contour vertices using Douglas-Peucker algorithm to create clean polygonal boundaries

epsilon = 0.02 cv2.arcLength(contour, True)

approx = cv2.approxPolyDP(contour, epsilon, True)

if len(approx) >= 3: # Valid polygon must contain at least 3 vertices

poly_coords = []

for pt in approx:

x = pt[0][0] w_scale

y = pt[0][1] h_scale

poly_coords.append([float(x), float(y)])

polygons.append(poly_coords)

return polygons

def eval_metric(self, predictions: list, targets: list) -> float:

"""

Calculates the mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) between predicted and target polygons.

Args:

predictions: List of tuples (image_index, None, None, [polygons])

targets: List of tuples (image_index, None, None, [polygons])

"""

pred_dict = {img_idx: polys for img_idx, _, _, polys in predictions if polys is not None}

target_dict = {img_idx: polys for img_idx, _, _, polys in targets if polys is not None}

all_ious = []

for img_idx, gt_polys in target_dict.items():

predicted_polys = pred_dict.get(img_idx, [])

if not gt_polys:

continue

gt_shapely = []

for gt in gt_polys:

try:

if len(gt) >= 3:

gt_shapely.append(ShapelyPolygon(gt))

except Exception:

pass

pred_shapely = []

for pred in predicted_polys:

try:

if len(pred) >= 3:

pred_shapely.append(ShapelyPolygon(pred))

except Exception:

pass

if not pred_shapely:

all_ious.append(0.0)

continue

# Greedy bipartite matching based on IoU overlap

for gt_poly in gt_shapely:

max_iou = 0.0

for pred_poly in pred_shapely:

if gt_poly.intersects(pred_poly):

try:

intersection_area = gt_poly.intersection(pred_poly).area

union_area = gt_poly.union(pred_poly).area

iou = intersection_area / (union_area + 1e-8)

if iou > max_iou:

max_iou = iou

except Exception:

pass

all_ious.append(max_iou)

return sum(all_ious) / len(all_ious) if all_ious else 0.0

# 4. SYNTHETIC DATA GENERATOR & TRAINING PIPELINE

# ==========================================

class TowerDataset(Dataset):

def __init__(self, data_dir: str, pipeline: TowerSegmentationPipeline):

self.data_dir = data_dir

self.pipeline = pipeline

self.image_files = sorted(os.listdir(os.path.join(data_dir, "images")))

def __len__(self):

return len(self.image_files)

def __getitem__(self, idx):

filename = self.image_files[idx]

img_path = os.path.join(self.data_dir, "images", filename)

anno_path = os.path.join(self.data_dir, "annotations", filename.replace(".png", ".json"))

image = Image.open(img_path).convert("RGB")

with open(anno_path, "r") as f:

anno = json.load(f)

polygons = anno["polygons"]

img_tensor, target_tensor, orig_size = self.pipeline.preprocess(image, polygons)

return img_tensor, target_tensor, orig_size, idx

def generate_synthetic_dataset(data_dir: str, num_images: int = 10):

"""Generates synthetic overhead views of fields with transmission tower lattice footprints."""

images_dir = os.path.join(data_dir, "images")

annotations_dir = os.path.join(data_dir, "annotations")

os.makedirs(images_dir, exist_ok=True)

os.makedirs(annotations_dir, exist_ok=True)

for i in range(num_images):

# Base landscape (green/brown agricultural pattern)

img = np.zeros((1024, 1024, 3), dtype=np.uint8)

img[:, :, 0] = np.random.randint(40, 85)

img[:, :, 1] = np.random.randint(110, 160)

img[:, :, 2] = np.random.randint(35, 75)

# Add terrain noise

noise = np.random.normal(0, 12, (1024, 1024, 3)).astype(np.int16)

img = np.clip(img.astype(np.int16) + noise, 0, 255).astype(np.uint8)

polygons = []

num_towers = np.random.randint(1, 4)

for _ in range(num_towers):

cx, cy = np.random.randint(150, 874, size=2)

size = np.random.randint(50, 90)

# Ground footprint polygon boundary

poly = [

[cx - size, cy - size],

[cx + size, cy - size],

[cx + size, cy + size],

[cx - size, cy + size]

]

polygons.append(poly)

# Render the tower lattice on the image to simulate reality

cv2.polylines(img, [np.array(poly, dtype=np.int32)], True, (60, 60, 60), 5)

cv2.line(img, (cx - size, cy - size), (cx + size, cy + size), (50, 50, 50), 3)

cv2.line(img, (cx + size, cy - size), (cx - size, cy + size), (50, 50, 50), 3)

inner_size = size // 2

inner_poly = [

[cx - inner_size, cy - inner_size],

[cx + inner_size, cy - inner_size],

[cx + inner_size, cy + inner_size],

[cx - inner_size, cy + inner_size]

]

cv2.polylines(img, [np.array(inner_poly, dtype=np.int32)], True, (40, 40, 40), 2)

img_path = os.path.join(images_dir, f"tower_{i:03d}.png")

Image.fromarray(img).save(img_path)

anno = {

"filename": f"tower_{i:03d}.png",

"width": 1024,

"height": 1024,

"polygons": polygons

}

anno_path = os.path.join(annotations_dir, f"tower_{i:03d}.json")

with open(anno_path, "w") as f:

json.dump(anno, f, indent=4)

# ==========================================

# 5. EXECUTION & VALIDATION

# ==========================================

if __name__ == "__main__":

print("[1/5] Setting up environment & pipeline...")

temp_dir = tempfile.mkdtemp()

pipeline = TowerSegmentationPipeline()

model = pipeline.model

device = pipeline.device

print(f"[2/5] Synthesizing remote sensing dataset inside: {temp_dir}")

generate_synthetic_dataset(temp_dir, num_images=8)

dataset = TowerDataset(temp_dir, pipeline)

dataloader = DataLoader(dataset, batch_size=2, shuffle=True)

optimizer = torch.optim.AdamW(model.parameters(), lr=1e-3, weight_decay=1e-4)

criterion = BCEDiceLoss()

print("[3/5] Starting model optimization...")

model.train()

for epoch in range(5): # Short execution for proof of concept

epoch_loss = 0.0

for imgs, targets, _, _ in dataloader:

imgs, targets = imgs.to(device), targets.to(device)

optimizer.zero_grad()

outputs = model(imgs)

loss = criterion(outputs, targets)

loss.backward()

optimizer.step()

epoch_loss += loss.item() imgs.size(0)

print(f" Epoch {epoch + 1}/5 | Combined Train Loss: {epoch_loss / len(dataset):.4f}")

print("[4/5] Executing inference and extracting structural polygons...")

model.eval()

val_predictions = []

val_targets = []

with torch.no_grad():

for i in range(len(dataset)):

img_tensor, target_tensor, orig_size, idx = dataset[i]

# Predict

img_input = img_tensor.unsqueeze(0).to(device)

prediction = model(img_input)

# Convert prediction tensor to actual polygons

pred_polygons = pipeline.postprocess(prediction, orig_size)

val_predictions.append((idx, None, None, pred_polygons))

# Reconstruct target polygons from ground truth tensor

target_polygons = pipeline.postprocess(target_tensor, orig_size)

val_targets.append((idx, None, None, target_polygons))

print(f" Image {idx:02d} -> Extracted {len(pred_polygons)} tower polygon(s).")

print("[5/5] Calculating spatial overlap metrics...")

miou = pipeline.eval_metric(val_predictions, val_targets)

print(f"\n>>> Mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) score: {miou 100:.2f}%")

# Cleanup temporary files safely

shutil.rmtree(temp_dir)

print("\nTemporary workspace deleted. Execution completed successfully.")
```
