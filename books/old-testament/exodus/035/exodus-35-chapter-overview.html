Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 35
Main Idea:

With the covenant renewed, Moses repeats the Sabbath command and then calls for willing offerings and skilled workers, and men and women whose hearts are stirred bring their gifts and their skill while the LORD equips Bezalel and Aholiab to do the work and to teach.

Setting:

After the golden calf and the renewed covenant (chapters 32–34), Israel begins to build what God commanded in chapters 25–31.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 35:1–3 — Moses Repeats the Sabbath Command Before the Work Begins

  • Exodus 35:4–19 — Moses Calls for Willing Offerings and Skilled Workers

  • Exodus 35:20–29 — Men and Women Bring Their Gifts with Willing Hearts

  • Exodus 35:30–35 — The LORD Fills Bezalel and Aholiab for the Work and for Teaching

Key Verse:

“The children of Israel brought a freewill offering to the LORD, all the men and women whose hearts were willing to bring material for all kinds of work which the LORD, by the hand of Moses, had commanded to be done.” (Exodus 35:29)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Before the first board is cut, Moses repeats the Sabbath. The seventh day is “a holy day for you, a Sabbath of rest to the LORD” (35:2), and not even the holy work of building God’s house may override it. The God who calls His people to work for Him first calls them to rest with Him.

Then Moses calls for gifts, and the key word is willing. “Whoever is of a willing heart, let him bring it as an offering to the LORD” (35:5). The response is overwhelming. “Everyone came whose heart was stirred, and everyone whose spirit was willing” (35:21). Men and women bring gold jewelry, fine cloth, skins, silver, bronze, and wood. Skilled women spin yarn, and the leaders bring precious stones, spices, and oil. Only a few chapters earlier these same people tore off their gold to make a calf. Now they bring their gold to the LORD for the dwelling He asked them to build.

Moses announces that God has called Bezalel by name and filled him with His Spirit for every kind of craft. God has also “put in his heart the ability to teach” (35:34), in him and in Aholiab. The work needed hands that could make and people who could help others learn.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 35:1–3 — Moses Repeats the Sabbath Command Before the Work Begins

Moses gathers the whole congregation and gives the LORD’s command to work six days and keep the seventh as a holy Sabbath of rest. Anyone who works on it is to be put to death, and no fire may be kindled in their homes that day.

Exodus 35:4–19 — Moses Calls for Willing Offerings and Skilled Workers

Moses invites everyone with a willing heart to bring an offering to the LORD and names the materials needed. He calls every skilled worker to come and make all that the LORD commanded, from the tabernacle and its furnishings to the priests’ holy garments.

Exodus 35:20–29 — Men and Women Bring Their Gifts with Willing Hearts

Everyone whose heart is stirred brings gifts for the LORD’s work: gold jewelry, fabrics, skins, silver, bronze, and wood. Skilled women spin yarn, the leaders bring precious stones, spices, and oil, and all Israel brings a freewill offering to the LORD.

Exodus 35:30–35 — The LORD Fills Bezalel and Aholiab for the Work and for Teaching

Moses announces that the LORD has called Bezalel by name and filled him with His Spirit for every kind of skilled work. The LORD has also given Bezalel and Aholiab the ability to teach and the skill to engrave, design, embroider, and weave.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 35 shows what God does with people who have failed Him. The same people who gave gold for an idol now give it gladly for God’s dwelling. Forgiveness did not leave them idle; it freed them to give and serve with willing hearts. God still builds His dwelling, now His people in Christ, from hearts He has stirred.

The chapter also honors every kind of contribution. Precious stones from the leaders, yarn spun by skilled women, and the craftsmanship of Bezalel all served the same dwelling. God gives His Spirit for practical skill and for teaching others. Rest came first, a reminder that the work belongs to God and that His people are His before they are His workers.

Key Themes to Watch For

Rest before work. The Sabbath is repeated before the building begins (35:1–3).

Willing hearts. Gifts come from everyone whose heart is stirred and whose spirit is willing (35:5, 21–22, 29).

Everyone contributes. Men and women, skilled workers, and leaders all give what they have (35:22–28).

Spirit-given skill. God fills Bezalel with His Spirit for skilled work (35:30–33).

Gifts to teach. God gives Bezalel and Aholiab the ability to teach others (35:34).

Key Words & Concepts

Willing heart (35:5). A heart freely moved to give. The tabernacle was built entirely from offerings given this way.

Freewill offering (35:29). A gift given voluntarily, not required by law.

Gifted artisans (35:10). Skilled workers whose abilities God used to build His dwelling.

The ability to teach (35:34). A gift God gave Bezalel and Aholiab so they could train others in the work.
