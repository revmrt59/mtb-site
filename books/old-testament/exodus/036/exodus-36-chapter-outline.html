Exodus 36 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Exodus 36 sees the work on the tabernacle begin: Bezalel, Aholiab, and the skilled workers receive the offerings, and the people give so generously that Moses has to stop them. The artisans then make the curtains, coverings, frame, veil, and screen of the tabernacle just as the LORD had shown Moses in Exodus 26.

Chapter Sections

1. Exodus 36:1–7 — The Work Begins and the People Must Be Told to Stop Giving

Bezalel, Aholiab, and every gifted artisan come to do the work and receive the offerings, while the people keep bringing freewill offerings every morning. When the craftsmen report, “The people bring much more than enough” (Exodus 36:5), Moses has it proclaimed that no one should bring more, for the material was sufficient for all the work, “indeed too much” (Exodus 36:7).

2. Exodus 36:8–19 — The Curtains, Tent, and Coverings Are Made

The artisans weave ten curtains of fine linen with cherubim and join them with gold clasps, “that it might be one tabernacle” (Exodus 36:13). They make eleven curtains of goats’ hair joined with bronze clasps for the tent, and coverings of ram skins dyed red and badger skins.

3. Exodus 36:20–34 — The Boards, Sockets, and Bars Are Made

Upright boards of acacia wood are made for the south, north, and west sides and the corners, set in silver sockets. Bars hold them together, with a middle bar running from end to end, and boards and bars are overlaid with gold.

4. Exodus 36:35–38 — The Veil and the Screen for the Door Are Made

The veil is woven with cherubim and hung on four pillars overlaid with gold, set in silver sockets. The screen for the tabernacle door is woven by a weaver and hung on five pillars with gold-covered capitals and bronze sockets.
