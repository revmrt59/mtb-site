Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 36
Main Idea:

The work on the tabernacle begins, the people give so generously that Moses must tell them to stop, and the skilled workers make the curtains, coverings, frame, veil, and screen exactly as the LORD had shown Moses.

Setting:

Exodus 36 continues the building that began with the call for willing gifts in chapter 35. Its construction account follows, almost word for word, the instructions of chapter 26.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 36:1–7 — The Work Begins and the People Must Be Told to Stop Giving

  • Exodus 36:8–19 — The Curtains, Tent, and Coverings Are Made

  • Exodus 36:20–34 — The Boards, Sockets, and Bars Are Made

  • Exodus 36:35–38 — The Veil and the Screen for the Door Are Made

Key Verse:

“and they spoke to Moses, saying, ‘The people bring much more than enough for the service of the work which the LORD commanded us to do.’” (Exodus 36:5)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Bezalel, Aholiab, and every skilled worker whose heart was stirred come to the work and receive the people’s offerings. Yet the people keep coming, bringing more freewill gifts “every morning” (36:3). Finally the craftsmen stop their work to tell Moses something unusual: “The people bring much more than enough” (36:5). Moses sends word through the camp that no one should bring any more, for the material is sufficient, “indeed too much” (36:7). The people who had once given gold for an idol now have to be restrained from giving to God.

Then the building begins, and the text describes it in nearly the same words God used when He gave the instructions in chapter 26. The workers weave the ten linen curtains with cherubim and join them with gold clasps, “that it might be one tabernacle” (36:13). They make the goats’ hair tent and its coverings, the gold-covered boards set in silver sockets, the bars that hold them, the veil, and the screen for the door. The repetition is the point. What God showed Moses on the mountain is now being made by willing hands, piece by piece, exactly as He commanded.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 36:1–7 — The Work Begins and the People Must Be Told to Stop Giving

Bezalel, Aholiab, and all the skilled workers begin the work and receive the offerings, while the people keep bringing more gifts every morning. When the craftsmen report that the people are bringing far more than enough, Moses has it announced that no one should bring anything more.

Exodus 36:8–19 — The Curtains, Tent, and Coverings Are Made

The workers weave ten curtains of fine linen with cherubim and join them with gold clasps into one tabernacle. They make eleven curtains of goats’ hair joined with bronze clasps for the tent, along with coverings of red-dyed ram skins and badger skins.

Exodus 36:20–34 — The Boards, Sockets, and Bars Are Made

Upright boards of acacia wood are made for the south, north, and west walls and the corners, set in silver sockets. Bars hold them together, including a middle bar running from end to end, and boards and bars are covered with gold.

Exodus 36:35–38 — The Veil and the Screen for the Door Are Made

The veil is woven with cherubim and hung on four gold-covered pillars set in silver sockets. The screen for the tent’s door is woven and hung on five pillars with gold-covered tops and bronze sockets.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 36 gives a rare picture of God’s people giving more than enough. Their generosity was not forced or calculated; it overflowed from hearts God had stirred. Moses’ decision to stop the giving also shows responsible stewardship. The gifts were for a purpose, and when the purpose was met, the collection ended.

The careful repetition of chapter 26 shows that obedience matters in the details. God gave a pattern, and the people carried it out faithfully. The dwelling God would fill with His glory was built exactly as He designed it, a reminder that God’s dwelling among His people is always on His terms and by His provision. The veil woven here guarded the way into the Most Holy Place. In Christ that way is opened, for believers now have “boldness to enter the Holiest by the blood of Jesus, by a new and living way” through the veil, that is, His flesh (Hebrews 10:19–20).

Key Themes to Watch For

Overflowing generosity. The people bring more than enough and must be told to stop (36:3–7).

Responsible stewardship. Moses ends the collection once the need is met (36:6–7).

Faithful execution of the pattern. The workers make each part as God commanded in chapter 26 (36:8–38).

One dwelling. The curtains and tent are joined so that the tabernacle is one (36:13, 18).

Key Words & Concepts

Freewill offerings (36:3). Voluntary gifts the people kept bringing every morning.

Restrained from bringing (36:6). The people had to be stopped because they had already given more than enough.

Clasps (36:13, 18). Fasteners of gold and bronze that joined the sets of curtains into one tent.

The veil (36:35). The woven curtain with cherubim that would separate the holy place from the Most Holy. Hebrews 10:19–22 sees in it the way to God that Christ has opened.
