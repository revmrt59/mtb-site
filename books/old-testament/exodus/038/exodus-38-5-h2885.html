RINGS - ( taba'ath , Strong's H2885 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word taba'ath refers to a signet, ring, or fastened band of metal, functioning in Exodus 38:5 as structural attachment points for carrying the bronze altar of burnt offering through the wilderness.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• Rooted in a verb meaning to sink, impress, or stamp, reflecting the original action of sealing or pressing a signet into clay or wax.

• Visually conveys an object that is firmly affixed, encircling, or deeply set into place, much like a permanent seal upon a document.

• In ancient Near Eastern culture, rings served not merely as ornamentation, but as symbols of authority, identity, and functional utility for transport and security.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• A ring, signet ring, or seal ring worn on the hand as a mark of authority or ownership.

• A fastened metal loop, ring, or collar used structurally for lifting, carrying, or securing structural elements of the tabernacle.

• The foundational semantic domain bridges personal insignia of power with practical architectural hardware.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Appears frequently in the tabernacle instructions and construction accounts (Exodus 25, 27, 37, 38) as essential hardware for the ark, table, altar, and ephod.

• Functions as the designated anchor points where the poles were permanently or semi-permanently housed, ensuring that holy things could be lifted and transported without human hands directly touching them.

• Maintains the vivid functional imagery of secure fastening and readiness for divine movement through the wilderness.

4. Theological Implications

• Highlights the holiness and transcendence of God, as the sacred furniture was designed for holy transport rather than casual handling or permanent settling in one location before entering the land.

• Illustrates the meticulous order and precision required by God in worship; every structural ring had a specific place, purpose, and theological boundary.

• Points typologically to the portability of the divine presence among His wandering people, assuring them that God traveled with them in every step of their journey.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Exodus 38:5, the taba'ath are cast in bronze and attached to the four corners of the bronze grating beneath the altar to hold the carrying poles.

• The specific placement at the base of the altar emphasizes structural stability and readiness for the congregation's journey from Sinai.

• The material (bronze) matches the altar itself, maintaining architectural consistency and durability for the rigorous demands of wilderness travel.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 25:12 - "You shall cast four rings of gold for it, and put them in its four corners: two rings shall be on one side, and two rings on the other side."

• Exodus 27:4 - "And you shall make for it a grate of network of bronze; and on the network you shall make four bronze rings at its four corners."

• Exodus 37:3 - "And he cast for it four rings of gold for its four corners, two rings on one side of it, and two rings on the other side of it."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The consistency of the design across both gold-plated furniture (ark, table, altar of incense) and bronze furniture (altar of burnt offering) reinforces the unified holiness of the entire tabernacle system.

• The instruction that the poles must not be removed from the rings of the ark (Exodus 25:15) underscores perpetual readiness, whereas the bronze altar rings facilitated portable sacrificial worship throughout the forty years of wandering.
