Exodus 38 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Exodus 38 completes the furnishings of the courtyard, the bronze altar and the bronze laver, and the courtyard itself with its hangings and gate. The chapter then gives an inventory of the gold, silver, and bronze used for the work, noting that the silver came from the ransom paid by every man counted in the census.

Chapter Sections

1. Exodus 38:1–8 — The Bronze Altar and the Laver

Bezalel makes the square altar of burnt offering of acacia wood overlaid with bronze, with its horns, utensils, grate, rings, and poles. He makes the bronze laver and its base “from the bronze mirrors of the serving women who assembled at the door of the tabernacle of meeting” (Exodus 38:8).

2. Exodus 38:9–20 — The Courtyard and Its Gate

The courtyard is enclosed with hangings of fine linen on pillars with bronze sockets and silver hooks and bands, one hundred cubits on the south and north and fifty on the west and east. A woven screen twenty cubits long forms the gate of the court, and all the pegs are bronze.

3. Exodus 38:21–23 — The Inventory Under Ithamar and the Work of Bezalel and Aholiab

At Moses’ command the inventory of the tabernacle of the Testimony is counted for the service of the Levites under the oversight of Ithamar, son of Aaron the priest. The work itself was made by Bezalel, who “made all that the LORD had commanded Moses” (Exodus 38:22), with Aholiab the engraver, designer, and weaver.

4. Exodus 38:24–31 — The Gold, Silver, and Bronze Used in the Work

The gold of the offering used in the holy place totals twenty-nine talents and 730 shekels, and the silver, a half shekel from each of 603,550 men counted, totals one hundred talents and 1,775 shekels, cast into the sockets of the sanctuary and the veil and into hooks and bands. The bronze offering of seventy talents and 2,400 shekels makes the sockets for the door, the bronze altar and its utensils, the court sockets, and the pegs.
