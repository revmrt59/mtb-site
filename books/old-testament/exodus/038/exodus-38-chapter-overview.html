Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 38
Main Idea:

Bezalel completes the bronze altar, the bronze laver, and the courtyard with its gate, and the chapter closes with a careful account of the gold, silver, and bronze given for the work, including the ransom silver that became the foundation of the sanctuary.

Setting:

Exodus 38 continues the building of the tabernacle (chapters 35–40), following the instructions of 27:1–19 and 30:17–21. The furnishings of the tent were finished in chapter 37.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 38:1–8 — The Bronze Altar and the Laver

  • Exodus 38:9–20 — The Courtyard and Its Gate

  • Exodus 38:21–23 — The Inventory Under Ithamar and the Work of Bezalel and Aholiab

  • Exodus 38:24–31 — The Gold, Silver, and Bronze Used in the Work

Key Verse:

“Bezalel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah, made all that the LORD had commanded Moses.” (Exodus 38:22)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The work moves out into the courtyard. Bezalel makes the great bronze altar of burnt offering, square and hollow, with horns at its corners and bronze tools for the sacrifices. Beside it he makes the bronze laver, the basin where the priests would wash, “from the bronze mirrors of the serving women who assembled at the door of the tabernacle of meeting” (38:8). Women who served at the entrance gave up their mirrors so that priests could be cleansed. Around everything rise the white linen hangings of the court, with one wide, beautiful gate on the east side.

Then the chapter does something unexpected: it counts. At Moses’ command, under the oversight of Aaron’s son Ithamar, the materials are recorded. Bezalel “made all that the LORD had commanded Moses” (38:22), with Aholiab beside him. The gold is weighed, then the silver. The silver came from the half shekel paid by every man counted in the census, 603,550 men, rich and poor alike giving the same ransom (30:15). That silver was cast into the sockets of the sanctuary and of the veil, and the rest was used for the hooks, tops, and bands of the pillars (38:27–28). Those sockets supported the frames of the dwelling, so the house of God stood on silver given as a ransom, a picture of redemption.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 38:1–8 — The Bronze Altar and the Laver

Bezalel makes the square altar of burnt offering of acacia wood covered with bronze, with its horns, tools, grate, rings, and poles. He makes the bronze laver and its stand from the bronze mirrors of the women who served at the entrance of the tabernacle.

Exodus 38:9–20 — The Courtyard and Its Gate

The courtyard is enclosed with linen hangings on pillars with bronze sockets and silver hooks and bands, one hundred cubits on the long sides and fifty on the ends. A woven screen twenty cubits wide forms the gate, and all the pegs are bronze.

Exodus 38:21–23 — The Inventory Under Ithamar and the Work of Bezalel and Aholiab

At Moses’ command the materials of the tabernacle are recorded for the Levites’ service under the oversight of Ithamar, Aaron’s son. Bezalel made everything the LORD commanded Moses, with Aholiab as engraver, designer, and weaver.

Exodus 38:24–31 — The Gold, Silver, and Bronze Used in the Work

The gold used for the holy place, the silver from the half shekel paid by each of the 603,550 men counted, and the bronze offering are all recorded. The silver becomes the sockets of the sanctuary and the veil and the hooks and bands of the pillars, while the bronze makes the door sockets, the altar, the court sockets, and the pegs.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 38 shows that gifts given to God’s work deserve careful accounting. Moses ordered an inventory, and every talent of gold, silver, and bronze was recorded. Paul followed the same principle when he handled the churches’ gifts with care so that no one could fault the ministry (2 Corinthians 8:20–21).

The chapter also gives a striking picture of redemption. The silver from the equal ransom paid by every counted man furnished the sockets beneath the sanctuary and the veil. That fact pictures God’s dwelling among His people resting on their redemption. The bronze altar and the laver point to Jesus, who gave Himself once for all and who cleanses His people so that they can serve Him (Hebrews 10:10–14; Ephesians 5:25–27).

Key Themes to Watch For

Sacrifice and cleansing in the court. The bronze altar and the laver stand in the courtyard (38:1–8).

Gifts from serving women. The laver is made from mirrors given by women who served at the tent’s entrance (38:8).

Obedience to the pattern. Bezalel makes all that the LORD commanded Moses (38:22).

Accountability for offerings. The materials are counted and recorded at Moses’ command (38:21, 24–31).

Built on redemption. The ransom silver becomes the sockets of the sanctuary and veil (38:25–27).

Key Words & Concepts

Laver (38:8). The bronze basin for the priests’ washing, made from donated bronze mirrors.

Ithamar (38:21). Aaron’s youngest son, who oversaw the inventory of the tabernacle.

Bekah (38:26). Half a shekel, the ransom each counted man paid (30:13–15).

603,550 men (38:26). The number of men twenty years and older counted in the census, as written (compare Numbers 1:46).

Sockets (38:27). Silver bases that held up the boards of the sanctuary and the pillars of the veil.
