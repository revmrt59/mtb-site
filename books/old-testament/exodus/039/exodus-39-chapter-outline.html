Exodus 39 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Exodus 39 records the making of the priestly garments, the ephod, breastplate, robe, tunics, and the gold plate, each made “as the LORD had commanded Moses” (Exodus 39:1). The finished work of the tabernacle is then brought to Moses, who sees that everything was done just as the LORD commanded and blesses the people.

Chapter Sections

1. Exodus 39:1–7 — The Ephod and Its Memorial Stones

The holy garments for Aaron are made of blue, purple, and scarlet thread, and the ephod is woven with gold beaten into threads. Onyx stones engraved with the names of the sons of Israel are set on its shoulders “as memorial stones for the sons of Israel” (Exodus 39:7).

2. Exodus 39:8–21 — The Breastplate with Twelve Stones

The breastplate is woven square and set with four rows of stones, “twelve stones according to the names of the sons of Israel” (Exodus 39:14). It is fastened to the ephod with gold chains, rings, and a blue cord so that it will not come loose.

3. Exodus 39:22–26 — The Blue Robe with Bells and Pomegranates

The robe of the ephod is woven all of blue, with a reinforced opening for the head. Its hem carries pomegranates and bells of pure gold, “a bell and a pomegranate” (Exodus 39:26) all around.

4. Exodus 39:27–31 — The Tunics, Turban, Sash, and Holy Crown

Tunics, a turban, hats, trousers, and a sash are made of fine linen for Aaron and his sons. The plate of the holy crown is made of pure gold, engraved HOLINESS TO THE LORD, and tied with a blue cord to the turban.

5. Exodus 39:32–43 — The Finished Work Is Brought to Moses and He Blesses the People

All the work of the tabernacle is finished, and the people bring Moses the tent with all its furnishings, the courtyard, and the priestly garments. Moses looks over the work and sees that “as the LORD had commanded, just so they had done it” (Exodus 39:43), and he blesses them.
