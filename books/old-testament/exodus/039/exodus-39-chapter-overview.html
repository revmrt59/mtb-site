Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 39
Main Idea:

The priestly garments are made “as the LORD had commanded Moses,” the finished work of the tabernacle is brought to Moses, and he sees that everything has been done just as the LORD commanded and blesses the people.

Setting:

Exodus 39 completes the building account of chapters 35–39. Its garment account follows the instructions of chapter 28, and only the setting up of the tabernacle in chapter 40 remains.

Chapter Outline:

  • Exodus 39:1–7 — The Ephod and Its Memorial Stones

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

The workers make the holy garments for Aaron, and after each one the same words return: “as the LORD had commanded Moses” (39:1). The phrase sounds seven times through the garment account. The ephod is woven with threads of beaten gold, and on its shoulders sit the onyx stones bearing the names of Israel’s sons. The breastplate holds twelve stones, one for each tribe, fastened securely over the place where Aaron’s heart will be. The blue robe is hemmed with golden bells and pomegranates, and the gold plate reads HOLINESS TO THE LORD. Not long before, Aaron had made a calf at the people’s demand. Now the garments for his priestly service are made exactly according to the LORD’s word.

Then the people bring everything to Moses: the tent and its frame, the coverings, the veil, the ark and mercy seat, the table and bread, the lampstand, the altars, the laver, the courtyard, and the priests’ garments. Nothing is missing. Moses looks over the whole work, “and indeed they had done it; as the LORD had commanded, just so they had done it. And Moses blessed them” (39:43). The scene recalls God looking over His finished creation and blessing it. Everything is ready. All that remains is for the tabernacle to be raised and for God to come.

Chapter Sections

Exodus 39:1–7 — The Ephod and Its Memorial Stones

The workers make the holy garments for Aaron from blue, purple, and scarlet thread, weaving the ephod with threads of beaten gold. Onyx stones engraved with the names of Israel’s sons are set on its shoulders as memorial stones, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Exodus 39:8–21 — The Breastplate with Twelve Stones

The breastplate is woven square and set with four rows of precious stones, twelve in all, engraved with the names of Israel’s tribes. It is fastened to the ephod with gold chains, rings, and a blue cord so that it will not come loose.

Exodus 39:22–26 — The Blue Robe with Bells and Pomegranates

The robe of the ephod is woven all of blue, with a strong opening for the head. Its hem carries pomegranates of blue, purple, and scarlet yarn alternating with bells of pure gold all the way around.

Exodus 39:27–31 — The Tunics, Turban, Sash, and Holy Crown

Linen tunics, a turban, caps, undergarments, and a sash are made for Aaron and his sons. The plate of the holy crown is made of pure gold, engraved HOLINESS TO THE LORD, and fastened to the turban with a blue cord.

Exodus 39:32–43 — The Finished Work Is Brought to Moses and He Blesses the People

All the work of the tabernacle is finished, and the people bring Moses the tent with its furnishings, the courtyard, and the priests’ garments. Moses inspects everything, sees that it has been done exactly as the LORD commanded, and blesses them.

Why This Chapter Matters

Exodus 39 shows what obedience looks like after failure. The people who had made a calf now did “all the work” according to God’s command (39:42). Their obedience did not earn God’s presence, but it prepared a place for Him, and Moses’ blessing honored their faithful work.

The garments carry forward the picture of the high priest who bears his people’s names on his shoulders and over his heart. That picture finds its fulfillment in Jesus, the High Priest who carries His people before the Father. The chapter also shows the joy of finished work done God’s way: everything ready, nothing left out, and the workers blessed.

Key Themes to Watch For

As the LORD commanded. The refrain marks every stage of the work as obedience (39:1, 5, 7, 21, 26, 29, 31, 42).

Names carried before God. Israel’s names are set on the shoulders and over the heart of the high priest (39:6–7, 14).

Holiness to the LORD. The gold plate marks the high priest as set apart to God (39:30).

The work completed. Every part of the tabernacle is finished and brought to Moses (39:32–41).

Blessing for faithful work. Moses inspects the work and blesses the people (39:43).

Key Words & Concepts

Ephod (39:2). The high priest’s outer garment, woven with gold threads and bearing two onyx stones on its shoulders.

Memorial stones (39:7). The engraved onyx stones that kept Israel’s names before the LORD.

Breastplate (39:8). The square pouch over the high priest’s chest, set with twelve stones for the tribes.

Holy crown (39:30). The gold plate engraved HOLINESS TO THE LORD, fastened to the front of the turban.

Tent of meeting (39:32). Another name for the tabernacle, the place where God would meet with His people.
