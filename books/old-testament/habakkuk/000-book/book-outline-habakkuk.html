Habakkuk Book Outline

Revision 1 November 6, 2026

Major Movements

I. Complaint over Violence and the Unexpected Answer (Habakkuk 1:1–11)

Habakkuk cries out, “O LORD, how long shall I cry,” because violence, strife, and perverted justice fill Judah while God seems not to hear or save (1:2–4). God answers that He is about to do something the prophet would not believe: “I am raising up the Chaldeans,” a swift and ruthless conqueror whose guilt lies in ascribing its power to its god (1:5–11). The answer shows that God has seen Judah’s violence, yet it confronts Habakkuk with a harder question than the one he first asked.

II. A Second Complaint, Waiting, and God’s Answer Concerning Proud Oppression (Habakkuk 1:12–2:20)

Habakkuk appeals to the everlasting Holy One, whose eyes are too pure to behold evil, and asks how God can stay silent while the Chaldeans haul in nations like fish in a net (1:12–17). He then declares, “I will stand my watch,” and the LORD answers with a vision to be written down for its appointed time: the proud man’s soul is not upright, “but the just shall live by his faith” (2:1–5). Five woes follow against the oppressor’s plunder, bloodshed, exploitation, and idolatry, while the promise that the earth will be filled with “the knowledge of the glory of the LORD” and the call for all the earth to keep silence before Him in His holy temple set that judgment within God’s holy rule (2:6–20).

III. Prayer, Divine Victory, and Rejoicing amid Loss (Habakkuk 3:1–19)

Having heard God’s word, Habakkuk prays that the LORD will revive His work in the midst of the years and pleads, “In wrath remember mercy” (3:1–2). He recalls God coming from Teman and Mount Paran in glory and marching out in vivid poetic imagery to save His people (3:3–15). Trembling at the calamity ahead, Habakkuk resolves that even if fig tree, vine, olive, field, flock, and herd all fail, he will rejoice in the God of his salvation, who gives him strength and sure footing on the heights (3:16–19).
