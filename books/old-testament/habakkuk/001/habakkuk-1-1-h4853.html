BURDEN - ( massa , Strong's H4853 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word massa refers to a heavy load or the act of carrying a physical burden, but in the context of prophetic literature, it signifies a weighty, solemn oracle or prophetic utterance of judgment. In Habakkuk 1:1, it describes the heavy message of divine sovereignty and impending judgment that the prophet was compelled by God to carry and declare, reflecting both the emotional weight felt by the messenger and the gravity of God's impending decree.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun massa is derived from the root verb nasa, which means "to lift up," "to carry," "to take," or "to bear." The literal word picture is that of a beast of burden or a laborer lifting a heavy load onto his shoulders to transport it. Over time, this concrete imagery was applied metaphorically to speech. To receive a prophecy was to have a heavy load placed upon one's life, and to deliver that prophecy was to "lift up" one's voice with a message of heavy consequence.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Literally, the word means a physical load, burden, or the carrying of a load. Metaphorically and prophetically, it designates a tribute, an utterance, a solemn oracle, or a threatening prophecy. In the prophetic books, it represents a message that carries immense divine weight, typically warning of impending doom, national judgment, and the unfolding of God’s sovereign purposes. It is a communication that cannot be taken lightly or ignored.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Physical burden: The term is used to describe the loads carried by pack animals, such as donkeys, or the physical tasks assigned to the Levites, who bore the heavy sacred furnishings of the tabernacle during Israel's wilderness wanderings.

• Metaphorical burden: It depicts Moses bearing the heavy emotional and leadership responsibility of guiding the complaining Israelites through the wilderness.

• Prophetic burden: It serves as a technical term introducing major prophetic announcements of judgment. The prophet is depicted as a porter who must carry this heavy divine revelation until he unloads it by proclaiming it to the recipients.

4. Theological Implications

• The Weight of Revelation: God's word is not light or trivial. A prophetic word possesses a spiritual weight (kabod) that demands reverence and response.

• The Prophet’s Sympathetic Suffering: The prophetic ministry is not detached. The prophet feels the weight of God’s grief over human sin and the sorrow of the coming judgment. It is a burden to the soul before it is a message for the ears.

• Divine Compulsion: When God places a massa on a prophet, it cannot be easily cast off. The prophet is divinely compelled to speak, finding it more painful to keep the message inside than to deliver a difficult truth to a rebellious audience.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 1:1, the prophet "sees" a "burden" (massa). This combination of sight and weight is highly significant. Habakkuk does not merely hear words; he is given a vivid, heavy mental and spiritual vision of the moral decay of Judah and the terrifying rise of the Chaldeans. The burden here is deeply personal; the entire book of Habakkuk consists of a painful dialogue between the questioning prophet and a sovereign God. The burden is the agonizing weight of trying to reconcile God's holiness with the prosperity of the wicked and the harshness of His chosen instrument of judgment.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Isaiah 13:1: "The burden against Babylon which Isaiah the son of Amoz saw."

• Jeremiah 23:33: "So when these people or the prophet or the priest ask you, saying, 'What is the burden of the LORD?' you shall then say to them, 'What burden? I will cast you off,' says the LORD."

• Malachi 1:1: "The burden of the word of the LORD to Israel by Malachi."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the days of Jeremiah, the word massa became a target of cynical wordplay. The people and false prophets mockingly asked, "What is the massa (burden/prophecy) of the Lord today?" using the term to sneer at the repeatedly gloomy nature of true prophetic messages. God responded to this mockery by stating that the people themselves had become a heavy, useless burden to Him, and He would cast them off. This historical abuse highlights the necessity of approaching God’s "burden" with fear and trembling rather than cynicism.
