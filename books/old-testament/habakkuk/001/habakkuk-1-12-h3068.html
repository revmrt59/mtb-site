LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh is the personal, covenant-keeping name of the God of Israel, self-revealed to Moses as the self-existent, eternal, and unchanging One. In Habakkuk 1:12, the prophet uses this sacred name in a moment of deep national crisis to anchor his faith in God’s unchanging character, covenant faithfulness, and sovereignty over the rising Babylonian threat.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The name Yahweh is the Tetragrammaton (YHWH), the most sacred and personal name of God in the Old Testament, occurring over 6,800 times. Its linguistic roots are deeply tied to the Hebrew verb hayah, meaning "to be" or "to exist."

• When God revealed this name to Moses at the burning bush in Exodus 3, He defined Himself as "I AM WHO I AM," highlighting His absolute self-existence, independence, and eternality. Unlike the pagan deities of the Ancient Near East who required sustenance, had origins, and were limited by geography, Yahweh is uncaused, self-sustaining, and sovereign over all creation.

• The name is deeply connected to God's covenant with Israel. It is not merely a philosophical concept of being, but an active, relational declaration: the God who is active, present, and faithful to His promises.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Yahweh refers to the self-existent, eternal, supreme God of Israel, the One who is sovereignly active in history to redeem His people and fulfill His promises.

• The primary semantic meaning centers on active presence and ontological self-sufficiency: the One who causes to be, or the One who is eternally present.

• It denotes absolute faithfulness, reliability, and immutability. Because He is Yahweh, His character does not change, and His covenant commitments to His people remain secure despite external circumstances.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The use of Yahweh in Scripture is often contrasted with the names and titles of false gods, representing the true and living God against dead, powerless idols.

• It is frequently paired with terms of relationship and protection. For example, He is Yahweh-Yireh (The LORD Will Provide), Yahweh-Nissi (The LORD My Banner), and Yahweh-Raah (The LORD My Shepherd).

• In the prophetic literature, the name serves as a guarantee of judgment on the wicked and salvation for the righteous, emphasizing that history is moving toward the ultimate manifestation of His glory and lordship.

4. Theological Implications

• Sovereignty and Immutability: As the self-existent One, God is completely independent of creation yet deeply involved in it. He does not change in His essence, attributes, or purposes.

• Covenant Faithfulness: Yahweh is the name associated with God’s binding promises to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. It assures His people that He will never abandon them, even when chastising them for their sins.

• Holiness: As Yahweh, He is utterly set apart from creation and sin, possessing perfect moral purity, which requires Him to address evil and injustice in the world.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Habakkuk 1:12, the prophet is reeling from the shocking divine revelation that God plans to use the ruthless and wicked Chaldeans (Babylonians) to punish Judah for her sins.

• Faced with this immense theological crisis, Habakkuk immediately appeals to the covenant name Yahweh: "Are You not from everlasting, O LORD, my God, my Holy One?"

• By addressing God as Yahweh, Habakkuk grounds his processing of pain in God's covenant loyalty. He reasons that because God is Yahweh (the eternal, faithful Covenant Keeper), and "my Holy One," Israel will not be completely annihilated: "We shall not die."

• The use of Yahweh here balances the tension between God's terrifying holiness in using the Chaldeans for "judgment" and "correction," and His ultimate covenant commitment to preserve His people. Habakkuk uses the name to preach to his own soul when circumstances scream otherwise.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 3:15 – "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

• Psalm 23:1 – "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

• Isaiah 42:8 – "I am the LORD, that is My name; And My glory I will not give to another, Nor My praise to carved images."

• Malachi 3:6 – "For I am the LORD, I do not change; Therefore you are not consumed, O sons of Jacob."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• In Jewish tradition, out of deep reverence and fear of taking the Lord's name in vain, the pronunciation of the Tetragrammaton was avoided. When reading Scripture, the word Adonai (Lord) or HaShem (The Name) was spoken instead.

• This tradition is reflected in most English Bible translations, including the NKJV, which render Yahweh as "LORD" in all capital letters to distinguish it from Adonai (rendered as "Lord").

• The New Testament translates Yahweh using the Greek word Kyrios (Lord), a title that is directly and boldly applied to Jesus Christ (e.g., Philippians 2:9–11), affirming His full deity and identity as the covenant God of Israel.
