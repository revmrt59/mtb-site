ESTABLISHED - ( yasad , Strong's H3245 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yasad literally means to lay a foundation, establish, or ordain. In the Old Testament, it frequently describes the architectural act of laying the foundation of a building, such as the temple, or God's creative work in founding the earth. In Habakkuk 1:12, the prophet uses this powerful term to declare that God has "established" or ordained the ruthless Chaldeans not for total annihilation, but as a firm, purposeful instrument for the correction and discipline of His covenant people.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root of yasad carries the concrete imagery of laying a heavy, immovable foundation stone upon which a massive structure is built.

• In ancient Near Eastern architecture, the foundation was the most critical part of any building, ensuring stability, alignment, and permanence.

• This term moves from a literal physical concept (laying stone foundations) to a metaphorical and theological one, signifying the sovereign establishment of kingdoms, decrees, and divine purposes that cannot be shaken, bypassed, or overturned by human will.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• yasad is a primary verb meaning to set, found, establish, appoint, or ordain.

• In the Qal stem, it refers to laying a foundation (of the earth, a building, or a city) or appointing a decree.

• In the Piel stem, it emphasizes the active, deliberate, and secure laying of a foundation.

• In the Pual stem, it describes that which is firmly and immovably founded or established.

• The lexical range encompasses:

• To lay a physical foundation or base for a structure.

• To establish, ordain, or appoint a person, nation, or decree for a specific, sovereign purpose.

• To fix firmly, settle securely, or establish order.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Creation Foundations: The word is poetically used to describe God's absolute sovereignty in "founding" the earth. It portrays the cosmos as a secure building constructed by a master architect (Psalm 24:2, Psalm 102:25).

• Temple Foundations: It frequently denotes the historic laying of the foundation of Solomon's Temple (1 Kings 5:17) and the Second Temple (Ezra 3:11), representing a visible, physical manifestation of God's dwelling place among His people.

• Divine Appointment: Metaphorically, yasad depicts God setting apart or ordaining a historical reality. When God "establishes" something, it is endowed with divine backing and a specific purpose that human agency cannot thwart.

4. Theological Implications

• Sovereignty Over Evil: By stating that God "established" the Chaldeans, the text reveals that even pagan, wicked empires are under the absolute sovereignty of Yahweh. They do not operate outside of His control; they are raised up and positioned by Him.

• Purpose in Discipline: God does not act arbitrarily. The foundations of His judgments are laid with precision. The Chaldeans were established not for chaotic destruction, but "for correction" (reproof and purification).

• Covenant Faithfulness: Because God is the "Rock" (the ultimate, unshakeable foundation), His acts of establishing judgment are consistent with His holy character and His long-term covenant commitment to redeem His people through discipline.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Habakkuk 1:12, the prophet is wrestling with the shocking revelation that God will use the wicked, violent Babylonians (Chaldeans) to punish Judah.

• Habakkuk appeals to God's eternal nature ("from everlasting") and covenant relationship ("my God, my Holy One"), concluding with the faithful assurance, "We shall not die."

• In this context, yasad provides immense comfort. The Chaldeans are not an uncontrolled force of nature or history; they have been deliberately "established" or "founded" by God as a temporary instrument of discipline. Their power has a boundary, a foundation laid by God, and a limited divine mandate ("for correction"), ensuring that God's people will be chastised but not utterly destroyed.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Joshua 6:26: "Then Joshua charged them at that time, saying, 'Cursed be the man before the LORD who rises up and builds this city Jericho; he shall lay its foundation with his firstborn, and with his youngest he shall set up its gates.'"

• 1 Kings 5:17: "And the king commanded them to quarry large stones, costly stones, and hewn stones, to lay the foundation of the temple."

• Psalm 24:2: "For He has founded it upon the seas, and established it upon the waters."

• Psalm 102:25: "Of old You laid the foundation of the earth, and the heavens are the work of Your hands."

• Isaiah 14:32: "What will they answer the messengers of the nation? That the LORD has founded Zion, and the poor of His people shall trust in it."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint) translates yasad in Habakkuk 1:12 using themelioo, which also means to lay a foundation, ground, or establish. This is the same root used by Jesus in Matthew 7:25 concerning the house "founded on the rock" and by Paul in Ephesians 3:17 ("grounded in love").

• The juxtaposition of God as the "Rock" (tsur) and God "founding" (yasad) the instrument of correction creates a powerful architectural metaphor. The true, eternal foundation is God Himself; any secondary foundation He lays (even using pagan nations) is entirely subject to His sovereign design.
