APPOINTED - ( śûm , Strong's H7760 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb śûm primarily means "to put, place, set, or appoint." In Habakkuk 1:12, it describes God's sovereign ordination of the Chaldeans (Babylonians) as an instrument of divine judgment against Judah. Rather than indicating passive permission or chaotic geopolitical shifts, this word underscores Yahweh's active, absolute sovereignty over pagan nations, positioning them precisely where He wills to accomplish His holy, corrective purposes.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root meaning of śûm is the physical act of putting or placing an object in a specific location. It evokes the mental image of a craftsman deliberately placing a tool, a builder setting a cornerstone, or a king positioning a guard. Over time, this concrete physical action expanded metaphorically to denote appointing people to offices, establishing laws, or setting boundaries. In the ancient Near East, the sovereignty of a deity or king was demonstrated by their power to "set" or "appoint" destinies, boundaries, and rulers. When applied to Yahweh, the word pictures His undisputed authority to arrange history, position empires, and direct nations according to His sovereign decree.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb śûm (alternatively transliterated as śîm) is a highly versatile root occurring over 580 times in the Old Testament. Its semantic range includes:

• To put, place, or set (physically placing an object, such as placing the ark in the tabernacle).

• To establish, ordain, or constitute (setting laws, appointing leaders, or establishing a covenant).

• To appoint or assign (designating a person or group for a specific task or destiny).

• To transform or make (changing something into a different state, such as making a city a ruin).

In Habakkuk 1:12, the verb is used in the Qal perfect aspect (śamtô - "you have appointed him/them"), indicating a completed, decisive act of divine appointment. The Lord has actively designated the Babylonian empire for the specific function of executing judicial discipline (mišpāṭ).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Setting a Mark or Seal: The word is used of God placing a mark on Cain (Genesis 4:15), indicating protective ownership and divine decree.

• Establishing Order: Job 38:5 uses śûm to describe God determining the measurements of the earth, conveying cosmic architectural precision.

• Appointing Leaders: In Deuteronomy 17:15, Israel is instructed to "surely set (śûm) a king over you whom the LORD your God chooses." The appointment is a deliberate act of choosing and installing authority.

• Divine Decrees: It is used of God "appointing" the sand as the boundary of the sea by a perpetual decree (Jeremiah 5:22).

4. Theological Implications

• Absolute Sovereignty: The use of śûm in Habakkuk 1:12 dismantles any notion of dualism or accidental history. The rise of the ruthless Chaldeans was not an oversight by God, nor was it a victory of pagan deities over Yahweh. God actively "appointed" them.

• The Instrument of Wrath and Mercy: God appoints pagan nations to serve His covenant purposes. Babylon is not sovereign; they are a tool in the hands of the Master Builder. They are set apart "for judgment" and "established for correction."

• Holy Character of God: Habakkuk wrestles with how a pure God can use an impure nation. By affirming that God "appointed" them, the prophet anchors his faith in the truth that God’s character remains unstained; He uses the wicked to discipline His people, but will ultimately judge the wicked as well.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 1:12, the prophet is in the midst of a profound spiritual crisis. God has just revealed (verses 5–11) that He is raising up the dreadfully violent Chaldeans to sweep through the land. Habakkuk responds by appealing to God’s eternal nature: "Are You not from everlasting, O LORD my God, my Holy One? We shall not die." Immediately following this, he confesses, "O LORD, You have appointed them for judgment; O Rock, You have established them for correction." The word "appointed" (śûm) acts as a theological anchor. Instead of succumbing to panic, Habakkuk uses this word to confess that the terrifying Babylonian threat is entirely under Yahweh's control. They are not acting independently; they have been deliberately placed (śûm) in this historical moment for a restricted, corrective purpose.

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The term śûm forms a sharp theological contrast with the proud boasts of the Chaldeans in Habakkuk 1:11, where they are described as "guilty men, whose own strength is their god." While the Babylonians attribute their conquest to their own power, Habakkuk's use of śûm reminds the reader that their power is entirely derivative, appointed by the true God.

• In the Greek Translation (LXX), the translators frequently used tassō (to appoint, ordain, order) or tithemi (to put, place) to translate śûm. In Habakkuk 1:12, the LXX uses etaxas (from tassō), which means "you have ordered" or "you have appointed." This is the same root used in Romans 13:1 to describe how civil authorities are "appointed (tassō) by God."
