HOLD YOUR TONGUE - ( charash , Strong's H2790 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb charash means to be silent, quiet, or speechless, emphasizing a deliberate decision to withhold speech or action. In Habakkuk 1:13, the prophet uses this word to express his deep distress over God's apparent passivity, questioning how a holy Creator can remain silent and inactive while the wicked devour those who are more righteous than they.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root charash contains two distinct semantic lines in biblical Hebrew. The first refers to plowing, engraving, or fabricating, which involves cutting, carving, or plotting. The second refers to being deaf or keeping silent. In ancient thought, deafness and muteness were closely linked; a person who could not hear was often unable to speak. When used in the context of silence, the word does not imply a mere lack of noise, but rather a deliberate withholding of voice, response, or action. It represents a conscious decision to refrain from speaking or executing an expected intervention.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

In the Hiphil (causative) stem, which is the form used in Habakkuk 1:13, charash means to exhibit silence, to keep quiet, to hold one's peace, or to remain inactive. The lexical definition focuses on the choice to withhold a response when one has the capacity to speak or act. It carries the nuance of staying still, refusing to answer, or showing a quiet tolerance toward a situation.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Silence as Acquiescence: Remaining silent when a statement is made can imply consent or agreement.

• Silence as Self-Restraint: Holding one's peace in the face of provocation or out of wisdom.

• Silence as Inactivity or Neglect: Standing by without speaking or taking action while a crisis unfolds.

• Divine Silence: God choosing not to answer prayer immediately, or refraining from executing immediate judgment upon the wicked.

4. Theological Implications

The silence of God is one of the most agonizing trials of faith in the Old Testament. Because God’s voice and action are synonymous with His presence, favor, and deliverance, His silence is often perceived as abandonment or indifference. However, scripture reveals that God's silence is never passive apathy. Instead, it is a manifestation of His sovereign timing, long-suffering patience, and inscrutable purposes. When God "holds His tongue," He tests the faith of His people, forcing them to trust His character even when His active intervention is invisible.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 1:13, the prophet is wrestling with a profound theological crisis. God has just announced that He is raising up the ruthless Babylonians to execute judgment on Judah. Habakkuk objects, noting that God is of "purer eyes than to behold evil." He asks why God remains silent (charash)—translated in the NKJV as "hold Your tongue"—while a pagan, treacherous nation devours a people more righteous than themselves. The use of charash here emphasizes the unbearable contrast between God’s absolute holiness and His apparent tolerance of Babylon's cruelty. Habakkuk is distressed that the Almighty seems to be a passive bystander while wickedness triumphs.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 34:5: "And Jacob heard that he had defiled Dinah his daughter. Now his sons were with his livestock in the field; so Jacob held his peace until they came."

• Exodus 14:14: "The LORD will fight for you, and you shall hold your peace."

• Psalm 35:22: "This You have seen, O LORD; Do not keep silence; O LORD, do not be far from me."

• Psalm 50:21: "These things you have done, and I kept silent; You thought that I was altogether like you; But I will rebuke you, And set them in order before your eyes."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The tension of God's silence in Habakkuk is resolved in the subsequent chapters. While the prophet demands an answer for God's silence in chapter 1, God commands the entire earth to be silent before Him in Habakkuk 2:20 ("Let all the earth keep silence before Him"). This shifts the weight of silence from God to humanity. Ultimately, God's silence is broken by His righteous judgment, demonstrating that He is never inactive, but is working out His perfect plan in His own time.
