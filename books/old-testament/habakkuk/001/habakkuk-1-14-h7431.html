CREEPING THINGS - ( remes , Strong's H7431 )

Summary:

In Habakkuk 1:14, the Hebrew word remes describes low-level, crawling, or swarming creatures that move without guidance or protection. The prophet laments that Yahweh has reduced humanity to the status of these leaderless "creeping things," leaving them vulnerable to the ruthless Babylonian empire. This term highlights a devastating reversal of the creation order, where humans—originally designed to rule over creeping things—are instead subjugated, harvested, and treated like insignificant pests by a tyrannical conqueror.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew noun remes is derived from the root verb ramas, which means to creep, crawl, or glide. The word picture is one of low-slung movement, close to the dust of the earth or swarming in the depths of the sea. It brings to mind creatures that lack individual significance, moving collectively in vast, nameless numbers. These are creatures that do not walk upright; they scurry, slide, or swarm. In the ancient Near Eastern mind, such creatures were characterized by their sheer volume and their lack of an obvious leader, king, or protector to guide them.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term remes denotes a collective category of animal life, typically translated as "creeping things," "moving things," or "teeming things." It encompasses land reptiles, insects, small rodents, and swarming aquatic life. Rather than referring to a specific species, it serves as a broad classification for life forms that occupy the lowest strata of the animal kingdom. Lexically, the word emphasizes a lack of stature, independence, and governance.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, remes is used to describe:

• The teeming, crawling land creatures created on the sixth day of creation.

• The reptiles and small animals that entered Noah's ark.

• The abundant, swarming marine life in the depths of the sea.

• In poetic literature, the word represents the vast, chaotic, and unnumbered mass of lower creation that depends entirely on God for daily sustenance.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, remes carries profound implications regarding the created order and the dignity of humanity. In the Genesis creation narrative, God created human beings in His image and explicitly granted them dominion over the "creeping things" (remes). Humanity was set apart to stand upright and rule as God's vice-regents over these lowly creatures. Therefore, when Scripture describes human beings being reduced to the level of remes, it signifies a catastrophic loss of human dignity, a breakdown of the covenantal order, and a state of spiritual and physical degradation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 1:14, the prophet is wrestling with a painful theological dilemma: How can a holy God use the wicked, pagan Chaldeans (Babylonians) to judge a people more righteous than they? Habakkuk describes the Babylonians' brutal conquest as an act of fishing. He laments that God has made men "like the fishes of the sea, like creeping things (remes) that have no ruler over them."

The nuance here is one of absolute helplessness and insignificance. By stripping Judah of her king, her walls, and her divine protection, God has left His people like leaderless insects or swarming sea creatures. The Babylonians do not see the covenant people of God as human beings made in the divine image; they view them merely as remes—worthless, swarming things to be swept up into a dragnet, gathered in heaps, and consumed at the conqueror's whim.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 1:24 - "Then God said, 'Let the earth bring forth the living creature according to its kind: cattle and creeping thing and beast of the earth, each according to its kind'; and it was so."

• Genesis 9:3 - "Every moving thing that lives shall be food for you. I have given you all things, even as the green herbs."

• 1 Kings 4:33 - "He spoke also of beasts, of birds, of creeping things, and of fish."

• Psalm 104:25 - "This great and wide sea, in which are innumerable teeming things, living things both small and great."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The tragedy of Habakkuk 1:14 lies in the complete reversal of the creation mandate. In Genesis 1:26, God said, "Let them have dominion... over every creeping thing that creeps on the earth." Under the judgment of the Babylonian exile, the conqueror exercises a perverted dominion, treating God's chosen people as the very remes they were commanded to rule. It serves as a stark reminder of what happens when humanity is abandoned to its own devices apart from the protective, shepherding rule of God.
