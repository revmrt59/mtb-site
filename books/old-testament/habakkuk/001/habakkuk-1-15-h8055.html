REJOICE - ( samach , Strong's H8055 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb samach refers to a spontaneous, deep-seated joy that often manifests in outward, visible expressions of gladness. While frequently used to describe holy rejoicing in the presence of Yahweh, in Habakkuk 1:15 it describes the perverse, self-congratulatory joy of the ruthless Chaldeans (Babylonians). They exult in their own military prowess and the successful capture of nations, treating human beings like helpless fish caught in a net. This word highlights how joy can be profoundly corrupted when detached from God and directed toward self-glory and oppression.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The verb samach conveys the idea of shining, brightening up, or being glad. It represents an emotion that starts within the heart and radiates outward, affecting one's facial expression, voice, and physical posture. It is a whole-body experience of gladness, often associated with a face that "lights up." Historically, this word is deeply rooted in communal celebrations, harvest festivals, and victory shouts. However, the background in Habakkuk 1:15 contrasts this sacred history with the pagan, militaristic celebrations of the Babylonian empire. Instead of rejoicing in God's provision, the Chaldeans rejoice in their own destructive efficiency.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb samach means to rejoice, be glad, or exult. It denotes a state of bright, cheerful, and merry disposition, ranging from internal feelings of pleasure to external, noisy demonstrations of delight. In the Qal stem (as used in Habakkuk 1:15), it expresses the active experience of this joy. It is frequently paired with other Hebrew verbs of gladness, such as gil (to rejoice/exult) and shush (to be glad), to emphasize the intensity of the emotion. In Habakkuk 1:15, it is paired with gil (NKJV: "are glad") to show the double-sided nature of their perverse celebration.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament uses samach in several primary contexts:

• Religious festivals: The covenant community rejoicing before God at the tabernacle or temple during the annual feasts (Deuteronomy 12:7).

• Relational joy: A husband rejoicing with his wife (Proverbs 5:18) or a father rejoicing over a wise son (Proverbs 23:24).

• Corrupted joy: The mockery of enemies rejoicing over the downfall of the righteous (Psalm 35:19) or, as in Habakkuk, the arrogant triumph of a conquering empire over its victims.

The word picture in Habakkuk 1:15 is paired with the metaphor of a fisherman. The Babylonians gather nations like fish in a dragnet, and upon seeing their massive catch, they throw a festival of self-worship, literally "sacrificing" to their own nets.

4. Theological Implications

Joy is not morally neutral; its character is determined by its object and source. True samach finds its source and object in God Himself. When human beings find their ultimate joy in their own achievements, strength, or wealth, samach becomes corrupted. The Babylonians' joy is an act of self-deification, attributing their success to their own "net" (military power) rather than the sovereign God who allowed them to conquer. This text warns that God-given emotions like joy can be weaponized into pride and cruelty when separated from the fear of the Lord.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 1, the prophet laments the violence and injustice in Judah. God responds that He is raising up the Chaldeans as an instrument of judgment. Habakkuk is deeply perplexed because the Chaldeans are even more wicked than Judah. In verses 14-17, Habakkuk describes their ruthless, dehumanizing conquest. In verse 15, the Chaldeans "rejoice (samach) and are glad." This double expression of joy emphasizes their complete lack of conscience. They do not merely execute judgment; they delight in the suffering of others and worship their own instruments of destruction. Their joy is a symptom of their spiritual blindness and impending doom, as God will eventually judge them for this arrogant self-exaltation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Deuteronomy 12:7: "And there you shall eat before the LORD your God, and you shall rejoice in all to which you have put your hand, you and your households, in which the LORD your God has blessed you."

• Psalm 104:34: "May my meditation be sweet to Him; I will be glad in the LORD."

• Psalm 105:3: "Glory in His holy name; Let the hearts of those rejoice who seek the LORD."

• Proverbs 5:18: "Let your fountain be blessed, And rejoice with the wife of your youth."

• Proverbs 17:5: "He who mocks the poor reproaches his Maker; He who is glad at calamity will not go unpunished."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The pairing of samach (rejoice) and gil (are glad) is a common poetic device in Hebrew literature to express complete and overflowing emotion. In holy contexts, this pairing describes the ultimate joy of salvation (e.g., Psalm 9:14, Isaiah 25:9). In Habakkuk 1:15, however, this poetic pairing is inverted to depict the absolute, unbridled arrogance of the wicked.

Furthermore, the transition from samach in Habakkuk 1:15 to the ultimate resolution in Habakkuk 3:18 is profound. While the wicked Chaldeans rejoice temporarily in their nets, the prophet Habakkuk declares at the end of his book: "Yet I will rejoice in the LORD, I will joy in the God of my salvation." The word for "rejoice" in Habakkuk 3:18 is alaz, but the thematic contrast is clear: the wicked rejoice in their own power, which fails, while the righteous rejoice in the unchanging Yahweh.
