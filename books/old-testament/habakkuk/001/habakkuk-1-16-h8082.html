SUMPTUOUS - ( shamen , Strong's H8082 )

Summary:

The Hebrew adjective shamen refers to that which is fat, rich, robust, or fertile. In the ancient Near East, fatness was a prized symbol of prosperity, health, and abundance, representing the very best and most productive portion of the land or flock. In Habakkuk 1:16, the prophet uses this word to describe the luxury and abundant wealth of the conquering Babylonians. Instead of giving glory to God for their material success, they worshiped their own military power, deifying the very tools that secured their sumptuous lifestyle.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the agrarian society of ancient Israel, "fatness" (shamen) was synonymous with the highest quality of life. Unlike modern cultures that often view fatness with negative connotations, the ancient world associated it with rich soil, heavy olive crops, healthy livestock, and divine blessing. To have a "fat" portion meant to enjoy the cream of the crop, the most nutrient-dense food, and the most secure lifestyle. The word picture is rooted in the glistening richness of olive oil and the choice, healthy cuts of meat. It represents a state of being well-nourished, physically robust, and materially prosperous.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Shamen is an adjective meaning fat, rich, fertile, robust, or plenteous. It is closely related to the noun shemen (oil or fatness) and the verb shaman (to grow fat). Lexically, the word describes the physical health of livestock, the agricultural yield of highly fertile soil, the rich nutritional quality of food, or the opulent state of a person's material portion. When applied to land, it indicates exceptional productivity; when applied to food, it denotes luxury and high quality; when applied to a person's portion or share, it refers to an abundant, luxurious livelihood.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Fertile Soil: The word describes the agricultural richness of the Promised Land, highlighting its capability to sustain a large population with abundance.

• Robust Livestock: It characterizes healthy, well-fed sheep or cattle, which stood in contrast to weak, diseased, or starved animals.

• Rich Provision: It is used of food that is deeply satisfying, royal, and plentiful, such as the finest bread or oil.

• The Portion of the Aggressor: In Habakkuk, it shifts from a description of natural blessing to the stolen, luxurious spoils of a predatory empire.

4. Theological Implications

While shamen represents the physical manifestation of God's blessing, it carries a severe spiritual danger. Throughout Scripture, material "fatness" frequently leads to self-sufficiency, spiritual apathy, and rebellion. When people enjoy a sumptuous portion, they easily forget the Giver of the wealth and begin to worship the creation rather than the Creator. The abundance that should provoke thanksgiving instead fosters a hardened heart. In the hands of the wicked, a sumptuous portion is not a sign of divine approval but of accumulated guilt, as they fatten themselves through the oppression of others.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 1:15–16, the prophet laments the ruthless rise of the Chaldeans (Babylonians), comparing them to an insatiable fisherman who sweeps up entire nations in his net. Instead of recognizing God as the ultimate sovereign who allowed their conquests, the Babylonians "sacrifice to their net, and burn incense to their dragnet." The reason for this idolatry is directly tied to their appetite: "because by them their share is sumptuous and their food plentiful." The word shamen highlights the physical motivation behind their imperial conquests. They worshiped their military might because their weapons brought them unparalleled luxury, high-quality food, and an easy, opulent lifestyle at the expense of conquered peoples. Their god was their belly, and their weapons were the means to feed it.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The connection between shamen (fat/rich) and shemen (oil) is vital for understanding biblical imagery. Olive oil was central to daily life in Israel, used for cooking, light, medicine, and anointing. A "sumptuous" portion is literally one that is saturated with oil—glistening, rich, and full of life. However, when this richness is secured through violence, it becomes a trap. The very wealth that the Babylonians worshiped eventually led to their pride, moral decay, and ultimate destruction by the Persians.
