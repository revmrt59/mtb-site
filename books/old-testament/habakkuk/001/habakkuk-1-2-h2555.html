VIOLENCE - ( chamas, Strong's H2555 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word chamas refers to cold-blooded, intentional wrong, social injustice, and ethical outrage. Far beyond mere physical aggression, it describes the systemic abuse of power, plunder, and cruelty that destroys lives, tears at community relationships, and violates God's covenantal standard of righteousness. In Habakkuk 1:2, it is a desperate prophetic outcry against the rampant lawlessness and domestic oppression prevailing in Judah, signaling a profound moral crisis that demands immediate divine intervention.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The term chamas evokes the imagery of a violent tearing, crushing, or ruthless disruption of established order. It is the antithesis of shalom, which represents wholeness, peace, and flourishing relationships.

• In the ancient world, chamas was understood as the weapon of the strong against the vulnerable. It is the active, malicious force that dismantles social justice and replaces it with the law of the jungle, where might makes right.

• Culturally, it describes the physical, structural, and legal mechanisms used by the powerful to exploit, oppress, and silence those who have no helper.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The word chamas denotes violence, cruelty, damage, injustice, and wrongful injury.

• The semantic range spans from physical assault and bloodshed to ethical and legal violations, such as perjury, extortion, and the systemic deprivation of human rights.

• It describes a willful, calculated hostility that rejects moral boundaries and treats others as mere objects to be plundered for personal gain.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Chamas is frequently depicted as a garment or accessory that the wicked wear proudly (Psalm 73:6), showing that violence defines their public identity and lifestyle.

• It is described as a substance that people store up in their strongholds (Amos 3:10), illustrating how wealth gained through exploitation becomes a corrupting accumulation of guilt.

• Scripture uses the phrase "witness of violence" (ed chamas) in Exodus 23:1 to describe a malicious, false witness who seeks to subvert justice through lies, showing that chamas can be committed with words and legal systems just as easily as with weapons.

4. Theological Implications

• Chamas is deeply offensive to God because it defiles His creation and assaults the divine image in humanity. It was the pervasive presence of chamas that prompted God to judge the pre-flood world with the deluge (Genesis 6:11-13).

• God identifies Himself as the ultimate defender of those suffering from chamas. He hears their cries, remembers their pain, and pledges to bring retribution against the violent.

• Within the covenant relationship, chamas represents the ultimate failure of God's people. When God expects justice (mishpat) and righteousness (tsedaqah), the presence of chamas signals apostasy and a total rejection of His law.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Habakkuk 1:2, the prophet cries out, "Violence!" (chamas). This is a technical legal cry, similar to a modern scream for "Help!" or "Murder!" in a public square. It is a formal appeal to the ultimate Judge for intervention in the face of ongoing crime.

• The immediate context is not foreign invasion, but internal, domestic corruption within Judah under the wicked reign of King Jehoiakim. The Judean leadership and wealthy elite were actively exploiting, robbing, and abusing the poor and vulnerable of their own nation.

• Habakkuk's lament reveals his deep distress over God's apparent silence. The prophet sees lawlessness, strife, and systemic injustice everywhere, yet God has not yet intervened to rescue the oppressed or judge the perpetrators. The cry of chamas here is a raw, agonizing plea for God to act according to His holy character.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 6:11: "The earth also was corrupt before God, and the earth was filled with violence."

• Psalm 73:6: "Therefore pride serves as their necklace; Violence covers them like a garment."

• Proverbs 4:17: "For they eat the bread of wickedness, And drink the wine of violence."

• Ezekiel 8:17: "And He said to me, 'Have you seen this, O son of man? Is it a trivial thing to the house of Judah to commit the abominations which they commit here? For they have filled the land with violence; then they have returned to provoke Me to anger...'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• Phonetically, chamas is a harsh, guttural word in Hebrew. Its very pronunciation mirrors the jarring, destructive reality of the action it describes.

• It stands in stark contrast to the characteristics of the Messianic King, of whom it is written that He will redeem the needy from oppression and violence, and their blood will be precious in His sight (Psalm 72:14).

• The ultimate eradication of chamas is promised in the future restoration of God's people, where violence will no longer be heard in the land, nor wasting or destruction within its borders (Isaiah 60:18).
