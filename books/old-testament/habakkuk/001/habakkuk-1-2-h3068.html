LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh (LORD) is the personal, covenant-keeping name of the God of Israel, representing His eternal, self-existent nature and His relational commitment to His people. In Habakkuk 1:2, this name carries profound weight as the prophet cries out to a personal, promise-keeping God amidst apparent silence and injustice, appealing to the very character of the One who bound Himself to Israel.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The name Yahweh is the personal name of God, revealed to Israel as a testimony of His eternal, unchangeable, and self-existent nature. This name is derived from the Hebrew verb hayah, meaning "to be" or "to become." It is intimately linked to the phrase "I AM WHO I AM" in Exodus 3:14. In ancient Semitic culture, a name was not merely a label but a revelation of a person's character, essence, and authority. By revealing this name, God invited His people into a personal relationship, demonstrating that He is the active, ever-present God who acts in history. In Jewish tradition, the name was regarded as so sacred that it was not pronounced aloud. Instead, the vowels of Adonai (Lord) were read in its place, leading to the traditional English rendering of "LORD" in all capital letters.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Yahweh is the proper noun representing the self-existent, eternal, and covenant-making God of Israel. It designates the supreme, uncreated Creator who has life in Himself and is independent of all external forces. The lexical core of the name signifies active existence, presence, and relational commitment. Unlike Elohim, which highlights God's power, majesty, and role as Creator of the universe, Yahweh specifically identifies God in His relational capacity—the One who binds Himself to His people through covenants, demonstrates steadfast love, and acts to redeem them from bondage.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The name is used over 6,000 times in the Old Testament, representing the primary designation of Israel's God.

• The Covenant Initiator: The name is consistently employed in contexts involving divine promises, covenants, and oaths. It depicts God as the One who initiates and faithfully maintains His pledges to humanity.

• The Relational God: It is used when God speaks intimately with His prophets and people, contrasting with generic references to deity which emphasize distance or abstract power.

• The Unchangeable Rock: The name portrays God as a consistent, dependable sanctuary, whose character does not shift like the false deities of surrounding nations.

4. Theological Implications

• Self-Existence and Aseity: God possesses life inherently and eternally; He is the ultimate reality and the source of all being.

• Absolute Faithfulness: Because the name is tied to God's unchanging nature, His promises are utterly reliable, ensuring that His redemptive purposes for His people cannot fail.

• Immanence and Presence: The name guarantees that God is not a distant watchmaker but is actively present in the midst of human history, hearing the cries of His people.

• Holiness and Sovereignty: As the eternal One, He rules over all nations and demands moral purity and justice from those who bear His name.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 1:2, the prophet cries out: "O LORD, how long shall I cry, and You will not hear?"

• An Appeal to Covenant Loyalty: Habakkuk intentionally addresses God as Yahweh, the covenant-keeping God of Israel. By using this personal name, the prophet is appealing directly to God's character. The crisis in Judah—marked by violence, injustice, and lawlessness—directly contradicts the holy and protective nature of Yahweh.

• Boldness of Covenant Relationship: The use of this name explains the boldness of Habakkuk’s lament. He does not approach God as a distant sovereign but as a personal covenant partner, holding Him to His own promises of justice and preservation for His people.

• The Pain of Apparent Silence: The tragedy for Habakkuk is that Yahweh, the God who promised to be ever-present and attentive to His people, seems to be silent and inactive in the face of rampant sin.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:4: "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens."

• Exodus 3:15: "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

• Psalm 23:1: "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

• Isaiah 42:8: "I am the LORD, that is My name; And My glory I will not give to another, Nor My praise to carved images."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• Connection to the New Testament: The Septuagint translates Yahweh as Kyrios, the very title the New Testament writers apply to Jesus Christ. This directly identifies Jesus with the covenant God of Israel.

• The Theology of Lament: Habakkuk's prayer demonstrates that addressing God by His personal name allows believers to express deep, honest frustration without falling into irreverence or apostasy.

• Divine Self-Sufficiency: The name stands as a constant reminder that God's actions are determined by His own nature, not by human performance or merit.
