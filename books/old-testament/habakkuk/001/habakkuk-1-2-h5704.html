LONG - ( 'ad , Strong's H5704 )

Summary:

The Hebrew preposition 'ad fundamentally denotes a boundary, limit, or duration, commonly translated as "until," "as far as," or "up to." When combined with the interrogative particle 'anah (meaning "whither" or "where"), it forms the poignant exclamation "how long?" ('ad-'anah). In Habakkuk 1:2, this expression captures the prophet's agonizing sense of duration and boundary-stretching delay as he cries out to God amidst unchecked societal wickedness, expressing the tension between human suffering in time and divine timing.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The literal root of 'ad points to the concept of progress, passage, or advancing up to a specific spatial or temporal boundary line. It sets a limit that marks the transition from one state or place to another. In the ancient Near Eastern mindset, time was not merely an abstract sequence of seconds and minutes, but a series of events bounded by God's sovereign appointments. Asking "until when" ('ad-'anah) is a cry that asks where the boundary line of suffering lies. It implies that the current state of affliction must have an end point ordained by the Creator, and expresses a desperate desire to see that boundary reached.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The preposition 'ad functions primarily as a marker of a spatial or temporal limit.

• In spatial contexts, it means "as far as," "even to," or "up to."

• In temporal contexts, it means "until," "while," "during," or "as long as."

• When paired with the interrogative 'anah, it becomes a temporal interrogative idiom meaning "until when?" or "how long?"

• It signifies an extension of time that has reached or is reaching its absolute limit, carrying a sense of urgency, exhaustion, and expectancy.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, 'ad is used to mark physical boundaries (such as the borders of Israel's inheritance) and temporal limits (such as the duration of a king's reign or a period of judgment). The word picture is that of a traveler walking toward a destination until they reach the boundary line. In prayer and lament literature, 'ad paired with 'anah creates a linguistic boundary-marker for pain. It is the language of a watchman scanning the horizon, asking when the darkness of night will finally give way to the dawn.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, 'ad reminds us that God is the sovereign ruler over both time and limits. He sets the boundary to the prosperity of the wicked and the suffering of the righteous. The cry "how long?" is a legitimate expression of biblical faith, not skepticism. It recognizes that God is the only one who can bring an end to a trial. This word bridges the gap between human limitation—experiencing time linearly and painfully—and divine eternity, where every delay serves a sovereign purpose.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 1:2, the prophet looks at the violence (chamas) and injustice running rampant in Judah. He has been crying out for help, but there seems to be no divine response. The use of 'ad-'anah ("how long") expresses a double burden: the burden of witnessing unchecked sin and the burden of God's apparent silence. The nuance here is one of extreme emotional and spiritual exhaustion. The boundary of Habakkuk's human endurance has been pushed to its absolute limit, prompting him to ask where God's boundary of patience with Judah's sin lies.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Numbers 14:11 - "Then the LORD said to Moses: 'How long will these people reject Me? And how long will they not believe Me, with all the signs which I have performed among them?'"

• Psalm 13:1 - "How long, O LORD? Will You forget me forever? How long will You hide Your face from me?"

• Job 19:2 - "How long will you torment my soul, and break me in pieces with words?"

• Genesis 28:15 - "Behold, I am with you and will keep you wherever you go, and will bring you back to this land; for I will not leave you until I have done what I have spoken to you."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Septuagint (LXX) translates 'ad-'anah in Habakkuk 1:2 using heōs pote, which literally means "until when?" This is the same phrase Jesus uses in the Gospels when addressing His unbelieving generation (e.g., Matthew 17:17: "How long shall I be with you? How long shall I bear with you?"). This demonstrates that the cry of 'ad is a thread running from Old Testament laments to the lips of Christ Himself, highlighting the struggle with human unbelief and the yearning for the consummation of God's righteous kingdom.
