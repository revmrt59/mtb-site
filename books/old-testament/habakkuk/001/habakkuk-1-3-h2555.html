VIOLENCE - ( chamas , Strong's 2555 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word chamas represents cold-blooded, unethical injustice, physical violence, and societal disruption that violates both human rights and God's covenantal law. In Habakkuk 1:3, it captures the prophet's agonizing cry over the unchecked lawlessness, oppression, and destruction running rampant within Judah, presenting a stark challenge to God's justice and demanding divine intervention.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The historical and cultural background of chamas root-concept points to a violent overstepping of established boundaries. It is not merely physical strength or energy used forcefully, but force exerted in direct defiance of moral, ethical, and legal standards. It pictures a society where the powerful exploit the weak, tearing down the protective boundaries of God's covenant. This word conjures the image of a roaring torrent or a wild beast that tears apart anything in its path, leaving ruin, fear, and chaos behind. It is the raw, unrestrained assertion of human will over others, destroying the peace (shalom) that God intends for His creation.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun chamas signifies unethical violence, severe damage, social injustice, and physical or moral outrage. It describes a violation of divine and human law through ruthless self-assertion. The semantic range of the word includes:

• Cruel and unjust physical force or assault.

• Systemic oppression, legal corruption, and economic exploitation.

• False and damaging testimony designed to ruin another's life (malicious witness).

• Ethical injury or wrong committed against a neighbor.

Unlike legitimate force used to maintain order, chamas is always wrongful, toxic, and destructive to covenantal relationships.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The term chamas first appears in the Genesis account of the Flood, where the pre-flood world is described as being "filled with violence." This usage establishes chamas as the ultimate symptom of human depravity and rebellion against God, necessitating catastrophic divine judgment. Throughout the Old Testament, the word is frequently paired with terms for plundering (shod), deceit, and bloodshed. It is pictured as a garment that wicked people wear, an appetite they seek to satisfy, or a weapon they brandish against the innocent. It is also used to describe the "fists of wickedness" and the vocalized lies of false witnesses who seek to destroy others in court.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, chamas is the direct antithesis of God's character and His desire for humanity. Yahweh is a God of justice (mishpat) and righteousness (tsedaqah), whereas chamas represents the absolute breakdown of both.

• It grieves the heart of God: The presence of chamas in the land defiles it and invites God’s swift and terrible wrath.

• It paralyzes the law: When chamas reigns, the vulnerable are left unprotected, and the righteous are surrounded by the wicked, rendering God's instructions ineffective in daily life.

• It demands a divine response: God cannot remain silent in the presence of chamas. The cry of the oppressed under the weight of chamas ascends to heaven, obligating the righteous Judge of the earth to act.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 1:3, chamas carries a deeply painful and specific nuance. Habakkuk is not looking at a pagan, foreign nation when he cries out; he is looking at his own countrymen in Judah. The prophet stands in the midst of a covenant community that has abandoned the Torah. He sees "plundering and violence" (shod wə-chamas) happening openly, yet God appears to be silent. The word chamas here highlights the internal moral rot of God's people. It is a cry of utter bewilderment: "Why do You show me iniquity, and cause me to see trouble?" Habakkuk uses chamas as a legal and moral indictment, questioning how a holy God can tolerate such blatant lawlessness and oppression among His chosen people.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 6:11: "The earth also was corrupt before God, and the earth was filled with violence."

• Psalm 72:14: "He will redeem their life from oppression and violence; And precious shall be their blood in His sight."

• Proverbs 4:17: "For they eat the bread of wickedness, And drink the wine of violence."

• Micah 6:12: "For her rich men are full of violence, Her inhabitants have spoken lies, And their tongue is deceitful in their mouth."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the ultimate demonstration of God's redemptive plan, the Messiah stands as the perfect contrast to chamas. Isaiah 53:9 prophesies of the Suffering Servant, stating that He would be buried with the rich, "Although He had done no violence, Nor was any deceit in His mouth." Jesus Christ took upon Himself the full weight of human chamas on the cross, absorbing the ultimate expression of human violence and injustice to purchase eternal peace and righteousness for His people.
