STRIFE - ( rib , Strong's H7379 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word rib represents strife, dispute, or controversy, often carrying a strong legal or lawsuit connotation in the Old Testament. In Habakkuk 1:3, it describes the social and moral breakdown of Judah, where interpersonal conflicts, systemic injustice, and hostile contentions replace the peace of God's covenant. It refers not merely to verbal arguments, but to a state of adversarial hostility and legalistic oppression that tears apart the fabric of the community, prompting the prophet's agonizing cry for divine intervention.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The background of rib is rooted in the ancient Near Eastern legal and judicial sphere. While it can refer to a simple verbal quarrel, its primary word picture is that of a formal dispute, accusation, or lawsuit—frequently referred to as a "covenant lawsuit." It depicts two adversaries standing before a judge or engaging in a public confrontation where rights are demanded, accusations are hurled, and relational harmony is completely shattered. It is the visual of an adversarial arena where peaceful coexistence has been replaced by formal, hostile, and aggressive litigation.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun rib means strife, dispute, controversy, or lawsuit. It is derived from the verbal root rub, which means to strive, contend, complain, or conduct a legal case. The semantic range of the noun includes:

• Interpersonal strife, bickering, and quarreling among individuals.

• Social and civil controversies that require arbitration.

• Formal judicial complaints and lawsuits brought before judges or elders.

• Metaphorical legal controversies, such as God prosecuting His covenant people for their unfaithfulness.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

In the Old Testament, rib is used to describe three main types of conflict:

• Interpersonal Quarreling: It is used of physical or verbal disputes between individuals, such as the friction between Abraham's herdsmen and Lot's herdsmen over land and water resources.

• Civil and Judicial Disputes: It represents formal legal cases brought before Israel's leaders. In this context, the rib is the legal case itself that must be heard, weighed, and judged righteously.

• The Divine Lawsuit: The prophets frequently use rib to describe God's legal controversy with Israel. Because Israel violated the Sinai Covenant, Yahweh acts as the prosecuting attorney and judge, bringing a formal rib (charge or complaint) against His people.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, the presence of rib is a primary indicator of spiritual and moral decline.

• The Loss of Shalom: God's ideal for His covenant people is shalom—wholeness, peace, and right relationships. When a society turns away from God, shalom is broken, and rib (adversarial strife) inevitably takes its place.

• The Weaponization of Justice: In a corrupt society, rib ceases to be a tool for seeking true justice and instead becomes a weapon. The powerful use legal disputes and corrupt lawsuits to exploit the poor, the widow, and the fatherless.

• Evidence of a Hardened Heart: Enduring strife and constant litigation show a refusal to submit to the law of love and the authority of God's Word, replacing covenant faithfulness with selfish ambition and hostile self-assertion.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 1:3, the prophet looks at his own society in Judah and cries out, "Why do You show me iniquity, and cause me to see trouble? For plundering and violence are before me; there is strife (rib), and contention arises."

• The context is the internal collapse of Judah, likely during the reign of the wicked King Jehoiakim. The Torah has become paralyzed, and justice is perverted because the wicked surround the righteous (v. 4).

• The nuance of rib in this specific verse is the systemic, litigious abuse occurring within the nation. The wealthy and powerful are engaging in predatory legal disputes against their fellow citizens.

• Instead of a community bound by covenant love and mutual support, Judah has become a battleground of hostile lawsuits, social exploitation, and domestic warfare. The "strife" Habakkuk sees is not just domestic bickering, but the systematic tearing apart of the community through corrupt legal battles and adversarial hostility.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Habakkuk 1:3, rib is paired with the Hebrew word madon (contention). While madon emphasizes the emotional heat, bickering, and internal drive to argue, rib emphasizes the structural, outward, and adversarial clash. Together, they form a hendiadys representing total relational breakdown. The internal heart of anger (madon) naturally translates into external, destructive legal and social warfare (rib). When a nation's relationship with God is broken, its internal relationships disintegrate into a chaotic cycle of anger and litigation.
