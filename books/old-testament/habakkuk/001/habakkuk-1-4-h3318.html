PROCEEDS - ( yatsa , Strong's H3318 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yatsa fundamentally means to go out, come forth, or emerge from a specific source. In Habakkuk 1:4, it describes the tragic outcome of a compromised legal and social system. Instead of true justice going forth to protect the vulnerable, what "proceeds" or issues from the courts is a twisted, perverted counterfeit. The term highlights the active, visible manifestation of a corrupt society, where public actions and legal verdicts inevitably reflect the wicked character of those in power.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• yatsa is a high-frequency Hebrew verb used over one thousand times in the Old Testament. Its primary physical movement is exiting a boundary, such as leaving a tent, departing a city, or the sun rising (going forth) in the sky. It conveys the movement from an internal, unseen domain to an external domain where it becomes visible, active, and impactful. In legal and royal contexts, it refers to the issuing of decrees, the pronouncement of verdicts, or the marching forth of an army. What is conceived in private chambers or within the heart "goes forth" into the public square to shape the lives of the people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning is to go out, come out, come forth, or proceed. In the Qal stem, as used in Habakkuk 1:4, it denotes the action of coming forth or emerging. When applied to abstract concepts like law or justice, it refers to the official pronouncement, execution, or practical application of these principles in society. It describes the tangible output of the judicial system. It represents the transition from a standard of law written on scrolls to its active enforcement in daily life.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The verb is used to describe the Exodus, the definitive historical "going out" of Israel from Egyptian bondage.

• It is used metaphorically for words coming out of a mouth, signaling that speech is the outward expression of internal thoughts.

• It represents water flowing out of a spring, indicating a continuous, natural stream.

• In judicial settings, it depicts a verdict leaving the judge's bench and entering the lives of the citizens. In Habakkuk 1:4, the word picture is of a polluted stream. True justice is blocked from flowing, and in its place, a distorted, crooked stream of judgments pours out into the land.

4. Theological Implications

God is a God of justice, and His law is intended to "go forth" to bring order, life, and righteousness to society. When a nation rejects God's covenant, the moral and legal order collapses. The "proceeding" of perverted judgment is a sign of deep spiritual decay, showing that external societal corruption is the direct product of internal spiritual rebellion. Humanity cannot remain neutral; some form of "judgment" will always proceed. If it is not God's righteous justice, it will inevitably be a twisted human counterfeit.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 1:4, yatsa is used twice, creating a powerful literary contrast. First, Habakkuk laments that "justice never goes forth." The mechanism of divine justice is paralyzed because the law is ignored and powerless. Second, the prophet declares that "perverse judgment proceeds." Instead of true justice, a mutated, distorted version of justice exits the courts. The wicked surround the righteous, and the very legal systems designed to protect the innocent are now actively producing crooked verdicts. The verb "proceeds" emphasizes that corruption is not passive; it is actively marching out and dominating Judah.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Deuteronomy 8:3: "...but man lives by every word that proceeds from the mouth of the LORD."

• Isaiah 2:3: "For out of Zion shall go forth the law, And the word of the LORD from Jerusalem."

• Isaiah 55:11: "So shall My word be that goes forth from My mouth; It shall not return to Me void..."

• Jeremiah 9:3: "For they proceed from evil to evil, And they do not know Me,” says the LORD."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The word mishpat (justice/judgment) is the subject of yatsa in both clauses of Habakkuk 1:4. In the first instance, it is true justice; in the second, it is qualified as me'uqqal (perverted, crooked). This demonstrates how the same action of "proceeding" can be hijacked by human sin. The Greek Septuagint translates yatsa in Habakkuk 1:4 with the verb exerchomai, which means "to go out" or "come forth," highlighting the public, outward movement of these legal pronouncements.
