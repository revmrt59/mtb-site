PERVERSE - ( aqal , Strong's H6127 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word aqal literally means to bend, twist, or deviate from a straight line. In Habakkuk 1:4, it describes judgment and justice that have been warped, distorted, and thoroughly perverted by the corrupt leaders of Judah. Instead of acting as a straight, level standard of divine truth, the judicial system is twisted to favor the wicked, leaving the righteous oppressed and the law paralyzed.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient Hebrew thought, abstract concepts are almost always rooted in concrete, physical realities. The verbal root aqal refers to the physical act of bending, curving, or twisting something out of its natural, straight alignment. It evokes the image of a bent rod, a crooked path, or a warped piece of wood that can no longer serve its structural purpose. While God's standard of holiness and law is always represented as a straight, level, and upright highway, human sin bends and warps this path. A perverse thing is not merely slightly off-course; it is physically and morally deformed, presenting a crooked reality where there should be absolute straightness.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb aqal appears in Habakkuk 1:4 in the intensive passive Pual form (me'uqqal), functioning as a participle. In this grammatical state, it expresses a completed and intense condition of being "perverted," "bent," "wrested," or "made crooked." The word indicates a deliberate and systemic distortion of what is inherently right. Lexically, it signifies the subversion of truth and equity. When applied to legal or moral decisions, it means to actively bend the law to serve corrupt ends, transforming the straight path of divine justice into a twisted maze of exploitation.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The verbal root aqal is exceptionally rare, appearing only here in the Hebrew Old Testament. However, its closely related cognates provide vivid color to its meaning. The feminine noun aqallath is used in Isaiah 27:1 to describe the winding, twisting, and writhing motion of the fleeing serpent, Leviathan. Additionally, the plural noun aqalqalloth occurs in Judges 5:6 and Psalm 125:5, referring to "crooked ways" or winding, treacherous bypasses that travelers take to avoid danger. Together, these cognates paint a powerful word picture of a slithering snake or a bypass that avoids the main highway. Perverse justice behaves like a serpent—slippery, evasive, and dangerous—evading the straightforward demands of the truth in order to strike at the innocent.

4. Theological Implications

Justice is not a human invention; it is a direct reflection of God's character and covenant. The law was given to Judah to keep the nation on a straight moral path. When those in power twist the law, they are not merely committing a civil infraction; they are committing a theological offense. They are actively distorting the character of God before the eyes of the world. Because God is straight and upright, any perversion of justice is an act of spiritual rebellion that desecrates His name, misrepresents His holiness, and ruptures the covenant relationship.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 1:4, the prophet looks out at his society and cries out in deep grief. The law has become paralyzed and ineffective, unable to restrain evil. The righteous are surrounded and outnumbered by the wicked. Consequently, "perverse judgment proceeds." The word me'uqqal modifies judgment. The very instrument that was designed to straighten out society's moral failures has itself been warped. The judges and rulers of Judah, who were called to enforce God's straight standard, are instead using their authority to twist the truth. The ruling is crooked; the verdict is bent. Instead of protecting the vulnerable, the legal system has been weaponized to exploit them.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

Because aqal is a hapax legomenon in the Hebrew Bible, its translation in the Greek Septuagint (LXX) uses the verb diastrepho (Strong's G1294), which means to distort, turn aside, or pervert. This same Greek word is utilized in the New Testament to capture the spiritual reality of moral crookedness:

• Matthew 17:17 - "Then Jesus answered and said, 'O faithless and perverse generation, how long shall I be with you? How long shall I bear with you? Bring him here to Me.'"

• Acts 13:10 - "and said, 'O full of all deceit and all fraud, you son of the devil, you enemy of all righteousness, will you not cease to pervert the right ways of the Lord?'"

• Acts 20:30 - "Also from among yourselves men will rise up, speaking perverse things, to draw away the disciples after themselves."

• Philippians 2:15 - "that you may become blameless and harmless, children of God without fault in the midst of a crooked and perverse generation, among whom you shine as lights in the world;"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The biblical remedy for crookedness is always divine alignment. Throughout Scripture, the coming of God's kingdom is described as a process of straightening what humanity has bent. Isaiah prophesied that when the glory of the Lord is revealed, the crooked places shall be made straight (Isaiah 40:4). Habakkuk's lament is answered not by God letting the crookedness slide, but by God bringing a sovereign, albeit shocking, correction through the Chaldeans. God will not allow His straight standards to remain permanently bent; if His people will not walk uprightly, He will send judgment to break the crooked structures they have built.
