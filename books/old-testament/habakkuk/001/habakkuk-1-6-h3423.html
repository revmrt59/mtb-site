POSSESS - ( yarash , Strong's H3423 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yarash carries the dual sense of dispossessing an existing inhabitant and taking possession of their property or land. In Habakkuk 1:6, it depicts the aggressive, violent seizure of foreign lands by the invading Chaldeans (Babylonians). While frequently used in the Old Testament to describe Israel inheriting the Promised Land by divine mandate, here it is used ironically and tragically: God raises up a ruthless, pagan nation to violently dispossess others of their homes as an instrument of His divine judgment.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• yarash is a root deeply tied to land, inheritance, and displacement. The word picture is not merely one of receiving a peaceful inheritance but often involves active displacement or driving out of the current occupants to claim what is theirs. It implies a transition of ownership that is physical, legal, and often military. When used of Israel, it is the covenantal taking of Canaan. When used of the Chaldeans in Habakkuk, it portrays a swift, predatory seizure of sovereign territory, emphasizing the disruption of established "dwelling places."

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meaning of yarash is to take possession of, inherit, or dispossess. In the Qal stem, it means to take possession of, inherit, or occupy. In the Hiphil (causative) stem, it frequently means to drive out, dispossess, or impoverish. In the context of Habakkuk 1:6, the verb is in the Qal infinitive construct (la-reshet), meaning "to take possession of" or "to seize." It denotes the physical occupation of land and homes through military conquest, stripping the rightful owners of their inheritance.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Legal Inheritance vs. Violent Seizure: In covenantal contexts, yarash is a gift from God (e.g., inheriting the land). In military contexts, it denotes the aggressive dispossession of an enemy.

• The Irony of Possession: In Habakkuk, the Chaldeans are not inheriting their own rightful land; they are taking possession of "dwelling places that are not theirs." The word picture highlights the violation of natural and divine boundaries by a nation that makes its own strength its god.

4. Theological Implications

• Sovereignty and Judgment: God is the one "raising up" the Chaldeans. The pagan nation's act of yarash (seizing/possessing) is ultimately under the sovereign oversight of Yahweh to discipline His covenant people.

• The Reversal of Blessing: Israel was given the land of Canaan to yarash (possess) it because of the wickedness of its former inhabitants. Now, due to Israel's own covenant unfaithfulness, God sends a bitter and hasty nation to yarash (possess) dwelling places, signaling a tragic reversal where Israel is the one being dispossessed.

• The Illusion of Human Ownership: The Chaldeans believe they are acquiring permanent possessions by their own power, but Habakkuk’s prophecy reveals that their conquest is temporary and subject to God's ultimate justice.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 1:6, the Chaldeans are described as "bitter and hasty" (or impetuous). Their march across the "breadth of the earth" is aimed at possessing dwellings that do not belong to them. The phrase "not theirs" (lo-lo) juxtaposed with yarash underscores the injustice and predatory nature of the Babylonian empire. While God uses them as His rod of anger, their greedy desire to possess what is not theirs constitutes a grievous sin that will eventually bring about their own doom (Habakkuk 2:6).

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 15:7 - "Then He said to him, 'I am the LORD, who brought you out of Ur of the Chaldeans, to give you this land to inherit [yarash] it.'"

• Numbers 33:53 - "you shall dispossess [yarash] the inhabitants of the land and dwell in it, for I have given you the land to possess [yarash] it."

• Deuteronomy 30:5 - "Then the LORD your God will bring you to the land which your fathers possessed, and you shall possess [yarash] it."

• Habakkuk 1:6 - "For indeed I am raising up the Chaldeans, A bitter and hasty nation, Who march through the breadth of the earth, To possess [yarash] dwelling places that are not theirs."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The root yarash can also mean "to become poor" or "impoverished" in certain stems (such as the Qal in Genesis 45:11 or Proverbs 20:13). This linguistic connection highlights that when one party takes possession (yarash), the other party is often left utterly destitute and impoverished (yarash). The Chaldean conquest would leave Judah completely empty-handed.
