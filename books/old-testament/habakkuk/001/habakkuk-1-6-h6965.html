RAISING - ( qum, Strong's H6965 )

Summary:

In Habakkuk 1:6, the word translated "raising up" is the Hebrew verb qum in its causative Hiphil stem, appearing as an active participle. In its root form, the word means to arise, stand up, or be established. In this specific context, the causative action denotes God actively bringing a nation onto the stage of world history to execute His sovereign will. Rather than the Babylonians ascending to power by mere geopolitical chance, Yahweh Himself is the active agent mobilizing this fierce and impetuous nation as an instrument of divine discipline against Judah.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root of qum evokes the physical action of moving from a state of inactivity, prostration, or sleep into an upright position of readiness and power. It is the posture of a warrior standing up for battle, a witness rising to testify in court, or a builder setting up a pillar that will stand firm. In the ancient Near Eastern context, the word carries a dynamic sense of initiation. It is not a passive existence but a decisive transition into action and authority.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of qum in the Qal stem is to arise, stand, or get up. However, in the Hiphil (causative) stem used in Habakkuk 1:6, the meaning shifts to "cause to stand," "erect," "set up," or "stir up." It is the standard Hebrew term for:

• Establishing or confirming a covenant or promise.

• Appointing, stirring up, or bringing a political or military leader onto the scene.

• Erecting physical structures, such as stones of remembrance or altars.

• Stirring up an adversary or agent of judgment to perform a specific historical task.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, qum is employed to describe both ordinary human movements and profound divine interventions.

• The Posture of Authority: When a leader or king is "raised up," it signifies that their authority is not self-generated but divinely authorized.

• Covenant Fidelity: God "establishes" (qum) His covenant, meaning He makes it stand firm and binding through history.

• Providential Mobilization: When God "raises up" an adversary, the word picture is that of God lifting a sleeping giant or commanding a silent force to stand to its feet and march under His orders.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of qum in the prophetic books is immense. It stands as a powerful defense of absolute divine sovereignty. History is not a series of random, chaotic events, nor is it governed by the autonomous wills of pagan kings. When God "raises up" the Chaldeans, it demonstrates that even the most wicked, self-determined empires are ultimately instruments in the hand of Yahweh. They rise because He bids them stand; they fall when He withdraws His support. This reveals that God's holiness and justice will be vindicated, even if He must employ a wicked nation to discipline His covenant people.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 1:6, the prophet has just complained about the rampant injustice, violence, and lawlessness in Judah. God's response is shocking: "For indeed I am raising up the Chaldeans..." The use of the active participle indicates an imminent, ongoing, and certain action. God is currently at work behind the scenes, stirring up this bitter and hasty nation. The nuance here is one of divine agency behind human agency. The Chaldeans believe they are marching by their own strength and for their own glory (Habakkuk 1:11), but qum reveals that their historical rise is entirely authored by God to answer the prophet's cry for justice.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 6:18 - "But I will establish My covenant with you; and you shall go into the ark—you, your sons, your wife, and your sons’ wives with you."

• Deuteronomy 18:15 - "The LORD your God will raise up for you a Prophet like me from your midst, from your brethren. Him you shall hear,"

• Judges 2:16 - "Nevertheless, the LORD raised up judges who delivered them out of the hand of those who plundered them."

• Amos 6:14 - "But behold, I will raise up a nation against you, O house of Israel,” Says the LORD God of hosts..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A critical pastoral insight from qum is the tension between divine sovereignty and human responsibility. Though God "raises up" the Chaldeans for His purposes, He does not condone their evil. Habakkuk 2 makes it clear that the Chaldeans will eventually be judged for their pride and cruelty. God can orchestrate the rise of an empire to accomplish a temporal purpose of discipline without being the author of their sin. For the believer, this provides deep comfort: no hostile power rises in our lives apart from God's sovereign oversight, and He remains in control of both the beginning and the end of their influence.
