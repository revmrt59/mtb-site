DIGNITY - ( se'eth , Strong's H7613 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun se'eth carries the literal meaning of rising, elevation, or lifting up, deriving from the root verb nasa (to lift up). In contexts of personal stature or royal authority, it represents dignity, majesty, eminency, or high rank. In Habakkuk 1:7, it describes the self-assumed, arrogant majesty of the invading Chaldeans (Babylonians), whose sense of justice and authority originates entirely from their own prideful self-determination rather than God's law.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The word se'eth is rooted in the common Hebrew verb nasa, which means "to lift up," "to carry," or "to bear."

• A concrete word picture of se'eth is a physical rising or swelling. In the levitical laws of leprosy, it designates a raised swelling or eruption on the skin.

• When applied metaphorically to human stature, character, or position, the word shifts from physical elevation to social and moral elevation. It represents the "lifting up" of a person's head, rank, or position above others, conveying the idea of natural dignity, royal majesty, or an exalted standing.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun se'eth denotes several distinct concepts depending on its context:

• Elevation of rank, high dignity, preeminence, or majesty.

• Exaltation or acceptance (as in a lifted, bright countenance rather than a fallen, downcast one).

• A physical swelling, rise, or tumor on the body.

• Pride, self-exaltation, or an inflated sense of self-importance.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Excellency of Birthright: In Genesis 49:3, Jacob uses se'eth to describe his firstborn, Reuben, as "the excellency of dignity." Here, it pictures the natural, elevated honor and priority that belonged to the firstborn son in patriarchal culture.

• Divine Majesty: In the book of Job, the word describes the overwhelming, terrifying majesty of God. God's se'eth is so high and exalted that it strikes awe and fear into the hearts of mortals (Job 13:11, Job 31:23).

• Arrogant Autonomy: In Habakkuk 1:7, the word takes on a dark, negative connotation. The Chaldean empire "lifts up" its own majesty, operating as its own supreme authority. Their dignity is not a gift from God, nor is it subject to divine law; it is a self-generated, prideful exaltation.

4. Theological Implications

• True dignity and majesty belong inherently to God. When human beings or nations possess genuine se'eth, it is a derivative gift meant to reflect God's order and justice.

• When humanity cuts itself off from God, se'eth quickly degenerates into sinful pride and self-deification. The Babylonians became their own source of standard, law, and grandeur.

• The narrative of Habakkuk serves as a warning against the illusion of self-made majesty. Any dignity or power that "proceeds from itself" rather than from the Creator is inherently unstable, diseased, and marked for divine judgment.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Habakkuk 1:7, the prophet is warned about the rise of the ruthless Chaldeans: "They are terrible and dreadful; Their judgment and their dignity proceed from themselves."

• The pairing of "judgment" (mishpat) and "dignity" (se'eth) is highly significant. Normally, justice and dignity are grounded in God's covenantal law.

• By stating that their dignity "proceeds from themselves," the text highlights the absolute lawlessness and tyrannical self-reliance of the Babylonians. They do not bow to any transcendent moral framework. They define their own greatness, establish their own rules of engagement, and elevate themselves by crushing other nations. Their "dignity" is actually an abusive, predatory supremacy.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 4:7 - "If you do well, will you not be accepted? And if you do not do well, sin lies at the door. And its desire is for you, but you must rule over it." (Here, se'eth is translated as "accepted," referring to the lifting up of Cain's fallen countenance).

• Genesis 49:3 - "Reuben, you are my firstborn, My might and the beginning of my strength, The excellency of dignity and the excellency of power."

• Job 13:11 - "Will not His majesty make you afraid, And His dread fall upon you?"

• Job 31:23 - "For destruction from God is a terror to me, And because of His magnificence I cannot endure."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• There is a striking medical-to-spiritual parallel in the use of se'eth. In Leviticus 13, the word is used repeatedly for an inflammatory skin "swelling" indicative of leprosy.

• In a spiritual sense, the self-asserted "dignity" of the Babylonians in Habakkuk 1:7 functions like a leprosy of the soul. It is an abnormal, prideful swelling of the ego. What the world views as imperial grandeur and majestic elevation, God views as a diseased, toxic inflammation that must be isolated and judged.
