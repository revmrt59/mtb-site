HASTENS - ( chush , Strong's H2363 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb chush conveys rapid, urgent, and swift movement, representing an immediate or hurried action. In Habakkuk 1:8, it describes the terrifying and relentless speed of the invading Chaldean (Babylonian) army, comparing their cavalry to an eagle swooping down rapidly to devour its prey. This word highlights the unstoppable and imminent nature of divine judgment executed through human agents, emphasizing that when God moves in judgment, it occurs with sudden, breathtaking speed.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The verb chush depicts physical acceleration and emotional urgency. It is the rapid beating of wings, the swift gallop of horses, or the desperate rush of someone seeking immediate rescue. In the ancient Near East, military campaigns relied heavily on the psychological impact of speed. An army that could strike swiftly before defenses could be organized was highly feared. The use of chush in the context of an eagle or vulture descending on its food evokes a vivid picture of a predator that does not hesitate, hover, or delay, but drops from the sky with lethal velocity.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb chush means to hasten, make haste, hurry, or act quickly. In the Qal stem, it denotes physical swiftness or a rapid state of transition, such as events rushing toward fulfillment or a person hurrying to perform an action. In the Hiphil stem, it carries the causative sense of causing haste, quickly showing up, or urging someone to speed up. In poetic and prophetic literature, it emphasizes the suddenness of impending events, whether they are acts of deliverance or the execution of judgment.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Swooping Predator: The word is used metaphorically for birds of prey descending upon their food (Habakkuk 1:8; Job 9:26). The focus is on the straight, unhindered, and rapid trajectory of the hunt.

• The Urgent Plea: In the Psalms, chush is frequently used in prayers of lament, where the psalmist cries out to God to "make haste" to help or deliver them. It reflects human desperation met by the hope of sudden divine intervention.

• The Swiftness of Judgment: It describes the rapid approach of destiny, doom, or prophetic fulfillment, showing that once God sets His decrees in motion, they advance without delay.

4. Theological Implications

• The Sovereign Timing of God: While human beings often perceive God’s timing as slow, chush reminds us that when God acts, He does so with absolute promptness and perfect speed. Judgment, though long-delayed by His patience, falls swiftly once the cup of iniquity is full.

• The Reality of Divine Discipline: God utilizes the rapid rise of worldly empires to accomplish His sovereign purposes. The swiftness of the Chaldeans was not merely a geopolitical event but a divinely ordained instrument of rapid chastisement upon a disobedient Judah.

• The Urgency of Faith: In contrast to the swiftness of judgment, God's people are called to be swift in obedience (as in Psalm 119:60). Delay in obeying God is a form of spiritual danger.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 1:8, the prophet receives a shocking answer from Yahweh. Habakkuk has complained about the lawlessness in Judah, and God responds that He is raising up the Chaldeans—a "bitter and hasty nation." The description of their cavalry is filled with velocity: "swifter than leopards," "more fierce than evening wolves," and "they fly as the eagle that hastens to eat."

The word chush here underscores the absolute futility of Judah's defense. The invasion will not be a slow, drawn-out siege that allows for easy escape, but a sudden, violent strike. Just as an eagle targets its prey and drops instantly to consume it, the Babylonian empire will rapidly swallow up the southern kingdom of Judah. It conveys terror, inevitability, and swift destruction.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Deuteronomy 32:35: "Vengeance is Mine, and recompense; Their foot shall slip in due time; For the day of their calamity is at hand, And the things to come hasten upon them."

• Psalm 22:19: "But You, O Lord, do not be far from Me; O My Strength, hasten to help Me!"

• Psalm 119:60: "I made haste, and did not delay To keep Your commandments."

• Psalm 70:5: "But I am poor and needy; Make haste to me, O God! You are my help and my deliverer; O Lord, do not delay."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between God's apparent silence and His sudden action is a major theme in Habakkuk. Habakkuk began by asking "How long?" (Habakkuk 1:2), feeling that God was slow to act. God's reply using chush demonstrates that when His judgment finally arrives, it will happen with such alarming speed that the prophet will be left astonished. This teaches the believer that God's patience must never be mistaken for indifference or inability to act.
