VIOLENCE - ( chamas , Strong's H2555 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word chamas represents cold-blooded, unethical, and destructive violence that violates both the laws of God and the rights of humanity. It is not merely physical force, but a systematic, malicious violation of moral boundaries, often manifesting as oppression, injustice, cruelty, and social devastation. In Habakkuk 1:9, it depicts the single-minded, rapacious focus of the invading Chaldeans (Babylonians), whose entire agenda and driving force is destructive conquest, pillage, and the utter disregard for human life and divine law.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root of chamas carries the idea of doing wrong, treating violently, or acting unjustly. It is distinct from the legitimate, judicial use of force, representing instead a wild, lawless aggression that disrupts the created order. Historically, the word is associated with the pre-Flood world's total moral collapse, where the earth was filled with violence. The background of the word evokes a picture of a tearing or plucking away of what belongs to another, leaving behind ruin, grief, and terror.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meaning of chamas is violence, wrong, harm, injustice, or cruelty. It denotes cold-blooded, unethical injury done to another's person, property, or rights. The term encompasses both physical bloodshed and systemic societal injustice, where those in power crush the weak and vulnerable. It represents a flagrant breach of the covenant relationship and cosmic order, characterizing actions that are deliberately hostile, destructive, and malicious.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Cry of the Oppressed: Chamas is frequently used in the context of a victim crying out for rescue, signaling that a moral boundary has been crossed and justice must intervene.

  • Cold-Blooded Cruelty: It is pictured as a weapon or a garment (e.g., "garment of violence"), showing how an individual or nation can wrap themselves in ethical defiance and predatory behavior.

  • Ethical and Social Ruin: It is paired with deceit, robbery, and bloodshed, painting a word picture of a society where truth has perished and raw power has replaced the law of God.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, chamas is the antithesis of mishpat (justice) and tsedaqah (righteousness). God hates chamas because it defiles the earth and harms those made in His image. Scripturally, the presence of unchecked chamas triggers divine judgment. God's response to systemic violence is to sweep it away, as seen in the narrative of the Flood and the eventual destruction of oppressive empires like Assyria and Babylon. It serves as a reminder that human lawlessness directly invites the active wrath and corrective justice of a holy God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 1:9, chamas describes the motivation and methodology of the oncoming Chaldeans. Habakkuk had originally complained to God about the chamas running rampant within Judah (Habakkuk 1:2). In a shocking display of divine sovereignty, God responds that He is raising up the Chaldeans—a nation whose very essence and purpose is chamas. When the text says, "They all come for violence," it means the entire Babylonian war machine is fueled by a ruthless desire to plunder, dominate, and destroy. Their drive is absolute, unchecked by conscience, and directed solely toward self-aggrandizement. The irony is tragic and heavy: God uses an instrument of supreme, international chamas to judge the domestic chamas of His covenant people.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 6:11: "The earth also was corrupt before God, and the earth was filled with violence."

  • Psalm 11:5: "The Lord tests the righteous, But the wicked and the one who loves violence His soul hates."

  • Psalm 72:14: "He will redeem their life from oppression and violence; And precious shall be their blood in His sight."

  • Proverbs 4:17: "For they eat the bread of wickedness, And drink the wine of violence."

  • Habakkuk 1:2: "O Lord, how long shall I cry, And You will not hear? Even cry out to You, 'Violence!' And You will not save."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In modern Hebrew, the root of chamas still strongly carries the classical biblical connotation of cruelty, injustice, and violent robbery. This highlights the enduring linguistic association of the root with terror and lawless devastation across millennia.
