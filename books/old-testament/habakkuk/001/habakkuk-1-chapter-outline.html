Habakkuk 1 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Habakkuk 1 opens with the prophet’s honest complaint that violence and perverted justice fill Judah while God seems not to hear or save, and God answers that He is raising up the Chaldeans, a swift and ruthless conqueror. That answer leads Habakkuk to a second complaint, asking how the eternal Holy One, whose eyes are too pure to behold evil, can stay silent while the wicked devour those more righteous than they.

Chapter Sections

1. Habakkuk 1:1–4 — Habakkuk’s First Complaint: How Long, O LORD?

The book opens as “The burden which the prophet Habakkuk saw” (Habakkuk 1:1), and Habakkuk cries, “O LORD, how long shall I cry, And You will not hear?” (Habakkuk 1:2). He asks why God makes him see iniquity, plundering, violence, and strife, so that the law is powerless and justice never goes forth because the wicked surround the righteous.

2. Habakkuk 1:5–11 — God Raises Up the Chaldeans

The LORD tells Habakkuk to look among the nations and be utterly astounded, for He will work a work the prophet would not believe: “For indeed I am raising up the Chaldeans” (Habakkuk 1:6). He describes a bitter and hasty nation whose horses are swifter than leopards, a people bent on violence that scoffs at kings and seizes every stronghold, yet transgresses by ascribing its power to its god.

3. Habakkuk 1:12–17 — Habakkuk’s Second Complaint: Why Is the Holy One Silent?

Habakkuk appeals to the everlasting LORD, his Holy One and Rock, confident that “We shall not die” (Habakkuk 1:12) and that God has appointed the Chaldeans for judgment, yet he asks why God, whose eyes are too pure to behold evil, holds His tongue while the wicked devours one more righteous than he, catching people like fish in a net and sacrificing to that net. The chapter ends with his question still unanswered: will the conqueror keep emptying his net and slaying nations without pity (Habakkuk 1:17)?
