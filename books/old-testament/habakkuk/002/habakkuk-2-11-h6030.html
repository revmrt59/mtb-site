ANSWER - ( 'anah , Strong's H6030 )

Summary:

In the Old Testament, the Hebrew verb 'anah primarily means to answer, respond, or testify. In the dramatic prophetic context of Habakkuk 2:11, it describes a responsive echo of judgment where the very building materials of a house built on blood and injustice testify against its builder. Rather than a normal conversational reply, the word here denotes a legal and cosmic witness—a responsive outcry that echoes the accusation of the victims, ensuring that hidden sins are exposed before God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root 'anah has ancient Near Eastern parallels that relate to responding, paying attention, or providing a corresponding statement. The concrete imagery behind the word involves a dynamic interaction where one voice calls out and another responds in kind. In a judicial or legal setting, it points to testifying or giving witness in response to a charge. In Habakkuk 2:11, the word picture is highly dramatic and poetic: the stones of the wall cry out in distress or accusation, and the wooden beams of the ceiling immediately answer, confirming and echoing the charge. This personification creates a courtroom-like atmosphere within the very walls of the oppressor's palace.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb 'anah occurs frequently in the Hebrew Scriptures and carries several primary meanings depending on its context:

  • To answer or respond verbally to a question or statement.

  • To testify, bear witness, or respond to a formal accusation in a legal setting.

  • To respond in song or shout in a liturgical or celebratory context.

  • To pay attention, heed, or grant an answer to a prayer (frequently used of God answering human pleas).

In the context of Habakkuk 2:11, the meaning is a responsive testimony, where the timber answers the stone in a back-and-forth chorus of condemnation.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The range of 'anah highlights the inevitability of response. When God speaks, creation must answer. When a legal charge is made, a witness must testify. The word picture is not merely conversational; it carries the weight of a formal echo or a corresponding reaction. In ancient construction, the stone walls and the wooden beams represented the structural integrity of a house. For these inanimate elements to "answer" one another is a vivid word picture of structural rebellion; the very house built by oppression refuses to remain silent, turning its own structural cohesion into a public testimony against the wicked builder.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, 'anah in this context underscores the omniscience and justice of God. Sins committed in secret, or under the guise of national security and architectural grandeur, cannot be hidden. The doctrine of retribution is built into the creation itself. If human voices are silenced by fear or death, inanimate creation will find a voice to answer on behalf of the oppressed. This aligns with biblical truths where the earth itself cries out or where stones would cry out if praise is silenced. The cosmos is moral, and it will eventually answer and testify to the reality of human actions.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:11, the prophet is pronouncing woes against the Chaldeans who built their empire through plunder, violence, and forced labor. The specific "woe" in verses 9–11 condemns the one who "covets evil gain for his house" to set his nest on high. The builder thinks his elevated, fortified palace secures him from disaster. However, the nuance of 'anah here reveals that his security is an illusion. The stone in the wall "cries out" (denoting an outcry against violence), and the wooden rafters "answer" it. The building itself becomes a hostile witness stand. The very materials bought with the blood of conquered peoples join in a dreadful, antiphonal chorus of judgment.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 30:33 – "So my righteousness will answer for me in time to come, when you come concerning my wages..."

  • Exodus 20:16 – "You shall not bear [answer as] false witness against your neighbor."

  • Job 14:15 – "You shall call, and I will answer You; You shall desire the work of Your hands."

  • Isaiah 58:9 – "Then you shall call, and the LORD will answer; You shall cry, and He will say, 'Here I am.'"

  • Micah 6:3 – "O My people, what have I done to you? And how have I wearied you? Testify [answer] against Me."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The dynamic between the "stone" and the "beam" in Habakkuk 2:11 foreshadows Jesus' words in Luke 19:40, where He states that if His disciples keep silent, "the stones would immediately cry out." In both testaments, creation possesses a latent voice ready to testify to God's glory or His impending judgment. The responsive nature of 'anah reminds the reader that no action occurs in a vacuum; every act of injustice initiates a moral echo that must eventually be answered.
