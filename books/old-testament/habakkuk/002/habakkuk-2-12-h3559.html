ESTABLISHES - ( kun , Strong's H3559 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb kun conveys the core concept of setting something up, preparing it, or making it firm, stable, and enduring. In the context of Habakkuk 2:12, it describes the futile human effort to secure and solidify an empire—specifically Babylon—through violence, injustice, and oppression. While mankind seeks to establish its own legacy apart from God, the passage warns that any security built on unrighteousness is inherently unstable and destined for divine judgment, as only what God establishes will ultimately stand.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root meaning of kun is to be erect, upright, or aligned. The physical imagery is that of setting up a pillar, laying a firm foundation, or leveling a structure so that it is perfectly upright and balanced. It is the picture of taking something that is loose, formless, or weak and making it rigid, stable, and permanent. In ancient near-eastern culture, building a city or a palace was not merely an engineering feat; it was a religious and political assertion of permanence and sovereignty. To "establish" a city was to secure one's name, lineage, and power for generations to come.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb kun occurs frequently in the Old Testament, expressing the action of making something firm, fixed, or secure. In the Po'lel stem (the specific grammatical form used in Habakkuk 2:12), the verb is highly active and causative, meaning "to set up," "to erect," "to found," or "to establish." It describes the deliberate, intensive action of bringing something into an enduring, unshakeable state of existence. It denotes not just a temporary setup, but a permanent fixing of status, position, or structure.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, kun is used to describe a variety of foundational realities:

  • Creation: God "established" the earth and the heavens, laying their foundations so they cannot be moved (Psalm 24:2; Proverbs 3:19).

  • Dynasties and Kingdoms: God promises to "establish" the throne of David forever, guaranteeing its security and perpetuity (2 Samuel 7:13).

  • Personal Walk and Heart: The righteous person's steps are "established" by the Lord (Psalm 37:23), and believers are exhorted to "prepare" or "establish" their hearts toward God (1 Samuel 7:3).

  • Contrast of Foundations: The word sets up a direct contrast between the enduring work of God's hands and the fragile, temporary works of human pride.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, kun reveals that true stability and permanence belong to God alone and to what He chooses to secure. When God establishes something—be it the cosmos, His covenant, or His people—it is unshakeable. Conversely, when human beings attempt to "establish" their lives, fortunes, or societies apart from God, they engage in a form of idolatry. This passage warns that human effort, no matter how powerful or physically imposing, cannot create lasting security when it bypasses God's moral laws. True establishment is a divine prerogative; human self-establishment is an illusion.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:12, the prophet issues a series of "woes" against the Chaldean empire. Babylon was the superpower of the day, rapidly expanding its borders, accumulating wealth, and constructing monumental architecture. The text states: "Woe to him who builds a town with bloodshed, who establishes a city by iniquity!" The Chaldeans were literally and figuratively trying to "establish" (kun) their empire—rendering it permanent and secure—by exploiting, conquering, and murdering other nations. The nuance here is deeply ironic: the very means they used to make their empire permanent (bloodshed and iniquity) guaranteed its collapse. According to God's moral order, a structure built on sin has a rotten foundation; it cannot stand, and the labor of those who build it will ultimately feed the fire (v. 13).

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 2 Samuel 7:13: "He shall build a house for My name, and I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever."

  • Psalm 90:17: "And let the beauty of the Lord our God be upon us, and establish the work of our hands for us; yes, establish the work of our hands."

  • Proverbs 3:19: "The Lord by wisdom founded the earth; by understanding He established the heavens;"

  • Proverbs 16:12: "It is an abomination for kings to commit wickedness, for a throne is established by righteousness."

7. Other Relevant Insights

For pastoral application, this word study provides a powerful diagnostic tool for the human heart. Modern people rarely build physical cities through literal bloodshed, but they frequently attempt to "establish" their lives, careers, and families using compromised means—such as deceit, manipulation, greed, or the exploitation of others. This text reminds us that the foundation of our labors matters just as much as the height of our achievements. If we build our lives on unrighteousness, our "established" security is nothing but a house of cards. We must trust God to establish the work of our hands (Psalm 90:17) by walking in obedience and integrity.
