INIQUITY - ( awlah , Strong's H5766 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word translated as "iniquity" in Habakkuk 2:12 refers to injustice, unrighteousness, and moral perversion, particularly in the context of social oppression and unethical gain. In Habakkuk’s prophecy, it exposes the wicked foundation of the Babylonian empire, which sought to establish its security and glory through the violent exploitation of conquered nations. This word serves as a sobering reminder that any human endeavor built on systemic injustice stands under the immediate judgment of a holy God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root of this noun carries the physical concept of bending, deviating, or distorting what is straight. Where righteousness is represented as a straight, level path, this word depicts a path that has become warped, crooked, or twisted. In the ancient Near East, the stability of a society depended upon maintaining straight standards of justice and fair measurements. When a society or leader deviated from this divine standard, they committed this form of iniquity. It is a visual picture of structural distortion—a building constructed with crooked lines that is structurally unsound and destined to collapse.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term denotes injustice, unrighteousness, wickedness, or dishonesty. It refers to a violation of moral rightness, especially in civil, legal, or social actions. Rather than a private, internal sin, it describes the active perversion of justice that harms others, often through violence, deceit, or political exploitation. It represents the opposite of equity and fair dealing, manifesting as systemic abuse where the powerful exploit the vulnerable.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, the term is frequently paired with concepts of dishonest commerce, corrupt courtrooms, and violent oppression.

  • It is used of dishonest scales and unjust measurements in trade, where a merchant alters the balances to cheat the poor.

  • It describes corrupt judges who show partiality, perverting the law to favor the wealthy.

  • The most vivid word picture, however, is architectural. The word represents the toxic mortar used to construct human kingdoms. When empires are built on the suffering and stolen labor of others, the very stones and beams of the buildings are stained with this moral distortion.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of this term rests on the character of God. The Lord is entirely free from any form of this unrighteousness. Because His character is the absolute standard of straightness, He is the defender of the oppressed and the judge of all crooked systems. Human progress is never ethically neutral; God evaluates not only what humanity builds, but how they build it. Any peace, prosperity, or civilizational achievement attained through the violation of God's moral law is an abomination to Him and cannot endure.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:12, the prophet pronounces a series of five "woes" against the Chaldeans (Babylon). In this third woe, Habakkuk cries, "Woe to him who builds a town with bloodshed, / And establishes a city by iniquity!" The Babylonians were master builders, famous for the hanging gardens and massive walls of Babylon. However, God pulls back the curtain of imperial splendor to reveal the foundation: slave labor, pillaged wealth, and the blood of conquered peoples. The "iniquity" here is the systemic, structural injustice that financed Babylon’s architectural marvels. The context warns that empires built on exploitation contain the seeds of their own destruction; the very materials of their cities will testify against them.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Leviticus 19:15 – "You shall do no injustice in judgment. You shall not be partial to the poor, nor honor the person of the mighty. In righteousness you shall judge your neighbor."

  • Deuteronomy 32:4 – "He is the Rock, His work is perfect; For all His ways are justice, A God of truth and without injustice; Righteous and upright is He."

  • Micah 3:10 – "Who build up Zion with bloodshed And Jerusalem with iniquity;"

  • Ezekiel 28:18 – "You defiled your sanctuaries By the multitude of your iniquities, By the iniquity of your trading; Therefore I brought fire from your midst; It devoured you, And I turned you to ashes upon the earth In the sight of all who saw you."

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a striking and tragic parallel between Habakkuk 2:12 and Micah 3:10. While Habakkuk uses this terminology to condemn the pagan empire of Babylon, Micah uses the exact same phrasing to condemn the leaders of Judah. This demonstrates the absolute impartiality of God's justice. God does not excuse systemic oppression when it is committed by His covenant people; in fact, their covenant status makes their exploitation of the vulnerable even more egregious.
