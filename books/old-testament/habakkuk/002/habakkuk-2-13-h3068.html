LORD - ( YHWH , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name YHWH, translated as "LORD" in all capital letters in the NKJV, is the sacred, personal, and covenantal name of the one true God of Israel. Derived from the Hebrew verb meaning "to be," it reveals God as the self-existent, eternal, and unchanging One who actively relates to His creation and keeps His promises. In Habakkuk 2:13, this supreme name is coupled with "hosts" (Sebaoth), demonstrating that the ultimate futility of proud, pagan empires is sovereignly orchestrated by the self-existent, almighty Covenant God of Israel.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • YHWH is the tetragrammaton, the sacred four-letter consonant name of God. Out of deep reverence for its holiness, Jewish tradition avoided pronouncing the name aloud, substituting Adonai ("Lord") during public reading. This practice is reflected in modern English translations by rendering the name as "LORD" in capital letters.

  • The historical background of the name is deeply anchored in Exodus 3:14, where God identifies Himself to Moses as "I AM WHO I AM" (ehyeh-aser-ehyeh). This reveals a God who is not dependent on anyone or anything else for His existence, power, or purpose. He is the ultimate reality, the constant and reliable presence who acts in history.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The name represents the supreme, personal name of the deity of Israel.

  • Etymologically, it is connected to the causative active form of the Hebrew verb hayah or havah ("to be" or "to become").

  • The lexical definition denotes self-existence, eternal presence, and immutability. As the self-existent Creator, He is the source of all being, life, and history. He is the One who is, who was, and who is to come.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • In the Old Testament, the name YHWH is used over 6,800 times, making it the most frequent designation for God.

  • Unlike generic titles for deity like Elohim (which emphasizes God's power and role as Creator), YHWH is His personal, covenantal name. It denotes intimacy, relationship, and faithful commitment to His people.

  • The name is frequently combined with other terms to create descriptive compound titles. In Habakkuk 2:13, it appears as YHWH Sebaoth (the LORD of hosts), depicting God as the commander of heavenly armies, the sovereign ruler over all earthly and cosmic forces.

4. Theological Implications

  • Sovereignty and Transcendence: Because YHWH is self-existent, all human empires exist only by His permission and under His sovereign control. Their rise and fall are subject to His eternal decrees.

  • Covenant Faithfulness: The name guarantees that God will fulfill His promises. No matter how dark the immediate circumstances look (such as the impending Babylonian invasion in Habakkuk's day), YHWH remains faithful to His covenant with His people.

  • Futility of Idolatry: The self-existent YHWH stands in stark, mocking contrast to lifeless idols made by human hands. He acts, speaks, and sovereignly directs history, while idols are silent, powerless, and empty.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Habakkuk 2, God pronounces woes upon the proud, predatory Chaldean (Babylonian) empire.

  • In verse 13, Habakkuk declares: "Behold, is it not of the LORD of hosts That the peoples labor to feed the fire, And nations weary themselves in vain?"

  • The contextual nuance is that the exhausting, monumental efforts of pagan empires to build great cities and monuments are destined to end in fire and futility because YHWH has decreed it. Human striving apart from and against the covenant God of Israel is ultimately empty. The destiny of nations is not determined by their military or economic might, but by the sovereign will of YHWH Sebaoth, who guarantees that the earth will eventually be filled with the knowledge of His glory (v. 14).

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 3:15 - "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

  • Psalm 23:1 - "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

  • Isaiah 42:8 - "I am the LORD, that is My name; And My glory I will not give to another, Nor My praise to carved images."

  • Malachi 3:6 - "For I am the LORD, I do not change; Therefore you are not consumed, O sons of Jacob."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • In the Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint / LXX), YHWH is translated as Kurios, which translates to "Lord." This Greek term is applied directly to Jesus Christ in the New Testament (e.g., Philippians 2:11), signaling that Jesus is the incarnate YHWH of the Old Testament, possessing the same self-existence, authority, and covenant-keeping nature.
