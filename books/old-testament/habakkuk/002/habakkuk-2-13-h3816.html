NATIONS - ( le'om , Strong's H3816 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun le'om refers to a nation, people group, or community of individuals bound together by common political, geographical, or ancestral ties. Often used in poetic parallel with other terms for peoples and nations, it carries a collective sense of human societies organized apart from God. In Habakkuk 2:13, the plural form of this word highlights the ultimate futility of human endeavors when great empires and global communities exhaust themselves building kingdoms destined for destruction, contrasting the fleeting glory of human nations with the sovereign, enduring purposes of the Lord of hosts.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun le'om derives from an unused root meaning to gather or associate. It portrays a group of people gathered together under a common banner, ruler, or social order. It is a poetic term used frequently in Hebrew poetry and prophetic literature to depict the geopolitical groupings of humanity. The word picture is one of collective human strength—societies organizing, building, and cooperating to establish their own legacy, defense, and security on the earth. It represents human civilization structured into distinct national identities.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun le'om designates a nation, folk, or people. It refers to a collective political or national group, often contrasted with individual persons or tribes. In its plural form, le'ommim, it encompasses the various Gentile nations of the world. The word carries a semantic range that includes a specific nation (such as Israel or Edom), foreign peoples, or the collective global community of mankind outside of the covenant people of God.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

In the Old Testament, le'om is frequently paired in parallel structure with 'am (people) or goy (nation).

  • It is used to describe the sovereignty of God over the geopolitical landscape, showing that God rules the nations righteously.

  • It is depicted as raging or plotting against the Lord, representing humanity's collective rebellion against divine authority.

  • It conveys the image of a vast, roaring sea, representing the turbulent, chaotic nature of human empires acting independently of their Creator.

4. Theological Implications

The word highlights the limitations of human sovereignty. No matter how powerful, organized, or technologically advanced a nation (le'om) becomes, it remains subject to the supreme authority of Yahweh. It underscores the vanity of human achievement apart from God. The collective labor of the nations to build enduring monuments and empires is exposed as empty and temporary when it does not align with God’s kingdom. Ultimately, it points toward the eschatological gathering of all nations under the Messiah's rule, where the chaotic striving of human communities is replaced by the peaceful, righteous government of God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:13, le'om (in the plural le'ommim, "nations") is used in parallel with "peoples" ('ammim). This verse occurs within a series of woes directed against the proud, aggressive Babylonian Empire. Babylon was conquering and subjugating numerous nations to build its vast empire through forced labor, bloodshed, and exploitation. Habakkuk exposes the tragic irony of this imperial ambition: the nations are exhausting themselves to build cities and empires that will ultimately be consumed by fire and vanish into nothingness. The nuance here is the absolute futility of pagan, humanistic efforts to build lasting security and glory apart from God. The intense activity of the world's great powers is ultimately empty and self-destructive under the judgment of the Lord of hosts.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 25:23 - "Two nations are in your womb, Two peoples shall be separated from your body; One people shall be stronger than the other, And the older shall serve the younger."

  • Psalm 2:1 - "Why do the nations rage, And the people plot a vain thing?"

  • Psalm 44:2 - "You drove out the nations with Your hand, But them You planted; You afflicted the peoples, and cast them out."

  • Proverbs 14:34 - "Righteousness exalts a nation, But sin is a reproach to any people."

  • Isaiah 43:9 - "Let all the nations be gathered together, And let the people be assembled."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between God's eternal kingdom and the temporary kingdoms of the nations is a major theme throughout prophetic literature. The word highlights that no national entity is immune to spiritual reality; sin degrades a nation, while righteousness exalts it. The exhaustion described in Habakkuk ("weary themselves") uses a Hebrew verb suggesting painful, dry, gasping labor, illustrating how painful and exhausting it is for nations to work against the grain of God's sovereign will.
