KNOWLEDGE - ( yada , Strong's H3045 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word yada, translated as "knowledge" in Habakkuk 2:14, represents an experiential, relational, and comprehensive recognition of God rather than mere intellectual awareness. In the context of this prophetic vision, it describes a future reality where the entire earth will be fully and undeniably aware of the majestic presence and sovereign authority of Yahweh, completely overshadowing the futile, temporary works of human empires.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient Near Eastern thought, knowledge was not an abstract, academic concept. While the Western mind, heavily influenced by Greek philosophy, often defines knowledge as the mental apprehension of facts, the Hebrew concept of yada is deeply relational, experiential, and holistic. To know something is to experience it, to care for it, and to be intimately acquainted with it.

The background of the word spans a variety of everyday human experiences. It is used to describe the intimate union between husband and wife, the hands-on expertise of a skilled craftsman, and the deep, mutual commitment of a covenant relationship. When applied to God, this knowledge is never passive. To know God means to live in active, faithful obedience to Him, recognizing His character and responding to His saving deeds with worship and loyalty.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The root verb yada means to perceive, find out, discern, distinguish, and know by experience. In Habakkuk 2:14, the word appears in the infinitive construct form (lada'at), functioning as a noun meaning "the knowing" or "the knowledge."

The lexical range of the term includes:

  • Experiential awareness: Learning or understanding through direct, personal encounter.

  • Covenantal recognition: Acknowledging the authority and rights of a king or deity.

  • Intimate acquaintance: Deep, personal relationship that shapes one's character and actions.

  • Practical wisdom: Knowledge that manifests in ethical living and justice.

Thus, the "knowledge of the glory of the LORD" is not a theological theory that people will simply agree with; it is a direct, overwhelming experience of God’s manifest presence that demands absolute surrender and worship.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Scripture employs yada in several vivid ways to paint a picture of its depth:

  • The Marital Bond: Used to describe the most intimate human relationship, highlighting that true knowledge is relational and creative.

  • Covenant Loyalty: Used of a king recognizing his loyal subjects, or Israel recognizing Yahweh as their only true King. It carries the weight of commitment and chosenness.

  • The Saturated Sea: In Habakkuk 2:14, the knowledge of God's glory is compared to water covering the sea. This is a powerful word picture of completeness, depth, and inescapability. Just as there is no part of the seabed untouched by the ocean, there will be no corner of the earth untouched by the experiential reality of who God is.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, yada in the prophets points to the ultimate goal of redemptive history. God does not merely want people to know about Him; He desires them to know Him personally.

  • The End of Idolatry: The absolute, universal knowledge of Yahweh's glory leaves no room for the silent, powerless idols manufactured by human hands.

  • The Hope of the New Covenant: This prophetic expectation aligns with the promise of the New Covenant, where no one will need to say to his neighbor, "Know the LORD," because all will know Him, from the least to the greatest.

  • The Sovereign Display of Glory: God reveals Himself through both judgment and salvation so that humanity will come to this saving, reverent knowledge of His name.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2, the prophet is wrestling with the rise of the violent, proud Chaldeans (Babylonians). God responds with a series of five "woes" against the exploiters, idolaters, and empire-builders.

In verse 13, God points out the futility of human ambition: "Behold, is it not of the LORD of hosts that the peoples labor only for fire, and the nations weary themselves for nothing?"

Immediately following this picture of human exhaustion and vanity, verse 14 provides the divine contrast. While man’s proud cities burn and turn to ash, God's kingdom will endure and expand. The Babylonian empire sought to fill the earth with its own terrifying fame and violent power. God declares that their efforts are empty, for the earth will instead be filled with the experiential knowledge of His own weight, beauty, and majesty (kabod). The nuance here is a promise of ultimate justice and restoration: the oppressive systems of the world will fade, and the entire cosmos will be brought into harmonious, worshipful alignment with its Creator.

  • Psalm 46:10 - "Be still, and know that I am God; I will be exalted among the nations, I will be exalted in the earth!"

  • Jeremiah 31:34 - "No more shall every man teach his neighbor, and every man his brother, saying, 'Know the LORD,' for they all shall know Me, from the least of them to the greatest of them, says the LORD."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A beautiful parallel exists between Habakkuk 2:14 and Isaiah 11:9. While Isaiah writes, "For the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the LORD," Habakkuk inserts the word "glory" (kabod), writing, "the knowledge of the glory of the LORD."

This addition emphasizes the visual, heavy, and majestic manifestation of God's presence. In the Old Testament, the "glory" of God was the visible manifestation of His holy presence, such as the cloud that filled the Tabernacle. Habakkuk’s prophecy assures the reader that what was once confined to the Holy of Holies will one day break out and saturate the entire created order.
