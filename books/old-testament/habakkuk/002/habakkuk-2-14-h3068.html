LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh (LORD) is the personal, covenantal, and self-existent name of the God of Israel. Revealed to Moses at the burning bush, it signifies God’s eternal presence, unchangeable character, and absolute fidelity to His promises. In Habakkuk 2:14, this name stands in stark contrast to the fleeting empires of men, assuring believers that the sovereign Creator and Redeemer will ultimately cover the earth with His glory, fulfilling His eternal covenant.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The name Yahweh is the sacred Tetragrammaton—the four-letter Hebrew name (YHWH) for the Creator. It is derived from the Hebrew verb hayah, which means "to be" or "to exist." This name is deeply connected to God's self-revelation to Moses in Exodus 3:14: "I AM WHO I AM." Unlike generic titles for deity, such as Elohim (which emphasizes God's power and role as Creator) or Adonai (which emphasizes His mastership and lordship), Yahweh is God's intensely personal name. It paints a picture of a God who is not distant or abstract, but one who actively enters into human history, binds Himself to His people by a solemn covenant, and remains eternally present with them.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name Yahweh means "the self-existent One" or "the One who causes to be." It denotes absolute, independent existence. God does not depend on anything outside of Himself for His life, power, or resources. He is the uncaused Cause, the eternal Present, possessing life in Himself. Lexically, this name signifies the faithful, covenant-keeping God of Israel. It represents the fullness of His character: His holiness, justice, mercy, truth, and immutability. When the Hebrew text uses this name, it points to the One who is faithful to His word, reliable in His judgments, and unwavering in His love for His people.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Yahweh is the most frequently used divine name in the Old Testament, occurring over 6,800 times. Throughout Scripture, this name is paired with vivid word pictures that describe God’s relationship with His people:

  • The Shepherd: The One who guides, feeds, and protects His flock.

  • The Rock: The immutable, strong foundation upon which His people can safely stand.

  • The Shield: The active defender who wards off the attacks of the enemy.

  • The Husband: The One who binds Himself to His people in deep, exclusive love.

  • In contrast to the mute, lifeless idols carved by human hands (described in Habakkuk 2:18-19), Yahweh is portrayed as the living, acting God who speaks, judges, and reigns from His holy temple.

4. Theological Implications

The theology of the name Yahweh centers on God’s sovereignty, self-sufficiency, and covenant faithfulness.

  • Covenant Faithfulness: Because God is Yahweh, He cannot break His word. His promises of redemption and restoration are as secure as His eternal existence.

  • Absolute Sovereignty: He governs the rise and fall of nations. Empires like Babylon may think they rule by their own strength, but they are merely instruments in the hand of Yahweh.

  • Exclusivity: There is no other god besides Him. He does not share His glory with carved images or worldly powers. His name demands exclusive worship and ultimate allegiance.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:14, the prophet proclaims: "For the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD, as the waters cover the sea." This declaration occurs in the middle of a series of severe "woes" pronounced against the proud, violent, and predatory Babylonian empire. Babylon built its kingdom on bloodshed, plunder, and the worship of false idols.

The use of Yahweh in this context is highly intentional. It reassures the weeping prophet and the anxious remnant of Judah that the wicked actions of Babylon will not have the final word. The labors of the godless nations will vanish like smoke, but the purposes of Yahweh will stand forever. The glory that will fill the earth is not the fleeting, blood-soaked glory of Babylon, but the eternal, holy glory of the covenant God of Israel. Just as water completely saturates every crevice of the ocean floor, so the presence and reign of Yahweh will one day completely saturate and rule over the entire creation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 3:15: "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

  • Psalm 23:1: "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

  • Isaiah 42:8: "I am the LORD, that is My name; and My glory I will not give to another, nor My praise to carved images."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint), Yahweh was translated as Kurios ("Lord"). This same title, Kurios, is applied directly to Jesus Christ in the New Testament (such as in Philippians 2:9-11). When the New Testament writers declare that "Jesus is Lord," they are identifying Jesus of Nazareth with Yahweh of the Old Testament. Furthermore, the ultimate fulfillment of Habakkuk 2:14 is realized in the New Covenant and the return of Christ, when the entire creation will be liberated from decay, and the dwelling place of God will be with humanity forever.
