GLORY - ( kabod , Strong's H3519 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word kabod denotes weight, substance, splendor, and honor. In Habakkuk 2:14, it represents the manifest presence, majestic splendor, and supreme reputation of Yahweh. While the world is currently filled with the temporary, hollow empires of men built on vanity and violence, God promises a coming day when the entire earth will be saturated with the experiential knowledge of His heavyweight majesty and ultimate reality, as universally and completely as water covers the ocean floor.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The root verb is kabad, which means to be heavy, weighty, or burdensome.

  • In the ancient Near East, value, significance, and wealth were measured by physical weight, especially when dealing with precious metals like gold and silver.

  • Consequently, kabod carries the conceptual idea of someone or something having "weight" or "substance." An honorable person is someone of weight and influence, whereas a worthless, wicked person is light, empty, and easily blown away like chaff.

  • When applied to Yahweh, the word picture conveys that God is the ultimate "heavyweight" of the universe. His character, presence, and sovereign authority possess infinite gravity, importance, and worth.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Kabod refers to abundance, wealth, honor, splendor, dignity, and glory.

  • In reference to God, it signifies the majestic brightness, awesome power, and visible manifestation of His holy character and presence. It is the outward shining of His inward, perfect being.

  • Rather than an abstract or static attribute, kabod is an active, impactful, and tangible reality that demands awe, reverence, and submission from creation.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Throughout the Old Testament, the kabod of God often manifested physically as a bright, consuming cloud (the Shekinah) that guided Israel through the wilderness, settled on Mount Sinai, and filled both the Tabernacle and Solomon's Temple (Exodus 40:34; 1 Kings 8:11).

  • It stands in sharp contrast to human glory, which is temporary, fragile, and eventually fades like the grass of the field.

  • The vivid word picture in Habakkuk 2:14 compares the spread of this glory to water covering the sea. Just as water completely saturates and leaves no part of the seabed dry, the knowledge of God’s majestic reality will one day saturate every corner of the earth, leaving no place untouched by His truth.

4. Theological Implications

  • Theology of Weight vs. Vanity: The book of Habakkuk contrasts the empty, lightweight labor of proud nations with the enduring weight of God's kingdom. Proud empires build their monuments on the backs of the vulnerable, but their efforts are hollow and destined for the fire. God's glory is the only true substance in the universe.

  • Eschatological Triumph: The promise of Habakkuk 2:14 guarantees a global transformation. History is not heading toward human self-destruction or the permanent triumph of evil empires, but toward the universal manifestation and recognition of Yahweh's sovereign majesty.

  • Experiential Knowledge: The text emphasizes the "knowledge" (da'at) of this glory. This is not mere intellectual awareness, but an experiential, relational, and saving acknowledgment of God’s supreme worth by all of humanity.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Habakkuk 2, God pronounces a series of five "woes" against the proud, predatory Babylonian Empire. Babylon sought to secure its own "glory" through violence, greed, and idolatry.

  • In verse 13, the Lord declares that human empires exhaust themselves for what will eventually burn up and become nothing.

  • Immediately following this exposure of human vanity, verse 14 introduces the divine alternative. The ultimate destiny of the world is not the glory of Babylon, Rome, or any modern superpower, but the filling of the earth with the heavy, inescapable weight of Yahweh’s majesty.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 33:18 - And he said, "Please, show me Your glory."

  • Psalm 19:1 - The heavens declare the glory of God; And the firmament shows His handiwork.

  • Isaiah 6:3 - And one cried to another and said: "Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts; The whole earth is full of His glory!"

  • Isaiah 40:5 - The glory of the LORD shall be revealed, And all flesh shall see it together; For the mouth of the LORD has spoken.

  • Ezekiel 43:2 - And behold, the glory of the God of Israel came from the way of the east. His voice was like the sound of many waters; and the earth shone with His glory.

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • In the Septuagint (the Greek translation of the Old Testament), kabod is translated as doxa, which in the New Testament carries the sense of divine majesty, brightness, and honor.

  • This Old Testament hope of the earth being filled with the glory of God finds its ultimate fulfillment in Jesus Christ, who is described as the "brightness of His glory and the express image of His person" (Hebrews 1:3).

  • The final culmination of this promise is seen in the New Jerusalem, which "had no need of the sun or of the moon to shine in it, for the glory of God illuminated it" (Revelation 21:23).
