COVER - ( kasah , Strong's H3680 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb kasah means to cover, conceal, overwhelm, or clothe, denoting a complete envelopment or saturation of one thing by another. In Habakkuk 2:14, it depicts a future reality where the earth is entirely engulfed and saturated by the experiential knowledge of Yahweh's glory, leaving no space untouched, just as the oceans completely submerge and fill the sea basins.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The primary word picture of kasah is that of a sweeping canopy, a thick garment, or an unstoppable flood that completely hides what lies beneath. In ancient Israel, to "cover" something was to assert dominance, to protect, or to change its visible character. When water covers the sea, there is no boundary between the water and the seabed; the water defines the sea. This paints a vivid picture of God's future kingdom: His glory will not merely visit the earth; it will drown out every rival power, leaving no dry ground for human pride.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb kasah is a basic Hebrew root meaning to cover, clothe, overwhelm, conceal, or keep secret. It appears approximately 152 times in the Old Testament, most frequently in the intensive Piel stem. It carries the weight of a thorough, complete covering that leaves the underlying object completely obscured or protected. Its semantic range covers literal actions, like covering oneself with a mantle, to metaphorical actions, like covering guilt or sin so that it is no longer visible to God’s judgment.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Physical Envelopment: It describes the cloud covering Mount Sinai (Exodus 24:15) or the tabernacle (Numbers 9:15), denoting the overwhelming, protective, and localized presence of God.

  • Destructive Submersion: It describes the waters of the deep rising up to overwhelm Pharaoh’s army (Exodus 15:5) or the global flood covering the mountains (Genesis 7:19). Here, the covering is an act of sovereign judgment and absolute erasure.

  • Merciful Concealment: In a spiritual sense, it describes the covering of sin (Psalm 32:1; Proverbs 10:12). To cover sin is to render it unseen and inactive, removing it from judicial sight.

4. Theological Implications

In biblical theology, kasah points to the ultimate victory of God’s sovereignty over creation. In Habakkuk, human history is marked by kingdoms trying to cover the earth with their own violence, monuments, and self-glory. However, God reserves the ultimate "covering" for Himself. The theological weight of this word in prophecy is eschatological: the current age, characterized by the hiding of God's face and the visible triumph of evil, will give way to an age where God's presence is as inescapable, heavy, and life-giving as the oceans.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:14, the prophet is speaking directly to a community trembling under the threat of the ruthless Babylonian empire. Babylon was expanding, "covering" nations with bloodshed and idolatry. God reassures His people that human empires labor only for the fire (verse 13). Their achievements will burn away, leaving nothing. In stark contrast, the earth will be filled with the knowledge of God's glory. The use of kasah here acts as an absolute comparison. The "sea" is not a dry basin waiting for water; the sea is water. Likewise, the earth will not merely have pockets of God's knowledge; the earth will be defined, saturated, and completely overtaken by the knowledge of His glory. Human tyranny will be drowned out by the absolute manifestation of Yahweh.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 7:19 - "And the waters prevailed exceedingly on the earth, and all the high hills under the whole heaven were covered."

  • Exodus 15:5 - "The depths have covered them; they sank to the bottom like a stone."

  • Numbers 9:15 - "Now on the day that the tabernacle was raised up, the cloud covered the tabernacle, the Tent of the Testimony..."

  • Psalm 32:1 - "Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered."

  • Proverbs 10:12 - "Hatred stirs up strife, but love covers all sins."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Septuagint, kasah in Habakkuk 2:14 is translated using the Greek verb kalypto (to cover, hide, veil). This Greek equivalent is the root of apokalyptein (to uncover, reveal, apocalypse). The irony is beautiful: what God covers (kasah) with His glory is simultaneously the ultimate unveiling (apocalypse) of His character to the cosmos.
