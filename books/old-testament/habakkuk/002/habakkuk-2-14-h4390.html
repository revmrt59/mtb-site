FILLED - ( māle' , Strong's H4390 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb māle' carries the primary meaning of being full, filling, or overflowing. In Habakkuk 2:14, it depicts a future reality where the earth is completely saturated, leaving no empty space, with the experiential knowledge of Yahweh's glory, just as waters cover the sea. This expresses a state of total, overwhelming completion and absolute divine saturation.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root māle' conveys a concrete, physical picture of a vessel filled to the brim with liquid, or a space packed so tightly that nothing else can fit. In the agricultural context of the Ancient Near East, this word described silos filled with grain, wells filled with water, or baskets filled with the harvest. It implies abundance, satisfaction, and the absolute absence of a void. When applied to God's presence or actions, it represents His sovereign occupation of a space where no competing element can remain.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb māle' occurs more than 250 times in the Old Testament and encompasses three primary semantic ranges depending on the grammatical stem:

  • To be full or become full (state of being).

  • To fill or overflow a space (active filling).

  • To fulfill, satisfy, or complete (as in a period of time, a vow, or a petition).

In Habakkuk 2:14, the verb appears in the Niphal (passive/reflexive) stem, meaning "will be filled" or "will fill itself." This indicates that the filling is a certain, divine action initiated and completed by God Himself, ensuring that the earth will be thoroughly occupied by His glory.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout Scripture, māle' is used in several rich contexts:

  • Physical Saturation: Jars filled with water (Genesis 24:16) or sacks filled with grain (Genesis 42:25).

  • The Temple and Tabernacle: The physical manifestation of God's presence—His glory—filling the Tabernacle (Exodus 40:34) and later the Temple (1 Kings 8:11), so that the priests could not stand to minister.

  • Emotional and Spiritual Capacity: Individuals being filled with anger (Esther 3:5), filled with the Spirit of God for craftsmanship (Exodus 31:3), or filled with wisdom (Exodus 28:3).

  • Temporal Completion: The time of pregnancy being completed (Genesis 25:24) or years of service being fulfilled (Genesis 29:21).

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of māle' in the Prophets is deeply eschatological. When God "fills" the earth, He is reclaiming His rightful domain.

  • The Reversal of the Fall: In Genesis 6:11, the earth was "filled" with violence. The prophetic hope is that the earth will instead be "filled" with the knowledge of God's glory.

  • The Global Temple: The imagery of God's glory filling the localized Temple is expanded to a global scale. The entire earth is destined to become the cosmic sanctuary of God, where His presence is manifest everywhere, without restriction or boundary.

  • Sovereign Certainty: The passive voice emphasizes that this is not a human achievement. Human empires weary themselves for nothing (Habakkuk 2:13), but God's plan to fill the earth is unstoppable.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:14, māle' stands in stark contrast to the hollow and temporary "filling" sought by the Babylonian empire. The Babylonians plundered nations to "fill" their houses with stolen goods (Habakkuk 2:6) and built cities on bloodshed, which ultimately ends in emptiness and fire (Habakkuk 2:13).

God’s filling is of an entirely different nature. The nuance here is one of absolute saturation. The prophet illustrates this with the simile: "as the waters cover the sea." The seabed is not merely damp; it is completely submerged, defined, and dominated by water. In the same way, the earth will be so thoroughly filled with the experiential knowledge of Yahweh's glory that no pocket of rebellion, ignorance, or idolatry will remain.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 6:11 - "The earth also was corrupt before God, and the earth was filled with violence."

  • Exodus 40:34 - "Then the cloud covered the tabernacle of meeting, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle."

  • Numbers 14:21 - "but truly, as I live, all the earth shall be filled with the glory of the LORD"

  • Psalm 72:19 - "And blessed be His glorious name forever! And let the whole earth be filled with His glory. Amen and Amen."

  • Isaiah 11:9 - "They shall not hurt nor destroy in all My holy mountain, for the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the LORD as the waters cover the sea."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament), māle' in Habakkuk 2:14 is translated using the Greek verb pimplemi, which means to fill to the brim or to influence fully. This connects the Old Testament hope of global filling to the New Testament reality of the Holy Spirit filling believers (Acts 2:4) and the ultimate destiny of Christ "filling all things" (Ephesians 4:10). The knowledge (da'at) that fills the earth is not mere intellectual assent, but an intimate, relational, and life-altering recognition of God's supreme authority and beauty.
