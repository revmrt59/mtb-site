LORD - ( YHWH , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name YHWH (traditionally pronounced Yahweh) is the personal, covenantal name of the one true God of Israel, revealed most profoundly to Moses at the burning bush. Derived from the verb hayah ("to be"), it points to God's self-existence, eternal unchangeableness, and absolute fidelity to His covenant promises. In the context of Habakkuk 2:16, this name represents the supreme sovereign who executes holy justice against the proud and oppressive Chaldeans, demonstrating that the covenant God of Israel is also the Lord of history and cosmic justice.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The name YHWH is the sacred Tetragrammaton, consisting of the four Hebrew consonants Yod, He, Waw, He. Out of deep reverence for the holiness of God, Jewish tradition substituted the word Adonai ("Lord") when reading the text aloud, a practice reflected in most English translations by rendering the name as "LORD" in capital letters. The background of this name lies in the self-revelation of God to Moses in Exodus 3:14: "I AM WHO I AM." It is not a generic title for deity (like Elohim), but a highly personal name indicating relational intimacy, faithfulness, and absolute independence. While pagan nations worshipped localized, temperamental gods, YHWH is the self-existent Creator who controls all nations and keeps His word forever.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name YHWH is derived from the archaic root hawah or hayah, meaning "to be," "to exist," or "to become." It is grammatically a third-person masculine singular imperfect form, meaning "He is," "He will be," or "He causes to be." It denotes the self-existent One who has life in Himself, possessing no beginning and no end. The core lexical meaning encompasses:

  • Self-existence and independence (aseity).

  • Eternal presence and unchangeability (immutability).

  • Covenant-keeping loyalty (faithfulness).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • YHWH is the most frequently used name for God in the Old Testament, occurring over 6,800 times. It is often coupled with specific attributes or metaphors to depict God’s relationship with His people:

  • YHWH Yireh (The LORD Will Provide)

  • YHWH Nissi (The LORD Is My Banner)

  • YHWH Shalom (The LORD Is Peace)

  • YHWH Sabaoth (The LORD of Hosts/Armies)

The name represents a rock-solid foundation of security for believers. In prophetic literature, it is often associated with the "cup of YHWH"—a metaphor for divine judgment, wrath, and retribution, which the wicked are forced to drink to the dregs.

4. Theological Implications

The theology of YHWH is deeply rooted in His sovereignty and covenant commitment.

  • Aseity: He depends on nothing outside of Himself for existence or power.

  • Covenant Faithfulness: When God uses YHWH, He is reminding His people of His promises to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Even when Israel is unfaithful, YHWH remains true to His name.

  • Cosmic Sovereignty: Though YHWH entered into a covenant with Israel, His authority is not localized. He is the supreme Judge of all the earth. The rise and fall of pagan empires like Babylon are entirely under His sovereign decree.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:16, the prophet addresses the arrogant Babylonian empire (the Chaldeans) who had greedily swallowed up nations and mocked other peoples. They forced others to drink to their shame. The tables are now turned by YHWH. The text says, "The cup of the LORD’s right hand will be turned against you." The nuance here is the absolute certainty of divine retribution. Babylon’s triumph was temporary; YHWH, the covenant keeper and sovereign Lord, is the one who hands them the cup of His wrath. The "right hand" symbolizes His omnipotent power to execute judgment. The use of YHWH (rather than a generic term like Elohim) assures Habakkuk and the suffering righteous that their covenant God has not abandoned them. The very God who pledged to protect Israel is the one actively dismantling their oppressors.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 15:6: "And he believed in the LORD, and He accounted it to him for righteousness."

  • Exodus 3:15: "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

  • Psalm 23:1: "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

  • Proverbs 18:10: "The name of the LORD is a strong tower; The righteous run to it and are safe."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The "cup of the LORD's right hand" in Habakkuk 2:16 finds its ultimate fulfillment and resolution in the New Testament. In the Garden of Gethsemane, Jesus Christ, the incarnate YHWH, prayed concerning "this cup" (Matthew 26:39). Instead of making His people drink the cup of His wrath, Jesus drank the cup of YHWH's right hand to the very dregs on the cross, taking the shame and judgment due to us, so that we might receive the cup of salvation. The transition from shame to glory in Habakkuk 2:16 contrasts with the believer's transition from shame to glory through the cross of Jesus Christ.
