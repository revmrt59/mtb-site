CUP - ( kos , Strong's H3563 )

Summary:

In the Old Testament, the cup (kos) is a common vessel for drinking, but it frequently serves as a powerful metaphor for a person's allotted portion in life, whether of blessing or, more frequently in prophetic literature, of divine judgment. In Habakkuk 2:16, the "cup of the LORD's right hand" represents the inevitable, overwhelming pouring out of God's wrath upon the proud and violent empire of Babylon. Having forced other nations to drink the cup of her own wrath and violence, Babylon is now forced to drink the cup of God's righteous retribution, resulting in total exposure, shame, and ruin.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • A kos is a relatively small, portable drinking vessel, typically made of earthenware or clay, though the wealthy and royal used cups crafted from bronze, silver, or gold.

  • In daily ancient Near Eastern life, the cup was filled by the host or the head of the household and distributed to the guests, representing their designated portion of food and drink.

  • This physical reality easily transitioned into a figurative expression. To "receive a cup" meant to accept one's divinely appointed lot or destiny, whether sweet (blessing and salvation) or bitter (judgment and suffering).

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The noun kos refers literally to a drinking vessel or cup.

  • In its literal sense, it is used for everyday drinking of water, wine, or other liquids.

  • In its figurative sense, it denotes the portion or destiny assigned to an individual or nation by God.

  • Specifically, in prophetic and poetic literature, it represents the cup of divine wrath—a metaphorical vessel filled with the wine of God's anger, which the wicked are forced to drink to the dregs, resulting in staggering, vulnerability, and public shame.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Cup of Blessing: In contexts of faith and thanksgiving, the cup represents God's abundant provision and salvation. The psalmist rejoices that his "cup runs over" (Psalm 23:5) and lifts up the "cup of salvation" (Psalm 116:13) as a testimony of thanksgiving.

  • The Cup of Wrath: Far more frequently in the prophets, the cup is filled with the wine of God's fury. The image is vivid: God offers a cup to the rebellious nations; as they drink, they become intoxicated, lose their strength, stagger, and fall in judgment.

  • The Cup of Retribution: The cup also operates on the principle of lex talionis (the law of retaliation). Those who forced others to drink the bitter cup of violence and oppression are themselves forced to drink the cup of God's judgment.

4. Theological Implications

  • The Sovereignty of God: God is the one who holds and dispenses the cup. Human history and the rise and fall of nations are not random; God measures out the exact portion of judgment that each wicked power deserves.

  • The Absolute Righteousness of Judgment: The cup signifies that judgment is not an arbitrary burst of anger, but a measured, poured-out consequence. The wicked drink exactly what their actions have brewed.

  • The Ultimate Cup Bearer: This Old Testament imagery of the cup of wrath directly prepares the way for the New Testament, where Jesus Christ prays in Gethsemane to let "this cup" pass from Him (Matthew 26:39), and ultimately drinks the cup of divine wrath on behalf of believers.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Habakkuk 2:15-16, God pronounces a woe against Babylon for her predatory behavior. Babylon is depicted as one who makes his neighbor drink, pouring out poison to make them drunk so as to gaze on their nakedness—a metaphor for stripping conquered nations of their dignity and glory.

  • In verse 16, the tables are turned. The "cup of the LORD’s right hand" is turned toward Babylon. The "right hand" symbolizes God's active power, authority, and execution of judgment.

  • Babylon, who forced others to drink her intoxicating cup of violence, must now drink from Yahweh's cup. The result of drinking this divine cup is that Babylon will be "filled with shame instead of glory" and "exposed as uncircumcised" (disgraced and rejected by God), showing that those who dishonor others will be utterly dishonored by God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Psalm 11:6 - "Upon the wicked He will rain coals; Fire and brimstone and a burning wind Shall be the portion of their cup."

  • Psalm 75:8 - "For in the hand of the LORD there is a cup, And the wine is red; It is fully mixed, and He pours it out; Surely its dregs shall all the wicked of the earth Drain and drink down."

  • Isaiah 51:17 - "Awake, awake! Stand up, O Jerusalem, You who have drunk at the hand of the LORD The cup of His fury; You have drunk the dregs of the cup of trembling, And drained it out."

  • Jeremiah 25:15 - "For thus says the LORD God of Israel to me: 'Take this wine cup of fury from My hand, and cause all the nations, to whom I send you, to drink it.'"

  • Ezekiel 23:31 - "You have walked in the way of your sister; therefore I will put her cup into your hand."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The turning of the cup (kos) in Habakkuk 2:16 also utilizes a powerful wordplay in Hebrew. The phrase translated "utter shame" (qiqalon) is a combination of qi (vomit) and qalon (shame/dishonor), indicating that Babylon will spew out her stolen glory in a state of shameful, drunken ruin under the influence of God's cup of judgment.

  • This prophetic motif of the cup of wrath reaches its ultimate climax in the New Testament book of Revelation, where those who worship the beast drink of "the wine of the wrath of God, which is poured out full strength into the cup of His indignation" (Revelation 14:10; 16:19).
