BE EXPOSED AS UNCIRCUMCISED - ( aral , Strong's H6188 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb aral represents the action of treating something as uncircumcised, forbidden, or unclean, and in its passive sense, to have one's uncircumcised state publicly exposed. In the context of biblical judgment, it denotes the stripping away of all worldly dignity to expose a person's true, covenant-less, and defiled spiritual condition before God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The physical act of circumcision was instituted by God as the sign of His covenant with Abraham and his descendants. It served as a physical marker of consecration, separation from pagan nations, and devotion to Yahweh. Conversely, to be uncircumcised was to be an outsider to the covenant promises, spiritually unclean, and profane. In the ancient Near East, forced public exposure was the ultimate humiliation for a defeated enemy. To expose someone's uncircumcised state was not only to shame them physically but to expose them as a godless pagan, stripped of all pride and standing under divine rejection.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb aral is a primitive root in biblical Hebrew that primarily means to leave uncircumcised, to treat as unclean or forbidden, or to expose the foreskin.

  • In the active voice, the verb refers to treating fruit from newly planted trees as forbidden or "uncircumcised" for the first three years.

  • In the passive voice (as seen in Habakkuk 2:16), it means to be exposed as uncircumcised, showing oneself to be outside of God's covenant and covered in shame.

  • Historically, some translations and textual traditions have associated a variant reading of this verb in Habakkuk with the root ra'al, which means to stagger or reel from intoxication. However, the Hebrew text uses the verb aral, drawing a deliberate connection between physical exposure, spiritual uncleanness, and ultimate humiliation.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The verb aral is utilized in two distinct word pictures in the Old Testament:

  • Agricultural Consecration: Newly planted trees in the Promised Land were treated as having "uncircumcised" fruit. For three years, their yield was considered forbidden, untouched, and ritually unclean, mirroring the status of an uncircumcised person who is not yet fit for holy use.

  • Judgment and Exposure: The proud and wicked, who hide their corruption behind a facade of majesty, are forced to drink the cup of God's wrath until they lose all control and expose their true, uncircumcised nature. This represents the total collapse of their self-made glory, exposing them as spiritually bankrupt and cut off from God's favor.

4. Theological Implications

  • The Revelation of True Character: God's judgment is not merely a penalty; it is an exposure. Proud, godless nations like Babylon may dress themselves in the garments of imperial glory, but divine judgment strips away these pretenses to reveal their true spiritual state as uncircumcised, unclean, and separate from God.

  • The Law of Retribution (Lex Talionis): Those who actively seek to humiliate, exploit, and expose others to shame will find themselves exposed to shame by the sovereign hand of God. The cup they forced on others will be handed back to them by Yahweh.

  • The Falsehood of Human Security: External strength, wealth, and military dominance cannot shield a person or nation from the reality of their spiritual standing before God. Apart from a covenant relationship with Him, all human achievement ends in nakedness and disgrace.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:15–16, the prophet delivers a powerful woe against the Babylonian empire for its predatory conquests. Babylon is depicted as an evil host who forces neighboring nations to drink, driving them to intoxication so that he can look upon their nakedness and mock them.

In verse 16, Yahweh reverses the roles. Babylon, filled with pride and temporary glory, is commanded to drink the cup of the Lord's right hand. By drinking this cup of wrath, Babylon will "be exposed as uncircumcised." The conqueror who took pleasure in exposing others is now publicly exposed. The shame they inflicted on others is poured back upon their own glory. The use of aral emphasizes that Babylon's ultimate defeat is not just military, but a profound spiritual exposure that reveals them as pagan outcasts who have no part in God's covenant.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

The verb aral appears in only two passages in the Hebrew Bible:

  • Leviticus 19:23 – "When you come into the land, and have planted all kinds of trees for food, then you shall count their fruit as uncircumcised. Three years it shall be as uncircumcised to you. It shall not be eaten."

  • Habakkuk 2:16 – "You are filled with shame instead of glory. Drink also, and be exposed as uncircumcised! The cup of the LORD’s right hand will be turned against you, And utter shame will be on your glory."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The Spiritual Heart: Throughout the Old Testament, the concept of uncircumcision is frequently applied metaphorically to describe a stubborn heart (Deuteronomy 10:16), deaf ears (Jeremiah 6:10), or uncircumcised lips (Exodus 6:12). In each case, it indicates a barrier that prevents a person from hearing, speaking, or receiving God's truth.

  • Textual Nuance: The Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament) translates the Hebrew phrase in Habakkuk 2:16 as "stagger" or "shake," likely reading the Hebrew letters in a slightly different order (ra'al instead of aral). This variant is also found in some Dead Sea Scrolls. While both readings fit the context of drinking a cup of wrath, the Masoretic Text's choice of aral ("be exposed as uncircumcised") provides a far more devastating theological critique, contrasting the superficial glory of Babylon with the permanent shame of being an unclean outsider.
