COVER - ( kasah , Strong's H3680 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb kasah fundamentally means to cover, clothe, conceal, or overwhelm. In the context of Habakkuk 2:17, it depicts a vivid and terrifying reversal of fortunes: the very violence that Babylon inflicted upon Lebanon (its forests and people) will rise up to overwhelm, submerge, and consume the conqueror. Rather than a protective covering, this represents a judicial inundation where the consequences of sin completely overtake the perpetrator.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root kasah carries the physical imagery of spreading a garment, canopy, or liquid over an object until it is completely hidden from view. It is used for covering the tabernacle furniture with protective cloths, water covering the earth during the Genesis flood, or a cloud obscuring a mountain. It implies total envelopment, leaving no part exposed. In a metaphorical sense, to be covered by something is to be completely dominated, characterized, or overwhelmed by it.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb kasah is a primary root meaning to cover, clothe, hide, conceal, or overwhelm. In the Qal stem, it refers to covering or hiding something (such as the earth being covered with water). In the Piel stem, which is used in Habakkuk 2:17, it functions intensively, meaning to completely overwhelm, thoroughly cover, submerge, or put an end to something by burying it under a weight. It expresses the absolute completeness of the action.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Water covering the earth: Just as the deep covered the mountains in Genesis 7:19 or the Red Sea covered Pharaoh's army in Exodus 15:5, kasah denotes an inescapable, drowning flood.

  • Shame and confusion: Frequently in the Prophets, a person's face or body is said to be "covered" with shame, dishonor, or confusion as a result of divine judgment (e.g., Psalm 44:15; Jeremiah 51:51).

  • Forgiveness: In a positive theological sense, God "covers" sin (Psalm 32:1), meaning He conceals it from His judicial sight, removing it from legal consideration.

4. Theological Implications

The use of kasah highlights the inexorable law of divine retribution (lex talionis). What a person sows, they reap. Babylon covered the earth with violence, blood, and destruction. Therefore, God's justice decrees that this very violence will turn back upon them, covering and consuming them. It demonstrates that sin carries its own inherent judgment; the wickedness unleashed against others ultimately becomes the flood that drowns the perpetrator.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:17, the prophet pronounces a woe against the Chaldeans (Babylonians). The phrase "For the violence done to Lebanon will cover you" uses kasah in the imperfect tense, indicating an assured future certainty. Lebanon was famous for its majestic cedars, which the Babylonians ruthlessly deforested for their imperial building projects, displacing wildlife and terrorizing the local population. The "violence" (hamas) done to this region is personified as an active force. It will return to "cover" (overwhelm, submerge, bury) Babylon under the weight of its own guilt and the subsequent judgment of God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 7:19 - "And the waters prevailed exceedingly on the earth, and all the high hills under the whole heaven were covered."

  • Psalm 32:1 - "Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven, Whose sin is covered."

  • Psalm 104:6 - "You covered it with the deep as with a garment; The waters stood above the mountains."

  • Proverbs 10:12 - "Hatred stirs up strife, But love covers all sins."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The grammatical construction in Habakkuk 2:17 uses the Piel imperfect third-person feminine singular of kasah with the second-person masculine singular suffix ("it will cover you"). The feminine subject is "the violence" (hamas), meaning that the act of devastation itself becomes the instrument of execution. Babylon is not merely punished by an external force; they are buried under the very atrocities they committed.
