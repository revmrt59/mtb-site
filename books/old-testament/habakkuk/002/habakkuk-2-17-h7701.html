PLUNDER - ( shod , Strong's H7701 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun shod refers to violent devastation, ruin, or the spoils of plunder. In the context of Habakkuk 2:17, it denotes the aggressive desolation and destruction inflicted by the Babylonian empire upon the natural world and wildlife of Lebanon. The term highlights how human cruelty and rapacity extend beyond human victims to ravage God's creation, illustrating that God's judgment will justly recoil upon those who violently exploit the earth and its creatures.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • shod derives from the root verb shadad, which carries the visceral image of assaulting, overpowering, and violently laying waste to a city, field, or populace.

  • The word picture is not merely quiet theft or stealthy larceny, but loud, chaotic, and devastating ruin. It describes the physical aftermath of an invading army that leaves fields trampled, homes smashed, forests stripped, and lives broken.

  • In the ancient Near East, conquering kings routinely boasted of deforesting mountains (especially the famous cedars of Lebanon) and slaughtering wildlife to construct their palaces and project imperial dominance. The noun shod captures the raw terror and total ruin left behind by these exploitative campaigns.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The noun shod signifies violence, havoc, ruin, devastation, destruction, or the despoiling of goods.

  • In legal and prophetic contexts, it frequently pairs with chamas (unjust ethical violence or cruelty) to represent systemic oppression and physical ruin.

  • Rather than denoting a simple act of stealing, it stresses the utter ruin and desolation left in the wake of the violent act. In Habakkuk 2:17, the word translates as "plunder" or "destruction," pointing to the devastating havoc wreaked upon the beasts of Lebanon.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The word is used to describe the wreckage of war, where land is left desolate and populations are displaced.

  • Prophets frequently cry out shod to describe the sudden, terrifying onset of divine judgment or the lawless oppression within a corrupt society.

  • It acts as an auditory alarm in prophetic literature; to cry "violence and plunder" (shod) is to announce a state of emergency where justice has failed and raw power rules.

  • In poetic parallelism, shod is often paired with terms like sheber (fracture, breach, ruin) to depict a landscape or society shattered beyond recognition.

4. Theological Implications

  • God holds empires accountable not only for how they treat human beings but also for how they treat the environment and animal creation. The devastation of Lebanon's ecosystems is treated as a moral outrage.

  • Creation is not a neutral, lifeless backdrop; it belongs to God. To despoil and ravage the earth for arrogant imperial expansion is to invite divine retribution.

  • The law of retribution (lex talionis) is active: the plunder (shod) of beasts will terrify and overwhelm the Babylonians because of the violence they committed against the land, the city, and its inhabitants. God's cosmic justice ensures that the devastation one sows is the devastation one will reap.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Habakkuk 2:17, the prophet pronounces a woe against Chaldea (Babylon) for its insatiable greed and violent conquest.

  • The "violence done to Lebanon" refers to the stripping of its famous cedar forests for Babylonian building projects.

  • The "plunder of beasts" refers to the savage slaughter or scattering of the wildlife that lived in those forests. The Babylonians hunted and destroyed these animals without cause, disrupting the ecological order.

  • The structure of the verse demonstrates a parallel guilt: as they terrified and ravaged the beasts, that very destruction will rise up to terrify them. Nature itself, under God's sovereignty, becomes a witness and instrument of judgment against the oppressor.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Jeremiah 6:7 - "As a fountain wells up with water, So she wells up with her wickedness. Violence and plundering are heard in her. Before Me continually are grief and wounds."

  • Jeremiah 20:8 - "For when I spoke, I cried out; I shouted, 'Violence and plunder!' Because the word of the Lord was made to me A reproach and a derision daily."

  • Isaiah 59:7 - "Their feet run to evil, And they make haste to shed innocent blood; Their thoughts are thoughts of iniquity; Wasting and destruction are in their paths."

  • Amos 3:10 - "'For they do not know to do right,' Says the Lord, 'Who store up violence and robbery in their palaces.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The Septuagint (LXX) often translates shod with Greek terms like talaipōria (misery, distress, ruin) or olethros (destruction). In Habakkuk 2:17, the Greek translators focused on the emotional and physical impact of this plunder, using terms that emphasize the terror and panic inflicted upon the beasts.

  • This term illustrates that systemic sin has ecological consequences. When human rulers abandon the fear of Yahweh, their greed inevitably leads to the exploitation of both people and the environment.
