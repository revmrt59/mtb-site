LIES - ( sheqer , Strong's H8267 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word sheqer denotes falsehood, deceit, deception, and that which is fundamentally untrustworthy or disappointing. In the context of biblical wisdom and prophetic literature, it represents not just a spoken untruth, but a false reality—an illusion that promises life, security, or blessing but delivers only emptiness and ruin. In Habakkuk 2:18, sheqer characterizes the false assurances provided by hand-made idols. These "teachers of lies" present a deceptive front, enticing their makers to trust in silent, powerless creations rather than the living Sovereign of the universe.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The root of sheqer carries the core concept of breaching a covenant, failing to meet an expectation, or acting deceptively.

  • It paints a picture of something that is fundamentally unreliable. It is like a dried-up brook that promises water to a thirsty traveler but offers only sand, or a deceptive weapon that fails a soldier in the heat of battle.

  • In the Ancient Near East, where physical idols were prominent, deities were expected to provide agricultural abundance, military safety, and personal protection. When a nation crafted an idol, they were projecting their desires onto wood, stone, or metal. To call these idols or their instructions sheqer was to declare them to be fraudulent, empty vessels that would inevitably disappoint and ruin those who leaned on them.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The noun sheqer refers to a lie, falsehood, deception, or fraudulent act.

  • It encompasses several distinct semantic nuances:

  • Deceptive or deceitful behavior that intentionally misleads others.

  • That which is false, groundless, or completely lacking a foundation in objective reality.

  • An object of false trust, particularly false prophets, deceptive alliances, or physical idols.

  • A violation of an oath or a covenant, representing perfidy, treachery, and unfaithfulness.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • sheqer occurs over 110 times in the Hebrew Bible, with a dense concentration in the Psalms, Proverbs, and the prophetic books of Jeremiah and Ezekiel.

  • It is frequently contrasted with emet (truth, stability, faithfulness). While truth is a firm rock upon which one can build a life, sheqer is a treacherous, shifting sand that collapses under pressure.

  • In legal contexts, sheqer describes a "false witness" who subverts justice by testifying to what is not true.

  • In prophetic contexts, it describes "lying prophets" who speak visions from their own minds rather than the mouth of Yahweh, offering false peace to a nation on the brink of judgment.

  • In wisdom literature, it is associated with "lying lips" or a "deceitful tongue," which the Lord hates because they destroy relationships and community cohesion.

4. Theological Implications

  • Idolatry is fundamentally an intellectual, moral, and spiritual lie. To replace the Creator with a creature is to exchange the truth of God for a lie.

  • Because God is the source of all ultimate reality and truth, anything that sets itself up in place of God is sheqer. It is an illusion that cannot deliver what it promises.

  • Trusting in sheqer has devastating spiritual consequences. It dulls the human conscience, blinds the intellect, and leads to spiritual captivity. A person who worships a lie eventually becomes as empty and useless as the lie they worship.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Habakkuk 2:18, the prophet delivers a series of divine "woes" against the proud, violent, and idolatrous Chaldeans (Babylonians).

  • The Chaldeans trusted in their military might and their hand-carved, gold-plated idols to secure their empire.

  • The phrase "a teacher of lies" refers directly to the idol itself and the religious system surrounding it. The idol "teaches" lies because its very presence proclaims a massive falsehood: that man can create his own god, and that this silent, breathless block of wood or stone has the power to govern human destiny.

  • The irony is sharp and tragic. The craftsman exerts his own energy, intelligence, and hands to shape the image, yet he turns around and places his trust in his own creation ("the maker of its mold"). The idol is a silent, "mute" non-entity, yet it is expected to speak oracle-like guidance. It is a teacher of absolute emptiness.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Psalm 119:104 - "Through Your precepts I get understanding; Therefore I hate every false way."

  • Proverbs 12:22 - "Lying lips are an abomination to the LORD, But those who deal truthfully are His delight."

  • Jeremiah 7:4 - "Do not trust in these lying words, saying, 'The temple of the LORD, the temple of the LORD, the temple of the LORD are these.'"

  • Jeremiah 10:14 - "Everyone is dull-hearted, without knowledge; Every metalsmith is put to shame by an image; For his molded image is falsehood, And there is no breath in them."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The Hebrew term for "idols" used at the end of Habakkuk 2:18 is elilim, which literally means "things of nought," "worthless things," or "non-entities." This lexical choice beautifully complements sheqer. The idol is a worthless nothing (elil) that teaches a deceptive falsehood (sheqer).

  • The modern equivalent of the "teacher of lies" is any secular or spiritual philosophy that promises human fulfillment, security, and salvation apart from the living God. Materialism, self-reliance, and political power can all become modern "teachers of lies" that demand our worship but leave us empty.
