TRUST - ( batach , Strong's H0982 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word batach denotes a deep state of security, confidence, and reliance that drives out fear and anxiety. It speaks of leaning one's entire weight upon someone or something, expecting it to hold. While this word normally describes the life-giving faith that a believer places in the sovereign Creator, in Habakkuk 2:18 it is used with tragic irony. Here, it describes the foolishness of a craftsman who places his ultimate confidence and reliance in a lifeless, carved idol of his own making, demonstrating how sin distorts the human heart's innate need for security.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The root meaning of batach relates to physical safety, security, and the act of lying down safely.

  • It carries the graphic picture of throwing oneself face forward upon the ground, or leaning heavily upon a staff. It is the posture of a helpless person casting themselves entirely upon the care of another, confident that they will not be dropped or abandoned.

  • In ancient Semitic contexts, the word conveys the opposite of fear, anxiety, or hesitation. To batach is to be bold, secure, and relaxed because the object of one's trust is completely reliable.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The word signifies having confidence, being secure, and feeling safe.

  • It refers to a state of mind free from care, outstandingly bold, and untroubled by fear.

  • In its active sense, it means to rely upon, depend upon, or put one's hope in someone or something.

  • Lexically, this trust is not merely an intellectual agreement but a practical reliance that manifests in a relaxed posture and confident actions.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Old Testament frequently uses batach to describe a physical state of dwelling in safety, undisturbed by enemies.

  • It is often contrasted with running away or trembling in fear. When a person trusts in the Lord, they are described as being secure like Mount Zion, which cannot be moved.

  • Conversely, when batach is placed in false security—such as military strength, wealth, or fortresses—it always results in sudden ruin and shame.

4. Theological Implications

  • Trust is the foundational requirement of the covenant relationship between God and His people. It is the practical outworking of faith.

  • Theologically, trust is exclusive. To trust in God requires a refusal to trust in human resources, political alliances, or material wealth.

  • Because humanity was created to be dependent, everyone trusts in something. If a person does not place their batach in the Creator, they will inevitably transfer that trust to a created thing. This is the essence of idolatry: placing ultimate reliance on a surrogate savior.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Habakkuk 2:18, the prophet is delivering a series of "woes" against the proud and wicked, specifically referencing the Chaldeans (Babylonians) and their pagan practices.

  • The verse exposes the sheer absurdity of idolatry: "What profit is the image, that its maker should carve it... That the maker of its mold should trust (batach) in it, To make mute idols?"

  • The irony is sharp and devastating. The craftsman uses his own hands, strength, and raw materials to mold an object, and then turns around to place his ultimate security, hope, and trust in the very object he created.

  • This represents a complete inversion of reality. Instead of the Creator sustaining the creature, the human creator trusts in his own helpless creation. The contrast is heightened by the description of the idols as "mute"—they cannot speak, guide, or deliver, making the craftsman's trust totally futile.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Psalm 56:3 - "Whenever I am afraid, I will trust in You."

  • Proverbs 3:5 - "Trust in the LORD with all your heart, And lean not on your own understanding;"

  • Isaiah 26:3 - "You will keep him in perfect peace, Whose mind is stayed on You, Because he trusts in You."

  • Psalm 115:9 - "O Israel, trust in the LORD; He is their help and their shield."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • There is a vital distinction between batach (trust) and aman (faith/believe). While aman emphasizes the certainty, truth, and stability of God's character, batach emphasizes the emotional and practical result of that faith—the actual state of feeling secure and resting quietly in that truth.

  • The tragedy of Habakkuk 2:18 is contrasted with the glorious resolution in Habakkuk 2:20: "But the LORD is in His holy temple. Let all the earth keep silence before Him." While the idol is mute and untrustworthy, the living God speaks, commands sovereign authority, and is the only worthy object of human trust.
