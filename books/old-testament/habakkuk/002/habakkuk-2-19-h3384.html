TEACH - ( yarah , Strong's H3384 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yarah fundamentally means to throw, shoot, or cast, such as shooting an arrow at a target. In its causative form, it transitions to the concept of pointing out the way, directing, instructing, or teaching. It portrays instruction not as mere lecturing, but as aiming at a target to hit the mark of truth and righteousness. In Habakkuk 2:19, the prophet uses the word mockingly to expose the absolute folly of idolatry: a lifeless block of wood or stone cannot direct, point the way, or provide divine instruction to anyone.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The concrete root of yarah is connected to the action of throwing, casting, or laying down. It is used in the Old Testament to describe casting lots, laying a foundation stone, or shooting arrows.

  • The transition from "shooting an arrow" to "teaching" provides a vivid word picture. To teach is to aim instruction directly at the heart or mind, guiding the hearer toward a specific target of truth.

  • It is also etymologically related to the noun torah (law, instruction, direction), which represents the ultimate target or guide for godly living.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • In its basic form, the verb means to throw, cast, or shoot.

  • In the causative stem (Hiphil), which is the form used in Habakkuk 2:19, it means to point out, direct, teach, instruct, or guide.

  • It describes the act of guiding someone along a specific path or pointing out the correct way to live, think, and act according to divine standards.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Shooting Arrows: Used of archers aiming and firing arrows to hit a target.

  • Laying Foundations: Casting stones to establish a firm, aligned structure.

  • Rain: It is used in the context of the early rain (or former rain) that waters the earth, suggesting a pouring down of blessing, refreshment, and instruction.

  • Teaching and Instruction: Pointing out the path of God's laws, showing the learner how to walk in the way of righteousness without straying to the left or right.

4. Theological Implications

  • True teaching (yarah) originates with God. He is the ultimate Teacher who points His people in the right direction.

  • Divine instruction is purposeful and intentional; it is designed to hit the mark of holiness, preventing God's people from wandering aimlessly.

  • Since God's law (torah) is derived from this root, true biblical teaching is always anchored in the objective revelation of God's character and will, rather than human speculation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Habakkuk 2:19, the prophet issues a woe against those who worship idols: "Woe to him who says to wood, 'Awake!' To silent stone, 'Arise!' It shall teach!"

  • The phrase "It shall teach!" (or "Can it teach?") is used as biting, rhetorical sarcasm. The idolater asks a mute, dead piece of wood or stone to act as a moral guide, to point out the way, or to give divine instruction.

  • The contrast is stark: while Yahweh speaks, reveals Himself, and instructs His people with living power, an idol is completely silent, breathless, and utterly incapable of pointing anyone toward truth.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 46:28: "Then he sent Judah before him to Joseph, to point out the way before him to Goshen. And they came to the land of Goshen."

  • Exodus 4:12: "Now therefore, go, and I will be with your mouth and teach you what you shall say."

  • 1 Samuel 12:23: "Moreover, as for me, far be it from me that I should sin against the LORD in ceasing to pray for you; but I will teach you the good and the right way."

  • Psalm 25:8: "Good and upright is the LORD; Therefore He teaches sinners in the way."

  • Psalm 119:102: "I have not departed from Your judgments, For You Yourself have taught me."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The connection between yarah (to teach/point out) and torah (law) highlights that God's law is not a set of cold, arbitrary rules. Instead, the law is God's gracious direction pointing humanity toward the path of life.

  • When human teachers or false gods attempt to direct others apart from God's truth, they inevitably miss the mark and lead their followers into error and destruction.
