VISION - ( chazon , Strong's H2377 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word chazon refers to a divine revelation, prophecy, or vision given by God to a prophet. Unlike a mere dream or human ambition, chazon represents God's objective, authoritative communication of His sovereign purposes and future actions. In Habakkuk 2:2, it is the specific, decreed message of judgment and ultimate restoration that Habakkuk must write down clearly so that a herald can run with it, assuring God's people that despite apparent delay, God's righteous plan will surely come to pass.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun chazon comes from the root verb chazah, which means "to see," "behold," or "perceive." It denotes more than simple optical sight; it refers to a deep, spiritual perception of reality as revealed by God. In the ancient Near East, prophets were sometimes called "seers" because they were granted the ability to "see" spiritual truths, divine decrees, and future events that were hidden from normal human eyes. The word picture behind chazon is that of a divine curtain being pulled back, allowing the prophet to look into the heavenly council or the unfolded future to receive a message that is entirely objective, authoritative, and originating from God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • chazon means a vision, revelation, or oracle. It denotes the specific vehicle of divine communication whereby God imparts His word, purpose, or warning to His chosen messengers. While it can refer to the ecstatic experience of receiving the revelation, it more frequently refers to the concrete content of the revelation itself—the divine message or prophecy that is heard, seen, and recorded. It stands in stark contrast to human imagination, wishful thinking, or false prophecies, representing instead the unshakeable, true decree of Yahweh.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Prophetic Titles: chazon is used 35 times in the Old Testament, predominantly in the prophetic books. It frequently serves as a title or introduction to an entire prophetic book (e.g., "The vision of Isaiah," "The vision of Obadiah"), signifying that the entire following message is of divine origin.

  • Active Divine Guidance: The presence of chazon is synonymous with God's active guidance and communication with His people. Conversely, the absence of chazon indicates spiritual famine, divine judgment, or a period when God has chosen to remain silent.

  • Permanent Record: In prophetic usage, a chazon is often meant to be recorded so that its truth can be preserved, witnessed, and verified upon its fulfillment.

4. Theological Implications

  • The Initiative of Revelation: God is the sole initiator of chazon. Humanity cannot conjure it up through effort or intellect. It demonstrates that God is not silent; He communicates His character, demands, and future plans to humanity.

  • The Certainty of Prophecy: Because chazon originates with the sovereign God, it is completely trustworthy and certain to be fulfilled. It carries the weight of divine decree; it cannot fail, lie, or be frustrated by human opposition.

  • The Necessity of Divine Word: Without God's revealed word (chazon), humanity is left in spiritual blindness. It is the light by which God's people navigate difficult times of trial and waiting.

  • Faith and Patience: Divine revelation demands a response of faith. The righteous must trust the content of the chazon and live by faith, even when the fulfillment seems delayed.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk, the prophet has complained to God about the violence and injustice in Judah, and God answered that He is raising up the Babylonians to execute judgment. This sparks a second complaint: How can a holy God use an even more wicked nation to judge a relatively more righteous one? Habakkuk then stands watch on the ramparts, waiting to see what God will answer.

God responds in Habakkuk 2:2 by telling him to write down the chazon (the vision/divine revelation concerning the ultimate doom of Babylon and the deliverance of the righteous). The chazon is to be written "plainly on tablets" so that a herald may run and proclaim it. It is not a subjective dream for Habakkuk's private comfort, but a public, permanent, and definitive divine decree. It has an "appointed time" and will not lie.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Samuel 3:1 - "And the word of the LORD was rare in those days; there was no widespread revelation [chazon]."

  • Proverbs 29:18 - "Where there is no revelation [chazon], the people cast off restraint; But happy is he who keeps the law."

  • Isaiah 1:1 - "The vision [chazon] of Isaiah the son of Amoz, which he saw concerning Judah and Jerusalem..."

  • Obadiah 1:1 - "The vision [chazon] of Obadiah. Thus says the Lord GOD concerning Edom..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament) translates chazon in Habakkuk 2:2 using the Greek word horasis (vision, appearance).

  • It is critical to distinguish the biblical chazon from modern, secularized uses of the word "vision." Today, "vision" often means a strategic business goal, a personal ambition, or a leader's dream for the future. In the biblical text, chazon is never a humanly generated plan; it is always a received divine revelation. Preachers should avoid using Habakkuk 2:2 to justify "vision-casting" for personal or corporate goal-setting.
