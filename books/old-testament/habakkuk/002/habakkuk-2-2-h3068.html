LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh is the personal, covenantal name of God, signifying His eternal self-existence, absolute sovereignty, and unshakeable faithfulness to His promises. In Habakkuk 2:2, this name assures the questioning prophet that the sovereign Creator of the universe is actively engaged, listening, and faithful to speak a trustworthy word of hope in times of deep national distress.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The tetragrammaton, YHWH, is the unique personal name of the God of Israel. Unlike titles such as Elohim (God) or Adonai (Lord/Master), this name was revealed to Moses at the burning bush as a declaration of God’s active, personal presence. It draws from the historical reality of a God who acts in time and space to deliver His people. It is not an abstract philosophical concept but the name of a relational God who binds Himself to His creation through covenants.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name is derived from the Hebrew verb hayah, which means "to be" or "to exist." It represents the third-person singular imperfect form, translating functionally as "He is," "He will be," or "He causes to be." The lexical meaning points to absolute self-existence. God is uncaused, self-sufficient, and the source of all life and existence. He depends on nothing outside of Himself, yet all of creation depends entirely on Him.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

In English translations, this name is represented by "LORD" in capital letters to distinguish it from the Hebrew word Adonai. Used over 6,800 times in the Old Testament, it serves as a continuous reminder of God’s personal relationship with humanity. It functions as a word picture of a secure fortress, a shepherd, and a deliverer. The name signifies that God is always present with His people, working actively in their history.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, the name Yahweh stands for the immutability and faithfulness of God. Because He is the self-existent One, He does not change. His character, purposes, and promises remain constant across generations. It carries the weight of ultimate authority; when Yahweh speaks, His words carry the force of absolute certainty. The name also guarantees redemption, as He is the God who hears the cries of the oppressed and steps down to save them.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:2, the prophet is standing watch, waiting for an answer to his agonizing questions about why a holy God would use the wicked Babylonians to judge Judah. When "the LORD answered," the text uses Yahweh. This choice of name is critical. It reminds Habakkuk and the readers that the one speaking is not a detached cosmic force, but their covenant-keeping God. Despite the impending judgment and chaotic geopolitical landscape, the covenant remains intact. The command to write the vision on tablets comes from the self-existent Creator who controls history and guarantees that His word will come to pass.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 3:15: "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

  • Psalm 23:1: "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

  • Isaiah 42:8: "I am the LORD, that is My name; and My glory I will not give to another, nor My praise to carved images."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Due to the extreme sanctity of the name, post-exilic Judaism avoided pronouncing it aloud to prevent violating the third commandment. When reading scripture publicly, they substituted the word Adonai (Lord) or HaShem (The Name). This practice led to the loss of the original pronunciation, though Yahweh is the widely accepted reconstruction. This historical reality underscores the profound reverence and holy fear that the name should evoke in the hearts of believers.
