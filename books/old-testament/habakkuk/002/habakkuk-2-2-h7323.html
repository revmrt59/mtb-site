RUN - ( rûts , Strong's H7323 )

Summary:

In Habakkuk 2:2, the Hebrew verb rûts refers to running with a specific message or purpose. Rather than a reader running away in fear, the context indicates that the herald reads the clearly written vision and runs to proclaim it swiftly to others. The word highlights the urgency, clarity, and rapid propagation of God's revealed truth in times of crisis.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near East, before the advent of modern telecommunications, the rapid transmission of critical news depended entirely on royal couriers or foot messengers. These runners (rûts) were trained for endurance and speed, carrying decrees from kings or reports from the battlefield to waiting cities. The arrival of a runner was a moment of intense anticipation; their speed reflected the urgency of the message they carried. In a military and prophetic culture, the image of a runner was synonymous with the delivery of highly consequential truth that required immediate action.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The Hebrew verb rûts means to run, travel quickly, hasten, or rush. At its core, the word denotes rapid physical movement. However, when used in a courier or heraldic context, it shifts from mere physical exertion to the official delivery of a message. It describes the swift dispatch of messengers carrying royal edicts or divine revelations. In the Hiphil (causative) stem, it can mean to bring quickly or drive away in haste, while in the Qal stem (as seen in Habakkuk 2:2), it simply denotes the active, rapid movement of the runner.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, rûts depicts several vivid scenarios:

  • Military Couriers: Running to deliver battlefield reports, such as Ahimaaz running to King David with news of victory.

  • Royal Escorts: Servants who ran before the king’s chariot to clear the path and announce his arrival.

  • Divine Obedience: The rapid, unhindered execution of God's will. For example, God’s word is said to run swiftly across the earth, executing His commands without delay.

  • Spiritual Alacrity: The joyful, rapid pursuit of God's law by a heart that has been set free.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, rûts in prophetic literature underscores the dynamic and urgent nature of God's revelation. God's word is not static; it is a message on the move. When God speaks, His words demand swift distribution and immediate response. The association of running with reading the divine vision implies that true understanding of God’s word naturally produces evangelical urgency. The believer does not merely contemplate the message in isolation; they are compelled to run with it, sharing the warning and hope of God’s decree with a dying world.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:2, the Lord commands the prophet, "Write the vision and make it plain on tablets, that he may run who reads it." Historically, some have interpreted this to mean that the reader runs away in flight or fear upon reading the impending judgment. However, the linguistic and cultural context points to the role of a herald.

The tablets are to be written so clearly and in such large characters that a courier can read them easily, even while preparing to run, or read them and immediately run to proclaim the message throughout the land. The clarity of the writing ("make it plain") is directly tied to the speed of the proclamation ("that he may run"). God’s revelation is so vital that there must be no ambiguity to slow down the messenger. The messenger reads, grasps the truth instantly, and runs to sound the alarm.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 18:2 - "So he lifted his eyes and looked, and behold, three men were standing by him; and when he saw them, he ran from the tent door to meet them, and bowed himself to the ground,"

  • 1 Samuel 17:17 - "Then Jesse said to his son David, 'Take now for your brothers an ephah of this dried grain and these ten loaves, and run to your brothers at the camp.'"

  • 2 Samuel 18:23 - "So he said, 'Whatever happens, let me run.' And he said to him, 'Run.' Then Ahimaaz ran by the way of the plain, and outran the Cushite."

  • Psalm 119:32 - "I will run the course of Your commandments, For You shall enlarge my heart."

  • Jeremiah 23:21 - "I have not sent these prophets, yet they ran. I have not spoken to them, yet they prophesied."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A powerful contrast is found in Jeremiah 23:21, where false prophets "ran" (rûts) even though God had not sent them. They possessed the physical urgency to run but lacked a true, clear message from God. In contrast, the true herald in Habakkuk 2:2 only runs because he has received a clearly defined, divinely inspired vision. Urgency without truth leads to deception, but truth combined with urgency leads to the faithful warning and salvation of others.
