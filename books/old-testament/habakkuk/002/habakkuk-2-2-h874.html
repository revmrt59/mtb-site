PLAIN - ( ba'ar , Strong's H874 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb ba'ar means to make clear, distinct, plain, or to engrave deeply so that something is easily understood. In Habakkuk 2:2, God commands the prophet to write down the prophetic vision and "make it plain" on tablets. This depicts an engraving process where letters are carved deeply and clearly, ensuring the message is completely legible to any passerby. In a spiritual sense, it highlights God's desire for His revelation to be accessible, unmistakable, and urgent, enabling the herald to run with the message and proclaim it effectively.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root meaning of ba'ar relates to digging, boring, or engraving. It is etymologically cognate with words for a physical "well" or "pit" (be'er), suggesting a deep digging out or carving into a surface. To make something "plain" is to carve it so deeply and clearly into stone, clay, or plaster tablets that the contours are sharp and unmistakable. In the ancient Near East, public inscriptions, laws, and royal decrees were engraved on large stone steles erected in public squares. This ensured that the text could withstand the elements, resist tampering, and be read clearly by the public.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

In the Piel stem, the verb ba'ar means to make distinct, plain, or to explain. The core lexical definition is to write or engrave clearly, or to expound and declare a message with absolute clarity so that there is no ambiguity. The term denotes both the physical act of engraving letters deeply to ensure legibility, and the intellectual act of explaining or expounding a text to make its meaning fully transparent to the mind.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Hebrew Bible uses ba'ar only three times, falling into two primary categories: physical engraving and clear exposition.

  • Physical Engraving for Maximum Legibility: In Deuteronomy 27:8, the Israelites are commanded to write all the words of the law on plastered stones "very plainly." Here, ba'ar functions alongside the Hebrew word for "well" or "good" (yateb) to emphasize an writing style so clear and deep that the law is impossible to misread or ignore.

  • Clear Exposition: In Deuteronomy 1:5, the word describes Moses beginning to "explain" or "declare" the law to Israel in the land of Moab. Here, the word transitions from physical legibility to conceptual clarity—taking the divine instruction and digging out its meaning so that it is plain to the minds of the listeners.

4. Theological Implications

  • God is a Clear Communicator: God does not speak in riddles or conceal His truth from those who seek Him. He desires His word to be "plain." Revelation is intended for public consumption, understanding, and obedience, not for an elite class of mystics.

  • The Permanence of Revelation: By commanding the vision to be engraved on tablets, God emphasizes the durability and unshakeable certainty of His word. What is ba'ar (deeply engraved) cannot be easily erased or altered.

  • The Urgency of Proclamation: The ultimate purpose of making the vision plain is functional: "that he may run who reads it." Divine truth demands immediate action, and clarity is the catalyst for that response.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:2, the prophet is waiting in anguish for God's reply to his complaints about systemic injustice and the impending Babylonian invasion. God's response is to give a vision of ultimate justice, but He first commands the physical preparation of the message. The "tablets" (luhot) were likely large clay or stone tablets meant for public display. By making the vision plain (engraving it clearly), God ensures that the message is legally binding, permanent, and publicly readable. The phrase "that he may run who reads it" indicates that anyone who glances at the public tablet can easily grasp the message on the move and run to spread the warning, or that the reader, catalyzed by the clarity of the message, can run as a herald of God's truth.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Deuteronomy 1:5: "On this side of the Jordan in the land of Moab, Moses began to explain this law, saying,"

  • Deuteronomy 27:8: "And you shall write very plainly on the stones all the words of this law."

  • Habakkuk 2:2: "Then the LORD answered me and said: 'Write the vision And make it plain on tablets, That he may run who reads it.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The transition of ba'ar from physical digging to mental explanation illustrates a vital biblical pattern: true teaching is the "digging out" of the deep things of God and presenting them in a way that is easily understood.

  • The Greek Septuagint (LXX) translates ba'ar in Habakkuk 2:2 using the adverb saphos, meaning "clearly" or "plainly," emphasizing the cognitive clarity of the message.

  • This verse stands as a foundational mandate for biblical preaching and teaching: the communicator's task is not to make simple things complex, but to make complex divine truths plain so that the hearer can act upon them.
