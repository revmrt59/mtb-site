TEMPLE - ( hekal , Strong's H1964 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun hekal refers to a great house, palace, or temple, signifying a place of royal residence and divine presence. In Habakkuk 2:20, it contrasts the living, sovereign God residing in His heavenly or earthly sanctuary with lifeless, silent idols. It calls for awe, submission, and absolute silence from all the earth in the presence of the cosmic King.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word hekal is a loanword from the Akkadian ekallu, which itself derives from the Sumerian E.GAL, meaning "great house" or "palace." In the Ancient Near East, a king's palace was the center of administrative power, judgment, and majesty. When applied to Yahweh, the term blends the concepts of a royal palace and a sacred temple. God is the sovereign King of the universe, and His sanctuary is His royal audience chamber where He sits enthroned.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun hekal possesses three primary meanings:

  • A palace, which is a royal residence for a human king (such as the palace of Ahab or the king of Babylon).

  • The temple of Yahweh, referring to either the physical temple in Jerusalem or the heavenly temple of God.

  • The nave or main hall of the temple, which is the Holy Place, distinguishing it from the Holy of Holies (debir).

In the context of divine habitation, it denotes the place where God manifests His glorious presence, receives worship, and rules over His creation.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, hekal is used to paint a vivid picture of royal dignity and ultimate authority.

  • It is a place of supreme authority. Just as subjects enter a royal palace with reverence and trembling, so humanity must approach the hekal of God.

  • It is depicted as a place of refuge and judgment. From His palace, the King hears the cries of His people and issues decrees that shake the nations.

  • It emphasizes the holiness of the space. Because the King who dwells there is holy, the palace itself is designated as holy.

4. Theological Implications

The use of hekal reinforces several key theological truths:

  • Divine Sovereignty: God is not merely a localized deity; He is the Great King over all the earth. His temple is His palace, the administrative headquarters of the universe.

  • Transcendence and Immanence: The hekal represents God's active presence among His people (immanence) but also His exalted majesty above them (transcendence). He is enthroned in glory, yet He hears prayers from His temple.

  • Righteous Judgment: God's temple is the supreme court of the cosmos. When God is in His hekal, judgment goes forth, and the earth must submit to His verdicts.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:20, the prophet is grappling with the rise of the ruthless Chaldeans (Babylonians) whom God is using to discipline Judah. God responds to Habakkuk's confusion by pronouncing woes against the proud and idolatrous (Habakkuk 2:18-19). Idols are silent, breathless wood and stone, carved by human hands, having no power to answer or save.

In sharp contrast, verse 20 declares: "But the LORD is in His holy temple (hekal)."

The contrast is both polemical and pastoral:

  • While idols are trapped inside their shrines, helpless and dead, Yahweh is alive, active, and reigning from His true, heavenly palace.

  • The physical temple in Jerusalem might soon be threatened by the Babylonians, but God's true royal dwelling is secure.

  • Because the supreme King is on His throne in His hekal, the entire earth—including the arrogant Babylonians and the questioning prophet—must hush. "Let all the earth keep silence before Him." The silence-inducing majesty of the King guarantees that justice will ultimately prevail.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Psalm 11:4 - "The LORD is in His holy temple, The LORD's throne is in heaven; His eyes behold, His eyelids test the sons of men."

  • Isaiah 6:1 - "In the year that King Uzziah died, I saw the Lord sitting on a throne, high and lifted up, and the train of His robe filled the temple."

  • Jonah 2:4 - "Then I said, 'I have been cast out of Your sight; Yet I will look again toward Your holy temple.'"

  • Micah 1:2 - "Hear, all you peoples! Listen, O earth, and all that is in it! Let the Lord GOD be a witness against you, The Lord from His holy temple."

7. Other Relevant Insights

For the modern believer, this study provides a powerful antidote to anxiety in times of national or personal crisis. When world events seem chaotic and wicked powers seem to triumph, the foundational truth remains: God has not vacated His hekal. He is still on the throne. The appropriate response to God's presence is quiet trust, worship, and reverent silence, rather than frantic worry or demanding explanations from the Almighty.
