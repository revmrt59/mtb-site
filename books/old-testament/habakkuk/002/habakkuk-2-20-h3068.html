LORD - ( YHWH , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name YHWH, translated as "LORD" in all capital letters in the NKJV, is the sacred, personal, and covenant-keeping name of the God of Israel. It reveals Him as the self-existent, eternal, and unchangeable Creator who binds Himself to His people by covenant promise. In Habakkuk 2:20, this name stands in stark contrast to the silent, lifeless idols of the nations. While man-made gods are powerless and require human support, the LORD is active, sovereign, and present in His holy temple, demanding reverent silence from all the earth.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The name YHWH is closely associated with the Hebrew verb hayah, which means "to be" or "to become." It was uniquely revealed to Moses at the burning bush as "I AM WHO I AM," suggesting a self-derived, self-sustaining existence. Unlike the gods of the ancient Near East, who were created, had origins, and relied on human care for survival, YHWH has no beginning, no ending, and requires nothing from His creation. Historically, this name is deeply tied to God's redemptive actions. He is the God who hears the cries of His people, remembers His promises, and acts with a mighty hand to deliver them.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name signifies the self-existent, eternal One. It denotes the absolute being who is sovereign over history, nature, and humanity. It represents the covenant Lord who is faithful, unchanging, and merciful. The term indicates the active, present reality of God in relation to His creation, particularly His chosen covenant partners.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • YHWH is the most frequently used divine name in the Old Testament, occurring over 6,800 times.

  • It is often associated with the "Shechinah" or the visible manifestation of God’s glory dwelling among His people in the tabernacle and the temple.

  • It is frequently paired with covenant actions, such as establishing, remembering, and fulfilling promises.

  • Scriptural metaphors picture the name of the LORD as a "strong tower" of safety, a defensive shield, and a shepherd, providing rich pastoral imagery of protection, guidance, and intimacy.

4. Theological Implications

  • Aseity and Self-Existence: God is independent of His creation, possessing life inherently within Himself.

  • Immutability and Faithfulness: Because He is YHWH, He does not change. His promises remain secure despite human failures or external crises.

  • Covenant Relationship: YHWH is not an abstract force but a personal God who enters into a relational bond with His people.

  • Sovereignty: As the active Lord of history, He orchestrates the rise and fall of empires to accomplish His divine purposes.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2, the prophet receives a series of five "woes" against the proud, violent, and idolatrous Chaldeans. Verse 19 mocks the idolater who says to a piece of wood, "Awake!" and to a silent stone, "Arise!" Verse 20 delivers the climactic contrast: "But the LORD is in His holy temple."

  • Unlike the silent, dead, gold-plated idols that have "no breath at all in them," YHWH is alive, supreme, and occupies His true, heavenly sanctuary.

  • The presence of the LORD in His temple means that despite the impending Babylonian invasion and the apparent chaos on earth, God remains on His throne. History is under His control.

  • The command "Let all the earth keep silence before Him" is a call for hushed awe, submission, and absolute trust in His sovereign timing, silencing all human complaints, doubts, and anxieties.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 3:15 - "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations.'"

  • Psalm 23:1 - "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

  • Proverbs 18:10 - "The name of the LORD is a strong tower; The righteous run into it and are safe."

  • Isaiah 42:8 - "I am the LORD, that is My name; And My glory I will not give to another, Nor My praise to carved images."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Due to the extreme reverence for the sacred tetragrammaton (YHWH), Jewish tradition avoided pronouncing the name aloud, substituting it with Adonai ("Lord"). This tradition influenced English translations, which render YHWH as "LORD" in capital letters. In the New Testament, the Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint) translated YHWH using Kyrios ("Lord"). The writers of the New Testament consistently apply this supreme, divine covenant identity directly to Jesus Christ, declaring Him as the Lord of all.
