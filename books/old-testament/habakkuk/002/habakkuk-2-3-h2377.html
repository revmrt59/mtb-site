VISION - ( chazon , Strong's H2377 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word chazon refers to a divine revelation, prophetic vision, or oracle communicated by God to His messengers. In the context of Habakkuk 2:3, it denotes the specific, concrete message of judgment and ultimate deliverance that God revealed to the prophet. This vision is not a human dream or wishful thinking, but a certain, sovereign decree of God that moves inexorably toward its historical fulfillment at the appointed time, demanding faith and patient endurance from the righteous.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • chazon derives from the root verb chazah, which means "to see," "behold," or "gaze."

  • In the ancient Near East, a prophet was often called a "seer" (chozeh). The noun chazon refers to the objective content of what the seer beholds through divine disclosure.

  • Unlike ordinary physical sight, chazon describes an inner, spiritual perception or a supernaturally granted revelation. It represents a pulling back of the historical curtain by God to show His servants His plans and purposes.

  • The term is deeply connected to the authority of the spoken and written word. It represents a visual communication that must be translated into authoritative verbal instruction for God's covenant people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • chazon refers to a divine vision, revelation, or oracle.

  • It signifies the prophetic communication received by a prophet, often concerning future events, the destiny of nations, or the spiritual state of God's people.

  • The semantic range spans from the psychological or spiritual experience of receiving a divine manifestation to the concrete, written record of the prophetic message itself.

  • It denotes objective revelation rather than subjective human imagination. When God speaks or shows His plans, the resulting chazon carries the weight of absolute truth.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The word is frequently associated with spiritual direction and national survival. Where chazon is absent, the people are left to their own ruinous devices because they lack the direct guiding word of God.

  • In prophetic literature, a chazon is depicted as something that must be "written" and "made plain on tablets" so that those who read it may run to proclaim it or escape impending judgment.

  • It serves as a spiritual anchor during times of national distress. By focusing on the chazon, the believer shifts their gaze from visible, chaotic circumstances to the invisible, sovereign reality of God's future purposes.

4. Theological Implications

  • Sovereign Initiative: A chazon never originates in human imagination, intellectual calculation, or political analysis. It is entirely initiated by God, representing His sovereign decree breaking into human history.

  • Absolute Certainty: Because the vision comes from God, its fulfillment is guaranteed. It "speaks" and "will not lie." It is bound to God's own character of truthfulness and power; what He reveals will come to pass.

  • The Necessity of Faith: In the interim between the reception of the chazon and its historical fulfillment, God's people must live by faith. The vision demands patient waiting, serving as the objective basis for the famous statement, "the just shall live by his faith" in Habakkuk 2:4.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Habakkuk 1, the prophet cries out to God concerning the violence and injustice in Judah, and God responds that He is raising up the ruthless Babylonians (Chaldeans) as an instrument of judgment.

  • This response plunges Habakkuk into a deeper moral dilemma: how can a holy God use a nation more wicked than Judah to execute justice?

  • Standing on his watchtower in Habakkuk 2:1, the prophet waits for God's reply. The chazon of Habakkuk 2:3 is God's direct answer to this crisis of faith.

  • The "vision" in Habakkuk 2:3 specifically promises the ultimate downfall of the proud Babylonian empire and the preservation of the righteous.

  • Though the fulfillment of this judgment seems delayed, God reassures the prophet that the chazon has an "appointed time." It is moving toward its climax and will not fail. The context shifts the believer's focus from "when" God will act to "who" God is, requiring the righteous to trust the certainty of the revelation over the discouraging immediate circumstances.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Samuel 3:1 - "Now the boy Samuel ministered to the LORD before Eli. And the word of the LORD was rare in those days; there was no widespread revelation [chazon]."

  • Proverbs 29:18 - "Where there is no revelation [chazon], the people cast off restraint; but happy is he who keeps the law."

  • Isaiah 1:1 - "The vision [chazon] of Isaiah the son of Amoz, which he saw concerning Judah and Jerusalem in the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, kings of Judah."

  • Daniel 8:1 - "In the third year of the reign of King Belshazzar a vision [chazon] appeared to me—to me, Daniel—after the one that appeared to me the first time."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament) translates chazon in Habakkuk 2:3 using the Greek word horasis (vision, appearance).

  • The writer of Hebrews quotes Habakkuk 2:3–4 in Hebrews 10:37–38, adapting the Greek text to apply it messianically: "For yet a little while, and He who is coming will come and will not tarry." Thus, the ultimate fulfillment of the chazon points forward to the person, work, and final return of Jesus Christ.

  • The contrast between the proud whose soul is "not upright" and the righteous who "live by faith" hinges entirely on their response to the chazon. The proud dismiss the delayed vision, while the righteous hold fast to it as an absolute reality.
