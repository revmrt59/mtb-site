END - ( qēṣ , Strong's H7093 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun qēṣ refers to an end, extremity, limit, or boundary of space or time. In prophetic literature, and specifically in Habakkuk 2:3, it denotes the designated culmination or final fulfillment of God’s sovereign plan. It is not merely a cessation of time, but the goal-oriented climax where God’s promises are fully realized and His justice is vindicated.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word qēṣ derives from the root qāṣaṣ, which means to cut off, shear, or sever. The visual picture is that of reaching the outermost edge or boundary line where a thread or cord is cut. It is the extreme limit of a physical space or a period of time. In the ancient Near Eastern mindset, time was not an endless, meaningless cycle, but a straight line moving toward a definitive, divinely appointed boundary marker—a point where God steps in to cut off one era and initiate another.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun qēṣ means an end, limit, boundary, or culmination. It has three primary categories of meaning:

  • An end of space, referring to the physical border, extremity, or farthest limits of a territory.

  • An end of time, indicating a termination point of a specific epoch, period of waiting, or human life.

  • A consummation or appointed outcome, referring to the final resolution of a historical process, judgment, or prophetic vision.

In prophetic contexts, it frequently describes the "time of the end" or the climatic point of divine intervention when God's secret purposes are laid bare.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The noun qēṣ is used in several vivid ways across the Old Testament:

  • The Limit of Patience: It describes the point at which God’s patience with wickedness reaches its absolute boundary and judgment must fall, as seen in the days of Noah when the "end" of all flesh came before God.

  • The Border of Territory: It outlines geographic extremities, representing the outer boundaries beyond which one cannot pass.

  • The Culmination of Suffering: It marks the boundary line of a period of affliction or trial, indicating that sorrow has a fixed shelf-life ordained by God.

  • The Prophetic Destination: It pictures a destination point toward which all preceding history is traveling. The vision does not wander aimlessly; it hastens toward a fixed finish line.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, qēṣ underscores the absolute sovereignty of God over time and history. History is not a series of random, chaotic events, nor is it an endless loop.

  • Divine Sovereignty: God sets the boundaries of time. He determines the exact moment when empires rise and fall, and when prophecies transition from prediction to historical reality.

  • The Certainty of God's Word: If God has decreed an "end" (qēṣ), that end is fixed and unalterable. Human rebellion, political upheaval, and demonic opposition cannot delay it.

  • Faith and Endurance: The existence of a qēṣ provides comfort to the believer. It reassures the suffering saint that evil has a shelf life, trials have an expiration date, and God’s promises are moving toward a guaranteed, victorious resolution.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:3, the prophet is grappling with the agonizing question of why a holy God would use the wicked Chaldeans (Babylonians) to punish Judah. God answers by telling Habakkuk to write down the vision, assuring him that though the fulfillment seems to linger, it is moving swiftly toward its "end" (qēṣ).

Here, qēṣ is the appointed time of fulfillment when the vision will "speak" (be vindicated) and not lie. The nuance is not merely the chronological end of the world, but the specific, divinely designated culmination of the prophecy concerning Babylon's judgment and Judah's deliverance. It teaches that God operates on a precise, uncompromised schedule. What looks like "tarrying" to human perception is actually a steady, uninterrupted march toward the pre-established qēṣ.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 6:13: "And God said to Noah, 'The end of all flesh has come before Me, for the earth is filled with violence through them; and behold, I will destroy them with the earth.'"

  • Psalm 39:4: "LORD, make me to know my end, And what is the measure of my days, That I may know how frail I am."

  • Daniel 8:17: "So he came near where I stood, and when he came I was afraid and fell on my face; but he said to me, 'Understand, son of man, that the vision refers to the time of the end.'"

  • Daniel 12:4: "But you, Daniel, shut up the words, and seal the book until the time of the end; many shall run to and fro, and knowledge shall increase."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The LXX Translation: The Septuagint (LXX) translates qēṣ in Habakkuk 2:3 using the Greek word synteleia (consummation) or related terms of completion. In Hebrews 10:37, the author quotes the Septuagint version of Habakkuk 2:3–4, applying the "end" messianically to the coming of Jesus Christ ("For yet a little while, and He who is coming will come and will not tarry"). This links the prophetic qēṣ directly to the person and return of Jesus.

  • The Contrast with Beginning: Just as God is the re'shit (the beginning or chief origin), He is the controller of the qēṣ. History is bookended by His sovereign decrees.
