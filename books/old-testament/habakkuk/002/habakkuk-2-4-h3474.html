UPRIGHT - ( yashar , Strong's H3474 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yashar means to be straight, smooth, right, or pleasing. In a moral and spiritual sense, it describes a life that is straight and aligned with God's character and standard, free from deviousness, pride, or distortion. In Habakkuk 2:4, it is contrasted with the "puffed up" or proud soul of the Chaldean, showing that a soul lacking this straightness is crooked, self-reliant, and out of alignment with God. Conversely, the "just" person lives by faith, finding their alignment and stability not in self-inflation, but in trusting God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete, physical root of yashar refers to making a path straight, level, or smooth, clearing it of obstacles, hills, or rugged terrain. When a road is yashar, it is direct, flat, and easy to travel. In the ancient Near East, before a king or dignitary traveled, a construction crew would go ahead of him to level the valleys and cut down the hills to prepare a straight, smooth highway. Metaphorically, this physical straightness shifted to represent moral integrity and ethical behavior. A life that is yashar is one that does not bend, warp, or deviate into crookedness, but walks straight along the path of God’s law.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

As a verb, yashar means to be straight, right, upright, or pleasing. In the Qal stem (the basic active voice used in Habakkuk 2:4), it denotes being straight, level, or right. When applied to the soul (nephesh), it indicates an inner posture that is honest, sincere, and aligned with God's truth. It is the opposite of being crooked, warped, or self-centered. The word communicates both a moral straightness and a functional rightness—living in a way that functions exactly as God designed human life to function.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Straight Paths: The word is frequently used to describe a level highway or path that has been cleared of obstacles, symbolizing a life directed by God.

  • Pleasing in the Eyes: The verb also carries the sense of what is "right" or "pleasing" in someone's estimation. When a course of action is straight, it meets the approval of the observer.

  • The Straight Edge: Just as a builder uses a plumb line or straight edge to ensure a wall is upright, yashar represents the divine standard against which all human actions and motives are measured.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, yashar reveals that God's standard is the absolute reference point for reality. God Himself is upright, and He created humanity to be upright. Sin, however, is a bending or warping of this original straightness.

  • The Anatomy of Pride: In scripture, pride is not merely an emotion; it is a structural distortion of the soul. A proud soul is "puffed up"—swollen, inflated, and uneven. Because it is swollen with self-importance, it cannot be straight (yashar).

  • Faith vs. Pride: The proud person relies on self and is morally crooked. The righteous person relies on God through faith (emunah), which keeps their life straight, stable, and pleasing to God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:4, the prophet stands on his watchtower waiting for God's answer regarding the violent and ruthless Chaldeans (Babylonians). God answers by drawing a stark contrast between two kinds of people.

The Chaldean is puffed up; his soul is "not upright in him." His inner life is warped by arrogance, greed, and self-deification. He relies on his own military might and intellect. Because his soul is crooked, his collapse is inevitable.

In contrast, the "just" (righteous) person will "live by his faith." While the proud Chaldean is unstable because of his crooked inner posture, the believer finds stability and life by trusting in the unchanging character of God. The nuance of yashar here exposes the hollow, twisted nature of self-reliance.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Proverbs 3:6: "In all your ways acknowledge Him, And He shall direct [yashar] your paths."

  • Isaiah 40:3: "The voice of one crying in the wilderness: 'Prepare the way of the Lord; Make straight [yashar] in the desert A highway for our God.'"

  • 1 Chronicles 13:4: "Then all the assembly said that they would do so, for the thing was right [yashar] in the eyes of all the people."

  • 1 Samuel 6:12: "Then the cows headed straight [yashar] for the road to Beth Shemesh, and went along the highway, lowing as they went, and did not turn aside to the right hand or to the left."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament), the Hebrew verb yashar in Habakkuk 2:4 is translated using the Greek verb eudokeo, which means "to be well-pleased" or "to take pleasure in." This Greek rendering is quoted in Hebrews 10:38: "But if anyone draws back, My soul has no pleasure in him."

This connection bridges the Old and New Testaments beautifully. A soul that is not straight (not yashar) cannot please God, because it lacks the faith and humility that align a human heart with its Creator. Only a life lived by faith is straight, stable, and pleasing in His sight.
