FAITH - ( 'emunah , Strong's H0530 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'emunah, translated as "faith" in Habakkuk 2:4, refers to a firm, steady, and unwavering commitment of trust and loyalty. It is not merely intellectual assent or passive belief, but an active, lived-out reliability and steadfastness in relationship with God. In times of national crisis and impending judgment, 'emunah is the operational trust that enables the righteous person to remain secure and spiritually alive, anchored in the absolute trustworthiness of God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun 'emunah derives from the verbal root 'aman, which means to support, confirm, uphold, or establish. In its physical sense, the root refers to something that is firm, solid, and reliable—such as a pillar that securely supports a massive ceiling or a nurse who holds a fragile infant safely in her arms. The underlying word picture is one of stability, security, and absolute dependability. When applied to human character or action, 'emunah describes a life that is steady, stable, and unyielding under pressure. It is the opposite of being shaky, double-minded, or fickle.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The semantic range of 'emunah centers on the concepts of firmness, fidelity, steadfastness, trustworthiness, and honesty.

  • Faithfulness and Fidelity: It denotes moral integrity, reliability, and loyalty in fulfilling one's obligations or covenants.

  • Steadfastness: It refers to physical or spiritual stability that remains unmoved by external circumstances.

  • Trust and Trustworthiness: It expresses the quality of being utterly reliable, as well as the attitude of relying completely on another who is trustworthy.

While modern translations often render this word as "faith" in Habakkuk 2:4, the primary Old Testament usage emphasizes "faithfulness" or "steadfastness." True biblical faith is inseparable from a lifestyle of loyal obedience and enduring reliability.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Scripture uses 'emunah in concrete, highly visual ways to illustrate its meaning:

  • Steady Hands: In Exodus 17:12, Moses' hands are described as 'emunah (steady) until the going down of the sun because Aaron and Hur supported them. This physical steadiness undergirded Israel's victory.

  • An Office of Trust: In the historical books, temple administrators, gatekeepers, and treasurers are appointed to their positions because of their 'emunah (honesty, fidelity, or trustworthiness). They dealt with sacred things with absolute integrity.

  • The Character of God: The word is frequently used to describe God’s own nature. His actions, His word, and His covenant loyalty are perfectly stable, true, and dependable. Human 'emunah is always a reflective response to God’s sovereign 'emunah.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, 'emunah bridges the gap between belief and behavior. It demonstrates that saving faith is never static or merely cognitive.

  • The Character of the Righteous: In Old Testament theology, the righteous person (tsaddiyq) is characterized by a life aligned with God's covenant. This alignment is sustained by 'emunah—a persistent, active trust in God's promises that manifests as obedience and loyalty.

  • A Contrast of Sovereignty: Habakkuk contrasts the proud Chaldeans, whose souls are puffed up and self-reliant, with the righteous, who live by their dependent trust in Yahweh. Self-reliance leads to destruction, whereas relational trust in God leads to life.

  • The Ground of Perseverance: Because God is faithful, His people can exercise faithfulness. Human fidelity is not self-generated but is anchored in the unchanging character of God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:4, the prophet is waiting for God's answer in the midst of overwhelming distress. The cruel and arrogant Babylonians are poised to invade Judah. God's response contrasts two ways of facing this crisis: the way of the proud, whose soul is "not upright," and the way of the "just" (the righteous).

In this context, "the just shall live by his faith ('emunah)" means that the righteous person will survive the coming judgment and find true spiritual vitality by remaining steadfast, loyal, and quietly confident in God's promises. While the proud rely on military might and self-exaltation (which will ultimately fail), the righteous survive by waiting patiently on the Lord, holding fast to their covenant relationship with Him despite visible circumstances.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 17:12 - "But Moses’ hands became heavy; so they took a stone and put it under him, and he sat on it. And Aaron and Hur supported his hands, one on one side, and the other on the other side; and his hands were steady ['emunah] until the going down of the sun."

  • Deuteronomy 32:4 - "He is the Rock, His work is perfect; For all His ways are justice, A God of truth ['emunah] and without injustice; Righteous and upright is He."

  • Psalm 36:5 - "Your mercy, O LORD, is in the heavens; Your faithfulness ['emunah] reaches to the clouds."

  • Proverbs 12:22 - "Lying lips are an abomination to the LORD, But those who deal truthfully ['emunah] are His delight."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Apostle Paul quotes Habakkuk 2:4 in Romans 1:17 and Galatians 3:11 to establish the doctrine of justification by faith alone (pistis in Greek). The author of Hebrews also quotes it in Hebrews 10:38 to exhort believers to endure under persecution.

While Paul emphasizes the initial and ongoing instrumental trust in Christ for justification, and Habakkuk emphasizes the physical and spiritual endurance of the righteous under trial, these two concepts are deeply unified. The New Testament pistis (faith/trust) is the spiritual root, and the Old Testament 'emunah (faithfulness/steadfastness) is the visible, relational fruit. One cannot exist without the other; the faith that justifies is a faith that endures in loyal obedience.
