CANNOT - ( lo' , Strong's H3808 )

Summary:

The Hebrew negative particle lo' expresses absolute, objective negation. In Habakkuk 2:5, when paired with the verb saba' ("to be satisfied"), it is translated in the NKJV as "cannot," indicating an absolute, constitutional inability to reach contentment. Rather than a temporary lack of satisfaction, it describes a permanent state of insatiable greed and spiritual hunger that characterizes the proud and wicked, specifically exemplified by the Chaldean empire.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The particle lo' is the primary adverb of negation in the Hebrew Bible, occurring over 5,000 times. Unlike temporary, subjective, or conditional negatives, lo' signifies an objective, permanent, or absolute "no." It establishes an unyielding boundary.

In the ancient Near Eastern context, describing a conqueror or an empire with an absolute negative regarding satisfaction paints a vivid picture of death and Sheol. Sheol (the grave) and Death were personified in ancient literature as monsters with gaping mouths that are never filled. By using lo' alongside "satisfied," the text demonstrates that the Babylonian empire functions under a law of perpetual, inescapable hunger, acting as an agent of consumption that can never say "enough."

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Lo' functions as the absolute negative particle. Its primary lexical features include:

  • Absolute Negation: It denies the existence, capability, or occurrence of an action or state completely and unconditionally.

  • Inability / Impossibility: When combined with an imperfect verb (such as yisba', "he is satisfied"), it denotes a continuous, ongoing, or absolute impossibility ("he cannot be satisfied" or "he is never satisfied").

  • Structural Prohibition: It is the same particle used in the Ten Commandments to denote permanent, non-negotiable prohibitions ("You shall not").

In Habakkuk 2:5, the negative particle negates the action of the verb completely, indicating that the state of being satisfied is entirely outside the realm of possibility for the proud man.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, lo' is used to draw sharp, absolute contrasts:

  • The Absolute Limit: It is used to depict ironclad limitations of fallen human nature, particularly the inability to find rest or fulfillment apart from God.

  • The Insatiable Maw: The word picture in Habakkuk 2:5 compares the proud man's appetite to Sheol and Death, which cannot (lo') be satisfied. No matter how many nations Babylon conquers, the negative particle emphasizes that the finish line of their desire does not exist. The boundary of their satisfaction is permanently negated.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of this absolute negation exposes the self-destructive nature of pride and sin:

  • The Illusion of Sin: Sin promises ultimate fulfillment but delivers an absolute "not" (lo') to the soul's deep hunger.

  • The Infinity of Lust: Because the proud man attempts to find ultimate satisfaction in finite things (conquest, wealth, power), he is met with an absolute, mathematically certain impossibility of satisfaction.

  • The Sovereign Boundary: Ultimately, God has designed the universe such that the wicked cannot find peace. This highlights the theological truth that true satisfaction is found only in Yahweh. Without Him, humanity is locked under the spiritual reality of lo'—forever consuming, yet never full.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:5, the prophet is addressing the arrogant, conquering Babylonian (Chaldean) empire. The text contrasts the righteous person who lives by faith (Habakkuk 2:4) with the proud man whose soul is not upright.

The use of lo' ("cannot") modifies the verb "be satisfied" to expose the futility of Babylon's geopolitical ambition. The empire's expansion is driven by an insatiable appetite like death itself. The nuance here is pastoral and prophetic: the very thing the proud man seeks (ultimate satisfaction through conquest) is the very thing he is absolutely barred from achieving. His greed is his undoing; because he cannot be satisfied, he will continue to heap up nations until he collapses under the weight of his own debt and judgment.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 13:6 - "Now the land was not [lo'] able to support them, that they might dwell together, for their possessions were so great that they could not [lo'] dwell together."

  • Genesis 32:12 - "And I will make your descendants as the sand of the sea, which cannot [lo'] be numbered for multitude."

  • Proverbs 27:20 - "Hell and Destruction are never [lo'] full; So the eyes of man are never [lo'] satisfied."

  • Proverbs 30:15 - "There are three things that are never [lo'] satisfied, Four that never [lo'] say, 'Enough'..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between the insatiable appetite of the proud man (lo' yisba') and the contentment of the righteous is a major theme throughout biblical wisdom literature. In the New Testament, this concept of absolute spiritual inability to find satisfaction apart from God is resolved in Jesus Christ. In John 4:14, Jesus promises that whoever drinks of the water He gives "will never thirst"—employing the Greek double negative (ou me) to establish an equally absolute negation of spiritual lack.
