STAY AT HOME - ( navah , Strong's H5115 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb navah carries the core meaning of dwelling, abiding, resting, or keeping at peace at home. In Habakkuk 2:5, it is used to expose the restless, insatiable greed of the arrogant Babylonian empire, which cannot remain peacefully within its own borders but must constantly conquer and consume other nations. While a healthy home represents peace, order, and contentment, the wicked man’s pride drives him to a state of perpetual agitation, proving that true rest is impossible apart from faith in God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The verb navah is deeply rooted in the pastoral and nomadic life of the ancient Near East. Finding a place to pitch a tent, settle, and find rest was crucial for survival. A home was not merely a physical structure but a place of security, established boundaries, and quiet satisfaction. The word picture contrasts the peace of domestic stability with the chaotic, unceasing movement of a restless wanderer or a ravenous beast. To stay at home implies a state of contentment with one's God-given portion and respect for the boundaries God has established for individuals and nations alike.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary definition of navah is to rest, remain quiet, or dwell peacefully within one's own domain. In its active form (Qal), the verb describes the physical act of staying or keeping at home. In its causative or intensive forms, it can also mean to prepare a home, beautify, or praise—linking the physical act of preparing a dwelling to the spiritual act of glorifying God. In Habakkuk 2:5, the verb is used in the Qal stem to denote the inability of the proud conqueror to stay at home or rest at ease within his own territory.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Domestic Rest vs. Imperial Conquest: In ancient biblical culture, a well-ordered home was a sign of divine blessing. The righteous man finds contentment in his inheritance and stays within his boundaries. Conversely, the aggressive conqueror cannot stay at home. His ambition acts as a driving force that makes him a wanderer, never satisfied with his own borders.

  • The Sanctuary and Praise: When used in relation to God, the root evokes the preparation of a dwelling place or sanctuary—beautifying a place for God's presence. This connects the physical act of dwelling with the spiritual reality of worship and peace, showing that true resting occurs where God's presence is acknowledged.

4. Theological Implications

  • Pride Breeds Restlessness: Pride makes contentment impossible. The Babylonian empire, characterized by arrogance and intoxication with power, is spiritually homeless because it rejects God's sovereignty.

  • Contentment vs. Covetousness: The righteous person lives by faith, finding rest and home in God. The wicked enlarges his desire like Sheol, seeking to find satisfaction in conquest but remaining permanently unsatisfied. True rest (navah) is a fruit of trusting God, whereas wickedness is inherently turbulent.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:5, the prophet contrasts the proud Chaldeans with the righteous who live by faith (Habakkuk 2:4). The Babylonian king's arrogance, fueled by wine and success, drives him to act like death and Sheol, expanding his borders without limit. His inability to "stay at home" is a self-inflicted curse; his lack of contentment leads to his ultimate downfall. The context demonstrates that geopolitical aggression is a spiritual disease rooted in a lack of internal rest. Because the conqueror refuses to stay at home, God will eventually strip him of his home.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 15:2 – "The LORD is my strength and song, And He has become my salvation; He is my God, and I will praise Him; My father's God, and I will exalt Him."

  • Habakkuk 2:5 – "Indeed, because he transgresses by wine, He is a proud man, And he does not stay at home. Because he enlarges his desire as hell, And he is like death, and cannot be satisfied, He gathers to himself all nations And heaps up for himself all peoples."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Greek translation (LXX) uses a phrasing that emphasizes the ultimate failure of the proud man's plans, stating he will "accomplish nothing." This translation dynamic highlights that a failure to stay at home—transgressing God's boundaries—leads to vanity and emptiness. The Hebrew Masoretic Text, however, preserves the vivid spatial and domestic metaphor: those who seek to capture the whole world end up losing their own home and soul.
