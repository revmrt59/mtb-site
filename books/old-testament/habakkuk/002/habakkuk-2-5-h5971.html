PEOPLES - ( am , Strong's H5971 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word am denotes a group of people bound together by common blood, history, language, or social ties. In the singular, it is frequently used in the Old Testament to designate Israel as Yahweh's special covenant people. However, in its plural form (ammim), as used in Habakkuk 2:5, it refers to the diverse nations and ethnic groups of the earth. In this prophetic context, the word highlights the insatiable, imperialistic greed of the Babylonian empire. Babylon is depicted as a proud, destructive conqueror that treats distinct human cultures and societies as mere plunder to be swallowed up and stacked together, contrasting human tyranny with God's ultimate sovereign ownership over the nations.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The term am is deeply rooted in the concept of relationship, association, and kinship. While other Hebrew terms for a nation, such as goy, tend to emphasize political or geographical boundaries, am focuses on the organic, relational bonds of a community. It carries a familial connotation, evoking the image of a clan or a group of people sharing a common life, identity, and ancestry. In the ancient Near East, conquering a am did not merely mean acquiring territory; it meant disrupting, enslaving, and dismantling a living, breathing community of families.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun am designates a people, nation, kinsfolk, or a collective group of citizens. When used in the singular, it expresses a unique, relational bond, most notably in the covenant phrase "My people" (ammi), used by Yahweh to describe Israel. In the plural form (ammim), the word shifts its focus to the multi-ethnic sea of humanity, encompassing the various Gentile nations of the world. Lexically, the term implies a collection of individuals united by subjective ties of common origin or mutual alliance, rather than a mere arbitrary gathering of people.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament employs am in two primary relational spheres:

  • The Covenant Sphere: Israel is identified as the "people of Yahweh" (am YHWH), set apart from the surrounding nations to be His personal possession.

  • The Universal Sphere: The plural ammim refers to the various global populations.

Poetic and prophetic literature often uses graphic word pictures to describe these peoples. They are sometimes compared to roaring, tumultuous waters that threaten to overwhelm the earth, or to a vast harvest. In prophetic visions, these diverse peoples are also pictured as pilgrims who will one day stream to the mountain of the Lord to worship the one true God in unity, exchanging their chaotic rebellion for divine order.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of ammim in Scripture underscores God's sovereign authority over human history and ethnic diversity. Though human empires attempt to homogenize, exploit, and control the populations of the earth, God is the creator and rightful ruler of every people group. The gathering of "all peoples" by a human conqueror is a tragic, arrogant parody of God's final, redemptive plan. Where human tyrants gather peoples through violence, bloodshed, and exploitation to feed their own pride, Yahweh gathers the peoples in righteousness, justice, and peace to share in His glory.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:5, the prophet exposes the insatiable greed and arrogance of the Chaldean (Babylonian) empire. The empire is personified as an arrogant man who "enlarges his desire as hell" and "is like death, and cannot be satisfied." The text states that he "heaps up for himself all peoples." The verb for "heaping up" or "gathering" evokes an agricultural or pastoral metaphor of harvesting grain or collecting sheep. Babylon treats whole nations and distinct ethnic communities as mere commodities to be piled up like wheat in a barn. This nuance highlights the profound dehumanization of imperial conquest, where human lives and cultures are reduced to a mass of plundered goods to satisfy a ruler's ego.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 25:23 - "And the LORD said to her: 'Two nations are in your womb, Two peoples shall be separated from your body; One people shall be stronger than the other, And the older shall serve the younger.'"

  • Psalm 67:3 - "Let the peoples praise You, O God; Let all the peoples praise You."

  • Psalm 96:10 - "Say among the nations, 'The LORD reigns; The world also is firmly established, It shall not be moved; He shall judge the peoples righteously.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The poetic structure of Habakkuk 2:5 utilizes synonymous parallelism, pairing "all nations" (goyim) with "all peoples" (ammim). This literary device intensifies the scope of Babylon’s wickedness. While "nations" highlights the political structures and governments that Babylon overthrew, "peoples" emphasizes the families, communities, and individuals whose lives were shattered. By using both terms, the prophet demonstrates that the pride of the conqueror destroys both the macro-structures of geopolitical kingdoms and the micro-structures of human communities.
