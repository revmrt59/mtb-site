HEAPS - ( qabats , Strong's H6908 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb qabats primarily means to gather, collect, or assemble people or objects into a centralized location or heap. In the vivid prophetic warning of Habakkuk 2:5, it portrays the voracious and imperialistic appetite of the Babylonian empire, which acts like death and the grave by aggressively collecting and hoarding nations to itself. Rather than a peaceful or protective assembly, this term here underscores an arrogant, predatory conquest that amasses human lives and resources to satisfy an insatiable, self-serving lust for power.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The agricultural and communal life of ancient Israel provides the background for qabats. It commonly describes the harvesting of grain, the gathering of water, or the assembling of a community for worship, counsel, or defense. In an agrarian society, gathering was essential for survival, representing preparation, community strength, and the blessing of a completed harvest. However, when applied to imperial conquest, the word becomes a grotesque parody of harvest. Instead of gathering grain to sustain life, the ruthless conqueror gathers human beings and sovereign nations like spoils of war, piling them up to feed his own ego and fuel his imperial machinery.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The root qabats occurs over 120 times in the Hebrew Bible. It carries the primary sense of drawing together things or individuals that have been scattered or distributed. The semantic range includes:

  • To gather or collect physical objects, such as food, wealth, or stones.

  • To assemble people for military action, national assemblies, or religious feasts.

  • To harvest crops or heap up resources.

In the intensive stems, the verb frequently describes God’s merciful gathering of His scattered covenant people from exile. In the simple active stem used in Habakkuk 2:5, it describes the active, grasping collection of nations by a tyrannical human agent.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • qabats is used of gathering water in the clouds or hands, representing containment and control.

  • It is used of storing up grain during times of plenty, as Joseph did in Egypt, symbolizing wise preparation.

  • In a tender pastoral metaphor, it describes a shepherd gathering young, vulnerable lambs into his arms to carry them close to his heart.

  • In negative contexts, it describes the foolish and greedy amassing of vanity, stolen goods, or dishonest wealth, which ultimately vanishes.

4. Theological Implications

The scriptures present a profound contrast between divine gathering and human gathering. God gathers in mercy, restoration, and covenant faithfulness. He gathers His scattered flock to protect, heal, and feed them, showing Himself to be the ultimate Husbandman and Shepherd. Conversely, proud human empires gather through violence, exploitation, and self-aggrandizement. Habakkuk reveals that such proud "heaping up" of nations is a direct affront to God's sovereignty. By violating the national boundaries set by God and treating human lives as mere commodities, the conqueror mimics the insatiable maw of Sheol. Ultimately, the scriptures warn that those who gather by violence will see their accumulated heaps plundered by God’s righteous judgment.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:5, the prophet exposes the character of the Chaldean (Babylonian) invader. The invader is compared to "death" and "hell" (Sheol), which are never satisfied. The verb qabats ("heaps up") is paired with asaph ("gathers"), creating a poetic parallel that emphasizes a relentless, compulsive hoarding of peoples. The Babylonians do not see nations as human beings made in the image of God, but as mere trophies or livestock to be swept up into their imperial collection. The nuance here is one of violent, unnatural aggregation—compiling nations into an unstable heap that will eventually collapse under the weight of its own injustice.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 41:35: "And let them gather all the food of those good years that are coming, and store up grain under the authority of Pharaoh, and let them keep food in the cities."

  • Deuteronomy 30:3: "that the Lord your God will bring you back from captivity, and have compassion on you, and gather you again from all the nations where the Lord your God has scattered you."

  • Psalm 106:47: "Save us, O Lord our God, And gather us from among the Gentiles, To give thanks to Your holy name, To triumph in Your praise."

  • Proverbs 13:11: "Wealth gained by dishonesty will be diminished, But he who gathers by labor will increase."

  • Isaiah 40:11: "He will feed His flock like a shepherd; He will gather the lambs with His arm, And carry them in His bosom, And gently lead those who are with young."

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a profound irony built into the structure of Habakkuk 2. The Babylonian conqueror "heaps up" (qabats) peoples to serve his glory, but in verse 6, the very nations he has gathered will "take up a taunt song against him." The heap of conquered peoples becomes the chorus of his ruin. The warning of Habakkuk 2:5 serves as a timeless spiritual principle: any accumulation that is driven by pride, greed, and the exploitation of others is inherently unstable and subject to divine undoing. What man gathers in pride, God will scatter in judgment.
