ENLARGES - ( rachab , Strong's H7337 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb rachab fundamentally means to grow wide, broad, or to enlarge. In Habakkuk 2:5, it portrays the insatiable, predatory greed of the Chaldeans (Babylonians), whose arrogant desire expands endlessly like Sheol (the grave) and death. This word contrasts the spacious freedom God desires for His people with the aggressive, self-serving expansion of the wicked, reminding believers that uncontrolled ambition ultimately leads to self-destruction.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The physical root of rachab refers to physical widening, broadening, or opening up a space. In the ancient Near East, space was intimately associated with freedom, safety, and prosperity. To be in a "broad place" meant to be free from distress, whereas narrowness or tight spaces represented affliction and pressure.

When applied metaphorically to human desire, rachab denotes an appetite or ambition that dilates, constantly stretching outward to consume more territory, wealth, and people. It evokes the picture of a throat opening wide to swallow everything in its path, or a net expanding to sweep up entire nations.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Rachab is a primitive root meaning to become wide, broad, or spacious.

  • In the Qal stem, it means to be or grow wide (such as a mouth opening wide in mock triumph or a border expanding).

  • In the Hiphil stem (the causative form used in Habakkuk 2:5), it means to make wide, enlarge, or extend. It can refer to God enlarging a nation's borders, making room for His people, or a wicked person widening their own throat in greed.

  • The semantic range spans physical expansion (territory, tents), emotional relief (enlargement of heart/relief from distress), and ravenous consumption (the widening of appetite/Sheol).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Physical enlargement: Used of extending the borders of a nation or the tent pegs of a dwelling (Exodus 34:24; Isaiah 54:2). Here, enlargement is a sign of blessing and growth under God's hand.

  • Relief and Deliverance: To have one's steps "enlarged" means to be freed from confinement, giving stability and freedom of movement (Psalm 18:36).

  • Arrogance and Appetite: To "enlarge" the throat or desire is to have an insatiable hunger. The grave (Sheol) is personified as "enlarging" its throat to swallow the dead (Isaiah 5:14). Similarly, the proud man "enlarges" his desire like Sheol.

4. Theological Implications

There is a profound biblical theology of space. God is the one who legitimately enlarges His people's territory and hearts in blessing, whereas the wicked attempt to enlarge their own territory through violence and greed.

The tragedy of self-enlargement is that it is a spiritual counterfeit. The proud man seeks to occupy more space at the expense of others, but this artificial expansion mimics Sheol and death. It is a spiritual law that those who try to enlarge their lives through sinful greed will find themselves swallowed by the very destruction they sought to inflict. Ultimate satisfaction is found only when God enlarges the heart to run in the way of His commandments (Psalm 119:32).

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:5, the prophet is describing the Babylonian empire (the Chaldeans). They are characterized by drunkenness, pride, and restlessness ("does not stay at home").

The Hiphil form yarchiyb (from rachab) describes how the proud man "enlarges his desire [nephesh] as hell [Sheol]." Here, nephesh (soul, throat, appetite) is coupled with rachab to describe a throat opened impossibly wide, like the maw of death itself. The Babylonian conquest was not a localized campaign; it was a global campaign of insatiable expansion. The irony is that while they "enlarge" their domain by gathering all nations, they are actually preparing their own ruin, as the subsequent woes (v. 6-20) demonstrate.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Deuteronomy 12:20 - "When the LORD your God enlarges your border as He has promised you, and you say, 'Let me eat meat,' because you long to eat meat, you may eat as much meat as your heart desires." (The Hiphil verb used of God expanding physical territory as a blessing).

  • Psalm 18:36 - "You enlarged my path under me, So my feet did not slip." (The Hiphil verb representing divine protection and freedom from restriction).

  • Isaiah 5:14 - "Therefore Sheol has enlarged its desire And opened its mouth beyond measure; Their glory and their multitude and their pomp And he who is jubilant shall descend into it." (The Hiphil verb showing Sheol's ravenous appetite, paralleling the imagery in Habakkuk).

  • Isaiah 54:2 - "Enlarge the place of your tent, And let them stretch out the curtains of your dwellings; Do not spare; Lengthen your cords, And strengthen your stakes." (The Hiphil imperative used of preparation for abundant blessing and growth).

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Contrast of Nephesh: In Habakkuk 2:4, the "proud" soul (nephesh) is "not upright." In verse 5, this same nephesh (translated "desire" or "appetite") is "enlarged" like Sheol. This shows the direct connection between inner moral corruption (pride) and outward insatiable greed (enlargement).

  • The Concept of Sheol: In Hebrew thought, Sheol is never satisfied (Proverbs 30:15-16). By comparing Babylon's enlargement of desire to Sheol, the prophet exposes the spiritual void at the heart of worldly empires—they are driven by a hunger that cannot be filled because it is detached from God.
