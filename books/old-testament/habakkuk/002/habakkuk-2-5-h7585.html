HELL - ( sheol , Strong's H7585 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word sheol represents the underworld, the grave, or the realm of the dead. In Habakkuk 2:5, it serves as a vivid metaphor for the insatiable greed and relentless ambition of the proud Chaldean empire. Just as the grave continually opens its mouth to consume the living and is never satisfied, so the aggressive conqueror possesses a bottomless desire for power, wealth, and territory. Understanding sheol in this context shifts the focus from a physical location to a spiritual condition—revealing that pride-fueled greed mimics the cold, consuming nature of death itself.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient Near Eastern thought and the Old Testament, sheol is the dark, silent place where all the dead depart, regardless of their moral standing in life. It is depicted as a subterranean realm, deep below the earth, characterized by dust, shadows, and silence. It is often personified as a ravenous beast with an insatiable appetite, possessing gates, bars, and cords that trap its victims. This vivid imagery conveys the absolute inevitability of death and the utter helplessness of humanity in its grip.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Sheol denotes the underworld, the grave, or the pit. It signifies the state or place of the dead. Rather than a place of eternal conscious torment (which is a later development associated with Gehenna in the New Testament), the Old Testament concept of sheol focuses on the termination of earthly life and the descent into the realm of physical decay and shadowy existence. It represents the ultimate destination of physical life, where human activity ceases and the body returns to the dust.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Sheol is frequently personified in Hebrew poetry. It is described as having a mouth that opens wide to swallow the living.

  • It acts as an aggressive hunter, casting cords or snares to trap its prey.

  • It is paired synonymously with terms like "the Pit" (bor), "destruction" (abaddon), and "death" (mavet).

  • The word pictures emphasize its dark, gloomy, and inescapable nature, drawing a stark contrast between the vibrant "land of the living" and the inactive silence of the grave.

4. Theological Implications

In the Old Testament, sheol serves as a sobering reminder of human mortality and the consequences of the Fall. It is a place cut off from the active praise of Yahweh on earth. However, God's sovereignty extends even to sheol. God has the power to ransom souls from its grasp, laying the foundation for the progressive revelation of resurrection and eternal life. Theologically, utilizing sheol as a metaphor for greed links systemic human oppression directly with the spiritual force of death, demonstrating that sinful pride is inherently destructive and anti-life.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:5, the prophet is describing the arrogance of the Chaldean (Babylonian) empire. The conqueror is characterized by a "desire" (literally, "soul" or "appetite," nephesh) that is as vast and unyielding as sheol. The parallel phrase, "And he is like death, and cannot be satisfied," solidifies the metaphor. Babylon's military campaigns and imperial expansion are not driven by rational necessity but by a spiritual pathology—an insatiable appetite for conquest. Just as sheol continually claims lives without ever saying "enough," the Babylonian empire swallows up nations and peoples without ever finding satisfaction. This reveals that unchecked pride and greed are self-destructive cycles that mimic the characteristics of death itself.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

The following passages use the exact Hebrew word sheol to convey the realm of the dead or its insatiable nature:

  • Numbers 16:30 - "But if the Lord creates a new thing, and the earth opens its mouth and swallows them up with all that belongs to them, and they go down alive into the pit [sheol], then you will understand that these men have rejected the Lord."

  • Psalm 16:10 - "For You will not leave my soul in Sheol [sheol], Nor will You allow Your Holy One to see corruption."

  • Proverbs 27:20 - "Hell [sheol] and Destruction are never full; So the eyes of man are never satisfied."

  • Isaiah 5:14 - "Therefore Sheol [sheol] has enlarged itself And opened its mouth beyond measure..."

  • Hosea 13:14 - "I will ransom them from the power of the grave [sheol]; I will redeem them from death. O Death, I will be your plagues! O Grave [sheol], I will be your destruction!"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The link between greed, pride, and sheol highlights a profound psychological and spiritual reality: sin creates an empty void that cannot be filled by worldly acquisition.

  • The comparison to sheol serves as a prophetic warning. Those who align themselves with the characteristics of sheol (devouring others) will ultimately be swallowed up by it. The Babylonians, who acted like sheol toward other nations, would soon experience the judgment of God and descend into physical and national ruin.

  • For the modern preacher, this word study provides a powerful transition from ancient geopolitical critique to a heart-level examination of human desire. When we seek to satisfy our souls with anything other than God, we develop a "Sheol-like" appetite—always consuming, never content.
