TRANSGRESSES - ( bagad , Strong's H898 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb bagad denotes acting faithlessly, deceitfully, or treacherously. In Habakkuk 2:5, it describes the treacherous character of the arrogant conqueror, whose insatiable greed and pride act like intoxicating wine, leading him to betray covenants and exploit other nations. Instead of finding security in his treacherous conquests, the oppressor is ultimately betrayed and undone by his own pride and deceptive security.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew verb bagad is intimately connected to the noun beged, which means a garment, cloak, or covering. This relationship yields a vivid word picture: treachery is a cloaking of one's true motives. To act treacherously is to wrap oneself in a deceptive cover, pretending loyalty, friendship, or peace while secretly harboring hostile intentions. It is the act of wearing a mask of fidelity while actively preparing to betray a trust or violate a covenant.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb bagad means to deal treacherously, act faithlessly, violate a covenant, or transgress a established relationship. The term does not merely describe a minor ethical failure; it identifies a fundamental breach of loyalty and trust. It is used to describe a husband or wife abandoning their marital vows, a political ally violating a treaty, or a covenant partner rebelling against God. It carries the heavy connotation of pillaging, plundering, or deceiving those who have a rightful claim to one's fidelity.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, the word is used in several specific contexts to paint a picture of faithlessness:

  • Marital Infidelity: It describes a spouse who treacherously departs from their marriage partner, breaking the sacred bond of companionship.

  • Covenant Violations: It represents Israel's faithlessness toward Yahweh, where the nation acts like a traitor by worshiping idols while claiming to belong to God.

  • Societal and Brotherly Betrayal: It characterizes those who exploit their neighbors or family members, using deceit to gain a material advantage.

  • Sovereign Plundering: It describes ruthless foreign empires that conquer and pillage weaker nations under the guise of treaty agreements or sheer, unprovoked aggression.

4. Theological Implications

Treachery is an offense against the very character of God, who is eternally faithful (emeth) and steadfast in His covenant love (chesed). Because God is the ultimate guardian of covenants and oaths, any act of faithlessness toward others is ultimately an act of rebellion against Him. The scriptures consistently warn that those who deal treacherously will eventually reap what they sow; their own deceit will catch up to them, and they will be dealt with treacherously by others under the righteous judgment of God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:5, the prophet is describing the rapacious Chaldean (Babylonian) empire. The NKJV translates the term as "transgresses" in the phrase, "Indeed, because he transgresses by wine, he is a proud man."

The contextual nuance here is brilliant. The Chaldeans are a treacherous nation, conquering others without cause. However, their treachery is fueled and mirrored by "wine." Wine is a deceiver; it promises strength, joy, and invincibility, but secretly robs a person of judgment and leads them to ruin. In the same way, the pride of the Chaldeans deceives them. They think they are secure in their treacherous gains, but their greed—which is as insatiable as Sheol and death—is actually setting a trap for their own destruction. Their faithlessness toward others is the very path by which they are betraying themselves.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Jeremiah 3:20 – "Surely, as a wife treacherously departs from her husband, so have you dealt treacherously with Me, O house of Israel,' says the LORD."

  • Hosea 6:7 – "But like men they transgressed the covenant; there they dealt treacherously with Me."

  • Malachi 2:10 – "Have we not all one Father? Has not one God created us? Why do we deal treacherously with one another by profaning the covenant of the fathers?"

  • Isaiah 33:1 – "Woe to you who plunder, though you have not been plundered; and you who deal treacherously, though they have not dealt treacherously with you! When you cease plundering, you will be plundered; and when you make an end of dealing treacherously, they will deal treacherously with you."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A powerful contrast exists between human treachery and divine faithfulness. When God's people or the nations act treacherously, God remains completely trustworthy. In the context of Habakkuk, while the prophet struggles with the reality of a treacherous nation like Babylon being used to discipline Judah, God reassures him that the proud and treacherous will not escape justice. The righteous person, by contrast, must live by his faith and fidelity (Habakkuk 2:4).
