HOW LONG - ( matai , Strong's H4970 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word matai, most frequently paired with the preposition ad to mean "how long," expresses a poignant cry for resolution, justice, or relief from suffering. In the prophetic context of Habakkuk 2:6, it serves as a rhetorical and emotional pivot point, voicing the collective groan of the oppressed under the weight of Babylonian tyranny. This word highlights the biblical truth that God sets boundaries on evil, assuring the faithful that human exploitation has a definitive, divinely appointed expiration date.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The linguistic root of matai points to an extension of time or a stretch of duration. Conceptually, it is an interrogative adverb of time, asking "when?" When prefaced with the preposition ad ("until"), it shifts from a simple inquiry of time to an agonizing, expectant plea: "until when?" or "how long?" Historically and culturally, this phrase is the classic language of lament in the Ancient Near East. It reflects the tension between the immediate, painful experience of suffering and the theological certainty of God's sovereign justice. It is not an abstract philosophical question about time but a deeply personal, existential cry for God to intervene and reestablish cosmic and moral order.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary lexical meaning of matai is "when." When combined as ad-matai, it functions as a single idiom meaning "how long?" or "until when?"

  • It denotes a limit or boundary to a state of affairs, implying that the current situation cannot and should not continue indefinitely.

  • It expresses impatience, weariness, grief, or a demand for accountability.

  • In legal and prophetic contexts, it serves to challenge the status quo, demanding to know when the righteous Judge of the earth will execute His sentence.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, matai (especially as ad-matai) occurs in three main settings:

  • Divine Impatience: God asking His rebellious people or stubborn leaders how long they will refuse to obey Him.

  • Human Lament: The righteous crying out to God in the midst of intense persecution or suffering, wondering when deliverance will arrive.

  • Prophetic Denunciation: A rhetorical device used to expose the absurdity and eventual end of wicked deeds.

The word picture is that of a stretched-out thread of time. The sufferer looks down the long line of their trial, unable to see the end, and cries out for God to cut it short.

4. Theological Implications

  • Matai reveals the linear nature of biblical history. Evil is not an eternal, cyclical reality; it has a starting point and a definitive end.

  • The question "how long?" honors God's sovereignty. By asking how long instead of if, the speaker affirms that God indeed has the power and the intent to end the injustice; they are simply pleading for the timing of that intervention.

  • It exposes human limitation. Believers live in the tension of the "already but not yet," experiencing the painful delay between the promise of God's justice and its physical manifestation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:6, the prophet delivers a "taunt-song" against the proud Chaldean (Babylonian) empire. The text reads: "Woe to him who increases what is not his—how long? And to him who loads himself with many pledges!"

Here, ad-matai ("how long?") is injected as an abrupt, dramatic exclamation. It represents the gasping cry of the nations plundered by Babylon. It functions as an indictment of greed. The text contrasts the rapid, unnatural accumulation of wealth with the suddenness of the end of that accumulation. The rhetorical question "how long?" assures the victims—and warns the oppressors—that this predatory behavior has an absolute, divinely decreed limit. The plunderer's time is running out.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 10:3: "So Moses and Aaron came in to Pharaoh and said to him, 'Thus says the LORD God of the Hebrews: "How long will you refuse to humble yourself before Me? Let My people go, that they may serve Me."'"

  • Numbers 14:27: "How long shall I bear with this evil congregation who complain against Me? I have heard the complaints which the children of Israel make against Me."

  • Psalm 6:3: "My soul also is greatly troubled; But You, O LORD—how long?"

  • Psalm 90:13: "Return, O LORD! How long? And have compassion on Your servants."

  • Proverbs 6:9: "How long will you slumber, O sluggard? When will you rise from your sleep?"

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Septuagint (LXX), matai in Habakkuk 2:6 is translated using heōs tinos (literally, "until when?"). This Greek equivalent is used frequently in the New Testament, notably by Jesus in Matthew 17:17 ("How long shall I be with you? How long shall I bear with you?") and by the martyrs in heaven in Revelation 6:10 ("How long, O Lord, holy and true, until You judge and avenge our blood...?"). This connects the prophetic laments of the Old Testament directly with the ultimate hope of cosmic justice in the New Testament.
