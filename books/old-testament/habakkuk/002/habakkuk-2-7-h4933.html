BOOTY - ( meshissah , Strong's H4933 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word meshissah refers to spoil, plunder, or booty taken during war. It describes the physical goods, wealth, and possessions stripped from a defeated enemy by a victorious army. In the context of biblical prophecy, it serves as a sobering reminder of divine justice: those who greedily plunder other nations will ultimately be plundered themselves. In Habakkuk 2:7, this word emphasizes the sudden, ironic reversal of fortunes awaiting the prideful Babylonian empire.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word meshissah is derived from the root verb shasah or shasash, which means "to plunder," "to spoil," or "to pillage." The term evokes the chaotic and devastating aftermath of an ancient military invasion. When a city fell, the conquering soldiers ransacked homes, palaces, and temples, seizing everything of value—silver, gold, livestock, and even people—to carry away as the spoils of war. This "booty" represents the tangible fruit of violent conquest and unchecked greed. In the ancient Near East, acquiring booty was a primary motivation for warfare, representing both economic enrichment and the absolute humiliation of the conquered nation.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Meshissah is a feminine noun that denotes the object of plundering: spoil, booty, prey, or plunder. The semantic range is tightly focused on the goods confiscated by force in times of war or lawlessness. It represents the state of being pillaged or the actual wealth that has been stripped away. The term conveys not just the loss of goods, but the complete vulnerability and subjugation of those who have been plundered.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

In the Old Testament, meshissah is used to describe both literal military plunder and metaphorical spiritual consequences. It is depicted as:

  • A consequence of covenant disobedience: When Israel turned away from God, He withdrew His protection, allowing their enemies to make them "booty" (2 Kings 21:14; Isaiah 42:24).

  • An instrument of divine judgment: God uses foreign nations to plunder His people as a disciplinary measure, but He also turns the tables of justice upon those conquerors.

  • A sudden and complete loss: The word picture denotes a rapid stripping away of security, leaving the victim utterly bare and impoverished.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of meshissah lies in the principle of divine retributive justice, often expressed as lex talionis (the law of retaliation). God rules over history with absolute equity. He does not allow violent oppression and theft to go unchecked. When nations amass wealth through extortion and bloody conquest, they build their empires on a foundation of sand. God ensures that the plunderers will eventually be plundered. This demonstrates that human power is temporary, wealth gained by oppression is fleeting, and God ultimately vindicates the oppressed by stripping the proud of their ill-gotten gains.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:7, the prophet delivers a series of "woes" against the Chaldeans (Babylonians). Babylon was an insatiable empire, swallowing up nations and gathering "booty" from across the known world. In verse 7, God asks a rhetorical question that carries a sudden, terrifying irony: "Will they not rise up suddenly who bite you? Will they not awaken who oppress you? And you will become booty for them?"

The nuance here is the absolute certainty and suddenness of Babylon’s downfall. The very nations Babylon terrorized and plundered would wake up, rise up, and turn Babylon itself into "booty" (meshissah). The predator becomes the prey. This serves as a pastoral comfort to Habakkuk, reminding him that although the wicked seem to prosper through violence in the short term, God's sovereign justice will inevitably balance the scales.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 2 Kings 21:14: "I will forsake the remnant of My inheritance and deliver them into the hand of their enemies; and they shall become victims of plunder and spoil for all their enemies,"

  • Isaiah 42:24: "Who gave Jacob for plunder, and Israel to the robbers? Was it not the Lord, He against whom we have sinned? For they would not walk in His ways, nor were they obedient to His law."

  • Jeremiah 30:16: "Therefore all those who devour you shall be devoured; and all your adversaries, every one of them, shall go into captivity; those who plunder you shall become plunder, and all who prey upon you I will make a prey."

  • Zephaniah 1:13: "Therefore their goods shall become booty, and their houses a desolation; they shall build houses, but not inhabit them; they shall plant vineyards, but not drink their wine."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The word meshissah underscores the futility of material security apart from God. The Babylonians believed their massive wealth and fortified walls made them invincible. However, when God decrees judgment, wealth is transformed instantly from an asset of security into a target for invaders. This Old Testament principle aligns with the New Testament warning of Jesus in Matthew 6:19-20 regarding treasures on earth, where moth, rust, and thieves destroy.
