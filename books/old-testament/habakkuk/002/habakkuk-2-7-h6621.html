SUDDENLY - ( peta' , Strong's H6621 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word peta' denotes an event occurring in a single, startling instant, completely catching those affected off guard. In Habakkuk 2:7, it characterizes the swift and unexpected arrival of divine judgment upon the oppressive Babylonian empire, illustrating that though God's justice may seem delayed, its execution will be immediate, decisive, and irreversible when the appointed time arrives.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root of peta' is linked to the concept of a blink or the sudden opening of an eye. It conveys the image of a split-second transition from one state to another—from peace to ruin, or from security to captivity. In the ancient Near East, empires built massive fortifications and relied on vast armies to secure their longevity. The word picture behind peta' shatters this illusion of permanent security. It compares the collapse of an empire not to a slow, predictable decay, but to a sudden, violent blink of an eye that catches the watchmen completely unaware.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word peta' is an adverb meaning "suddenly," "in an instant," or "unexpectedly." It describes an occurrence that happens without warning, leaving no opportunity for preparation, evasion, or defense. It denotes a swift, abrupt intervention that interrupts the normal, expected flow of life, bringing about an immediate change in circumstances.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

In the Old Testament, peta' is frequently paired with another Hebrew word for suddenly (pit'om) to create an intense literary emphasis on extreme rapidity, often translated as "in an instant, suddenly" or "very suddenly." It is commonly used in contexts of:

  • Unexpected physical death or sudden casualty.

  • The abrupt, catastrophic collapse of structural walls.

  • The sudden onset of ruin upon those who persist in wickedness and ignore godly reproof.

The word paints a picture of a snare snapping shut or a dam bursting; there is a long period of quiet pressure, followed by a single, catastrophic moment of release.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, peta' teaches that God's patience must never be mistaken for God's approval or apathy. While divine judgment may tarry for generations, its ultimate execution is instantaneous. The word highlights the fragility of human power and the absolute sovereignty of God over time and history. Human systems of oppression, no matter how deeply entrenched, exist at the pleasure of a sovereign God who can dismantle them in a single moment. It serves as a reminder that the spiritual reality of divine accountability is always closer than the physical senses suggest.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:7, the prophet addresses the proud Chaldeans (Babylonians). They have plundered many nations, accumulated wealth through extortion, and built their empire on bloodshed. They believe they are untouchable. However, God asks a rhetorical question: "Will they not rise up suddenly who oppress you?" The use of peta' here indicates that the very nations Babylon plundered will unexpectedly rise up like a sleeping creditor waking up to demand payment with interest. The collapse of Babylon will not be a gradual decline, but a shocking, rapid overthrow that will leave them completely bewildered and despoiled.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Proverbs 29:1: "He who is often rebuked, and hardens his neck, Will suddenly be destroyed, and that without remedy."

  • Isaiah 30:13: "Therefore this iniquity shall be to you Like a breach ready to fall, A bulge in a high wall, Whose breaking comes suddenly, in an instant."

  • Proverbs 6:15: "Therefore his calamity shall come suddenly; Suddenly he shall be broken without remedy."

7. Other Relevant Insights

For the pastor and teacher, peta' serves as both a solemn warning and a profound comfort. It warns the complacent that their apparent peace is a dangerous illusion, as judgment can strike in a heartbeat. Conversely, it comforts the suffering saint. When believers face long, grueling seasons of trial and oppression, peta' reminds them that deliverance, like judgment, can occur in an instant. God can alter a nation's trajectory, a community's status, or a believer's circumstances in the blink of an eye.
