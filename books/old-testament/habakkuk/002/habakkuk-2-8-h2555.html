VIOLENCE - ( chamas , Strong's H2555 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word chamas refers to cold-blooded, unethical violence, social injustice, and cruel oppression. It goes beyond mere physical force, representing a systemic violation of God's moral order and the legal rights of others. In the context of Habakkuk 2:8, it describes the ruthless plunder and devastating destruction inflicted by the Babylonian Empire upon nations, cities, and the land itself, rendering them subject to God's ultimate justice of retaliatory retribution.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun chamas evokes the shattering of societal and moral boundaries. In the Ancient Near East, it stood as the exact opposite of mishpat (justice) and tsedaqah (righteousness). Rather than describing a simple physical conflict, it paints a picture of societal collapse where the powerful prey upon the weak without restraint. It is the law of the jungle where brute strength replaces covenant faithfulness. The word first appears in a major theological context in Genesis 6, where the "earth was filled with violence," prompting God to cleanse the creation with the Flood. It represents an offense so egregious that it threatens to undo the very order of God's creation.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word chamas is a masculine noun that means violence, wrong, cruelty, injustice, and damage. It indicates physical harm that violates ethical and legal boundaries, cold-blooded murder, ruthless plunder, and systemic oppression. The corresponding verb chamas means to treat violently, wrong, or strip away by force. It carries a heavy moral connotation, signifying an intentional, malicious disregard for the rights of God and fellow human beings. It is not accidental harm, but a deliberate act of self-aggrandizement at the expense of another's life, property, or dignity.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Systemic and Societal Oppression: It is frequently paired with shod (ruin/destruction) to depict the structural evil in societies where the wealthy and powerful exploit the vulnerable.

  • Legal Injustice: The term is used in legal contexts to describe a false or malicious witness (ed chamas), whose testimony seeks to destroy another's life through the manipulation of the legal system.

  • Metaphor of Clothing: The wicked are described as wearing violence like a garment, showing that cruelty has become their defining characteristic, visible to all.

  • Stolen Treasures: It is used metaphorically for the wealth accumulated through extortion and robbery, which is described as storing up violence in one's palaces.

4. Theological Implications

In the Old Testament, chamas is deeply offensive to the character of Yahweh. God is the ultimate protector of the vulnerable, and His soul hates those who love violence. Because God is holy and just, He cannot remain silent in the presence of chamas. Thus, widespread violence is the primary catalyst for divine judgment. When societies abandon covenant justice and embrace violence, they invite God's wrath, which often comes in the form of lex talionis—the law of retaliation—where the violent are destroyed by their own violence.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:8, the word chamas is used in God’s second response to the prophet. Habakkuk had initially cried out in chapter 1 because of the chamas he saw within Judah. God responded by raising up the Chaldeans (Babylonians) as an instrument of judgment. However, the Chaldeans themselves became guilty of extreme chamas on a global scale.

In Habakkuk 2:8, God pronounces a woe against Babylon: "Because you have plundered many nations... because of men's blood and the violence of the land and of the city." Here, chamas refers to the ruthless military conquests, geopolitical devastation, and ecological destruction wrought by the Babylonian empire. They plundered lands, slaughtered populations, and destroyed Jerusalem ("the city"). The contextual nuance is ironclad: Babylon’s empire was built on chamas, and therefore, by the righteous decree of God, they would be plundered by the remnant of the nations. Their own violence would return upon their heads.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 6:11: "The earth also was corrupt before God, and the earth was filled with violence."

  • Psalm 11:5: "The LORD tests the righteous, But the wicked and the one who loves violence His soul hates."

  • Proverbs 4:17: "For they eat the bread of wickedness, And drink the wine of violence."

  • Amos 3:10: "'For they do not know to do right,' Says the LORD, 'Who store up violence and robbery in their palaces.'"

  • Habakkuk 1:2: "O LORD, how long shall I cry, And You will not hear? Even cry out to You, 'Violence!' And You will not save."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A striking linguistic reality exists for modern readers: the Hebrew word chamas is phonetically identical to the name of the modern militant organization "Hamas." While the Arabic name "Hamas" is an acronym meaning "zeal" or "enthusiasm," the homophonic connection to the ancient Hebrew word for "cruel violence" and "injustice" provides a somber biblical resonance. Additionally, the term chamas is often used by the prophets as a cry of distress. To shout "Violence!" (chamas!) in the ancient street was the equivalent of shouting "Help! Murder!" or "Police!" It was a formal legal appeal to the community or the king for immediate intervention against an active crime.
