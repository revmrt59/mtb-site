DWELL - ( yashab , Strong's H3427 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yashab means to sit, remain, dwell, or inhabit. In the context of Habakkuk 2:8, it refers to those who permanently reside or inhabit a land or city, specifically emphasizing the helpless population of Judah and Jerusalem who became the victims of Babylonian aggression and imperial violence.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • yashab is a foundational Hebrew root appearing over 1,000 times in the Old Testament.

  • The concrete, physical origin of the word relates to the physical act of "sitting down" or "taking a seat," such as a king sitting on a throne or an individual sitting on the ground.

  • From this physical posture of sitting, the word expanded semantically to denote "remaining," "staying," and ultimately "dwelling" or "permanently residing."

  • The word picture represents settled existence, stability, and peaceful occupation of a territory. It contrasts sharply with wandering, nomadism, or displacement. To "dwell" is to have a home, a community, and a place of belonging.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The basic semantic range of yashab includes:

  • To sit down, seat oneself, or remain sitting.

  • To dwell, inhabit, or reside in a house, city, or country.

  • To abide, remain, or stay (temporarily or permanently).

  • To sit as a judge, king, or assembly, implying authority and governance.

  • In the active participle form (yosheb), the word functions as a noun meaning "inhabitant" or "resident." In Habakkuk 2:8, the plural participle describes "all the inhabitants" or "all who dwell" in the land and the city.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Throne and Rule: Sitting on a throne (yashab al-kisse) represents ruling with authority. God Himself is described as "enthroned" or "dwelling" in the heavens, ruling over creation in absolute sovereignty.

  • Secure Dwelling: Dwelling in safety (yashab labetach) is a common covenantal idiom for peace, security, and agricultural prosperity under God's protective care.

  • The Defeated Inhabitant: When a city is conquered, its "dwellers" are either slain or violently displaced. The loss of the ability to yashab in one's land is the ultimate picture of societal collapse and exile.

4. Theological Implications

  • God's covenantal desire is for His people to dwell securely in the Promised Land. The land was given as a permanent inheritance where families could cultivate homes, pass down heritages, and worship Yahweh in peace.

  • Violating this peaceful dwelling through systemic bloodshed and injustice is a direct affront to God's moral order. The earth was created to be inhabited (yashab) and cultivated, not ravaged by violence.

  • Because God champions the cause of the vulnerable, those who violently uproot and slaughter peaceful "dwellers" face divine retribution. Babylon's destruction of peaceful habitations guaranteed its own eventual overthrow by a sovereign God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Habakkuk 2:8, the prophet pronounces a woe of judgment against Chaldea (Babylon) for plundering nations, shedding human blood, and committing "violence of the land and of the city, and of all who dwell in it."

  • "All who dwell in it" refers to the vulnerable population of Jerusalem and Judah. These were not active combatants in an equal war, but peaceful residents whose daily lives, families, and homes were shattered by imperial greed.

  • By targeting the "dwellers," Babylon did not merely engage in geopolitical warfare; they shattered the basic order of human life that God established. The contrast is between the aggressive, restless plunderer and the peaceful, settled inhabitant.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 13:12 - "Abram dwelt in the land of Canaan, and Lot dwelt in the cities of the plain and pitched his tent even as far as Sodom."

  • Psalm 91:1 - "He who dwells in the secret place of the Most High Shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty."

  • Isaiah 40:22 - "It is He who sits above the circle of the earth, And its inhabitants are like grasshoppers, Who stretches out the heavens like a curtain, And spreads them out like a tent to dwell in;"

  • Jeremiah 25:5 - "They said, 'Repent now everyone of his evil way and his evil doings, and dwell in the land that the LORD has given to you and your fathers forever and ever.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • yashab is frequently contrasted with gur (to sojourn or stay temporarily as a stranger). While sojourning implies temporary vulnerability and a lack of land rights, dwelling (yashab) implies permanent rights, citizenship, and security.

  • Babylon's invasion stripped the permanent "dwellers" of their status, turning them into exiled sojourners in a foreign land.

  • In prophetic literature, a land with "no one dwelling in it" serves as the ultimate picture of divine judgment, signaling that the life, joy, and order of a region have been completely extinguished.
