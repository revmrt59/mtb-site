PLUNDERED - ( shalal , Strong's H7997 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb shalal refers to the act of stripping a defeated enemy of their valuable goods, property, and assets, typically in the context of warfare or violent conquest. In Habakkuk 2:8, it describes the aggressive, predatory looting committed by the Babylonian empire against neighboring nations. This word conveys more than mere theft; it represents the systematic, violent stripping of a people's livelihood. The divine irony of the passage is that the plunderer will ultimately become the plundered, demonstrating God's sovereign justice where the measure used by the oppressor is measured back to them.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near East, warfare was not merely about territorial expansion or political dominance; it was highly driven by economic gain. When a city or nation fell, the victorious army did not simply occupy the land. They systematically stripped it bare of its wealth, including gold, silver, livestock, agricultural produce, and even the conquered people themselves who were taken as slaves. This process of stripping and carrying away the spoils of war is the core of shalal. It presents a vivid, tragic picture of complete humiliation and sudden destitution for the victim, who is left entirely empty-handed and exposed.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb shalal means to spoil, plunder, loot, or strip. It denotes the active, forceful seizure of booty by a victor. Lexically, the word emphasizes the physical act of gathering and carrying off the property of another after rendering them helpless. It is distinct from secret theft, carrying the weight of open, violent, and insolent exploitation. In its grammatical forms, it describes both the initiation of the plunder and the state of being completely stripped of resources.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, shalal is used to paint pictures of aggressive prey-capture.

  • It is used of military conquests where armies divide the spoil as a celebratory climax to victory.

  • It is used metaphorically to describe the actions of wicked rulers and greedy exploiters who treat the poor and vulnerable as their personal plunder, stripping them of their basic human rights and dignity.

  • The word picture is that of a predatory beast hovering over its kill, tearing away every valuable piece of flesh until nothing but bare bones remain.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, shalal highlights God’s intense hatred for exploitation and ill-gotten gain. The Scriptures consistently reveal that wealth accumulated through the violent plundering of others is an abomination to Yahweh. Furthermore, the use of this word establishes the biblical principle of retributive justice (the law of retribution). God is the ultimate defender of the weak. When empires or individuals enrich themselves by plundering the defenseless, God promises to reverse their fortunes. The sovereign Lord of history ensures that those who live by plundering will ultimately become the plunder of others under His divine judgment.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:8, the prophet is delivering a series of "woe" oracles against the Chaldeans (Babylon). Babylon was a ravenous empire that built its opulence on the back of conquered nations. The text states, "Because you have plundered many nations..." This refers to Babylon's ruthless stripping of Jerusalem and surrounding kingdoms. However, the verse immediately shifts to divine retaliation: "...all the remnant of the people shall plunder you." The specific nuance here is the absolute certainty of reciprocal justice. The exact same verb is used for both Babylon’s crime (shallota) and their eventual punishment (yeshalluka). This linguistic symmetry emphasizes that Babylon's judgment will not merely be generic ruin, but a precise, mirroring punishment where they are stripped in the exact manner they stripped others.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Jeremiah 50:10: "And Chaldea shall be a plunder; All who plunder her shall be satisfied," says the LORD.

  • Ezekiel 38:12: "to take plunder and to take booty, to stretch out your hand against the waste places that are again inhabited, and against a people gathered from the nations, who have acquired livestock and goods, who dwell in the middle of the land."

  • Ezekiel 39:10: "They will not take wood from the field nor cut down any from the forests, for they will burn the weapons with fire; they will plunder those who plundered them, and pillage those who pillaged them," says the Lord GOD.

7. Other Relevant Insights

While shalal is an Old Testament Hebrew word, its conceptual truth carries over powerfully into the New Testament. Spiritual plundering is reversed through the work of Jesus Christ. In Matthew 12:29, Jesus speaks of entering the strong man’s house and "plundering" his goods. Through His death and resurrection, Christ disarmed the principalities and powers of darkness, plundering the kingdom of Satan to set the captives free. What the enemy plundered through sin and death, Christ has reclaimed and restored in victorious triumph.
