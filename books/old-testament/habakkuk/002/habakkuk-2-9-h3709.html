POWER - ( kaph , Strong's H3709 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word kaph refers literally to the hollow of the hand, the palm, or the sole of the foot. Metaphorically, it represents a person's immediate grasp, custody, control, or power. In Habakkuk 2:9, the term denotes the clutching grip or reach of calamity. The prideful oppressor builds his high stronghold in a desperate, self-reliant attempt to escape the reach or "grasp" of divine judgment and earthly disaster.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete, physical origin of kaph is the curved, hollowed palm of the human hand or the sole of the foot. It is derived from a root meaning to curve, bend, or bow down. Unlike yad, which represents the hand and arm as instruments of active strength, direction, and agency, kaph focuses on the cupped hand—the palm that holds, grasps, clenches, or covers. It evokes the image of catching water, holding small objects, or physically clutching something within one's immediate grasp. This physical image transitions naturally into a legal and theological metaphor for possession, power, and inescapable custody.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The literal meaning of kaph is the curved palm of the hand or the curved sole of the foot. It is also used to describe curved objects, such as the hollow socket of a hip joint or the gold pans and incense spoons used in tabernacle worship.

Metaphorically, kaph denotes:

  • Power, control, or custody (being "in the grasp of" someone).

  • The reach or clutching power of an enemy or predator.

  • Responsibility or personal accountability, often symbolized by clean or blood-stained palms.

In Habakkuk 2:9, the word is translated as "power," specifically referencing the grasping reach of adversity or ruin.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament utilizes kaph to paint several vivid pictures:

  • The Grip of the Enemy: When David is delivered from his enemies and from Saul, he celebrates being rescued from their kaph—their physical clutch or palm. To be in an enemy's kaph is to be completely at their mercy, waiting for the hand to squeeze shut.

  • The Sole of the Foot: The dove sent from Noah’s ark found no resting place for the kaph of her foot, illustrating a world completely submerged and hostile to life.

  • Worship and Offering: The golden spoons or pans in the temple were kappoth (the plural of kaph), representing hollowed vessels designed to hold precious incense before the presence of God.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, kaph reminds believers of the limits of human sovereignty and the absolute reach of divine justice. Humans naturally seek to build fortresses to keep themselves out of the "grasp" of vulnerability, suffering, and judgment.

  • The Illusion of Escape: The wicked seek to lift themselves above the reach of calamity, but no earthly height can place a person beyond the reach of God's sovereign hand.

  • The Contrast of Grasp vs. Grace: While the wicked try to escape the grasp (kaph) of disaster through evil gain, scripture contrasts this with the ultimate security of being inscribed on the palms (kappayim) of God’s hands. True security is not found in escaping the reach of trouble by human effort, but in being held by the sovereign hand of God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:9, the prophet pronounces a divine "woe" over the Babylonian Empire (and by extension, any prideful, greedy person). The oppressor "covets evil gain for his house" to "set his nest on high." This is the image of an eagle building its nest on an inaccessible cliffside, secure from ground predators.

The goal of this high-altitude security is "that he may be delivered from the power [grasp] of disaster." The oppressor believes that if he gains enough wealth and builds high enough walls, the clutching hand (kaph) of calamity will not be able to reach up and grab him. However, the context reveals this security is a delusion; the very stones of the wall and timber of the house will cry out in judgment against him, bringing him down to the dust.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 8:9: "But the dove found no resting place for the sole of her foot, and she returned to him into the ark, for the waters were on the face of the whole earth."

  • 2 Samuel 22:1: "Then David spoke to the LORD the words of this song, on the day when the LORD had delivered him from the hand of all his enemies, and from the hand of Saul."

  • Psalm 91:12: "In their hands they shall bear you up, Lest you dash your foot against a stone."

  • Psalm 119:109: "My life is continually in my hand, Yet I do not forget Your law."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Hebrew idioms, to put one's life in one's kaph (as seen in Psalm 119:109) means to take a massive risk, exposing oneself to immediate danger where life could slip through the fingers at any moment. In Habakkuk 2:9, the prideful ruler thinks he has successfully taken his life out of danger's reach, placing his destiny safely in his own hands. God's response in the subsequent verses demonstrates that true safety is never a matter of geography or wealth, but of righteousness and trust in the Lord.
