SET - ( sum , Strong's H7760 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb sum (or sim) is a fundamental and highly versatile root in the Old Testament, primarily meaning "to put, place, set, or establish." In Habakkuk 2:9, it is used to describe the arrogant attempt of the Chaldeans to secure themselves against calamity by building their "nest" on high. It conveys a deliberate, willful action of positioning oneself in a place of perceived absolute security and sovereignty, representing a prideful effort that directly opposes the sovereign purposes of God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root sum fundamentally conveys the action of deliberately putting or placing an object in a specific location or state. In the ancient Near East, safety and security were often sought by building fortresses on high, inaccessible rocky cliffs, much like an eagle positions its nest out of reach of predators. The word evokes a physical picture of someone carefully selecting a spot, lifting an object, and securing it firmly in place so that it cannot be easily moved, disturbed, or conquered.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb sum means to put, place, set, lay, or establish. Depending on the grammatical and narrative context, its semantic range includes:

  • To position an object physically in a specific place (e.g., placing hands on someone, setting a crown on a head).

  • To establish or ordain a state of affairs, such as making a covenant, establishing laws, or appointing leaders.

  • To direct one's attention or mind toward something (e.g., "setting one's heart" on something).

  • To transform or make something into another state (e.g., making a city a heap of ruins).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, sum appears in various figurative and literal contexts that paint vivid pictures:

  • The Nest on High: In Habakkuk 2:9 and parallel passages, setting a nest on high symbolizes prideful self-sufficiency and the illusion of escaping divine judgment through human effort and strategic strongholds.

  • Placing of the Name: God chooses a physical place to put or set His Name, signifying His covenant ownership, presence, and authority.

  • Setting the Heart: The phrase "to set the heart" implies deep, focused, and purposeful attention, whether in obedience to God or in stubborn opposition.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, sum contrasts human attempts at self-establishment with God's sovereign acts of establishing. When humans attempt to "set" their own destiny, security, or moral boundaries independent of God, it constitutes rebellion. True security and permanent establishment belong to God alone, who sets the boundaries of the earth, sets kings on their thrones, and establishes His people. The human attempt to self-secure through ill-gotten gain is a direct challenge to the Creator's right to establish justice and judgment.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 2:9, the prophet pronounces a woe against the Neo-Babylonian Empire. The empire built its security on extortion, plunder, and violence. The phrase "That he may set his nest on high" compares Babylon to an eagle building an inaccessible nest on a mountain crag. The nuance of sum here is one of defiant self-preservation and supreme arrogance. The Babylonians believed they could position themselves beyond the reach of calamity. However, their deliberate setting of their nest is met by God's sovereign promise to bring them down, proving that no human placement is secure against divine judgment.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:8: "The LORD God planted a garden eastward in Eden, and there He put the man whom He had formed."

  • Numbers 24:21: "Then he looked on the Kenites, and he took up his oracle and said: 'Firm is your dwelling place, and your nest is set in the rock;'"

  • Job 1:8: "Then the LORD said to Satan, 'Have you considered My servant Job, that there is none like him on the earth, a blameless and upright man, one who fears God and shuns evil?'"

  • Obadiah 1:4: "'Though you ascend as high as the eagle, and though you set your nest among the stars, from there I will bring you down,' says the LORD."

  • Psalms 66:9: "Who keeps our soul among the living, and does not allow our feet to be moved."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The metaphor of the "nest" combined with sum is a classic ancient Near Eastern idiom for building fortified citadels on high hills. The Edomites, residing in the rocky clefts of Petra, shared this exact delusion of impregnability. This highlights a universal human tendency: using wealth and strategic positioning to escape the vulnerability of human existence, forgetting that the highest heights are fully accessible to God's hand.
