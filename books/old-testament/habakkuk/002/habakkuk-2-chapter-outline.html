Habakkuk 2 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Habakkuk 2 begins as the prophet stands his watch, and the LORD answers with a vision to be written plainly and awaited at its appointed time, setting the proud, whose soul is not upright, against the just who “shall live by his faith” (Habakkuk 2:4). Five woes taken up by the plundered nations then announce the downfall of the proud oppressor, while the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the LORD’s glory and all the earth is called to keep silence before Him in His holy temple.

Chapter Sections

1. Habakkuk 2:1–3 — The Watch and the Written Vision

Habakkuk resolves, “I will stand my watch” (Habakkuk 2:1), to see what God will say to him. The LORD tells him to write the vision and make it plain on tablets, for it is for an appointed time and will surely come: “Though it tarries, wait for it” (Habakkuk 2:3).

2. Habakkuk 2:4–5 — The Proud and the Just

The LORD sets the proud, whose soul is not upright in him, against the righteous: “But the just shall live by his faith” (Habakkuk 2:4). The proud man is never satisfied, enlarging his desire like hell and death and gathering all nations and peoples to himself.

3. Habakkuk 2:6–8 — The First Woe: The Plunderer Plundered

The nations take up a taunt against the oppressor: “Woe to him who increases What is not his” (Habakkuk 2:6). Because he has plundered many nations and shed men’s blood, his creditors will rise up suddenly and the remnant of the peoples will plunder him.

4. Habakkuk 2:9–11 — The Second Woe: A House Built on Evil Gain

Woe comes to the one who covets evil gain for his house so that he may set his nest on high and escape disaster, for he has cut off many peoples and sinned against his own soul. The very stone of the wall and the beam of the timbers will cry out against him.

5. Habakkuk 2:12–14 — The Third Woe: A City Built with Bloodshed

Woe comes to the one who builds a town with bloodshed, for by the LORD of hosts the nations weary themselves in vain. “For the earth will be filled With the knowledge of the glory of the LORD, As the waters cover the sea” (Habakkuk 2:14).

6. Habakkuk 2:15–17 — The Fourth Woe: The Cup of Shame

Woe comes to the one who makes his neighbor drunk to look on his nakedness, for he will be filled with shame instead of glory when the cup of the LORD’s right hand is turned against him. The violence he did to Lebanon and the plunder of its beasts will cover him.

7. Habakkuk 2:18–20 — The Fifth Woe: Mute Idols and the LORD in His Holy Temple

A carved image is a teacher of lies, and woe comes to the one who tells wood and silent stone to awake and teach, for there is no breath in them at all. In contrast, “the LORD is in His holy temple. Let all the earth keep silence before Him” (Habakkuk 2:20).
