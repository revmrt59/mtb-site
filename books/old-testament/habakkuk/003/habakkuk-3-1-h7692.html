SHIGIONOTH - ( shigyônôt , Strong's H7692 )

Summary:

The Hebrew term shigyônôt refers to a highly emotional, rapid, or irregular musical rhythm or poetic meter used in worship. Derived from a root meaning to reel, stray, or wander, the word describes a song or prayer characterized by passionate, variable emotions and rapid shifts in tone. In Habakkuk 3:1, it designates the musical and liturgical style for the prophet’s triumphant prayer of faith, signaling to the director that the prayer should be accompanied by intense, stirring, and dramatic musical accompaniment.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The term is the plural form of shiggayon.

  • The verbal root shagah means to err, wander, reel, or go astray, often associated with intoxication or intense, overwhelming emotion.

  • Culturally, this suggests a composition that does not follow a static, rigid structure but instead flows with passionate, variable movements. It represents an emotional, ecstatic, or rhapsodic musical expression.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Shigyônôt is a technical musical or liturgical term indicating a wild, passionate, or irregular character. It denotes a song or prayer of variable, rapid rhythms and intense emotion.

  • The singular form indicates a wild, passionate song or poem with rapid changes of rhythm. The plural form in Habakkuk 3:1 indicates that the following prayer contains multiple shifts in rhythm, tempo, and emotion, fitting its transition from fearful awe to ecstatic praise.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The root shagah paints a picture of someone reeling or wandering under the influence of strong wine or overwhelming passion. Translated into music, this represents a melody that reels or wanders through various keys, tempos, and dynamics.

  • It is not a chaotic mess, but a deliberate artistic representation of overwhelming spiritual emotion, where the soul is so captivated by God's majesty that it cannot be contained in a standard, predictable cadence.

4. Theological Implications

  • Worship is not merely intellectual; it engages the deep affections of the soul. The use of shigyônôt demonstrates that complex, heavy theological truths—such as God’s judgment and His sovereign mercy—rightfully evoke intense emotional responses.

  • Biblical prayer can be raw, dynamic, and dramatic. The shifting tempos of the music mirror the shifting spiritual state of the believer, transitioning from trembling fear of divine judgment to ecstatic joy in divine salvation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Habakkuk 3:1, this term introduces the prophet’s magnificent prayer. Habakkuk has just heard God's announcement of the coming Babylonian invasion and God's ultimate justice.

  • The prayer begins with terror and trembling and ends with triumphant, dancing joy.

  • The shigyônôt format is perfectly suited for this transition. The irregular, rapid musical style accommodates both the dark, thunderous movements of God's marching judgment and the soaring, joyful leaps of faith at the conclusion.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Since shigyônôt is the plural form, it only occurs once in the Hebrew Bible (Habakkuk 3:1). However, its singular form, shiggayon, occurs in Psalm 7:1.

  • Psalm 7:1 — "A Shiggaion of David, which he sang to the LORD concerning the words of Cush, a Benjamite."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The Septuagint translates this phrase in Habakkuk 3:1 as meta odes, meaning "with a song" or "upon songs," recognizing its musical character but omitting the specific nuance of the irregular rhythm.

  • The musical direction at the end of the chapter, "To the Chief Musician. With my stringed instruments" in Habakkuk 3:19, confirms that the entire third chapter was intended for public, liturgical performance in the temple, utilizing the specific shigyônôt style.
