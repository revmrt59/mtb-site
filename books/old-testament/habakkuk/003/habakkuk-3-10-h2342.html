TREMBLED - ( chul , Strong's H2342 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb chul represents a violent, physical reaction of twisting, writhing, or shaking, most commonly associated with the agonizing labor pains of childbirth. In the context of Habakkuk 3:10, it describes the ancient, seemingly immovable mountains twisting and shuddering in traumatic awe at the visual descent of Yahweh. The word moves beyond mere cognitive fear to depict a profound, involuntary physical response of creation to the presence of its Creator, signaling both judgment and majestic power.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The root meaning of chul lies in circular motion, twisting, spinning, or writhing. It describes a physical motion of turning or shaking under extreme force, pressure, or agony.

  • This physical writhing is most vividly demonstrated in the labor pains of a woman giving birth (travail). It captures the involuntary, rhythmic, and inescapable nature of physical contraction.

  • When applied to non-human elements of creation, such as mountains or the earth, it portrays these massive, solid structures as if they were living, breathing entities reacting to an overwhelming, irresistible power. The mountains are personified as being in labor, convulsing under the heavy weight of God's glory.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The verb chul (or chil) has a broad semantic range including to twist, spin, writhe, dance, tremble, be in pain, or bring forth.

  • In the Qal stem, it means to dance, writhe with pain, or tremble.

  • In the Hiphil stem, it means to cause to tremble, shake, or bring to birth.

  • In the Polal stem, it refers to being brought forth or born, or made to writhe.

  • The specific nuance in poetic prophetic literature refers to the convulsive shaking of the earth and mountains under the weight of divine judgment and theophany. It is not a gentle vibrating but a violent, twisting convulsion.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Childbirth and Travail: Used extensively to describe the sudden, intense spasms of a woman in labor. This imagery highlights both the agony and the inevitability of the outcome.

  • Dancing and Whirling: Used of rhythmic, circular movement in dance or celebration. The common thread is the dynamic, physical movement of twisting or turning.

  • Physical Trembling and Fear: Used of nations shaking in dread of God's judgment. The psychological terror is so profound that it manifests as physical trembling.

  • Creation's Convulsion: Used to describe the earth shaking at the presence of God. It presents nature as completely subservient to the Creator, unable to remain still when He approaches.

4. Theological Implications

  • Sovereignty Over Creation: Creation is not an independent, static mechanism; it is intimately responsive to its Maker. The mountains—symbols of permanence, strength, and age—are reduced to helpless writhing before God.

  • Theophany and Judgment: The physical shaking of the earth accompanies the presence of God to demonstrate that His holiness cannot be contained or easily withstood by the physical universe.

  • Cosmic Reconstruction: Creation's trembling is a form of labor; it signifies that God's judgment and intervention, though painful, are birthing a new order of justice and salvation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage (Habakkuk 3:10)

  • Habakkuk 3 is a prayer of the prophet, written as a poetic psalm recalling God's past redemptive actions (such as the Exodus and Sinai) to find hope for the impending Babylonian invasion.

  • In verse 10, "The mountains saw You and trembled," the term chul emphasizes that even the oldest and most stable parts of the physical world lose their stability at God's gaze.

  • The mountains "saw" (personified) and immediately "writhed/trembled." This is an involuntary, instinctual reaction. It contrasts sharply with the stubbornness of human nations who refuse to tremble before Him.

  • It occurs alongside the overflowing of waters and the deep uttering its voice, painting a picture of total cosmic upheaval where every element of the natural order yields to God's triumphant march to save His people.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Psalm 97:4: "His lightnings light up the world; The earth sees and trembles."

  • Psalm 114:7: "Tremble, O earth, at the presence of the Lord, At the presence of the God of Jacob,"

  • Jeremiah 5:22: "Do you not fear Me?’ says the Lord. ‘Will you not tremble at My presence, Who have placed the sand as the bound of the sea By a perpetual decree, that it cannot pass beyond it?"

  • Joel 2:6: "Before them the people writhe in pain; All faces drain of color."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The use of chul in Habakkuk 3:10 serves as a powerful pastoral comfort. If the giant mountains—which have stood for millennia—tremble and submit to God's presence, then the terrifying Babylonian empire (which seemed like an immovable mountain to Habakkuk) is nothing before God.

  • This word exposes the absurdity of human pride. When God arises, the solid earth turns fluid, and the static mountains writhe like a woman in labor. Human kingdoms that stand against God will likewise collapse and shake.
