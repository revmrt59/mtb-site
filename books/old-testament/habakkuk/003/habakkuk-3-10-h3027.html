HANDS - ( yad , Strong's H3027 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word yad literally refers to the physical hand, but it extends metaphorically to denote power, authority, agency, and worship. In the dramatic, prophetic vision of Habakkuk 3:10, the "deep" (the primeval ocean) is personified as lifting its "hands" on high. This represents the chaotic forces of creation yielding in absolute submission, terror, and adoration before the majesty of Yahweh as He marches forth to deliver His people.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The literal hand is the primary instrument of human action, strength, and communication. In the ancient Near Eastern world, the hand symbolized a person’s power, control, or agency. To be in someone's "hand" meant to be completely subject to their authority or custody. Because the hand is the means by which a person acts, the word represents the active presence of an individual. Furthermore, lifting the hands was the universal physical posture for taking an oath, crying out for help, surrendering to a victor, or offering adoration in worship.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary definition of yad is the physical hand or wrist of a human being. It also refers to the paw of an animal. Semantically, the term expands to mean:

  • Strength, power, or might.

  • Control, custody, dominion, or possession.

  • A portion, share, or monument (literal "marker").

  • In poetic and prophetic personification, it denotes the active reach or expression of inanimate forces, such as the power of the grave or the roaring waves of the sea.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, yad appears in several vivid anthropomorphic and metaphorical expressions:

  • "The Hand of the Lord": Refers to God’s sovereign power in action, whether executing judgment on His enemies or bringing comfort, guidance, and prophetic inspiration to His servants.

  • "Filling the Hand": A technical phrase used for consecrating priests, signifying that their hands were filled with sacrifices to be offered to God.

  • "Striking Hands": A gesture indicating the making of a covenant, a pledge, or a business guarantee.

  • "Lifting the Hand": Used to describe an oath of absolute certainty or a gesture of desperate prayer and praise.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, yad underscores the personal and active nature of both human and divine agency. God is not an abstract force; He has a "hand" that saves, delivers, guides, and disciplines. When creation is depicted as having "hands," it bridges the gap between the material world and spiritual reality. It demonstrates that the physical cosmos is not a mute, mechanical entity, but an active participant in recognizing and responding to the lordship of God. The shaking, shouting, and lifting of hands by the natural world declare that Yahweh is the undisputed King over all chaotic forces.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:10, the prophet recounts a spectacular theophany—a visible manifestation of God—reminiscent of the Exodus, the Red Sea crossing, and the Sinai covenant. As God marches in His war chariot of salvation, the entire cosmos reacts with violent trembling. The "deep" (tehom), which represents the vast, chaotic, untamed waters of the ocean, "uttered its voice" (the roaring of the waves) and "lifted its hands on high."

The lifting of the deep's "hands" refers to the massive, towering waves reaching toward the heavens. In this context, the gesture is not one of rebellion or defiance, but of terrified submission and involuntary worship. The chaotic deep, which ancient mythologies viewed as a rival deity, is shown here as a helpless servant, throwing up its hands in absolute surrender to the sovereign power of Yahweh.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 17:11: "And so it was, when Moses held up his hand, that Israel prevailed; and when he let down his hand, Amalek prevailed."

  • Psalm 134:2: "Lift up your hands in the sanctuary, And bless the LORD."

  • Psalm 141:2: "Let my prayer be set before You as incense, The lifting up of my hands as the evening sacrifice."

  • Isaiah 49:16: "See, I have inscribed you on the palms of My hands; Your walls are continually before Me."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Habakkuk 3:10, there is a striking parallel between the "voice" (qol) and the "hands" (yad) of the deep. This mimics the liturgical actions of ancient worshipers who raised their voices in lamentation or praise while lifting their hands to heaven. Even the most formidable and frightening elements of the created order—the deep ocean—are ultimately brought into liturgical order, compelled to bow and lift their "hands" in recognition of the Creator’s supreme authority.
