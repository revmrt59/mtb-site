LIFTED - ( nasa , Strong's H5375 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb nasa is a rich, highly versatile term meaning to lift, bear, carry, or take up. In the majestic poetic landscape of Habakkuk 3:10, it depicts the personified deep ("the abyss") lifting its hands high in awe, submission, and terror before the cosmic march of God. This action visualizes creation's recognition of its Creator's supreme authority, presenting a vivid picture of worship and fear.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew verb nasa conveys the physical movement of elevating something from a lower plane to a higher plane. It is a root deeply embedded in the ancient Near Eastern culture of physical expression, where internal attitudes were outwardly manifested through bodily gestures. Raising one's hands, eyes, or head communicated surrender, petition, pride, or worship. By personifying the physical elements of the cosmos, the biblical writers projected these human expressions onto creation itself to demonstrate its response to the Creator's voice and presence.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The semantic range of nasa spans several distinct categories of action:

  • Elevation and raising: To lift up a physical object, body parts (such as the hands, head, or eyes), or the voice.

  • Bearing and carrying: To support a load, tolerate a burden, or sustain a pressure.

  • Taking away and removing: To lift off a burden, which serves as the primary Old Testament metaphor for the forgiveness of sins (carrying away iniquity).

In Habakkuk 3:10, nasa appears in its active Qal form, expressing the direct, physical action of raising up.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The posture of prayer: Lifting the hands (nasa yadayim) is the classic biblical gesture of prayer, expressing vulnerability, dependence, and the expectation of receiving help from above.

  • The carrying of a child: The verb describes Yahweh carrying Israel through the desert wilderness as a father carries his son, showing tender care and sustenance.

  • The scapegoat ritual: On the Day of Atonement, the goat "bore" (nasa) the iniquities of the people into the wilderness, visually carrying the burden of sin away from the camp.

4. Theological Implications

  • Absolute Sovereignty: Unlike pagan mythologies where the sea or the deep represents an opposing chaos monster that battles the creator god, the Hebrew Scriptures depict the deep as immediately submissive to Yahweh. The deep does not fight; it trembles and lifts its hands in recognition of its Sovereign.

  • The Cosmic Symphony of Praise and Fear: Creation is an active participant in God's redemptive history. The mountains, rivers, and deep oceans possess a responsive agency that trembles and bows, rebuking human hardheartedness and illustrating the cosmic scale of God's power.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:10, the prophet recalls the terrifying majesty of God's historical self-revelation. The "deep" (tehom—the primeval, surging ocean depths) utters its voice, a roaring sound of crashing waters. Simultaneously, it "lifted its hands on high." This refers to the towering waves reaching upward toward heaven. In this context, the lifting of the deep's "hands" is a gesture of profound terror and absolute surrender. The chaotic waters, which normally threaten human order, are completely subdued and rendered helpless before the marching presence of God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 13:10: "And Lot lifted up his eyes and saw all the plain of Jordan, that it was well watered everywhere..."

  • Exodus 34:7: "keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin..."

  • Psalm 24:7: "Lift up your heads, O you gates! And be lifted up, you everlasting doors!"

  • Psalm 121:1: "I will lift up my eyes to the hills—From whence comes my help?"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The Hebrew word for "on high" (mishgeh) used alongside lifted in Habakkuk 3:10 denotes extreme height or an elevated place. This accentuates the dramatic physical imagery of the ocean waves being propelled skyward, as if the sea itself is trying to reach the heavens in utter desperation.

  • The personification of the sea lifting its hands highlights a recurring biblical theme: nature is highly sensitive to the presence of God. While humanity often remains stubborn and unyielding, the wildest forces of nature instantly submit to their Creator's authority.
