VOICE - ( qol , Strong's H6963 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun qol refers to a voice, sound, noise, or call. It is a highly versatile term used in the Old Testament to describe the auditory manifestation of humans, animals, inanimate objects, natural forces, and God Himself. In Habakkuk 3:10, qol represents the roaring "voice" of the deep sea (tehom). Placed in the context of a dramatic cosmic theophany, this word highlights how nature vocalizes its terror, awe, and ultimate submission to the sovereign majesty of Yahweh as He marches forth to deliver His people.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word qol is an onomatopoeic noun reflecting the sharp, resonant sound of a cry or call. In the ancient Near Eastern world, sounds were not viewed merely as passive acoustic waves but as active extensions of the source's power and presence. To hear someone's qol was to encounter their physical presence. When applied to nature, such as the crashing of waves, the howling of the wind, or the rolling of thunder, these sounds were understood as the creation speaking. For Israel, these cosmic sounds did not belong to chaotic pagan deities; rather, they were voices pointing back to the supreme power of the Creator.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meaning of qol is "voice" or "sound." Its semantic range includes:

  • The human voice speaking, crying out, or singing.

  • The voice of Yahweh, which can manifest as a direct command, an articulate message, or the terrifying roar of thunder.

  • The sound or noise of inanimate objects, such as the rumbling of chariot wheels, the blast of a trumpet, or the rustling of leaves.

  • The vocalization of the animal kingdom, like the roaring of lions or the chirping of birds.

  • The roar or noise of natural elements, including rushing waters, thunder, and rain.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Sound of Thunder: Qol is frequently used in the plural (qolot) to denote thunder. In this usage, thunder is anthropomorphized as the audible declaration of God's power shaking the earth.

  • The Voice of the Blood: In a striking personification, the spilled blood of Abel has a qol that cries out from the ground to God for justice, demonstrating that even silent, hidden actions have a vocal witness before the Creator.

  • The Sound of the Sea: Water in great volumes produces a massive, overwhelming sound. The Old Testament writers often picture the roaring sea as lifting up its qol to proclaim the majesty of God or to cry out in distress when God rebukes it.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, qol underscores the communicative nature of both God and His creation. God is not silent; He speaks, and His qol demands a response of obedience. Conversely, creation is never mute; it responds vocally to its Maker.

In poetic and prophetic literature, when God appears in judgment or salvation (a theophany), the natural order is thrown into convulsion. The fact that the "deep" (tehom) utters its qol demonstrates that even the wild, untamable, and chaotic forces of the ancient world are entirely subject to Yahweh. The deep does not roar in defiance, but rather cries out in recognition of its Lord's supreme authority.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:10, the prophet experiences a vision of Yahweh marching from Teman in radiant glory. As God approaches, the physical cosmos reacts: "The mountains saw You and trembled; The overflowing of the water passed by. The deep uttered its voice, And lifted its hands on high."

The word qol here represents the deep, roaring sound of the sea (tehom). In ancient Near Eastern mythology, the deep was a personified monster of chaos that fought against the gods. In the biblical truth of Habakkuk, however, the deep is completely subjugated. Its qol is a cry of submission, synchronized with the lifting up of its "hands" (the towering waves) in a gesture of surrender and worship. The roaring waves are not a threat to God; they are the ocean's vocal acknowledgement of His absolute sovereignty.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 4:10 - "And He said, 'What have you done? The voice of your brother's blood cries out to Me from the ground.'"

  • Psalm 29:3 - "The voice of the Lord is over the waters; The God of glory thunders; The Lord is over many waters."

  • Psalm 93:3 - "The floods have lifted up, O Lord, The floods have lifted up their voice; The floods lift up their waves."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Hebrew expression "to obey" is frequently written as shama l'qol, which literally means "to hear/hearken to the voice." This idiomatic connection emphasizes that biblical hearing is never passive. To hear the qol of God is to immediately act upon it. In Habakkuk 3:10, the deep obeys this principle perfectly. Upon seeing the presence of God, the deep immediately hears, trembles, and utters its qol in active, visible submission—serving as a powerful object lesson for humanity to do the same.
