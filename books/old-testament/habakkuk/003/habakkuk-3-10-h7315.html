HIGH - ( rom , Strong's H7315 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun rom refers to physical height, elevation, or a lofty place, while also carrying a figurative meaning of pride, loftiness, or arrogance. In the majestic poetic landscape of Habakkuk 3:10, this word describes the vast, deep waters of the ocean lifting their hands "on high" in absolute, awestruck submission to the sovereign presence of Yahweh. It paints a vivid theological picture of creation recognizing and bowing before its Creator, demonstrating that even the most chaotic forces of nature are subject to His supreme authority.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun rom is derived from the root verb rum, which means to be high, rise, or be exalted. In the ancient Near Eastern world, physical height was intrinsically linked to power, authority, and the divine realm. Mountains, the heavens, and lofty towers were symbols of strength and unapproachability. Conversely, the "deep" represented the chaotic, untamable forces of the world that threatened order. By stating that the deep lifts its hands to the rom (the heights), the biblical imagery masterfully subverts ancient pagan cosmologies. Rather than a chaotic force fighting the Creator, the deep is personified as a worshiper lifting its hands to the heavens in recognition of Yahweh's ultimate supremacy.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun rom denotes a state of being elevated or high. It possesses two primary dimensions of meaning:

  • Spatial/Physical Height: Referring to the physical heavens, the upper atmosphere, or the highest point of a structure or geographical feature.

  • Figurative Loftiness: Describing an attitude of pride, self-exaltation, or haughtiness. When paired with "eyes" or "heart," it signifies a person looking down on others from a self-assumed position of superiority.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament employs rom to convey both natural majesty and moral failure.

  • Natural Majesty: In cosmic descriptions, it refers to the highest heights where God's glory is manifest. The natural world reacts dynamically to God's presence, with the heights and the depths responding in unison.

  • Anthropomorphic Worship: In Habakkuk 3:10, the deep ocean waves are pictured as "hands" thrown upward toward the sky. The physical cresting of waves during a tempest is transformed into a spiritual posture of adoration and surrender.

  • Haughty Demeanor: Figuratively, the word portrays human pride as a physical lifting up of the eyes or heart. It represents the tragic human attempt to occupy the "high place" that belongs solely to God.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, rom serves as a boundary marker between the Creator and the creation.

  • True loftiness belongs to Yahweh alone. He dwells in the high and holy place, and His sovereignty extends over both the highest heavens and the deepest oceans.

  • When creation encounters its Maker, its natural response is to elevate His name and lower itself. The deep lifting its hands to the rom demonstrates that there is no realm of chaos outside of God's governing hand.

  • Human pride is an illicit usurpation of God's attribute. When humans adopt a "high" look (rom), they attempt to put themselves in the place of God. Consequently, God promises to bring down everything that falsely exalts itself.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3, the prophet is praying for God to revive His miraculous deeds of old. He describes a massive, dread-inducing divine warrior march. As Yahweh advances to deliver His people, the foundations of the earth shake. In verse 10, the mountains writhe in pain, the raging waters sweep by, and the deep utters its voice while lifting its hands "on high" (rom). The word functions adverbially here, describing the direction and destination of the deep's reach. The deep does not rage against God; rather, in terror and awe, it throws its wave-hands up to the highest heaven in absolute surrender. The nuance is one of complete cosmic subordination to the conquering King.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Proverbs 21:4: "A haughty look, a proud heart, And the plowing of the wicked are sin." (Here, "haughty" is the literal translation of rom eyes, depicting pride).

  • Isaiah 10:12: "Therefore it shall come to pass, when the Lord has performed all His work on Mount Zion and on Jerusalem, that He will say, 'I will punish the fruit of the arrogant heart of the king of Assyria, and the glory of his high looks.'" (The phrase "high looks" translates rom eyes, indicating the king's self-exaltation).

  • Jeremiah 48:29: "We have heard of the pride of Moab (He is exceedingly proud), Of his loftiness and his arrogance and his pride, And the haughtiness of his heart." (The word "loftiness" is rom, highlighting Moab's groundless self-importance).

7. Other Relevant Insights

The root rum is frequently used in the Psalms to describe God being "exalted" above the nations and the heavens. This verbal connection enhances the noun rom in Habakkuk. The very heights to which the deep lifts its hands are the heights where God is actively being exalted by His cosmic creation. Understanding this word helps the believer recognize that when life feels like an overwhelming, chaotic abyss, even those "deep" situations are already lifting their hands in submission to the Lord of the heights.
