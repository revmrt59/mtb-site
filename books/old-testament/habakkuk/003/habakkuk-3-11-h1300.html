GLITTERING - ( baraq , Strong's H1300 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word baraq fundamentally refers to a flash of lightning or the brilliant, dazzling gleam of a polished weapon. In the context of Habakkuk 3:11, it describes the terrifying radiance of God's spear as He marches forth in cosmic judgment. Rather than a mere aesthetic sparkle, this "glittering" represents the overwhelming, blinding light of divine majesty and holy warfare, striking awe and terror into creation and His enemies alike.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root baraq invokes the raw, sudden power of lightning slicing through a dark storm sky. In the ancient Near East, lightning was the ultimate symbol of swift, unstoppable heavenly power. When applied to weaponry, such as swords and spears, baraq describes the polished metal reflecting light so intensely that it mimics lightning. This creates a vivid word picture of divine warfare: God’s weapons are not crafted of earthly metal but are forged from the very elements of celestial fire, flashing with lethal brilliance as He executes judgment.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term baraq functions as both a verb (meaning "to flash lightning") and a noun (meaning "lightning," "lightning flash," or "gleam/glitter"). Lexically, the word denotes:

  • A bright, sudden flash of light, specifically atmospheric lightning.

  • The brilliant, dazzling gleam of highly polished metal, particularly defensive and offensive weapons like swords and spears.

  • Figuratively, the flashing splendor of divine judgment and the terrifying appearance of angelic or divine beings.

In Habakkuk 3:11, the noun is used in a construct state with "spear" (hanit), literally meaning "the lightning of Your spear" or "the flashing of Your spear," which the NKJV translates as "Your glittering spear."

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, baraq is frequently paired with other elements of major storms, such as thunder, clouds, and darkness, to depict the active presence of Yahweh (theophanies).

  • The Weapon of God: The word pictures associated with baraq depict God as a divine warrior. When He whets His sword or brandishes His spear, the reflective glint is not a static shine but an active, piercing flash of lightning designed to disorient and destroy His enemies.

  • Overwhelming Speed and Power: Lightning is instantaneous and irresistible. The use of baraq to describe God's weapons emphasizes that His judgments are executed with absolute speed and unstoppable power.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, baraq underscores the absolute transcendence and holiness of God.

  • Theophany and Judgment: When God reveals Himself, creation cannot endure the fullness of His unshielded glory. The "glittering" of His weapons represents the localized, visible expression of His holy anger against sin.

  • The Cosmic Warrior: Habakkuk draws on ancient Israelite descriptions of Yahweh as the Lord of Hosts who fights on behalf of His covenant people. The glittering spear shows that the forces of nature are merely instruments in the hand of the Creator.

  • The Limit of Human Power: Facing the baraq of God's weapons, earthly kingdoms and their armies are rendered completely helpless. Human weapons cannot compare to the celestial fire of the Almighty.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:11, the prophet is caught in a majestic, terrifying vision of a theophany—God marching from Teman to deliver His people.

  • The cosmic order is disrupted: the sun and moon stand still in their dwellings, paralyzed by the superior brightness of Yahweh's presence.

  • The "light of Your arrows" and the "shining of Your glittering spear" are parallel poetic lines. The arrows and spear of God are not literal wooden shafts with iron tips; they are the lightning bolts and celestial flashes of the storm.

  • The "glittering" (baraq) of the spear is so bright that it outshines the sun and moon, forcing them to retreat or stand still. The nuance here is one of supreme, blinding authority that eclipses the primary light-bearers of the created order.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

To understand the semantic range of baraq, examine how the same word is used across the Old Testament:

  • Deuteronomy 32:41: "If I whet My glittering sword, And My hand takes hold on judgment, I will render vengeance to My enemies, And repay those who hate Me."

  • Job 20:25: "It is drawn, and comes out of the body; Yes, the glittering point comes out of his gall. Terrors are upon him."

  • Ezekiel 21:10: "Sharpened to make a dreadful slaughter, Polished to flash like lightning!"

  • Nahum 3:3: "Horsemen charge with flashing sword and glittering spear! There is a multitude of slain, A great number of bodies, Countless corpses— They stumble over the corpses—"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The Connection to Barak: The proper name "Barak" (the military commander in Judges 4) is derived directly from this root, meaning "lightning" or "thunderbolt." This name pointed to his role as a swift instrument of divine judgment against the Canaanites.

  • LXX Translation: The Septuagint (Greek translation of the Old Testament) translates baraq in Habakkuk 3:11 using astrape, the Greek word for "lightning" or "brilliant flashing" (the same word used in the New Testament to describe the sudden and glorious return of Christ in Matthew 24:27).
