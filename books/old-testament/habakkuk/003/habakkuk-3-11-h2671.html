ARROWS - ( chets , Strong's H2671 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word chets refers literally to a projectile shot from a bow, but in the poetic and prophetic literature of the Old Testament, it frequently serves as a powerful metaphor for God's swift, precise, and inescapable judgments. In Habakkuk 3:11, these "arrows" represent the flashing light of lightning bolts unleashed by God as a divine warrior marching to deliver His people, demonstrating His absolute sovereignty over creation and His enemies.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near East, the bow and arrow were the premier weapons for distance warfare, hunting, and defense. An arrow (chets) was crafted from light wood or reed, tipped with a sharpened head of flint, bronze, or iron, and fletched with feathers to ensure a true flight. The efficacy of an arrow lay in its speed, silent approach, and ability to pierce the armor of an adversary from afar. In the cultural mindset of Israel's neighbors, gods of tempest and war were often depicted holding bows and shooting arrows of lightning. The Old Testament writers intentionally hijacked this familiar cultural imagery to declare that Yahweh alone is the true Lord of the storm and the cosmic Divine Warrior who fights on behalf of His covenant people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun chets refers to an arrow, dart, or archer’s missile. It is derived from a root verb meaning to divide, pierce, or cut, which emphasizes the penetrative, wounding capacity of the weapon. While its primary and literal definition belongs to the realm of ancient military hardware, its expanded semantic range includes:

  • Divine lightning bolts or flashes of light used by God in cosmic warfare.

  • Personal, physical, or spiritual afflictions sent by God to discipline or judge.

  • Devastating human words, lies, and slander designed to wound the innocent.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Hebrew Bible, chets is employed in several vivid word pictures:

  • The Storm of Divine Judgment: God is depicted bending His bow and launching arrows of lightning to scatter His enemies. The forces of nature are His personal arsenal.

  • The Sickness of the Soul: Job and the Psalmists describe their intense personal suffering as being pierced by the "arrows of the Almighty." Here, the arrow is a metaphor for the sharp, deep pain of spiritual and physical trials that feel as though they have been directly targeted at the individual by God.

  • The Poison of Slander: Wicked people who plot in secret are described as bending their bows to shoot "bitter words" like arrows at the upright. This highlights the lethal, distant, and cowardly nature of gossip and deceit.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of chets in prophetic literature rests on the doctrine of divine justice and God’s active intervention in human history.

  • The Reality of Divine Wrath: God is not a passive observer of wickedness. The imagery of the arrow reminds the reader that God’s judgment is active, targeted, and highly effective. His arrows never miss their mark.

  • The Assurance of Redemption: For the believer, the fact that God shoots His arrows against the wicked is a source of profound comfort. It means God will vindicate His people, overthrow oppressors, and establish His righteous kingdom.

  • Sovereignty over Creation: By casting lightning as God’s arrows, the text asserts that what pagan nations worshipped as independent nature deities (such as Baal or Hadad) are actually inanimate weapons in the hand of the one true God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Habakkuk 3 is a sublime prayer and song of praise (a shiggaion) wherein the prophet recalls God's historic, redemptive acts to find hope during the impending Babylonian invasion. In Habakkuk 3:11, the prophet describes a terrifying cosmic theophany: "The sun and moon stood still in their habitation; At the light of your arrows they went, At the shining of your glittering spear."

In this highly poetic context, the "arrows" are the brilliant flashes of lightning that accompany the divine presence. The brilliance of God's lightning-arrows is so intense that the sun and the moon—the ruling lights of heaven—withdraw in shame and hide in their "habitation," utterly eclipsed by the superior glory of their Creator. The verse assures the trembling prophet that the same God who shook the earth at the Red Sea and Sinai is marching once again to deliver His anointed.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Deuteronomy 32:23 - "‘I will heap disasters on them; I will spend My arrows on them."

  • Job 6:4 - "For the arrows of the Almighty are within me; My spirit drinks in their poison; The terrors of God are arrayed against me."

  • Psalm 18:14 - "He sent out His arrows and scattered them, Lightning in abundance, and He vanquished them."

  • Psalm 38:2 - "For Your arrows pierce me deeply, And Your hand presses me down."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Habakkuk 3:11, the word "arrows" (chets) is parallel to "glittering spear" (barak chanith, literally "lightning-spear"). This poetic parallelism solidifies the interpretation that the weapons of Yahweh are the elements of the storm. The passage serves to turn the believer's eyes away from the overwhelming power of human empires (like Babylon) and toward the cosmic scale of God's power. A nation of archers and chariots is nothing compared to the God who shoots the lightning and commands the sun to halt.
