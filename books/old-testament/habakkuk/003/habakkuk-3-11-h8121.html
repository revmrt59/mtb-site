SUN - ( shemesh , Strong's H8121 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word shemesh refers to the physical sun, the celestial body created by God to govern the day and serve as a reliable marker of times and seasons. In the biblical worldview, the sun is not a deity to be worshipped, but a subordinate creation that testifies to the Creator's power, consistency, and sovereign control. In prophetic and poetic contexts, such as Habakkuk 3:11, the sun's regular course can be interrupted, suspended, or eclipsed by the overwhelming majesty and flashing glory of God Himself when He marches forth for the salvation of His people.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • shemesh originates from a root common to Semitic languages, representing the solar disk or the heat and light radiating from it. In the ancient Near East, the sun was widely personified and worshipped as a primary deity (such as Shamash in Babylon or Ra in Egypt) associated with justice, light, and life. The Old Testament deliberately de-mythologizes the sun, describing it simply as a "greater light" designed to serve God’s ordering of the cosmos. Instead of a god ruling the heavens, the sun is a servant in Yahweh’s sky, obeying His commands and even hiding its face when He appears in judgment.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun shemesh primarily denotes the physical sun as a celestial body. It refers to:

  • The solar disk that rises in the east and sets in the west, defining the daily cycle.

  • The light or heat emitted by the sun (e.g., "in the heat of the sun").

  • The direction of the sunrise, indicating the east (literally, "the rising of the sun").

  • Metaphorically, a condition of public visibility, exposure, or openness (e.g., "before the sun" or "in the sight of the sun").

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • shemesh is used across the Old Testament to convey several powerful concepts:

  • Constancy and Endurance: The enduring nature of the sun serves as a picture of the permanence of God's covenant with David and the longevity of the righteous (Psalm 72:5, Psalm 89:36).

  • Unavoidable Exposure: Actions done "before the sun" are completely exposed to public view, carrying the idea of absolute transparency or public judgment (2 Samuel 12:11).

  • Earthly Limitation: In Ecclesiastes, the phrase "under the sun" (tachat hashshemesh) is used repeatedly to describe life lived solely from a human, horizontal, and temporary perspective, apart from the eternal reality of God.

  • Divine Favor and Blessing: The sun is pictured as a source of warmth and life, and God Himself is called a "sun and shield," providing light, guidance, warmth, and protection to His people (Psalm 84:11).

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of shemesh reinforces the doctrine of creation and absolute divine sovereignty. Because the sun is a created instrument, it has no independent authority. God commands the sun to rise on the evil and the good, and He can obstruct its light at His pleasure to demonstrate wrath, sign, or wonder. When Yahweh intervenes in history, the sun's normal, dependable operations are disrupted (Exodus 10:21-23; Joshua 10:12-13). This demonstrates that nature is not a closed, self-sustaining loop, but an active canvas for the Creator's personal will and redemptive acts.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:11, the prophet reflects on a dramatic, poetic vision of Yahweh marching in judgment to deliver His people. The phrase "The sun and moon stood still in their habitation" recalls the historical miracle in Joshua 10, but here it functions with profound theological and literary intensity. The word for "habitation" (zebul) refers to a lofty dwelling place or temple. Poetically, the sun and moon retreat into their celestial chambers, shrinking back in fear. Their natural light is utterly eclipsed and rendered obsolete by the far superior, uncreated brightness of Yahweh's "arrows" and "glittering spear"—metaphors for flashing lightning. This highlights that when God manifests His presence, even the most dominant and reliable physical forces of the universe bow in submission and yield their glory to Him.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Joshua 10:12 - "Then Joshua spoke to the LORD in the day when the LORD delivered up the Amorites before the children of Israel, and he said in the sight of Israel: 'Sun, stand still over Gibeon; And Moon, in the Valley of Aijalon.'"

  • Psalm 84:11 - "For the LORD God is a sun and shield; The LORD will give grace and glory; No good thing will He withhold From those who walk uprightly."

  • Ecclesiastes 1:9 - "That which has been is what will be, That which is done is what will be done, And there is nothing new under the sun."

  • Malachi 4:2 - "But to you who fear My name The Sun of Righteousness shall arise With healing in His wings; And you shall go out And grow fat like stall-fed calves."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The root of shemesh is also related to the Hebrew name Samson (Shimshon), which means "sunny" or "little sun." This stands in sharp contrast to his life, which ultimately ended in physical darkness (blindness) but spiritual victory. Additionally, the prophetic pattern of the sun being darkened or withdrawing its light is consistently associated with the "Day of the Lord" (Joel 2:10; Amos 8:9), a theme that culminates in the New Testament description of cosmic disturbances preceding Christ’s return and the ultimate reality of the New Jerusalem, which needs no sun because the glory of God illuminates it (Revelation 21:23).
