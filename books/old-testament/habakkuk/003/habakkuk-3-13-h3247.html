FOUNDATION - ( yesod , Strong's H3247 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun yesod refers to the lowest supporting part of a structure, such as a wall, building, or altar. In Habakkuk 3:13, it depicts the absolute destruction of the wicked power opposing God's people. By "laying bare" this enemy from "foundation to neck," God dismantles their entire system of security and strength from the bottom up, leaving them utterly ruined and exposed. This term highlights the futility of any human foundation built in opposition to the Sovereign Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient Near Eastern architecture, the foundation was the most critical part of any structure. Buildings, city walls, and temples were constructed by digging down to bedrock or laying massive stone courses to ensure stability against torrential rains, floods, and earthquakes. If the foundation remained intact, a damaged building could be rebuilt. However, to destroy a structure's foundation was to guarantee its permanent, irreversible ruin. Poetically and militarily, "uncovering" or "laying bare" a foundation represented the ultimate humiliation and total defeat of an enemy city or empire.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun yesod means a foundation, base, or bottom-most part. It derives from the verbal root yasad, which means to establish, found, or lay a base. In the Old Testament, the noun is used in two primary physical contexts:

  • The structural foundations of buildings, houses, or city walls.

  • The base of the sacrificial altar of burnt offering, where the blood of purification offerings was poured out.

Metaphorically, yesod represents the fundamental security, moral grounding, or underlying strength of a nation, dynasty, or spiritual system.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Altar's Base: In the Levitical sacrificial system, yesod describes the base of the altar. The blood of the sin offering was not merely sprinkled; its bulk was poured out at the very base of the altar. This word picture demonstrates that the entire sacrificial system and the ministry of atonement rest upon a foundation of shed blood.

  • The Exposure of Ruin: In poetic literature, the destruction of a city is characterized by the exposing of its yesod. When God judges a corrupt nation, He strips away its outer defenses and exposes its very foundations to the open air, revealing its structural weakness and moral decay.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, yesod reveals that God's judgments are thorough and radical. When God acts in history to deliver His people and judge evil, He does not merely damage the surface of wicked systems; He destroys their very foundation.

Furthermore, this concept contrasts the fragile, temporary foundations of human pride with the unshakable foundation of God’s covenant. Human kingdoms build their foundations on wealth, military might, and idolatry, all of which fail under divine judgment. Conversely, the righteous rely on the foundation laid by God, which stands secure forever.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:13, the prophet recounts a spectacular divine warrior song, detailing God's march to save His covenant people and His anointed. The text states: "You struck the head from the house of the wicked, By laying bare from foundation to neck."

This verse utilizes a striking, mixed metaphor of a house and a human body to describe the Babylonians (or the archetypal enemy of God):

  • The "head" represents the leadership or the top of the house.

  • The "neck" represents the transition point.

  • The "foundation" (yesod) represents the very bottom.

By striking the head and laying bare the structure down to its yesod, God executes a top-to-bottom demolition. The enemy's "house" (dynasty, empire, and system of power) is left completely exposed to its underground base. The noun yesod here emphasizes that nothing of the enemy's power remains hidden or standing; the ruin is absolute, irreversible, and visible to all.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 29:12 - "You shall take some of the blood of the bull and put it on the horns of the altar with your finger, and pour all the blood beside the base of the altar."

  • Leviticus 4:7 - "And the priest shall put some of the blood on the horns of the altar of sweet incense before the LORD, which is in the tabernacle of meeting; and he shall pour the remaining blood of the bull at the base of the altar of the burnt offering, which is at the door of the tabernacle of meeting."

  • Psalm 137:7 - "Remember, O LORD, against the sons of Edom The day of Jerusalem, Who said, 'Raze it, raze it, To its very foundation!'"

  • Lamentations 4:11 - "The LORD has fulfilled His fury, He has poured out His fierce anger. He kindled a fire in Zion, And it has devoured its foundations."

  • Micah 1:6 - "Therefore I will make Samaria a heap of ruins in the field, Places for planting vineyards; I will pour down her stones into the valley, And I will uncover her foundations."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament), yesod is regularly translated with the Greek noun themelios. This links the Old Testament concept of the physical and sacrificial foundation directly to the New Testament's theological use of themelios. Jesus Christ is described as the only true foundation (themelios) of the church (1 Corinthians 3:11), and the household of God is built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Jesus Himself as the chief cornerstone (Ephesians 2:20). While the foundations of the wicked are laid bare and destroyed, the foundation of God stands sure.
