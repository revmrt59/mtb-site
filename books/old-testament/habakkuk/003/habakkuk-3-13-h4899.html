ANOINTED - ( mashiach , Strong's H4899 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word mashiach refers to one who is consecrated and set apart for a specific divine office or task through the symbolic act of anointing with oil. In Habakkuk 3:13, the term depicts God's chosen instrument of deliverance—historically represented by the Davidic king, but ultimately fulfilled in the Messianic Savior—marching alongside Yahweh to secure the salvation of His covenant people.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun mashiach is derived from the root verb mashach, which means to smear, rub, or pour oil upon a person or object. In the ancient Near East, anointing was not a mere cosmetic or medical treatment; it was a formal, sacred rite of consecration. When sacred oil was poured over a person’s head, it signified that they were being set apart from common use for a holy, divinely authorized purpose. The oil itself symbolized the presence, favor, and empowering of the Holy Spirit for the task at hand. This ritual was used to consecrate priests, prophets, and kings, indicating that their authority was derived directly from God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term mashiach functions primarily as a passive adjective meaning "anointed" or a noun meaning "anointed one." It designates an individual who has been formally commissioned by God. The semantic range of the word encompasses:

  • The consecrated high priest, designated as the "anointed priest."

  • The ruling Davidic king, frequently referred to as "the Lord's anointed."

  • The promised, ultimate Davidic Deliverer and King, who would establish God's eternal kingdom (the Messiah).

  • In rare poetic contexts, the patriarchs or the corporate nation of Israel as God's chosen, set-apart instruments in the world.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The word mashiach occurs 39 times in the Hebrew Bible. The usage paints a vivid picture of divine election and protection:

  • The Protected Sovereign: To touch the mashiach was to invite the judgment of God, as the anointed king was God’s earthly representative.

  • The Mediating Priest: The anointed high priest carried the responsibility of presenting the blood of atonement on behalf of the nation, standing between a holy God and a sinful people.

  • The Consecrated Vessel: The physical act of anointing left a visible, fragrant shine on the individual, serving as an outward testimony of inward consecration and divine ownership.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of mashiach is foundational to the biblical narrative of redemption.

  • Divine Authorization: It establishes that true spiritual and civil authority is not self-appointed but granted by God. The mashiach rules and ministers under the lordship of Yahweh.

  • Spirit Empowerment: It demonstrates that whom God calls, He equips. The oil symbolizes the Holy Spirit, illustrating that divine offices cannot be executed by human strength alone.

  • The Messianic Hope: Because the earthly, historical kings and priests failed to fully embody God's righteousness, the term increasingly pointed forward to a singular, future Mashiach. He would perfectly unite the offices of Prophet, Priest, and King, bringing ultimate redemption.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:13, the prophet is caught up in a majestic, poetic vision of Yahweh marching out in cosmic warrior fashion to deliver His covenant people. The text reads: "You went forth for the salvation of Your people, For salvation with Your Anointed."

The contextual nuance here operates on three complementary levels:

  • The Corporate and Representative Identity: In Hebrew poetry, "Your people" and "Your Anointed" are placed in parallel structure. The mashiach (the Davidic king) represents the entire nation. When God saves His anointed king, He saves His covenant people.

  • The Historical Context: During the Babylonian crisis, the Davidic dynasty seemed fragile and near extinction. Habakkuk’s prayer is a plea for God to act on behalf of His anointed king, preserving the royal line through which the covenant promises would be fulfilled.

  • The Typological / Messianic Fulfilment: The word for "salvation" in this verse is yesha, which shares a root with the name Jesus (Yeshua). God's ultimate march of salvation was executed through His ultimate Mashiach, Jesus Christ. In Christ's death and resurrection, God "struck the head of the house of the wicked," dealing a decisive blow to Satan and the powers of darkness.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Samuel 24:6 – "And he said to his men, 'The LORD forbid that I should do this thing to my master, the LORD's anointed, to stretch out my hand against him, seeing he is the anointed of the LORD.'"

  • Psalm 2:2 – "The kings of the earth set themselves, And the rulers take counsel together, Against the LORD and against His Anointed..."

  • Psalm 132:17 – "There I will make the horn of David grow; I will prepare a lamp for My Anointed."

  • Daniel 9:25 – "Know therefore and understand, That from the going forth of the command To restore and build Jerusalem Until Messiah the Prince, There shall be seven weeks and sixty-two weeks..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

When the Hebrew Scriptures were translated into Greek (the Septuagint), the translators rendered mashiach as christos. This Greek word likewise means "anointed one." In the New Testament, this title became the proper name for Jesus: Iesous Christos—Jesus the Christ, or Jesus the Anointed One. Thus, every Old Testament reference to the mashiach is a direct semantic ancestor of the name of Jesus, linking the Old Testament hope of deliverance directly to the person of Christ.
