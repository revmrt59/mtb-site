LAYING BARE - ( arah , Strong's 6168 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb arah refers to the act of uncovering, stripping, pouring out, or exposing something to its very foundations. In a military or judgmental context, it denotes utter exposure, vulnerability, and complete destruction, leaving an enemy totally defenseless. In Habakkuk 3:13, the term vividly depicts God shattering the wicked house of Israel's enemies, stripping its protective structure from the top down to its barest foundational stones.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete imagery of arah begins with the act of stripping away a covering. It can refer to pouring liquid out of a container, leaving the container entirely empty, or stripping a person of their garments, leaving them exposed and vulnerable. When applied to structures, buildings, or cities, arah describes the violent act of leveling a building to the ground so that its underground foundations are exposed to the open air. This word picture conveys total ruin; nothing is left hidden, protected, or standing.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meaning of arah is to make bare, uncover, strip, or empty. Depending on the grammatical stem used, the nuances adjust slightly:

  • In the active intensive stem (Piel), it signifies to demolish, raze, or completely expose a structure or entity.

  • In the causative stem (Hiphil), it means to pour out, empty, or expose to danger.

  • In its nominal and verbal derivatives, the root consistently carries the idea of removing barriers, safety, or coverings, resulting in absolute vulnerability or emptiness.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament employs arah in several vivid contexts:

  • Structural Demolition: It describes the complete leveling of a city's defenses, where adversaries tear down walls to their very foundations (Psalm 137:7).

  • Pouring Out Life: It portrays the ultimate sacrifice of the Servant of the Lord who empties Himself of life, pouring out His soul to the point of death (Isaiah 53:12).

  • Uncovering or Exposing: It denotes the stripping of protective coverings, such as beautiful cedar paneling in a palace, leaving it ruined and open to the elements (Zephaniah 2:14).

4. Theological Implications

Theological truths flow richly from this term when used of God's judgment and redemption.

  • Absolute Divine Judgment: When God judges evil, His work is thorough. He does not merely damage the surface of wicked systems; He strips them bare to their foundations, showing their inherent weakness and vanity.

  • The Collapse of False Security: Humanity continually builds "houses"—empires, philosophies, wealth, and self-righteousness—to secure itself against God. Arah demonstrates that God can easily expose these structures as fragile and temporary.

  • Divine Protection vs. Exposure: While the wicked are laid bare to judgment, God's covenant people are covered and protected. The ultimate judgment lays bare the enemy, while the Anointed One (Messiah) is poured out to provide a covering for His people.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:13, the prophet is caught in a majestic, terrifying vision of God marching forth for the salvation of His people. The target of God's wrath is "the house of the wicked."

The text uses a highly compressed, mixed metaphor of a house and a human body: "You struck the head from the house of the wicked, by laying bare from foundation to neck."

God strikes the "head" (the leader or the roof) and "lays bare" (arah) the structure down to its "foundation" and "neck." The grammatical form of arah here functions as an intensive infinitive, emphasizing the absolute certainty and totality of the demolition. God does not merely damage the enemy's house; He strips it completely of its walls, roof, and defenses. The enemy is left utterly exposed, naked, powerless, and ruined before the sovereign power of God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 24:20 - "So she quickly emptied her pitcher into the trough, ran back to the well to draw water, and drew for all his camels."

  • Psalm 137:7 - "Remember, O Lord, against the sons of Edom the day of Jerusalem, who said, 'Raze it, raze it, to its very foundation!'"

  • Isaiah 53:12 - "Therefore I will divide Him a portion with the great, and He shall divide the spoil with the strong, because He poured out His soul unto death, and He was numbered with the transgressors..."

  • Zephaniah 2:14 - "...For He will lay bare the cedar work."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The word arah in Habakkuk 3:13 highlights the futility of fighting against God. The "house of the wicked" represents Babylon historically, but typologically represents all spiritual and earthly powers that oppose God's kingdom. The striking contrast between God "laying bare" the enemy and "going forth for the salvation of Your people" reassures believers that no matter how imposing the structures of evil appear, they can be reduced to bare ground in a single moment by the breath of God.
