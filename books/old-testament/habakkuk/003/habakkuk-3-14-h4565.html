SECRET - ( mistar , Strong's H4565 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word mistar refers to a secret place, a hiding place, or a covert location where actions are done in concealment or stealth. In Habakkuk 3:14, it paints a vivid, chilling portrait of wicked oppressors who, like lions waiting in ambush or bandits lurking in dark corners, delight in destroying the vulnerable and defenseless when no one is watching. By exposing this "secret" wickedness, the prophet underscores God's absolute sovereignty and sudden, dramatic intervention to rescue His people and shatter the hidden traps of their adversaries.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • mistar evokes the rugged, broken geography of the ancient Near East, which was filled with deep ravines, caves, and thickets. In biblical times, these secluded areas served two primary purposes: they were places of safety and shelter for those fleeing danger, or they were tactical hiding spots for predators and bandits lying in wait for unsuspecting travelers. The word picture is inherently tied to the behavior of a lion lurking in the thick brush or a highwayman hidden behind a boulder. It implies an intentional retreat from public view to gain a deceptive advantage over the weak, moving the term from a physical description of geography to a moral description of deceitful, predatory intent.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun mistar means a hiding place, secret place, covert, or shelter. It is derived from the primitive root verb satar, which means to hide, conceal, or shield. Lexically, mistar specifically denotes places of concealment used either for protection or, more frequently in the poetic and wisdom literature, for malicious ambush. In Habakkuk 3:14, the term appears in the adverbial construct bammistar, meaning "in secret" or "in hidden places," describing the stealthy, predatory malice of invaders who feast upon the defenseless out of public view.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Scripture employs mistar in two contrasting conceptual frameworks:

  • The Predator's Den: The wicked utilize mistar to avoid detection, operating in moral darkness. They are compared to lions lurking in "secret places" to catch the poor, symbolizing the cowardice and cruelty of those who exploit the vulnerable from a position of hidden power.

  • The Prophet's Grief: The word is also used to describe deep, internal, and unseen grief. In moments of intense sorrow over the spiritual rebellion of the nation, a prophet's soul weeps "in secret" where only God can witness the depth of the pain.

4. Theological Implications

  • The Folly of Supposed Secrecy: The use of mistar highlights the spiritual blindness of the wicked, who act under the illusion that their covert deeds are hidden from the eyes of the Lord. Scripture consistently exposes this folly by showing that nothing is hidden from God's sight.

  • God as the Champion of the Helpless: God's justice is intimately concerned with what happens in secret. Because the helpless have no human advocate in the hidden places of oppression, the Sovereign Lord acts as their ultimate defender.

  • Poetic Divine Retribution: When God intervenes, He uses the very plots and weapons of the wicked to destroy them. The secret ambush of the oppressor becomes the stage for God's public, glorious vindication of His people.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3, the prophet recounts a majestic, terrifying vision of the Lord marching in judgment to deliver His people. In verse 14, the focus shifts to the ruthless Babylonian invaders who swept through the land. The text describes them coming like a whirlwind to scatter the helpless, their "rejoicing" likened to "feasting on the poor in secret."

The invaders did not merely wage open warfare; they took sadistic, covert pleasure in completely devouring the defenseless Judeans, much like bandits raiding a helpless village in the dark. However, the context is one of sudden, ironic reversal. Just as the enemy prepares to consume the poor in their "secret" onslaught, the Lord thrusts them through with their own spears. Their secret plots are instantly exposed and shattered by the blinding light of God’s victorious presence.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Psalm 10:9: "He lies in wait secretly, as a lion in his den; He lies in wait to catch the poor; He catches the poor when he draws him into his net."

  • Psalm 17:12: "He is like a lion eager to tear his prey, And like a young lion lurking in secret places."

  • Psalm 64:4: "That they may shoot in secret at the blameless; Suddenly they shoot at him and do not fear."

  • Jeremiah 13:17: "But if you will not hear it, My soul will weep in secret for your pride; My eyes will weep bitterly And run down with tears, Because the LORD’s flock has been taken captive."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The spiritual reality of mistar is that evil always seeks the dark to avoid accountability. In the New Testament, this matches the reality described by Christ, who noted that those who do evil hate the light lest their deeds be exposed. However, for the believer, the ultimate comfort is that while the enemy plots in secret, God's people are hidden in a far superior "secret place"—the shadow of the Almighty. The hidden machinations of the enemy can never penetrate the sovereign shelter of God.
