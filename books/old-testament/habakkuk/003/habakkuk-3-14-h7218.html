HEAD - ( ro'sh , Strong's H7218 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word ro'sh translates literally as "head" but carries rich metaphorical weight, representing leadership, prominence, the summit of a mountain, the beginning of time, or the chief representative of a group. In Habakkuk 3:14, the word refers to the leader, chief, or topmost commander of the enemy's forces ("the head of his villages" or "warriors"), illustrating God's decisive judgment in striking down the very source of the enemy's power and pride to deliver His people.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The physical head is the topmost, most prominent, and most vital part of the human or animal body. It contains the sensory organs—the eyes, ears, and mouth—making it the seat of intelligence, direction, and authority. In ancient Near Eastern thought, the head represents the whole person, and what happens to the head happens to the individual. This concrete physical reality easily expands into abstract concepts of leadership, authority, spatial height, and temporal beginnings. Whether describing the top of a mountain, the chief of a tribe, or the first month of the year, this term designates that which is foremost, supreme, and primary.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meanings of this word include:

  • The literal head of a human or animal.

  • The top, summit, or peak of a physical structure or geographical feature, such as a mountain, tower, or pillar.

  • The chief, leader, captain, or ruler of a family, clan, tribe, or nation.

  • The beginning, front, or start of a series, journey, or period of time.

  • The finest, choicest, or most excellent portion of something (such as the "chief" of spices).

In Habakkuk 3:14, the term functions as a designation for the leadership, chief warriors, or governing elite of the oppressive enemy nation.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

This word appears over 600 times in the Old Testament, showcasing its versatility through several key word pictures:

  • The Head of Authority: Used to designate leaders chosen to govern. Moses chose able men to be "heads" over Israel, acting as rulers and representatives of the people.

  • The Top of the Summit: Used to describe the peak of Mount Sinai where the glory of the Lord descended, symbolizing the meeting place between heaven and earth.

  • Anointing the Head: Pouring oil on the head symbolizes consecration, joy, and the overflowing blessing of God upon an individual's life.

  • Crushing the Head: A powerful motif of total defeat. To strike or crush the head of an enemy means to neutralize their power completely, ensuring they can never rise again.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, the term plays a critical role in the motif of divine warfare and messianic victory. From the first messianic prophecy in Genesis 3:15, redemption is framed as crushing the "head" of the serpent. When God judges the wicked, He does not merely inflict minor wounds; He strikes the "head." This represents the ultimate dismantling of evil. In the context of God's covenant loyalty, He promises to make His people the "head" and not the tail when they walk in obedience, reversing their status from oppressed to victorious.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:14, the prophet describes a spectacular, cosmic display of divine judgment. The context is a poetic prayer recalling the Exodus and God's historical deliverance of Israel. The enemy had come out "like a whirlwind" to scatter the helpless, gloating as if they were about to devour the poor in secret. However, the Lord intervenes dramatically.

By targeting the "head of his villages" (or "the head of his warriors"), God strikes the command structure of the enemy. The supreme irony in this verse is that God uses the enemy's "own arrows" (or staves) to pierce their leader. This structural destruction causes immediate panic and collapse among the invading forces. It demonstrates that the ultimate source of pride and opposition to God is inherently self-destructive under the weight of divine justice.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 3:15 - "And I will put enmity Between you and the woman, And between your seed and her Seed; He shall bruise your head, And you shall bruise His heel."

  • Exodus 18:25 - "And Moses chose able men out of all Israel, and made them heads over the people: rulers of thousands, rulers of hundreds, rulers of fifties, and rulers of tens."

  • Psalm 23:5 - "You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies; You anoint my head with oil; My cup runs over."

  • Habakkuk 3:13 - "You went forth for the salvation of Your people, For salvation with Your Anointed. You struck the head from the house of the wicked, By laying bare from foundation to neck."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Deuteronomy 28:13, the Lord promises, "And the LORD will make you the head and not the tail; you shall be above only, and not be beneath..." This contrast highlights that being the "head" is synonymous with dignity, freedom, and leadership, whereas being the "tail" signifies subservience and dependency. For the pastor or teacher, this word study offers a powerful reminder that while spiritual forces and earthly oppressors may seem terrifyingly strong, God's victory targets the very "head" of their power, guaranteeing their final and complete overthrow.
