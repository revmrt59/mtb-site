WALKED - ( darak , Strong's H1869 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb darak primarily means to tread, march, or trample, carrying a powerful graphic connotation of stepping down hard or marching forth with conquering force. In the context of Habakkuk 3:15, it describes God's triumphant march through the chaotic depths of the Red Sea during the Exodus, subduing the waters as a divine warrior riding His chariot of salvation. For the believer, this word serves as a vivid reminder of God's sovereign control over chaotic circumstances and His active, unstoppable march to deliver His people.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The verb darak is rooted in the physical act of treading or placing one's foot firmly on a surface. It is the action of pressing down with the weight of the body. This word was used in everyday agrarian life for treading grapes in a winepress to squeeze out the juice, or by archers who would "tread" (bend) a large bow by stepping on it to string it. The word picture conveys weight, pressure, authority, and progress. When a king or a warrior "walks" or "treads" in this sense, he is not merely strolling; he is asserting dominance, establishing a pathway, and subduing whatever is beneath his feet.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb darak possesses a range of meanings focused on physical contact between the foot and a surface:

  • To tread, trample, or march.

  • To bend a bow (literally, to tread on the bow to bend and string it).

  • To tread the winepress (to squeeze out juice).

  • To walk or proceed along a path.

In the Qal stem (as used in Habakkuk 3:15), it signifies walking, marching, treading, or stamping down. It highlights the direct, physical, and victorious action of the subject crossing over a terrain.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Treading the Winepress: The word captures the crushing power of judgment. In prophetic literature, the Lord treads the winepress alone in His anger, representing His victory over His enemies.

  • Bending the Bow: Archers would step on their large wooden bows to bend them and attach the string. The image of God bending His bow represents imminent divine justice.

  • The Triumphant March: It depicts God stepping upon the high places of the earth. This highlights His absolute transcendence and ownership over all creation.

4. Theological Implications

  • Sovereignty Over Chaos: In ancient Near Eastern literature, the sea represented chaos, death, and uncontrollable forces. By walking or treading upon the sea, Yahweh demonstrates His absolute mastery over the forces of chaos. The sea does not swallow Him; rather, it becomes His highway.

  • The Divine Warrior: Habakkuk 3 is a poetic theophany depicting God as a mighty warrior coming to deliver His people. The use of darak shows that God's deliverance is active, powerful, and physical. He does not watch from a distance; He marches through the storm to save His anointed.

  • Unstoppable Progress: When God walks or treads, nothing can stand in His way. The deep waters of the Red Sea must yield to His horses. His path of redemption is sovereignly cleared through the most impossible obstacles.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Habakkuk 3:15 occurs within the prophet's prayer and song of praise. Terrified by the impending Babylonian invasion, Habakkuk recalls God's past saving deeds to fuel his hope. The immediate context recounts the Exodus and the Red Sea crossing. Verse 15 states, "You walked through the sea with Your horses, Through the heap of great waters." Here, darak highlights the ease with which God subdues the mighty ocean depths. While Israel stood terrified on the banks, and Egypt's army drowned in the depths, Yahweh rode His war-chariots and walked right through the surging waters as if they were solid ground. It turns an obstacle of death into a highway of victory.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Deuteronomy 1:36 - "except Caleb the son of Jephunneh; he shall see it, and to him and his children I am giving the land on which he walked, because he wholly followed the Lord."

  • Job 9:8 - "He alone spreads out the heavens, And treads on the waves of the sea;"

  • Psalm 7:12 - "If he does not turn back, He will sharpen His sword; He bends His bow and makes it ready."

  • Isaiah 63:3 - "I have trodden the winepress alone, And from the peoples no one was with Me. For I have trodden them in My anger, And trampled them in My fury; Their blood is sprinkled upon My garments, And I have stained all My robes."

  • Micah 1:3 - "For behold, the Lord is coming out of His place; He will come down And tread on the high places of the earth."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The root derek (noun) meaning "way, path, road, or journey" is directly derived from darak. Our daily "way" of life is the path we tread.

There is a powerful literary connection in the New Testament when Jesus walks on the water (Matthew 14:22–33). By walking on the stormy Sea of Galilee, Jesus was performing the same divine action described in Habakkuk 3:15 and Job 9:8. He was not just performing a miracle; He was revealing His identity as the Divine Warrior, Yahweh, who treads upon the waves of the sea.
