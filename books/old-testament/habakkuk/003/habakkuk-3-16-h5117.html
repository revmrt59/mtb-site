REST - ( nuach , Strong's H5117 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb nuach refers to settling down, finding quietness, or being at peace after a period of movement, wandering, or intense distress. In Habakkuk 3:16, it describes a profound spiritual composure—a quiet confidence and surrender to God's sovereign plan in the midst of impending national judgment and calamity.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • nuach carries the physical concept of coming to settle in a specific place after flight or wandering. The primary word picture is that of Noah's ark resting on Mount Ararat after the turbulent waters of the flood began to subside, or a dove finding a place to rest her foot. It conveys the transition from frantic, turbulent agitation to peaceful stability and safety. It is the root from which the Hebrew name "Noah" (comfort, rest) is derived.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb nuach means to rest, settle down, dwell, or remain. In its active forms, it means to cause to rest, give rest, or leave quiet. When applied to a person's inner state, it denotes finding quietness, resting from anxiety, or waiting patiently. Unlike mere physical sleep, nuach represents a state of security, relief from toil, and freedom from fear. It is a cessation of struggle and a settling into a position of safety.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Resting Ark: The ark "rested" on the mountains of Ararat, symbolizing safety, security, and the end of cosmic upheaval (Genesis 8:4).

  • Divine Rest: Yahweh "rested" on the seventh day after creation, setting a pattern of sacred rest and satisfaction for humanity (Exodus 20:11).

  • The Resting Spirit: The Holy Spirit "rested" upon the seventy elders, signifying the abiding presence and equipping of God (Numbers 11:25).

  • Rest from Enemies: God promised to give Israel "rest" from all their enemies, establishing national security and peace in the Promised Land (Deuteronomy 12:10).

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, nuach points to the deep-seated security found only in a covenant relationship with Yahweh. True rest is not the absence of trouble, but the presence of God within the trouble. It highlights the biblical truth that ultimate safety is not found in geopolitical strength, military might, or favorable circumstances, but in active trust in the sovereign God who governs human history.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:16, the prophet faces the terrifying reality of the imminent Chaldean (Babylonian) invasion. His physical reaction is visceral: his body trembles, his lips quiver, and decay seems to enter his bones. Yet, in this state of physical terror, he speaks of "resting" in the day of trouble. Here, nuach means to wait quietly or remain in peaceful submission. It is a deliberate choice of faith over fear. Instead of panicking, fleeing, or raging against God's decree, Habakkuk resolves to anchor his soul in God's ultimate justice and mercy. Spiritual rest coexists with physical suffering and uncertainty.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 8:4 - "Then the ark rested in the seventh month, the seventeenth day of the month, on the mountains of Ararat."

  • Exodus 20:11 - "For in six days the LORD made the heavens and the earth, the sea, and all that is in them, and rested the seventh day. Therefore the LORD blessed the Sabbath day and hallowed it."

  • Joshua 3:13 - "And it shall come to pass, as soon as the soles of the feet of the priests who bear the ark of the LORD, the Lord of all the earth, shall rest in the waters of the Jordan, that the waters of the Jordan shall be cut off..."

  • Esther 9:17 - "This was on the thirteenth day of the month Adar; and on the fourteenth day of the month they rested and made it a day of feasting and gladness."

  • Isaiah 11:2 - "The Spirit of the LORD shall rest upon Him, The Spirit of wisdom and understanding, The Spirit of counsel and might, The Spirit of knowledge and of the fear of the LORD."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Greek equivalent often used in the Septuagint (LXX) to translate nuach is anapauo, which carries the sense of refreshing and giving rest. This links the Old Testament hope of rest directly to Jesus' invitation in Matthew 11:28 to "come to Me... and I will give you rest." The quiet confidence Habakkuk cultivated in the face of national disaster is the very rest that Jesus offers to all who are weary and heavy-laden.
