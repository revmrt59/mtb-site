BONES - ( 'etsem , Strong's H6106 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'etsem refers primarily to the skeletal bones of a human or animal, representing the deepest, most foundational structure of physical life, health, and human strength. In Habakkuk 3:16, the prophet uses this word to describe the overwhelming, paralyzing effect of God’s awesome presence and impending judgment, where his very core and innermost strength dissolved into "rottenness." It highlights how divine encounters and sovereign warnings completely strip away all human self-reliance, leaving the believer entirely dependent on God's mercy.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • In the ancient Near East, bones were understood as the physical framework of the body, the very seat of health, strength, and vitality. Strong bones equated to vigor and life, while dry, broken, or rotting bones symbolized extreme grief, disease, divine judgment, or utter helplessness.

  • As the last part of the body to decay after death, bones represented a person's enduring substance. Therefore, the concept of "rottenness" entering the bones while a person was still alive depicted the ultimate collapse of one's physical and spiritual strength from the inside out. It was a vivid picture of absolute structural failure at the core of human existence.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The noun 'etsem means bone, skeleton, body, or outward form. It derives from a root verb meaning to be vast, mighty, numerous, or strong.

  • In the idiomatic thought of the Hebrew Bible, 'etsem frequently denotes the "self," the "very thing," or the "essence" of something. For instance, the phrase "on the selfsame day" literally translates as "on the bone of this day."

  • In poetic and wisdom literature, the word represents the seat of deep-seated emotions, physical durability, and spiritual condition. When used in contexts of pain, fear, or distress, it describes an agony that penetrates past the skin and flesh directly into the core of human consciousness.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Seat of Health and Joy: Moistened bones or bones delivered from fear represent ultimate physical and spiritual restoration, indicating a life that is fully aligned with God's blessing.

  • The Seat of Pain and Sorrow: Broken bones or burning bones depict deep spiritual anguish, guilt, and the consuming power of God's hand of discipline or His burning Word.

  • Rottenness of the Bones: Used metaphorically to describe the devastating, inward decay caused by shame, envy, or, in the case of Habakkuk, a staggering vision of the Lord's holy majesty and coming wrath.

4. Theological Implications

  • Human Fragility vs. Divine Majesty: When God reveals His holiness or His decree of judgment, human strength is exposed as utterly fragile. The bones, which normally support the body, fail immediately, demonstrating that no flesh can stand secure in its own power before Him.

  • The Internalizing of Divine Revelation: Prophetic revelation is not merely an intellectual or academic exercise; it affects the entire person—physically, emotionally, and spiritually. Habakkuk's body trembles and his bones rot because he has truly grasped the weight of God's holiness and the reality of the coming Chaldean invasion.

  • Preparation for Grace: Before the prophet can declare his triumphant joy in the God of his salvation in the closing verses of his book, his self-sufficiency must be completely broken. The "rottenness in the bones" represents the necessary death of self-reliance that precedes true, unshakeable faith.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Habakkuk 3:16, the prophet hears the report of God's coming judgment. His immediate response is a total physical and emotional collapse: his belly trembles, his lips quiver, and rottenness enters his bones.

  • The word for "rottenness" (raqab) refers to decay, caries, or wood-rot. By stating that rottenness entered his 'etsem, Habakkuk expresses that his skeletal framework—his ultimate support system—has suddenly turned to dust. He is completely emptied of physical capacity.

  • This deep, visceral terror is not a lack of faith, but the appropriate response of a mortal man standing before a holy God. It is this very shattering of his bones that prepares him to "rest in the day of trouble."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:23: "And Adam said: 'This is now bone of my bones And flesh of my flesh; She shall be called Woman, Because she was taken out of Man.'"

  • Job 19:20: "My bone clings to my skin and to my flesh, And I have escaped by the skin of my teeth."

  • Psalm 32:3: "When I kept silent, my bones grew old Through my groaning all the day long."

  • Psalm 51:8: "Make me hear joy and gladness, That the bones You have broken may rejoice."

  • Proverbs 14:30: "A sound heart is life to the body, But envy is rottenness to the bones."

  • Jeremiah 20:9: "Then I said, 'I will not make mention of Him, Nor speak anymore in His name.' But His word was in my heart like a burning fire Shut up in my bones; I was weary of holding it back, And I could not."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • In Hebrew thought, there is no strict dualistic division between the physical and the spiritual. What affects the soul immediately manifests in the bones, meaning physical ailments or sensations of weakness often pointed directly to spiritual realities.

  • The preservation of bones was highly valued in Israelite culture. To have one's bones disturbed, left unburied, or rotted was considered a curse, making Habakkuk's voluntary submission to this internal "rotting" a powerful sign of his total yielding to God's sovereign will.

  • The ultimate restoration of "dry bones" in Ezekiel 37 demonstrates that only God can breathe life back into a skeletal frame that has been completely desiccated by judgment, mirroring Habakkuk's transition from physical collapse to spiritual triumph.
