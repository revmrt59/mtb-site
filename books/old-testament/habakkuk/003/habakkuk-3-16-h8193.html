LIPS - ( saphah , Strong's H8193 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word saphah primarily refers to the physical lips, but metaphorically extends to speech, language, and the boundary of the mouth. In Habakkuk 3:16, it describes the prophet’s involuntary physical response to a terrifying divine revelation. Rather than a controlled expression of speech, the quivering of Habakkuk's lips signifies overwhelming awe, trembling submission, and the collapse of human self-sufficiency in the presence of the holy, sovereign God who is about to judge.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The noun saphah denotes an edge, border, or lip. It is used literally of the lips of the human mouth, but also of geographical and structural boundaries, such as the shore of a sea, the bank of a river, the brim of a vessel, or the hem of a garment.

  • The physical word picture is that of a boundary or threshold. The lips serve as the physical gateway of the mouth—the gatekeepers of speech and the instruments of communication. When applied to speech, it represents not just the physical organ but the words, dialect, or confession that cross over this threshold.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The word saphah has a distinct semantic range:

  • The physical lips as the organs of speech, kissing, or eating.

  • Speech, language, dialect, or talk (the words produced by the lips).

  • An edge, margin, border, shore, or brink of a body of water or physical object.

  • In the context of a physical human reaction to a divine encounter, the trembling or quivering of the lips denotes the loss of motor control under intense emotional and spiritual distress.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Metaphorically for Speech and Confession: "Unclean lips" in Isaiah 6:5 points to moral defilement in communication.

  • Metaphorically for Character and Deceit: "Lying lips" in Proverbs 12:22 represents a deceptive character, while "restraining one's lips" in Proverbs 10:19 represents wisdom and self-control.

  • Structural and Geographical Boundaries: The "lip of the sea" (Genesis 22:17, translated as "seashore") and the "lip of the vessel" (1 Kings 7:26, translated as "brim") illustrate its use as an edge or boundary.

  • Sacrificial Praise: The "fruit of our lips" (Hosea 14:2) portrays verbal praise and confession as a spiritual offering to God.

4. Theological Implications

  • The physical reaction of the lips reveals the state of the inner man. When God reveals Himself in judgment or majesty, human strength is utterly undone, and the physical body reacts to the weight of the divine presence.

  • The quivering of the lips represents the silencing of human pride. Before God speaks, humanity often has much to say; when God reveals His awesome power, all human speech is reduced to silent trembling.

  • True spiritual awe is not merely intellectual; it penetrates the entire physical being, capturing the very organs of communication. This highlights the reality of "trembling at His word" (Isaiah 66:2).

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Habakkuk 3:16, the prophet has just witnessed a vision of God’s marching wrath and impending judgment on Israel through the Chaldeans, alongside the ultimate salvation of His people.

  • The phrase "My lips quivered at the voice" portrays a state of profound shock and holy dread.

  • Rather than speaking, debating, or complaining—as Habakkuk did in chapters 1 and 2—his lips can now only tremble. The self-assertive questioning of the prophet is completely silenced.

  • The quivering lips show that the messenger is entirely subdued by the reality of the message. This physical undoing precedes his transition to absolute trust and joy in God alone (Habakkuk 3:17-19).

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Isaiah 6:5 - "So I said: 'Woe is me, for I am undone! Because I am a man of unclean lips, And I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips; For my eyes have seen the King, The LORD of hosts.'"

  • Proverbs 10:19 - "In the multitude of words sin is not lacking, But he who restrains his lips is wise."

  • Psalm 51:15 - "O Lord, open my lips, And my mouth shall show forth Your praise."

  • Proverbs 12:22 - "Lying lips are an abomination to the LORD, But those who deal truthfully are His delight."

  • Job 2:10 - "But he said to her, 'You speak as one of the foolish women speaks. Shall we indeed accept good from God, and shall we not accept adversity?' In all this Job did not sin with his lips."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • There is a powerful contrast in Habakkuk's spiritual journey. In chapter 1, his lips were filled with questioning and complaint. In chapter 2, he stood watch to see what God would say to his reproof. By chapter 3, having heard the report of God's majesty and judgment, his lips no longer debate or argue; they quiver in holy surrender. This teaches that true prayer and encounter with God ultimately move the believer from vocal protest to trembling, quiet trust.
