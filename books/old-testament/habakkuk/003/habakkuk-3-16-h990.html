BODY - ( beten , Strong's H990 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word beten primarily refers to the physical abdomen, belly, or womb, but it frequently serves as a vivid physiological metaphor for a person's innermost being, emotions, and soul. In Habakkuk 3:16, the prophet's "body" (NKJV) or "belly" trembles in response to the terrifying majesty of God's impending judgment and deliverance. This usage highlights how intense spiritual encounters and deep theological realities directly manifest as physical sensations, demonstrating the profound integration of body and spirit in Hebrew thought.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word beten represents the central cavity of the human torso. It is the place of gestation (the womb), the organ of digestion (the stomach), and metaphorically the seat of the deepest human emotions, anxieties, and desires. In ancient Near Eastern thought, the internal organs—such as the heart, kidneys, and belly—were not merely biological components but the control centers of human personality, volition, and feeling. To experience a shaking or trembling in the beten is to feel an emotion so overwhelming that it bypasses intellectual processing and strikes directly at the core of one's physical existence.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, beten has three primary ranges of meaning:

  • Literally, the abdomen, belly, or stomach of a person or animal.

  • The womb, as the place of conception, pregnancy, and birth.

  • Metaphorically, the innermost part of a person, representing the seat of mental, emotional, and spiritual activity.

In the context of Habakkuk 3:16, the term denotes the physical body or internal organs reacting violently to overwhelming dread and awe.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, beten is used as a poignant word picture:

  • The Seat of Anxiety and Sorrow: In Psalm 31:9, the psalmist cries out that his soul and his "belly" (NKJV: "body") are consumed with grief.

  • The Vessel of Wisdom: Proverbs 22:18 advises keeping the words of the wise within one's "belly" (NKJV: "within you"), portraying the inner person as a storehouse for truth.

  • The Source of Deceit or Empty Words: Job 15:35 describes the wicked as conceiving trouble and bringing forth futility from their "belly" (NKJV: "womb").

4. Theological Implications

The use of beten to describe spiritual experience points to the holistic nature of biblical anthropology. Scripture does not separate the spiritual life from physical reality. When a person encounters the living God, the reaction is not merely intellectual or "spiritual" in an abstract sense; it affects the entire person—heart, mind, bones, and belly. Furthermore, it illustrates the proper creaturely response to the absolute holiness and sovereign power of God. True reverence is not a cold theological assent but a gut-wrenching realization of human weakness in the presence of Almighty God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:16, the prophet has just witnessed a vision of God marching through the earth in indignation, crushing the wicked, and riding on horses of salvation. The sheer scale of this divine theophany, combined with the realization that Judah must first endure the devastating invasion of the Chaldeans, induces a profound physical shock. The translation of beten as "body" in the NKJV captures the holistic impact of this fear: "When I heard, my body trembled; My lips quivered at the voice; Rottenness entered my bones." Habakkuk's entire physical frame collapses under the weight of God's word. This somatic response of his beten is the necessary prelude to his ultimate statement of faith in verses 17-19. Before he can joyfully triumph in the God of his salvation, he must be completely emptied of self-reliance, a process physically felt in his very gut.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Psalm 31:9 - "Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I am in trouble; My eye wastes away with grief, Yes, my soul and my body!"

  • Job 32:19 - "Indeed my belly is like wine that has no vent; It is ready to burst like new wineskins."

  • Jonah 2:2 - "And he said: 'I cried out to the Lord because of my affliction, And He answered me. Out of the belly of Sheol I cried, And You heard my voice.'"

  • Proverbs 18:8 - "The words of a talebearer are like tasty trifles, And they go down into the inmost body."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the New Testament, the Greek equivalent to beten is koilia. This connection is vividly seen in John 7:38, where Jesus promises that "out of his heart [literally 'belly' or koilia] will flow rivers of living water." Just as Habakkuk's beten trembled with divine awe, the believer's innermost being becomes the source of spiritual vitality through the Holy Spirit. This demonstrates the consistent biblical pattern of using the physical center of the body to describe the deepest realities of spiritual experience.
