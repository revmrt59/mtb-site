LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh (translated as "LORD" in all capital letters in the NKJV) is the personal, covenantal, and self-revealed name of the God of Israel. Derived from the Hebrew verb meaning "to be," it signifies God’s eternal, self-existent, and unchanging nature. In Habakkuk 3:18, this name serves as the anchor for the prophet's joy. Even in the face of imminent national collapse and total agricultural devastation, Habakkuk resolves to rejoice in Yahweh, the faithful covenant partner who guarantees salvation and strength to His people.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • Yahweh is the sacred Tetragrammaton, the four-letter consonant name (YHWH) of God.

  • The origin of this name is deeply connected to God’s revelation to Moses at the burning bush in Exodus 3:14, where God declared His name as "I AM WHO I AM" (Ehyeh Asher Ehyeh).

  • It represents the self-existent One who is wholly independent of His creation, yet intimately involved in history.

  • Historically, the Jewish people held this name in such high reverence that they avoided pronouncing it aloud to prevent taking it in vain, substituting the word Adonai ("Lord") during public readings. This tradition is reflected in modern English translations by printing the name in capital letters as "LORD."

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Yahweh is the proper noun denoting the one true God, occurring over 6,800 times in the Old Testament.

  • It is derived from the archaic root hawah, which is synonymous with hayah, meaning "to be," "to exist," or "to become."

  • Lexically, the name expresses the absolute self-existence of God. He has no beginning and no end; He is the source of all being.

  • As a proper name, it is non-transferable. Unlike generic titles for God (such as Elohim), Yahweh refers exclusively to the covenant God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The name Yahweh is fundamentally relational and covenantal. Whenever God interacts with humanity in terms of grace, redemption, covenants, and personal relationship, Yahweh is almost always the name used.

  • It is often paired with other descriptive titles to form compound names that illustrate God's character, such as Yahweh Yireh ("The LORD Will Provide") or Yahweh Raah ("The LORD Is My Shepherd").

  • In Hebrew thought, a person's name represents their character, presence, and authority. To call upon the name of Yahweh is to invoke His actual personal presence and His absolute commitment to His promises.

4. Theological Implications

  • Sovereignty and Immutability: Because Yahweh is the self-existent One, He is entirely self-sufficient and does not change. His purposes cannot be thwarted by historical crises, human failures, or geopolitical shifts.

  • Covenant Faithfulness: The name is the ultimate guarantee of God's promises. If God is Yahweh, He cannot fail to fulfill His covenant obligations. He remains faithful even when His people are unfaithful.

  • Redemptive Deliverance: Yahweh is the God who hears the cries of His people and comes down to deliver them. The name is forever linked to the Exodus, the defining act of redemption in the Old Testament.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Habakkuk 3:17, the prophet describes a worst-case scenario: the failure of the fig tree, the vines, the olive labor, the fields, the flock, and the herd. This represents absolute economic collapse, starvation, and the loss of God's material blessings due to the impending Babylonian invasion.

  • In Habakkuk 3:18, the prophet shifts his focus dramatically: "Yet I will rejoice in the LORD..."

  • By choosing the name Yahweh, Habakkuk anchors his joy not in the gifts of the covenant (crops, livestock, land), but in the Giver of the covenant Himself.

  • Even if God's blessings are temporarily stripped away, the Person of Yahweh remains. His character is unchanged, His promise of salvation is secure, and He remains the living source of the prophet's joy and ultimate triumph.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4: "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens,"

  • Exodus 3:15: "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

  • Psalm 23:1: "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

  • Isaiah 40:28: "Have you not known? Have you not heard? The everlasting God, the LORD, The Creator of the ends of the earth, Neither faints nor is weary. His understanding is unsearchable."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • In the Septuagint (LXX), the Greek translation of the Old Testament, Yahweh was translated as Kurios ("Lord").

  • In the New Testament, this same title Kurios is applied directly to Jesus Christ (such as in Philippians 2:9-11). By doing so, the New Testament writers clearly identify Jesus as the incarnation of Yahweh, the covenant God of Israel.

  • Jesus further confirmed this connection in His "I AM" statements in the Gospel of John, pointing directly back to the self-existent name revealed to Moses.
