GOD - ( elohim , Strong's H430 )

Summary:

In Habakkuk 3:18, the word translated "God" is elohim, the plural noun of majesty that denotes the sovereign, almighty Creator and Judge of the universe. In this climax of Habakkuk’s prophecy, the term functions as a declaration of absolute trust. By anchoring his joy in the "God of my salvation," Habakkuk shifts his focus from the imminent devastation of his circumstances to the transcendent, covenant-keeping Ruler who possesses the ultimate power to save and sustain His people.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word elohim is the primary plural title used for the one true God in the Old Testament. Morphologically, it is the plural form of eloah (majesty/strength) and is related to the shorter form el (might/power). Rather than indicating polytheism, when used of the God of Israel, elohim is paired with singular verbs and adjectives. This grammatical feature represents a "plural of majesty" or "plural of intensification." It conveys the idea of supreme greatness, the fullness of divine attributes, and unmatched power. In the ancient Near Eastern context, where nations worshiped local, limited deities, elohim stood out as the name for the singular, transcendent Ruler of all creation.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term elohim denotes God as the ultimate power, the supreme Deity, the Creator of the universe, and the Governor of history. It signifies:

  • The absolute, supreme deity, distinct from pagan idols.

  • The sovereign Ruler who possesses limitless power and authority over nature and nations.

  • The divine Judge who executes justice and establishes cosmic order.

  • The source of life, strength, and deliverance for His covenant people.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Plural of Majesty: It paints a picture of a God who is so immense, complex, and glorious that singular terms cannot fully capture His essence. He is the accumulation of all power and authority.

  • Cosmic Creator: The word is inherently linked to creation, where elohim speaks the universe into existence by His sovereign will.

  • Transcendent Authority: Unlike regional gods who were bound by geography, elohim represents the cosmic King whose jurisdiction spans the entire earth, directing the rise and fall of empires like Babylon.

4. Theological Implications

  • Absolute Sovereignty: The use of elohim reminds the reader that God is not a passive observer of human suffering. He is the master of history, orchestrating all things according to His holy purposes.

  • The Ground of Faith: By identifying God as elohim, Scripture bases human hope not on favorable circumstances, but on the unshakeable character and infinite power of the Creator.

  • Covenant Faithfulness: When elohim is linked with personal pronouns ("my God" or "God of my salvation"), it reveals that the infinite, transcendent Ruler of the cosmos has entered into a personal, binding relationship with His people.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:18, the prophet faces a terrifying reality: the impending invasion of the Chaldeans, which will result in total economic and agricultural ruin (no fruit on the vines, no cattle in the stalls). In the face of this absolute barrenness, Habakkuk makes a deliberate choice: "Yet I will rejoice in the LORD, I will joy in the God of my salvation."

Here, Habakkuk pairs the covenant name Yahweh (translated "LORD") with elohim (translated "God"). This pairing is a profound theological statement. The LORD who made a covenant with Israel is the same elohim who possesses all cosmic power. By calling Him "the God of my salvation," Habakkuk declares that even if the earth fails to produce, the sovereign Creator remains his secure refuge. His joy is not anchored in the harvest, but in the majestic, unchanging character of elohim.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 1:1 - "In the beginning God [elohim] created the heavens and the earth."

  • Psalm 46:1 - "God [elohim] is our refuge and strength, A very present help in trouble."

  • Isaiah 40:28 - "Have you not known? Have you not heard? The everlasting God [elohim], the LORD, the Creator of the ends of the earth, Neither faints nor is weary..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The structure of Habakkuk 3:18 utilizes Hebrew poetic parallelism. "I will rejoice in the LORD" is paralleled with "I will joy in the God of my salvation." This demonstrates that Yahweh and Elohim are one and the same. The covenant partner of Israel is the almighty God of the universe. This truth transforms Habakkuk’s fear into triumphant worship, proving that genuine joy is a spiritual reality found only in a relationship with the sovereign Creator.
