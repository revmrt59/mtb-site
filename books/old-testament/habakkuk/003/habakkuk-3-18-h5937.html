REJOICE - ( alaz , Strong's H5937 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb alaz refers to an exuberant, vocal, and triumphant expression of joy. In Habakkuk 3:18, it describes a willful decision to exult in Yahweh even in the midst of utter material ruin, demonstrating that true spiritual joy is anchored in the character and salvation of God rather than outward circumstances.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root of alaz carries a physical, demonstrative connotation of leaping for joy, exulting, or swelling with delight. It is closely associated with victory, harvest celebrations, and national triumph. Unlike a quiet, internal state of happiness, alaz paints a picture of a joy that cannot be contained. It is an active, visible, and vocal expression of gladness. In the ancient Near East, such exultation was common when a king was crowned, when a battle was won, or when the harvest was bountiful.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb alaz means to exult, be jubilant, or triumph. It refers to a state of intense, demonstrative gladness. Lexically, it shares close semantic space with giyl (to spin around, rejoice) and samach (to be glad, brighten up). The term denotes a celebratory state of mind and body that manifests in praise, physical expression, and bold declarations of confidence. It is a triumphant joy that rises above opposition or trial.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, alaz is used to describe the righteous exulting in God's protection, the creation itself shouting for joy, and even adversaries triumphing falsely over the fallen. The word pictures associated with alaz include:

  • The heart leaping or swelling with confidence in God.

  • A soldier shouting in victory after a hard-fought battle.

  • The fields and trees exulting in the presence of the Creator.

These pictures emphasize that the joy expressed is not a passive feeling but a dynamic reaction to a profound reality.

4. Theological Implications

Joy in the Old Testament is not merely an emotion but a theological response to the covenant faithfulness of God. When alaz is directed toward Yahweh, it reflects a deep-seated assurance that God is sovereign, victorious, and protective. This triumphant joy acts as a spiritual weapon, shifting the believer's focus from earthly losses to eternal security in God. It affirms that God is sufficient, even when all temporal blessings are stripped away. It is a faith-filled declaration that God has the final victory.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:18, alaz occurs immediately after a devastating description of total agricultural and economic collapse in verse 17. The fig tree does not blossom, vines produce no fruit, the olive crop fails, the fields yield no food, and the folds are empty of livestock. Despite this absolute ruin, Habakkuk declares, "Yet I will rejoice in the LORD."

The adversative "Yet" (wa) signals a conscious, defiant choice. By pairing alaz ("rejoice/exult") with giyl ("joy/spin with delight"), the prophet shows that his satisfaction is anchored in "the God of my salvation," not in the harvest. His joy is not circumstantial but relational and covenantal. Even when the earth offers nothing to celebrate, Yahweh Himself is more than enough to evoke triumphant exultation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Psalm 9:2: "I will be glad and rejoice in You; I will sing praise to Your name, O Most High."

  • Psalm 28:7: "The LORD is my strength and my shield; My heart trusted in Him, and I am helped; Therefore my heart greatly rejoices, And with my song I will praise Him."

  • Psalm 149:5: "Let the saints be joyful in glory; Let them sing aloud on their beds."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Septuagint (LXX) translates alaz in Habakkuk 3:18 using the Greek verb agalliao, which means to exult, be exceedingly glad, or leap for joy. This Greek term is the same word used by Jesus in the Beatitudes to command His disciples to "rejoice and be exceedingly glad" under persecution (Matthew 5:12) and by Mary in the Magnificat (Luke 1:47). This highlights a continuous biblical thread: triumphant joy in God is a defining characteristic of faith in times of trial.
