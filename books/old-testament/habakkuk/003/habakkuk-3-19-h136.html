GOD - ( adonai , Strong's H136 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word adonai represents God's supreme ownership, sovereign authority, and absolute lordship over all creation. Derived as a plural of majesty from the root adon ("lord" or "master"), it emphasizes that God is not merely a distant ruler, but the ultimate Master who possesses rightful ownership and authority over His servants. In Habakkuk 3:19, the prophet pairs this title with the covenant name of God (YHWH) to declare that the sovereign Lord of the universe is his personal source of strength, providing supernatural endurance, stability, and joy even in times of severe national devastation.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word adonai is rooted in the common noun adon, which refers to a master, owner, or lord who exercises authority over a household, land, or servants. In ancient Near Eastern culture, the relationship between a master and a servant was defined by absolute ownership, protection, and provision. The servant owed undivided loyalty and obedience to the master, while the master was responsible for the servant's well-being, defense, sustenance, and ultimate safety.

When applied to the true God, the plural form adonai is used as a plural of majesty. This grammatical feature expresses intensive greatness, supreme majesty, and the fullness of lordship. It elevates the concept of human lordship to its absolute, infinite degree. It speaks of a deeply personal relationship where the believer submits completely to God's authority, finding rest in the reality that the Master is fully responsible for the servant's care.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning of adonai is "my Lord" or "Sovereign Lord." Grammatically, it is the plural of adon combined with a first-person singular pronominal suffix ("my"), which historically transitioned into a proper noun used exclusively for God to denote His supreme majesty.

Lexically, the word signifies absolute sovereignty, master, ultimate authority, and supreme ruler. When it is paired with the covenant name YHWH (which is sometimes written as Yehovih and translated as "God" or "LORD" depending on the Hebrew vowel pointing), it highlights the dual nature of God. He is both the self-existent, faithful, covenant-keeping God of relationship and the absolute, sovereign Master of all circumstances.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, adonai is used to express deep reverence, humility, and submission in prayer and worship. Servants of God frequently use this title when they recognize their own weakness and God's absolute right to direct their lives.

  • The Master of the House: The word picture of adonai is that of a master standing over his household. He directs the work, allocates the resources, defends the estate against enemies, and ensures that every member is supplied. The servant does not have to worry about the future or supply their own strength; they simply obey, trusting the master's abundant resources.

  • The Sovereign Ruler: It depicts a king whose decrees cannot be thwarted. To call God adonai is to declare that He has the final word over history, nature, and individual destinies.

4. Theological Implications

  • Absolute Ownership: Adonai establishes the foundational truth of God's absolute ownership of creation. He is the Lord of all because He is the Creator of all.

  • Transcendent yet Immanent: This title bridges the gap between God's transcendent majesty and His personal, immanent care. While adon can refer to human lords, the specific form adonai is reserved exclusively for God, showing that His lordship is unique, holy, and supreme.

  • The Privilege of Service: It underscores the believer's role as a willing servant (ebed). True faith recognizes that because God is adonai, the believer has no independent rights but enjoys the immense privilege of being under the protection of the ultimate Sovereign.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:19, the prophet is standing in the ruins of his circumstances. The northern kingdom has fallen, and the ruthless Chaldeans are about to invade Judah. Habakkuk has just painted a bleak picture of agricultural and economic collapse—no blossoms on the fig tree, no fruit on the vines, and empty stalls (Habakkuk 3:17-18).

Yet, Habakkuk declares, "The LORD God is my strength." In the Hebrew text, this is Yehovih Adonai. By using Adonai (translated as "God" here to accompany "LORD"), Habakkuk acknowledges that despite the impending national disaster, God remains the absolute Sovereign Master of the universe. The circumstances are not out of control; they are under the authority of Adonai.

Because Adonai is his master, Habakkuk does not need to rely on his own strength. The Master supplies the strength. This sovereign Master enables him to run securely upon the heights of suffering, giving him "feet like deer's feet" to navigate treacherous spiritual terrain with grace and stability.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Joshua 7:7 - "And Joshua said, 'Alas, Lord GOD, why have You brought this people over the Jordan at all—to deliver us into the hand of the Amorites, to destroy us?'"

  • Psalm 86:12 - "I will praise You, O Lord my God, with all my heart, and I will glorify Your name forevermore."

  • Isaiah 6:1 - "In the year that King Uzziah died, I saw the Lord sitting on a throne, high and lifted up, and the train of His robe filled the temple."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Scribal Substitution: Out of intense reverence for the covenant name of God (YHWH), ancient Jewish scribes and readers substituted the pronunciation of YHWH with Adonai. This practice underscores the supreme sanctity associated with the name Adonai. When the vowel points of Adonai were placed under the consonants of YHWH, it eventually led to the English hybrid word "Jehovah."

  • New Testament Connection: The New Testament writers utilized the Greek equivalent Kyrios to translate both YHWH and Adonai, applying this exalted title of absolute lordship directly to Jesus Christ (e.g., Philippians 2:11), establishing His deity and absolute authority over all.
