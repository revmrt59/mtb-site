STRENGTH - ( chayil , Strong's H2428 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word chayil represents more than mere physical muscle or intellectual capability; it denotes an all-encompassing force, efficiency, wealth, valor, and substance. When the prophet Habakkuk declares that the Lord God is his "strength" (chayil) amidst national devastation and personal lack, he is not merely referring to an inner feeling of fortitude. He is laying hold of God Himself as his ultimate resource, his army, his wealth, and his invincible defense. This word shifts the believer's focus from fragile human reserves to the inexhaustible, sovereign power of the Almighty, enabling triumphant perseverance through the darkest trials.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root of chayil is associated with twisting, spinning, or writhing, pointing to the concept of firm endurance, tight binding, or the gathering of strength—much like the wound strands of a heavy rope. In the ancient Near Eastern context, chayil carried military connotations, describing an army, a host of warriors, or a display of valiant force. It also depicted material substance, representing wealth, efficiency, and capability. Thus, the word picture is one of consolidated, dynamic power—whether found in a formidable army, a wealthy estate, or a person of noble and resolute character.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun chayil has a broad semantic range centered on capability and power:

  • Strength, might, power, and physical efficiency.

  • An army, host, military force, or band of soldiers.

  • Wealth, substance, riches, and resources.

  • Virtue, capability, integrity, and valor.

In the context of personal devotion and theological confession, it denotes the absolute capacity to endure, prevail, and accomplish God's purposes through divine enablement.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Warrior's Might: Often translated as "valiant" or "valor" (as in gibbor chayil, "mighty man of valor"), portraying a warrior who is fully equipped, brave, and victorious in battle.

  • The Army of God: Used to describe military hosts, such as Pharaoh's army or the armies of Israel, representing a collective force of irresistible momentum.

  • Socioeconomic Capability: Denotes wealth or substance, illustrating that true riches and the capacity to produce wealth ultimately come from God.

  • Moral and Functional Excellence: Used in Proverbs to describe the "virtuous" (chayil) wife, pointing to her capability, strength of character, and industry.

4. Theological Implications

  • God as the Ultimate Resource: When applied to God or derived from Him, chayil teaches that true strength is not self-generated. Human energy and material wealth are fleeting, but God is an eternal, unchanging repository of power.

  • Strength in Weakness: The Old Testament frequently contrasts human frailty with divine chayil. True faith confesses that when all earthly resources fail, God remains the believer's ultimate portion and power.

  • Sovereign Sufficiency: Because God is the Lord of hosts (who commands the armies, or chayil, of heaven), those who trust in Him have access to an infinite supply of spiritual and physical endurance.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:19, the prophet stands amidst the ruins of his nation's economy and security. The fig tree does not blossom, the vines yield no fruit, and the fields lie barren. Yet, Habakkuk makes a deliberate choice to rejoice in the God of his salvation. By declaring "The LORD God is my strength (chayil)," Habakkuk redefines his source of security. He has lost his material wealth (chayil), his nation's military defenses (chayil) are shattered by the Chaldeans, and his physical energy is spent. In their place, God Himself becomes his chayil—his wealth, his army, and his vital force. This divine strength produces immediate, practical results: it makes his feet "like deer's feet," allowing him to navigate the treacherous, slippery high places of affliction with sure-footed confidence and joy.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 18:21 - "Moreover you shall select from all the people able [chayil] men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness; and place such over them to be rulers of thousands, rulers of hundreds, rulers of fifties, and rulers of tens."

  • Ruth 3:11 - "And now, my daughter, do not fear. I will do for you all that you request, for all the people of my town know that you are a virtuous [chayil] woman."

  • Psalm 18:32 - "It is God who arms me with strength [chayil], And makes my way perfect."

  • Proverbs 31:10 - "Who can find a virtuous [chayil] wife? For her worth is far above rubies."

  • Zechariah 4:6 - "So he answered and said to me: 'This is the word of the LORD to Zerubbabel: "Not by might [chayil] nor by power, but by My Spirit," Says the LORD of hosts.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The dramatic shift from fear to faith in Habakkuk's prayer is marked by his vocabulary. In Habakkuk 3:16, his body trembled and decay entered his bones. In verse 19, his trembling is replaced by the sovereign chayil of God. This reveals a profound spiritual principle: divine strength is not merely an addition to human strength; it is a replacement for it. When we are emptied of our own chayil (our capability, security, and wealth), we are finally positioned to receive His.
