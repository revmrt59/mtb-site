REVIVE - ( chayah , Strong's H2421 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb chayah fundamentally means to live, breathe, be alive, or be restored to life and vigor. In the Piel grammatical stem, which is used in Habakkuk 3:2, the word functions intensively and causatively, meaning to "preserve alive," "restore to life," "quicken," or "vigorously renew." Used in Habakkuk's passionate prayer, it describes a cry for God to actively preserve, sustain, and breathe fresh vitality into His covenant people and His divine purposes during a dark season of impending national judgment and exile. It is a plea for spiritual and physical preservation when death, decay, and destruction seem otherwise inevitable.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root concept of chayah is closely tied to the breath of life. In the ancient Near Eastern mindset, life was not merely the absence of physical death, but a state of flourishing, safety, and functional vitality. To have "life" was to be connected to the Source of life (Yahweh) and to experience His blessing. Conversely, sickness, oppression, and exile were seen as states of creeping death. Thus, to "revive" (chayah) is to rescue someone from the grip of death, decay, or ruin and restore them to a state of robust, active life. It evokes the image of a dying ember being gently but powerfully blown upon until it bursts back into flame, or a withered plant receiving rain after a devastating drought.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Basic Meaning: To live, have life, remain alive, or sustain life.

  • Piel Stem Meaning: To preserve alive, keep alive, nourish, quicken, restore to life, rebuild, or renew.

  • Grammatical Nuance in Habakkuk 3:2: The verb appears as a Piel imperative (chayyehu), which serves as an urgent petition for God to actively cause His work to live, revitalize it, and keep it from fading away. It carries the double nuance of both keeping a remnant physically alive during calamity and renewing them spiritually.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Physical Resurrection: The word is used to describe physical restoration from death to life, such as the widow's son being brought back to life by the prophet Elijah.

  • Rebuilding and Restoration: In Nehemiah 4:2, the word is used metaphorically to describe "reviving" or rebuilding burned stones from heaps of rubbish to recreate the protective walls of Jerusalem.

  • Spiritual Reinvigoration: It is frequently used in the Psalms to describe restoring a crushed, discouraged, or backslidden soul to joy, strength, and communion with God.

  • Preserving Life in Famine: It describes how God sent Joseph ahead to Egypt to "preserve life" during the devastating famine, showing God's hand in keeping His covenant people from extinction.

4. Theological Implications

  • God as the Sole Source of Life: The use of chayah underscores that life and spiritual vitality are not self-generated. Only Yahweh can quicken what is dead, dying, or decaying.

  • Grace in the Midst of Judgment: Revival is requested not because Israel deserves it, but because of God's covenant mercy. It highlights the tension between God's holy wrath and His redeeming love.

  • The Continuity of God's Covenant Work: The prayer assumes that God has a historical "work" (His saving plan) that cannot fail, even if it must pass through the crucible of discipline.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Habakkuk 3:2, the prophet has just received the terrifying revelation that God is raising up the ruthless Babylonians to judge Judah. Habakkuk is trembling with fear ("I have heard Your speech and was afraid").

  • Instead of arguing with God, Habakkuk prays. The "midst of the years" refers to the coming period of Babylonian captivity—a prolonged season of spiritual darkness, silence, and national death.

  • When Habakkuk prays, "revive Your work in the midst of the years," he is asking God not to let His covenant purposes die during this dark epoch. He asks God to keep the flame of His redemptive work alive in the interim, to preserve a remnant, and to manifest His power and mercy even while executing His righteous judgment.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Psalm 85:6 – "Will You not revive us again, That Your people may rejoice in You?"

  • Psalm 119:25 – "My soul clings to the dust; Revive me according to Your word."

  • Psalm 138:7 – "Though I walk in the midst of trouble, You will revive me; You will stretch out Your hand Against the wrath of my enemies, And Your right hand will save me."

  • Hosea 6:2 – "After two days He will revive us; On the third day He will raise us up, That we may live in His sight."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • In the Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint), chayah in Habakkuk 3:2 is translated with forms of zoopoieo (to make alive, quicken), emphasizing the divine action of breathing supernatural life into a desperate situation.

  • True revival in the biblical sense is not an emotional excitement manufactured by human organization; it is the sovereign, life-giving work of God restoring His people from a state of spiritual decay and death to vibrant, obedient fellowship with Himself.
