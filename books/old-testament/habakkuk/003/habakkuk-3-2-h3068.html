LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh is the personal, covenant-keeping name of the God of Israel, revealed most prominently to Moses at the burning bush. Representing God's self-existence, eternal presence, and unchanging faithfulness, this name signifies that God is not an abstract force but a relational Being who binds Himself to His people by oaths and promises. In Habakkuk 3:2, as the prophet faces the imminent terror of the Babylonian invasion, he appeals directly to Yahweh—the covenant Lord—entreating Him to remember His historical faithfulness, revive His life-giving work in the present era of judgment, and temper His righteous wrath with covenant mercy.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The name Yahweh is the personal proper name of the God of Israel, often referred to as the Tetragrammaton because it consists of four Hebrew consonants (Y-H-W-H). Out of deep reverence and fear of violating the Third Commandment, ancient Jewish readers avoided pronouncing the name aloud, substituting it with the title Adonai (Lord) during public scripture readings. In modern English translations, this substitution is preserved by rendering Yahweh in capital letters as "LORD."

Grammatically, the name is intimately linked to the Hebrew verb hayah, meaning "to be" or "to become." When God declared His name to Moses in Exodus 3:14, He used the phrase Ehyeh Asher Ehyeh ("I AM WHO I AM"), which contracts to Yahweh in the third person ("He is" or "He brings into being"). The word picture behind the name is one of absolute reality, self-contained existence, and active presence. While the gods of the ancient Near East were bound by geography, limited in power, and dependent on human maintenance, Yahweh is the self-sustained Creator who is actively present with His creation, sovereignly ruling over history, and initiating personal relationships with His people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Yahweh is the self-existent, eternal, and unchangeable God who acts dynamically in history to save and to judge. The lexical core of the name contains several critical dimensions:

  • Self-Existence (Aseity): God possesses life in Himself and is dependent on no external source for His existence, power, or continuation.

  • Eternity and Immutability: He is the same yesterday, today, and forever. His character, decrees, and covenant commitments do not fluctuate.

  • Relational Presence: The name is not a cold philosophical concept of bare existence; it describes a God who is actively "being-present" with and for His people. It is the name of God in covenant relationship.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

As the most frequent name for God in the Old Testament, occurring over 6,800 times, Yahweh is consistently associated with redemption, covenant security, and absolute sovereignty. The Scriptures use various relational word pictures to flesh out what the name Yahweh means to His people:

  • The Shepherd: Yahweh is the one who guides, feeds, and protects His flock (Psalm 23:1).

  • The Rock: He is the unshakeable foundation, a refuge of strength in times of trouble (Deuteronomy 32:4).

  • The Husband: This picture portrays Yahweh as tenderly committed to His people, choosing them in love and demanding exclusive faithfulness (Isaiah 54:5).

  • The Warrior: He fights on behalf of His helpless people, delivering them from overwhelming adversaries (Exodus 15:3).

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of Yahweh undergirds the entire biblical narrative of redemption.

  • The Foundation of Faithfulness: Because He is Yahweh, He cannot deny Himself or break His word. Human faith is not a leap into the dark but a resting upon the unchangeable character of the Covenant-Keeper.

  • The Standard of Holiness: His covenant name is holy, demanding that those who bear His name must reflect His moral purity and justice.

  • The Assurance of Grace: As Yahweh, He remains faithful to His covenant promises even when His people are unfaithful. His discipline is designed to restore, not to utterly destroy, because His covenant love (chesed) is everlasting.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:2, the prophet is in transition from a state of questioning and perplexity to a state of worship and trust. God has just revealed that He will use the ruthless Babylonians to execute judgment upon Judah. Habakkuk does not cry out to a generic deity (Elohim), but specifically addresses Yahweh twice in this single verse: "O LORD, I have heard Your speech and was afraid; O LORD, revive Your work..."

By invoking the name Yahweh, Habakkuk grounds his prayer in God's historical covenant faithfulness. The prophet is essentially saying, "You are the same God who delivered us from Egypt, who parted the Red Sea, and who bound Himself to us at Sinai. Though we deserve judgment, You are still our covenant Lord."

The petition to "revive Your work" is a plea for Yahweh to repeat His historical acts of deliverance in the dark days of the Babylonian exile. Furthermore, the cry "in wrath remember mercy" is uniquely compatible with the name Yahweh. A generic God of justice might simply consume a wicked nation, but Yahweh, the God of covenant mercy, is bound by His own character to preserve a remnant and fulfill His redemptive promises.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4: "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens,"

  • Exodus 3:15: "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

  • Psalm 23:1: "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

  • Isaiah 42:8: "I am the LORD, that is My name; And My glory I will not give to another, Nor My praise to carved images."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the New Testament, the Greek word Kurios (Lord) is used by the Septuagint translators to render the Hebrew name Yahweh. When the New Testament writers declare that "Jesus Christ is Lord" (Philippians 2:11) or quote Old Testament passages about Yahweh and apply them directly to Jesus (such as Romans 10:13 quoting Joel 2:32), they are making a profound theological claim: Jesus is Yahweh in human flesh. The self-existent, covenant-keeping God of Habakkuk 3:2 is the very God who walked among us, took our judgment upon Himself, and secured our ultimate redemption.
