MIDST - ( qereb , Strong's H7130 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun qereb designates the inner part, the center, or the midst of a person, place, or timeframe. In Habakkuk 3:2, the prophet pleads for God to revive His work "in the midst of the years." Here, qereb represents the dark, painful, and uncertain middle of a period of divine judgment and waiting. Rather than asking to escape the trial, Habakkuk petitions God to manifest His life-giving power and mercy directly inside the deepest, most difficult part of their historical struggle. This word underscores that God is intimately present and active within the core of human suffering.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near Eastern mindset, qereb carried a deeply visceral meaning. It physically referred to the internal organs, the "innards," or the "guts" of a human or animal. It was the seat of life, vitality, and deep-seated emotions. When used to describe a location or an event, qereb did not merely point to a clinical or geometric midpoint. Instead, it described the organic center, the very heart, or the "belly" of a situation. To be in the qereb of something was to be at its rawest, most essential, and most concentrated point.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun qereb denotes the interior or inward part of something. When used with a preposition (such as beqereb), it is translated as "in the midst of," "among," "within," or "in the inner part."

  • It refers physically to the internal organs of sacrificial animals or humans.

  • It refers metaphorically to the inner being, the heart, the mind, or the seat of thoughts and emotions.

  • It refers spatially and relationally to the middle of a community, a city, or a geographic area.

  • It refers temporally to the middle of a specific period of time or historical epoch.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament employs qereb in several distinct ways to convey proximity, intimacy, and centrality:

  • The Sacrificial Inward Parts: In the Levitical sacrificial system, the qereb (the entrails or inner parts) of the animal had to be washed with water before being offered as a sweet aroma to God. This symbolized the need for inward purity before a holy God.

  • The Seat of Human Emotion and Thought: The inner life of humanity is described using qereb. It is the place where thoughts are formulated, pain is felt, and the spirit dwells. When Sarah laughed "within herself," the text uses qereb to show the internal nature of her reaction.

  • The Dwelling Place of God: The most glorious use of the word relates to God's presence. God desires to dwell "in the midst" of His people. His presence is not distant or superficial; He occupies the very center of the camp, the city, and the assembly.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, qereb reveals that God operates from the inside out. He is not a distant deity who merely observes from the periphery.

  • Inward Transformation: Under the Old Covenant, God's law was external, but He promised a day when He would put His law in their "inward parts" (qereb), transforming them from the inside.

  • Sovereignty in the Middle: The use of qereb in historical and temporal contexts demonstrates that God is Lord of the process, not just the beginning and the end. He acts in the messy, unresolved middle of human history. Faith is forged in the "midst" where God's promises have not yet met their final realization, but His presence is desperately needed.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:2, the prophet prays: "O LORD, revive Your work in the midst of the years! In the midst of the years make it known; in wrath remember mercy."

  • Judah was standing on the precipice of devastating Babylonian invasion and exile. The "years" represent the long, grueling period of discipline that lay ahead.

  • The "midst of the years" (qereb shanim) is the painful duration of the exile—the time between the announcement of judgment and the final deliverance. It is the twilight zone of waiting where faith is tested to its limits.

  • Habakkuk does not pray for the avoidance of the trial. Instead, he asks God to "revive His work" and "make it known" directly inside that dark period of waiting. The prophet asks God to step into the very "gut" of their historical suffering, bringing spiritual revival and a fresh revelation of His character when they need it most.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 33:3 - "Go up to a land flowing with milk and honey; for I will not go up in your midst, lest I consume you on the way, for you are a stiff-necked people."

  • Psalm 46:5 - "God is in the midst of her, she shall not be moved; God shall help her, just at the break of dawn."

  • Psalm 51:10 - "Create in me a clean heart, O God, And renew a steadfast spirit within me."

  • Zephaniah 3:17 - "The LORD your God in your midst, The Mighty One, will save; He will rejoice over you with gladness, He will quiet you with His love, He will rejoice over you with singing."

7. Other Relevant Insights

It is helpful to contrast qereb with tawek, another common Hebrew word translated as "midst" or "middle." While tawek is typically used for spatial, physical, or geometric midpoints (such as a tree in the middle of a garden, or standing in the middle of a room), qereb possesses an organic, internal, and highly relational quality. When Habakkuk asks God to work in the qereb of the years, he is not pointing to a chronological midpoint on a calendar. He is making an appeal to God's intimate, life-giving involvement within the living experience of His people during their time of affliction.
