YEARS - ( shanah , Strong's H8141 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word shanah refers to a year, a designated period of time, or an entire lifetime. In Habakkuk 3:2, the plural form "years" points to a specific, challenging epoch of history during which God's people find themselves suspended between His past mighty deeds and His future promised deliverance. It conveys the concept of chronological, historical time as the arena of divine discipline, testing, and ultimate redemption, prompting a desperate plea for God to revive His work and reveal His mercy in the dark middle of human history.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The term shanah is derived from a root verb meaning "to repeat" or "to do again."

  • This linguistic background paints a word picture of a year as a cyclical, repeating unit of time characterized by the predictable return of seasons, harvests, and astronomical alignments.

  • Time is not viewed as a chaotic, linear drift, but as a structured, rhythmic creation of God.

  • This repeating cycle suggests both the reliability of God’s covenant with creation and the ongoing opportunity for renewal and reflection as each cycle restarts.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The primary definition of shanah is a year, representing the complete revolution of the seasons.

  • In the singular, it denotes a single calendar year or a specific period of time designated for a particular purpose, such as a year of release or favor.

  • In the plural form (shanim), it refers to a succession of years, a person’s age or lifespan, or a long, historical period.

  • The word indicates chronological time where events unfold in succession under the sovereign watch of God.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • shanah is used to mark the aging process and the brief, limited nature of human life. The repeating cycle of years measures the mortality of humans against the immortality of God.

  • It is used in legal and covenantal frameworks, such as the "fiftieth year" (the Year of Jubilee) to signal complete liberty, debt cancellation, and restoration.

  • The word picture of "the midst of the years" in Habakkuk 3:2 functions as a metaphor for the stressful, dark, and uncertain middle chapter of a long historical narrative, where God's past miracles seem distant and His future promises have not yet arrived.

4. Theological Implications

  • God is the Lord of the shanim (years). He established the sun and moon to govern them, and He remains sovereign over the rise and fall of nations throughout successive generations.

  • The years of human history are not random; they are purposeful intervals wherein God executes His decrees, judgments, and covenants.

  • The passage of years serves as a crucible for faith. When God delays His final deliverance, the years become a period of spiritual refinement where the righteous must learn to live by faith, trusting in the unchanging character of God despite the weariness of time.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Habakkuk 3:2, the prophet prays, "O LORD, revive Your work in the midst of the years! In the midst of the years make it known; In wrath remember mercy."

  • The "midst of the years" refers specifically to the dark period of Judah's impending exile under the ruthless Babylonians. It is the agonizing interim between God’s historic redemptive acts (like the Exodus) and the final fulfillment of His promises to crush the enemy.

  • Habakkuk does not ask God to remove His people from time or completely bypass the years of suffering. Instead, he petitions God to manifest His power, life-giving presence, and mercy within the very boundaries of this painful historical period. The repetition of the phrase highlights the urgency and desperation of the prophet's plea.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 1:14 - "Then God said, 'Let there be lights in the firmament of the heavens to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs and seasons, and for days and years;'"

  • Leviticus 25:10 - "And you shall consecrate the fiftieth year, and proclaim liberty throughout all the land to all its inhabitants."

  • Psalm 90:4 - "For a thousand years in Your sight Are like yesterday when it is past, And like a watch in the night."

  • Psalm 90:10 - "The days of our years are seventy years; And if by reason of strength they are eighty years, Yet their boast is only labor and sorrow; For it is soon cut off, and we fly away."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The Greek translation of the Old Testament (LXX) translates shanim in Habakkuk 3:2 using the plural form of etos (years), reinforcing the literal historical framework of the prophet's prayer.

  • The petition to "revive" God's work in the midst of the years reveals that without divine intervention, historical periods of judgment and waiting naturally lead to spiritual decay, death, and despair. God must breathe life into history to keep His covenant people alive.
