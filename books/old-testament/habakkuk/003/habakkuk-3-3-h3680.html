COVERED - ( kasah , Strong's H3680 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb kasah means to cover, clothe, conceal, or overwhelm. In its physical sense, it refers to spreading a canopy, clothing a person, or enveloping an object so that it is completely hidden from view. Theologically, it describes how God’s magnificent glory blankets the heavens, rendering His majestic presence inescapable. It is also the primary root used for the covering of sins, signaling atonement where offenses are hidden from God's holy wrath. In Habakkuk 3:3, it paints a breathtaking picture of the divine warrior whose splendor completely envelopes the celestial canopy, prompting universal praise.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the concrete, agrarian world of ancient Israel, kasah conveyed the action of placing a physical barrier or garment over something to render it invisible, protected, or contained. It describes a farmer covering a pit to prevent accidents, a person wrapping themselves in a warm mantle, or a thick cloud bank completely obscuring the peak of Mount Sinai. The word picture is one of total envelopment. When something is covered (kasah), its previous state or form is entirely obscured by the covering agent. This visual background translates powerfully into theological realities: when God covers sin, it is hidden from His sight; when His glory covers the heavens, nothing else can be seen but His majesty.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb kasah occurs over 150 times in the Hebrew Bible, predominantly in the intensive Piel stem. Its core lexical definitions include:

  • To cover or clothe: To put garments on a body or to spread a physical covering over an object.

  • To conceal or hide: To keep a matter secret, or to prevent something from being seen or known.

  • To overwhelm or engulf: To flood or saturate, as water covers the dry land or as shame and violence overwhelm a person.

  • To canopy or blanket: To spread across a vast expanse, obscuring what lies beneath, such as clouds covering the sky or glory filling the heavens.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament employs kasah in several vivid metaphorical and literal word pictures:

  • The Flood Waters: During the Deluge, the waters prevailed until even the highest mountains under heaven were covered. This is a picture of complete engulfment and divine judgment.

  • The Tabernacle Cloud: In the wilderness, the cloud covered the Tabernacle by day, serving as a visible manifestation of the localized, protective presence of Yahweh.

  • Atonement and Forgiveness: The covering of sin represents a vital aspect of redemption. When transgression is covered, it is not merely swept under a rug; rather, it is legally hidden from the Judge's eyes through an acceptable covering, preventing wrath.

  • The Garment of Shame: When individuals or nations face judgment, they are described as being covered with shame, confusion, or violence, indicating that their disgrace is visible to all and impossible to hide.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, kasah speaks directly to the nature of God's self-revelation and His redemptive work.

  • The Incompatibility of Sin and Holiness: Because God is holy, sin cannot exist in His presence. It must either be exposed and punished, or covered (kasah) by grace. The gospel seed is planted in this Old Testament concept: human attempts to cover their own sin fail, but the covering God provides brings ultimate blessedness.

  • The Unveiling of Divine Majesty: When God reveals Himself in cosmic theophany, His glory is so immense that it cannot be contained. It blankets the heavens. This signifies that God's presence is the dominant reality of the universe; nothing can escape His view, and no rival power can diminish His shining splendor.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:3, the prophet is engaged in a passionate prayer of remembrance, recalling God’s historical acts of deliverance during the Exodus and the Sinai covenant. Habakkuk writes, "His glory covered the heavens."

  • The verb kasah is used in the Piel stem, emphasizing the active, intense, and complete nature of this covering.

  • As God marches from Teman and Mount Paran, His sovereign majesty (hod) acts like a massive celestial canopy. The light of His presence is so brilliant that it completely swallows up the sky, rendering the sun and stars mere shadows by comparison.

  • This cosmic blanketing serves a pastoral purpose: Habakkuk was trembling at the threat of the impending Chaldean invasion. By visualizing God's glory covering the heavens, the prophet is reminded that the sovereign Creator is infinitely greater than the terrestrial armies of Babylon. If His glory blankets the sky, His protective care can certainly cover His remnant people.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 7:19 - "And the waters prevailed exceedingly on the earth, and all the high hills under the whole heaven were covered."

  • Psalm 32:1 - "Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven, Whose sin is covered."

  • Psalm 85:2 - "You have forgiven the iniquity of Your people; You have covered all their sin. Selah"

  • Proverbs 10:12 - "Hatred stirs up strife, But love covers all sins."

  • Proverbs 28:13 - "He who covers his sins will not prosper, But whoever confesses and forsakes them will have mercy."

  • Isaiah 6:2 - "Above it stood seraphim; each one had six wings: with two he covered his face, with two he covered his feet, and with two he flew."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A profound spiritual contrast exists between human and divine covering. When mankind attempts to cover (kasah) sin through denial, justification, or religious performance, it results in spiritual death and lack of prosperity. However, when God covers sin through the blood of the covenant, it results in absolute justification and peace.

Furthermore, the cosmic covering of God’s glory in Habakkuk 3:3 acts as a beautiful parallel to the prophetic promise found in Habakkuk 2:14: "For the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the Lord, as the waters cover (kasah) the sea." What currently covers the heavens will one day completely saturate and cover the earth, bringing about the consummation of His kingdom.
