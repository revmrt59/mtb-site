FULL - ( male' , Strong's H4390 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb male' expresses the concept of filling to maximum capacity, overflowing, or reaching a state of absolute completion. In Habakkuk 3:3, it describes the entire earth as being completely saturated, permeated, and overwhelmed with the praise of God. When the Holy One reveals His majestic presence, His glory does not merely touch the earth; it floods it entirely, leaving no space untouched by His supreme worthiness.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete imagery behind male' is that of a vessel filled to its brim, such as a cup overflowing with water or a basket packed with harvest grain. It conveys a state of abundance, density, and completeness where there is no remaining void. In the ancient world, this word was used to describe a river overflowing its banks during flood season, or a sanctuary filled with the heavy, fragrant smoke of incense. The root also extends to the concept of time being fulfilled or a task being fully accomplished. It is a word of dramatic density, painting a picture of a reality so vast and weighty that it drives out everything else to occupy the entire space.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb male' means to fill, to be full, to overflow, or to fully satisfy. Lexically, it functions in three primary ways:

  • Stative (to be full): Describing a state of being completely occupied, where no empty space remains.

  • Active/Transitive (to fill): The action of pouring into, packing, or saturating a space or vessel.

  • Metaphorical/Spiritual: Describing a person or place dominated by an attribute, such as being filled with wisdom, anger, violence, or the Holy Spirit.

In the passive and intensive stems (Piel), it is also used for the act of consecration, literally "filling the hand" of a priest to equip and authorize them for holy service.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout Scripture, male' serves as a key theological term to describe the manifest presence of God.

  • The Tabernacle and Temple: When God took up residence in the Tabernacle and later the Temple, His glory filled (male') the sacred space, preventing even the priests from entering because of the sheer density of His holiness.

  • Spatial and Cosmic Completeness: The word describes how God's sovereign presence permeates creation, emphasizing that there is no geographical or spiritual pocket of the universe hidden from Him.

  • Ethical and Social States: Conversely, the word is used negatively to describe the earth being filled with violence or blood during times of rampant sin, showing how moral corruption can saturate a society.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, male' reveals that God's glory and praise are not passive or localized realities. When God acts, His presence demands absolute occupancy. In the context of biblical eschatology, the ultimate destiny of creation is to be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the Lord. The term underscores that God does not share His glory with another; when His praise fills the earth, it displaces all false gods, human pride, and worldly kingdoms. The fullness of His praise speaks to the ultimate triumph of His kingdom, where every square inch of the cosmos is brought into perfect, harmonious alignment with His worship.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:3, the prophet is reflecting on a powerful, poetic vision of God's historic deliverance, marching from Teman and Mount Paran. As God moves in majestic power, His glory covers the heavens like a brilliant canopy. Consequently, the earth is "full" (male') of His praise. The contextual nuance here is one of irresistible, cosmic response. The earth does not merely choose to praise God; the physical and spiritual creation is so utterly overwhelmed by the sight of the descending God of Sinai that it is flooded with His praise. It is an objective reality: the sheer weight of who God is saturates the terrestrial realm, leaving no room for fear, doubt, or the boasting of Babylon. For a prophet trembling in the midst of national distress, this fullness of praise offered a rock-solid assurance that God's ultimate reign will consume all earthly chaos.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 40:34 - "Then the cloud covered the tabernacle of meeting, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle."

  • Numbers 14:21 - "but truly, as I live, all the earth shall be filled with the glory of the LORD—"

  • Psalm 104:24 - "O LORD, how manifold are Your works! In wisdom You have made them all. The earth is full of Your possessions."

  • Jeremiah 23:24 - "“Can anyone hide himself in secret places, So I shall not see him?” says the LORD; “Do I not fill heaven and earth?” says the LORD."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Habakkuk 3:3, the verb male' (māl’āh) is in the perfect tense, which can be understood as a prophetic perfect. Even though the prophet is looking at a dark historical reality of looming Babylonian invasion, he speaks of the earth being "full of His praise" as an accomplished, absolute fact. To the eye of faith, God's ultimate victory and the universal worship due to His name are so certain that they can be described as already having filled the earth.
