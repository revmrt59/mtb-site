PRAISE - ( tehillah , Strong's H8416 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun tehillah refers to adoration, thanksgiving, and praise offered to God, often manifested in song, public celebration, or a lifestyle that displays His glory. Derived from the root halal (to shine, boast, or rave), tehillah is not merely an internal feeling but an outward, audible, and visible expression of adoration. In Habakkuk 3:3, as the prophet remembers God's majestic historical redemption, he envisions the entire earth filled with the praise and adoration due to the Creator's manifest glory.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The noun tehillah is derived from the verbal root halal, which carries the primary meaning of "to shine," "to flash forth light," "to boast," or "to rave." To offer tehillah is to make God's greatness shine brightly, to boast in His mighty deeds, and to celebrate His character with enthusiastic, unashamed devotion.

  • In ancient Near Eastern culture, praise was not a private or silent meditation. It was a public event, often accompanied by instrumental music, singing, and dancing. Tehillah represents the audible, visible demonstration of a community or individual bragging about the greatness of Yahweh.

  • The plural form of tehillah (tehillim) serves as the Hebrew title for the Book of Psalms, identifying the entire collection of Israel's prayers and songs as "Praises."

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Tehillah denotes a song of praise, adoration, or thanksgiving directed toward God.

  • It refers to the praise or glory received by someone, particularly the renown, fame, and reputation of God among the nations.

  • It represents the qualities or acts of God that make Him praiseworthy. He is not only the recipient of praise but is Himself the praise of His people (Deuteronomy 10:21).

  • The semantic range includes the act of singing hymns, public proclamation of God’s attributes, and the state of being highly valued or renowned.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Enthroned on Praise: God is poetically described as dwelling or being enthroned upon the tehillot (praises) of Israel, depicting corporate worship as the very throne room where God manifests His active presence.

  • The Garment of Praise: Praise is pictured as a protective and beautifying garment (tehillah) that replaces the spirit of heaviness or despair, illustrating how active adoration transforms the emotional and spiritual posture of the believer.

  • Fruit of the Lips: It is portrayed as a sacrifice offered continuously in the sanctuary, a physical offering of vocalized honor that rises to God.

4. Theological Implications

  • Praise is the ultimate end of all redemptive history. God saves, delivers, and restores His people so that they—and the entire creation—might declare His tehillah.

  • True praise requires an objective revelation of who God is. It is never empty emotionalism but a rational, heart-felt response to God's holiness, justice, mercy, and power.

  • The offering of tehillah has evangelistic power. When God’s covenant people sing His praises, the surrounding nations hear of His mighty acts, leading to the spread of His renown throughout the earth.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Habakkuk 3:3, the prophet is engaged in a dramatic prayer of remembrance and petition. Facing the terrifying prospect of the imminent Babylonian invasion, Habakkuk shifts his gaze from the impending judgment to the historical reality of God’s past deliverance during the Exodus and the giving of the Law at Sinai.

  • The parallel structure of the verse pairs "His glory covered the heavens" with "the earth was full of His praise." As the divine glory (hod) fills the heavenly realm with blinding, majestic light, the corresponding reaction on earth is tehillah—the entire created order and His people are filled with awe-inspired adoration and His renown.

  • The usage of tehillah in this crisis context teaches that praise is not dependent on comfortable circumstances. Habakkuk recognizes that even when the earth is shaken by judgment, Yahweh's sovereign majesty remains supreme, and His global reputation for holiness and power is absolute. Praise becomes the weapon that drives out fear.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Psalm 22:3: "But You are holy, Enthroned in the praises of Israel."

  • Psalm 34:1: "I will bless the LORD at all times; His praise shall continually be in my mouth."

  • Psalm 100:4: "Enter into His gates with thanksgiving, And into His courts with praise. Be thankful to Him, and bless His name."

  • Isaiah 61:3: "To console those who mourn in Zion, To give them beauty for ashes, The oil of joy for mourning, The garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness; That they may be called trees of righteousness, The planting of the LORD, that He may be glorified."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Tehillah differs from yadah (another primary Hebrew word for praise). While yadah emphasizes the physical gesture of throwing out the hands in thanksgiving or confessing God’s deeds, tehillah highlights the vocal, musical, and celebratory singing of God’s specific virtues.

  • Because tehillah is tied to God's acts in history, it is inherently narrative. It does not just say "God is good," but sings of how He is good, retelling the stories of His faithfulness from generation to generation.
