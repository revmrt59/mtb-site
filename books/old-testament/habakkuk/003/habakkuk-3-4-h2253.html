HIDING - ( chebyon , Strong's H2253 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun chebyon refers to a hiding, a concealment, a covering, or a veil. Occurring only once in the Old Testament in Habakkuk 3:4, this word describes the striking paradox of divine revelation: even in God's most brilliant self-manifestations, His ultimate essence and full power remain veiled and concealed. The flashing rays of His glory do not fully expose His infinite being; rather, they serve as a covering that protects finite creation from the consuming intensity of His absolute majesty and power.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun chebyon is derived from the primitive verbal root chabah (Strong's H2244), which means to hide, withdraw, or shelter. In the ancient Near Eastern context, majestic power was often associated with blinding light and storm-clouds. However, biblical theology uniquely balances God's self-revelation (manifested in brilliant light) with His incomprehensible transcendence (manifested in mystery and concealment). The word picture of chebyon evokes a veil or an envelope—a deliberate covering that restrains the full force of something immensely powerful. It suggests that what God reveals of Himself, though brilliant like the sun, is merely the outer fringe or "hiding place" of His limitless power, keeping the fullness of His infinite majesty shielded from human sight.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun chebyon means a hiding place, a veil, a covering, or a concealment. It designates the act or state of wrapping something up or keeping it out of public view. While light typically functions to expose and make visible, chebyon is used paradoxically in Habakkuk 3:4 to indicate that the radiant light surrounding Yahweh acts as a screen or veil. The sheer intensity of His visible glory serves as a garment that conceals the true, unapproachable depth of His omnipotence.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Veil of Light: In Habakkuk 3:4, the prophet describes God coming in judgment. His brightness is "like the light" (or the sun), and "rays flashing from His hand" are visible. Yet, the text immediately declares, "And there was the hiding [chebyon] of His power." The word picture depicts the radiant, flashing light itself as the very veil that hides His ultimate essence.

  • The Shield for Creation: Because God is a consuming fire, His unveiled presence would destroy sinful humanity and overwhelm the physical universe. The chebyon is the divine mercy that shields creation; the outer glory is a boundary marker.

  • The Restrained Omnipotence: The word picture implies that the spectacular displays of God's power in history—such as the Exodus or the conquest—are only a minute fraction of His total strength. The bulk of His power remains hidden, held back in reserve.

4. Theological Implications

  • Divine Transcendence and Immanence: Chebyon encapsulates the tension between God's immanence (He makes His presence known in spectacular, visible ways) and His transcendence (He remains fundamentally incomprehensible and hidden). God is never fully exposed to human analysis; He dwells in unapproachable light.

  • Revelation as Accommodation: God must accommodate His revelation to human limitations. If He did not "hide" His power behind the veil of His created manifestations, humanity could not survive the encounter.

  • The Hiddenness of God: Even when God is actively working in history, His deepest purposes and the fullness of His power are often concealed from human eyes. Faith requires trusting in the God who hides Himself even as He reveals Himself.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3, the prophet is praying in a state of fear and awe, recalling God's past mighty acts of redemption to find hope for the impending Babylonian invasion. In verse 4, Yahweh is pictured as a warrior marching in radiant majesty. The "rays flashing from His hand" depict lightning or bright beams of light. But the climax of this description is a stunning reversal: this blinding display of power is actually a "hiding" of His power. In the context of Habakkuk's distress, this nuance is highly pastoral. It assures the prophet that the small portion of God's power currently visible in their bleak circumstances does not represent the limit of His capability. The vast storehouse of His omnipotence remains hidden, ready to be unleashed in His perfect timing for the salvation of His people.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

The noun chebyon is a hapax legomenon, occurring only once in the Hebrew Bible (Habakkuk 3:4). To understand its semantic range, we look to its direct verbal root chabah (Strong's H2244), meaning "to hide" or "to conceal," which appears in the following key passages:

  • Genesis 31:27: "Why did you flee away secretly and steal away from me, and not tell me, that I might have sent you away with joy and songs, with timbrel and harp?"

  • Joshua 10:16: "But these five kings had fled and hidden themselves in a cave at Makkedah."

  • Isaiah 26:20: "Come, my people, enter your chambers, and shut your doors behind you; hide yourself, as it were, for a little moment, until the indignation is past."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The Septuagint (LXX) translation of Habakkuk 3:4 differs significantly from the Masoretic Text, reflecting a difficult textual tradition. The Greek translation reads, kai etheto agapēsin krataian ischyos autou ("and he established a mighty love of his strength"), likely confusing the root chabah (to hide) with chabab (to love). However, the Hebrew Masoretic Text preserves the profound theological paradox of the "hiding of His power."

  • The concept of light acting as a garment or a hiding place is echoed elsewhere in the Psalms. Psalm 104:2 describes God as Him "who cover Himself with light as with a garment." Here, light is not just a medium of revelation, but the actual fabric of His concealment.
