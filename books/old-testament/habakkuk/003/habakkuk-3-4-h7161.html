RAYS - ( qeren , Strong's H7161 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word qeren fundamentally refers to a horn, representing power, strength, and glory. In the context of Habakkuk 3:4, it is used in its dual form (qarnayim) to depict dual beams or rays of flashing light emanating from God's hand. This vivid imagery conveys the majestic, blinding splendor of the divine presence during His triumphant march. Rather than a mere decorative feature, these rays signify the active, radiating omnipotence of God, serving as both a revelation of His glory and a veil that shrouds His infinite power from mortal eyes.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The primary meaning of qeren is the horn of an animal, such as a ram, goat, or wild ox. In the ancient world, horns were an animal's primary weapon, defense, and crowning glory, symbolizing offensive power, strength, and dignity. From this physical object, the word expanded metaphorically to describe the horns of an altar, mountain peaks, and projecting rays of light, which visually resemble the pointed, tapered shape of horns. The connection between a horn and a ray of light is deeply rooted in this visual metaphor of a sharp, piercing projection cutting through the darkness.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun qeren carries several distinct meanings depending on its context:

  • An animal horn used as a weapon of defense and attack.

  • A horn flask used as a vessel for oil, perfume, or as a wind instrument (the shofar).

  • A corner or projecting point, specifically the "horns of the altar" at the four corners of the sacrificial altar.

  • A mountain peak or hill.

  • Beams or rays of light projecting from a luminous source.

In the dual form qarnayim used in Habakkuk 3:4, the word refers specifically to dual rays or flashing beams of light shooting forth from God's hands.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Horn of Strength: In ancient Near Eastern culture, to lift up or "exalt the horn" of a person meant to bestow victory, dignity, and high status. Conversely, to cut off the horn meant to strip someone of their power and reduce them to defeat.

  • The Horn of Salvation: This usage portrays a mighty deliverer. Just as a wild animal uses its horn to conquer its enemies, God acts as the mighty "horn of salvation" for His people, crushing their spiritual and physical oppressors.

  • Flashing Rays: The visual imagery of a ray of light as a horn is dynamic. It depicts light not as a soft, passive glow, but as a sharp, piercing instrument. Just as a horn projects outward from the head of a strong beast, these rays of light project aggressively from the source, cutting through darkness with sovereign power.

4. Theological Implications

  • Divine Splendor and Holiness: God is inherently light, and His self-manifestation is marked by blinding brilliance. The rays in Habakkuk 3:4 represent His unapproachable majesty.

  • Power Masked in Revelation: A profound theological paradox exists in the phrase "there was the hiding of His power." The very rays that reveal God's presence also serve to conceal His full essence. God's blinding light acts as a veil; His infinite power is so overwhelming that humanity cannot look upon Him directly and live. Revelation is simultaneously a merciful concealment.

  • Sovereign Judgment and Deliverance: In Habakkuk's prayer, this luminous manifestation accompanies God's march to judge the wicked nations and rescue His covenant people. His radiant glory is not static; it is an active, kinetic force deployed for the salvation of His church.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:4, the prophet describes a majestic theophany—a visible manifestation of God. Recalling the historical wonders of the Exodus, Mount Sinai, and the wilderness journey, Habakkuk sees God approaching with blinding, sun-like brightness. The phrase "He had rays flashing from His hand" describes brilliant, pencil-like shafts of lightning or intense light bursting from God’s hands. Because the hand represents active agency and execution, these flashing rays signify that God's hands are charged with omnipotent power, ready to strike down His enemies and deliver His people.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The close linguistic relationship between the noun qeren ("horn/ray") and the verb qaran ("to shine/send out rays") explains a famous historical translation misunderstanding. In Exodus 34:29-30, when Moses descended from Mount Sinai, the skin of his face "shone" (qaran). The Latin Vulgate translated this verb as cornuta esset, meaning "was horned." This led medieval and Renaissance artists, most notably Michelangelo, to depict Moses with physical horns on his head. The correct understanding is that Moses' face was radiating beams or rays of light, reflecting the heavy weight of Yahweh’s glory.
