PESTILENCE - ( deber , Strong's H1698 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word deber refers to a devastating, contagious epidemic or plague sent as an instrument of divine judgment. In Habakkuk 3:5, it is personified as a royal vanguard marching ahead of Yahweh as He goes forth in majestic, holy wrath to deliver His people and execute justice upon the earth. This term reminds us that nature and disease are not chaotic, random forces, but are entirely subject to the sovereign command of God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The word deber is associated with widespread, lethal disease that sweeps through human or animal populations. It is not an isolated sickness but an unstoppable scourge.

  • In the ancient Near East, pestilence was often feared as a capricious demonic force. The Old Testament, however, demythologizes pestilence, placing it firmly under the absolute sovereignty of Yahweh. It is His servant, His arrow, or His vanguard.

  • In the context of Habakkuk 3, the prophet is reciting a "theophany"—a poetic description of God appearing in glory. Here, deber acts as a herald or bodyguard, marching ahead of the divine Warrior, clearing the way and demonstrating His absolute power over life and death.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • deber denotes a plague, pestilence, or epidemic disease that results in widespread death.

  • It refers specifically to infectious, fatal diseases striking humans or livestock as a direct expression of judgment or sovereign power.

  • It does not carry the connotation of natural decay, but rather of a sudden, severe stroke or scourge.

  • The root meaning is tied to destruction or ruin, emphasizing the devastating impact of the affliction.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • deber is frequently grouped in a solemn triad of judgments alongside the sword (hereb) and famine (ra'ab), particularly in the books of Jeremiah and Ezekiel. This triad represents the complete collapse of a society under divine judgment.

  • It is often pictured as a physical force that "walks" or "strikes" (Psalm 91:6).

  • In Habakkuk 3:5, it is personified. Along with "fever" (or pestilence/burning heat, reseph), deber serves as an attendant marching with the Almighty. This dramatic word picture casts God as a mighty conqueror whose arrival is so holy and intense that even the elements and diseases serve as His advance guard.

4. Theological Implications

  • Sovereignty of God: Disease is not outside of God's control. It is an instrument in His hand. This provides deep comfort to the believer; even the most terrifying epidemics are subject to the boundary lines drawn by the Creator.

  • Holy Judgment: deber is a covenant sanction (Leviticus 26; Deuteronomy 28). It represents the physical consequence of spiritual rebellion.

  • Redemption and Deliverance: While deber destroys God's enemies, it serves to deliver His people. God's terrible majesty is deployed to rescue Israel from their oppressors.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • Habakkuk is trembling in fear of the coming Babylonian invasion, yet he prays for God to revive His work (Habakkuk 3:2). In response, he recalls God's past acts of deliverance (the Exodus and Conquest) using epic, poetic imagery.

  • In Habakkuk 3:5, deber is God's advance agent. Before Him goes the pestilence. This tells Habakkuk that the same God who used plagues to dismantle the power of Egypt is still on the throne. The Babylonians may be terrifying, but they are nothing compared to the God who commands pestilence itself. The immediate focus is to inspire awe, fear, and ultimate trust in a trembling prophet.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 9:3 - "behold, the hand of the Lord will be on your cattle in the field, on the horses, on the donkeys, on the camels, on the oxen, and on the sheep—a very severe pestilence."

  • Leviticus 26:25 - "And I will bring a sword against you that will execute the vengeance of the covenant; when you are gathered together within your cities, I will send pestilence among you; and you shall be delivered into the hand of the enemy."

  • 2 Samuel 24:13 - "Shall seven years of famine come to you in your land? Or will you flee three months before your enemies, while they pursue you? Or shall there be three days of pestilence in your land?"

  • Psalm 91:6 - "Nor of the pestilence that walks in darkness, Nor of the destruction that lays waste at noonday."

  • Jeremiah 14:12 - "When they fast, I will not hear their cry; and when they offer burnt offering and grain offering, I will not accept them. But I will consume them by the sword, by the famine, and by the pestilence."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The Septuagint (LXX) often translates deber with thanatos (death) or loimos (pestilence/plague).

  • In Habakkuk 3:5, the Septuagint uses logos (word) due to a variant reading of the unpointed Hebrew consonants (d-b-r), which can spell either dabar (word) or deber (pestilence).

  • However, the poetic parallelism of the passage—matching the first line with "fever" or "burning heat" (reseph) in the second line—strongly confirms that "pestilence" is the correct contextual reading. This highlights the value of Hebrew parallelisms in interpreting poetic biblical texts.
