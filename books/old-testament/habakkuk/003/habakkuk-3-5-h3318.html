FOLLOWED - ( yatsa , Strong's H3318 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yatsa is a primary root meaning "to go out," "to come forth," or "to proceed." In Habakkuk 3:5, it describes the outward march of divine judgment, where "fever" (or burning plague) goes forth at the feet of Yahweh as He advances in holy majesty. This word highlights the absolute authority of God over creation and calamity; plagues do not act independently but march out as His obedient servants, executing His sovereign decrees.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The verb yatsa is one of the most frequent verbs in the Hebrew Bible, conveying the basic physical action of crossing a boundary from inside to outside—such as exiting a tent, leaving a city gate, or the sun rising in the sky. In the ancient Near East, the term carried strong military and royal connotations, serving as the standard expression for a king or an army "going forth" to battle.

In the poetic prayer of Habakkuk 3, the prophet uses this imagery to depict a theophany—a visible manifestation of God. Yahweh is described as a warrior marching from the southern desert to rescue His covenant people and execute judgment on His enemies. The background of yatsa in this verse evokes a royal military procession, where the King's personal retinue, armed forces, and servants march out in His train.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • yatsa means to go out, come out, go forth, proceed, or rise. In the Qal stem, which is used in Habakkuk 3:5, the verb denotes the literal or figurative movement of emerging from a source, departing from a location, or setting out on a campaign. When applied to abstract forces, commands, or diseases, the verb signifies being dispatched, issued, or sent abroad to accomplish a specific task. In this passage, it describes the outward departure or marching forth of "fever" or "burning plague" from the presence of God, following closely in His footsteps.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Rising Sun: The word describes the sun "going forth" to illuminate the earth, emerging from its nightly chamber to run its course.

  • The Military Campaign: It is the technical term for soldiers and commanders "going out" to wage war against an adversary.

  • The Divine Decree: It is used for the law and the word of the Lord "going forth" from Zion to instruct the nations.

  • The Exodus: It is the foundational verb used for Israel "coming out" of the physical boundaries of Egypt.

  • In Habakkuk 3:5, the word picture is highly dramatic and personified. Pestilence marches before Yahweh as a vanguard, while burning fever "goes forth" (NKJV: "followed") at His feet as a rearguard. Judgment and plague are represented as royal attendants or cosmic soldiers marching in the train of the Almighty Warrior.

4. Theological Implications

  • Sovereignty over Creation and Calamity: Pestilence and fever are not chaotic, independent forces of nature acting at random. They are entirely subject to the command of God. They only "go forth" when He marches, acting as His obedient agents.

  • The Demands of Divine Holiness: When God manifests His presence, the fallen world cannot stand. His holiness naturally expels and consumes wickedness, represented by the plague and fever that accompany Him.

  • Covenant Faithfulness: The imagery recalls the plagues of Egypt and the wilderness journey where God demonstrated His control over disease to judge His enemies and discipline His people, proving that His judgments are purposeful and covenantal.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:5, the prophet is recalling the historic, saving acts of God to find hope in a dark day of impending Babylonian invasion. The phrase "fever followed at His feet" (literally, "burning heat went forth at His feet") emphasizes that wherever God steps, His holiness and judgment leave an immediate, powerful impact. The "fever" does not merely follow at a distance; it is bound to His footsteps. The choice of yatsa emphasizes that these judgments are actively dispatched from His immediate presence to accomplish His sovereign will.

  • Isaiah 2:3: "Many people shall come and say, 'Come, and let us go up to the mountain of the LORD, To the house of the God of Jacob; He will teach us His ways, And we shall walk in His paths.' For out of Zion shall go forth the law, And the word of the LORD from Jerusalem."

  • Zechariah 14:3: "Then the LORD will go forth And fight against those nations, As He fights in the day of battle."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Symmetric Poetic Structure: The verse displays a classic Hebrew poetic parallelism: "Before Him went [halak] pestilence" is paired with "And fever followed [yatsa] at His feet." The two verbs of movement, halak (to walk/go) and yatsa (to go out/go forth), work in tandem to depict the forward progress of God's holy march.

  • Ancient Greek Translation: In the Septuagint (LXX), yatsa in Habakkuk 3:5 is translated using exeleusetai (from exerchomai), which means "will go out/forth." This confirms the ancient understanding of the text as depicting a literal outward movement of divine judgment following in the wake of God's steps.
