MOUNTAINS - ( harar , Strong's H2042 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word harar refers to a mountain, hill, or mountainous ridge, symbolizing stability, antiquity, and permanence in the ancient world. In biblical poetry, mountains represent the most enduring and unshakeable elements of physical creation. When God appears in His sovereign majesty, even these "everlasting mountains" shatter and scatter, demonstrating that the Creator's power infinitely transcends the most permanent structures of the earth.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near East, mountains were regarded as the pillars of heaven, the foundations of the earth, and the dwelling places of the gods. They represented the ultimate symbols of strength, durability, and unchanging permanence. To the ancient mind, a mountain was the most secure and immovable object in existence. The word harar evokes this imagery of towering majesty, ancient resilience, and absolute stability. These geological giants stood as silent witnesses to generations of human history, outlasting kingdoms, empires, and human lives.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun harar signifies a mountain, hill, or mountainous region. It is a poetic variant of the more common Hebrew word har. It refers to literal geological heights but is frequently used in poetic and prophetic literature to depict majestic prominence, strength, and age-old endurance. Lexically, the term emphasizes the towering, looming nature of these physical structures, which stand high above the plains and valleys, representing the apex of physical strength on earth.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

In Scripture, harar and its variants are used to paint vivid theological word pictures:

  • Symbols of Security: Mountains represent strongholds, fortresses, and places of refuge where men flee for safety from enemies.

  • Symbols of Eternity: Because they survive generations, they are poetically called "ancient" or "everlasting."

  • Symbols of Pride: High places often represent human and national arrogance that exalts itself against the sovereignty of God.

  • The Dwelling of God: Mount Zion, though physically modest, is elevated theologically as the mountain of the Lord, outlasting all pagan heights.

4. Theological Implications

The theological core of harar lies in the contrast between the creature and the Creator. Physical creation possesses a derived permanence—mountains are "everlasting" relative to human life—but God possesses underived, absolute permanence. When God manifests His presence (theophany), the physical creation dissolves. The shattering of the mountains reveals that nothing in the created order is truly absolute; only God's character, covenant, and decrees are genuinely immutable. The collapse of these ancient structures shows that the cosmos is completely subject to the voice and will of its Creator.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:6, the prophet is recounting a majestic theophany, recalling God's historic deliverance of Israel at the Exodus and Sinai, while projecting this power into his current crisis. The text states: "And the everlasting mountains were scattered, the perpetual hills bowed." Here, harar is paired with `ad ("eternity" or "everlasting") to describe the "mountains of old" or "everlasting mountains." The contextual nuance is one of absolute cosmic upheaval. The prophet uses hyperbole and poetic imagery to show that the things humanity deems most secure—the literal foundations of the earth—are like dust before the steps of Yahweh. If the everlasting mountains scatter and the perpetual hills bow, then no earthly empire (such as the looming Chaldean threat) can stand against His judgment. The collapse of the mountains prepares the way for the salvation of God’s people.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 49:26: "The blessings of your father / Have excelled the blessings of my ancestors, / Up to the utmost bound of the everlasting hills."

  • Deuteronomy 33:15: "With the chief things of the ancient mountains, / With the precious things of the everlasting hills,"

  • Psalm 30:7: "Lord, by Your favor You have made my mountain stand strong; / You hid Your face, and I was troubled."

  • Habakkuk 3:6: "He stood and measured the earth; / He looked and startled the nations. / And the everlasting mountains were scattered, / The perpetual hills bowed. / His ways are everlasting."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A profound literary contrast exists in Habakkuk 3:6 between the "everlasting mountains" (harere-`ad) and "His ways are everlasting" (halikot `olam lo). The physical mountains are called "everlasting," yet they crumble and scatter. God's ways are also described as "everlasting," and they endure forever. The passage structurally mock-elevates creation only to shatter it, leaving God's sovereign path as the only truly eternal reality. For the believer, this provides an enduring lesson: the "mountains" of earthly security—wealth, power, and human institutions—are temporary and fragile, whereas the purposes and paths of God are utterly unshakable.
