STARTLED - ( natar , Strong's H5425 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb natar in Habakkuk 3:6 conveys a sudden, terrifying movement—literally meaning to shake, leap, start up, or cause to tremble. In the context of Habakkuk’s prophetic prayer, it describes the reaction of the nations when God merely looks at them. At His glance, the nations are instantly thrown into panic and convulsed with fear. This word captures the overwhelming, disruptive majesty of the Creator, whose sudden presence shatters all human stability and exposes the helplessness of worldly powers.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The physical action of natar involves a quick, sudden motion—such as a grasshopper jumping, or a person leaping up in sudden alarm or fright. When used of people or nations experiencing the presence of God, it depicts a violent shaking or sudden starting back in sheer terror. The word-picture is that of a wild animal suddenly startled by a brilliant flash of light or a loud noise, instantly jumping in fright. This is the physiological and psychological reaction of the nations before the gaze of Yahweh. It is a sudden, convulsive movement of panic and disarray rather than a slow, gradual shift.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Natar operates in two primary semantic spheres: to leap or start up, and to loose or set free. In the causative stem (Hiphil), which is used in Habakkuk 3:6, it means to cause to tremble, to drive asunder, or to startle. The word describes an action of causing something to leap out of its place due to sudden shock. It refers to the dismantling of stability, where kingdoms that seemed firmly rooted are suddenly shaken loose from their foundations by the presence of the Lord.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Leaping of Locusts: The word describes the rapid, spring-like motion of jumping legs. This highlights the agility and suddenness of the movement.

  • The Leaping Heart: The word is used to describe a heart "leaping" out of its chest in sudden terror or awe. When a person hears the thunderous voice of God, their physical heart convulses in fear.

  • The Shattering of Nations: In the prophetic imagery of judgment, God's gaze acts like a sudden impact that causes the entire political and geographic landscape of pagan nations to jump, scatter, and dissolve in panic.

4. Theological Implications

  • The Divine Gaze: God's look is never passive. In Scripture, when God "looks," He acts. His gaze brings judgment, exposes sin, and upends the natural order. A single look from Yahweh accomplishes what armies cannot.

  • The Fragility of Human Sovereignty: The nations, which seem so secure and powerful to human eyes, are revealed to be utterly fragile. They are startled and scattered by a single glance from Yahweh.

  • Cosmic Sovereignty: God stands supreme above history and creation. Just as He measures the earth with a single stride, He convulses the nations with a glance. There is no human power or institution that can remain stable when He manifests His presence.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3, the prophet prays for God to revive His work in the midst of the years while facing the terrifying prospect of a Babylonian invasion. He recalls God's ancient march during the Exodus and Sinai events to find comfort.

Habakkuk 3:6 contains parallel structures: "He stood and measured the earth; He looked and startled the nations." Just as measuring the earth shows God's authority over the physical land, "startling" the nations shows His absolute control over human history and kingdoms.

The translation "startled" captures the sudden shock, but the Hebrew carries a more violent, shaking quality. The nations do not merely gasp; they are convulsed, scattered, and thrown into chaotic flight. This directly correlates with the next line: "And the everlasting mountains were scattered." If even the ancient, seemingly permanent mountains crumble, the proud nations have no hope of standing against Him.

  • Job 37:1 - "At this also my heart trembles, And leaps out of its place."

  • Psalm 105:20 - "The king sent and released him, The ruler of the people, and let him go free."

  • Psalm 146:7 - "Who executes justice for the oppressed, Who gives food to the hungry. The LORD frees the prisoners."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The Contrast of Stability: In Habakkuk 3:6, the "everlasting mountains" and "perpetual hills" are contrasted with God's "everlasting" ways. The creation and the nations appear permanent, but they shake and scatter. Only God's ways are truly eternal and unshakeable.

  • Pastoral Comfort: For a prophet facing the terrifying prospect of a Babylonian invasion, this truth is deeply comforting. The Babylonians may seem like an unstoppable, terrifying force, but to God, they are easily startled and scattered. Believers today can take comfort that the most intimidating global powers are completely subject to the sovereign glance of God.
