EVERLASTING - ( 'ad , Strong's H5703 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'ad denotes unlimited duration, perpetuity, or eternity. Often translated as "everlasting," "forever," or "always," it refers to time without end, stretching into the distant future or reflecting ancient, enduring history. In Habakkuk 3:6, it is used to describe the "everlasting mountains"—ancient, seemingly permanent structures of the earth—that nevertheless shatter before the presence of the eternal Creator, whose majesty and ways truly transcend time.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root of 'ad is associated with the idea of passing on, advancing, or continuing indefinitely. Unlike abstract philosophical concepts of eternity, the Hebrew mind understood time dynamically. To describe something as 'ad is to picture it as continuously marching forward, crossing every boundary of time without stopping. It represents duration that is unceasing, uninterrupted, and ongoing.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun 'ad means perpetuity, continuous existence, or eternity. It expresses duration of time that is ongoing and unlimited.

  • It refers to the endless future, such as living forever or reigning forever.

  • It describes a long, continuous duration from the ancient past, such as ancient hills or mountains.

  • It is frequently paired with 'olam (another word for eternity) in the phrase 'olam va'ed ("forever and ever") to emphasize ultimate, unending perpetuity.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

In the Old Testament, 'ad is used to describe several realities:

  • The Eternal God: The Creator is the one who inhabits eternity, existing beyond the bounds of temporal limitations.

  • His Attributes and Word: God’s righteousness, testimonies, and praise endure perpetually.

  • The Messianic Throne: The kingdom and rule of the Messiah are established without end.

  • The Ancient Created Order: Physical structures of the earth, like mountains, which have stood since the dawn of history, are described using 'ad to emphasize their relative permanence to human eyes.

4. Theological Implications

The word 'ad contrasts the relative permanence of the created order with the absolute, infinite permanence of God. Created things, like the "everlasting mountains," are called 'ad because they have stood since the ancient past and appear unshakeable to mortal human beings. However, their "everlasting" nature is only relative to human history.

True, absolute perpetuity belongs only to God, His character, His decrees, and His kingdom. When God acts in judgment or deliverance, even the most durable parts of creation crumble. This demonstrates that God alone is supreme and eternal, and no earthly power or structure can withstand His presence.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:6, the prophet is recalling a grand, poetic vision of God's arrival (a theophany) to judge the wicked and rescue His people. The NKJV translates this: "And the everlasting mountains were scattered, The perpetual hills bowed."

The pairing of 'ad (everlasting) with 'olam (perpetual/ancient) in this verse is a classic Hebrew poetic parallel structure. By describing the mountains as 'ad, Habakkuk highlights their ancient, enduring, and seemingly indestructible nature. Mountains are the ultimate symbols of stability and permanence on earth.

Yet, when Yahweh marches forth, these "everlasting" mountains are shattered. The contrast is stark: what is "everlasting" to humans is fragile and temporary to God. The true, absolute "everlasting" character belongs to God's "ways" mentioned in the same verse. Earthly monuments of strength collapse, but God's eternal purposes remain firm.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 15:18 - "The LORD shall reign forever and ever."

  • Psalm 9:5 - "You have rebuked the nations, You have destroyed the wicked; You have blotted out their name forever and ever."

  • Psalm 45:6 - "Your throne, O God, is forever and ever; A scepter of righteousness is the scepter of Your kingdom."

  • Isaiah 9:6 - "For unto us a Child is born, Unto us a Son is given; And the government will be upon His shoulder. And His name will be called Wonderful, Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace."

  • Isaiah 57:15 - "For thus says the High and Lofty One Who inhabits eternity, whose name is Holy: 'I dwell in the high and holy place, With him who has a contrite and humble spirit, To revive the spirit of the humble, And to revive the heart of the contrite ones.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The "Everlasting Father" Connection: In Isaiah 9:6, the Messiah is given the title "Everlasting Father" (abi'ad). This title does not confuse the Son with the First Person of the Trinity, but rather uses 'ad to designate the Messiah as the "Father of Eternity"—the One who is eternally a fatherly protector, provider, and King to His people.

  • Superlative Eternity: The compounding of 'olam (forever) and 'ad (ever) in phrases like 'olam va'ed serves as a Hebrew superlative. Because human language struggles to define infinite time, Hebrew writers stacked these terms together to stretch the mind toward the limitless horizon of God's eternal reality.
