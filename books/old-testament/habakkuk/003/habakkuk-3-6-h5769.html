PERPETUAL - ( olam, Strong's H5769 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word olam carries the foundational concept of long duration, antiquity, futurity, or existence that extends beyond the horizon of human vision. In the context of Habakkuk 3:6, translated as "perpetual," it describes the ancient, seemingly indestructible hills that bow before the presence of the Almighty Creator. While these majestic geological formations represent the pinnacle of earthly stability and permanence to human eyes, they are temporary and fragile when confronted by the eternal God, whose own ways are truly and infinitely olam. This word contrasts the finite endurance of the created order with the absolute, infinite eternity of its Creator.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • Olam is derived from a root verb meaning to hide, conceal, or obscure. The concrete word picture behind olam is that of a point of time or duration that is hidden from the observer's view—the distant past or the indefinite future. It represents a horizon where the human eye can no longer see. Rather than a modern, abstract mathematical concept of timelessness, olam provides a vivid ancient Hebrew picture of time stretching out to the vanishing point. Whether looking backward to time immemorial or forward to the endless future, the boundary is out of sight.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The lexical definition of olam denotes a long duration, antiquity, futurity, or perpetuity. The precise nuance of the word is determined by its context:

  • In reference to the past, it means ancient times, olden days, or time immemorial (e.g., "days of old").

  • In reference to the future, it signifies continuous, unending existence, everlastingness, or perpetuity.

  • When applied to physical structures or institutions, it highlights their enduring nature relative to human lifespans.

  • When applied to God, His attributes, His decrees, or His kingdom, it denotes absolute, infinite eternity.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Scripture frequently pairs olam with physical monuments of durability to emphasize structural permanence.

  • It is associated with the "perpetual hills" and "ancient ruins," depicting things that outlast generations of humanity.

  • It describes the "everlasting covenant" established by God with His people, emphasizing its unbreakable, enduring character.

  • It is used as a descriptive title for God Himself: El Olam (the Everlasting God), presenting Him as the One who exists beyond the horizons of time, remaining faithful through every generation.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of olam lies in the distinction between the created olam and the divine olam. Physical creations (like mountains and hills) are called "perpetual" because they outlast human generations, yet they are still temporal and subject to shaking. Only God’s character, His covenant, and His ways possess true, unshakeable perpetuity. The shaking of the "perpetual hills" demonstrates that the physical universe, no matter how permanent it appears, is subordinate to the voice and presence of the eternal Lord. God stands outside of humanly measured time, yet He steps into history to act on behalf of His people.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:6, the prophet is reflecting on a monumental theophany—a divine appearance of God marching in judgment and salvation. The "perpetual hills" (gibeot olam) represent the most stable, ancient, and unshakeable elements of the natural world. In ancient Near Eastern thought, mountains were viewed as the pillars of the earth, symbolizing ultimate stability. Yet, at the presence of Yahweh, these "perpetual hills bowed." The contrast is sharp: the hills that seemed "perpetual" collapse, while "His ways are everlasting (olam)." The created "perpetual" gives way to the Creator's true "everlasting" nature. This comforted Habakkuk's trembling heart by showing that geopolitical storms (like the Chaldean invasion) are temporary, but God's eternal purposes are immovable.

  • Genesis 21:33 - "Then Abraham planted a tamarisk tree in Beersheba, and there called on the name of the LORD, the Everlasting God."

  • Exodus 31:16 - "Therefore the children of Israel shall keep the Sabbath, to observe the Sabbath throughout their generations as a perpetual covenant."

  • Psalm 90:2 - "Before the mountains were brought forth, Or ever You had formed the earth and the world, Even from everlasting to everlasting, You are God."

  • Jeremiah 31:3 - "The LORD has appeared of old to me, saying: 'Yes, I have loved you with an everlasting love; Therefore with lovingkindness I have drawn you.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a profound wordplay in Habakkuk 3:6. The prophet states that the "perpetual (olam) hills bowed," and immediately follows it with, "His ways are everlasting (olam)." This structural juxtaposition underlines that while physical creation has a relative, finite perpetuity, only God has essential, infinite perpetuity. The parallel structure of "everlasting mountains" (harere-ad) and "perpetual hills" (gibeot olam) uses classic Hebrew poetic synonyms for endless duration, highlighting the shocking nature of their collapse when God marches.
