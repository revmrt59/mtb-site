CURTAINS - ( yeri'ah , Strong's H3407 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word yeri'ah refers to a tent curtain or hanging fabric. While prominently used to describe the sacred drapes of the Tabernacle, in poetic and prophetic literature it serves as a metonymy for nomadic tents and dwellings. In Habakkuk 3:7, the trembling of the "curtains of the land of Midian" represents the profound dread and physical shaking of desert tribes under the majestic and terrifying march of Yahweh in judgment.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun yeri'ah is derived from a root that suggests stretching out or quivering. In the ancient Near East, nomadic life revolved around the tent, which was constructed from large sheets of woven goat hair or coarse fabric draped over wooden poles. These sheets or "curtains" formed the walls and ceiling of the home. To the desert dweller, the yeri'ah was the primary symbol of shelter, security, and home. In a nomadic context, the shaking or collapse of these curtains signified the total disruption, vulnerability, and exposure of the household to external forces.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term designates a curtain, tent-curtain, drapery, or hanging sheet of fabric. In the majority of its Old Testament occurrences, it refers specifically to the ten woven linen curtains and the eleven goat-hair curtains that formed the protective covering and structure of the Tabernacle. In poetic contexts, the term functions as a synonym or parallel term for tent or dwelling, representing the shelter of nomadic peoples or the expansive canopy of the heavens.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Tabernacle Sanctuaries: The word is used extensively in Exodus to describe the precise, God-designed fabric coverings of the Tabernacle. These curtains separated the holy from the profane and shielded the glory of God from human eyes.

  • The Heavens as a Canopy: The term is used metaphorically to describe the physical heavens. God stretches out the sky like a curtain, displaying His cosmic sovereignty and creative power.

  • Nomadic Dwellings: The word represents the temporary, fragile nature of human habitation, especially in judgment scenes where tents are suddenly ruined and their curtains torn down.

4. Theological Implications

The theological significance of yeri'ah shifts between the holy and the humble. In the Tabernacle, the curtains represent God’s desire to dwell among His people, yet they also emphasize His holiness and the necessity of mediation; the beautifully embroidered curtains set a boundary between a holy God and sinful humanity. Conversely, in the prophetic books, the shaking or tearing of yeri'ah demonstrates that no human shelter—no matter how flexible or established—can withstand the presence of the Almighty. When God rises in judgment, the structures of man offer no refuge.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:7, the prophet beholds a powerful theophany—a vision of Yahweh marching from Teman and Mount Paran in majestic fury. As the Lord advances, the neighboring nomadic nations, Cushan and Midian, are seized with terror. The phrase "the curtains of the land of Midian trembled" uses poetic parallelism with "the tents of Cushan in affliction." The trembling of the yeri'ah (tent curtains) indicates that the fierce, wind-swept power of God's arrival physically shakes the very habitations of Israel’s historical adversaries. The curtains, which normally fluttered gently in the desert breeze, now violently shudder under the weight of divine judgment, revealing the utter helplessness of these nations before the Creator.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 26:1 - "Moreover you shall make the tabernacle with ten curtains of fine woven linen, and blue, purple, and scarlet thread; with artistic designs of cherubim you shall weave them."

  • Psalm 104:2 - "Who cover Yourself with light as with a garment, Who stretch out the heavens like a curtain."

  • Isaiah 54:2 - "Enlarge the place of your tent, and let them stretch out the curtains of your dwellings; do not spare; lengthen your cords, and strengthen your stakes."

  • Jeremiah 4:20 - "Destruction upon destruction is cried, for the whole land is plundered. Suddenly my tents are plundered, and my curtains in a moment."

  • Jeremiah 49:29 - "Their tents and their flocks they shall take away; they shall take for themselves their curtains, all their vessels and their camels; and they shall cry to them, 'Fear is on every side!'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Structural Parallelism: The trembling of the curtains in Habakkuk 3:7 balances the shaking of the mountains and the overflowing of the waters in the surrounding verses. It demonstrates that God's presence affects both the natural creation and human civilizations.

  • Prophetic Reversal: Midian was historically an oppressor of Israel, living in vast, intimidating tent encampments. In Habakkuk's vision, the very strength of Midian—their vast nomadic dwellings—becomes a theater of panic as their curtains shake before the God of Israel.
