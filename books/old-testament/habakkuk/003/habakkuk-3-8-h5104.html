RIVERS - ( nahar , Strong's H5104 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word nahar refers to a perennial, continuously flowing river, stream, or current. In Habakkuk 3:8, the plural form depicts the powerful, chaotic forces of nature that God commands and redirects. Rather than expressing literal anger toward His creation, God uses the rivers and seas as a stage to demonstrate His sovereign power and deliver His covenant people. This imagery reassures believers that even the most formidable, overwhelming forces in this world are entirely under His absolute control.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near East, rivers were both life-giving arteries and terrifying barriers. Perennial rivers like the Tigris, the Euphrates, and the Nile sustained vast empires, but their powerful currents and unpredictable flooding could also bring swift destruction. In a dry, arid landscape, a flowing river (nahar) represented constant life, prosperity, and divine blessing. Conversely, large rivers symbolized chaos, depth, and untamable power that stood in the way of human progress. To command, dry up, or divide a river was a feat attributed only to the supreme Creator. For Israel, the crossing of the Jordan River and the Red Sea stood as historical monuments to God's ultimate power over these formidable waters.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun nahar refers to a natural, continuously flowing stream of water, such as a river, canal, or current. Unlike a nachal (a seasonal torrent valley or wadi that dries up in the heat of summer), a nahar is a perennial river fed by a constant source. The word can designate specific historical rivers—such as the Euphrates (often simply called "the River"), the Nile, or the Tigris—as well as ocean currents or the chaotic waters of the deep. The noun is related to a root associated with flowing or shining, highlighting the sparkling, moving nature of water currents.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Literal Rivers: It is used of physical rivers, such as the four rivers that flowed out of the Garden of Eden (Genesis 2:10).

  • Metaphor for Abundance and Peace: The continuous, unstoppable flow of a river serves as a metaphor for divine peace and righteousness (Isaiah 48:18).

  • Symbol of Nations and Adversity: Overwhelming enemy nations are compared to the flooding, irresistible currents of a great river (Isaiah 8:7).

  • Cosmic Currents: It refers to the deep currents of the ocean, representing the foundational depths of the earth or chaotic forces (Jonah 2:3).

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, nahar demonstrates God’s absolute sovereignty over creation and history. In the Old Testament, the rivers obey God's voice; they dry up at His rebuke and split open to make a way for His redeemed. The rivers serve as His pathways, and their floods are under His dominion (Psalm 93:3-4). Furthermore, God uses the imagery of rivers to promise future restoration. In the wilderness of human suffering, He promises to open "rivers in desolate heights" (Isaiah 41:18), proving that His grace can penetrate and revive the most barren areas of our lives.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:8, the prophet looks back at God's historical redemptive acts to find hope during a time of impending Babylonian invasion. The verse asks a series of rhetorical questions: "O LORD, were You displeased with the rivers, Was Your anger against the rivers, Was Your wrath against the sea...?" The clear answer is "No." God’s anger was not directed at the inanimate rivers or the sea. Rather, God shook and divided the rivers (such as the Jordan) to clear a path of salvation for His people. The verse portrays God as a divine warrior riding His chariots of salvation to rescue His covenant partners. For the believer, this means that when the "rivers" of life's circumstances seem ready to overflow and destroy, they are merely instruments in the hand of a sovereign Savior who uses them to display His power and secure our deliverance.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Joshua 24:2 - "And Joshua said to all the people, 'Thus says the LORD God of Israel: "Your fathers, including Terah, the father of Abraham and the father of Nahor, dwelt on the other side of the River in old times; and they served other gods."'"

  • Psalm 46:4 - "There is a river whose streams shall make glad the city of God, The holy place of the tabernacle of the Most High."

  • Isaiah 48:18 - "Oh, that you had heeded My commandments! Then your peace would have been like a river, And your righteousness like the waves of the sea."

  • Jonah 2:3 - "For You cast me into the deep, Into the heart of the seas, And the floods surrounded me; All Your billows and Your waves passed over me."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In ancient Near Eastern mythology, the storm god engaged in violent combat to conquer the chaotic river god (often named Nahar). The Hebrew Scriptures intentionally demythologize this concept. Habakkuk does not depict a battle between equal deities. Instead, the one true God sovereignly commands the rivers. They do not fight Him; they submit to Him. When God marches, creation bows, and the rivers split at His command. This provides profound pastoral comfort: the chaotic forces that terrify us are merely servants to the sovereign will of our Father.
