ARROWS - ( matteh , Strong's H4294 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun matteh primarily refers to a staff, rod, shaft, or branch, representing support, authority, or chastisement. In the poetic and military imagery of Habakkuk 3:9, it refers to the shafts of arrows shot from a warrior's bow. This word highlights God's active, powerful intervention on behalf of His covenant people, portraying Him as a divine warrior whose weapons of judgment are sworn to execute His sovereign decrees.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete word picture behind matteh is a branch or shoot cut from a tree or vine. It is something flexible yet strong, capable of being fashioned into a tool. In agrarian and pastoral life, this branch became a shepherd’s staff used for guidance, defense, and leaning. Over time, this physical object transitioned into a symbol of leadership, power, and ruling authority (such as a scepter). When used in a military context, the word shifts from a heavy walking staff to the slender wooden shaft of a spear or an arrow. Thus, the word picture moves from a peaceful tool of pastoral care to a sharp instrument of lethal judgment in the hand of a mighty warrior.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun matteh is derived from the root verb natah, which means to stretch out, spread out, extend, or turn aside. Lexically, matteh carries several distinct definitions based on its context:

  • A staff or rod used for walking, support, or by a shepherd.

  • A rod used as an instrument of punishment, correction, or discipline.

  • A staff or scepter signifying political, royal, or spiritual authority.

  • A shaft of a weapon, specifically referring to spears or the shafts of arrows.

  • A tribe or clan, representing a branch of a larger family tree (specifically the twelve tribes of Israel).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Rod of Miraculous Power: In the Exodus narrative, the rod in Moses' hand is designated as "the rod of God." It represents divine authority and the extension of God's power over Pharaoh, the plagues, and the splitting of the Red Sea.

  • The Scepter of Rule: The word represents the ruler’s staff or scepter. It denotes the right to govern and exercise sovereign dominion over subjects and enemies alike.

  • The Rod of Discipline: God uses the rod metaphorically to describe foreign nations (like Assyria or Babylon) as instruments of His paternal discipline against His backsliding people.

  • The Arrow Shafts of Divine Warfare: In poetic depictions of God’s judgment, the natural elements of lightning and tempest are pictured as the glowing arrows of Yahweh. The matteh is the shaft that carries the destructive payload to its target.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, matteh demonstrates the active presence and sovereignty of God in history. When God wields the matteh, history bends to His will. It underscores that God is not a passive observer but an active participant who fights for His people and executes justice on the earth.

Furthermore, the dual nature of matteh as both a shepherd's staff and a warrior's weapon reveals the complex beauty of God’s character. To His flock, His rod is a source of comfort, protection, and gentle correction. To the rebellious nations who oppress His people, that same rod becomes a weapon of terrifying judgment. This reveals that God's holiness demands the destruction of evil to secure the salvation of His covenant people.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:9, the prophet records a majestic, poetic vision of a theophany—a visible manifestation of God coming to deliver His people. The verse reads: "Your bow was made quite ready; Oaths were sworn over Your arrows. Selah."

The Hebrew text here is famously compressed and difficult, literally reading "sworn are the rods/shafts (mattot) of the word (omer)." The NKJV renders mattot as "arrows," which fits perfectly with the preceding mention of the "bow."

The contextual nuance reveals God as a Divine Warrior who has uncovered His bow, pulling back the string to release His arrows of judgment. These arrows represent the cosmic forces of nature (lightning, pestilence, and earthquakes) that obey His voice. The "oaths sworn" over these arrow shafts indicate that God's judgment is not a chaotic, uncontrolled outburst of rage. Rather, it is the deliberate, reliable execution of His eternal covenant promises (His "word") to vindicate His people and judge wickedness.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The connection between matteh meaning "staff/rod" and matteh meaning "tribe" is deeply rooted in the ancient tribal structure of Israel. Each tribe was represented by a leader who carried a staff engraved with the family name (as seen in Numbers 17 during the budding of Aaron’s rod). Thus, the rod became synonymous with the tribe itself. When Habakkuk mentions the "arrows" or "rods" of God, ancient readers would also hear a subtle wordplay reminding them that Israel, as God’s "tribes," is ultimately protected and vindicated by the very weapons of judgment God unleashes against their enemies.
