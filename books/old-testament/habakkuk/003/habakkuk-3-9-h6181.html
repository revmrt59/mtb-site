QUITE - ( eryah , Strong's H6181 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word eryah refers to nakedness, bareness, or being stripped of a covering. In the context of Habakkuk 3:9, the NKJV translates this intensive Hebrew idiom—literally "with nakedness your bow was made naked"—using the word "quite" to modify "ready." This graphic metaphor depicts Yahweh as a divine warrior who has completely stripped the protective leather sheath from His bow, leaving it fully exposed, strung, and prepared for immediate, devastating judgment to deliver His covenant people.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The root verb behind eryah is arah, which means to bare, uncover, strip, or pour out.

  • In ancient Near Eastern warfare, an archer kept his bow in a leather case or sheath to protect the wood, sinew, and bowstring from moisture, grit, and heat.

  • To "bare" the bow meant to slide it out of its protective sleeve. This was a deliberate, final act of preparation before drawing the bowstring to launch an arrow.

  • By using the noun eryah, the prophet paints a vivid picture of God actively preparing for battle. He is not merely holding a weapon in reserve; He has uncovered it. The protective sheath is cast aside, and the weapon of divine wrath is visible, drawn, and active.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The noun eryah literally means "nakedness," "bareness," or "emptiness."

  • In its most basic sense, it refers to a person being stripped of clothing, exposing them to elements or shame.

  • In Habakkuk 3:9, the noun is paired with the verb ur (to bare or expose) in an intensive cognate construction (eryah te'or), which translates literally to "with nakedness your bow was bared."

  • This double-emphasis highlights the complete and absolute exposure of the weapon. The NKJV captures this superlative force by translating the phrase as "Your bow was made quite ready."

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • In the vast majority of its Old Testament occurrences, eryah carries a negative connotation of vulnerability, poverty, and public humiliation. It is frequently used in the prophets to describe a nation stripped bare by invading forces as a consequence of spiritual adultery.

  • In a dramatic rhetorical reversal, Habakkuk uses this word of exposure not to describe a vulnerable victim, but to describe God’s weapon. God "uncovers" His bow, turning the vulnerability of nakedness into an active, terrifying display of sovereign power.

  • The bared bow represents the unhindered release of God's power. Nothing stands between His holy wrath and the enemies of His people.

4. Theological Implications

  • The Divine Warrior: The unsheathed bow demonstrates that Yahweh is actively involved in human history. He is not a passive spectator; He personally steps onto the battlefield to execute justice.

  • The End of Forbearance: The baring of the bow signals that the period of warning has ended. The weapon is out of its case, indicating that judgment is no longer a distant threat but an imminent reality.

  • Covenant Faithfulness: God's judgment is never arbitrary. He bares His bow to rescue His anointed and save His people (Habakkuk 3:13). His wrath against the wicked is the direct expression of His covenant love and protection for His chosen ones.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • Habakkuk 3 is a poetic prayer (a shigyonoth) where the prophet recalls God's past redemptive acts at the Exodus, Sinai, and the conquest of Canaan to find hope during the impending Babylonian invasion.

  • In verse 9, the prophet beholds a terrifying theophany—a visible manifestation of God marching through the earth.

  • The phrase "Your bow was made quite ready" is immediately followed by "Oaths were sworn over Your arrows." This links the bared weapon to God's sworn promises. God’s uncovered bow is the instrument by which He will fulfill His ancient oaths to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Ezekiel 16:7 - "You thrived, grew, and became very beautiful. Your breasts were formed, your hair grew, but you were naked and bare."

  • Ezekiel 16:22 - "And in all your abominations and acts of harlotry you did not remember the days of your youth, when you were naked and bare, struggling in your blood."

  • Ezekiel 16:39 - "I will also give you into their hand, and they shall throw down your shrines and break down your high places. They shall also strip you of your clothes, take your beautiful jewelry, and leave you naked and bare."

  • Ezekiel 23:29 - "They will deal hatefully with you, take away all you have labored for, and leave you naked and bare. The nakedness of your harlotries shall be uncovered, both your lewdness and your harlotries."

  • Micah 1:11 - "Pass by in naked shame, you inhabitant of Shaphir; The inhabitant of Zaanan does not go out. Beth Ezel mourns; Its place to stand is taken away from you."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The construction of Habakkuk 3:9 is notoriously difficult to translate. The Hebrew words eryah te'or qašteka have puzzled translators for centuries, leading to various interpretations. However, the core image remains consistent: the uncovering of the bow symbolizes the active mobilization of divine judgment.

  • The placement of "Selah" at the end of verse 9 serves as a liturgical pause. The reader is instructed to halt, quiet their heart, and meditate on the awesome, fearsome reality of God standing with His bow fully unsheathed.
