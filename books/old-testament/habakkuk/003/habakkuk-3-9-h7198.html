BOW - ( qesheth , Strong's H7198 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word qesheth refers to a bow used in hunting or warfare, constructed of wood, horn, or metal, designed to shoot arrows. In Scripture, it frequently serves as a powerful symbol of military might, political strength, and divine judgment. In Habakkuk 3:9, the bow represents God’s active, prepared intervention as a Divine Warrior marching to deliver His people and execute justice on His enemies. It transitions from a weapon of human warfare to a cosmic instrument of sovereign judgment and redemption.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The bow (qesheth) was the premier long-range offensive weapon in the ancient Near East.

  • Crafted from wood or composite materials like horn and sinew, a bow required immense physical strength to bend, string, and shoot.

  • The physical action of "treading" or bending the bow is often associated with the word, indicating a state of readiness for battle.

  • Historically, a broken bow symbolized complete loss of power and military defeat, while a strung, raised, or bared bow symbolized readiness, dominance, and impending judgment.

  • In cosmic terms, the rainbow in Genesis 9 is also qesheth, representing God's weapon of war hung up in the clouds, pointed away from the earth as a visual promise of peace.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The word qesheth primarily denotes a bow used for launching arrows in hunting or warfare.

  • It refers directly to the physical weapon used by archers in battle.

  • Metaphorically, qesheth represents military power, national strength, personal influence, and political dominion.

  • Theologically, it designates the weapon of God’s wrath and righteous judgment, depicting Him as a sovereign archer who aims arrows of calamity, plague, or lightning at His adversaries.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Weapon of War: Used by soldiers and hunters to wage war or secure food, demonstrating human skill and lethal force.

  • The Symbol of Might: Broken bows represent shattered empires and the stripping away of human self-reliance.

  • The Rainbow: A symbol of the covenant, where God hangs up His battle-bow in the clouds as a sign of truce with creation.

  • The Divine Archer: God is portrayed bending His bow against the wicked, showing that His judgment is targeted, deliberate, and unavoidable.

4. Theological Implications

  • The Divine Warrior Motif: Israel’s God is not a passive bystander but an active warrior fighting on behalf of His covenant people.

  • Sovereignty and Judgment: God's bow is infallible; His arrows never miss their mark. His judgment is deliberate, prepared, and perfectly targeted.

  • Redemption through Judgment: The unsheathing of God's bow brings terror to the nations but salvation and comfort to His remnant, proving that God's anger against sin serves His ultimate plan of redemption.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • Habakkuk 3 is a prophetic prayer and a majestic, terrifying theophany (an appearance of God).

  • In Habakkuk 3:9, "Your bow was made quite ready" (literally, "naked was bared your bow") indicates that God has stripped the cover from His weapon. In antiquity, bows were kept in leather cases to protect them from moisture and damage. To bare the bow means to draw it out of its case, readying it for immediate use.

  • The context is God marching through the wilderness to rescue His anointed. The bared bow demonstrates that His judgment is no longer hidden, delayed, or passive; it is active, imminent, and visible to the world.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 9:13 - "I set My rainbow in the cloud, and it shall be for the sign of the covenant between Me and the earth."

  • Psalm 7:12 - "If he does not turn back, He will sharpen His sword; He bends His bow and makes it ready."

  • Lamentations 2:4 - "Like an enemy He has bent His bow; With His right hand stretched out like an adversary..."

  • Jeremiah 49:35 - "Thus says the Lord of hosts: 'Behold, I will break the bow of Elam, The foremost of their might.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The phrase "Your bow was made quite ready" in Habakkuk 3:9 uses a repetitive Hebrew root ('arar) meaning to make bare, emphasizing the sudden, unmistakable visibility of God’s power.

  • This image comforted Habakkuk, who was terrified by the impending Chaldean invasion. Knowing that God's cosmic bow was bared and His arrows of oath-bound deliverance were ready gave the prophet the strength to rejoice even in the midst of historical devastation.
