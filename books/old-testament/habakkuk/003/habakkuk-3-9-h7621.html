OATHS - ( shebu'ah , Strong's H7621 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun shebu'ah refers to a solemn oath, curse, or sworn covenant that binds parties together under divine witness. Derived from the root word for "seven," it carries the underlying word picture of "sevening oneself"—binding oneself securely through a complete, sacred pledge. In the difficult poetic context of Habakkuk 3:9, this word describes the absolute certainty of God’s judgments and promises. As God readies His bow for battle, His actions are not erratic or impulsive; they are the execution of His sworn oaths to His people, guaranteeing both vindication for the righteous and chastisement for the wicked.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun shebu'ah is intrinsically linked to the Hebrew cardinal number sheba, meaning "seven." In the ancient Near East, seven was the number of completeness, perfection, and sacred order. To make an oath was to "seven oneself," a concept originating from the practice of giving seven pledges—such as the seven ewe lambs Abraham gave to Abimelech at Beersheba ("the Well of the Seven" or "Well of the Oath") to seal their covenant. An oath was therefore not a casual promise, but a solemn, self-binding transaction that invoked God as the ultimate witness and judge of the pledge.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word shebu'ah means an oath, a sworn statement, or a curse. It denotes a formal declaration made to confirm a promise, establish a covenant, or attest to the truth of a matter. The term carries legal and covenantal weight, representing a binding obligation that cannot be broken without incurring divine judgment. In the plural form (shebu'ot), as seen in Habakkuk 3:9, it refers to multiple oaths or a highly solemn, repeated swearing of promises.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament uses shebu'ah in several key ways:

  • Covenantal Pledges: The most significant use is in the context of God's unilateral covenants with the patriarchs, where He binds Himself by an oath to fulfill His redemptive promises.

  • Legal Assertions: In Israel's legal system, an oath of the Lord was used to settle disputes when there was no physical evidence or eye-witness testimony available.

  • Curse of Failure: An oath often contained an implied or explicit self-malediction (curse), where the swearer invited divine judgment upon themselves if they failed to keep their word.

  • The overall word picture is one of ultimate security. An oath "sevens" the matter, placing it beyond dispute or alteration.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, shebu'ah underscores the absolute truthfulness and unchangeable nature of God. Because God cannot lie, His word is already perfectly reliable; yet, to accommodate human weakness and provide assurance, He condescends to swear an oath. When God swears an oath, He pledges His own character, holiness, and existence as the guarantee of fulfillment. In prophetic literature, God's oaths represent the ultimate anchor of hope for Israel, assuring them that despite exile and judgment, His covenant promises will never fail.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Habakkuk 3:9, the prophet recounts a majestic, terrifying vision of God marching forth in battle array to save His people: "Your bow was made quite bare; / Rods of chastisement were sworn. / Selah." The Hebrew phrase shebu'ot mattot omer is condensed and poetic. The plural noun shebu'ot is translated here as the verbal action "were sworn," modifying the "rods" of judgment.

The contextual nuance is profoundly reassuring. God's judgment is not an uncontrolled outburst of divine anger. The "rods of chastisement" (symbols of divine correction and victory) are "sworn"—meaning they are aligned with His covenant oaths and His sovereign word. God's unsheathed bow and His flying arrows are the execution of the promises He swore to the patriarchs and the tribes of Israel. His wrath against the wicked is the direct consequence of His covenant faithfulness to deliver His people.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 26:3: "Dwell in this land, and I will be with you and bless you; for to you and your descendants I give all these lands, and I will perform the oath which I swore to Abraham your father."

  • Deuteronomy 7:8: "but because the Lord loves you, and because He would keep the oath which He swore to your fathers, the Lord has brought you out with a mighty hand, and redeemed you from the house of bondage, from the hand of Pharaoh king of Egypt."

  • Nehemiah 10:29: "these joined with their brethren, their nobles, and entered into a curse and an oath to walk in God’s Law, which was given by Moses the servant of God, and to observe and do all the commandments of the LORD our Lord, and His ordinances and His statutes;"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The concept of the shebu'ah is the Old Testament foundation for the New Testament's assurance of faith. In Hebrews 6:13–18, the author argues that because God could swear by no one greater, He swore by Himself to Abraham. This oath, paired with God's unchangeable promise, forms two immutable things in which it is impossible for God to lie. When we read of the "sworn" rods in Habakkuk, we are reminded that God’s redemptive judgments are backed by this same unyielding, self-attesting commitment.
