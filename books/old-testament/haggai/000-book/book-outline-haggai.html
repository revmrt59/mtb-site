Haggai Book Outline

Revision 2 November 6, 2026

Major Movements

I. Consider Your Ways: The Call to Rebuild (Haggai 1:1–11)

On the first day of the sixth month in Darius's second year, the LORD answers the people's claim that "The time has not come" by setting their paneled houses beside his house lying in ruins. Twice he tells them, "Consider your ways!" He explains their poor harvests and the drought as his own response to their neglect, and he calls them to bring wood and build so that he may take pleasure in the house and be glorified.

II. The Remnant Obeys and the LORD Stirs Them (Haggai 1:12–15)

Zerubbabel, Joshua, and all the remnant obey the voice of the LORD and fear him. The LORD answers their obedience with a short promise, "I am with you," and stirs up the spirit of the leaders and the people. On the twenty-fourth day of the sixth month they begin working on his house, and the book turns from rebuke to encouragement.

III. The Promise of Greater Glory (Haggai 2:1–9)

On the twenty-first day of the seventh month, the LORD speaks to builders who look at the modest new structure and see it as nothing beside the temple's former glory, and he commands leaders and people to be strong and work. He grounds their courage in his presence and in the word he gave when they came out of Egypt, assuring them, "My Spirit remains among you; do not fear!" He then promises to shake heaven, earth, and the nations, to fill the house with glory, and to make "the glory of this latter temple" greater than the former, giving peace in that place.

IV. From Defilement to Blessing (Haggai 2:10–19)

On the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month, Haggai asks the priests two questions about the law, and their answers show that a garment carrying holy meat does not make other food holy when its edge touches that food, while uncleanness from contact with a dead body does spread. Haggai applies that contrast to the people, their work, and their offerings. The LORD then reminds them of their meager harvests, calls them to consider the day he is marking, and promises, "But from this day I will bless you."

V. Zerubbabel, the LORD's Chosen Signet (Haggai 2:20–23)

On the same day, a second word comes for Zerubbabel alone: the LORD will shake heaven and earth and overthrow the thrones and armies of kingdoms. In that day he will take Zerubbabel, whom he calls "My servant," and make him like a signet ring, "for I have chosen you." The book closes with God's sovereign choice and a renewed hope for David's royal line.
