Haggai 2 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Haggai 2 brings three more dated messages to people already at work on the temple, as the LORD encourages builders who see the new house as nothing beside its former glory, promising His presence, His Spirit among them, and a latter glory greater than the former. He then shows through two questions to the priests that the people and their offerings are unclean, promises, “But from this day I will bless you” (Haggai 2:19), and closes the book with a word to Zerubbabel, whom He will make like a signet ring.

Chapter Sections

1. Haggai 2:1–5 — Be Strong and Work, for I Am with You

On the twenty-first day of the seventh month, the LORD asks who remembers the temple in its former glory and whether the new house seems as nothing in comparison. He commands Zerubbabel, Joshua, and all the people of the land to be strong and work, for He is with them (Haggai 2:4), and according to the word He covenanted with them when they came out of Egypt, He assures them, “My Spirit remains among you; do not fear!” (Haggai 2:5).

2. Haggai 2:6–9 — The Glory of This Latter Temple

The LORD promises that in a little while He will shake heaven and earth, the sea and dry land, and all nations, and fill this temple with glory, for the silver and the gold are His. He declares that “The glory of this latter temple shall be greater than the former” (Haggai 2:9), and that in this place He will give peace.

3. Haggai 2:10–14 — Holy Meat, Uncleanness, and a Defiled People

On the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month, Haggai asks the priests whether holy meat carried in a garment makes other food holy when the garment touches it, and they answer no; he then asks whether a person unclean from a dead body makes food unclean by touching it, and they answer that it does. Haggai applies the rulings, as the LORD says, “So is this people, and so is this nation before Me” (Haggai 2:14), and every work of their hands and what they offer there is unclean.

4. Haggai 2:15–19 — From This Day I Will Bless You

The LORD calls the people to consider the time before stone was laid on stone in His temple, when heaps of grain and wine vats yielded far less than expected and He struck their labors with blight, mildew, and hail, yet they did not turn to Him. He marks the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month, the day the foundation of the temple was laid, and He asks whether the seed is still in the barn; though the vine, the fig tree, the pomegranate, and the olive tree have not yet yielded fruit, He promises, “But from this day I will bless you” (Haggai 2:19).

5. Haggai 2:20–23 — Zerubbabel, the LORD’s Chosen Signet

On the same day a second word comes for Zerubbabel, governor of Judah: the LORD will shake heaven and earth, overthrow the throne of kingdoms, and destroy the strength of the Gentile kingdoms. In that day He will take Zerubbabel His servant and make him like a signet ring, “for I have chosen you” (Haggai 2:23).
