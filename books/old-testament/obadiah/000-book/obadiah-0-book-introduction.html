Book Introduction: Obadiah

Background

Book

Obadiah

Author

Obadiah ("Servant" or "Worshiper of Yahweh"). Nothing certain is known about the prophet beyond this book. His anonymity keeps the focus on God's message rather than the messenger.

Pastoral Insight: Faithful servants point people to God rather than themselves. As John the Baptist said, "He must increase, but I must decrease" (John 3:30).

Audience

Primarily Edom, with Judah also receiving assurance that God would judge their enemies and restore His people.

Pastoral Insight: Although addressed to Edom, the message ultimately comforts God's people by reminding them that He sees injustice and will act in His perfect time.

Date

c. 586-585 BC, shortly after the fall of Jerusalem (most widely accepted view).

Historical Setting

Jerusalem had fallen to Babylon in 586 BC. The city was destroyed, its people were killed or taken into exile, and Judah appeared defeated. Instead of coming to Judah's aid, the Edomites rejoiced over Jerusalem's downfall, joined in the plundering, and betrayed fleeing survivors to the Babylonians.

The Edomites were descendants of Esau, Jacob's brother. Their shared ancestry with Israel made Edom's betrayal especially grievous before God.

Pastoral Insight: God expects His people to help their brothers and sisters in times of need, not ignore them, rejoice over their failures, or take advantage of their weakness.

Occasion

God raised up Obadiah to pronounce judgment against Edom for its pride, violence, and betrayal of Judah while assuring His people that His justice would prevail.

Pastoral Insight: God may delay judgment, but He never ignores sin or forgets His people.

Message

Purpose

To announce God's judgment on Edom for pride and violence, reassure Judah that divine justice would prevail, and proclaim God's coming kingdom and the restoration of His people.

Theme(s)

  • The danger and deceptiveness of pride
  • God's concern for how people respond to the suffering of others
  • Accountability for indifference, gloating, and exploitation
  • The certainty of divine justice
  • The hope of restoration and the certainty of the Lord's reign

Big Idea

God sovereignly judges the proud, vindicates His people, and ultimately establishes His righteous kingdom over all nations through His promised King.

Pastoral Insight: Obadiah reminds us that God is holy, just, and faithful. As He exposes the pride, violence, and indifference of Edom, He also invites us to examine our own hearts, especially in the way we treat our brothers and sisters. His desire is not merely to expose sin, but to make us more like Christ.

Key Verse(s)

Obadiah 15 "For the day of the LORD upon all the nations is near; As you have done, it shall be done to you; Your reprisal shall return upon your own head." (NKJV)

Obadiah 21 "Then saviors shall come to Mount Zion To judge the mountains of Esau, And the kingdom shall be the LORD'S." (NKJV)

Outline

  • The Judgment of Edom Announced (1–9)
  • The Reason for the Judgment Explained (10–16)
  • The Lord's Kingdom Established (17–21)

Christ in This Book

Points forward to Jesus Christ as the ultimate Savior, Deliverer, and King who judges evil with perfect righteousness, rescues His people, and establishes the everlasting Kingdom of God, fulfilling the promise of Obadiah 21.
