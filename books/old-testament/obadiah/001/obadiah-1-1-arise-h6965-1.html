ARISE - ( qum , Strong's H6965 )

Qum - ( qum , H6965 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb qum fundamentally means to stand up, get up, arise, or establish. It spans a wide range of contexts in the Old Testament, from literal physical movement from a seated or reclining position to rising up in hostility, taking decisive action, or establishing a covenant or decree. In Obadiah 1:1, qum appears as an urgent plural command ("Arise") issued to surrounding nations by a divine envoy. It serves as God's divine summons mobilizing human agents to execute His holy judgment against the proud nation of Edom.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root qum is a foundational verbal root in Hebrew and ancient Semitic languages. In everyday life across the ancient Near East, the term described simple physical movements: standing up from sitting, rising from sleep, or standing upright in a royal court or legal assembly.

Beyond physical movement, the verb developed important functional usages in civil, covenantal, and military settings. In legal contexts, qum was used for validating testimonies, establishing decrees, or confirming covenant promises. In military contexts, it functioned as an imperative battle cry, commanding troops to move out of camp and prepare for combat. In prophetic literature, qum frequently describes divine initiative—God arising from His throne to judge the wicked or deliver His oppressed people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

At its core, qum denotes the act of assuming an upright posture or initiating motion. Standard lexical authorities identify its core semantic nuances across several distinct categories:

  • Physical Movement: Standing up, getting up from lying down or sitting, or rising early in the morning.

  • Mobilization and Action: Transitioning from a state of inactivity to action, such as setting out on a journey, undertaking a task, or preparing for war.

  • Opposition and Conflict: Rising up against an opponent, enemy, or authority figure in rebellion or combat.

  • Establishment and Permanence: Standing firm, enduring, or being established, confirmed, or validated (such as a word, covenant, or kingdom).

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout Old Testament literature, qum is employed in both narrative prose and elevated poetic discourse. In historical narratives, the verb frequently marks the beginning of a decisive sequence of events, where a character "arose and went" to perform a specific task.

In prophetic and poetic literature, qum carries strong theological weight. When human beings are commanded to "arise," it signals a mandate to align with God's immediate purpose. When the verb describes God Himself arising, it signifies the dramatic execution of divine justice or redemption.

In Obadiah 1:1, the verb appears in the Qal stem, imperative form, masculine gender, and plural number. In this grammatical form, it functions as a direct, authoritative command addressed to a group, summoning them to collective action.

4. Theological Implications

The usage of qum in prophetic judgment literature highlights important theological principles regarding God's reign over human history:

  • Sovereign Lordship over Nations: God is not merely the deity of Israel; He exercises sovereign authority over all surrounding nations. When God issues the command "Arise" to pagan powers, they are mobilized as instruments to accomplish His decrees.

  • The Execution of Divine Justice: Human pride and opposition to God's covenant people do not go unchecked. Edom had arrogantly presumed her mountain fortresses made her invincible, but God's word summons nations to bring down her pride.

  • The Certainty of God's Word: When God decrees judgment, His mandate initiates historical events that fulfill His holy intentions. Human military movements serve as the execution of divine sovereignty.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Obadiah opens his brief prophecy by identifying his message as a vision from the Lord God concerning Edom. Immediately following this superscription, the text introduces a report heard from the LORD and an envoy dispatched among the nations.

The message delivered by the messenger begins with the command: "Arise, and let us rise up against her in battle." In Obadiah 1:1, qum appears in the Qal stem, imperative form, masculine gender, and plural number. This direct command ("Arise") is issued to the surrounding nations, compelling them to mobilize for war against Edom.

This command is immediately coupled with the first-person plural cohortative form of the same verb ("let us rise up"), showing the envoy uniting with the summoned nations to carry out the military campaign. The placement of qum at the head of the envoy's message sets a tone of immediate urgency. Edom believed she was secure in her high rock dwellings, but God's sovereign initiative summons the nations to march against her, demonstrating that no human fortification can withstand the decree of the Lord.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Psalms 9:19 — "Arise, O LORD, do not let man prevail; let the nations be judged in Your sight." Here the word is translated "Arise", serving as a prayerful petition for God to intervene in judgment against worldly arrogance.

  • 2 Kings 1:3 — "But the angel of the LORD said to Elijah the Tishbite, 'Arise, go up to meet the messengers of the king of Samaria...'" Rendered "Arise", functioning as a direct command from God mobilizing the prophet for an urgent proclamation.

  • Deuteronomy 28:9 — "The LORD will establish you as a holy people to Himself, just as He has sworn to you..." Rendered "will establish", highlighting the sense of covenantal confirmation and structural permanence.

  • Zephaniah 3:8 — "'Therefore wait for Me,' says the LORD, 'until the day I rise up for plunder...'" Rendered "rise", describing the Lord Himself standing up to execute universal judicial decree.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The literary structure of Obadiah 1:1 creates a strong thematic contrast with Edom's self-perception later in the prophecy. In verse 3, Edom is described as saying in her heart, "Who will bring me down to the ground?" Edom viewed herself as elevated, secure, and unreachable. However, the prophetic word opens with God issuing the mandate to "Arise" against her. While Edom boasts in her physical height and natural defences, the Lord's word mobilizes surrounding nations to bring her down.
