EDOM - ( e.dom , Strong's H123 )

Summary:

The proper noun ʾedom denotes "red" and designates Esau, his descendants, and the mountainous country southeast of the Dead Sea settled by his lineage. In Obadiah 1:1, ʾedom identifies the specific target nation receiving Yahweh's prophetic decree of impending judgment. By naming Edom at the outset, the verse establishes the central subject of Obadiah's prophecy, highlighting God's sovereign accountability over a historically brother-nation that turned against Judah in her day of distress.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew name ʾedom is rooted in the narrative of Genesis, where Esau was named Edom after selling his birthright for red stew (ʿadom). The name also corresponds to the red sandstone mountain range of Mount Seir, south of Moab and east of the Arabah, which became the geographical homeland of Esau’s descendants.

Geographically and strategically, Edom occupied a high plateau with fortified rock clefts, commanding lucrative trade routes such as the King's Highway. Throughout Old Testament history, Israel and Edom shared a complex relationship characterized by shared ancestry—as Jacob and Esau were twin brothers—intermingled with persistent military and diplomatic conflict.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, ʾedom functions as a proper noun referring to:

  • Esau personally as the progenitor of the nation.

  • The collective ethnic descendants of Esau (the Edomites).

  • The territorial region and geopolitical kingdom of Edom.

In Obadiah 1:1, the noun is parsed as a masculine singular noun in the absolute state. Lexical authorities (such as Brown-Driver-Briggs and the Theological Wordbook of the Old Testament) trace the proper noun to the root ʾdm, reflecting redness, which highlights both Esau's physical features at birth, the stew he traded his birthright for, and the reddish topography of the region of Seir.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Hebrew Scriptures, ʾedom appears in three distinct literature types:

  • Historical Narrative: Describing geographical boundaries, royal genealogies, and military encounters during the Exodus, the Monarchy, and the divided kingdom.

  • Wisdom & Poetic Literature: Referencing the legendary wisdom of Edomite region leaders (such as those from Teman).

  • Prophetic Oracles: Functioning as a representative nation under divine judgment due to pride, violence, and treachery against covenantally related Israel.

The term consistently carries ethnic, political, and spatial nuances, serving as a reminder of family ties that were repeatedly severed by hostility and geopolitical rivalry.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of ʾedom carries weight regarding God's righteousness and international justice:

  • Sovereign Accountability: Edom’s inclusion in prophetic judgment demonstrates that Yahweh’s moral governing authority extends beyond Israel to all earth’s nations.

  • Kinship and Covenant Responsibility: The prophetic indictments against Edom emphasize the severity of betraying fraternal bonds. Edom was held accountable for failing to display brotherhood toward Judah.

  • The Fallacy of Human Pride: Edom's natural topography fostered a sense of invincible security. The prophetic word against Edom exposes human self-reliance as futile before God's judgment.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:1, ʾedom is introduced as the explicit object of divine communication: "Thus says the Lord GOD concerning Edom." Grammatically, as a masculine singular proper noun in the absolute state, it designates the entire nation as a single moral entity standing before God's tribunal.

The immediate sentence pairs the vision given to Obadiah with a global summons: an envoy is sent among the nations to rally forces against Edom. Contextually, Obadiah addresses Edom's gloating, plundering, and violence when Jerusalem was invaded. By placing ʾedom as the first word of divine decree in the prophecy, the text establishes that no geographic fortification or historic kinship will shield the nation from divine justice when it breaches moral obligations toward its neighbor and brother.

  • Numbers 20:23 – "And the LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron in Mount Hor, on the border of the land of Edom..."

  • Ezekiel 25:14 – "'I will lay My vengeance on Edom by the hand of My people Israel, that they may do in Edom according to My anger and according to My fury; and they shall know My vengeance,' says the Lord GOD."

  • Amos 9:12 – "That they may possess the remnant of Edom, and all the Gentiles who are called by My name,' says the LORD who does this thing."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Verbal and Nominal Wordplay: The Hebrew root ʾdm connects ʾedom (Edom) phonetically and conceptually with ʾadam (man/mankind) and ʾadamah (ground/earth). In prophetic literature, Edom frequently serves as a dramatic representation of fallen humanity in rebellion against God's reign.

  • Historical Transition: In the Intertestamental and Greco-Roman periods, the territory and people of Edom became known by the Greek designation Idumea, maintaining a persistent role in inter-ethnic tensions within the region.
