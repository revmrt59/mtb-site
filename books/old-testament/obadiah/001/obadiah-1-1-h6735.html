MESSENGER - ( tsir , Strong's H6735 )
Summary:
In Obadiah 1:1, the Hebrew word translated "messenger" is tsir, which carries the distinct meaning of an envoy, ambassador, or official diplomatic representative dispatched on a high-level mission. Unlike the standard Hebrew word for messenger (mal'ak), tsir emphasizes the authoritative, binding, and representative nature of the sender. In this context, God dispatches an envoy among the nations to rally them for judgment against Edom. This highlights God's absolute sovereignty over geopolitical affairs—He mobilizes nations as His instruments to carry out His holy decrees.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background
The noun tsir originates from a root that connects conceptually to a pivot or hinge upon which a door turns, or to pressing agency. As a title for an official, a tsir is an envoy or ambassador who acts as the personal representative of a sovereign king. An ambassador does not speak on his own authority; he carries the full weight, legal standing, and royal mandate of the monarch who sent him. When a tsir arrives, the authority of the throne arrives with him.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition
According to Old Testament lexicons (such as BDB and HALOT), tsir refers strictly to an ambassador, envoy, or messenger sent on official state business.
- An official political messenger sent between nations or sovereigns to convey alliances, declarations, or royal decrees.
- A representative entrusted with an urgent, weighty message that requires complete fidelity to the sender.

3. Usage and Word Pictures
In Hebrew thought, the tsir serves as a vital link between powers. The word paints several vivid pictures across the Old Testament:
- The Diplomatic Envoy: Sent on long journeys across borders to deliver royal declarations or negotiate international matters (Isaiah 18:2; 57:9).
- The Refreshing Agent: A faithful tsir brings relief and clarity to his master, like the cold of snow during the heat of harvest, because he reliably delivers the truth (Proverbs 25:13).
- The Bringer of Healing: A trustworthy ambassador restores broken diplomatic relations and brings health and peace (Proverbs 13:17).

4. Theological Implications
- Sovereign Control Over the Nations: The Lord GOD (Adonai YHWH) sends His envoy among pagan nations. Earthly empires believe they move by their own political ambitions, but they are actually responding to the sovereign mobilization of God.
- Infallible Execution of Divine Decrees: When God dispatches a tsir to declare judgment, the outcome is guaranteed. God's word sent forth through His messenger will accomplish its full purpose.
- God as the Supreme Monarch: Israel's God is not merely a localized deity; He commands the international arena and summons armies to execute His righteous judgment against arrogance and violence.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage
In Obadiah 1:1, Edom had exalted itself in pride, relying on its mountainous fortress and historical hostility toward Judah. God reveals to Obadiah a heavenly perspective on earthly events:
- The report (shemu'ah) has been heard from the LORD, and a tsir has been dispatched among the nations.
- The envoy's command is direct: "Arise, and let us rise up against her for battle!"
- While the NKJV translates tsir as "messenger" here (and as "ambassador" in the parallel text of Jeremiah 49:14), the nuance is that of an imperial herald summoning armies. The messenger acts as God's instrument to incite and unify the nations against Edom, proving that Edom's impending ruin is orchestrated directly by God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)
- Proverbs 13:17 – "A wicked messenger falls into trouble, But a faithful ambassador brings health."
- Proverbs 25:13 – "Like the cold of snow at harvest time Is a faithful messenger to those who send him, For he refreshes the soul of his masters."
- Isaiah 18:2 – "Which sends ambassadors by the sea, Even in vessels of reeds on the waters, saying, 'Go, swift messengers, to a nation tall and smooth of skin...'"
- Isaiah 57:9 – "You went to the king with ointment, And increased your perfumes; You sent your messengers far off, And even lowered yourself to Sheol."
- Jeremiah 49:14 – "I have heard a report from the LORD, And an ambassador has been sent to the nations: 'Gather together, come against her, And rise up to battle!'"

7. Other Relevant Insights
- Pastoral Application: Believers can rest in the absolute sovereignty of God over global events. Political movements, military conflicts, and international shifts are never outside the control of the Lord God, who sends His providential decrees across the earth.
- Homiletical Note: When preaching Obadiah 1:1, highlight the contrast between Edom's false security in her rocks and God's sovereign reach. Edom thought she was unapproachable, but God easily sent an envoy into the international community to organize her total defeat.
