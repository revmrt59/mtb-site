HEARD - ( sha.ma , Strong's H8085 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb shamaʿ fundamentally means to hear, pay attention to, listen attentively, or respond to an audible sound or spoken message. In Obadiah 1:1, the verb appears in a first-person plural form ("We have heard"), denoting the reception of a divine decree from Yahweh concerning impending judgment upon Edom. By using shamaʿ here, the prophet grounds his message not in human speculation or political sentiment, but in authoritative revelation received directly from God regarding sovereign rule over the nations.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the Ancient Near East and throughout the Old Testament, hearing was understood as an active, relational engagement rather than merely a passive acoustic sense. To hear a message meant to receive it into the mind, recognize its source, and respond appropriately. Prophetic ministry in Israel relied entirely upon receiving divine speech; a true prophet did not invent messages but served as a herald of what Yahweh had declared. In the historical setting of Obadiah, the nation of Edom—descendants of Esau—had acted with pride and violence against Judah during a time of distress. The prophet opens his book by establishing that Israel’s prophetic observers have received a definitive announcement from God regarding Edom’s ultimate accountability.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

At its core, shamaʿ carries the basic meaning of physical hearing—perceiving sound through the ear. Beyond mere acoustic perception, the semantic range of shamaʿ expands depending on context to include listening with intent, paying attention, understanding, granting a request, and heeding or obeying a command. In covenantal contexts, shamaʿ is frequently translated "obey" or "heed," as true covenantal hearing naturally results in action. In legal or diplomatic contexts, it refers to receiving an official report, decree, or testimony.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, shamaʿ serves as a primary verb for God's communication to humanity and humanity's response to God. When used of God listening to people, it signifies His merciful attention to prayer or cries of distress. When used of human beings listening to God, it highlights either faithful obedience or tragic disregard. In prophetic contexts, shamaʿ frequently denotes the reception of a divine word or report (shemuʿah), emphasizing that the message originated with God Himself. The verb captures the moment revelation is delivered to human ears, establishing an absolute standard of truth for those who receive it.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of shamaʿ in prophetic revelation underscores the initiative of God in making His will known. Human history is not governed by blind fate or geopolitical power dynamics, but by the spoken word of Yahweh. When God speaks, His word possesses inherent authority and power to accomplish His purpose. Furthermore, the corporate expression of hearing in prophetic literature reveals that God's covenant community is called to be an attentive audience to His sovereign declarations, recognizing that God executes justice among all nations, not merely within Israel.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:1, the prophet writes, "We have heard a report from the LORD." The target word shamaʿ is parsed in the Qal stem, perfect form, first-person plural. The Qal stem represents simple, active action, establishing that the act of hearing occurred directly. The perfect form presents the hearing as a complete, established reality—the divine message has already been communicated and stands firm. The first-person plural ("We") unites Obadiah with the prophetic community or the broader remnant of Israel who collectively recognize Yahweh's authority.

The context places shamaʿ in direct connection with "a report from the LORD" and the diplomatic envoy sent among the nations. The report is not a vague rumor; it is a clear revelation of Yahweh's impending judgment on Edom. By declaring "We have heard," Obadiah validates his whole prophecy: what follows is not a personal grievance against Edom, but the unalterable word of God that has been received and must now be proclaimed.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 3:10 – "So he said, 'I heard Your voice in the garden, and I was afraid because I was naked; and I hid myself.'"

  • Exodus 24:7 – "Then he took the Book of the Covenant and read in the hearing of the people. And they said, 'All that the LORD has said we will do, and be obedient [shamaʿ].'"

  • Isaiah 30:21 – "Your ears shall hear a word behind you, saying, 'This is the way, walk in it,' whenever you turn to the right hand or whenever you turn to the left."

  • 2 Chronicles 34:27 – "because your heart was tender, and you humbled yourself before God when you heard His words against this place and against its inhabitants... I also have heard you, says the LORD."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Obadiah 1:1, the verb shamaʿ ("we have heard") is paired with the noun shemuʿah ("report"), which shares the same underlying Hebrew root. This intentional literary pairing—essentially stating "we have heard a hearing"—heightens the certainty and solemnity of the message. The prophet stresses that the message received is fundamentally characterized by its divine transmission, leaving no room for Edom's prideful self-reliance to resist Yahweh's decreed justice.
