LORD - ( a.do.na , Strong's H136 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word adonay means "Lord," "Master," or "Sovereign Owner." It expresses God's absolute sovereignty, supreme authority, and rightful ownership over all creation. In Obadiah 1:1, adonay functions as a royal title attached to the divine covenant name (YHWH), forming the composite title "Lord GOD" (Adonay YHWH). This title establishes that the prophetic message against Edom is not merely human opinion or local geopolitical commentary, but the binding decree of the universal Master who possesses supreme authority to judge nations, shatter human pride, and execute righteous justice across international history.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The term adonay is one of the primary titles used in the Hebrew Scriptures to acknowledge God's absolute authority and rule. Derived from the root adon, which refers to a master, ruler, lord, or person possessing authority over subordinates (such as a king over subjects or a master over servants), adonay represents the divine title par excellence for God's ruler-master relationship to His creation and His people.

In ancient Near Eastern culture, lordship was defined by authority, responsibility, and total jurisdiction over a realm. To address someone as lord (adon) was to yield deference and recognize their legitimate power to command, protect, and direct. When applied to God, adonay carries this relational and governmental weight in an absolute sense. God is not merely one master among many; He is the supreme Master and ultimate Sovereign over all earthly rulers, powers, and natural orders.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, adonay signifies "Lord," "Master," or "Sovereign." It emphasizes divine dominion, supreme power, ownership, and rightful governance.

  • Primary Sense: Sovereign Master, supreme Ruler, owner possessing absolute rights of command and disposition.

  • Relational Aspect: Denotes the proper stance of creation toward the Creator—submission, obedience, deference, and service.

  • Form and Usage: While related to human terms for master (adon), adonay functions in standard Hebrew biblical usage specifically as a divine title denoting God's ultimate majesty and ruler-ship. In the Old Testament text, it frequently pairs with the personal covenant name of God (YHWH), producing the formal title Adonay YHWH ("Lord GOD"), which stresses both His sovereign cosmic reign (Adonay) and His faithful covenant commitment (YHWH).

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, adonay appears prominently in prayer, intercession, worship, and prophetic commissioning. It regularly marks a crucial recognition of God's rights over human lives and international destiny.

  • Prophetic Invocations: Prophets frequently introduce divine speeches with "Thus says the Lord GOD (Adonay YHWH)." This formula underscores that the prophet acts merely as a herald or ambassador; the authority behind the message rests entirely in the sovereign Lord who issued the decree.

  • Reverent Prayer: Godly leaders address God as adonay when seeking mercy, acknowledging their absolute dependence, or bowing before His majestic holiness. Calling God adonay acknowledges that the petitioner is a servant making appeal to the supreme Master.

  • Cosmic & National Governance: Adonay is used not only regarding Israel's internal covenant life but also regarding God's absolute jurisdiction over pagan nations. God's adon-ship (lordship) is universal; no nation falls outside His jurisdiction or beyond His power to summons, judge, or destroy.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of adonay centers on the reality of divine sovereignty and authority:

  • Absolute Sovereign Jurisdiction: God is the supreme Ruler of human history. Kings and empires rise and fall under His command. No human stronghold, geographical fortification, or military alliance can thwart His sovereign decrees.

  • God's Title as Owner and Master: Creation belongs to God by right of creation and providence. Humans are servants within His domain, accountable to His standards and subject to His sovereign choices.

  • The Basis of Prophetic Authority: Prophetic revelation carries absolute weight because it originates from Adonay. When the Sovereign Lord speaks, human history must conform to His word.

  • Divine Majesty in Judgment and Salvation: As Adonay, God possesses both the legal authority to pass judgment on arrogant rebellion and the supreme power to deliver His oppressed people.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:1, the target occurrence of adonay is morphologically a masculine singular noun in the absolute state, serving as part of the divine title Adonay YHWH ("Lord GOD"). The passage opens: "The vision of Obadiah. Thus says the Lord GOD concerning Edom..."

The immediate context of Obadiah's prophecy focuses on the judgment of Edom, a nation descended from Esau that gloated over Judah's downfall and assisted enemy invaders. Edom boasted in its secure geographical location—nestled in the rugged, elevated mountain strongholds of Seir—and considered itself invincible ("Who will bring me down to the ground?").

In this setting, the opening title Adonay YHWH establishes immediate contrast and narrative authority:

  • Countering Human Arrogance: Edom believes its natural fortresses render it immune to overthrow. But Obadiah introduces the true Sovereign—Adonay, the Master of all terrain, nations, and powers. Edom's rock strongholds are nothing before the supreme Master who rules the entire earth.

  • Grounding Prophetic Certainty: Obadiah does not present a personal grievance or nationalistic opinion against Edom. The messenger formula ("Thus says the Lord GOD") places the decree squarely in the court of the cosmic Master whose decisions cannot be vetoed.

  • The Intersection of Sovereignty and Covenant: By combining Adonay (Sovereign Lord) with YHWH (the covenant God of Israel), the text signals that Israel's covenant God is simultaneously the absolute Master of the Gentile nations. Edom's treachery against Judah was not merely a dispute between neighbors; it was an offense against the sovereign Ruler of the universe.

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Canonical Resonance in New Testament Translation: In the Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint), adonay was consistently rendered using the Greek term kyrios ("Lord"). This same Greek term kyrios was subsequently used by New Testament writers to refer to God the Father and to confess Jesus Christ as Lord ("Jesus is Kyrios"). Understanding adonay as the Old Testament background for supreme divine lordship clarifies the profound theological claim made when early Christians confessed Jesus as Lord of all.

  • The Functional Pair Adonay YHWH: In prophetic texts, when adonay precedes YHWH, Jewish tradition out of reverence read adonay for YHWH, leading to the combined reading represented in English translations as "Lord GOD" (with "GOD" in small caps representing the underlying divine name). Functionally, this double title intensifies the solemnity of divine proclamations, emphasizing that the speaker possesses ultimate royal dominion over every subject, nation, and force in existence.
