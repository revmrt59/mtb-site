LORD - ( ye.ho.vah , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun yhwh is the proper personal name of the God of Israel. Lexically, it functions as the distinct divine name revealed to God's covenant people. In Obadiah 1:1, yhwh identifies the sovereign divine Author behind the prophet's message concerning Edom. By grounding the prophetic vision in the personal name of Israel's God, the text establishes that the judgment pronounced against Edom is not a product of human hostility, but the authoritative decree of the supreme Ruler of the nations.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The term yhwh is the unique personal name of God in the Hebrew Bible, set apart from generic terms for deity such as el or elohim. Historically revealed in connection with God's redemptive work for Israel, yhwh designates the God who makes Himself known to His people and interacts with human history. In English translations such as the NKJV, yhwh is rendered in small capital letters as "LORD," or as "GOD" when preceded by the divine title Adonai ("Lord"). In Obadiah 1:1, yhwh occurs alongside Adonai within the standard prophetic messenger formula ("Thus says the Lord GOD"), signaling that the prophet speaks as an authorized messenger of the Almighty.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, yhwh is a proper noun denoting the personal divine name of God (Layer 1). Grammatically, the target occurrence in Obadiah 1:1 is a masculine singular proper noun in the absolute state.

Careful lexical distinction requires separating the name itself from the rich theological truths associated with it across the biblical canon. While broader canonical revelation connects yhwh with God's self-existence, immutability, and covenant faithfulness (Layer 3), the lexical core of the lemma itself functions specifically as God's proper name. It identifies who God is in contrast to false regional deities, serving as the personal subject of divine actions, declarations, and covenant relationships.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, yhwh appears extensively in narrative, legal, wisdom, and prophetic texts. In historical narratives, yhwh is the God who acts decisively to deliver, guide, and discipline His people. In the Law, yhwh is the lawgiver who binds Israel to Himself in covenant relationship.

In prophetic literature, yhwh serves as the ultimate source of divine revelation. Prophets regularly introduce their utterances with statements anchored in yhwh, emphasizing that their declarations carry absolute authority. When yhwh speaks, His word possesses creative and judicial force, governing not only Israel but every kingdom and nation on earth.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of yhwh in prophetic pronouncements carries profound canonical and theological significance:

  • Universal Jurisdiction: Although yhwh entered into a specific covenant relationship with Israel, His authority is not localized or national. As the true God, yhwh holds all nations—including Edom—morally accountable to His righteous standards.

  • The Ground of Prophetic Authority: Human messengers possess no inherent authority to pronounce doom upon foreign powers. The validity of Obadiah's prophecy rests entirely on the reality that yhwh has spoken.

  • Divine Righteousness in Judgment: God's judgments are never arbitrary. When yhwh acts against a nation, He does so in perfect holiness and justice, addressing human pride, violence, and malice.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Obadiah 1:1 opens with a formal decree: "The vision of Obadiah. Thus says the Lord GOD concerning Edom..." In this sentence, yhwh functions to establish the supreme authority behind the upcoming message of judgment.

The historical and contextual background of Obadiah involves the pride of Edom, a nation that boasted in its secure mountain strongholds and celebrated the calamity of Judah. From a human perspective, Edom appeared unassailable in its rocky terrain. However, by introducing the prophecy with the divine name yhwh, the text creates a stark contrast between Edom's self-confident security and the absolute sovereignty of Israel's God. The use of yhwh demonstrates that Edom's ultimate reckoning will not come from earthly rivals, but from the Creator and Lord of heaven and earth.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The invocation of yhwh at the start of Obadiah highlights the moral accountability of the nations. Edom shared a historical kinship with Israel through Esau and Jacob, yet Edom acted with cruel hostility during Jerusalem's distress. By declaring that yhwh has spoken, the passage confirms that divine justice reaches into international affairs. No arrogance can withstand the word of yhwh, and no act of injustice escapes His notice.
