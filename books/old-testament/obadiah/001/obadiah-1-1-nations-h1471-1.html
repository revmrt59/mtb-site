NATIONS - ( goy , Strong's H1471 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun goy primary refers to a nation, political body, or distinct group of people defined by territorial, governmental, or cultural parameters. While it can designate any political entity—including Israel—in prophetic literature it frequently refers in the plural form to foreign or Gentile nations surrounding God's covenant people. In Obadiah 1:1, goy (appearing in the plural form goyim) identifies the surrounding Gentile nations summoned by the Lord as instruments of divine judgment against Edom. Its presence underscores Yahweh's universal lordship over non-covenant nations, demonstrating that even foreign powers serve His sovereign decree to humble Edom's pride.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The noun goy denotes a cohesive national or political body. In ancient Near Eastern context, human groupings were understood through tribal affiliations, family lineages, or defined territorial states. Hebrew possesses distinct terms for social aggregation: while 'am emphasizes relational, blood, or covenant ties among a people, goy highlights the political, institutional, or territorial expression of a nation.

In the prophetic narrative of Obadiah, this political orientation is vital. Obadiah pronounces judgment against Edom, a nation descended from Esau and historically related to Israel. Edom had relied on its natural mountain fortresses and regional political alliances, participating in prideful hostility against Judah during Jerusalem's distress. The opening verse establishes that Yahweh's authority extends beyond Judah's borders, exercising administrative command over the foreign goyim to execute His righteous decree against Edom.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, goy signifies a "nation," "people group," or "body politic."

  • Basic Meaning: A distinct collective group of human beings forming a political or territorial entity.

  • Plural Usage (goyim): Frequently translated as "nations," "Gentiles," or "heathen," referring to populations outside the covenant community of Israel.

  • Spectrum of Meaning:

  • A formal, political nation state under a ruling structure.

  • Foreign Gentile peoples distinguished from Israel.

  • Israel itself when viewed as a defined national entity established by God.

The word does not inherently carry a derogatory or moral condemnation within its lexical core; rather, its moral character is determined by how a given nation relates to Yahweh and His righteous standards.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament literature, goy functions across several distinct contexts:

  • National Designation for Israel: God promises Abraham that He will make of him a great goy and later forms Israel into a "holy nation." In these contexts, the focus rests on Israel's status as a distinct, organized body under divine law.

  • Foreign Gentile Powers: Most frequently in the prophetic texts, the plural form designates non-Israelite nations. These nations are depicted as historical actors, sometimes used by Yahweh to discipline Israel, and at other times brought under divine reckoning for their own injustice and idolatry.

  • Subjects of Sovereign Redemptive Plan: Prophetic literature frequently looks forward to a time when the foreign nations (goyim) will recognize the God of Israel, seek His instruction, and share in the blessing promised through Abraham's line.

4. Theological Implications

The usage of goy in prophetic literature establishes critical theological doctrines concerning God's universal sovereignty and human accountability:

  • Universal Lordship: Yahweh is not a localized deity whose influence stops at the border of Judah. He rules over all goyim, directing international movements according to His sovereign will.

  • Instrumental Providence: God uses human political powers—even those that do not acknowledge Him—to execute His sovereign purposes in history.

  • Impartial Divine Justice: Pride, betrayal, and violence committed by any nation bring divine judgment. Edom's familial relation to Israel provided no immunity from the universal moral order governed by Yahweh.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:1, the noun appears in the masculine plural form in the absolute state. Obadiah begins his prophecy by framing his message as a divine revelation: "Thus says the Lord GOD concerning Edom." Immediately following this declaration, the text states, "We have heard a report from the LORD, And an envoy has been sent among the nations: 'Arise, and let us rise up against her for battle.'"

The grammatical form (plural, absolute) indicates a multiplicity of foreign powers. Yahweh dispatches a divine messenger or herald among these surrounding Gentile nations (goyim) to mobilize them into action. Edom believed her high rocky dwellings and strategic alliances rendered her invincible. However, the text reveals that the very surrounding goyim whom Edom might have regarded as peers or allies are mobilized by Yahweh to assemble against her in battle.

The word goyim here establishes that international geopolitics move at the command of the God of Israel. Edom's ultimate undoing does not come by mere chance or local conflict, but through Yahweh's sovereign summoning of foreign nations to execute His judgment.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Psalms 2:1 – "Why do the nations rage, And the people plot a vain thing?" The plural noun identifies earthly political kingdoms arrayed in futile rebellion against Yahweh and His anointed King.

  • Psalms 33:10 – "The LORD brings the counsel of the nations to nothing; He makes the plans of the peoples of no effect." The word highlights international political powers whose designs are frustrated by divine sovereignty.

  • Jeremiah 1:10 – "See, I have this day set you over the nations and over the kingdoms, To root out and to pull down..." Used to describe the broad international scope of Jeremiah’s prophetic commission under Yahweh's authority.

  • Ezekiel 36:36 – "Then the nations which are left all around you shall know that I, the LORD, have rebuilt the ruined places..." Refers to the surrounding foreign peoples who observe Yahweh's faithful restoration of Israel.

  • Isaiah 52:15 – "So shall He sprinkle many nations. Kings shall shut their mouths at Him..." Describes foreign nations standing in awe before the unexpected redemptive work of the Servant of the Lord.

7. Other Relevant Insights

A meaningful distinction exists in Hebrew between goy and 'am. While 'am emphasizes kinship, family ties, and personal covenant connection, goy centers on the formal, political, or territorial entity of a people group.

In Obadiah, this distinction enhances the tragedy of Edom's conduct. Edom was related to Judah by blood through Esau and Jacob, yet Edom abandoned fraternal obligation ('am) and acted as a hostile foreign power. Consequently, Yahweh dispatches foreign nations (goyim) to bring Edom to account. The broader canonical witness demonstrates that while foreign nations often act in hostility against God's purposes, Yahweh's ultimate intention involves bringing the goyim to acknowledge His reign and receive His blessing.
