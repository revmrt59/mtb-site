REPORT - ( she.mu.ah , Strong's H8052 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun shemuʿah denotes news, a report, a message, or tidings brought from an outside source. It describes information received through hearing, ranging from human news of war and fame to authoritative divine announcements delivered by God's messengers. In Obadiah 1:1, shemuʿah refers to a divinely initiated report heard by the prophets, announcing Yahweh's sovereign summons to the nations to execute His judgment against Edom. It establishes that Edom's downfall is not the result of random geopolitical strife, but the realization of an authoritative decree issued directly from the LORD.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The noun shemuʿah is derived from the common Hebrew verb shamaʿ, which signifies the act of hearing, listening, or obeying. As a noun, shemuʿah refers to that which is heard—a report, rumor, message, or tidings. In the culture and ancient setting of the Old Testament, where oral communication was the primary vehicle for transmitting urgent news across distances, a shemuʿah carried substantial weight. It represented dispatch from afar, whether brought by a runner reporting battle outcomes, a traveler recounting the fame of a monarch, or a prophet receiving a direct revelation from God.

In prophetic contexts, shemuʿah functions as a technical term for a divine message or report communicated by Yahweh to His spokesmen. When used of God's speech, it emphasizes that the prophetic word is not human invention or political analysis, but objective news originating from the heavenly court.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, shemuʿah refers to information communicated orally and received by hearing. Standard Hebrew lexicons (such as BDB and TWOT) define the term as:

  • A report or tidings (whether good or bad).

  • News or announcement brought from a distance.

  • A divine message or revelation received by a prophet.

The term encompasses a broad semantic range depending on context. It can describe military news regarding conflict, public rumors regarding tragic events, reports of a ruler's renown, or a solemn message of judgment sent forth by Yahweh.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Hebrew Scriptures, shemuʿah appears in narrative, wisdom, and prophetic contexts:

  • Narrative Literature: The word frequently designates urgent news arriving from a battlefield or distant region, such as news concerning the death of royal family members or national defeat. It can also describe widespread report or fame, such as the tidings brought to Solomon regarding his international renown.

  • Wisdom Literature: In the Psalms and Proverbs, shemuʿah highlights the psychological and spiritual impact of news on the human heart. It can refer to "evil tidings" that test a person's faith or "good news" from a far country that brings refreshment to a weary soul.

  • Prophetic Literature: In the prophets, the term often takes on a solemn theological nuance. It describes the divine decree dispatched by God against prideful nations or the prophetic message regarding Yahweh's redemptive work. In judgment contexts, the arrival of a divine shemuʿah creates panic among the wicked, whereas for the prophet, it represents the authoritative word of God that must be declared.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical use of shemuʿah carries profound theological significance:

  • The Sovereignty of God Over Nations: When a shemuʿah originates "from the LORD," it demonstrates that God exercises total control over international history. Geopolitical shifts and military conflicts are not driven merely by human ambition, but by the execution of Yahweh's sovereign word.

  • The Authority of Prophetic Revelation: By framing prophetic vision as a shemuʿah from God, Scripture affirms that true prophecy is received, not constructed. Prophets are hearers and heralds of news established in heaven before it manifests on earth.

  • The Necessity of Faith: Human responsibility in scripture is frequently tied to how one responds to a shemuʿah. Hearing a divine report demands trust and submission; refusing to believe God's message results in spiritual blindness and judgment.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:1, shemuʿah appears in the feminine singular absolute form. Obadiah introduces his vision against Edom by declaring: "We have heard a report [shemuʿah] from the LORD, And an envoy has been sent among the nations."

The context highlights several vital nuances:

  • Divine Origin: The phrase "from the LORD" specifies the ultimate source of this report. Obadiah's prophecy against Edom is not motivated by personal animosity or political bias from Judah; it is divine news sent straight from Yahweh.

  • Corporate Prophetic Reception: The plural subject ("We have heard") indicates that this divine report was recognized by God's prophetic messengers. It reflects a shared consensus in the prophetic tradition regarding God's impending decree against Edom.

  • Summons to Sovereign Judgment: The shemuʿah is immediately linked with the sending of an envoy to rally the nations for battle against Edom. Edom had arrogantly gloated over Jerusalem's destruction and mistreated her brother Jacob. The shemuʿah serves as the official heavenly announcement that Edom's pride will be brought low through God's sovereign ordering of international forces.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Jeremiah 49:14 — "I have heard a report from the LORD, And an envoy has been sent to the nations: 'Gather together, come against her, And rise up to battle!'"

  • Isaiah 53:1 — "Who has believed our report? And to whom has the arm of the LORD been revealed?"

  • Psalms 112:7 — "He will not be afraid of evil tidings; His heart is steadfast, trusting in the LORD."

  • Proverbs 25:25 — "As cold water to a weary soul, So is good news from a far country."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Parallel in Jeremiah: The striking literary parallel between Obadiah 1:1 and Jeremiah 49:14 demonstrates a shared prophetic vocabulary regarding Edom. Both prophets utilize shemuʿah to convey that Yahweh's decree against Edom was established and circulated across the prophetic community.

  • Receptive Heart vs. Fear: In Psalm 112:7, the righteous person is preserved from the dread of bad news (shemuʿah) because his heart trusts in Yahweh. Conversely, for nations living in rebellion like Edom, a divine shemuʿah signals unavoidable destruction.

  • Messianic Weight: In Isaiah 53:1, the failure of Israel to believe the prophetic shemuʿah underscores that human beings often reject God's report when it contradicts their expectations. Yet, whether believed or rejected, God's report remains absolute truth.
