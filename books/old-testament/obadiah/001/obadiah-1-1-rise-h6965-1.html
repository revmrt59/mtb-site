RISE - ( qum , Strong's H6965 )

Summary:

The primary physical meaning of the Hebrew verb qum is to stand up, rise from a seated or prone position, or erect a structure. In dynamic or metaphorical contexts, it frequently describes taking decisive action, initiating a military campaign, rising up in judgment, or establishing a covenant or decree. In Obadiah 1:1, qum expresses a collective summons to war, as nations mobilize under divine providence to execute judgment against Edom. It reveals that human military mobilization against prideful oppression ultimately fulfills Yahweh's sovereign decree.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root qum is one of the most common ancient Semitic verbs expressing vertical motion, posture change, or the initiation of activity. In the ancient Near Eastern cultural setting, sitting often represented rest, subjection, or administrative deliberation, whereas rising signified the assumption of authority, preparation for work, or entry into battle.

In Israel’s covenantal and historical narrative, rising up is frequently linked to divine initiative. When God "rises," He steps into human history to deliver the oppressed or execute righteous judgment. Conversely, when people "rise," they either respond to a divine summons or take concerted action, whether righteous or rebellious. In prophetic literature, the term carries strong solemnity, marking the transition from quiet forbearance to active judicial oversight.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

At its foundational lexical level, qum denotes moving into an upright posture—to stand up or rise. From this concrete physical concept, its semantic range develops along several distinct lines:

  • Physical Posture and Movement: To stand up from sitting or lying down, or to reach an erect position.

  • Initiation of Action: To set about doing something, to bestir oneself, or to prepare for a task.

  • Military Mobilization: To assemble, prepare, and march for combat against an enemy.

  • Legal and Theological Establishment: To stand firm, be established, endure, or come to pass (as with a vow, word, or covenant).

  • Elevation to Position or Office: To raise up a leader, king, prophet, or deliverer.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Hebrew Scriptures, qum appears in narrative, legal, poetic, and prophetic contexts. Its specific nuance is dictated by the subjects and syntactic framework in which it operates:

  • Judicial and Sovereign Action: When applied to God, qum stresses His active intervention in human affairs. When Yahweh rises, human pride is shattered and divine righteousness is vindicated.

  • Covenantal Surety: In legal or covenantal settings, the verb signifies permanence and fulfillment. A promise or prophecy that "stands" (qum) is one that achieves realization and remains steadfast.

  • Military Engagement: In conflict narratives, qum marks the shift from strategy to execution. Soldiers rise up to attack or defend, signaling the onset of hostilies.

In terms of morphology, the verb appears in various stems. In the Qal stem, it describes the simple action of rising or standing up on the part of the subject.

4. Theological Implications

The theological significance of qum centres on divine sovereignty and the certainty of God's purpose. Human plans may be proposed, but only what God establishes will truly stand. When human powers rise up in hubris against God or His people, their ascension is temporary; God's subsequent rising in judgment brings their collapse.

Furthermore, qum highlights God’s sovereign control over international history. pagan nations do not act in a theological vacuum. Even when foreign armies gather and march, their mobilization can serve as the instrument of divine righteous decree. The verb emphasizes that history is not driven by blind fate or autonomous human ambition, but by the sovereign God who summons kingdoms to carry out His righteous sovereign will.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:1, the prophet introduces a vision concerning Edom, prefaced by a report from Yahweh: "A messenger has been sent among the nations, saying, 'Arise, and let us rise up against her in battle.'"

The target word "rise" translates qum in the Qal stem, imperfect form, 1st person plural. Syntactically, it functions as a rallying call spoken by the nations among themselves in response to the divine envoy. The 1st person plural form ("let us rise up") highlights unified, collective action. The nations encourage one another to mobilize their military forces against Edom.

Contextually, Edom had gloated over Judah's calamity and participated in violence against her brother Jacob. Edom believed her high mountain fortresses rendered her secure and invincible. However, Yahweh dispatched an envoy to stir up surrounding nations. The nations' call to "rise up" against Edom is the historical mechanism through which God brings low the arrogant. The word demonstrates that while the nations believe they are acting out of their own strategic interests, they are actually fulfilling the word of Yahweh, who brings down the proud and defends His covenant people.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In prophetic discourse, the pairing of divine decrees with human speech creates a powerful contrast between divine initiative and human response. In Obadiah 1:1, the heavenly proclamation sets earthly history in motion. The nations respond to the divine envoy by exhorting each other to "rise up." This reveals the biblical doctrine of concurrence: God's sovereign intent is realized through the voluntary decisions and actions of human agents. The nations willingly assemble for war, yet their gathering is the direct execution of God's sentence against Edom's pride.
