SAYS - ( a.mar , Strong's H559 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'amar basic lexical meaning refers to the act of speaking, uttering, declaring, or saying something, and can also denote internal speech (thinking or intending). In Obadiah 1:1, this word appears in the foundational messenger formula ("Thus says the Lord GOD"), serving to establish that the prophet Obadiah is not delivering his own personal sentiments or political opinions regarding Edom, but is instead conveying the direct, authoritative, and binding decree of the Sovereign Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root 'amar is one of the most common verbs in Hebrew literature, appearing across every genre of the Old Testament, including narrative, law, poetry, wisdom, and prophecy. Its standard function is to communicate spoken language, whether between human beings or from God to mankind. In ancient Near Eastern treaty and diplomatic contexts, messengers delivered instructions or decrees using a fixed formula that began with the equivalent of "Thus says [the king or lord]." In Israel's prophetic tradition, this standard diplomatic phrase was taken up under divine inspiration to signal that the prophet acted as God's official herald. In Obadiah 1:1, the verb introduces the divine judgment against Edom, framing the entire prophecy as royal divine speech.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, 'amar denotes the expression of words or thoughts. Its semantic range encompasses:

  • To speak, say, or utter audible words.

  • To declare, proclaim, or command with authority.

  • To say to oneself, intend, or think within one's mind.

In the context of divine communication, particularly when combined with the particle koh ("thus"), 'amar functions as a formal declaration indicating that what follows is the verbatim utterance or sovereign determination of the speaker.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament narrative and prophecy, 'amar indicates the transition from thought or purpose to verbalized communication. When human beings speak, 'amar registers simple dialogue, requests, or declarations. When God speaks, His speech carries creative power, covenantal obligation, or judicial certainty.

In prophetic literature, 'amar frequently introduces divine oracles. For example, in Ezekiel 17:3, the same Hebrew word introduces a parabolic message of judgment ("Thus says the Lord GOD"), underscoring that the vision comes directly from God. Similarly, in Jeremiah 6:9, the term introduces divine instruction to the prophet ("Thus says the LORD of hosts"). Rather than merely describing a past conversation, 'amar in these contexts functions as an authoritative signpost, calling the hearer to reverent attention before the sovereign Word.

4. Theological Implications

The theological significance of 'amar in prophetic introductions rests on the doctrine of divine revelation and the absolute authority of God's Word. When the Scriptures record "Thus says the Lord," several major theological truths are asserted:

  • Objective Revelation: God's word is not a vague human impression or psychological feeling; it is clear, propositional speech given by God Himself.

  • Sovereign Authority: God's utterances are not mere suggestions. When God speaks, His decree carries the full weight of His divine majesty and holy character.

  • Delegation and Inspiration: The prophet serves strictly as an ambassador. The authority of the message resides entirely in the divine Sender, not in the human instrument who transmits it.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:1, the verb 'amar appears in the Qal stem, perfect form, third-person masculine singular, translated "says" in the prophetic intro formula "Thus says the Lord GOD."

The opening line of Obadiah establishes a dual attribution: "The vision of Obadiah. Thus says the Lord GOD concerning Edom." While the vision was received and transcribed through Obadiah, the message itself originates from Adonai YHWH (the Lord GOD). The use of the perfect form in this opening formula presents the divine utterance as an established, settled decree.

This opening is critical to the message of Obadiah. Edom had acted with pride and violence against Judah during her calamity. The prophet does not approach Edom with personal resentment or nationalistic bluster. By invoking 'amar in the royal messenger formula, Obadiah roots the upcoming declaration of judgment in the sovereign will and righteousness of God Himself. Edom's pride will be brought down not merely because Obadiah wills it, but because the Sovereign Lord has spoken it.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 22:16: "'By Myself I have sworn, says the LORD, because you have done this thing, and have not withheld your son, your only son...'"

  • Deuteronomy 1:42: "And the LORD said to me, 'Tell them, "Do not go up nor fight, for I am not among you; lest you be defeated before your enemies."'"

  • Jeremiah 6:9: "Thus says the LORD of hosts: 'They shall thoroughly glean as a vine the remnant of Israel; pass your hand again over the branches as a grape-gatherer.'"

  • Ezekiel 17:3: "...and say, 'Thus says the Lord GOD: "A great eagle with large wings and long pinions, full of feathers of various colors, came to Lebanon and took from the cedar the highest branch."'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

In ancient heraldic customs, when an ambassador declared "Thus says the king," the audience was expected to receive the message as if the king himself were standing before them in person. By opening his brief prophecy with 'amar under divine inspiration, Obadiah demands immediate accountability from his hearers. The fulfillment of the prophecy against Edom does not depend on human political power, but on the unshakeable veracity of the God who declared it.
