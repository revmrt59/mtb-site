VISION - ( cha.zon , Strong's H2377 )

Summary:

The noun chazon refers to a divine revelation, prophetic vision, or authoritative oracle granted by God to a prophet. It denotes both the supernatural mode of communication and the objective message communicated. In Obadiah 1:1, chazon functions as the title and opening declaration of the entire book, establishing that Obadiah's prophecy against Edom is not a product of human malice or political speculation, but an authoritative, God-given revelation of divine judgment and sovereign triumph.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew noun chazon is derived from the root verb chazah, which signifies perceiving, beholding, or seeing in a spiritual or visionary sense. While physical sight is expressed by other standard Hebrew terms, chazon specifically designates the spiritual perception or divine disclosure granted to seers and prophets.

In the Old Testament, the word appears in historical narratives, wisdom literature, and extensively within prophetic books. It serves both to describe individual visionary experiences and to function as a formal title or superscription for complete prophetic writings. The term emphasizes the objective reality of God’s self-disclosure to His chosen messengers, contrasting divine revelation with human imagination.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, chazon refers to a revelation, vision, or prophetic oracle given by God. Standard Hebrew lexicons (such as Brown-Driver-Briggs and the Theological Wordbook of the Old Testament) define the word primarily as:

  • A divine communication or revelation granted to a prophet.

  • A prophetic vision, whether received in a trance, a dream, or through conscious spiritual perception.

  • An authoritative prophetic oracle or collected prophecy recorded as Scripture.

The term encompasses both the act of revelation and the content of the message delivered. It does not refer to speculative human foresight or personal ambition, but to God revealing His mind and purpose to humanity.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament literature, chazon carries several distinct nuances depending on context:

  • Formal Title for Prophecy: The word often introduces an entire prophetic work (as seen in Obadiah 1:1 and Nahum 1:1). In this setting, it establishes the divine authority of the written words that follow.

  • Presence or Absence of Revelation: In periods of spiritual decline or transition, the availability of a chazon indicates God's active guidance, whereas its absence signifies judgment or spiritual famine (e.g., 1 Samuel 3:1; Proverbs 29:18).

  • True versus False Revelation: True chazon comes directly from God. False prophets are condemned when they proclaim a "vision" generated from their own minds or hearts rather than received from the mouth of the Lord (e.g., Jeremiah 23:16; Ezekiel 13:16).

4. Theological Implications

The usage of chazon underlines critical biblical doctrines regarding revelation and divine sovereignty:

  • Divine Origin of Scripture: Chazon highlights that true prophetic speech originates with God. The human prophet is the recipient and transmitter, not the author, of the revelation.

  • Sovereign Judgment and History: Prophetic visions frequently unveil God’s sovereign control over nations and history. Through chazon, God reveals His righteous assessments and impending judgments long before human circumstances appear to render them likely.

  • Spiritual Order and Restraint: Divine revelation provides the necessary moral and spiritual foundation for God's covenant community. Without divine instruction, human society devolves into self-directed lawlessness.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:1, chazon appears in the masculine singular construct state, grammatically bound to the proper name Obadiah ("The vision of Obadiah"). The immediate continuation of the verse—"Thus says the Lord GOD concerning Edom"—functions alongside this title to clarify the precise relationship between God, the prophet, and the message.

The construct relationship connects the vision to Obadiah as its human vessel, while the formula "Thus says the Lord GOD" identifies Yahweh as the ultimate Source. The historical context involves Edom's arrogant gloating and participation in the affliction of Judah during a period of calamity. By introducing the prophecy as a chazon, the text asserts that the impending downfall of Edom and the restoration of God's people are guaranteed by divine decree. The word elevates the short book from a local conflict or human grievance to a cosmic declaration of God's righteous rule and covenant faithfulness.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Samuel 3:1 – "Now the boy Samuel ministered to the LORD before Eli. And the word of the LORD was rare in those days; there was no widespread vision."

  • Proverbs 29:18 – "Where there is no revelation [vision], the people cast off restraint; but happy is he who keeps the law."

  • Jeremiah 23:16 – "Thus says the LORD of hosts: 'Do not listen to the words of the prophets who prophesy to you. They make you worthless; they speak a vision of their own heart, not from the mouth of the LORD.'"

  • Nahum 1:1 – "The burden against Nineveh. The book of the vision of Nahum the Elkoshite."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Function as a Canonical Superscription: The use of chazon in Obadiah 1:1 as a header places the short single-chapter book on equal theological footing with longer prophetic works. It signals to the reading community that the text is holy, authoritative Scripture.

  • Critique of False Prophets: In prophetic polemics (such as Ezekiel 13:16 and Jeremiah 23:16), the claim to have a chazon was evaluated strictly by its source. The biblical text exposes false visions as human delusion, reinforcing that a true chazon must proceed from God's word.

  • Grammatical Precision: The construct form in Obadiah 1:1 succinctly links the messenger to the divine word without obscuring the true author; Obadiah acts as the steward of a vision that belongs entirely to the Sovereign Lord.
