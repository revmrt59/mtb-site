COVER - (kâçâh, Strong's H3680)

Summary:

The primary lexical meaning of the Hebrew verb kasah is to cover, conceal, clothe, or overwhelm. Depending on context, it describes a physical covering, such as a cloud enveloping a structure or water submerging an object, or a figurative covering, such as shame or disgrace engulfing a person or group. In Obadiah 1:10, kasah describes the complete public disgrace that will overwhelm the nation of Edom as divine retribution for their treacherous violence against their brother nation, Judah. By declaring that "shame shall cover you," the prophetic message emphasizes that Edom's arrogant pride will be replaced by total humiliation.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root kasah is a foundational Hebrew verb used across the Hebrew Bible in legal, historical, poetic, and prophetic literature. Its basic concept involves placing a layer over something to hide, protect, clothe, or overwhelm it.

In the short prophetic book of Obadiah, the prophet addresses the nation of Edom, descendants of Esau. When Jerusalem fell to Babylonian invaders, Edom did not merely stand aloof; they actively gloated over Judah's downfall, cut off fugitives, and assisted in plundering their brother nation. In response to this betrayal of fraternal obligation, God issued a severe sentence of judgment. In Obadiah 1:10, kasah appears in God's formal indictment and sentence: because Edom mistreated "brother Jacob," public shame will thoroughly cover them.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, kasah encompasses a broad range of nuance depending on the stem and context:

  • To cover or clothe (e.g., placing garments over the body or spread coverings over sacred items).

  • To conceal or hide (e.g., keeping a matter secret, hiding guilt, or concealing evidence).

  • To submerge or overwhelm (e.g., waters covering the sea floor or a flood overwhelming a landscape).

  • To envelop figuratively (e.g., being engulfed by shame, reproach, fear, or confusion).

In the intensive Piel stem, the verb frequently carries an active or thorough sense—meaning to completely cover, hide from view, or submerge.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout Old Testament literature, kasah operates in both physical and figurative contexts. Physically, it describes God's glory or a cloud covering the tabernacle, waters covering the sea bed, or a person putting on a robe.

Figuratively, the word often appears in wisdom and prophetic texts regarding human conduct and divine judgment:

  • Concealing Sin or Iniquity: The verb describes human efforts to hide transgressions from public view or God's refusal to let unrepentant sin remain hidden.

  • Overwhelmed by Disgrace: Prophets and psalmists frequently use kasah to describe emotional or social realities, where feelings or public manifestations of shame (boshet), reproach, or terror completely envelope a defeated transgressor.

Care must be taken not to confuse kasah with other theological roots like kaphar (often translated "make atonement"). While kasah can describe covering sin, its fundamental idea remains the act of placing something over an object or enveloping a person, rather than ritual expiation.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of kasah in Obadiah 1:10 rests upon the principle of divine justice and appropriate retribution (lex talionis). Edom exalted itself in pride, believing its rocky strongholds were invincible, and attempted to exploit Judah's exposed vulnerability. In response, God reverses their status:

  • Human pride exposed vs. divine shame covering: Edom sought to cover themselves in honor and glory at Judah's expense; instead, God sentenced them to be completely covered in shame.

  • Unchecked violence against covenant kin brings unavoidable disgrace: God holds nations—especially those with shared ancestral bonds—accountable for arrogance and cruelty.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:10, the author uses kasah in the Piel stem, imperfect form, 3rd person feminine singular ("shame shall cover you"). The feminine singular subject matching the verb is "shame" (boshet).

The Piel stem highlights the thoroughness of the action: shame will not merely touch Edom, but completely envelop the nation. The imperfect form functions within the prophetic sentence to express the dynamic action of this coming reality.

The immediate context heightens this contrast. Edom was guilty of "violence against your brother Jacob." The use of the word "brother" highlights the unnatural betrayal committed by Esau's descendants against Jacob's offspring. Consequently, instead of enjoying the fruit of their arrogance, Edom will be enveloped by public humiliation (boshet) and ultimately "cut off forever."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Micah 7:10 – "Then she who is my enemy will see, and shame will cover her who said to me, 'Where is the LORD your God?'"

  • Jeremiah 51:51 – "We are confounded because we have heard reproach. Shame has covered our faces, for strangers have come into the sanctuaries of the LORD's house."

  • Exodus 40:34 – "Then the cloud covered the tabernacle of meeting, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle."

  • Nehemiah 4:5 – "Do not cover their iniquity, and do not let their sin be blotted out from before You; for they have provoked You to anger before the builders."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The personification of "shame" (boshet) as an active mantle that wraps around Edom provides a vivid image of total defeatedness. In the Ancient Near East, honor and shame were dominant cultural metrics. To be "covered in shame" was not merely a private psychological state, but a public, permanent loss of status, prestige, and national viability. The prophetic declaration guarantees that Edom's self-exaltation will end in total social and political ruin.
