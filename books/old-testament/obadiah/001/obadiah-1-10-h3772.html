CUT OFF - ( karath , Strong's H3772 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb karath fundamentally means to sever, slice, or cut down. While widely recognized for its association with "cutting" (establishing) a covenant, its use in judgment contexts signifies complete, permanent excision, destruction, or extermination from a community, land, or existence. In Obadiah 1:10, it describes the absolute and irreversible erasure of Edom as a nation due to their malicious violence against their brother Jacob.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

Literally, karath refers to the physical act of cutting down trees, felling timber, or severing a body part. It carries the vivid picture of a sharp blade severing an object from its source of life, support, or connection. When a branch is cut off from a vine or a tree is chopped down, its life source is instantly severed, leading to rapid decay and permanent death. This physical imagery transitions seamlessly into legal, covenantal, and judgment metaphors in the Old Testament.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb karath occurs over 280 times in the Hebrew Scriptures. Its semantic range includes:

• To cut off, cut down, or fell (such as trees, forests, or idolatrous images).

• To eliminate, exterminate, destroy, or wipe out (people, names, or descendants).

• To make or "cut" a covenant, which refers to the ancient Near Eastern practice of slaughtering and cutting in half sacrificial animals to ratify an agreement.

• In passive forms, it denotes being excluded, banished, or executed, often as a divine penalty for violating God's holiness or covenant commands.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Covenant-Cutting: The common idiom "to make a covenant" (karath berith) derives from the practice of walking between the split halves of slaughtered animals (Genesis 15). This acted as a graphic warning: "May I be cut in pieces like these animals if I break this covenant." Thus, karath is intrinsically tied to life-and-death consequences.

• Divine Excision (The Karet Penalty): In the Pentateuch, to be "cut off from his people" is a severe divine judgment where God Himself removes an individual from the covenant community, resulting in premature death, exile, or the termination of their family line.

• Judicial Felling: Like a majestic cedar chopped down by an axe, prideful nations and rulers are "cut down" by God's sovereign judgment.

4. Theological Implications

Divine justice is absolute. When God cuts off a nation or individual, it is not a temporary discipline but an ultimate, sovereign termination of their historical legacy and future. It highlights the contrast between covenant protection and covenant rejection. Israel is disciplined but promised a remnant, whereas hostile pagan nations like Edom face an absolute karath—utter obliteration without a future remnant. Because Edom touched God's anointed people with violent contempt, they triggered the self-maledictory curse of the covenant, leading to their own excision.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:10, God pronounces: "For violence against your brother Jacob, shame shall cover you, and you shall be cut off forever."

• Edom (descended from Esau) stood by and actively participated in the destruction of Jerusalem by the Babylonians, gloating over his brother Jacob's ruin and cutting off Judah's escape routes.

• The phrase "cut off forever" (karath le'olam) contrasts sharply with Israel's temporary exile. Israel would return; Edom would be completely extinguished from history.

• This was fulfilled historically. The Nabataeans eventually pushed the Edomites out of their rock fortress of Petra, and by the end of the first century AD, the Edomites (Idumeans) completely disappeared from the historical record.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 15:18: "On the same day the LORD made a covenant with Abram, saying: 'To your descendants I have given this land...'"

• Leviticus 17:10: "And whatever man of the house of Israel, or of the strangers who dwell among you, who eats any blood, I will set My face against that person who eats blood, and will cut him off from among his people."

• Psalm 34:16: "The face of the LORD is against those who do evil, To cut off the remembrance of them from the earth."

• Zechariah 13:8: "And it shall come to pass in all the land," Says the LORD, "That two-thirds in it shall be cut off and die, But one-third shall be left in it."

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a profound, tragic irony in Obadiah's prophecy. Edom actively sought to "cut off" the survivors of Judah at the crossroads as they fled from the Babylonians (Obadiah 1:14). Because they sought to physically sever and eliminate the remnant of Israel, God meted out the exact same measure of justice. Edom itself was "cut off" forever, demonstrating the principle of divine retribution (lex talionis): what you have done to others will be done to you.
