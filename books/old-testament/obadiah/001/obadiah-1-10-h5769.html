FOREVER - ( olam , Strong's H5769 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word olam refers to a long duration, time out of mind, antiquity, or futurity. In the context of divine judgment, it carries the weight of irreversible and permanent consequence. In Obadiah 1:10, it describes the finality of Edom's destruction—not merely a temporary setback, but a complete and perpetual removal from history as a distinct national entity because of their prideful violence against Israel.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The root of olam is historically connected to the concept of that which is hidden, secret, or beyond the horizon. It points to a horizon of time where the end is out of sight. It does not always represent philosophical "eternity" (time without beginning or end) but rather a duration whose boundary is indefinite, unlimited, or continuous. It is the vast span of time that stretches beyond human vision, whether looking backward into the distant past or forward into the unending future.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Olam indicates a long, indefinite duration, perpetuity, or continuous existence. It can refer to the ancient past (days of old) or an unending future (everlasting).

• When applied to God, His covenants, or His decrees, it denotes absolute, unending perpetuity.

• When applied to human institutions, nations, or physical structures, it signifies a duration that continues for the entirety of that subject's natural lifespan or historical existence, culminating in irreversible permanence.

• In judicial contexts, it marks a sentence or state of ruin that cannot be undone or reversed by human effort.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Olam functions like a vanishing point on the horizon. Just as the physical eye cannot see past the curve of the earth, olam represents a stretch of time whose end is hidden from view or nonexistent.

• It is used to describe the "everlasting hills" (Genesis 49:26), emphasizing stability and endurance relative to the short span of human life.

• It characterizes the "everlasting covenant" (Genesis 17:7), illustrating God's unshakeable, permanent commitment to His people.

• In the context of judgment, it depicts a ruin so complete that the smoke of its destruction rises "forever"—an ongoing testimony of divine wrath and the total termination of the judged entity (Isaiah 34:10).

### 4. Theological Implications

• Divine Justice is Permanent: When God pronounces an olam judgment, it reflects His sovereign authority to permanently alter history. Human empires rise and fall, but when God decrees the end of a nation like Edom, no geopolitical maneuvering can resurrect it.

• The Contrast of God's Covenants: While God's covenant with Israel is olam (everlasting in grace, restoration, and life), His judgment upon the unrepentant enemies of His people is olam (everlasting in destruction and cutting off). This highlights the dual nature of God's holiness—protecting His own while permanently dismantling those who seek to destroy them.

• Brotherly Betrayal and Divine Vindication: Edom's sin was against "your brother Jacob." God's permanent cutting off of Edom shows that familial betrayal and cruelty against the covenant community are met with irreversible divine retribution.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Obadiah 1:10, olam modifies the verb "cut off" (karat). The phrase "cut off forever" (tikkaret l'olam) carries intense covenantal weight. Karat is the word used for cutting a covenant, but here it is used in the negative sense of being excised or severed.

• Edom (the descendants of Esau) took pride in their high, secure rocky fortresses, believing they were invincible. But God declares that they will not just experience a temporary defeat; they will be totally extinguished.

• Historically, this was fulfilled. Though the Nabataeans pushed them out, and they later became the Idumeans of the New Testament era, after the destruction of Jerusalem in A.D. 70, the Idumeans completely vanished from history. They were "cut off forever" as a distinct people group.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 9:16 - "The rainbow shall be in the cloud, and I will look for it to remember the everlasting (olam) covenant between God and every living creature of all flesh that is on the earth."

• Psalm 90:2 - "Before the mountains were brought forth, Or ever You had formed the earth and the world, Even from everlasting (olam) to everlasting (olam), You are God."

• Isaiah 26:4 - "Trust in the Lord forever (olam), For in YAH, the Lord, is everlasting (olam) strength."

• Jeremiah 51:39 - "In their excitement I will prepare their feasts; I will make them drunk, That they may rejoice, And sleep a perpetual (olam) sleep And not awake,' says the Lord."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• Covenant Contrast: It is highly significant that Jacob's descendants (Israel) were also judged and exiled, but God promised them restoration. Edom, however, received no promise of restoration. Their cutting off was olam, whereas Israel's discipline was temporary.

• Edom as an Archetype: In prophetic literature, Edom often serves as an archetype of the prideful, hostile world system that opposes God and persecutes His people. The "forever" destruction of Edom prefigures the ultimate, eternal destruction of all powers that stand against the Kingdom of Christ.
