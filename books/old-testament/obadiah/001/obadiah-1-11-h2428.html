FORCES - ( chayil , Strong's H2428 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word chayil encompasses the ideas of strength, power, wealth, and military forces. In the context of Obadiah 1:11, it refers to the resources, wealth, or military strength of Jerusalem that foreign invaders plundered and carried away while Edom stood by as an inactive, complicit spectator. This term highlights the complete stripping of Judah's defense and vitality, emphasizing Edom's profound betrayal of their brother Jacob in his hour of absolute vulnerability.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The root of chayil is associated with twisting or whirling, which points to the gathering of strength, energy, or endurance. In ancient Semitic culture, strength was not a static concept but dynamic, manifesting as physical capability, military might, or material substance. It represents the collective capacity of a nation or individual to secure their existence. For a city like Jerusalem, its chayil was the sum total of its defensive army, its economic wealth, and its young, capable men who could fight and rebuild.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word chayil has a broad semantic range that adapts to its context:

• Physical strength, power, or efficiency.

• An army, host, or military force.

• Wealth, substance, riches, or property.

• Moral strength, virtue, or capability.

In prophetic judgment literature, and specifically in Obadiah 1:11, the word denotes either the military personnel ("forces") or the collective material wealth ("substance") of Jerusalem. Because the parallel line mentions foreigners entering the gates and casting lots, it refers broadly to the vital assets of the city—both its defenders and its treasures—being stripped away.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Military Host: Frequently used to describe Pharaoh's army or the armies of Israel. It portrays a dense, organized gathering of power designed to protect or conquer.

• Material Wealth: Often parallel with gold, silver, and agricultural abundance. In this sense, chayil is the "strength" of a man's labor accumulated over time.

• Valiant Character: Associated with courage and integrity (gibbor chayil or "mighty man of valor"). It denotes a person whose inner strength makes them reliable in crisis.

### 4. Theological Implications

• Chayil reveals that human strength, wealth, and military defenses are ultimately fragile and subject to God's sovereign hand. When God withdraws His protection from His covenant people due to their rebellion, their chayil is easily carried away by "strangers."

• The passage warns against the sin of passive complicity. Edom did not actively conquer Jerusalem, but by watching their brother’s "forces" and wealth be plundered without intervening, they participated in the sin. In God's eyes, indifference in the face of injustice is equivalent to active hostility.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Obadiah 1:11, chayil is translated as "forces" or "wealth." The historical setting is likely the Babylonian siege and destruction of Jerusalem (586 BC).

• As the Babylonians ("strangers" and "foreigners") broke through Jerusalem's defenses, they stripped the city of its chayil—capturing its soldiers and looting its temple and palace treasures.

• Edom, descended from Esau, stood "on the other side" (aloof). Instead of helping their brother Jacob (Judah), they gloated over the loss of Judah’s chayil. The tragedy is doubled: Judah lost its earthly strength, and its closest neighbor acted as an enemy.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 14:28 - "Then the waters returned and covered the chariots, the horsemen, and all the army (chayil) of Pharaoh that came into the sea after them."

• Ruth 3:11 - "And now, my daughter, do not fear. I will do for you all that you request, for all the people of my town know that you are a virtuous (chayil) woman."

• Proverbs 13:22 - "A good man leaves an inheritance to his children’s children, But the wealth (chayil) of the sinner is stored up for the righteous."

• Joel 2:25 - "So I will restore to you the years that the swarming locust has eaten... My great army (chayil) which I sent among you."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between chayil as human strength and divine strength is central to Old Testament theology. Zechariah 4:6 declares, "Not by might (chayil), nor by power, but by My Spirit, says the LORD of hosts." Judah placed its trust in its own chayil, which failed them in the day of calamity. True security lies only in the Lord.
