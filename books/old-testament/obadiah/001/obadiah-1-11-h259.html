ONE - ( 'echad , Strong's H0259 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'echad primarily denotes numerical unity, singularity, or a single entity. In the context of Obadiah 1:11, however, it carries a chilling comparative and covenantal weight. By standing aloof and actively participating in the plunder of Jerusalem, Edom—the brother of Israel—forfeited their familial obligation and became indistinguishable from the pagan invaders. They were counted as "one of them," transforming a word often associated with sacred unity and covenant bond into an indictment of treasonous solidarity with God's enemies.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The cardinal number 'echad is foundational to Hebrew thought, most famously utilized in the Shema of Deuteronomy 6:4 to declare that the Lord is "one." It communicates unity, uniqueness, singularity, and corporate alignment. In ancient Near Eastern culture, the concept of oneness was deeply tied to family solidarity, treaty partnerships, and covenantal bonds. To act in unity with another was to share their identity, purpose, and ultimately, their destiny. In Obadiah 1:11, this concept of oneness is inverted. Instead of being "one" with their brother Jacob in covenantal solidarity, Edom chose to align themselves with the pagan plunderers, becoming "one" with those who sought Israel's destruction.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word 'echad means a single unit, one, first, or each. It indicates a singular person, place, or thing, but can also denote a collective unity where multiple entities function as a single unit. In comparative constructions, when prefixed with the preposition ke ("as" or "like"), ke'achad means "as one" or "like one." In this usage, it signifies complete identification, absorption, or assimilation into a group, making no practical distinction between the subject and the rest of the collective. In Obadiah 1:11, this prefix creates the phrase "as one of them" (ke'achad mehem), meaning Edom became entirely identified with the heathen conquerors in God's ledger of guilt.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, 'echad is employed to paint vivid word pictures of unity and identification:

• Covenantal Unity: In Genesis 2:24, husband and wife become "one flesh" (basar 'echad), denoting a deep relational and physical unity designed by God.

• Singular Uniqueness: In Deuteronomy 6:4, Yahweh is declared "one," highlighting His absolute uniqueness and incomparable nature.

• Corporate Action: In Judges 20:8, the people of Israel rise up "as one man" (ke'ish 'echad), acting with total synchronicity, shared purpose, and communal resolve.

• Complete Assimilation: In Obadiah 1:11, the word picture is a tragic, sinful reversal of holy unity. Edom, who shared a common ancestry with Israel through Esau and Jacob, stood shoulder-to-shoulder with Babylonians and foreigners. By their actions, they became "one" with the pagan forces of destruction.

4. Theological Implications

The theological implications of Edom being "as one of them" are profound:

• The Guilt of Complicity: God evaluates not just active hostility, but passive complicity. Edom stood "on the other side" while foreigners ransacked Jerusalem. In God’s eyes, neutrality in the face of evil is an illusion. By refusing to help their brother, Edom partnered with the enemy.

• The Treason of Broken Covenant: Brotherhood carries divine obligations. Edom’s alignment with Babylon was not merely a political calculation; it was a spiritual betrayal of their shared heritage.

• The Principle of Divine Evaluation: God judges people based on their actual moral and spiritual alignments, not just their historical pedigree. Edom’s physical descent from Abraham did not exempt them; because they acted like the heathen, they were judged like the heathen.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Obadiah 1:11 describes the tragic day of Jerusalem’s fall (likely the Babylonian invasion of 586 B.C.). As foreigners entered the gates of Jerusalem and cast lots for its treasures, Edom stood by. The Hebrew text employs the particle gam ("even" or "also") before "you" to emphasize the shocking nature of this betrayal: "Even you were as one of them." It is an exclamation of divine grief and anger. Edom did not merely watch; they gloated, intercepted Hebrew fugitives, and handed them over to the enemy. Their behavior was so perfectly aligned with the pagan plunderers that God erased the distinction between the heathen invader and the brother Edom.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 3:22 - "Then the LORD God said, 'Behold, the man has become like one of Us, to know good and evil...'"

• Deuteronomy 6:4 - "Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one!"

• Judges 20:8 - "So all the people arose as one man, saying, 'None of us will go to his tent, nor will any of us turn back to his house;'"

• 2 Samuel 9:11 - "As for Mephibosheth," said the king, "he shall eat at my table as one of the king's sons."

7. Other Relevant Insights

This passage serves as an urgent pastoral warning against spiritual apathy and compromised associations. For the modern believer, "standing on the other side" when others are suffering is a form of active alignment with the oppressor. James 4:17 echoes this truth: "to him who knows to do good and does not do it, to him it is sin." We are called to be "one" with Christ and His body, the Church. If we align our values, behaviors, and loyalties with a broken and rebellious world, we run the risk of being treated "as one of them" when God executes His righteous judgment.
