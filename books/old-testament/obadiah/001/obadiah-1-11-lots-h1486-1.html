LOTS - (gôwrâl, Strong's H1486)

Summary:

The Hebrew word goral refers fundamentally to a small pebble, stone, or lot used to make decisions, divide property, or discern divine allotment. By extension, it signifies an assigned share, portion, or sovereignly appointed destiny. In Obadiah 1:11, goral describes the callous, predatory division of Jerusalem's plunder and captives by foreign invaders while Edom stood by as an approving accomplice. In this passage, the term highlights Edom's betrayal of their brother nation, participating in and celebrating Jerusalem's tragic subjugation and dismemberment.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Near Eastern culture, casting lots (goral) was a widespread method for making impartial decisions, resolving disputes, and determining the division of property or military spoils. Small stones, potsherds, or wooden sticks were typically marked, placed into a container, and drawn or shaken out onto the ground. In Israel's covenantal life, casting lots was explicitly sanctioned by God as a means of seeking divine direction without human bias or political favoritism. It was employed to divide the Promised Land among the tribes, assign priestly service duties, and identify specific individuals under divine instruction.

In the historical setting of Obadiah, the prophet addresses the kingdom of Edom, the descendants of Esau. When foreign armies invaded Jerusalem, breached its gates, and ransacked its wealth, Edom did not assist their brother nation Israel. Instead, they observed the catastrophic ruin of Jerusalem, joining in spirit and deed with the predatory forces who cast lots over the city's inhabitants and treasures.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, goral possesses a clear primary meaning and a few distinct contextual extensions:

  • Literal Lot / Token: A physical object (such as a stone or pebble) cast or drawn to determine a outcome.

  • The Process of Casting Lots: The physical action or ritual of selecting by lot.

  • Allotment or Portion: An assigned share of land, property, or spoils received through the casting of lots.

  • Appointed Destiny or Fate: Figuratively, the divine allotment or final portion assigned to an individual or group, whether a heritage of blessing or a destiny of judgment.

The word itself does not carry inherent moral virtue or vice; its ethical character is entirely defined by who is casting the lot, under what authority, and for what purpose.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament literature, goral appears in several distinct theological and structural contexts:

  • Covenantal Distribution of Land: In the books of Numbers and Joshua, goral is the primary instrument used to allocate tribal territories in Canaan, demonstrating that Israel's inheritance was established by divine sovereign decree rather than human conquest alone.

  • Ritual and Sacred Assignment: In Leviticus, goral is utilized on the Day of Atonement to designate which goat would be sacrificed to the LORD and which would serve as the scapegoat (azazel). In 1 Chronicles, lots determine the order of priestly and Levitical service in the temple.

  • Judicial Resolution: Proverbs emphasizes that casting lots settles contentious disputes between powerful parties by appealing to a resolution beyond human manipulation.

  • Hostile Exploitation and Plunder: In prophetic literature, goral describes pagan conquerors casually dividing human captives and stolen goods like common merchandise.

  • Eschatological Inheritance: In Daniel, the word describes the righteous standing in their final, divine allotment at the end of the age.

4. Theological Implications

The theological significance of goral centers on divine sovereignty. When used rightly within Israel, goral affirmed that human history, land ownership, and sacred duties are ordered by the LORD. What appears to human eyes as a random roll of a pebble is governed by God's providence.

Conversely, when pagan invaders and treacherous neighbors cast goral over Jerusalem, they were attempting to strip God's covenant people of their divine inheritance. However, prophetic Scripture maintains that those who cast lots over the oppressed will ultimately face God's inescapable judgment. Human beings may cast lots for temporal plunder, but God holds the ultimate goral over the destiny of nations.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:11, goral occurs in the masculine singular absolute form. The prophet convicts Edom for their treasonous stance during Jerusalem's darkest hour: "In the day that you stood on the other side—In the day that strangers carried away captive his forces, When foreigners entered his gates And cast lots for Jerusalem—Even you were as one of them."

The contextual syntax sets up a profound moral indictment. Obadiah contrasts the actions of the "strangers" and "foreigners" with the passive complicity of Edom, Israel's brother. The phrase "cast lots for Jerusalem" portrays foreign invaders treating the holy city and its inhabitants as mere booty to be casually distributed among themselves through a game of chance.

By stating that Edom was "as one of them," Obadiah demonstrates that remaining silent and gloating while enemies divide God's people by goral is morally equivalent to taking part in the plunder. Edom adopted the heart and posture of the heathen plunderers, stripping away brotherly compassion and participating in Jerusalem's humiliation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Leviticus 16:8 — "Then Aaron shall cast lots for the two goats: one lot for the LORD and the other lot for the scapegoat."

  • Joshua 18:8 — "Then the men arose to go away; and Joshua charged those who went to survey the land, saying, 'Go, walk through the land, survey it, and come back to me, that I may cast lots for you here before the LORD in Shiloh.'"

  • Proverbs 18:18 — "Casting lots causes contentions to cease, and keeps the mighty apart."

  • Joel 3:3 — "They have cast lots for My people, have given a boy as payment for a harlot, and sold a girl for wine, that they might drink."

  • Daniel 12:13 — "But you, go your way till the end; for you shall rest, and will arise to your lot at the end of the days."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A stark canonical contrast exists between the holy use of goral and its predatory misuse. While God instructed His people to use the goral to receive their sovereignly given inheritance in Canaan, hostile nations used the goral to dismember Israel and steal that very inheritance.

This tragic motif reaches its narrative and theological climax in the New Testament. Just as foreign nations cast lots over Jerusalem in Obadiah 1:11 and Joel 3:3, Roman soldiers cast lots for the garments of Jesus at the foot of the cross (Psalm 22:18; Matthew 27:35). In both instances, wicked men callously divided property that did not belong to them, unaware that through these very events, God was fulfilling His sovereign purpose to secure an eternal inheritance for His people.
