SIDE - (neged, Strong's H5048)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun neged carries the core spatial meaning of "front," "counterpart," "that which is opposite," or "in the presence of." It denotes proximity within view, positioned directly facing or across from a person or object. In Obadiah 1:11, the word describes Edom's positioning during Jerusalem's invasion: standing directly opposite or aloof while foreigners plundered the city. Rather than offering aid to their brother nation, Edom assumed a posture of observing distance—a spatial stance that the prophet highlights to demonstrate Edom's active moral complicity in Jerusalem's destruction.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root of neged relates to being prominent, present, or directly in front of someone or something. In ancient West Semitic languages and classical Hebrew usage, the term functions primarily as a spatial marker. It designates a position directly facing an object, standing before a leader, or maintaining a specific visual distance opposite an event.

environmental and social life in ancient Israel depended heavily on spatial arrangements—whether in camp organization around the tabernacle, architectural layouts of the temple, or legal testimony presented before witnesses. The spatial positioning indicated by neged signifies being within direct line of sight or in the immediate presence of an action.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, neged functions as a noun indicating "the front side" or "counterpart," but it frequently serves as a preposition or adverb meaning "opposite," "before," "in front of," or "at a distance facing."

The semantic range includes:

  • Positioned directly in front of someone (in their presence or sight).

  • Positioned opposite to or across from a person, place, or object.

  • Standing at a visible distance while facing the object of attention.

  • Corresponding to or matching (as in a counterpart facing another).

In narrative and prophetic contexts, neged often combines spatial proximity with moral accountability. To stand neged during an event means one is fully aware, observing, and positioned where action could occur or where sight is undeniable.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament literature, neged appears in a variety of literary genres to express physical orientation, spiritual awareness, and moral accountability.

  • Physical and Spatial Orientation: In architectural and geographic descriptions, neged indicates alignment or facing direction, such as standing opposite a city gate or facing a mountain.

  • Divine Presence and Omniscience: Psalms and prophetic texts frequently employ neged to express human actions standing directly "before" God's eyes or God's promises remaining continually "before" Him. Nothing hidden escapes God's sight when it is neged (in front of) Him.

  • Moral and Relational Distance: When used in circumstances of suffering or trial, neged can denote standing at a distance while remaining a witness. The observer is close enough to see the tragedy unfolding yet intentionally refrains from drawing near to intervene.

4. Theological Implications

The spatial concept embodied in neged conveys key biblical truths regarding divine omniscience and covenant responsibility:

  • God's Unbroken Sight: Scripture utilizes neged to emphasize that human thoughts, actions, and sufferings are continuously before God. Humanity lives open and exposed before His gaze.

  • The Guilt of Passive Observation: Biblical covenant ethics demand active love and rescue toward one's neighbor and kin. Positioning oneself neged—standing within sight as an unaffected spectator during a brother's calamity—is not neutral. In God's theological evaluation, deliberate passive detachment in the face of evil constitutes alignment with the transgressors.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:11, the prophet indicts the nation of Edom for their conduct when Jerusalem was invaded and plundered: "In the day that you stood on the other side [neged]... Even you were as one of them."

The grammatical form appearing in this verse is a masculine singular noun construction serving prepositionally. The prophet uses neged to establish Edom's spatial and moral posture during Judah's disaster. Edom did not merely happen to be absent; they took a deliberate position neged—standing opposite, facing the destruction, watching as strangers carried off Jerusalem's wealth and cast lots for its heritage.

The surrounding context deepens this charge. Edom was descended from Esau, making them the fraternal neighbor of Israel (descended from Jacob). Contextually, Obadiah contrasts what Edom should have done (acted as a brotherly deliverer) with what Edom did do (stood across, gazing upon the tragedy). Obadiah asserts that by choosing to stand neged—observing the ruin without intervening—Edom erased the distinction between spectator and aggressor. The passage concludes with the severe divine verdict: "Even you were as one of them." Their physical position directly opposite the tragedy demonstrated their inward heart of betrayal.

  • Joshua 6:20 – "So the people shouted when the priests blew the trumpets... and the people went up into the city, every man straight ahead [neged]."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The spatial nuance of neged in Obadiah 1:11 highlights the prophetic critique of cold detachment. In Hebrew thought, standing neged while someone suffers is equivalent to abandoning them. A similar spatial and relational dynamic appears in Psalm 38:11, where the sufferer laments that friends stand neged (aloof/opposite) during affliction. Obadiah expands this grief to an international and covenantal scale: Edom's posture of standing neged was an explicit violation of fraternal duty, transforming passive presence into active rebellion against God's covenant order.
