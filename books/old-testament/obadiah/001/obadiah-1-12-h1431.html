PROUDLY - ( gadal , Strong's H1431 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb gadal fundamentally means to grow, become great, or be magnified. In Obadiah 1:12, it is paired with the Hebrew word for "mouth" (peh) in the causative Hiphil stem, literally translating to "make your mouth great." In this context, it describes the arrogant, mocking, and boastful speech of the Edomites as they gloated over the tragic downfall of their brother nation, Judah. This word serves as a sobering warning against the human tendency to exalt oneself and rejoice in the misfortunes of others, reminding believers that speech is the ultimate barometer of a proud heart.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root gadal carries the physical image of growth, enlargement, and towering height. It is used to describe towers growing tall, trees reaching into the sky, and children growing into adulthood. From this physical concept of enlargement comes the mental and spiritual concept of pride. When a person "magnifies" himself, he is attempting to make himself appear larger, more powerful, and more significant than he actually is.

In the ancient Near East, speech was viewed as an extension of one’s power and status. To "enlarge the mouth" (tagdel pika) was a physical gesture of mockery. It paints a picture of someone stretching their mouth wide in insolent laughter, shouting insults, and boasting of their own superiority while watching a rival suffer. For Edom, this was not just generic pride; it was a cruel, vocal betrayal of their closest brother, Judah.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary root gadal refers to being or becoming great, large, or powerful. It operates across several semantic nuances depending on the grammatical stem:

  • Qal Stem: To grow up, become great, wealthy, or powerful.

  • Piel Stem: To bring up, cause to grow, make great, or praise.

  • Hiphil Stem: To make great, magnify, or do great things. When used of humans acting against God or others, it means to act arrogantly, boast, or magnify oneself.

  • Hithpael Stem: To magnify oneself, behave proudly, or show oneself great.

In Obadiah 1:12, the verb appears in the Hiphil stem as tagdel ("you should [not] have spoken proudly"), which literally means "you should not have magnified your mouth." The lexical focus is on the verbal expression of pride—using one's words to elevate oneself while diminishing another.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Scripture uses gadal in several distinct ways to illustrate the contrast between true greatness (which belongs to God) and false greatness (which characterizes human pride):

  • Divine Magnification: The Hiphil stem is frequently used to describe God doing "great things" for His people or magnifying His own name (e.g., Joel 2:21; 2 Samuel 7:26).

  • The Boastful Mouth: When combined with human action, gadal represents a usurpation of God's glory. To "magnify the mouth" is to claim a status of self-sufficiency and triumph over others.

  • Arrogant Exaltation: The word pictures a person standing over a fallen victim, looking down with a sense of security and gloating, falsely believing that their own strength has spared them from a similar fate.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of gadal in Obadiah lies in the relationship between Edom and Judah. Edom was descended from Esau, and Judah from Jacob. They were brothers. Consequently, Edom's pride was a violation of brotherly covenant and natural affection.

Theologically, pride is the act of magnifying oneself in the place of God. When Edom spoke proudly during Judah’s distress, they were not only mocking Judah; they were mocking God’s sovereign administration of covenant discipline. By celebrating Judah's ruin, Edom assumed they were immune to judgment. God’s response in Obadiah demonstrates that those who magnify themselves will be brought low, while those who are brought low by God's discipline will ultimately be restored.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:12, the prohibition "nor should you have spoken proudly" is part of a rhythmic series of eight "do not" (or "should not") warnings (verses 12-14). There is a terrifying progression in the sins of Edom:

1. Gazing: They stood by and watched Judah’s disaster with passive satisfaction ("should not have gazed").

2. Rejoicing: The passive satisfaction turned into active internal and external celebration ("should not have rejoiced").

3. Speaking Proudly (gadal): The inward joy found expression in outward, mocking boasts ("should not have spoken proudly").

4. Acting Hostilely: The verbal boasting quickly escalated into physical looting, violence, and betrayal (verses 13-14).

Speaking proudly (gadal) is the critical bridge between sinful thoughts and sinful actions. Once Edom allowed their mouths to mock Judah, they felt justified in joining the Babylonians in looting and capturing the Jewish fugitives.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Lamentations 1:9 – "Her uncleanness is in her skirts; She did not consider her destiny; Therefore her collapse was awesome; She had no comforter. 'O Lord, behold my affliction, For the enemy has magnified himself!'" (gadal used of the enemy arrogantly exulting over fallen Jerusalem).

  • Ezekiel 35:13 – "Thus with your mouth you have boasted against Me and multiplied your words against Me; I have heard them." (gadal used in the Hiphil with "mouth" to describe Edom’s boasting directly against God).

  • Psalm 55:12 – "For it is not an enemy who reproaches me; Then I could bear it. Nor is it one who hates me who has exalted himself against me; Then I could hide from him." (gadal used of a close friend turning and arrogantly magnifying himself against the psalmist).

  • Psalm 35:26 – "Let them be ashamed and brought to mutual confusion Who rejoice at my hurt; Let them be clothed with shame and dishonor Who exalt themselves against me." (gadal used of enemies magnifying themselves over the psalmist's suffering).

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The Overflow of the Heart: Jesus taught that out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks (Luke 6:45). Edom's "magnified mouth" in Obadiah 1:12 was simply the outward manifestation of their "magnified heart" described in Obadiah 1:3 ("The pride of your heart has deceived you").

  • Lex Talionis (Law of Retribution): Those who make their mouths great against others will find themselves made small by God. In Obadiah 1:2, God tells Edom, "Behold, I will make you small among the nations; You shall be greatly despised." Edom tried to be gadal (great), but God made them qaton (small).
