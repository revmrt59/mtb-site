BROTHER - ( 'ach , Strong's H251 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'ach refers to a male sibling born of the same parents, but it frequently extends to a kinsman, a relative, or a member of a kindred tribe or nation. In the context of Obadiah 1:12, it highlights the deep, covenantal, and familial bond that should have existed between Edom (descendants of Esau) and Judah (descendants of Jacob). Instead of acting as a protective sibling in Judah's day of calamity, Edom betrayed this sacred relationship, gloating over their ruin. The term serves as a sharp indictment of Edom's unnatural cruelty, emphasizing that fraternal obligation makes betrayal all the more grievous in the eyes of God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near East, the concept of a brother ('ach) carried intense legal, social, and emotional expectations. Brotherhood was not merely a matter of shared biology; it was a covenant of mutual protection, loyalty, and redemption. A brother was expected to stand as a defender and advocate in times of crisis, distress, or poverty. The ultimate historical background of Obadiah's use of this word trace back to the twin brothers Jacob and Esau in the book of Genesis. Despite their early rivalry, they were of the same womb, and their descendants—Israel and Edom—were bound by blood. God commanded Israel to respect this family tie, telling them they must not abhor an Edomite because he was their "brother."

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary definition of 'ach is a male sibling sharing one or both parents. However, the word has a broad semantic range in the Old Testament:

  • A biological brother born of the same father and/or mother.

  • A relative or kinsman within an extended family, such as an uncle, nephew, or cousin.

  • A fellow member of the same tribe, nation, or covenant community.

  • An ally or associate bound by a political treaty or common interest.

  • A companion, neighbor, or peer who shares a similar status or condition.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Scripture uses 'ach to paint vivid pictures of relational intimacy and societal responsibility. A brother is depicted as one's natural ally in battle and adversity. When Abraham rescued Lot, he did so because Lot was his 'ach (relative), demonstrating that brotherly love goes to great lengths to redeem family members in danger. Conversely, the scriptures warn that the betrayal of a brother is a profound disruption of the created order. The word is used metaphorically to describe close companions, such as when Job laments that he has become a "brother to jackals," indicating an unwanted and painful kinship with mourning creatures.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, 'ach underscores the corporate nature of God's people and the moral obligations of kinship. God is the Creator of family, and He holds human beings accountable for how they treat those bound to them by blood and covenant. The violation of brotherly duty is treated in Scripture not merely as a personal offense, but as a sin against God Himself. This is why the prophets frequently cite the violation of brotherly covenants as a primary cause for divine judgment. God expects those who share a heritage to share a burden, and when they refuse, they invite His righteous indignation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:12, the use of 'ach is highly targeted and emotionally charged. The prophet charges Edom: "But you should not have gazed on the day of your brother in the day of his captivity." By calling the suffering kingdom of Judah "your brother," God strips away all of Edom's political rationalizations for their hostility. To Edom, Judah was a geopolitical rival; to God, Judah was Edom's brother. Edom’s crime was not merely political opportunism; it was fratricide. They stood by, rejoiced, and even participated in the slaughter and plunder of their own flesh and blood during the Babylonian crisis. The word 'ach stands as a silent witness against Edom's heartless betrayal of their ancestral bond.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 4:9 - Then the LORD said to Cain, "Where is Abel your brother?" He said, "I do not know. Am I my brother's keeper?"

  • Genesis 13:8 - So Abram said to Lot, "Please let there be no strife between you and me, and between my herdsmen and your herdsmen; for we are brethren."

  • Deuteronomy 23:7 - "You shall not abhor an Edomite, for he is your brother. You shall not abhor an Egyptian, because you were an alien in his land."

  • Proverbs 17:17 - A friend loves at all times, and a brother is born for adversity.

  • Amos 1:11 - Thus says the LORD: "For three transgressions of Edom, and for four, I will not turn away its punishment, because he pursued his brother with the sword, and cast off all pity; his anger tore perpetually, and he kept his wrath forever."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The failure of Edom to act as a brother ('ach) serves as a shadow of the ultimate Brother, Jesus Christ. While Edom stood far off and rejoiced in Judah's ruin, Jesus, our elder Brother, did not look upon our captivity with indifference. He condescended to become our Brother, taking on flesh and blood, to redeem us from our captivity. The New Testament advances this concept through the Greek word adelphos, reminding the Church that believers are now spiritual brothers and sisters. Edom's judgment warns the church of the severe spiritual danger of harboring bitterness, gloating over a brother's fall, or failing to assist those who belong to the household of faith.
