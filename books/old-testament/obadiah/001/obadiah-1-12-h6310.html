SPOKEN - ( peh , Strong's H6310 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word peh literally means "mouth" but functions in various idiomatic expressions to denote speech, command, or edge. In Obadiah 1:12, it is paired with the verb gadal ("to make great" or "magnify") to form the idiomatic expression "magnify the mouth," translated in the NKJV as "spoken proudly." This vivid word picture portrays the Edomites opening their mouths wide in arrogant derision, mocking and gloating over the downfall of their brother nation, Judah, during their time of severe calamity.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • peh is a primary noun in the Semitic language family, representing the physical mouth of a human, animal, or metaphorically, the opening of a well, sack, or cave, as well as the "edge" of a sword. The physical mouth is the organ of eating, drinking, and, most importantly, speaking. In ancient Near Eastern thought, the mouth represents the outward expression of the inner heart. What fills the heart overflows through the mouth. In the context of Edom's sin against Judah in Obadiah 1:12, the mouth becomes an instrument of warfare and psychological cruelty. The Edomites did not merely watch Judah's destruction; they used their mouths to gloat, boast, and mock their suffering brothers.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The fundamental meaning of peh is the physical mouth. The broader semantic range includes:

  • The organ of speech, used to express thoughts, commands, praise, or curses.

  • An instrument of eating or consumption.

  • A metaphor for an opening, entrance, or edge (e.g., the mouth of a cave, the collar of a robe, or the edge of a sword).

  • An idiomatic marker for dictation, instruction, or command (e.g., "according to the mouth/word of").

In Obadiah 1:12, the combination of gadal (to make great, enlarge) and peh (mouth) literally means "to make great the mouth" or "to enlarge the mouth." This idiom denotes speaking with pride, arrogance, or derisive boasting.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament frequently uses peh in concrete idioms to describe pride and arrogance. For example, "opening the mouth wide" against someone signifies contempt and aggressive mockery. "Making great the mouth" (as in Obadiah 1:12) is a graphic portrayal of a person stretching their mouth wide in a sneer of derision or shouting loud insults of triumph over a fallen foe. Conversely, the mouth is also meant to be the instrument of God's praise. When used for proud gloating, it represents a complete inversion of its divine purpose.

4. Theological Implications

  • Speech as a Revelation of the Heart: Scripture consistently ties the use of the mouth to the moral state of the soul. Edom’s proud speech reveals a deep-seated hatred, jealousy, and lack of brotherly love toward Israel.

  • Divine Judgment on Arrogant Speech: God holds nations and individuals accountable not just for their violent actions, but also for their words. Gloating over the misfortune of others is an offense against the Creator, who is the ultimate judge of all people.

  • The Principle of Brotherly Responsibility: Edom was related to Judah through Jacob and Esau. Their proud speech violated the covenantal and familial expectation of empathy, illustrating how pride blinds people to basic human and familial obligations.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:12, the prophet delivers a series of eight prohibitions ("you should not...") exposing Edom's progressive hostility during Babylon's siege of Jerusalem. The progression moves from passive looking ("gazed on"), to emotional satisfaction ("rejoiced over"), to active verbal assault ("spoken proudly" / "magnified your mouth"). The "day of distress" was the moment Judah needed brotherly compassion. Instead, Edom's "enlarged mouth" added insult to injury, showing that verbal gloating is a form of active participation in the enemy's destruction.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 1 Samuel 2:3: "Talk no more so very proudly; Let no arrogance come from your mouth, For the LORD is the God of knowledge; And by Him actions are weighed."

  • Psalm 17:10: "They have closed up their fat hearts; With their mouths they speak proudly."

  • Psalm 35:21: "They also opened their mouths wide against me, And said, 'Aha, aha! Our eyes have seen it.'"

  • Proverbs 13:3: "He who guards his mouth preserves his life, But he who opens wide his lips shall have destruction."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Greek equivalent in the Septuagint (LXX) for this idiom is megalorremonein (to speak boastfully, to talk big). The LXX translates the phrase as "neither should you have boasted in the day of their affliction." The direct link between proud speech and subsequent destruction is a major theme in wisdom literature. Edom's "enlarged mouth" in the day of Judah's distress directly led to their own mouth being silenced in judgment, as predicted later in Obadiah.
