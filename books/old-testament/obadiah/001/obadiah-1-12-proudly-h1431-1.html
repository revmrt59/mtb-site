PROUDLY - (gâdal, Strong's H1431)

Summary:

The primary lexical sense of the Hebrew verb gadal is "to be great," "to become large," or "to grow." Across its various verbal stems, it encompasses physical growth, increase in status, the magnification of God's name, or the arrogant self-exaltation of human pride. In Obadiah 1:12, where the prophet delivers a severe divine rebuke to Edom for its conduct during Jerusalem's downfall, the verb appears in the Hiphil stem combined with the noun "mouth" to signify boastful, arrogant speech—literally "making big the mouth." The word contributes to the passage by identifying how Edom's underlying malice broke out into overt, haughty taunting over their brother nation's catastrophe.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Near Eastern culture, speech and posture were understood as direct extensions of a person's inner moral disposition before God and neighbor. Words spoken during times of national distress carried heavy spiritual and social weight. The book of Obadiah addresses the kingdom of Edom, a nation genealogically linked to Israel through Esau, the twin brother of Jacob. This familial relationship made Edom's attitude during Judah's calamity particularly egregious. When enemy forces attacked Jerusalem, Edom did not merely stand at a distance; they observed, celebrated, and spoke boastfully against their kin. The concept of "making great" (gadal) is a recurring thematic motif in Old Testament prophetic literature, drawing a sharp divide between God magnifying His own glory and human beings attempting to magnify themselves or their speech against others in pride.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

At its core, gadal means to increase in size, stature, honor, or significance. Standard Hebrew lexicography outlines a clear range of meanings across different verbal stems:

  • Basic sense: to grow, become great, or become prominent.

  • Simple active development: physical growth, maturity, or gaining higher stature.

  • Causative / Intensive senses: to cause to grow, bring up children, make prominent, or magnify.

  • Reflexive / Arrogant senses (frequently in the Hiphil stem): to make oneself great, act boastfully, or magnify one's speech or conduct against someone else.

In Obadiah 1:12, the lexical expression pairs gadal in the Hiphil stem with the Hebrew noun for "mouth" (peh). While the verb inherently denotes making something great or large, its combination with "mouth" creates a recognized idiom for boasting, scoffing, or speaking with arrogant superiority in a context of hostility.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

The semantic range of gadal spans from literal, physical growth to profound theological declarations:

  • Physical Growth and Maturity: It describes children growing into adulthood or crops reaching full development.

  • Royal or Social Prominence: It denotes the exaltation of kings, officers, or individuals to positions of great honor and authority.

  • Divine Magnification: It frequently expresses the exaltation of Yahweh's name, power, and covenant loyalty, where worshippers call for God to be "magnified" among the nations.

  • Arrogant Self-Exaltation: When applied to proud human rulers or hostile nations, the Hiphil stem often carries a negative nuance, describing individuals or kingdoms that "magnify themselves" against Yahweh or His covenant people.

4. Theological Implications

The theological significance of gadal centers on the exclusive right of Yahweh to be high and lifted up. In Old Testament theology, God alone possesses true, inherent greatness. When human beings attempt to "magnify" themselves, their power, or their speech, they invade a prerogative that belongs solely to the Creator.

Furthermore, boasting over the affliction of God's people constitutes an attack on the Lord Himself. Edom’s haughty speech was not viewed merely as bad etiquette or unkindness between neighbors; it was a proud self-magnification against the sovereign God who was disciplining His people. Prophetic theology repeatedly emphasizes that those who magnify themselves in pride will ultimately be brought low by divine judgment, whereas Yahweh will ultimately magnify His own righteousness and name.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:12, the target occurrence of gadal carries the authoritative morphological features of a Hiphil stem, imperfect form, 2nd person, masculine, singular. The 2nd person masculine singular form directly addresses Edom as a singular collective entity, personifying the entire nation as an individual standing before God's judgment seat.

The immediate context of verse 12 forms part of an intense series of prohibitions and rebukes issued by Yahweh through Obadiah regarding Edom's behavior in "the day of distress":

  • Looking upon a brother's calamity with malicious satisfaction.

  • Rejoicing over the children of Judah in the day of their destruction.

  • "Speaking proudly" (literally, making big the mouth) in the day of distress.

The text presents a clear progression of hostile behavior: gazing maliciously leads to internal rejoicing, which then spills out into external, boastful speech. By using gadal alongside "mouth," the author emphasizes that Edom gave free rein to haughty taunts and mocking self-aggrandizement while Judah was powerless. The Hiphil form here captures the deliberate act of enlarging one's speech—exaggerating one's superiority and casting contempt on the suffering.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 2:10 — "And the child grew, and she brought him to Pharaoh’s daughter, and he became her son."

  • Jeremiah 48:26 — "Make him drunk, for he has exalted himself against the LORD. Moab also shall wallow in his vomit, and he shall also be in derision."

  • Lamentations 1:9 — "O LORD, behold my affliction, for the enemy has magnified himself!"

  • Ezekiel 38:23 — "Thus I will magnify Myself and sanctify Myself, and I will be known in the eyes of many nations. Then they shall know that I am the LORD."

  • Malachi 1:5 — "Your eyes shall see, and you shall say, ‘The LORD is magnified beyond the border of Israel.’"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The structural composition of Obadiah 1:12–14 highlights a dramatic crescendo of sin. Edom's opposition begins with the eye (looking on calamity), moves to the heart (rejoicing over destruction), proceeds to the mouth (speaking proudly with gadal), and culminates in physical action (looting and cutting off fugitives). This shows that boastful speech is an intermediate gateway through which inner malice converts into open violence.

Additionally, a striking canonical contrast exists between how gadal is used for Edom in Obadiah 1:12 and how it is used for God in prophetic literature. While Edom enlarged its mouth in arrogant judgment against Judah, Yahweh ultimately enlarges His own salvation and magnifies His holiness in the sight of the nations. Human self-magnification is temporary and leads to humiliation, whereas divine magnification endures forever.
