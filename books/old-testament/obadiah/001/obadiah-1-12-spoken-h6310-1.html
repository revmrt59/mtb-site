SPOKEN - (peh, Strong's H6310)

Summary:

The primary lexical meaning of peh is "mouth," denoting the physical body part used for speaking, eating, and breathing, as well as spatial openings or edges (such as the mouth of a cave or the edge of a sword). In legal and covenant contexts, it often designates spoken testimony, decrees, or declarations.

In Obadiah 1:12, peh forms the core of an expressive Hebrew idiom—literally "to make large the mouth"—translated in the NKJV as "spoken proudly." In this passage, the word highlights Edom's arrogant boasting, public mockery, and malicious gloating over their fraternal neighbor Judah during Jerusalem's catastrophic fall. Rather than offering aid, Edom opened their mouth wide in insolent triumph, drawing Yahweh's explicit divine indictment.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew noun peh is a foundational term across the Old Testament text, appearing in virtually every genre of ancient Hebrew literature, including historical narrative, legal codes, wisdom literature, psalmody, and classical prophecy. Physically, the term refers to the mouth of humans or animals. Figuratively, it extends to non-anatomical "mouths," such as the opening of a sack, a well, or a cave, or the sharp edge of a blade.

In the prophetic book of Obadiah, the prophet delivers a concentrated announcement of judgment against the nation of Edom. Historically descended from Esau, Edom shared a brotherly heritage with Israel (descended from Jacob). When Jerusalem was invaded and devastated, Edom failed to act as a covenant brother; instead, they stood aloof, rejoiced over Judah's downfall, and joined in the verbal degradation of God's covenant people. Obadiah 1:10–14 presents a series of divine rebukes detailing Edom's offenses, culminating in their arrogant verbal gloating described through the image of the wide-opened mouth.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, peh encompasses several defined senses:

  • Anatomical Mouth: The physical organ of speech, consumption, and breath in humans and animals.

  • Utterance / Speech / Testimony: Spoken communication, command, decree, or witness (e.g., "by the mouth of two witnesses" or "at the mouth of the LORD").

  • Opening / Entrance: The aperture of a container, well, spring, or cave.

  • Edge / Capacity: The sharp cutting edge of a sword (peh cherev) or an exact measure/portion based on need or capacity.

The term carries no inherent moral charge; its theological and moral color is determined entirely by whose mouth is speaking and what is produced from it. A mouth may express righteous praise and divine revelation, or deceit, violence, and haughty arrogance.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Hebrew Scriptures, peh plays a prominent role in describing human and divine communication:

  • Divine Speech: When paired with Yahweh, peh designates authoritative, unfailing, and creative divine speech. What comes forth from the mouth of the LORD is absolute truth and covenant certainty.

  • Human Moral Character: In wisdom literature and the Psalms, the mouth is a major indicator of moral character. The mouth of the righteous yields wisdom and life, whereas the mouth of the wicked pours out destruction, perjury, and pride.

  • Idiomatic Expressions: Hebrew frequently combines peh with verbs of action to paint vivid word pictures. "Opening the mouth" can signify beginning a formal speech or prayer. "To enlarge the mouth" (higdil peh) means to speak arrogance, boasting loudly and maliciously over another person's distress.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical usage of peh underscores several key theological truths:

  • Accountability for Speech: Scripture consistently teaches that human speech is not neutral or insignificant. God scrutinizes the utterances of the mouth, treating arrogant verbal cruelty as a direct offense against His holiness.

  • The Sin of Gloating: Edom's broad-mouthed boasting over fallen Judah reveals that gloating over another's suffering is detestable to Yahweh. Pride expressed through the mouth invites divine humiliation.

  • Solidarity with God's People: Because Judah was God's covenant nation, Edom's proud speech against Judah was viewed by Yahweh as arrogance directed against Himself. God identifies with His afflicted people, hearing every proud word spoken against them.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:12, the prophet uses peh within a structured series of prohibitions highlighting Edom's calloused behavior during Judah's catastrophe. The NKJV translates the phrase ve-al-tagdel pika as "Nor should you have spoken proudly."

Literally, the Hebrew syntax reads, "And do not make large your mouth in the day of distress." The noun peh appears here in the masculine singular absolute form. Combined with the verb gadal (to make great, enlarge), peh forms a vivid mental picture of someone opening their mouth wide in insolent laughter, loud boasting, and wide-mouthed mockery over an opponent's calamity.

The context intensifies the severity of this action:

  • Timing: Edom did this "in the day of distress" and "in the day of their destruction." Speech that might be offensive at any time becomes uniquely heinous when hurled at a brother who is currently undergoing ruin.

  • Kinship: The broader passage emphasizes the brotherhood between Edom and Judah. Edom's mouth should have uttered lamentation, intercession, or comfort; instead, it enlarged itself in malicious triumph.

  • Divine Reversal: The larger argument of Obadiah is that as Edom did to Judah, so it would be done to Edom. The proud mouth that opened wide over Jerusalem's fall would ultimately be silenced by Yahweh's coming judgment.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

Below are representative uses of peh drawn from different biblical genres:

  • 1 Kings 8:24 — "You have kept what You promised Your servant David my father; You have also spoken with Your mouth and fulfilled it with Your hand, as it is this day."

  • Context Insight: Solomon highlights the absolute integrity of God's covenant word. What God promises with His mouth, He brings to execution by His sovereign power.

  • Psalms 51:15 — "O Lord, open my lips, and my mouth shall show forth Your praise."

  • Context Insight: In David's prayer of deep repentance, the purified mouth becomes an instrument of genuine, humble praise once God forgives and restores the sinner.

  • Proverbs 18:20 — "A man's stomach shall be satisfied from the fruit of his mouth; from the produce of his lips he shall be filled."

  • Context Insight: Wisdom literature stresses the inescapable consequences of human speech. The utterances that proceed from a person's mouth bring tangible outcomes back upon the speaker.

  • Micah 4:4 — "But everyone shall sit under his vine and under his fig tree, and no one shall make them afraid; for the mouth of the LORD of hosts has spoken."

  • Context Insight: In a glorious prophetic vision of future peace and restoration, the absolute certainty of the promise rests upon the reality that the mouth of Yahweh Himself has declared it.

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Speech as the Mirror of the Heart: While Obadiah focuses on the physical action of Edom enlarging their mouth, Old Testament theology consistently links the mouth to the hidden condition of the heart. Edom's enlarged mouth was merely the outward expression of an enlarged, prideful heart that despised Jacob's descendants.

  • Ethical Weight of Silence vs. Speech: Obadiah indicts Edom both for what they did not do (they stood by in silence without helping) and for what they did do (they enlarged their mouth in boastful speech). In times of severe distress among God's people, perverse speech compound the sin of callous indifference.
