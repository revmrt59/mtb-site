SUBSTANCE - ( chayil , Strong's H2428 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word chayil is a multi-faceted term representing strength, power, wealth, capability, and resources. In the context of Obadiah 1:13, it refers to the material wealth, substance, and possessions of the Judeans which the Edomites greedily plundered during Jerusalem's downfall. This word emphasizes not just abstract riches, but the collective strength and vital resources of a people, which were ruthlessly stripped away by their brother nation.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The primary root of chayil relates to strength, force, or power. Historically, this strength was expressed in two primary ways: military might (such as an army or a host of valiant warriors) and economic capability (such as wealth, substance, or efficiency). The word bridges the gap between physical force and material prosperity, as in the ancient Near East, the ability to accumulate and defend wealth was directly tied to strength and power. Thus, chayil is not merely passive currency but the active, tangible substance of a person's or nation's labor, security, and vitality.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The semantic range of chayil is extensive and depends heavily on context. It denotes:

• Strength, power, or physical might.

• Wealth, riches, substance, or property.

• An army, military force, or host.

• Ability, efficiency, virtue, or valor.

In passages dealing with socioeconomic contexts, it represents the accumulated assets, goods, and resources that sustain a household or nation. In Obadiah 1:13, the term specifically denotes physical goods, riches, and substance.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Military Power as Substance: The term frequently refers to an army (chayil), which represents a nation's ultimate physical force. In a poetic parallel, a nation's material substance is also its chayil, indicating that wealth is the economic engine that powers and sustains national survival.

• Human Capability: When applied to individuals, chayil represents moral strength or competence (often translated as "virtue" or "valor"). A person of chayil is someone of substance, standing, and integrity, possessing both the character and the means to affect their community positively.

• Divine Gift: Wealth is portrayed as a resource originating from divine empowerment. It is God who gives the capacity to produce chayil, making the unjust seizure of a nation's chayil not only a crime against humanity but an affront to God's providential provision.

4. Theological Implications

The plundering of chayil in Scripture carries profound theological weight. Wealth is not inherently evil; rather, it is a sign of God's blessing, covenantal provision, and the fruit of human labor. When Edom laid hands on Judah's chayil in their day of calamity, they were not merely stealing; they were exploiting God's judgment upon His people for their own selfish gain. This act demonstrated a total lack of brotherly love, fear of Yahweh, and covenant solidarity. God's subsequent judgment on Edom reveals that He holds nations accountable for how they treat the vulnerable and how they handle the plundered resources of others.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:13, the prophet delivers a series of divine rebukes against Edom for their behavior during the Babylonian invasion of Jerusalem. The verse progresses through a sequence of sins: entering the gate, gazing upon the affliction, and finally, laying hands on the "substance" (chayil) of Judah. Edom, descended from Jacob's brother Esau, should have acted as a protector. Instead, they stood aloof, gloated over Judah's ruin, and actively participated in the looting. The use of chayil here emphasizes that Edom stripped away the surviving Judeans' means of recovery and survival, leaving them completely destitute in their darkest hour.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Deuteronomy 8:18: "And you shall remember the LORD your God, for it is He who gives you power to get wealth, that He may establish His covenant which He swore to your fathers, as it is this day."

• Ruth 2:1: "There was a relative of Naomi's husband, a man of great wealth, of the family of Elimelech. His name was Boaz."

• Proverbs 13:22: "A good man leaves an inheritance to his children's children, But the wealth of the sinner is stored up for the righteous."

• Proverbs 31:10: "Who can find a virtuous woman? For her worth is far above rubies."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The parallel structure in Obadiah 1:13 highlights the step-by-step moral degradation of Edom. They progressed from passive looking ("gazed on their affliction") to active looting ("laid hands on their substance"). This serves as a timeless warning for God's people: indifference to a brother's suffering easily paves the way for active exploitation of their vulnerability.
