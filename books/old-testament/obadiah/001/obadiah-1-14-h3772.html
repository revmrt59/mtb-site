CUT OFF - ( karath , Strong's H3772 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb karath primarily means to cut, fell, or sever. In Obadiah 1:14, it is used in the causative sense (Hiphil) to describe Edom's ruthless efforts to trap, intercept, and destroy the Jewish refugees fleeing the Babylonian siege of Jerusalem. Instead of showing brotherly kindness, Edom actively blocked their escape routes to ensure they were completely cut off and delivered over to their enemies.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root karath physically describes the act of cutting down, slicing, or severing objects using a sharp instrument. It is commonly associated with cutting down trees, felling timber, or slicing pieces of wood. This physical imagery extends into ancient covenant ceremonies, where animals were sliced in two, and parties walked between the pieces to seal an agreement. The imagery conveys a definitive separation, a dividing of one part from another, or the absolute termination of an object's growth and life.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb karath means to cut off, eliminate, or destroy. In the basic active stem (Qal), it denotes felling trees, cutting off parts of the body, or making (literally, "cutting") a covenant. In the causative stem (Hiphil), which appears in Obadiah 1:14, the verb shifts to mean exterminating, wiping out, or completely destroying a group of people. It refers to an intentional, systematic elimination that prevents a group or individual from continuing to exist or escape.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Felling of Trees: The verb illustrates physical destruction, such as cutting down trees to build siege works or chopping down Asherah poles in judgment.

• Cutting a Covenant: The phrase karath berith denotes the solemn process of ratifying a treaty or covenant. Slicing sacrificial animals symbolizes the fate of the covenant-breaker who would likewise be "cut off" if they violated the terms.

• Judicial Severance: The term describes the divine penalty of being "cut off" from the covenant community. This represents a loss of identity, heritage, and physical life due to gross disobedience or violation of holiness.

• Military Extermination: The word is used to describe the total military elimination of enemies, ensuring that no survivors remain to threaten the land.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, karath underlines the severe reality of divine judgment and covenant accountability. Because God is holy, He does not tolerate persistent rebellion, and those who set themselves against His purposes are eventually cut off from His presence and blessings. Furthermore, the word demonstrates the serious nature of human relationships under God's sovereignty. To cut off others—especially those who are helpless or under judgment—is to invite the same reciprocal judgment from God. The ultimate consequence of being cut off is the permanent loss of legacy, name, and life before the Lord.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:14, karath exposes the depth of Edom's cruelty toward Judah during the Babylonian invasion of Jerusalem. The Edomites did not merely remain passive observers; they actively stationed themselves at the mountain passes and strategic crossroads. Their purpose was to intercept the surviving Judeans who had escaped the siege and were fleeing for their lives. By standing at these checkpoints to "cut off" the fugitives, Edom prevented any chance of survival, either slaughtering them on the spot or capturing them to hand over to the Babylonians. The use of karath here emphasizes the cold-blooded, absolute, and finalized nature of Edom's betrayal of their brother Jacob.

• Ruth 4:10 – "Moreover, Ruth the Moabitess, the widow of Mahlon, I have acquired as my wife, to perpetuate the name of the dead through his inheritance, that the name of the dead may not be cut off from among his brethren and from his position at the gate. You are witnesses this day."

• Psalm 34:16 – "The face of the LORD is against those who do evil, To cut off the remembrance of them from the earth."

• Zechariah 13:8 – "And it shall come to pass in all the land," Says the LORD, "That two-thirds in it shall be cut off and die, But one-third shall be left in it."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Greek translation of the Old Testament (Septuagint) renders karath in Obadiah 1:14 with the verb exolethreuō, which carries the intense meaning of utterly destroying or completely rooting out. This same Greek term is carried over into the New Testament to describe the ultimate, irreversible spiritual destruction of those who reject God's Messiah (Acts 3:23). Thus, the physical blocking and destruction Edom sought to inflict on Israel's remnants reflects a deeper, spiritual reality of being permanently severed from God's covenant lineage and salvation.
