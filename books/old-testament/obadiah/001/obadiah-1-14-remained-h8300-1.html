REMAINED - (sârîyd, Strong's H8300)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun sarid denotes a survivor, refugee, or remnant—someone who has escaped a major catastrophe, slaughter, or divine judgment. In Obadiah 1:14, the noun appears as a masculine plural absolute form referring to the Judhite refugees who survived the foreign invasion of Jerusalem. By using sarid, the prophet highlights the extreme vulnerability of these remaining survivors and underscores the cruelty of Edom, who intercepted and betrayed their own kin in their moment of deepest distress.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The noun sarid is derived from a root meaning to escape or survive a mortal threat. It functions primarily in military, historical, and prophetic contexts to designate individuals or groups who remain alive after a devastating conflict, siege, or judgment.

In the historical context of Obadiah, the word appears within a bitter oracle against Edom. Following the catastrophic fall of Jerusalem to foreign invaders, Edom—descended from Esau and thus brother to Israel—did not merely watch Judah's destruction but actively participated in exploiting Judah's defeat. The term sarid places the focus on the helpless remnants fleeing the fallen city.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, sarid refers to a "survivor," "remnant," or "one who has escaped." It describes the concrete state of having survived a lethal event that swept away others.

The word itself is neutrally descriptive of survival; it does not inherently possess spiritual or ethical character apart from its context. Depending on how it is used, sarid can denote:

  • Military survivors escaping from a battlefield.

  • Entire populations reduced by war or divine judgment until few or none remain.

  • A preserved remnant spared by Yahweh according to His mercy and divine purpose.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament literature, sarid is used in two primary ways:

First, it is frequently employed in military defeat formulas, often in total destruction narratives where "no survivor" (lo-shir'ir sarid) was left. In conquest contexts, the absence of any sarid emphasizes total military victory and the execution of divine judgment against doomed populations.

Second, in prophetic and lament literature, sarid refers to the surviving population of Judah or Israel undergoing divine chastisement. In these settings, the presence or absence of a sarid highlights either the severity of the judgment (if few survive) or the persistent mercy of God (if a remnant is preserved).

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, sarid connects to the broader biblical motif of the Remnant. While judgment may be thorough and overwhelming, God’s covenant commitment ensures that judgment rarely results in absolute obliteration for His people. Where divine justice demands destruction, sovereign mercy preserves a sarid.

Additionally, the ethical treatment of a sarid carries major covenantal weight. God holds surrounding nations accountable for how they treat His vulnerable, surviving people. To harm, capture, or betray those who have just escaped divine chastisement is to invite direct judgment from God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:10–14, the prophet delivers a series of eight sharp "should not have" indictments against Edom, detailing their progressive participation in Jerusalem’s downfall:

  • Standing aloof while strangers carried off Judah's wealth.

  • Rejoicing over the sons of Judah in the day of their destruction.

  • Boasting in the day of their distress.

  • Entering the gate of God's people in the day of calamity.

  • Gloating over their affliction.

  • Laying hands on their substance.

  • Standing at the crossroads to cut off those who fled.

  • Handing over those who "remained" (sarid).

The target word in Obadiah 1:14 is tagged morphologically as a masculine plural absolute noun. In this sentence, sarid functions as the direct object of Edom's betrayal: Edom "delivered up those among them who remained."

The context creates a stark contrast between the survivors' fragile condition and Edom's ruthless exploitation. The Judhites had already endured the agonizing trauma of invasion and siege; those who survived were the remaining few seeking refuge. Rather than offering brotherly asylum, Edom actively captured these survivors and handed them back to the enemy. By using sarid, Obadiah emphasizes the victimhood of the survivors and the heinous nature of Edom's betrayal, which ultimately triggers the lex talionis (law of retaliation) in Obadiah 1:15: "As you have done, it shall be done to you."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Deuteronomy 3:3 – "So the LORD our God also delivered into our hands Og king of Bashan and all his people, and we defeated him until no survivor was left to him."

  • Joshua 10:40 – "So Joshua conquered all the land... he left none remaining, but utterly destroyed all that breathed, as the LORD God of Israel had commanded."

  • Isaiah 1:9 – "Unless the LORD of hosts Had left us a very small remnant, We would have become like Sodom, We would have been made like Gomorrah."

  • Joel 2:32 – "For in Mount Zion and in Jerusalem there shall be deliverance, As the LORD has said, Among the survivors whom the LORD calls."

  • Lamentations 2:22 – "In the day of the LORD’s anger There was no survivor or fugitive. Those whom I have borne and brought up My enemy has destroyed."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A striking thematic irony exists between Obadiah 1:14 and Obadiah 1:18. In verse 14, Edom actively hands over the sarid (survivors) of Israel to destruction. However, later in the vision, Obadiah prophesies that the house of Jacob will be a fire and the house of Esau stubble, concluding that "no survivor shall remain of the house of Esau" (Obadiah 1:18). Edom’s attempt to eradicate Israel's survivors results in Edom itself being left without a single survivor under God's ultimate judgment.
