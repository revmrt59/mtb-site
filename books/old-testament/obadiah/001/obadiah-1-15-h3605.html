ALL - ( kol , Strong's H3605 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word kol denotes the whole, the entirety, or every individual part of a collective group. In Obadiah 1:15, it acts as a universal modifier indicating that God's coming judgment on the "Day of the LORD" is not localized to Edom alone but extends comprehensively to "all" nations. It underscores the global, inescapable reach of divine justice, demonstrating that no hostile power falls outside God’s sovereign jurisdiction or His righteous retribution.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word kol is derived from a root meaning to complete, perfect, or make whole. It provides a foundational biblical picture of a boundary that encloses everything within its scope, leaving nothing outside. In ancient Near Eastern thought, power was often viewed as localized, with different gods ruling over specific territories or nations. By using kol, the biblical writers shatter this localized view. The word functions as a linguistic net that sweeps up every geographic region, political power, and individual person under the singular authority of the Creator.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word kol is a noun expressing totality, the whole, every, or all. When used in a construct chain with a plural noun, it signifies "every one of" or "the entirety of" the group mentioned. In Obadiah 1:15, it modifies "the nations" (haggoyim). Lexically, this denotes the absolute totality of the Gentile nations. The definition excludes any exceptions; there is no nation so remote, so powerful, or so insignificant that it can escape the designated event.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, kol is used to establish absolute categories.

• It is used in creation contexts to describe the "whole" earth or "everything" God made, emphasizing complete sovereignty and ownership.

• It is used in covenant contexts to demand "all" of a person's heart, soul, and strength, leaving no room for divided loyalty.

• In prophetic literature, when kol is joined with "nations," it paints a picture of a global courtroom. It acts as an equalizer, stripping away the unique political defenses, military achievements, and geographic barriers of individual empires, grouping them into a single, accountable collective before the heavenly throne.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of kol in Obadiah 1:15 is immense.

• Universal Sovereignty: Yahweh is not merely the local deity of Israel; He is the sovereign Judge of the entire earth. His moral standards apply universally, and His authority to execute judgment is global.

• Universal Accountability: No nation is autonomous. Every geopolitical entity is accountable to God for how it treats others, particularly how it treats God's covenant people.

• The Scope of the Day of the LORD: While the immediate context of Obadiah addresses the pride and fall of Edom, the insertion of kol expands the prophecy into an eschatological reality. Edom's localized judgment is a historical pattern and a warning of the ultimate, final judgment that will come upon the entire world.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Prior to verse 15, the prophet Obadiah focuses intensely on Edom’s specific sins—their pride, their geographical self-security in the clefts of the rock, and their cruel exploitation of Judah’s defeat. In verse 15, the lens suddenly and dramatically widens. The transition from the singular "Edom" to "all the nations" serves a dual purpose. First, it reminds Edom that they cannot hide behind alliances or get lost in the crowd of nations; they are fully included. Second, it warns the rest of the world that the same standard of justice applied to Edom will be applied universally. The principle of lex talionis ("as you have done, it shall be done to you") is declared as a universal law governing all nations.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 1:31 - "Then God saw everything that He had made, and indeed it was very good." (Expresses the absolute completeness of God's original creation).

• Psalm 103:19 - "The LORD has established His throne in heaven, And His kingdom rules over all." (Declares the total, cosmic scope of God's sovereign dominion).

• Joel 3:2 - "I will also gather all nations, And bring them down to the Valley of Jehoshaphat..." (Parallel prophetic usage of kol describing the global gathering of nations for final judgment).

7. Other Relevant Insights

Grammatically, in Obadiah 1:15, kol is preceded by the preposition al (upon) and followed by the definite article with the plural noun haggoyim (the nations). This specific construction, al-kol-haggoyim, creates a sense of an impending, heavy canopy of judgment descending upon the entirety of the Gentile world. It emphasizes that the Day of the LORD is not a localized storm, but a global climate shift of divine justice.
