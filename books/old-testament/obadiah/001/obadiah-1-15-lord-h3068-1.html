LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

The personal and covenantal name of the God of Israel, revealing Him as the self-existent, eternal, and sovereign Creator who binds Himself to His people by promise and rules over all human history. In Obadiah 1:15, this name identifies the absolute divine authority who summons every Gentile nation to judgment, establishing that Israel's redemptive Lord is also the supreme Ruler over all earthly powers.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The divine name yhwh is the distinct personal name by which God revealed Himself to Israel, most prominently in His covenantal encounter with Moses at the burning bush. Derived from the root verb signifying existence or active presence (hayah or hawah), the name conveys God's uncaused, independent, and unchanging nature. While adjacent ancient Near Eastern cultures worshipped regional deities with localized domains, Israel’s Scriptures present yhwh as the self-existent Lord over all creation, sovereign over space, time, and nations.

In the book of Obadiah, this covenant name is proclaimed within a prophetic announcement of cosmic judgment. Edom, a brother nation descended from Esau, had betrayed Judah during Jerusalem’s destruction. By introducing yhwh into this international crisis, the prophet Obadiah demonstrates that Israel's God is not merely a regional deity defending His tribe, but the righteous King of the universe who executes perfect retribution against arrogant oppressors.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, yhwh is the formal proper noun designating the one true God of Israel. It denotes His dynamic, active presence and unchanging self-consistency. Basic lexical senses include:

  • The personal name of God, emphasizing His self-existence and eternal identity.

  • The God of the covenant, who reveals His character, will, and law to His chosen people.

  • The ultimate Lord and Sovereign over human history and creation.

Care must be taken to distinguish between the base lexical meaning of the name and the theological doctrines derived from context. Lexically, yhwh identifies the person of Israel's God; the full revelation of His righteous judgment, grace, and international authority derives from the broader biblical narrative and specific prophetic contexts.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In Old Testament usage, yhwh appears across narrative, law, poetry, and prophecy. The name routinely highlights two interrelated aspects of God’s character:

  • Covenantal Faithfulness: God acts under this name to deliver, redeem, and sustain His people according to His oath to the patriarchs.

  • Sovereign Holiness and Justice: God asserts His moral authority, holding both His covenant people and pagan nations accountable to His standards of righteousness.

In prophetic literature, the expression "the day of yhwh" designates a decisive temporal climax where God intervenes directly in history to punish wickedness, destroy pride, and vindicate His covenant promise. Thus, yhwh signifies both a refuge for the faithful and an inescapable terror for the unrepentant rebel.

4. Theological Implications

The divine name yhwh grounds several fundamental Old Testament doctrines:

  • Absolute Self-Existence: God depends on nothing outside Himself for His life, authority, or purpose.

  • Covenantal Fidelity: Because yhwh is unchanging, His promises to His people remain unshakable despite human failure or geopolitical chaos.

  • Universal Governance: Although yhwh established a special covenant with Israel, His jurisdiction is not confined within Israel’s borders. Every nation, king, and empire stands under His moral government.

In the context of divine judgment, yhwh operates as the righteous Judge whose retribution is strictly proportionate. The ancient principle of lex talionis ("as you have done, it shall be done to you") reflects His perfect, uncorrupted justice.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:15, the name appears in the singular absolute state as a masculine proper noun (yhwh), identifying the specific divine Person executing history's final reckoning. The clause reads, "For the day of the LORD [yhwh] upon all the nations is near."

The immediate context heightens the significance of yhwh:

  • Universal Scope: Edom believed its mountain fortresses secured it against human retaliation, but Obadiah brings Edom face-to-face with yhwh, whose day comes "upon all the nations."

  • Moral Retribution: The standard of judgment is declared in the exact mirror of Edom’s violence: "as you have done, it shall be done to you; your reprisal shall return upon your own head."

  • Covenant Vindication: By placing yhwh at the center of this announcement, the passage assures suffering Judah that their God has not abandoned them. The same Lord who disciplined Jerusalem will right every global wrong and re-establish His sovereign kingdom on Mount Zion.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Numbers 14:35 — "I the LORD have spoken this. I will surely do so to all this evil congregation who are gathered together against Me."

  • Psalms 103:6 — "The LORD executes righteousness and justice for all who are oppressed."

  • Psalms 129:4 — "The LORD is righteous; He has cut in pieces the cords of the wicked."

  • Jeremiah 31:33 — "But this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, says the LORD: I will put My law in their minds, and write it on their hearts; and I will be their God, and they shall be My people."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The prophetic phrase "the day of the LORD" (yom yhwh) serves as a foundational motif across the minor prophets. Obadiah links Edom’s pride to the broader day of global judgment, establishing that cruelty against God's people is ultimately an assault against yhwh Himself. The revelation of yhwh in Obadiah 1:15 reminds the reader that national oppression and human arrogance are temporary; divine accountability under the sovereign Lord of history is absolute and inevitable.
