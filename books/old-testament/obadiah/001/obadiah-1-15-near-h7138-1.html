NEAR - (qârôwb, Strong's H7138)

Summary:

The Hebrew adjective qarov denotes proximity, describing that which is close or near in spatial distance, temporal occurrence, or relational kinship. In Obadiah 1:15, qarov describes the imminent approach of "the day of the LORD upon all the nations." In this prophetic context, the word establishes the inescapable temporal proximity of divine judgment, signaling to Edom and all hostile nations that Yahweh's decisive intervention and righteous retribution are at hand, moving from a distant concept to an imminent reality.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The adjective qarov derives from a Hebrew root associated with approaching, drawing near, or coming into close proximity. In ancient Hebrew usage, the term spans three distinct dimensions of life: physical geography (spatial nearness), temporal events (chronological nearness), and familial relationships (kinship nearness).

In prophetic literature, particularly within announcements of divine visitation and judgment, qarov functions as an urgent herald. Rather than describing an abstract or far-off future, prophets used qarov to interrupt human complacency and declare that God's active intervention was pressing directly upon the immediate horizon of history.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, qarov signifies "near," "close," or "imminent." Its semantic range encompasses three primary uses:

  • Spatial Proximity: Physically close in distance or geography, such as a neighboring town, land, or road.

  • Temporal Proximity: Chronologically close, describing an event that is impending, soon to occur, or at hand.

  • Relational Proximity: Closely related by blood or covenant, designating a near kinsman or relative who holds specific family rights and duties.

The core lexical definition remains simple—denoting proximity. The theological and dramatic weight of the word is supplied by its context and by the subject or event it qualifies.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Hebrew Scriptures, qarov adapts its nuance to its literary genre:

  • In Historical and Narrative Texts: The word often designates geographic convenience or short travel distance, as well as the proximity of family members bound by legal obligations of redemption.

  • In Wisdom and Poetic Literature: The term describes spiritual accessibility—such as God being near to those who call upon Him, or His word being near in the mouth and heart.

  • In Prophetic Oracles: The word frequently qualifies the phrase "the day of the LORD" (yom YHWH). When applied to divine judgment, qarov communicates both certainty and temporal urgency. It stresses that human injustice will not be tolerated indefinitely; God's tribunal is rapidly approaching.

4. Theological Implications

When applied to "the day of the LORD," qarov carries significant theological weight:

  • Sovereignty over Human History: The imminence expressed by qarov affirms that God actively governs international history. Human powers may feel secure in their pride, but divine intervention is near.

  • Moral Retribution and Moral Accountability: In Obadiah, the nearness of the day is directly connected to the principle of divine justice: "as you have done, it shall be done to you." The proximity of Yahweh's arrival leaves no room for delayed repentance or continued exploitation.

  • The Tension of Prophetic Time: Prophetic proclamations of qarov bring the ultimate end into the present awareness of the hearer. While human observers often misinterpret God's patience as delay, the prophetic word asserts that judgment is pressing against the door.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:15, qarov appears in the masculine singular absolute form, serving as the predicate descriptor for "the day of the LORD" (yom YHWH).

The immediate context of Obadiah centers on God's indictment of Edom for their arrogant gloating, violence, and looting during Judah's collapse. Edom relied on its strategic mountain fortresses, allies, and perceived wisdom, assuming immunity from harm. However, Obadiah expands his vision in verse 15 from Edom specifically to "all the nations," making Edom an exemplar of world rebellion against Yahweh.

By declaring that the day of the LORD is qarov ("near"), the prophet shatters Edom's false sense of security. The immediate syntactic sequence links the nearness of the day directly to retribution: "as you have done, it shall be done to you; your reprisal shall return upon your own head." The word qarov signals that the window of Edom's opportunistic pride has closed, and the arrival of divine justice is an impending, unavoidable reality.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Deuteronomy 30:14 — "But the word is very near you, in your mouth and in your heart, that you may do it." Here the word describes spiritual accessibility, emphasizing that God's covenant command is not far off or unattainable, but immediately present for obedience.

  • Exodus 13:17 — "...God did not lead them by way of the land of the Philistines, although that was near..." In this historical narrative, the same Hebrew word denotes physical, geographical proximity along a direct coastal trade route.

  • Zephaniah 1:14 — "The great day of the LORD is near; it is near and hastens quickly." Like Obadiah 1:15, this prophetic herald uses the word to declare the imminent, unstoppable arrival of divine reckoning.

  • Leviticus 25:25 — "If one of your brethren becomes poor, and has sold some of his possession, and if his redeeming relative comes to redeem it..." In covenant law, the term refers to relational proximity, identifying a close family member who holds the legal right and duty of redemption.

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Prophetic Formula: The phrase "near is the day of the LORD" functions as a standard prophetic warning across several books (such as Ezekiel 7:7, 30:3, and Zephaniah 1:14). It serves as a solemn alarm to wake dormant consciences.

  • Contrast in Obadiah: While Obadiah 1:15 declares that judgment is near for the hostile nations, the later verses of the oracle promise deliverance and restoration on Mount Zion (Obadiah 1:17). The same day that brings near retribution to the proud brings near liberation to God's remnant.
