CONTINUALLY - (tâmîyd, Strong's H8548)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun tamid basic lexical meaning denotes continuity, regularity, perpetuity, or an unbroken duration, frequently functioning as an adverb meaning "continually," "constantly," or "perpetually." In Obadiah 1:16, tamid describes the unrelenting, inescapable nature of divine judgment executed upon the hostile nations. The word contributes to Obadiah's pronouncement of exact talionic justice: as Edom callously drank in revelry on God's holy mountain during Jerusalem's fall, so all the pagan nations will be compelled to drink the cup of God's righteous wrath unceasingly until their judgment is absolute.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The word tamid is a foundational Hebrew term expressing the concept of continuity, regularity, or perpetuity. Derived from an unexpressed root meaning to stretch or continue, the term functions substantially as an abstract noun meaning "continuity" or "perpetuity," and adverbially as "continually," "always," or "regularly."

In the ancient Near Eastern context and Israelite life, tamid often designated routine, standard, and unbroken practices—most notably the ongoing, daily sacrificial ritual (the olat tamid or daily burnt offering) performed in the tabernacle and temple. Beyond the cultic realm, tamid described unbroken personal devotion, persistent historical conditions, or unending states of divine intervention and judgment.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, tamid signifies that which is continuous, persistent, or recurring according to an established standard or without interruption. Standard Old Testament lexicons (such as Brown-Driver-Briggs and the Theological Wordbook of the Old Testament) define its core semantic range as:

  • Continuity or perpetuity (a state of uninterrupted duration).

  • Regularity or routine (occurring consistently, as in daily fixed duties).

  • Adverbially: continually, constantly, perpetually, habitually, without end.

In Obadiah 1:16, the word functions adverbially in a masculine, singular, absolute form, qualifying the action of drinking the cup of divine indignation.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Hebrew Scriptures, tamid carries distinct nuances depending on the genre and context:

  • Cultic and Ritual Usage: In the Torah, tamid frequently describes the regular, uninterrupted offerings and duties of the priesthood, such as the perpetual light, the regular grain offering, and the daily morning and evening sacrifices. Here, it denotes faithful, scheduled regularity.

  • Devotional and Personal Usage: In the Psalms and wisdom literature, tamid captures the persistent posture of a believer’s heart—setting the Lord always before oneself, keeping sin in constant view during repentance, or offering continuous praise.

  • Judicial and Prophetic Usage: In prophetic literature, tamid underscores the ongoing nature of severe conditions, whether describing the ceaseless oppression suffered by God’s people or the unceasing execution of divine justice upon ungodly powers.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, tamid reveals how God operates both in covenant grace toward His people and in holy justice toward persistent rebellion:

  • Perpetual Covenant Care vs. Unending Wrath: While God provides continuous (tamid) presence, guidance, and protection for His covenant people, those who stubbornly oppose God and abuse His saints encounter a judgment that is similarly tamid—relentless and complete.

  • The Absolute Nature of Divine Justice: Divine judgment is not a fleeting burst of temper; it is an enduring, measured, and complete vindication of God’s holiness. When God pours out His wrath upon systemic, unrepentant wickedness, the retribution continues until evil is fully eradicated.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:15–16, the prophet delivers a solemn message concerning the "Day of the LORD" coming upon all nations. Edom had gloated over Judah’s destruction, participated in looting Jerusalem, and celebrated on God’s "holy mountain."

Obadiah constructs a striking literary and ironic contrast through the imagery of drinking:

  • Edom’s act of drinking on Mount Zion was a sinful, defiant celebration over the ruin of God's sanctuary and people.

  • God's response is strict measure-for-measure justice: "as you drank... so all the nations shall drink continually."

Here, tamid ("continually") intensifies the picture of judgment. The pagan nations will be forced to drink from the cup of God’s indignation, not as a brief or passing sip, but in an unbroken, unceasing sentence. They will "drink and swallow" until they are completely consumed and rendered as though they had never existed. The word tamid ensures that the reader understands this divine wrath will not be cut short or mitigated until its purifying and devastating purpose is fully realized.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • 2 Samuel 9:13 — "So Mephibosheth dwelt in Jerusalem, for he ate continually at the king’s table." Here tamid emphasizes covenant kindness and unending royal favor extended to David's covenant partner's son.

  • Psalms 16:8 — "I have set the LORD always before me; because He is at my right hand I shall not be moved." In this devotional context, tamid (translated "always") illustrates unbroken trust and continual focus on Yahweh.

  • Psalms 51:3 — "For I acknowledge my transgressions, and my sin is always before me." Here David uses tamid to express the unrelenting weight and constant consciousness of his sin prior to assurance of pardon.

  • Isaiah 52:5 — "Now therefore, what have I here,” says the LORD, “that My people are taken away for nothing? Those who rule over them make them wail,” says the LORD, “and My name is blasphemed continually every day." In this prophetic complaint, tamid highlights the ongoing, unceasing reproach brought against God's holy name by foreign oppressors.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The poetic retribution in Obadiah 1:16 utilizes the common prophetic motif of the "cup of divine wrath." The transition from Edom's temporary, boastful drinking on Zion to the nations' tamid (continual) drinking of judgment demonstrates that human triumphs over God's kingdom are short-lived, whereas divine retribution against unrepentant hostility is exhaustive and final.

The word tamid thus shifts the perspective from temporal earthly events to the total, unceasing vindication of God's sovereignty over the nations.
