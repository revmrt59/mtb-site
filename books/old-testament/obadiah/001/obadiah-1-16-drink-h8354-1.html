DRINK - (shâthâh, Strong's H8354)

Summary:

The primary Hebrew verb shatah means "to drink," referring to the physical consumption of liquids for refreshment, nourishment, or fellowship, as well as the figurative imbibing of wisdom, futility, or divine retribution. In Obadiah 1:16, the verb describes the inescapable execution of God's judgment upon hostile nations. As God's holy mountain experienced the dark cup of suffering and judgment, so all surrounding rebellious nations will be compelled to drink continually of divine retribution until they are completely consumed and brought to nothing.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root shatah is a foundational Hebrew verb used throughout the Old Testament canon to express the act of drinking water, wine, milk, or other beverages. It appears regularly alongside akal ("to eat") to depict basic human sustenance, everyday hospitality, festive banqueting, covenant ratification meals, or conditions of severe famine and drought. In ancient Near Eastern imagery and Israel's prophetic tradition, the physical act of imbibing liquid developed a powerful metaphorical sense: to drink from a cup handed out by Yahweh was to receive and absorb one's appointed portion, whether that portion brought blessing and salvation or terrible judgment and utter ruin.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, shatah denotes the physical act of ingesting liquid into the body. Its basic semantic range includes:

  • Satisfying basic physical thirst (drinking water in the desert or at a well).

  • Consuming wine or strong drink in context of celebrations, feasts, or revelry.

  • Partaking in a ritual or covenantal meal before God.

  • Metaphorically imbibing an experience, internalizing wisdom, or swallowing divine wrath.

In prophetic discourse, shatah frequently functions as a key component of the "cup of judgment" motif. In this figurative extended sense, to "drink" means to suffer the direct, unavoidable consequences of divine justice against human pride and rebellion.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament literature, shatah carries nuances shaped by context:

  • Physical Sustenance and Hospitality: Narrative passages emphasize God's provision of water or the basic human necessity of refreshment (e.g., Genesis 24:14; Deuteronomy 9:9).

  • Covenant Fellowship: Drinking signifies shared peace and communion, such as when Israel's leaders beheld God on Mount Sinai and ate and drank in His presence (e.g., Exodus 24:11; Genesis 26:30).

  • Frustration and Deprivation: Under covenant curses, drinking becomes a picture of insufficiency or terror, where individuals drink without being satisfied or drink water with trembling anxiety (e.g., Haggai 1:6; Ezekiel 12:19).

  • Figurative Divine Judgment: Prophetic literature uses the verb to describe nations forced to swallow the deadly draught of God's holy anger against wickedness (e.g., Jeremiah 51:7; Obadiah 1:16).

4. Theological Implications

The metaphorical application of shatah to divine retribution highlights several key theological principles:

  • Sovereign Initiative: The cup of judgment is prepared and administered by God Himself. Those who drink do not choose their potion; they are subjected to God's holy decree.

  • Exhaustive Justice: The physical act of drinking requires absorbing the fluid into oneself. When applied to judgment, it signifies that divine retribution penetrates to the very core of the guilty nation, leaving no escape or residual innocence.

  • Universal Moral Order: Judgment may begin with God's covenant people at His holy mountain, but it inevitably spreads to all proud and oppressive nations. God's moral governance requires that every empire consuming others will ultimately drink its own condemnation.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:16, shatah occurs in the Qal stem and imperfect form (3rd person masculine), presenting the action as an impending and unfolding reality for the surrounding pagan powers. The verse establishes a solemn comparison: "For as you drank on My holy mountain, so shall all the nations drink continually."

Whether "you drank" refers to Judah drinking the bitter cup of divine chastisement during Jerusalem's fall, or to Edom arrogantly carousing and celebrating over Zion's ruin, the prophetic lens shifts swiftly to the universal fate of the nations. The nations will "drink continually" (yishtu tamid). This unceasing drinking indicates that divine justice will not be a temporary setback but an uninterrupted, overwhelming deluge of judgment.

Furthermore, Obadiah pairs shatah with "swallow" (yala'u), reinforcing that the nations must drain the cup to its dregs. The effect of this drinking is absolute obliteration: "they shall be as though they had never been." The word shatah here moves far beyond simple physical ingestion to portray the complete absorption of God's righteous wrath by Edom and all oppressive worldly powers.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 24:11 – "But on the nobles of the children of Israel He did not lay His hand. So they saw God, and they ate and drank."

  • Proverbs 9:5 – "Come, eat of my bread and drink of the wine I have mixed."

  • Haggai 1:6 – "You have sown much, and bring in little; you eat, but do not have enough; you drink, but you are not filled with drink..."

  • Ezekiel 12:19 – "They shall eat their bread with anxiety, and drink their water with dread, that her land may be emptied of all who are in it..."

  • Jeremiah 51:7 – "Babylon was a golden cup in the LORD's hand, that made all the earth drunk. The nations drank her wine; therefore the nations be mad."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The combination of shatah ("drink") and yala'u ("swallow") in Obadiah 1:16 forms a vivid poetic parallelism emphasizing total consumption. The sentence leaves no room for partial judgment; the penalty must be fully imbibed.

  • The stark contrast between drinking in fellowship with God (as seen in covenant context like Exodus 24:11) and drinking God's retribution (as in Obadiah 1:16) reveals the dual trajectory of human destiny before Yahweh: holy communion or sovereign condemnation.
