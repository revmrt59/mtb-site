SWALLOW - ( la'ah , Strong's H3886 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb la'ah means to swallow down, gulp, or consume greedily. In its unique biblical occurrence in Obadiah 1:16, it describes the complete and irreversible judgment of the pagan nations. Rather than a refreshing drink, the enemies of God's people will be forced to gulp down the cup of divine wrath to the very dregs. This violent, involuntary swallowing results not in satisfaction, but in absolute annihilation, leaving them "as though they had never been." The word conveys the terrifying speed, intensity, and finality of God's sovereign justice.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root la'ah evokes the physical action of rapidly swallowing or gulping down a liquid. It is a highly descriptive, almost visceral word that depicts someone drinking so greedily or rapidly that they cannot pause. It carries the imagery of open throats, gasping, and helpless consumption. In ancient Near Eastern culture, the metaphor of drinking from a cup was frequently used to describe experiencing one's destiny. To drink a sweet, abundant cup represented blessing, while drinking a bitter cup represented judgment. The action of swallowing (la'ah) indicates that the judgment cannot merely be tasted and set aside; it must be fully internalized, drained to the bottom, and absorbed.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb la'ah means to swallow, swallow down, or gulp down. It refers to the rapid, involuntary, or total throat action of consuming liquid. The word is closely related to the homonym la'a, which can mean to talk rashly or speak wildly under pressure, suggesting a throat that is out of control or choking. In the context of drinking, however, it denotes the physical act of swallowing completely. The semantic range is narrow, focused on the complete, physical ingestion of a substance. It contrasts with ordinary, leisurely drinking by emphasizing the rapid, overwhelming, and exhaustive nature of the intake.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

In poetic and prophetic literature, swallowing is a potent symbol of destruction, defeat, and conquest. The earth "swallows" the wicked, and Sheol "swallows" the living. In Obadiah 1:16, la'ah is paired with "drink" (shathah). Together, these verbs paint a picture of forced, excessive consumption. The nations do not merely sip the cup of God's wrath; they gulp it down. The physical image is one of choking on a liquid that is forced down the throat, leading to drowning or total incapacitation. This word picture illustrates a reversal of fortune. Edom and the nations rejoiced and drank celebratory drinks on God's holy mountain after Jerusalem's fall. Now, they must drink a different cup—one of divine judgment—and swallow it down until they are completely consumed.

4. Theological Implications

  • The Wrath of God: The cup of God's wrath is a prominent theological motif throughout Scripture. The use of la'ah emphasizes that God's judgment is total. None of His wrath can be avoided, diluted, or spilled; it must be fully swallowed and internalized by those who reject Him.

  • The Law of Retribution (Lex Talionis): Edom's celebratory "drinking" on Mount Zion is met with a divine, forced "drinking" and "swallowing." God's judgment is perfectly fitted to the sin. Those who gloated over the ruin of God's people are forced to swallow the consequences of their own arrogance.

  • Total Annihilation of Evil: The ultimate result of this swallowing is that the wicked "shall be as though they had never been." Evil does not have the final word; it is swallowed up and erased from history by the sovereign justice of Yahweh.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:16, the prophet addresses the house of Jacob and contrasts their past suffering with the future fate of Edom and the Gentile nations. Edom had stood by and even participated in the Babylonian sack of Jerusalem, celebrating on God’s "holy mountain." The transition is stark: "For as you drank on My holy mountain, So shall all the nations drink continually." The nations will drink "continually"—an unceasing judgment. The climax of this forced drinking is that they will "swallow." This means they will not be able to spit it out or escape its deadly effects. The nuance here is not of a refreshing beverage, but of a lethal poison. By swallowing it, they internalize their own destruction, leading to their absolute disappearance from the global stage.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

Because la'ah is a hapax legomenon (occurring only once in this specific verbal form in the Hebrew Bible), direct exact-word cross-references are limited to this verse. However, the closely related Hebrew root la'a (Strong's H3216), which shares the same semantic space of throat action/gulping, and the primary target verse help contextualize the usage:

  • Obadiah 1:16 – "For as you drank on My holy mountain, So shall all the nations drink continually; Yes, they shall drink, and swallow, And they shall be as though they had never been."

  • Job 6:3 – "For now it would be heavier than the sand of the sea; Therefore my words have been rash." (Uses the related root la'a, referring to words choked out or swallowed wildly under intense grief).

  • Proverbs 20:25 – "It is a snare for a man to devote rashly what is holy, And afterward to reconsider his vows." (Uses the related root la'a, depicting a sudden, unthinking gulping or rash commitment).

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Greek Septuagint (LXX) translates the Hebrew wela'u ("and they shall swallow") with katabesontai, which means "they shall go down" or "they shall descend." This translation highlights the downward trajectory of those who drink this cup—they descend straight into the grave (Sheol). Alternative manuscript traditions suggest the Hebrew root here might mean "to stagger" or "reel." However, the primary Masoretic text preserves the meaning of "swallow" or "gulp down," which provides a far more intense and terrifying image of complete consumption that perfectly fits the next clause: "And they shall be as though they had never been."
