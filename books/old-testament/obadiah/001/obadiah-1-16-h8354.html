DRANK - ( shathah , Strong's H8354 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb shathah refers primarily to the physical act of drinking, but in prophetic literature, it frequently carries a potent metaphorical meaning related to swallowing the cup of divine judgment. In Obadiah 1:16, the word is used in a dual sense: first, to describe the profane, celebratory drinking of Edom and the nations upon God's holy mountain, and second, to depict the forced, continuous drinking of the cup of God's wrath by those very same nations. This contrast highlights a profound biblical truth: those who mock God's holiness through self-indulgent revelry will ultimately consume the bitter dregs of His righteous judgment, drinking until they are utterly undone.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The root shathah depicts the basic human necessity and pleasure of drinking liquids, most commonly water, wine, or milk. In ancient Near Eastern culture, drinking together sealed covenants, marked festivals, and symbolized fellowship or shared destiny. However, in prophetic imagery, sharing a drink is transformed into a sobering metaphor. To drink from a cup handed over by Yahweh means to fully receive and experience whatever portion He has allocated—whether it is the cup of salvation and blessing, or the staggering cup of wrath and ruin.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb shathah is a highly active verb meaning to drink, imbibe, feast, or swallow. The basic meaning is to take liquid into the mouth and swallow it. In the Qal stem, it refers to drinking as a physical action, participating in a banquet or feast, or drinking figuratively (such as drinking violence, iniquity, or judgment). In passive or figurative constructions, it denotes being saturated with or thoroughly experiencing the consequences of an action.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Literal Banqueting: It is frequently paired with the verb akal (to eat) to describe feasting, rejoicing, or daily sustenance.

• The Cup of Wrath: The most intense metaphorical use of shathah is drinking the cup of the Lord's fury. This cup causes staggering, madness, and ultimate destruction to those who consume it.

• The Poisoned Draught: In prophetic judgment, the wicked do not merely take a sip; they are forced to drink, swallow, and drain the cup to its dregs, symbolizing a judgment that is complete, irreversible, and thoroughly internalized.

### 4. Theological Implications

• The Principle of Retributive Justice: The drinking of the nations mirrors their sin. They drank in drunken celebration over Judah's downfall on God's holy mountain; therefore, they must drink the cup of God's wrath continuously.

• Sovereign Allocation: God is the one who pours the drink. Whether mercy or wrath, humanity must ultimately consume what God administers. No nation can refuse the cup of His sovereign decree.

• The Ultimate Cup: This Old Testament imagery of shathah lays the theological foundation for the New Testament "cup" that Jesus Christ prayed to avoid in Gethsemane but ultimately drank to the dregs on behalf of believers, sparing them from drinking the wrath described in Obadiah.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:16, the verb shathah occurs twice to create a stark, poetic contrast: "For as you drank on My holy mountain, so shall all the nations drink continually; yes, they shall drink, and swallow, and they shall be as though they had never been."

• The first "drank" refers to the historical sacrilege committed by the Edomites and surrounding nations who caroused, celebrated, and desecrated Jerusalem (God's holy mountain) after its fall to Babylon. Their drinking was an act of arrogant triumph.

• The subsequent uses of "drink" shift the meaning to compulsory, endless drinking of divine judgment. The shift from the specific historical sin to the universal judgment ("all the nations") shows that Edom's pride and fate represent the ultimate end of all hostile world powers who oppose God's people.

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

The root shathah is closely related to the noun mishteh, which means a feast, banquet, or drinking-bout. This highlights the feast-like, celebratory atmosphere that the enemies of God enjoyed at the expense of God's suffering people before the tables were turned. Additionally, the second verb in Obadiah 1:16, "swallow" (la'a), intensifies shathah. It indicates gulping down or choking on the liquid, showing that the judgment is not just tasted but fully absorbed, leaving the nations devastated and "as though they had never been."
