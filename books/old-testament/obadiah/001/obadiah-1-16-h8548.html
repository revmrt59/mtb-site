CONTINUALLY - ( tamid , Strong's H8548 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word tamid denotes continuity, uninterrupted duration, or regularity. In biblical usage, it often describes the regular, daily ritual duties of the tabernacle and temple, such as the perpetual fire, the daily morning and evening sacrifices, and the showbread. In the prophetic context of Obadiah 1:16, it underscores the unceasing and unrelenting nature of God's judgment upon the hostile nations, contrasting their temporary, arrogant revelry with the permanent, ongoing consequence of divine justice.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun tamid is derived from an unused root meaning to stretch or extend. It acts adverbially to describe actions that are constant, habitual, or scheduled to recur at regular, unbroken intervals. Rather than representing an absolute, infinite eternity, the word pictures a reliable, continuous flow or an established, unending routine. In the ancient Near Eastern context, where religious rituals were easily disrupted by war, political shifts, or negligence, the concept of a tamid (perpetual) service represented total stability, devotion, and alignment with the divine order. It is the steady heartbeat of devotion that does not stop.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Tamid refers to continuity, perpetuity, or constant occurrence. It has two primary semantic nuances depending on the context:

• Uninterrupted, non-stop activity: Something that is constant, ongoing, and without pause (such as a fire that never goes out).

• Regular, daily recurrence: Something that happens consistently at its appointed time day after day (such as the daily morning and evening sacrifices).

Lexically, it functions as a substantive noun used adverbially to mean "continually," "perpetually," "always," or "regularly."

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Perpetual Fire: The fire on the bronze altar was commanded to burn tamid (Leviticus 6:13), symbolizing that the way of access to God through sacrifice was always open and that Israel's devotion must never sleep.

• The Showbread: Known as the "bread of the presence," it was to be set before the Lord tamid (Exodus 25:30), picturing God's perpetual provision and fellowship with the twelve tribes.

• The High Priest's Breastplate: Aaron wore the breastplate of judgment over his heart tamid (Exodus 28:29) as a memorial before the Lord, illustrating God's constant remembrance of His people.

• Personal Devotion: The psalmists use tamid to describe a life of unbroken communion, where God's praise is tamid in the mouth (Psalm 34:1) and the believer's eyes are tamid toward the Lord (Psalm 25:15).

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, tamid highlights the intersection of time and worship. God is not served by sporadic bursts of emotional zeal but by steady, covenantal faithfulness. The perpetual ordinances of the Old Covenant foreshadowed the permanent, once-for-all priesthood and intercession of Jesus Christ, who lives "always" to make intercession for His people. When applied to judgment, the word takes on a sobering theological weight: it demonstrates that God’s holy opposition to sin and His execution of justice are not brief outbursts of anger, but are constant, fixed, and permanent realities.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:16, the prophet addresses the proud nation of Edom and the surrounding pagan nations: "For as you drank on My holy mountain, so shall all the nations drink continually; Yes, they shall drink, and swallow, and they shall be as though they had never been."

• The "drinking" on God's holy mountain refers to the Gentile nations desecrating Jerusalem, carousing in victory, or the cup of wrath that Judah was forced to drink under Babylon.

• The turning point is the word tamid ("continually"). While Judah's drinking of the cup of discipline was temporary and had an end, the hostile nations will drink the cup of divine wrath continually.

• In this context, tamid means that the judgment of the nations will be unceasing and irreversible. There will be no relief, no intermission, and no restoration for those who set themselves against God's covenant people. The drinking of judgment is so absolute and continuous that it results in their total consumption—they "shall be as though they had never been."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 34:1: "I will bless the LORD at all times; His praise shall continually be in my mouth."

• Proverbs 6:21: "Bind them continually upon your heart; Tie them around your neck."

• Isaiah 58:11: "The LORD will guide you continually, And satisfy your soul in drought, And strengthen your bones; You shall be like a watered garden, And like a spring of water, whose waters do not fail."

• Hosea 12:6: "So you, by the help of your God, return; Observe mercy and justice, And wait on your God continually."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In second-temple Judaism and Rabbinic literature, the daily morning and evening burnt offering (the olat tamid) became so central to Jewish identity that it was referred to simply as "The Tamid" (as seen in the Mishnah tractate Tamid). When Antiochus Epiphanes sought to destroy Jewish worship in the Danielic prophecies, his primary target was the removal of the tamid—the daily, continual sacrifice. This underscores how the word represents the very life-support system of the covenant relationship between God and His people.
