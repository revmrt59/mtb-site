SWALLOW - (lûwaʻ, Strong's H3886)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb laʿaʿ basic lexical meaning denotes gulping down, swallowing eagerly or hastily, or making a choking/swallowing motion with the throat. In Obadiah 1:16, the word describes the complete, unrestrained consumption of the cup of God's wrath by the wicked nations. By stating that the nations will drink and "swallow down," Obadiah emphasizes that the execution of divine judgment will be full, unavoidable, and overwhelming, leaving the wicked entirely consumed and reduced to nothingness.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root laʿaʿ belongs to a cluster of Hebrew verbs concerned with the throat, swallowing, and physical ingestion. In ancient Near Eastern imagery, drinking and swallowing carried both literal and metaphorical significance. To swallow something whole signified taking it in completely without reservation or ability to regurgitate it.

While related physical terms describe ordinary eating or drinking, laʿaʿ conveys an intense, uncontrolled, or desperate act of gulping or swallowing down. In poetic imagery, the throat represents the channel through which life is either sustained by food or choked by distress and divine wrath. In prophetic judgment literature, the metaphor of forced drinking and swallowing illustrates the unyielding force of God's holy retribution against arrogant nations.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, laʿaʿ means "to swallow," "to gulp down," "to choke down," or "to sip hastily." Standard Hebrew lexicons classify its core sense around the mechanical action of taking liquid or food rapidly into the throat.

The lexical semantic range encompasses:

  • To gulp down liquid rapidly or greedily.

  • To swallow back or speak choked, rash words under extreme physical or emotional distress.

  • To ingest something thoroughly so that it disappears completely.

It is critical to distinguish between the basic physical action of swallowing (Layer 1) and the theological metaphor of divine judgment (Layer 3) that the context of Obadiah imports into the word. The word itself carries the core sense of rapid or complete swallowing; the surrounding prophetic imagery defines what is being swallowed—namely, the figurative cup of Yahweh's indignation.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In poetic and prophetic contexts, laʿaʿ conveys an irresistible or overwhelming taking-in of liquid or speech. The nuance depends heavily on whether the action is self-induced through anguish or imposed through judgment.

When describing speech born of agony, the term paints a picture of words choked down or uttered rashly as a person gasps for breath under intense pressure. When describing judgment, the term emphasizes the thoroughness of the punishment: those who drink cannot merely sample the cup; they are compelled to gulp it down entirely.

The verb highlights the intensity of the experience. It is not passive receiving, but active, thorough ingestion that leaves nothing remaining in the cup.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, laʿaʿ in prophetic literature contributes to the biblical doctrine of divine justice and the "cup of wrath." Throughout the Old Testament, God's righteous judgment against evil is portrayed as a cup filled with intoxicating wine handed to rebellious nations.

1. The Inevitability of Divine Retribution: God's judgment is not symbolic or partial. When the wicked are forced to drink, they must swallow the judgment entirely. Human pride cannot resist or reject the decree of God.

2. Lex Talionis (Eye for an Eye Justice): In Obadiah, Edom celebrated Jerusalem's ruin by drinking in revelry upon Mount Zion. God turns their sinful consumption back upon them: as they drank in mocking triumph, so they and all hostile nations will be forced to swallow down the bitter wrath of God.

3. Total Destruction of Hostile Powers: The act of gulping down the cup leads directly to total annulment—they become "as though they had never been." Human empires that set themselves against God's covenant people will be fully consumed by their own judgment.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:16, the prophet addresses the prideful nation of Edom and the surrounding Gentile nations that gloated over Judah's calamity. Edom had treated God's holy mountain, Zion, as a venue for arrogant feastings and plunder during Jerusalem's fall.

Obadiah uses a sharp lexical turn using the image of drinking: "For as you drank on My holy mountain, so all the nations shall drink continually; yes, they shall drink and swallow down, and they shall be as though they had never been."

The grammatical form of laʿaʿ in this target passage is the Qal perfect, 3rd person plural. The Qal stem portrays the subject directly performing the action, while the perfect form presents the action as a completed, definitive reality in the prophetic vision. The 3rd person plural subject refers to "all the nations" who opposed Yahweh and His people.

The contrast in the verse is stark:

  • Edom drank in arrogant revelry upon God's holy mountain.

  • The nations will be forced to drink and "swallow down" (laʿaʿ) the cup of God's judgment.

The addition of laʿaʿ alongside the standard verb for drinking (shatah) creates a cumulative effect. The nations will not simply sip or sample the judgment; they will gulp it down completely until the cup is empty and their existence is brought to an end.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

Outside of Obadiah 1:16, the same Hebrew verb occurs in the Old Testament poetic corpus to describe speech choked down under anguish:

  • Job 6:3 — "For now it would be heavier than the sand of the sea; therefore my words have been rash." [Here translated as rash/choked down speech under heavy grief].

7. Other Relevant Insights

The poetic parallelism in Obadiah 1:16 links the act of swallowing (laʿaʿ) directly with the outcome: "and they shall be as though they had never been." This demonstrates how prophetic Hebrew poetry uses physical verbs of ingestion to convey irreversible ontological consequences. To swallow judgment completely means to be swallowed up by judgment entirely. What begins as a forced gulping down of God's righteous wrath ends in the total removal of the proud nation from the stage of history.
