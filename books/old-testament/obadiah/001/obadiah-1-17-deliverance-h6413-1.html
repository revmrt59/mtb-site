DELIVERANCE - (pᵉlêyṭâh, Strong's H6413)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun peletah refers to an escape, a means of deliverance, or a surviving remnant spared from judgment, destruction, or catastrophe. Derived from a verbal root expressing rescue and deliverance, the word highlights the preservation of life in the wake of overwhelming disaster. In Obadiah 1:17, peletah designates the surviving remnant that will be established on Mount Zion. Within Obadiah's prophecy, the word establishes a decisive contrast between Edom, which faces complete destruction without survivors, and God's covenant people, who are promised an enduring place of safety, holiness, and restored inheritance.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The prophecy of Obadiah addresses the ancient conflict between Israel and Edom, descendants of Jacob and Esau. Following the calamity that befell Jerusalem under foreign invasion, Edom actively participated in gloating over Judah's downfall, looting her possessions, and intercepting fugitives. In response to this pride and hostility, God announced inescapable judgment against Edom. Within this prophetic backdrop, peletah serves as a crucial turning point in the book's structure. While Edom is threatened with total devastation and the elimination of its survivors, God promises that Jerusalem and Mount Zion will hold a surviving remnant. Across the Old Testament canon, peletah functions within historical narratives, wisdom contexts, and prophetic announcements to identify those who escape divine wrath, military devastation, or natural ruin.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, peletah denotes "escape," "deliverance," or "that which has escaped" (i.e., a remnant or group of survivors). The word can refer either abstractly to the act or reality of escaping, or concretely to the entity—the human remnant—that survives a life-threatening catastrophe. It is closely related to the verbal root palat, which signifies delivering, rescuing, or bringing into safety. In Classical Hebrew usage, peletah does not merely imply a fortunate flight from danger, but specifically highlights the merciful preservation of life following a period of judgment, conquest, or affliction.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Hebrew Scriptures, peletah displays a consistent semantic range focused on survival and preservation:

  • In historical narratives, it describes physical survivors who escape famine, military invasion, or destruction (e.g., Joseph's recognition of God's providence in preserving a remnant in Egypt).

  • In post-exilic historical texts, such as Ezra and Nehemiah, peletah identifies the fragile community of Jewish exiles who survived the Babylonian captivity and returned to rebuild Jerusalem.

  • In prophetic literature, the term carries deep theological weight, often linked with eschatological restoration. Prophets like Isaiah, Joel, and Obadiah use peletah to describe the holy remnant gathered on Zion whom the Lord preserves through judgment and sets apart for His kingdom purposes.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, peletah demonstrates the enduring tension between divine justice and divine mercy. Judgment against sin is real and severe, yet God's covenant promises ensure that destruction is never ultimate for His chosen people. The existence of a peletah reveals that God always preserves a remnant according to His sovereign grace. Furthermore, the term underscores that deliverance is not humanly engineered; rather, escape from ultimate ruin is an act of divine mercy. This OT remnant motif forms an essential foundation for the canonical understanding of God's redeemed community, which is preserved through trial and ultimately established in holiness under God's sovereign rule.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:17, the prophet declares: "But on Mount Zion there shall be deliverance [peletah], and there shall be holiness; the house of Jacob shall possess their possessions." In this verse, peletah occurs as a feminine singular noun in the absolute state.

The immediate context emphasizes a dramatic reversal of fortunes:

  • In verses 10–14, Edom mistreated the fugitives of Judah during Jerusalem's disaster.

  • In verse 18, Obadiah announces that "no survivor [sarid] shall remain of the house of Esau."

  • In contrast, verse 17 promises that "on Mount Zion there shall be deliverance [peletah]."

Through peletah, Obadiah highlights that Mount Zion will become the sanctuary of survival. The passage tightly links this deliverance with two further realities: "holiness" (qodesh) and the reclamation of covenant land ("possess their possessions"). The surviving remnant is not merely saved from physical destruction; they are established in a holy place and restored to their God-given inheritance.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 45:7 — "And God sent me before you to preserve a posterity for you in the earth, and to save your lives by a great deliverance."

  • 2 Kings 19:30 — "And the remnant who have escaped of the house of Judah shall again take root downward, and bear fruit upward."

  • Joel 2:32 — "For in Mount Zion and in Jerusalem there shall be deliverance, as the LORD has said, among the remnant whom the LORD calls."

  • Ezra 9:8 — "And now for a little while grace has been shown from the LORD our God, to leave us a remnant to escape, and to give us a peg in His holy place..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A notable canonical connection exists between Obadiah 1:17 and Joel 2:32. Both prophets specifically locate peletah ("deliverance" / "those who escape") on "Mount Zion" and "Jerusalem." Joel highlights the calling of the Lord upon those who survive, while Obadiah emphasizes the holiness of Zion and the restoration of the covenant inheritance. Together, these prophetic witnesses demonstrate a unified canonical theme: God establishes Zion as the place of refuge and ultimate restoration for His preserved remnant.
