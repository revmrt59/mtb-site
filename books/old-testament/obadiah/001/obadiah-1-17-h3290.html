JACOB - ( ya'aqob , Strong's H3290 )

Summary:

The name Jacob historically carries the meaning of "heel-catcher" or "supplanter," reflecting a character marked by grasping, striving, and deception. However, in the prophetic context of Obadiah 1:17, "the house of Jacob" represents the reunited, restored covenant people of God. In sharp contrast to the total destruction promised to Edom (the house of Esau) for their pride, Jacob is promised deliverance, holiness, and the reclamation of their inherited land. The name serves as a monument of divine grace, showing how God takes a flawed, struggling people and transforms them into the victorious heirs of His eternal promises.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The name ya'aqob is derived from the verbal root aqab, which means "to grasp by the heel," "to supplant," "to circumvent," or "to overreach." Historically, this name was given to the younger twin son of Isaac and Rebekah because he emerged from the womb holding onto his brother Esau’s heel.

Throughout his early life, Jacob lived up to his name, using deception to secure both the birthright and the patriarchal blessing from his brother. The name represents human striving, manipulation, and weakness. Yet, after his life-changing wrestling match with the Angel of the Lord at Peniel, where he was crippled and blessed, his identity was transformed. In the prophetic literature, the name "Jacob" is frequently used to refer to his descendants, the nation of Israel, reminding them of both their humble, flawed origins and the relentless grace of the God who chose them.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, ya'aqob is a proper noun that refers to:

• The historical patriarch Jacob, the son of Isaac, grandson of Abraham, and father of the twelve tribes of Israel.

• The corporate nation of Israel, encompassing all the descendants of the patriarch.

• The "house of Jacob" (bet ya'aqob), which functions as a theological term for the covenant community of God, especially in times of exile, trials, or restoration.

In Obadiah 1:17, the term "house of Jacob" designates the rightful heirs of the covenant promises of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Though they have been severely disciplined and temporarily dispossessed of their land, they remain the objects of God's unyielding covenant loyalty.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

The name ya'aqob occurs hundreds of times in the Old Testament, frequently paired in Hebrew poetry with "Israel." In the prophets, several word pictures are associated with Jacob:

• The Needy but Chosen Servant: Jacob is pictured as weak, small, and vulnerable, yet upheld by the powerful right hand of God.

• The Consuming Fire: In Obadiah 1:18, the "house of Jacob" is described as a blazing fire that will consume the stubble of the "house of Esau." This picture shifts Jacob from a victim of foreign aggression to an instrument of divine judgment.

• The Re-established House: The "house of Jacob" is pictured as a rebuilt, secure dwelling place on God’s holy mountain, standing firm while the strongholds of pagan nations crumble.

### 4. Theological Implications

The use of "Jacob" in Obadiah carries profound theological weight:

• Sovereign Election: The division between Jacob and Esau goes back to the womb, where God sovereignly chose the younger over the elder. Obadiah's prophecy reinforces that God's electing love is irreversible. Edom’s pride led to its utter extinction, whereas Jacob’s election guaranteed its ultimate survival.

• Covenant Faithfulness (Hesed): Despite Jacob’s historical failures, sins, and subsequent judgments, God does not abandon His covenant. The preservation of Jacob is rooted entirely in the character of Yahweh, not the merit of the nation.

• Eschatological Restoration: The restoration of the house of Jacob foreshadows the final victory of the kingdom of God. It promises that the people of God will ultimately triumph over every hostile worldly power that seeks their destruction.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:17, the prophet details a dramatic reversal of fortunes. Edom had gloated over the destruction of Jerusalem, actively participated in the slaughter of Jewish fugitives, and helped themselves to Israel’s wealth.

However, God declares that "on Mount Zion there shall be deliverance." While Mount Esau (Edom) will be laid waste, Mount Zion will be characterized by "holiness." In this context, "the house of Jacob shall possess their possessions." The phrase "possess their possessions" indicates a complete reclamation of the inheritance that was violently stripped from them. The use of "Jacob" here emphasizes that the very people whom Edom despised, exploited, and sought to eliminate will, by God's power, outlast them and inherit the land forever.

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

In Obadiah 1:18, the prophet mentions both "the house of Jacob" and "the house of Joseph." This is a significant geographic and political detail. The "house of Jacob" typically represents the southern kingdom of Judah, while the "house of Joseph" represents the northern kingdom of Israel. By naming both, the prophet looks forward to a day of complete national reunification. The ancient divisions that fractured the twelve tribes will be healed, and a unified, restored people of God will stand together to inherit the promises.
