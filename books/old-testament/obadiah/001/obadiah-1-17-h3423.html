POSSESS - ( yarash , Strong's H3423 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yarash carries a dynamic dual meaning of dispossessing an enemy and taking possession of their land as an inheritance. In Obadiah 1:17, it signifies a dramatic reversal of fortunes: the house of Jacob, once plundered and displaced by Edom, will be restored by God to reclaim and occupy their rightful covenant inheritance, demonstrating God's ultimate sovereignty and covenant faithfulness.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The verb yarash is a foundational Old Testament covenant word. It describes more than mere passive ownership; it depicts an active process of driving out previous occupants to claim an inheritance designated by a higher authority. The word picture is deeply rooted in the conquest of Canaan, where Israel had to dispossess the Canaanite nations in order to possess the Promised Land. It implies displacement, victory, and the subsequent settlement of a physical territory given as a divine gift.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • yarash means to take possession of, inherit, or dispossess.

  • In the active sense (Qal stem), it denotes seizing control of land, inheriting property, or succeeding someone as an heir.

  • In the causative sense (Hiphil stem), it means to drive out, dispossess, ruin, or impoverish another party.

  • The noun form used alongside it in Obadiah 1:17 (moras) refers to the possession or inheritance itself. Thus, the verb acts as the reclamation of that which was legally and divinely designated as one's own.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Inheritance as Gift and Duty: Under the Mosaic Covenant, yarash emphasizes that the land is a gift from God, yet Israel must actively step in and drive out the current inhabitants to occupy it. Faith and action are intertwined.

  • Heirship: It describes the legal and relational right of a son to succeed his father and inherit the family estate, as seen when Abraham questioned who would inherit his house.

  • Dispossession of the Wicked: It highlights God's judgment, where the wicked are dispossessed of their security and the righteous inherit the land.

4. Theological Implications

  • Covenant Faithfulness: yarash is tied directly to the Abrahamic Covenant. God promised the land to Abraham's descendants; Israel's possessing of the land is the concrete realization of God's ancient promise.

  • Divine Sovereignty and Judgment: God is the ultimate owner of the earth. He dispossesses nations due to their wickedness and grants the land to whom He chooses. Israel's right to possess is entirely dependent on God's sovereign decree.

  • Restoration and Hope: In exilic and post-exilic prophetic literature, yarash represents the physical and spiritual homecoming of God's remnant, symbolizing the restoration of fellowship, security, and inheritance.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Obadiah addresses the pride and cruelty of Edom, who gloated over Judah's downfall and helped foreign invaders plunder Jerusalem. In Obadiah 1:17, the phrase "the house of Jacob shall possess their possessions" represents a profound historical and poetic reversal.

The very people who were dispossessed, humiliated, and scattered will return to Mount Zion. The use of yarash here shows that Israel will not merely return to a peaceful coexistence; they will dispossess those who illegally occupied their land (specifically Edom). The holiness and deliverance on Mount Zion provide the spiritual foundation for this physical reclamation. What was stolen by enemies will be restored by God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 15:7: "Then He said to him, 'I am the LORD, who brought you out of Ur of the Chaldeans, to give you this land to inherit [yarash] it.'"

  • Numbers 33:53: "you shall dispossess [yarash] the inhabitants of the land and dwell in it, for I have given you the land to possess [yarash] it."

  • Deuteronomy 30:5: "Then the LORD your God will bring you to the land which your fathers possessed [yarash], and you shall possess [yarash] it."

  • Psalm 37:11: "But the meek shall inherit [yarash] the earth, And shall delight themselves in the abundance of peace."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament), yarash is frequently translated by the Greek verb kleronomeo, which means "to inherit." This connects the physical land promises of the Old Testament to the New Testament concept of the believer's eternal inheritance in Christ. Furthermore, the wordplay in Obadiah 1:17 ("possess their possessions" / yarsu et morasehem) underscores the absolute certainty of the reversal. The phonetic similarity between the verb and the noun emphasizes that what they are possessing is indeed their own rightful, divinely appointed heritage.
