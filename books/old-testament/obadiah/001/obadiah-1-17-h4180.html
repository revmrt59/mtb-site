POSSESSIONS - ( morash , Strong's H4180 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word morash refers to a possession, inheritance, or something held as a rightful property. Derived from the root yarash, which means to inherit, occupy, or take possession of, this noun carries deep theological weight regarding divinely appointed ownership. In the context of Obadiah 1:17, it represents the restoration of God's covenant people to the land and blessings that were rightfully theirs by divine decree, which had been temporarily seized or contested by hostile neighbors like Edom. It signifies not merely material property, but the concrete realization of God's sovereign promises to His people.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient Israel, land and inheritance were not mere commodities to be bought and sold at whim; they were sacred trusts allocated by Yahweh to specific tribes and families. The root yarash describes the action of occupying a land, driving out usurpers, and claiming an inheritance. The noun morash represents the concrete reality of that heritage—the actual possession. To be dispossessed of one's morash was to lose one's visible token of covenant standing and divine favor. Therefore, the return of a dispossessed heir to reclaim their morash is a powerful picture of restoration, justice, and the vindication of God’s covenant faithfulness.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Morash means a possession, inheritance, or land holding. It denotes that which is inherited or held by right of succession or divine allotment. In its physical sense, it refers to geographical territory assigned to a nation or group. In poetic and wisdom literature, the term extends metaphorically to the inner life, referring to the "possessions" of the mind—specifically, one's innermost thoughts, plans, and cherished purposes.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The word is used sparingly in the Hebrew Bible, but each occurrence presents a vivid word picture:

• An Inheritance Reclaimed: In territorial contexts, it depicts a rightful heir returning to evict intruders and re-establish their boundary lines.

• Shattered Hopes: In poetic contexts, it pictures the private "possessions" of the human heart—the dreams, designs, and aspirations that a person hoards within themselves, which can be broken or swept away by tragedy.

• Desolate Ownership: In prophetic judgments, it depicts a city stripped of human inhabitants and handed over as a "possession" to wild beasts, illustrating total reversal and divine judgment.

4. Theological Implications

• Sovereign Allotment: God is the ultimate owner of the earth, and He reserves the right to distribute nations, territories, and possessions according to His sovereign plan.

• Covenant Security: Though God's people may lose their possessions temporarily due to their unfaithfulness and exile, their ultimate inheritance is secure because God's covenant promises are irrevocable.

• Eschatological Reversal: The final reclamation of morash on Mount Zion points to the ultimate triumph of the kingdom of God. It assures believers that all things stolen, broken, or usurped by the enemies of God will be fully restored to the rightful heirs of the kingdom.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:17, the setting is the tragic aftermath of Jerusalem's fall. The Edomites, descendants of Esau, had gloated over Judah's calamity, intercepted fleeing refugees, and moved in to occupy Jewish lands. Obadiah uses a striking wordplay: "the house of Jacob shall possess (yarash) their possessions (morash)."

This statement guarantees a complete reversal of fortunes. Edom sought to steal what God had not given them, but they would be dispossessed. Jacob, stripped of everything, would return to reclaim their divinely deeded heritage. The recovery of their morash is directly tied to Mount Zion becoming a place of deliverance and holiness, showing that true possession of God's blessings is always linked to spiritual renewal and the presence of God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Job 17:11 – "My days are past, my plans are broken, / The thoughts [possessions] of my heart." (Here, morash refers metaphorically to the cherished plans and inner thoughts that Job felt were being snatched away from him in his suffering.)

• Isaiah 14:23 – "I will also make it a possession for the porcupine, / And marshes of muddy water; / I will sweep it with the broom of destruction," says the LORD of hosts." (In this judgment against Babylon, the pride of the empire is brought low as its magnificent capital is handed over as a physical "possession" to wild desert creatures.)

• Obadiah 1:17 – "But on Mount Zion there shall be deliverance, / And there shall be holiness; / The house of Jacob shall possess their possessions." (In this restoration prophecy, the word guarantees that God's people will re-occupy and enjoy the inheritance promised to their ancestors.)

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between Esau (Edom) and Jacob (Israel) runs throughout the Old Testament. Esau famously despised his birthright and inheritance for a temporary meal. Later, his descendants tried to violently seize Jacob’s inheritance. God's preservation of the morash for the house of Jacob underscores a vital spiritual truth: those who value God's covenant promises will ultimately inherit them, while those who despise or try to steal them will end up with nothing.
