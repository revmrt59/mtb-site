DELIVERANCE - ( peleytah , Strong's H6413 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun peleytah refers to an escape, a deliverance, or a remnant that survives a catastrophic judgment. In Obadiah 1:17, it points to the preservation of a holy remnant on Mount Zion, serving as a beacon of hope and restoration for God's covenant people amidst the devastating judgment of the surrounding nations.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• Peleytah derives from the root verb palat, which means to escape, slip away, or deliver.

• The word pictures a narrow escape from a sweeping disaster, war, or divine judgment. It evokes the image of a small group of survivors emerging from the ruins of a collapsed structure or fleeing a battlefield where the rest of their companions have fallen.

• In ancient Near Eastern warfare, total annihilation of an enemy was common. The peleytah is the exception to this rule—the small portion that survives by divine providence or narrow escape, ensuring the continuation of the family line.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The semantic range of peleytah includes "escape," "deliverance," "survivors," or "that which has escaped."

• It functions both abstractly (referring to the act or means of deliverance) and concretely (referring to the actual group of people who survive).

• In a theological context, it designates the preserved remnant of Israel spared by God's sovereign grace through a period of national exile and chastisement.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The word is frequently used alongside agricultural or military metaphors, depicting a seed or a small shoot left behind after a harvest or wildfire, keeping the hope of future growth alive.

• It highlights God's ultimate ownership over life and history; even when judgment is total from a human standpoint, God secures an escaped portion to fulfill His covenant promises.

• Historically, the term is applied to survivors of famine, sword, and captivity, emphasizing that survival is never accidental but is a direct act of divine preservation.

4. Theological Implications

• Peleytah is foundational to the biblical doctrine of the Remnant. It demonstrates that God's judgment of His covenant people is never utterly destructive; He always preserves a seed of faith.

• It showcases the faithfulness of God. Despite Israel's deep disobedience, God does not allow them to be entirely consumed like Sodom and Gomorrah.

• It connects salvation directly to God's chosen place. True deliverance and escape are ultimately found on Mount Zion, anchoring hope in God's presence, sanctuary, and messianic promises.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Obadiah 1:17, peleytah stands in stark contrast to the fate of Edom described in the subsequent verses. While Esau (Edom) will face absolute destruction with no survivor left (v. 18), Mount Zion will experience deliverance.

• The escape of Judah is not merely physical survival from Babylonian or Edomite forces, but a spiritual restoration. The delivered remnant will dwell in "holiness" and possess their rightful inheritance.

• This contrast underscores the security of those who belong to the Lord. While the proud nations of the world face complete ruin, God's suffering people are guaranteed a future, a hope, and a secure escape.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 45:7 - "And God sent me before you to preserve a posterity for you in the earth, and to save your lives by a great deliverance."

• Joel 2:32 - "And it shall come to pass That whoever calls on the name of the Lord Shall be saved. For in Mount Zion and in Jerusalem there shall be deliverance, As the Lord has said, Among the remnant whom the Lord calls."

• Ezra 9:15 - "O Lord God of Israel, You are righteous, for we are left as a remnant, as it is this day."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• While the wicked seek to escape judgment through their own strength, wealth, or fortresses (such as Edom's high rocks in Obadiah 1:3-4), true peleytah is only found in God's provision.

• This Old Testament concept of the delivered remnant prepares the way for New Testament teachings on election and grace, where God preserves a holy remnant of believers through faith in Jesus Christ (Romans 11:5).
