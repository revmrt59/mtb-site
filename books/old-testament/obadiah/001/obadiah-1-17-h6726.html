ZION - ( Tsiyyon , Strong's H6726 )

Summary:

Zion (Tsiyyon) refers originally to the ancient Jebusite fortress in Jerusalem captured by King David, which expanded semantically to represent the Temple Mount, the entire city of Jerusalem, the covenant people of God, and ultimately the eschatological place of divine presence, refuge, and reign. In Obadiah 1:17, Mount Zion stands in stark contrast to Mount Esau (Edom); while Edom faces total destruction, Zion is established as the sanctuary of deliverance, holiness, and the restoration of God's people to their promised inheritance.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The word Tsiyyon has its geographic roots in the topography of Jerusalem. Originally, it designated the southern hill or ridge of the city, a natural fortress bounded by the Kidron Valley on the east and the Tyropoeon Valley on the west. This was the highly defensible Jebusite stronghold. When King David conquered this citadel, he renamed it the "City of David" and brought the Ark of the Covenant there, transforming a pagan military stronghold into the spiritual center of the covenant community. As Jerusalem grew, the name Zion expanded to include the northern hill (Mount Moriah) where Solomon built the temple, and eventually, it became a poetic synonym for the entire city of Jerusalem and its inhabitants.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Tsiyyon is a proper noun meaning "Zion." The exact etymological origin is debated, but it likely derives from a root meaning "citadel," "fortress," or "stronghold." An alternative root suggests a connection to "dry land," "parched place," or "sunny hill."

Lexically, the term has a tiered semantic range:

• The literal geographic hill or citadel captured by King David.

• The Temple Mount, viewed specifically as the earthly dwelling place of Yahweh.

• The entire city of Jerusalem, representing the political and spiritual capital of Israel.

• The nation of Israel or the covenant people personified, often referred to as "the daughter of Zion."

• The spiritual, eschatological city of God, where Yahweh's final salvation, justice, and rule are realized.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, Zion is used as a rich literary and theological metaphor:

• The Mountain of Divine Choice: Zion is not the highest or grandest mountain geographically, but it is elevated above all others by virtue of God's choice to dwell there. It represents the mystery of divine election.

• A Place of Refuge and Safety: Because Yahweh is present in Zion, she is portrayed as a secure fortress that cannot be shaken. Even when surrounded by hostile nations, Zion remains secure under the wings of the Almighty.

• A Mother of Nations: In the Psalms, Zion is pictured as a mother who gives birth to and nurtures God's people, including those from foreign, formerly hostile nations.

• The Throne of the Great King: Zion is the seat of the Messiah's sovereign rule over the earth, from which His scepter of power extends.

### 4. Theological Implications

Theologically, Zion is the earthly intersection of heaven and earth. It is where God condescends to dwell among His people, making Himself accessible. It is inextricably linked to the Davidic Covenant and God's promises of an eternal kingdom.

Zion represents the holiness of God. Because God is holy, His dwelling place must be holy, demanding ethical and ritual purity from those who approach it. Crucially, Zion is the geographic focal point of salvation history. Redemption, the giving of the law, the coronation of the King, and the final judgment of the nations all flow outward from Zion.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:17, Tsiyyon is set in a sharp, dramatic contrast with "the mount of Esau" (Edom/Mount Seir). The book of Obadiah is a prophecy of judgment against Edom for their pride, violence, and malicious betrayal of Judah during the Babylonian invasion. While Edom boasted of its impenetrable rocky strongholds in Petra, God declares that Mount Esau will be thoroughly searched, looted, and left desolate with "no survivor" (v. 18).

In contrast, Mount Zion—which had been temporarily desecrated and desolated by foreign invaders—is promised a glorious restoration. Obadiah declares three realities for Zion:

• Deliverance (peleytah): Zion will not be destroyed. It will be the place where a saved remnant survives and gathers.

• Holiness (qodesh): The mountain will be cleansed of foreign defilement and restored to its sacred, set-apart state.

• Restoration ("possess their possessions"): The house of Jacob will reclaim the lands and inheritance that had been stolen by Edom and other foreign powers. Zion becomes the launching pad for the kingdom of God to be established over all the earth.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 2:6 - "Yet I have set My King On My holy hill of Zion."

• Psalm 125:1 - "Those who trust in the LORD Are like Mount Zion, Which cannot be moved, but abides forever."

• Isaiah 2:3 - "Many people shall come and say, 'Come, and let us go up to the mountain of the LORD, To the house of the God of Jacob; He will teach us His ways, And we shall walk in His paths.' For out of Zion shall go forth the law, And the word of the LORD from Jerusalem."

• Isaiah 52:7 - "How beautiful upon the mountains Are the feet of him who brings good news, Who proclaims peace, Who brings glad tidings of good things, Who proclaims salvation, Who says to Zion, 'Your God reigns!'"

• Joel 2:32 - "And it shall come to pass That whoever calls on the name of the LORD Shall be saved. For in Mount Zion and in Jerusalem there shall be deliverance, As the LORD has said, Among the remnant whom the LORD calls."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• New Testament Fulfilment: In the New Testament, the physical concept of Zion is elevated to a spiritual and heavenly reality. Believers in Jesus Christ have not come to Mount Sinai (terror and law) but to "Mount Zion and to the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem" (Hebrews 12:22).

• Messianic Fulfillment: Peter quotes Isaiah 28:16 in 1 Peter 2:6, identifying Jesus Christ as the "chief cornerstone" laid in Zion. He is the ultimate foundation of the spiritual Zion, the Church, where those who believe will never be put to shame.

• Eschatological Hope: Revelation 14:1 depicts the Lamb standing on Mount Zion with the 144,000, representing the final, triumphant gathering of God’s redeemed people in secure, holy fellowship.
