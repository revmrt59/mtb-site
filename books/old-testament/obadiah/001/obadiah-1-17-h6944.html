HOLINESS - ( qodesh , Strong's H6944 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word qodesh refers to that which is set apart, consecrated, and dedicated entirely to God. Derived from a root meaning to cut or separate, it denotes a state of sacredness that is distinct from the common, profane, or unclean. In Obadiah 1:17, this word marks a dramatic shift from Israel’s humiliation and defilement by foreign enemies to a future state of restoration, purification, and divine possession. Mount Zion, once desecrated by Edom and other nations, is reclaimed by Yahweh as a sanctuary of absolute holiness, serving as both a refuge for the redeemed remnant and a monument to God's faithful presence among His people.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The underlying root of qodesh carries the primary image of cutting, dividing, or setting apart. To understand qodesh, one must visualize a boundary line drawn in the sand or a fence erected to separate two completely different spheres. On one side lies the common, the ordinary, and the everyday (known in Hebrew as chol). On the other side lies the qodesh—that which belongs exclusively to God and is marked off from common touch.

In the Ancient Near East, Mount Zion was not merely a political capital; it was the earthly footstool of Yahweh. When pagan nations conquered Jerusalem, they did not just occupy a city; they defiled a sacred space. By promising that Mount Zion would once again be qodesh, the prophet uses a powerful spatial word picture. The mountain would be cordoned off once more, cleansed of pagan pollution, and re-consecrated as the exclusive domain of the Holy One of Israel.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun qodesh expresses the quality of holiness, sacredness, or consecrated status. It designates a person, place, object, or period of time that has been separated from common use and dedicated to the service and glory of God.

Its semantic range includes:

• The Sacredness of God's Character: The absolute moral purity and transcendent majesty of Yahweh, who is entirely distinct from His creation.

• Consecrated Places and Things: The sanctuary (the Holy Place and the Most Holy Place), the altar, the priestly garments, and the sacrifices.

• Consecrated Times: The Sabbath and the annual feasts, which are set apart from normal labor.

• A Consecrated People: Israel as a nation called out from the world to be uniquely devoted to Yahweh.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

In the Old Testament, qodesh is frequently paired with concrete physical rituals to illustrate its meaning.

• The Veil: The veil in the Tabernacle separated the Holy Place (qodesh) from the Most Holy Place (qodesh haqodashim). This physical barrier served as a stark visual reminder of the division between sinful humanity and the absolute purity of God.

• The Priestly Diadem: The High Priest wore a gold plate on his turban engraved with the words, "Holiness to the LORD" (qodesh le-YHWH). This indicated that the priest’s entire life and office were set apart for divine service.

• Sacrificial Meat: Offerings designated as qodesh could only be eaten by clean individuals in holy places. To treat these items as ordinary was a capital offense, reinforcing that holiness is a matter of life and death.

### 4. Theological Implications

Theologically, qodesh reminds us that holiness is not merely the absence of sin; it is the presence of God. It is a positive, dynamic reality.

• God is the Source: Nothing is holy in and of itself. Things, places, and people only become qodesh because of their relationship to Yahweh. It is His presence that sanctifies.

• The Threat of Defilement: Because God is holy, He cannot tolerate the presence of sin, rebellion, or uncleanness in His sanctuary. Defilement must be cleansed, and violators must be removed or atoned for.

• Sovereign Reclaiming: The ultimate theological message of qodesh in prophecy is that God will not allow His holy name or His holy hill to be permanently desecrated. Though He may permit temporary judgment, His zeal for His own holiness guarantees the ultimate purification and restoration of His people and His land.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:17, the term qodesh is used in a specific eschatological and geographic context. The prophet has just described the devastating pride and cruelty of Edom, who rejoiced over the destruction of Jerusalem and participated in its plunder. Edom treated Mount Zion as a common, conquered hill.

The turning point of the prophecy occurs in verse 17: "But on Mount Zion there shall be deliverance, and there shall be holiness..."

Here, qodesh functions as the antithesis of the pagan desecration described earlier.

• Purification from Pagan Defilement: The mountain will no longer be trampled by foreign oppressors or polluted by idols. It will be restored to its pristine, consecrated status.

• The Sanctuary of the Remnant: The "deliverance" (those who escape) and "holiness" are joined. The restored community will live in a state of covenant purity, reflecting the holiness of the God who saved them.

• The Foundation for Possession: Only when Zion is restored to holiness can the house of Jacob safely "possess their possessions." True inheritance and spiritual authority are directly contingent upon the restoration of holiness.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 15:11: "Who is like You, O LORD, among the gods? Who is like You, glorious in holiness, Fearful in praises, doing wonders?"

• Psalm 29:2: "Give to the LORD the glory due to His name; Worship the LORD in the beauty of holiness."

• Isaiah 35:8: "A highway shall be there, and a road, And it shall be called the Highway of Holiness. The unclean shall not pass over it, but it shall be for others. Whoever walks the road, although a fool, shall not go astray."

• Zechariah 14:20: "In that day “HOLINESS TO THE LORD” shall be engraved on the bells of the horses: The pots in the LORD’s house shall be like the bowls before the altar."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• The New Testament Connection: The spatial holiness of Mount Zion in Obadiah points forward to the spiritual holiness of the Church. Believers in Christ are called "saints" (holy ones, hagioi), and the local church is described as a holy temple in the Lord (Ephesians 2:21). The boundary line of qodesh is now drawn around the hearts and lives of God’s redeemed people.

• Eschatological Fulfillment: The ultimate fulfillment of Obadiah 1:17 is realized in the New Jerusalem (Revelation 21:2, 27), where no unclean thing will ever enter, and the city itself is called the "holy city."

• Pastoral Encouragement: For those who feel ruined, defiled, or plundered by the enemy, Obadiah 1:17 offers a powerful promise: God is in the business of reclaiming what was lost, cleansing what was defiled, and restoring qodesh to His people.
