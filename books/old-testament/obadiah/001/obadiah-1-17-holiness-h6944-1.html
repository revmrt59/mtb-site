HOLINESS - (qôdesh, Strong's H6944)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun qodesh denotes sacredness, holiness, a dedicated holy place, or something set apart exclusively for Yahweh's divine domain. In Obadiah 1:17, qodesh describes the consecrated condition and sanctuary character of Mount Zion following divine judgment upon Edom. It establishes that God's holy mountain, having been invaded and profaned by hostile foreign powers, will be fully purified, restored to its sacred state, and set apart once again as Yahweh's inviolable domain where His redeemed people abide.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The term qodesh lies at the core of Old Testament theology regarding the nature of God and the sacred order. The root concept pertains to that which is marked off, dedicated, or separated from common (chol) or profane use and brought into the sphere of God's sovereign presence and ownership.

In the ancient Near Eastern world, places and objects were often considered sacred by virtue of ritual consecration, but in biblical revelation, qodesh derives its definition entirely from the character of Yahweh. Because God alone is intrinsically holy, any person, place, vessel, time, or mountain that belongs to Him acquires the status of qodesh. In prophetic literature, Mount Zion is frequently designated as God's "holy mountain," signifying that Jerusalem was chosen by Yahweh as His earthly dwelling place among His covenant people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, qodesh refers to:

  • Sacredness, holiness, or set-apartness.

  • A holy place, sanctuary, or dedicated sacred precinct.

  • Holy things, consecrated gifts, or sacrificial offerings.

The word operates primarily in the realm of status and sphere: it distinguishes the divine and consecrated from the mundane and defiled.

In Obadiah 1:17, the noun qodesh is masculine singular in the absolute state. Syntax indicates that "holiness" functions substantively to define the state or essential quality of Mount Zion in the era of restoration ("there shall be holiness").

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, qodesh appears in several distinct categories of usage:

  • Sacred Spaces: Describing the tabernacle, the temple, the Holy Place, the Most Holy Place, and Mount Zion.

  • Sacred Objects and Offerings: Referring to consecrated sacrifices, anointed priest garments, tithes, and sanctuary furniture that could not be used for common purposes.

  • The Divine Name and Nature: Highlighting the absolute purity and unapproachable majesty of Yahweh Himself (e.g., His "holy name").

  • Eschatological Restoration: Characterizing the future state of Jerusalem and the land of Israel when God purifies His people and removes all defilement from their midst.

The nuance of qodesh is not merely negative (separation from sin) but positive (consecration unto God). When applied to a geographical location like Mount Zion, it denotes that the location is owned, governed, and sanctified by God's manifest presence.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of qodesh in prophetic promises underscores the ultimate purpose of God's redemptive judgment. God does not merely deliver His people from political oppression; He restores them to a right, consecrated relationship with Himself within a purified sphere.

In Obadiah, the contrast between Mount Esau (Edom) and Mount Zion (Israel) is sharp. Edom represents arrogant pride, self-sufficiency, and violence against God's covenant family. Zion represents the place of divine election and sanctuary. Edom faces ultimate destruction, whereas Zion is marked by deliverance (peleytah) and holiness (qodesh). The promise that Zion will be qodesh confirms that Yahweh's covenant commitments will overcome historical desecration and that holiness will ultimately triumph over pagan wickedness.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

The book of Obadiah centers on Yahweh's judgment against Edom for their violence, gloating, and plunder during Jerusalem's downfall (Obadiah 1:10–14). Edom had entered the gates of God's people, rejoiced over their destruction, and profaned the land.

In verse 17, the prophet shifts from judgment on the nations to the vindication and restoration of Israel: "But on Mount Zion there shall be deliverance, and there shall be holiness; the house of Jacob shall possess their possessions."

The contextual role of qodesh here is profound:

  • Counteract Desecration: Edom treated Zion as common plunder; God declares it will be restored as qodesh—an inviolable sanctuary where profane nations can no longer intrude.

  • Connection with Deliverance: Deliverance (peleytah) and holiness (qodesh) stand side by side. Deliverance secures the survivors from physical destruction, while holiness restores the spiritual order and sanctuary presence of God.

  • Foundation for Inheritance: Only when Mount Zion is re-established in qodesh can "the house of Jacob... possess their possessions." Holiness is the necessary environment for the enduring fulfillment of God's territorial and covenantal promises to His people.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Leviticus 21:22 – "He may eat the food of his God, both the most holy and the holiness [holy]."

  • Psalms 105:3 – "Glory in His holy [holiness] name; let the hearts of those rejoice who seek the Lord!"

  • Jeremiah 31:23 – "Thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel: 'They shall again use this speech in the land of Judah and in its cities, when I bring back their captivity: "The Lord bless you, O home of justice, and mountain of holiness!"'"

  • Ezekiel 43:12 – "This is the law of the temple: The whole area surrounding the mountaintop shall be most holiness [holy]. Behold, this is the law of the temple."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The pairing of "deliverance" (peleytah) and "holiness" (qodesh) in Obadiah 1:17 provides a crucial pattern in Old Testament prophetic literature. Salvation from enemies is never an end in itself; it serves the higher goal of establishing a consecrated domain for Yahweh's worship and reign.

Furthermore, Jeremiah 31:23 presents a direct thematic parallel to Obadiah 1:17, using qodesh to describe Mount Zion as the "mountain of holiness" upon the return of the captives. Both prophets highlight that post-exilic and eschatological hope centers on the re-consecration of Zion as God's sacred dwelling place.
