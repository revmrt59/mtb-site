ZION - (Tsîyôwn, Strong's H6726)

Summary:

The Hebrew proper noun tziyyon (Zion) designates the fortress hill of Jerusalem, the temple mount, and by canonical expansion, the holy city as the place of God's dwelling and sovereign rule. In Obadiah 1:17, tziyyon refers specifically to Mount Zion as the sacred locus of divine reign and covenant restoration. It serves as the spiritual and geographical counterpoint to Edom's Mount Esau, marking the precise site where God establishes deliverance, holiness, and the full recovery of His people's inheritance.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The proper noun tziyyon originally designated the ancient Jebusite stronghold situated on the south-eastern hill of Jerusalem. Upon its capture by King David, it became known as the "City of David." With the transfer of the Ark of the Covenant to the hill and the subsequent construction of the Solomon temple, the scope of tziyyon expanded poetically and prophetically to encompass the temple mount, the entire city of Jerusalem, and the covenant community gathered under Yahweh's reign.

In the prophetic vision of Obadiah, tziyyon is set within a dramatic geopolitical and spiritual conflict. Obadiah pronounces judgment against Edom for its pride, violence, and malicious gloating over Judah's downfall. Edom boasted in its inaccessible rocky heights and mountain fastnesses (Mount Esau). In sharp contrast, Obadiah points to Mount Zion—not as a monument of human military might, but as the place chosen and preserved by Yahweh Himself.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, tziyyon is a proper noun that functions as a geographic and theological place name. While various etymologies have been suggested (such as roots signifying a "parched place" or a "citadel/stronghold"), lexical analysis establishes that the word functions primarily as a designation for the sacred mountain of Jerusalem where Yahweh enthroned His presence.

In Hebrew lexicography, tziyyon carries a rich range of associations:

  • The physical hill and citadel of ancient Jerusalem.

  • The temple mount where God reveals His glory and accepts worship.

  • The holy city of Jerusalem in its covenantal identity.

  • The eschatological sanctuary where God delivers His remnant and gathers the nations under His rule.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, tziyyon appears extensively in poetic and prophetic literature, particularly in Psalms, Isaiah, Jeremiah, and the Minor Prophets.

The term carries distinct nuances depending on its context:

  • Physical and Topographical: Identifying the geographical location of Jerusalem's central height.

  • Cultic and Relational: Highlighting Zion as Yahweh's chosen habitation, the place where He dwells among His people and receives their praise.

  • Royal and Covenantal: Denoting the seat of the Davidic dynasty and God's eternal covenant promises to Israel.

  • Prophetic and Eschatological: Representing the sanctuary of divine salvation, where the Lord judges the nations, purifies a remnant, and extends His righteous kingdom to the ends of the earth.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, tziyyon embodies the doctrine of God's dwelling presence and sovereign protection. It is the place where Yahweh establishes His sanctuary, demonstrating that divine grace, rather than human strength, secures true stability.

  • Sovereignty over Human Pride: Zion stands in theological opposition to human strongholds. While human fortresses fail under divine scrutiny, Zion remains secure because God resides within her.

  • Sanctuary of Deliverance and Holiness: Zion is not merely a political capital; it is a holy place defined by God's character. Salvation from Zion brings both safety from enemies and consecration to God.

  • Kingdom Vindication: The ultimate victory of God's kingdom is tied to Zion, signaling the triumph of Yahweh's rule over all hostile earthly powers.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:17, tziyyon appears as a singular proper noun in the absolute state, combined with the word for mountain (har) to form the phrase "Mount Zion." Grammatically and structurally, this phrase stands at a crucial turning point in Obadiah's prophecy.

The immediate context contrasts the fate of Mount Esau with that of Mount Zion:

  • Mount Esau (representing Edom) sought security in its physical height, but is sentenced to utter plundering, shame, and destruction (Obadiah 1:8–10, 18).

  • Mount Zion (representing God's covenant people) becomes the place where divine deliverance (peleytah) emerges, where holiness (qodesh) reigns, and where the house of Jacob reclaims its rightful inheritance.

Obadiah uses tziyyon here to anchor God's promise of covenant reversal. Though Jerusalem was temporarily breached and humiliated by foreign invaders with Edom's complicity, God declares that Mount Zion will remain the permanent center of His redemptive activity. The presence of tziyyon in this verse guarantees that God's judgment on hostile nations directly results in the restoration and sanctification of His redeemed remnant, culminating in the declaration that "the kingdom shall be the LORD's" (Obadiah 1:21).

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Psalms 48:2 — "Beautiful in elevation, the joy of the whole earth, is Mount Zion on the sides of the north, the city of the great King." Here tziyyon is celebrated as the exalted throne-city of Yahweh, reflecting His majesty and defense of His people.

  • Isaiah 2:3 — "Many people shall come and say, 'Come, and let us go up to the mountain of the LORD, to the house of the God of Jacob; He will teach us His ways, and we shall walk in His paths.' For out of Zion shall go forth the law, and the word of the LORD from Jerusalem." Here tziyyon highlights the sacred center from which divine instruction and truth extend to all nations.

  • Joel 2:15 — "Blow the trumpet in Zion, consecrate a fast, call a sacred assembly;" Here tziyyon functions as the spiritual gathering place where God's covenant community convenes to humble themselves and seek divine mercy.

  • Isaiah 37:32 — "For out of Jerusalem shall go a remnant, and those who escape from Mount Zion. The zeal of the LORD of hosts will do this." Here tziyyon is designated as the refuge from which an escaped remnant emerges by the sovereign initiative and zeal of Yahweh.

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Canonical Parallelism: Obadiah's promise of deliverance on Mount Zion closely echoes Joel 2:32 ("For in Mount Zion and in Jerusalem there shall be deliverance"), affirming a unified prophetic witness that God's holy mountain serves as the ordained sanctuary for His preserved remnant.

  • Prophetic Inversion: In Obadiah, human pride builds high strongholds in the rocks of Edom only to be brought low, whereas God's humble hill of Zion is exalted as the enduring realm of holiness, deliverance, and eternal divine dominion.
