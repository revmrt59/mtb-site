DEVOUR - (ʼâkal, Strong's H398)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb ʾakhal basic meaning is "to eat" or "to consume." In its literal usage, it describes the physical act of taking in food for nourishment. In prophetic and figurative contexts, it frequently describes complete destruction, such as fire consuming fuel, a sword devouring victims, or judgment sweeping away a rebellious nation. In Obadiah 1:18, ʾakhal describes the total, consuming judgment executed upon the house of Esau (Edom). Under the vivid metaphor of fire consuming stubble, the word contributes to Obadiah’s prophecy by emphasizing that Edom’s destruction will be total, leaving behind no remnants or survivors.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root ʾakhal is one of the most common and essential verbs in the Hebrew scriptures. At its simplest level, it describes human and animal eating, foundational to daily survival and communal fellowship. Because food and eating are woven into ancient Near Eastern covenant meals, sacrifices, and daily existence, the verb appears across every genre of the Old Testament—from early narrative and legal codes to wisdom literature, psalms, and prophetic announcements.

Beyond physical ingestion, biblical authors frequently employ ʾakhal metaphorically. Fire is said to "eat" or "devour" wood and offerers' sacrifices; pestilence and sword "eat" populations; and hostile nations "eat" or swallow up vulnerable populations. In prophetic judgment literature, this metaphor illustrates total eradication, where an object of judgment is so thoroughly consumed that nothing usable or viable remains.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, ʾakhal denotes the act of eating, consuming, or devouring.

  • In literal contexts, it refers to partaking of bread, meat, fruit, or sacrificial offerings.

  • In metaphorical contexts, it signifies destructive consumption: fire burning fuel, locusts strip-mining vegetation, or judgment utterly destroying a kingdom or family line.

In Obadiah 1:18, the verb appears in the Qal stem, perfect form, third-person plural. The Qal stem represents the simple active action ("they shall devour" or "they devoured"), depicting the reunited house of Israel acting as the fire and flame that thoroughly consumes the dry stubble of Edom.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

The semantic range of ʾakhal moves smoothly between ordinary physical eating and intense metaphorical consumption:

  • Physical Nourishment & Covenant Feasts: It describes partaking of food provided by God, eating sacrificial meals before the Lord, or sharing hospitality.

  • Prohibitions & Laws: In legal contexts, the word governs dietary boundaries, such as prohibitions against eating blood, unclean animals, or sacred portions reserved exclusively for priests.

  • Judgment by Divine Fire: Fire sent by God is frequently portrayed as "eating" hostiles or sacrificial altars, demonstrating God's holiness and non-negotiable holiness.

  • National Devastation: Prophetic warnings describe invading armies or agricultural disasters (like locust swarms) as eating up the land, crops, and wealth of a target nation.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of ʾakhal in prophetic judgment passages rests on God's divine justice and retributive righteousness (lex talionis).

In the book of Obadiah, Edom had previously stood by, rejoiced over, and actively participated in the plundering and devouring of Judah during Jerusalem's calamity. God’s righteous order dictates that the measure Edom meted out would return upon its own head. By employing ʾakhal to portray Israel as the fire and Edom as the stubble, the text communicates that judgment is not merely external or accidental; it is the sovereign God executing complete justice. The devouring fire leaves no prideful arrogance intact, demonstrating that opposition to God's covenant kingdom results in ultimate reduction to nothingness.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:18, the immediate setting is a dramatic poetic contrast and judgment oracle against Edom (the house of Esau). Obadiah writes: "The house of Jacob shall be a fire, and the house of Joseph a flame; but the house of Esau shall be stubble; they shall kindle them and devour them, and no survivor shall remain of the house of Esau, for the LORD has spoken."

The grammar—Qal stem, perfect form, third-person plural—presents the action as a completed, definitive event in the prophetic vision. The subjects ("they") are the combined house of Jacob and house of Joseph—representing the full, restored people of God.

The immediate imagery relies on agricultural reality: dry stubble (qash) caught in a raging fire does not merely singe; it vanishes in an instant. By using ʾakhal alongside "kindle" (dalaq), Obadiah emphasizes that Edom will be completely consumed. The passage underscores this total end with the explicit outcome: "and no survivor shall remain of the house of Esau." The word ʾakhal therefore establishes the thoroughness of the destruction, validated by the final divine signature: "for the LORD has spoken."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:17 — "but of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil you shall not eat, for in the day that you eat of it you shall surely die."

  • Judges 9:15 — "And the bramble said to the trees... 'let fire come out of the bramble and devour the cedars of Lebanon!'"

  • Jeremiah 2:3 — "Israel was holiness to the LORD, the firstfruits of His increase. All that devour him will offend; disaster will come upon them, says the LORD."

  • Joel 2:25 — "So I will restore to you the years that the swarming locust has eaten..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The combination of "fire," "flame," and "stubble" in Obadiah 1:18 creates a standard prophetic judgment motif found elsewhere in Old Testament poetry. Dry stubble left in a field after harvest is entirely defenseless against flame. By framing Edom as stubble and Israel as the flame empowered by God, the text visually demonstrates that human pride and geographic fortresses (such as Edom's rocky strongholds mentioned earlier in the book) offer zero protection when divine retribution burns.
