FLAME - ( lehabah , Strong's H3852 )

Summary:

The word lehabah refers to a flame of fire, symbolizing intense heat, rapid consumption, and bright illumination. In the Old Testament, it frequently represents God’s consuming judgment, His holy presence, or the destructive power of warfare. In Obadiah 1:18, it depicts the house of Joseph as an unstoppable, burning flame that completely devours the house of Esau (stubble), emphasizing the swift, total, and sovereign execution of divine justice through God's covenant people.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The word lehabah derives from an unused root meaning to gleam or flash. It describes the bright, licking tongue of a fire.

• It captures both the visual brilliance of a leaping fire and its intense physical effect of heat and consumption.

• In some contexts, the word is used metaphorically for the glittering, flashing blade of a sword or the polished head of a spear. This links the visual of a leaping flame to the flashing of a weapon in battle, conveying both sudden destruction and piercing power.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The noun lehabah means "flame," "hot flame," or "blade (of a sword/spear)."

• It designates the luminous, burning gas of a fire, especially when rising high and spreading rapidly.

• In theological contexts, it denotes the manifest wrath of God, the instruments of His judgment, or His majestic, purifying presence.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• lehabah is used to describe physical flames, such as the miraculous flame of the altar that ascended to heaven in the sight of Manoah and his wife, or the flame of the burning bush where God appeared to Moses.

• It is used to depict military devastation, where conquering armies leave cities in ashes, turning homes into a rising flame of ruin.

• It serves as a vivid metaphor for divine judgment, where God's wrath behaves like a roaring forest fire or a desert wind that fans a flame to consume the wicked, leaving them without root or branch.

4. Theological Implications

• Holiness and Judgment: Fire and flame represent God's unapproachable holiness and His active judgment against sin. What is unholy and proud cannot survive in the presence of the lehabah.

• Sovereign Reversal: God uses His redeemed people as the instruments of His judgment. The weak, exiled, and formerly oppressed (Jacob and Joseph) are transformed into a holy flame, while the proud and secure (Esau/Edom) are reduced to dry stubble.

• Inescapable Wrath: Dry stubble has no defense against a roaring flame. The pairing of these images highlights the absolute ease with which divine judgment overcomes human pride and self-sufficiency.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Obadiah 1:18, the prophet uses a powerful tri-fold metaphor: Jacob is "fire," Joseph is "flame" (lehabah), and Esau is "stubble."

• Historically, Edom (Esau) gloated over the destruction of Jerusalem, actively assisted its Babylonian conquerors, and seized Jewish territory. Edom believed its high, rocky fortresses made it completely secure and invincible.

• The term lehabah highlights the active, spreading agent of destruction. Combined with the "fire" of Jacob, this flame will completely consume Edom.

• The image of "stubble" emphasizes that Edom's pride offers zero resistance to God's judgment; their destruction will be swift, complete, and leave no survivors, vindicating God's covenant faithfulness to Israel.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 3:2: "And the Angel of the LORD appeared to him in a flame of fire from the midst of a bush..."

• Judges 13:20: "And it happened, as the flame went up toward heaven from the altar..."

• Psalm 106:18: "A fire was kindled in their company; The flame burned up the wicked."

• Isaiah 5:24: "Therefore, as the tongue of fire devours the stubble, And the dry grass sinks down in the flame..."

• Joel 2:3: "A fire devours before them, And behind them a flame burns..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The parallel between Jacob (the southern kingdom) and Joseph (the northern kingdom) reuniting as "fire" and "flame" indicates a fully restored, unified Israel executing God's judgment. Though once divided and defeated, under God's sovereignty they become a single, consuming fire.

• This prophecy warns against the illusion of human security. Just as dry stubble cannot negotiate with or stop a roaring flame, human strength and pride cannot withstand the consuming fire of God's holy justice.
