SURVIVOR - ( sarid , Strong's H8300 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun sarid denotes a survivor, a remnant, or one who escapes a life-threatening catastrophe, battle, or divine judgment. Derived from a root meaning to flee or escape, it describes the few individuals left alive after a sweeping devastation. In the prophetic literature, particularly in Obadiah 1:18, sarid underscores the absolute totality of God's judgment against Edom (the house of Esau). While Israel is promised a surviving remnant, Edom is warned that they will have no survivors remaining—signifying complete national erasure and the finality of divine justice.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near East, warfare was devastatingly thorough, but the absolute annihilation of an entire people group was rare. Normally, even in the most brutal conquests, a few individuals managed to slip through the military dragnet, fleeing into the mountains, deserts, or neighboring territories. These individuals were the sarid—the survivors.

In a tribal, lineage-based society, the survivor carried a weight far beyond mere physical existence. The sarid was the sole spark remaining to keep the family name, lineage, and heritage alive. To be left without a sarid was the ultimate ancient tragedy; it meant a family line or an entire nation was completely blotted out from remembrance under heaven, leaving no historical footprint.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The masculine noun sarid refers to a survivor, a remnant, or an escaper from a catastrophe. It is derived from the verbal root sarad, which carries the primary meaning of escaping, fleeing, or breaking away from danger.

Lexically, the term refers to the physical preservation of life amidst overwhelming destruction. While other Hebrew terms for "remnant" can carry a more spiritual or positive theological hope, sarid is highly concrete, focusing on the literal, physical escapees who survive a sword, famine, pestilence, or military campaign.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The term sarid is frequently paired with negative particles to describe total military defeat. To "leave no survivor" is a standard biblical idiom for total, uncompromising conquest.

  • The Sparing of None: During the conquest of Canaan, the term is repeatedly used to show that Israel left "no survivor" of the hostile kings and kingdoms they fought, emphasizing the thoroughness of the divine judgment executed through Joshua.

  • The Post-Battle Fugitive: It paints the picture of a lonely fugitive running from a smoking battlefield. The sarid is the one who ran fast enough, hid well enough, or was overlooked by the victors.

  • The Loss of Lineage: In wisdom literature, particularly in Job, the word is used to describe the utter ruin of the wicked, who lose both their wealth and their offspring, leaving no survivor to inherit their dwelling places.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, sarid highlights the limits of human strength and the certainty of divine sovereignty. When God decrees judgment, human escape mechanisms fail. No speed, cunning, or fortified wall can produce a sarid if God has determined a complete end.

Furthermore, the word highlights the contrast between God's dealings with His covenant people versus pagan nations. When God judged Israel, He promised to always preserve a remnant (she'ar or sarid). Though Israel was severely disciplined, God never allowed them to be completely wiped out; a survivor always remained to carry forward the line of the Messiah. In contrast, when God executed final judgment on unrepentant, hostile nations who opposed His kingdom, He left them with no sarid—signifying permanent, irreversible historical termination.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:18, the prophet declares: "The house of Jacob shall be a fire, and the house of Joseph a flame, but the house of Esau shall be stubble; they shall kindle them and devour them, and no survivor [sarid] shall remain of the house of Esau, for the LORD has spoken."

The immediate context is one of poetic justice. Earlier in the vision, God rebukes Edom for standing at the crossroads to cut off Judah's fugitives and hand over their survivors in the day of their distress (Obadiah 1:14). Because Edom actively hunted down and eliminated Judah’s survivors during the Babylonian crisis, God decrees that Edom will receive the same treatment.

The agricultural metaphor of fire, flame, and stubble emphasizes the speed and absolute nature of this judgment. Stubble burns instantly and completely, leaving nothing but worthless ash. By declaring that there will be "no survivor" (sarid) of the house of Esau, God pronounces the total erasure of Edom as a distinct national identity. This prophecy was historically fulfilled; after centuries of displacement and subjugation, the Edomites were permanently absorbed into other populations and ceased to exist as a distinct people group after the Roman destruction of Jerusalem in A.D. 70.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Deuteronomy 2:34 - "We took all his cities at that time, and we utterly destroyed the men, women, and little ones of every city; we left no survivor."

  • Deuteronomy 3:3 - "So the LORD our God also delivered into our hands Og king of Bashan and all his people, and we defeated him until no survivor was left to him."

  • Job 18:19 - "He has neither son nor posterity among his people, nor any survivor in his dwellings."

  • Jeremiah 42:17 - "So shall it be with all the men who set their faces to go to Egypt to dwell there. They shall die by the sword, by famine, and by pestilence. And none of them shall remain or escape from the disaster that I will bring upon them." (Here, the noun sarid is translated "escape" to emphasize the lack of any surviving remnant).

7. Other Relevant Insights

It is helpful to contrast sarid with palit (another word for fugitive or escapee) and she'ar (remnant). While she'ar often carries a highly theological, hopeful, and spiritual expectation of restoration and covenant faithfulness, sarid is primarily physical and historical. When a text states there is no sarid, it is a declaration of complete physical extinction in history. The absence of a sarid for the house of Esau stands as a sobering warning of the finality of falling under the judgment of the living God.
