KINDLE - (dâlaq, Strong's H1814)

Summary:

Lexically, dalaq means to set ablaze, burn, or kindle. It is also used metaphorically in the Old Testament to describe being inflamed with emotion or engaging in a "hot" or feverish pursuit. In Obadiah 1:18, the word is used within a vivid metaphor of judgment, describing how the restored houses of Jacob and Joseph will ignite and entirely consume the house of Esau, just as fire kindles dry stubble.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The Hebrew root dalaq is fundamentally associated with heat, burning, and inflammation. In the ancient Near Eastern context, fire was a daily necessity for cooking and warmth, but it was also a devastating force when it caught hold of dry agricultural refuse (stubble or chaff). The word operates in two primary spheres in biblical literature: the literal ignition of a fire, and the metaphorical "heat" of intense human actions, such as a fierce pursuit in battle or the burning of passions.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic lexical meaning of dalaq is to burn, set on fire, or kindle. When used literally, it describes the physical act of starting a fire or feeding flames with fuel. When applied metaphorically, the semantic range of dalaq extends to describe intense, heated actions. It can mean to pursue hotly (chasing an enemy or a fleeing person with burning intensity), to be inflamed by intoxicating drink, or to speak with burning, fervent emotion.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

The Old Testament usage of dalaq demonstrates a fluid transition between literal and metaphorical heat.

Literally, it is used for kindling a physical fire under a pot or on a hearth.

Metaphorically, it frequently describes the heat of conflict. When an army or an angry individual chases someone down, they are said to "hotly pursue" (dalaq) them. It is also used to describe the internal burning of a person who is inflamed by wine or the fervent, burning nature of deceptive lips. In prophetic literature, the literal sense of kindling a fire is often employed as a metaphor for divine judgment, where the wicked are likened to highly flammable material.

4. Theological Implications

Fire is one of the most consistent biblical symbols for the holiness, purity, and judgment of God. When dalaq is used in the context of judgment, it underscores the suddenness and inevitability of the destruction. Stubble cannot resist being kindled; it is entirely defenseless against the flame. Theologically, the use of this imagery in Obadiah highlights the principle of divine retribution and the reversal of fortunes. Edom (Esau), who had proudly participated in the destruction of Jerusalem, would find themselves reduced to dry stubble, completely consumed by the very people they had previously victimized.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:18, the prophet employs a powerful agricultural metaphor: "The house of Jacob shall be a fire, And the house of Joseph a flame; But the house of Esau shall be stubble; They shall kindle them and devour them..."

The word dalaq functions as the point of ignition in this imagery. The morphology of the verb (Qal perfect 3rd person plural) grammatically presents the houses of Jacob and Joseph as the subjects carrying out the action of kindling against Esau. The context makes clear that this is not a refining fire, but a consuming one. The act of kindling leads immediately to the next verb, "devour," resulting in the finality that "no survivor shall remain." The word dalaq here vividly communicates the total, unresisting consumption of Edom's pride and power when faced with God's decreed judgment through His restored people.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Ezekiel 24:10 — "Heap on the wood, Kindle the fire; Boil the meat well, Mix in the spices, And let the cuts be burned up."

  • Genesis 31:36 — "Then Jacob was angry and rebuked Laban, and Jacob answered and said to Laban: 'What is my trespass? What is my sin, that you have so hotly pursued me?'"

  • Isaiah 5:11 — "Woe to those who rise early in the morning, That they may follow intoxicating drink; Who continue until night, till wine inflames them!"

  • Proverbs 26:23 — "Fervent lips with a wicked heart Are like earthenware covered with silver dross."

  • 1 Samuel 17:53 — "Then the children of Israel returned from chasing the Philistines, and they plundered their tents."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The imagery of stubble being kindled by fire is a recurring prophetic motif for the day of the Lord (seen also in Isaiah 5:24 and Malachi 4:1). In Obadiah, the specific use of dalaq connects the historical animosity between the twin brothers, Jacob and Esau, to its final eschatological conclusion. The "heat" of Esau's historical violence against Jacob is ultimately answered by the consuming fire of God's justice, kindled by Jacob upon Esau.
