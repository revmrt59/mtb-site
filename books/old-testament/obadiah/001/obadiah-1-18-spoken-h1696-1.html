SPOKEN - (dâbar, Strong's H1696)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb davar primary signifies the act of speaking, declaring, conversing, or issuing a command or decree. Across the Old Testament, it denotes articulate, intentional communication ranging from ordinary human discourse to binding royal and divine proclamations. In Obadiah 1:18, davar appears in the prophetic closure "for the LORD has spoken," serving as the ultimate divine seal that guarantees the complete judgment of Edom and the ultimate vindication of God's covenant people.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Semitic contexts, spoken communication carried weight proportional to the authority of the speaker. The verbal root dbr is central to classical Hebrew, distinct from verbs indicating mere sound or vocalization. In the Piel stem (davar), the verb actively stresses the articulation and delivery of a message, whether through direct dialogue, legal decrees, or prophetic declarations. Rather than representing an abstract concept, speech in the Old Testament context is understood as an authoritative act that establishes purpose, conveys covenantal instruction, or sets historical events into motion.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, davar in the Piel stem means "to speak," "to utter," "to declare," "to converse," or "to command." Its semantic range includes:

  • Expressing thoughts or directives through formal speech.

  • Communicating covenant stipulations, laws, and divine ordinances.

  • Delivering prophetic announcements of divine intention, judgment, or redemption.

  • Interchanging words in dialogue or legal testimony.

The standard lexical meaning remains clear communication through speech. Any deeper theological significance arises not from a hidden etymology, but from the speaker's identity—most notably when the speaker is God Himself.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament narrative, law, poetry, and prophecy, davar functions as the primary verb for verbal communication.

  • In legal contexts, davar describes God giving His statutes or the leaders communicating judgments to Israel.

  • In narrative contexts, it marks personal dialogue, historical negotiations, or official royal directives.

  • In prophetic literature, davar frequently operates as a technical term for divine revelation. Prophets speak because Yahweh has spoken to them, and they deliver His exact words to kings and nations.

When Yahweh is the subject of davar, the speech act carries divine authority and absolute reliability. A divine declaration is never mere promise or threat; it is the ultimate cause that shapes history.

4. Theological Implications

Theological reflection on davar centers on the trustworthiness and sovereignty of God's revealed word. Because God is faithful, what He speaks cannot fail or return void. The divine speech act establishes covenant relationship, reveals God's righteousness, and announces His righteous decrees over the nations. Prophetic authority rests entirely on whether a message originates from what Yahweh has spoken. Consequently, human obedience or disobedience is framed by how one responds to the words Yahweh has declared.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:18, the prophet delivers a dramatic oracle of judgment against Edom. The verse depicts a complete historical reversal: "The house of Jacob shall be a fire, and the house of Joseph a flame; but the house of Esau shall be stubble; they shall kindle them and devour them, and no survivor shall remain of the house of Esau."

To seal this terrifying sentence of complete devastation, the verse concludes with the absolute justification for its certainty: "for the LORD has spoken."

  • The target word is parsed in the Piel stem, perfect form, 3rd person masculine singular.

  • The 3rd person masculine singular subject is explicitly Yahweh Himself.

  • The perfect form depicts the action as a settled, completed divine decree.

  • The introductory conjunction ("for") provides the ultimate ground for the prophecy: Edom's utter collapse and Israel's restoration are not contingent upon human strength, political alliance, or military strategy, but upon the inviolable word that Yahweh has already declared.

This phrase directly counters Edom's arrogant self-reliance mentioned earlier in the vision ("Who will bring me down to the ground?", Obad. 1:3). Edom's boastful words are rendered meaningless by the binding decree of Yahweh.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 24:3 — "So Moses came and told the people all the words of the LORD and all the judgments. And all the people answered with one voice and said, 'All the words which the LORD has spoken we will do.'"

  • 2 Chronicles 6:10 — "So the LORD has fulfilled His word which He spoke; and I have filled the position of my father David, and sit on the throne of Israel, as the LORD promised, and have built the temple for the name of the LORD God of Israel."

  • Jeremiah 37:2 — "But neither he nor his servants nor the people of the land listened to the words of the LORD which He spoke by the prophet Jeremiah."

  • Daniel 9:6 — "Neither have we heeded Your servants the prophets, who spoke in Your name to our kings and our princes, our fathers and all the people of the land."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The prophetic formula "for the LORD has spoken" serves throughout the prophetic books as a formal guarantee of fulfillment. In Obadiah's brief message, this closing phrase establishes that human opposition to God's covenant purposes will ultimately fail. Edom's prideful pride led to violence against Jacob, but Yahweh's spoken word stands as the final sentence over history, ensuring that judgment against unrighteousness and deliverance for His people will come to pass exactly as decreed.
