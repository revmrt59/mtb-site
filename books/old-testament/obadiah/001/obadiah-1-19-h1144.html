BENJAMIN - ( binyamin , Strong's H1144 )

Summary:

The Hebrew name Binyamin literally means "son of the right hand." In the prophetic context of Obadiah 1:19, the term designates the tribe of Benjamin as a representative of the restored remnant of God's covenant people. Following the devastating exile and the hostile betrayal of Edom, the sovereign God promises a geographical and spiritual reversal. The small, historically defensive tribe of Benjamin will expand far beyond its traditional boundaries to possess the fertile territory of Gilead east of the Jordan River, demonstrating that God's grace abundantly restores what was lost.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The name Binyamin originates in Genesis 35:18 during a moment of maternal grief and patriarchal hope. As Rachel lay dying while giving birth, she named her newborn son Ben-Oni, meaning "son of my sorrow." Jacob, however, immediately renamed him Binyamin, meaning "son of the right hand."

In the ancient Near East, the right hand was the hand of honor, strength, authority, and blessing. To be the "son of the right hand" was to be uniquely favored and strengthened by the patriarch. Geographically, the right hand also pointed to the south when facing east, meaning the name could also carry the connotation of "son of the south."

The tribe that descended from Benjamin became known for its fierce warriors and skilled archers, often described as a "ravenous wolf" in early tribal blessings (Genesis 49:27). Despite its small size and central position between the larger territories of Judah and Ephraim, Benjamin held great historical significance. It produced Saul, Israel’s first king, hosted the city of Jerusalem on its border, and remained loyal to the Davidic dynasty when the northern tribes broke away.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The proper noun binyamin is a compound Hebrew name:

• ben (Strong's H1121): meaning "son" or "builder of the family name."

• yamin (Strong's H3225): meaning "right hand," "strength," or "south."

Lexically, the word represents:

• The youngest biological son of Jacob and Rachel.

• The Israelite tribe descended from Benjamin.

• The specific territorial inheritance allotted to this tribe within the Promised Land.

• The returned southern remnant in post-exilic prophetic literature, frequently paired with Judah to represent the faithful survivors of the exile.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout Scripture, the name Benjamin is used to illustrate several deep spiritual realities:

• The Beloved Protected by God: In Deuteronomy 33:12, Moses blesses Benjamin as "the beloved of the LORD" who dwells in safety, sheltered "between His shoulders." This presents a tender picture of a father carrying his child, highlighting God's protective intimacy over this small tribe.

• Fierce Loyalty and Courage: Benjamin is pictured as a warrior tribe. Despite their small numbers, they were capable of defending their borders against overwhelming odds, representing the strength God provides to the weak.

• Territorial Expansion: In Obadiah, Benjamin becomes a picture of supernatural boundary-breaking restoration. Instead of being confined to their historically small, landlocked territory west of the Jordan, they are pictured crossing the river to claim Gilead. This represents a complete reversal of their post-exilic reality, shifting from a defensive, compressed existence to a broad, prosperous inheritance.

### 4. Theological Implications

The inclusion of Benjamin in Obadiah’s prophecy of restoration carries profound theological weight:

• God’s Covenant Faithfulness: Edom sought to permanently dispossess Israel, and the Babylonian exile seemed to seal the end of the nation. By naming Benjamin—a tribe that was nearly wiped out in Judges 20 and later carried away into exile—God demonstrates that His covenant promises cannot be nullified by human failure or foreign oppression.

• Grace Over Past Failures: Benjamin’s history was marred by severe sin and civil war. Yet, in the final restoration, they are not forgotten or relegated to second-class status; they are designated as active co-inheritors of the expanded kingdom.

• The Sovereign Reversal of Fortune: God delights in using the small and marginalized to overcome the proud. While proud Edom is laid low and dispossessed, the small tribe of Benjamin is exalted and given a massive territorial inheritance.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:19, the prophet outlines the precise borders of the restored kingdom of God. Following the judgment of Edom (the mountain of Esau), the survivor-remnant of Israel will expand in every direction:

• The southern survivors (the Negev) will possess the hill country of Esau.

• The lowland survivors (the Shephelah) will possess the coastal plains of the Philistines.

• The restored people will reclaim the northern fields of Ephraim and Samaria.

• Benjamin will possess Gilead.

The contextual nuance of Benjamin possessing Gilead is geographically striking. Historically, Gilead lay on the east side of the Jordan River and belonged to the northern tribes of Gad, Reuben, and the half-tribe of Manasseh. Under Obadiah's prophecy, Benjamin—historically situated west of the Jordan near Jerusalem—is given the mandate to possess this eastern territory. This indicates that the post-exilic remnant (primarily consisting of Judah and Benjamin) would not merely return to their old, cramped boundaries, but would inherit the fullness of the land promised to all twelve tribes. God’s restoration is always larger and more abundant than what was originally lost.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 35:18 - "And so it was, as her soul was departing (for she died), that she called his name Ben-Oni; but his father called him Benjamin."

• Deuteronomy 33:12 - "Of Benjamin he said: 'The beloved of the LORD shall dwell in safety by Him, Who shelters him all the day long; And he shall dwell between His shoulders.'"

• Jeremiah 32:44 - "'Men will buy fields for money, sign deeds and seal them, and take witnesses in the land of Benjamin, in the places around Jerusalem, in the cities of Judah, in the cities of the mountains, in the cities of the lowland, and in the cities of the South; for I will cause their captives to return,' says the LORD."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Apostle Paul was a biological descendant of this tribe, a fact he states with godly heritage in Philippians 3:5.

• In the book of Judges, the tribe of Benjamin was nearly annihilated due to their own moral compromise (Judges 20). God’s preservation of this tribe throughout history, leading up to the prophetic promises in Obadiah and Jeremiah, stands as an enduring monument to His redeeming and preserving grace.

• In the ultimate fulfillment of God's kingdom in Revelation 7:8, the tribe of Benjamin is specifically listed among the sealed tribes of Israel, proving that God's plan for this "son of the right hand" extends into eternity.
