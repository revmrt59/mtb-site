MOUNTAINS - ( har , Strong's H2022 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word har refers to a mountain, hill, or mountainous region, serving as a prominent geographical feature in the ancient Near East that carries profound theological and pastoral significance. In the Old Testament, mountains represent stability, places of divine encounter, military strongholds, and symbols of prideful security. In the context of Obadiah 1:19, the "mountains of Esau" represent the rugged, fortified territory of Edom (Mount Seir), which they believed made them invincible. The possession of these mountains by God's people signifies the complete reversal of fortune where God humbles human pride and restores His sovereign rule, demonstrating that no earthly stronghold can withstand the purposes of the Lord.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The noun har derives from an ancient root associated with elevation, rising up, and projection. In the topography of the ancient Levant, mountains were not merely aesthetic features; they were vital to survival, defense, and religious identity. The rugged terrain of the promised land and its surroundings meant that mountains and hills (such as the Judean hills, Mount Zion, Mount Hermon, and Mount Seir) defined tribal boundaries and served as natural fortresses. To ancient peoples, a mountain was a symbol of permanence, strength, and closeness to the heavens. Living in the mountains provided major military advantages, as steep slopes made cities easy to defend and difficult to besiege. Edom, descended from Esau, inhabited the rugged, rocky highlands of Mount Seir—a highly defensible mountainous region south of the Dead Sea, characterized by narrow gorges, steep cliffs, and high red-sandstone peaks.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Physical Elevation: The primary meaning of har is a mountain, hill, or hill country. It can denote a single prominent peak (such as Mount Sinai, Mount Hor, or Mount Nebo) or an entire mountainous region (such as the hill country of Judah or the hill country of Ephraim).

• Territorial Construct: In construct states, har identifies a specific mountainous territory associated with a particular people or historical figure, such as the "mount of Esau" (har 'esaw) or the "mountain of the Lord's house" (har beyt-YHWH).

• Figurative Extension: Beyond physical geography, the term represents kingdoms, empires, centers of political power, and spiritual strongholds.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Symbols of Pride and False Security: Because of their height and inaccessibility, mountains are frequently used in Scripture to picture human pride, self-sufficiency, and false security. Edom’s dwelling in the clefts of the rock on the high mountains made them boast, "Who will bring me down to the ground?" (Obadiah 1:3).

• Places of Divine Revelation and Worship: God frequently chose mountains as the stage for His self-revelation. Mount Sinai (Horeb) is where the covenant was established, and Mount Zion is the designated dwelling place of His presence.

• Metaphors for Obstacles: Mountains represent insurmountable human difficulties that are easily leveled by divine power (Zechariah 4:7).

• Permanence and Stability: The mountains are described as "ancient" and "everlasting," yet even they tremble and melt at the presence of Yahweh, emphasizing His absolute supremacy over creation (Psalm 97:5; Habakkuk 3:6).

### 4. Theological Implications

• The Illusion of Earthly Fortresses: Scripture consistently deconstructs the idea that physical, financial, or military strongholds offer ultimate safety. The "mountains of Esau" represent the pinnacle of human defense, yet Obadiah declares that God will bring them down. True security is found only in the Lord, who made heaven and earth (Psalm 121:1-2).

• Divine Sovereignty and Judgment: God's judgment reaches the highest peaks. No elevation can hide a nation from His wrath, and no geographical barrier can hinder His decrees.

• Restoration and Kingdom Inheritance: The reclaiming of the mountains by God's people is a major theme in prophetic literature. The mountain of the Lord's house will be established as the highest of the mountains (Isaiah 2:2), signifying the global, visible, and supreme rule of God. In Obadiah, the reclamation of Esau's mountains by the remnant of Jacob shows that God keeps His covenant promises and will ultimately establish His kingdom.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Obadiah 1:19, the term har appears in the construct phrase "the mountains of Esau" (har 'esaw, literally "the mount of Esau"). This refers to Mount Seir, the steep, rocky highland territory that Edom occupied.

• The context of Obadiah is a prophecy of judgment against Edom for their pride and their betrayal of their brother Jacob (Israel) during Jerusalem's destruction. Edom gloated over Israel's downfall, believing their own mountainous stronghold kept them safe.

• Obadiah 1:19 promises a complete reversal of fortunes: "But those of the South shall possess the mountains of Esau." The "South" (the Negev, a dry, flat wasteland) will expand to possess the high, fortified mountains of their historic oppressor.

• This geographical detail highlights the thoroughness of God's restoration. The dispossessed and humbled people of God will inherit the very strongholds of those who once mocked them. The pride of Esau is brought low, and the territory of Esau is absorbed into the expanded boundaries of God's kingdom.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 22:14: "And Abraham called the name of the place The-Lord-Will-Provide; as it is said to this day, 'In the mount of the Lord it shall be provided.'"

• Exodus 19:3: "And Moses went up to God, and the Lord called to him from the mountain, saying, 'Thus you shall say to the house of Jacob, and tell the children of Israel...'"

• Psalm 121:1: "I will lift up my eyes to the hills—From whence comes my help?"

• Isaiah 2:2: "Now it shall come to pass in the latter days That the mountain of the Lord’s house Shall be established on the top of the mountains, And shall be exalted above the hills; And all nations shall flow to it."

• Obadiah 1:21: "Then saviors shall come to Mount Zion To judge the mountains of Esau, And the kingdom shall be the Lord’s."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Contrast between Mountains: Obadiah structures his prophecy around a stark contrast between two mountains: the "mountains of Esau" (representing human pride, worldly power, and impending judgment) and "Mount Zion" (representing God’s presence, deliverance, holiness, and ultimate victory). While the mountains of Esau will be searched out and plundered (Obadiah 1:6), Mount Zion will be a place of deliverance and holiness (Obadiah 1:17).

• Sovereign Reallocation of Land: In the ancient Near East, losing ancestral mountainous land was the ultimate sign of defeat. The possession of the mountains of Esau by the exiles of Israel serves as a physical, historical demonstration of God’s covenant faithfulness and His role as the ultimate Landowner who distributes territory according to His sovereign will.
