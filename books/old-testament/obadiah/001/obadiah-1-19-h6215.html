ESAU - ( 'Esav , Strong's H6215 )

Summary:

The name Esau refers historically to the firstborn son of Isaac and twin brother of Jacob, but in the prophetic books, it frequently represents the nation of Edom, his descendants. In the context of Obadiah 1:19, "Esau" (specifically "the mountains of Esau") designates the rugged, mountainous territory of Edom (Mount Seir). The passage prophesies a dramatic reversal of fortunes: the southern conquerors from Judah (the Negeb) will dispossess and inherit the seemingly impregnable strongholds of Edom, demonstrating God's sovereign justice and the ultimate triumph of His covenant people over their historical adversaries.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

Esau, the twin brother of Jacob, was born covered in hair, a physical characteristic that some associate with the etymological root of his name (suggesting "hairy" or "rough"). He was a man of the outdoors, a skilled hunter who preferred the immediate gratification of his physical appetites over his spiritual birthright. This character profile defined not only the man but also the nation that came from him: Edom. The Edomites settled in the rugged, rocky terrain of Mount Seir, south of the Dead Sea. This territory, known for its high cliffs, narrow gorges, and natural fortresses, became synonymous with the "mountains of Esau." The physical geography of their homeland fostered a spirit of pride, self-sufficiency, and perceived invincibility, which ultimately led to their divine judgment.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The proper noun 'Esav designates the historical patriarch Esau, the son of Isaac and Rebekah. Nationally and geographically, it serves as a collective singular noun representing the descendants of Esau (the Edomites) and the territory they occupied. In prophetic literature, the name shifts from a personal identifier to a representative term for a hostile nation characterized by perpetual enmity toward Israel. In Obadiah 1:19, the plural construct phrase "mountains of Esau" (har 'Esav) refers specifically to the mountainous region of Seir, symbolizing Edom's pride, power, and territorial security.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, Esau is used in several distinct ways:

• The Historical Figure: The brother who sold his birthright for a bowl of red stew and was subsequently called Edom ("red"). He represents the profane and secular man who despises spiritual blessings.

• The National Entity: A collective term for the nation of Edom. God asserts His sovereign choice in Malachi by stating, "Jacob I have loved, but Esau I have hated," contrasting His covenant choice of Israel with His rejection of Edom's pride.

• The Geographic Territory: "The mountain of Esau" or "mountains of Esau" represents Mount Seir, a high, red-sandstone mountain range. This physical high ground was a word picture for Edom's lofty pride. They believed they were secure from invasion in their clefts of the rock, yet God promised to bring them down.

### 4. Theological Implications

The name Esau carries profound theological weight regarding God's sovereignty, election, and justice. Esau represents the fleshly line that, despite possessing the physical priority of the firstborn, was bypassed in God's covenant plan in favor of Jacob. Furthermore, the conflict between Jacob and Esau is a perpetual biblical motif of the struggle between the spirit and the flesh, and between the kingdom of God and the kingdoms of this world. The eventual conquest of the mountains of Esau by God's people signifies that earthly security, military strength, and natural advantages cannot withstand divine judgment. It assures believers that God's covenant promises are immutable, and those who oppose His people will ultimately be brought under His righteous rule.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:19, "the mountains of Esau" stands in direct contrast to "the South" (the Negeb). Obadiah's prophecy deals extensively with Edom’s betrayal of Judah during the Babylonian invasion. Edom had stood by, rejoiced in Judah's calamity, and even intercepted fugitives. God declares that the house of Jacob will be a fire and the house of Esau stubble. In verse 19, the geographical borders of a restored Israel expand significantly. The survivors dwelling in the arid Negeb (the South) will go up and possess the fertile, rugged highlands of Edom. This is a brilliant contextual nuance: the very heights where Edom felt secure and from which they looked down upon defeated Israel will be permanently occupied by those they once despised.

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

The geographical transition of Esau's descendants into the region of Idumea in New Testament times continues this historical trajectory. Herod the Great, an Idumean (descendant of Esau), ruled over Judea and sought to destroy the infant King of the Jews, Jesus (a descendant of Jacob). This New Testament historical reality mirrors the ancient, ongoing hostility of Esau against Jacob, demonstrating how the physical and spiritual opposition to God's covenant purposes persisted until the coming of the Messiah.
