DESPISED - (bâzâh, Strong's H959)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb bazah basic meaning is to hold in contempt, disdain, or regard as worthless and insignificant. In Obadiah 1:2, the word describes the complete loss of status and dishonor that Yahweh inflicts upon the proud nation of Edom. By declaring that Edom will become "greatly despised," the prophet underscores a dramatic divine reversal: the nation that arrogantly exalted itself in its mountain fortresses will be reduced to an object of low esteem and mockery among the surrounding peoples.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root bazah operates within the sphere of ancient Near Eastern concepts of honor, shame, status, and covenant value. In the ancient world, a nation's security, reputation, and divine favor were closely tied to its visible strength, geographic elevation, and military alliances. Edom, situated in the rugged, high-altitude territory of Mount Seir, considered itself secure against foreign aggression. Edomite pride grew from its natural rock fortresses and economic leverage along trade routes.

When Yahweh speaks through the prophet Obadiah, He addresses this entrenched self-sufficiency. The term bazah exposes the disparity between Edom’s inflated self-assessment and its actual standing before God. Rather than enjoying the glory and prestige it sought through arrogance and hostility toward Judah, Edom is assigned a place of social and international contempt.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, bazah signifies regarding someone or something as trivial, light, or devoid of value. It stands in contrast to the Hebrew concept of honor (kabad), which literally means to be heavy or weighty. To honor is to treat an individual or institution as carrying great weight, whereas to despise (bazah) is to treat an entity as light, worthless, or beneath respect.

The semantic range includes:

  • Viewing an object, person, or sacred duty with disdain.

  • Holding a covenant or authority figure in low regard.

  • The state of being reduced to absolute ignominy or public contempt under divine judgment.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, bazah is employed in both human relationships and divine-human interactions. It appears in contexts where individuals treat sacred matters with contempt, such as despising a holy covenant, despising the word of the Lord, or despising Yahweh’s name. It also appears in wisdom literature and psalms to describe the poor or righteous being unjustifiably looked down upon by the arrogant, or conversely, the proud being brought low by divine intervention.

In Obadiah 1:2, the word appears in the Qal stem as a passive participle. This grammatical form presents Edom as receiving the action, occupying a resulting condition or state of being despised. The passive participle highlights Edom’s objective standing in the eyes of the nations following Yahweh’s judgment—she does not merely experience a momentary act of disdain, but becomes characterized as contemptible.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of bazah in prophetic literature centers on divine justice and the principle of righteous retribution. God routinely opposes human pride and exalts the humble. When a person or nation exalts itself in opposition to God's covenant purposes, Yahweh sovereignly reverses their status.

There is also a profound thematic irony in Obadiah’s use of bazah. Edom was descended from Esau, who famously despised (bazah) his birthright for a single meal. The character trait that marked the ancestor—viewing sacred value with disdain—ultimately becomes the judgment visited upon his descendants. The nation that historically despised God’s covenant promises and brotherly obligations is ultimately rendered despised by God among the nations.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Obadiah 1:2 opens Yahweh’s direct declaration of sentence against Edom: "Behold, I will make you small among the nations; You shall be greatly despised." The immediate context contrasts Edom’s internal delusion with God's sovereign decree. In verse 3, the prophet notes, "The pride of your heart has deceived you, you who dwell in the clefts of the rock." Edom thought it was high, unreachable, and formidable.

Yahweh’s declaration shatters this illusion through two paired statements:

1. "I will make you small among the nations" (reducing their size, power, and influence).

2. "You shall be greatly despised" (reducing their honor, prestige, and reputation).

The adverbial modifier "greatly" intensifies the degree of contempt Edom will suffer. The context demonstrates that Edom’s reduction is not the result of mere geopolitical chance; it is Yahweh’s active judgment. The surrounding nations, whom Edom trusted or looked down upon, will view Edom as utterly insignificant and dishonored.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 25:34 – "And Jacob gave Esau bread and stew of lentils; then he ate and drank, arose, and went his way. Thus Esau despised his birthright."

  • 1 Samuel 2:30 – "Therefore the Lord God of Israel says: 'I said indeed that your house and the house of your father would walk before Me forever.' But now the Lord says: 'Far be it from Me; for those who honor Me I will honor, and those who despise Me shall be lightly esteemed.'"

  • Jeremiah 49:15 – "For indeed, I will make you small among nations, despised among men."

  • Malachi 1:6 – "A son honors his father, and a servant his master. If then I am the Father, where is My honor? And if I am a Master, where is My reverence? Says the Lord of hosts to you priests who despise My name."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A striking canonical parallel to Obadiah 1:2 exists in Jeremiah 49:15, where the exact same prophetic language is used against Edom. This repetition emphasizes the certainty of the divine decree within the prophetic tradition.

Furthermore, the biblical theology of honor and shame reveals that attempting to manufacture self-exaltation outside of submission to Yahweh always results in shame. Edom sought glory through strategic alliances, geographical security, and ruthless self-interest, yet the divine verdict permanently branded the nation with the status of bazah.
