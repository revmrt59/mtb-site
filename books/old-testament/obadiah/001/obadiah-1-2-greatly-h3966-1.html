GREATLY - (mᵉʼôd, Strong's H3966)

Summary:

The Hebrew word meʾod denotes force, abundance, or intensity, most commonly functioning as an adverbial modifier meaning "very," "exceedingly," or "greatly." In Obadiah 1:2, the word intensifies the divine declaration of Edom's upcoming subjugation, emphasizing that Edom will not merely suffer a minor setback, but will be brought into extreme, severe contempt among the surrounding nations as a direct result of Yahweh's judgment.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The prophet Obadiah delivers a targeted message of judgment against the nation of Edom. Geographically situated in the rugged, elevated territory of Mount Seir, Edom fostered a deep sense of security and military invulnerability. This physical security nurtured national pride, leading Edom to act with hostility and contempt toward Israel during times of crisis.

In response to Edom's pride, Yahweh speaks through the prophet to announce an unavoidable decree of divine humbling. In Obadiah 1:2, the Lord declares that He will make Edom small among the nations. To emphasize the degree of this humbling, the prophet employs the term meʾod. Rather than remaining a formidable power in the region, Edom is destined to be held in utter dishonor by neighboring peoples.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, meʾod carries the primary noun sense of "force," "might," or "abundance." In standard Old Testament usage, however, it functions predominantly as an adverbial intensifier. When modifying verbs, adjectives, or other terms, it shifts the degree of the state or action to an extreme level, translated as "very," "exceedingly," "greatly," or "severely."

In Obadiah 1:2, the word appears in the masculine singular absolute state. In this position, it functions adverbially to modify the statement regarding Edom's low standing, elevating the severity of the dishonor Edom will experience from an ordinary reduction of status to a state of complete and utter contempt.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament literature, meʾod serves to heighten the description of physical attributes, emotional states, divine actions, and human conditions:

  • Intensity of Action or Condition: It qualifies descriptions of fear, joy, grief, or humbling, signaling that the emotion or condition has reached a maximal degree.

  • Magnitude of Blessing or Judgment: In covenantal contexts, it describes extraordinary multiplication or wealth; conversely, in prophetic judgment, it underscores the completeness of divine devastation.

  • Heightened Attribute: When attached to qualities, it designates that the subject possesses that trait in an extraordinary measure.

In prophetic discourse, the term frequently appears at crucial climaxes of divine declarations to ensure the listener understands that God's action will leave no room for mitigation or partial effect.

4. Theological Implications

The presence of meʾod in Obadiah 1:2 reinforces several foundational theological principles regarding divine justice and human pride:

  • The Proportion of Divine Retribution: Edom exalted itself to lofty heights in its own imagination. God's judgment corresponds to this pride by bringing Edom down to an extreme level of dishonor.

  • The Unstoppable Efficacy of God's Word: When God decrees a reduction in status, no natural defenses, geographic fortifications, or political alliances can soften the impact. The judgment is executed with absolute thoroughness.

  • The Reversal of Worldly Standing: Human self-exaltation before God inevitably culminates in severe humiliation. The nation that thought itself superior among neighboring lands is rendered contemptible in their eyes.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Obadiah 1:2 forms the initial thematic decree of the book's opening oracle. The text presents Yahweh as the direct actor: "Behold, I will make you small among the nations." This baseline reduction ("small") is immediately amplified in the parallel clause by the addition of meʾod to the word translated "despised."

The target word meʾod, configured in the masculine singular absolute state, directly intensifies the contempt Edom will suffer. The structural pairing of "small" (low quantity or influence) with "greatly despised" (extreme low quality of reputation) establishes a complete reversal of Edom's self-image. While Edom viewed itself as secure, elevated, and feared, Yahweh's intervention guarantees that the surrounding nations will view Edom with severe disdain. The word meʾod thus supplies the rhetorical weight that underscores the total efficacy of Yahweh's judgment.

  • Genesis 47:27 — "So Israel dwelt in the land of Egypt, in the country of Goshen; and they had possessions there and grew and multiplied exceedingly."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The placement of meʾod in Obadiah 1:2 provides a stark structural contrast with the language of verse 3. In verse 3, Edom's internal disposition is described as pride of heart, asserting "Who will bring me down to the ground?" By employing meʾod in verse 2, the prophetic word establishes God's verdict prior to exposing Edom's boast. Before Edom's arrogant self-assessment is even articulated in the prophecy, God's decree has already declared Edom's disgrace to be extreme and irreversible.
