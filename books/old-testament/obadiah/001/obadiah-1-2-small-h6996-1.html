SMALL - (qâṭân, Strong's H6996)

Summary:

The Hebrew adjective qatan refers to something that is small in physical dimensions, minor in scope, young in age, or low in social rank and political status. In Obadiah 1:2, Yahweh announces His decree to Edom: "Behold, I will make you small among the nations." In this specific prophetic context, the word signifies a divine sentence of geopolitical reduction and humbling, turning a proud, self-sufficient nation into an insignificant and powerless kingdom stripped of its former stature.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The adjective qatan is a foundational Hebrew term used throughout the Old Testament to describe relative smallness. It functions across physical, temporal, social, and political categories. Geographically and historically, the nation of Edom occupied the rugged, mountainous territory south of the Dead Sea, centered around high, natural rock fortresses. Confident in its impregnable position, strategic alliances, and reputation for wisdom, Edom viewed itself as secure and formidable. Obadiah's prophecy opens with a divine summons to the nations against Edom, immediately followed by Yahweh's personal declaration that He Himself will render Edom qatan—small, diminished, and easily disregarded by the surrounding world.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

According to standard Hebrew lexicography (such as Brown-Driver-Briggs and the Theological Wordbook of the Old Testament), qatan carries the core meaning of "small," "little," "insignificant," or "youngest."

  • Physical Size and Quantity: Refers to objects, items, or groups that are small in scale, size, or weight.

  • Age and Chronology: Identifies the youngest child or younger sibling within a family unit.

  • Rank, Status, and Power: Denotes low social standing, lack of influence, or reduced political and military capacity.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In Hebrew literature, qatan frequently appears in dynamic contrast with gadol ("great" or "large"). When paired together ("from the small to the great"), the idiomatic expression emphasizes totality, universality, or impartiality—encompassing every individual across the entire spectrum of age, wealth, or status.

  • Expressions of Personal Humility: The word is used by individuals to express their own inadequacy, dependency, or lack of status before God or authority figures.

  • Legal and Social Impartiality: The Law of Moses utilizes qatan to instruct judges that justice must not favor the great over the small, ensuring equal weight for all people regardless of stature.

  • Geopolitical Standing: When applied to nations or groups, qatan highlights a loss of strength, population, or influence, placing a kingdom in a vulnerable position relative to its neighbors.

4. Theological Implications

The theological significance of qatan centers on God's absolute sovereignty over human pride and international destiny. Scripture consistently reveals that human arrogance often measures greatness by physical fortresses, military power, or geopolitical leverage. However, Yahweh is the supreme arbiter who exalteth the humble and abaseth the proud. By declaring that He will make Edom qatan, Yahweh demonstrates that human greatness is transitory and entirely subject to His sovereign will. No natural barrier or human alliance can prevent God from bringing down those who exalt themselves against His covenant purpose.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:2, qatan functions as a predicate adjective in the masculine, singular, absolute state, describing the nation of Edom personified as a single corporate entity. The immediate context creates a deliberate contrast between Edom's self-perception and God's sovereign determination:

  • Human Estimation vs. Divine Sentence: In verse 3, Edom's heart is described as deceived by pride, asking, "Who will bring me down to the ground?" Edom considered itself elevated and secure. Yahweh's response in verse 2 anticipates this arrogance by declaring that He will reduce Edom to a qatan status—insignificant among the surrounding powers.

  • Divine Agency: The construction emphasizes God's direct involvement ("I will make you"). Edom's coming reduction is not an accidental turn of history, but a deliberate act of divine judgment executed through secondary agents.

  • Comprehensive Humiliation: Linked directly with being "greatly despised," the quality of being qatan in this verse involves not only physical or numerical reduction, but also the complete loss of respect and authority among the nations.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The thematic contrast provided by qatan in prophetic literature often serves to highlight God's unexpected ways. While God frequently chooses the qatan (the younger son, the smaller tribe, or the humble leader) to accomplish His redemptive purposes, He active reduces the self-exalted to a state of being qatan as an act of judgment. In Obadiah, Edom's pride rooted in geographical height and security is answered by Yahweh's decree that reduces them to lowliness among the nations.
