CANAANITES - (Kᵉnaʻanîy, Strong's H3669)

Summary:

The Hebrew gentilic noun kenaʿani primarily designates an inhabitant or native of the land of Canaan, derived from the regional name kenaʿan. In wisdom and poetic contexts, the term carries a secondary semantic sense referring to a "trader" or "merchant," reflecting the widespread commercial activities of ancient coastal Canaanite/Phoenician populations. In Obadiah 1:20, the term is used in its ethno-geographic sense to mark the northern boundary of Israel's promised restoration, indicating that the returned exiles of Israel will inherit and occupy the coastal land historically inhabited by the Canaanites as far north as Zarephath.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The term kenaʿani is an ethnic and geographic designation identifying people belonging to or originating from Canaan. Geographically, Canaan encompassed the coastal plains, lowland valleys, and hill country west of the Jordan River, extending northward into regions associated in later periods with Phoenicia (including Tyre and Sidon).

In ancient Near Eastern history, the inhabitants of this region were famous for maritime commerce, trade, and luxury production (such as purple dyes). Because of this deep cultural association with commercial enterprise, the word kenaʿani developed a secondary lexical usage in classical Hebrew to designate a commercial merchant or trader, regardless of strict ethnic descent. In prophetic and legal literature, however, the gentilic identification remains predominant, identifying either the primary inhabitants of the Promised Land prior to the Israelite conquest or the northern coastal populations adjacent to Israel.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, kenaʿani functions as both a gentilic noun and an adjective.

  • Primary Sense: "Canaanite"—a native inhabitant or descendant of Canaan (e.g., Genesis 12:6; Exodus 13:11).

  • Secondary/Derived Sense: "Merchant" or "trader"—one who engages in commercial buying and selling (e.g., Proverbs 31:24).

The primary sense identifies ethnic groups living within the land promised to Abraham's descendants. The secondary sense arises directly from the reputation of coastal Canaanite cities as major trading hubs in the ancient Mediterranean world.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament historical narratives, kenaʿani frequently serves as an umbrella term for the indigenous populations of the Levant whom God commanded Israel to dispossess during the Conquest. It is often listed alongside other regional groups (such as Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites, and Jebusites), but it also stands independently as a broad designation for the non-Israelite inhabitants of the region.

In post-conquest literature and historical books, the presence of the Canaanites represents both an unfinished conquest and a continual covenant temptation when Israel compromised by serving their deities. In later wisdom and prophetic poetry, when kenaʿani appears without ethnic or geographic qualifiers, the context often determines whether the focus is on native territorial inhabitants or on commercial traders who buy and sell merchandise.

4. Theological Implications

The biblical usage of kenaʿani carries rich theological significance regarding God's covenant, holy living, and territorial promise:

  • Covenant Promise and Divine Gift: God promised the land of the Canaanites to Abraham and his offspring as an unconditional covenant grant, highlighting God's absolute sovereignty over earthly boundaries and international territory.

  • Holiness and Divine Judgment: The displaced status of the Canaanites in sacred history is consistently tied to divine moral judgment against unrighteousness, serving as a solemn warning that residence in God's presence requires covenant faithfulness.

  • Eschatological Restoration: In prophetic literature, the eventual total subjection or transformation of Canaanite territories underscores the ultimate reach of God's kingdom, where holy space is completely purified and covenant promises are fully realized.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:20, the prophet Obadiah presents a vision of divine vindication and territorial restoration following Edom's malicious celebration over Jerusalem's destruction. The target word kenaʿani appears in the masculine plural absolute form (kenaʿanim, translated "Canaanites").

The verse sets up a dramatic geographic and historical contrast. Edom had sought to profit from Israel's collapse, seizing territory and joyfully participating in Judah's tragedy. In response, God promises a comprehensive reversal of fortunes:

  • The exiled host of Israel (gelut) will not remain impoverished or homeless; rather, they will possess the former territory of the Canaanites as far north as Zarephath (a Phoenician coastal city between Tyre and Sidon).

  • Simultaneously, the exiles from Jerusalem residing in Sepharad will possess the southern cities of the Negev.

By using kenaʿani here, Obadiah specifies the northern expansion of the returned exiles. The phrase defines the vast scope of Israel's territorial inheritance—reaching into the historical stronghold of the coastal Canaanites. What was lost during the Babylonian exile will be restored beyond its former limits under the sovereign triumph of Yahweh.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 12:6 – "Abram passed through the land to the place of Shechem, as far as the terebinth tree of Moreh. And the Canaanites were then in the land."

  • Deuteronomy 7:1 – "When the Lord your God brings you into the land which you go to possess, and has cast out many nations before you... the Canaanites..."

  • Joshua 17:13 – "And it came to pass, when the children of Israel grew strong, that they put the Canaanites to forced labor, but did not utterly drive them out."

  • Nehemiah 9:8 – "You found his heart faithful before You, and made a covenant with him to give the land of the Canaanites... to give it to his descendants."

  • Proverbs 31:24 – "She makes linen garments and sells them, and supplies sashes to the merchants."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Geographic Anchor of Zarephath: Obadiah's reference to Zarephath specifically anchors the territory of the kenaʿani in the northern coastal strip of Phoenicia. This demonstrates that the prophetic scope of restoration included territories traditionally recognized as ancient Canaanite strongholds.

  • Commercial Contrast: While Proverbs 31:24 uses kenaʿani to highlight honourable economic diligence ("merchants"), prophetically the non-covenant practices associated with historical Canaanite culture stand in opposition to the holy order established when the exiles return to rule from Mount Zion.
