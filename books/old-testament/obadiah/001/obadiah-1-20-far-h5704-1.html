FAR - (ʻad, Strong's H5704)

Summary:

The Hebrew preposition ʿad functions as a marker of boundary, limit, or extent, translated as "as far as," "until," "unto," or "even to." Depending on context, it denotes spatial endpoints, temporal duration, or degree of extent. In Obadiah 1:20, ʿad designates the expansive northern limit of Israel's promised territory ("as far as Zarephath"). By marking this distant geographical boundary, the word highlights the extraordinary magnitude of God's divine restoration, assuring the exile-weary remnant that God's kingdom will extend beyond their former borders to encompass regions once far out of reach.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The preposition ʿad is a fundamental relational word in Biblical Hebrew used to specify targets, limits, and terminal points. In ancient Near Eastern geography and narrative, establishing boundaries was essential for defining national identity, covenant inheritances, and political jurisdiction. Rather than describing an abstract direction, ʿad marks a concrete reaching toward or arrival at a designated boundary.

In the book of Obadiah, the prophet pronounces judgment against Edom for its pride and violence against Judah during Jerusalem's downfall. Following the announcement of Edom's impending ruin, the prophecy transitions into a glorious assurance of Israel's ultimate restoration and the establishment of the LORD's kingdom (Obadiah 1:17–21). Within this prophetic vision of territorial and spiritual recovery, ʿad is employed to define the physical scope of the promised inheritance.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, ʿad operates as a particle expressing spatial, temporal, or figurative reach. Its primary senses include:

  • Spatial Extent ("as far as," "unto"): Indicating the geographic limit or endpoint of movement, travel, conquest, or territorial expansion.

  • Temporal Duration ("until," "up to"): Marking the point in time at which an action, state, or era reaches its terminus or fulfillment.

  • Degree or Extent ("even to," "as much as"): Functioning to emphasize the extreme degree or inclusive boundary of a given situation.

As a non-inflectional particle, ʿad derives its precise directional or temporal force from the noun, place name, or phrase that immediately follows it.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, ʿad appears in narratives, legal frameworks, poetry, and prophetic discourse to establish definitive boundaries.

In narrative and historical literature, ʿad routinely demarcates military conquests or geographic borders. When describing land grants or military campaigns, the preposition defines the full perimeter of an action, demonstrating that a movement was not partial but carried through to its intended terminus.

In poetic and worship contexts, ʿad often transitions from spatial boundaries to temporal infinity, marking duration that stretches from past ages into future eternity. Whether marking spatial limits on a map or temporal limits in historical narrative, ʿad provides precision by specifying exactly how far a reality extends.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of ʿad is tied directly to God's faithfulness in fulfilling His covenant commitments. God does not execute His plans vaguely; He establishes real boundaries in time and space.

  • Sovereign Determination of Boundaries: God sets the limits of nations, judgments, and restorations. When God decrees that an inheritance reaches ʿad a specific location, He guarantees the power to accomplish that expansion.

  • Completeness of Divine Restoration: In prophetic contexts, ʿad demonstrates that divine restoration is thorough. God does not merely grant a partial recovery of lost ground; He expands the territory of His people to fulfill His ultimate kingdom purpose.

  • Reversal of Captivity: While judgment drives people away into exile, covenant restoration gathers them back, extending their boundary ʿad distant lands previously beyond their administrative reach.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:20, the prophet details the re-possession of the land by the returned exiles: "And the captivity of this host of the children of Israel shall possess the land of the Canaanites as far as [ʿad] Zarephath."

Zarephath was a Sidonian town located far to the north, situated between Tyre and Sidon in Phoenician territory—famously known as the place where Elijah was sustained during the drought (1 Kings 17:9). Historically, this region lay outside the traditional core territory occupied by the southern kingdom of Judah.

By using ʿad before Zarephath, Obadiah constructs a vivid spatial boundary. The exiles from Israel will not return to a cramped, vulnerable enclave; their possession will extend all the way north to Zarephath, while the captives of Jerusalem will possess the southern cities of the Negev. The preposition ʿad provides the structural marker that defines this immense enlargement. It assures a displaced, dispossessed remnant that God’s future for them is characterized by vast, secure boundaries under His divine reign.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 13:12 – "Abram dwelt in the land of Canaan, and Lot dwelt in the cities of the plain and pitched his tent as far as [ʿad] Sodom."

  • Joshua 10:41 – "So Joshua conquered them from Kadesh Barnea as far as [ʿad] Gaza, and all the country of Goshen, even as far as [ʿad] Gibeon."

  • Psalms 90:2 – "Before the mountains were brought forth, or ever You had formed the earth and the world, even from everlasting to [ʿad] everlasting, You are God."

  • Lamentations 3:50 – "Till [ʿad] the LORD looks down and sees from heaven."

7. Other Relevant Insights

As a non-inflectional particle, ʿad carries no personal, gender, or number inflections, remaining structurally fixed while modifying the spatial or temporal terms around it. This structural stability reflects its function in Biblical Hebrew grammar: anchoring dynamic actions—such as marching, possessing, enduring, or waiting—to definitive endpoints.

In Obadiah 1:20, ʿad links a group of displaced exiles to a concrete geographical marker. The preposition transforms what might otherwise sound like a vague hope into a specific, geographical promise.
