CHILDREN - ( ben , Strong's H1121 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word ben primarily denotes a "son" or "child," but in the Hebrew Bible, it frequently refers to descendants, offspring, or members of a specific covenant group. In Obadiah 1:20, the plural construct phrase bney Yisra'el ("children of Israel") signifies the collective national and spiritual identity of the covenant people. Even while suffering in exile and captivity, they remain the legitimate heirs of God's promises, destined to return, overcome their enemies, and possess their promised inheritance.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The word ben is derived from the root verb banah, which means "to build." This etymological relationship provides a beautiful word picture: children are the "builders" of the family line, carrying the name, heritage, and legacy of the father into the next generation.

  • Just as a physical house is built of stones, a family household is built and established through its sons and daughters.

  • In the ancient Near East, lineage was paramount. One's identity, land inheritance, and spiritual standing were bound up in one's ancestry. To be a child of someone was to carry their identity and legal rights.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Primarily, ben indicates a male offspring or a son.

  • In the plural and construct states (bney), it represents descendants, posterity, or members of a larger collective, such as a tribe or nation (e.g., "children of Israel").

  • It designates membership in a certain class, guild, character, or condition (e.g., "sons of the prophets," "sons of valor").

  • It can also refer to the young of animals or the shoots and branches of a fruitful vine.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Living Stones of the Covenant: As offspring, the bney are the living stones of the ancestral household, preserving the covenant lineage from generation to generation.

  • The Covenant Nation: Combined with "Israel" or "Jacob," the term transcends mere biological descent to mark out the chosen people of God who are heirs to the Abrahamic, Mosaic, and Davidic covenants.

  • Relational Belonging: The term carries a rich sense of intimacy and belonging. God views Israel collectively as His "son" (Exodus 4:22), and their restoration is a fatherly act of redemption.

4. Theological Implications

  • The term ben highlights God's absolute faithfulness to His covenant promises. Despite centuries of rebellion, exile, and scattering among pagan nations, Israel remains the "children of Israel." Their identity is not erased by captivity.

  • The use of ben connects the scattered, hopeless captives to the original promises made to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Inheritance of the promised land is directly tied to being a legal child of the promise.

  • It emphasizes restoration and hope. God does not permanently abandon His children; the covenant family will be gathered, built up, and returned to their ancestral borders.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Obadiah 1:20, the phrase "children of Israel" (bney Yisra'el) is placed in stark contrast to the "captives of this host." Though they are currently powerless exiles, their identity as the children of Israel secures their future triumph.

  • The context of Obadiah is the judgment of Edom (the descendants of Esau) and the vindication of Jacob (the children of Israel). Edom's pride led to its ultimate destruction, while Israel's status as God's chosen offspring guarantees their restoration and expansion.

  • The "children of Israel" will dispossess their captors and neighbors, expanding their borders to Zarephath and the cities of the South, demonstrating that the covenant lineage will ultimately possess the fullness of the promised land.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 32:32 - "Therefore to this day the children of Israel do not eat the muscle that shrank, which is on the hip socket..."

  • Exodus 4:22 - "Then you shall say to Pharaoh, 'Thus says the LORD: "Israel is My son, My firstborn."'"

  • Deuteronomy 14:1 - "You are the children of the LORD your God; you shall not cut yourselves nor shave the front of your head for the dead."

  • Joel 3:16 - "The LORD also will roar from Zion, and utter His voice from Jerusalem; the heavens and earth will shake; but the LORD will be a shelter for His people, and the strength of the children of Israel."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The plural construct bney points to a spiritual unity. Though historically divided into the Northern Kingdom (Israel) and Southern Kingdom (Judah), the term "children of Israel" in prophetic restoration texts looks forward to a reunited family under one Divine Shepherd.

  • In the New Testament, this theological concept expands. All who are of faith are counted as "sons [children] of Abraham" (Galatians 3:7) and adopted into the family of God, sharing in the spiritual inheritance of the ultimate Ben—Jesus Christ, the Son of God.
