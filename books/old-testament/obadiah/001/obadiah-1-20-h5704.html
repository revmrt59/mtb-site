FAR - ( 'ad , Strong's H5704 )

Summary:

The Hebrew preposition 'ad, translated as "as far as" in Obadiah 1:20, is a spatial and temporal marker denoting the ultimate boundary, limit, or extent of an action, journey, or possession. In the context of biblical restoration, this small but potent word demonstrates that God does not restore His people halfway. It marks the outer limits of the land that the returned exiles will reclaim, assuring them that God's covenant faithfulness will stretch to the absolute boundaries of His promise, reclaiming even the pagan territories that once stood far beyond their reach.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near East, boundaries were of paramount importance. Land inheritance was defined by natural landmarks, rivers, and specific cities. To move a boundary stone was considered a severe covenant violation. The Hebrew word 'ad acts as a linguistic boundary marker. It draws a conceptual line, stretching from a starting point and stopping precisely at a designated terminus. The word picture behind 'ad is that of a traveler journeying until they reach their final destination, or a surveyor pulling a measuring line to its absolute limit to mark the edge of an inheritance. It represents the fullness of a journey or the complete extent of a territory.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The preposition 'ad indicates motion, extension, or duration up to a certain limit. Depending on the context, its semantic range includes:

  • Spatial limit: "as far as," "even unto," "up to."

  • Temporal limit: "until," "while," "as long as."

  • Degree or intensity: "even to," "to the point of."

The word defines the boundary of an action or state. It signifies that the subject reaches the designated terminus, emphasizing that no part of the journey or territory is left incomplete.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • Spatial Delineation: In geographical descriptions, the word is used to map out the borders of the Promised Land, marking the precise extent of God's geographical provision.

  • Temporal Endurance: In historical narratives, it establishes how long an event or state of being persists, often pointing to a climatic turning point (e.g., "until" a specific day).

  • Inclusive Abundance: When describing restoration, it functions as a promise that God's blessing will extend to the outermost edge of the designated area, leaving no pocket of land unclaimed or unredeemed.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, 'ad underscores the meticulous completeness of God’s promises. When God establishes a boundary of blessing, protection, or judgment, He executes it to the exact millimeter.

  • Covenantal Completeness: God does not offer partial restoration. When He promises to return His people, He reclaims their heritage to its absolute limits.

  • Sovereignty Over Borders: God defines the limits of nations and empires. The boundaries of the wicked are cut short, while the borders of His kingdom are expanded.

  • Reclamation of the Lost: By setting the boundary of inheritance deep into pagan territory, God demonstrates that His redemptive reach extends to places once deemed unreachable or unclean.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:20, the prophet addresses an exiled, devastated remnant. Edom had rejoiced over Jerusalem's destruction and actively participated in the displacement of God's people. In this bleak context, Obadiah delivers a message of radical reversal.

The prophet declares that the captives of Israel will return to possess the land of the Canaanites 'ad (as far as) Zarephath. Zarephath was a Phoenician city located far to the north, well outside the traditional borders of the divided kingdom of Israel. By utilizing 'ad to extend the boundary to Zarephath, the prophecy reveals that the restored kingdom will not merely match the old, compromised borders; it will overflow them. The grace of God's restoration will stretch "as far as" the northernmost limits of Phoenicia, turning a land of exile into a vast, unified inheritance.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 12:6: "Abram passed through the land to the place of Shechem, as far as the terebinth tree of Moreh. And the Canaanites were then in the land."

  • Genesis 13:12: "Abram dwelt in the land of Canaan, and Lot dwelt in the cities of the plain and pitched his tent even as far as Sodom."

  • Joshua 13:5: "...the land of the Gebalites, and all Lebanon, toward the sunrise, from Baal Gad below Mount Hermon as far as the entrance to Hamath;"

  • 1 Samuel 17:52: "Now the men of Israel and Judah arose and shouted, and pursued the Philistines as far as the entrance of the valley and to the gates of Ekron. And the wounded of the Philistines fell down by the way to Shaaraim, even as far as Gath and Ekron."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The mention of Zarephath in Obadiah 1:20 carries deep historical and spiritual resonance. Zarephath was the Phoenician town where God sent the prophet Elijah during a severe famine, and where Elijah multiplied the widow's oil and raised her son from the dead. By promising that Israel's possession will reach 'ad Zarephath, the text hints at a restoration that transcends ethnic boundaries, foreshadowing the expansive, global reach of the Messianic kingdom where God's miraculous provision and resurrection power are extended to the nations.
