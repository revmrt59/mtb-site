ZAREPHATH - ( Tsarephath , Strong's H6886 )

Summary:

Zarephath, a Phoenician port city situated between Tyre and Sidon, represents the northernmost boundary of the land promised to Israel's returning exiles. In Obadiah 1:20, the mention of Zarephath serves as a powerful geographical and theological marker, signifying the complete restoration and expansion of Israel's territory to its divinely intended limits. Historically known as a place of refining and testing—most notably during the ministry of Elijah—its inclusion in Obadiah's prophecy of restoration assures God's people that the very places of their historical affliction and foreign dominance will ultimately be possessed by the redeemed remnant of Israel.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

Zarephath was an ancient Phoenician coastal town located on the Mediterranean Sea, approximately halfway between Tyre and Sidon (modern-day Sarafand in Lebanon). The name is derived from the Hebrew root verb tsaraph, which means "to smelt, refine, test, or purge" as a metalworker refines precious metals in a crucible.

In antiquity, Zarephath was known for its industrial glassmaking and metal refining workshops, making its etymological connection to smelting highly appropriate. In biblical history, it is famously known as the place where God sent Elijah during the great drought. It was there that God sustained Elijah through a poor Gentile widow whose jar of flour and jug of oil did not run dry, and whose son Elijah raised from the dead.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The proper noun Tsarephath designates the specific Phoenician city of Zarephath.

• It literally carries the meaning of "refinement," "smelting place," or "crucible."

• In a geographical sense, it identifies the extreme northern boundary of the land of Canaan along the Phoenician coast.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Crucible of Refining: Because the root verb tsaraph refers to the purging of impurities from gold and silver by fire, the town of Zarephath functions as a geographical word picture for spiritual refinement. It represents a place of extreme testing where faith is purified and proven genuine.

• The Monument of Provision: In the narrative of Elijah, Zarephath represents unexpected provision in a barren land. It was a place of extreme scarcity that became a monument of supernatural multiplication and life-giving power.

• The Territory of Reclaimed Dominion: In prophetic usage, Zarephath represents the outer limits of foreign pagan influence. Reclaiming "as far as Zarephath" portrays the total rollback of pagan dominance and the complete victory of God's covenant people over their former boundaries.

### 4. Theological Implications

• Sovereignty over the Borders: God's covenant promises are grounded in tangible history and geography. The mention of Zarephath demonstrates that God's plan for Israel is not partial but complete. He exercises absolute sovereignty over foreign nations, declaring that even the heartland of Phoenician paganism will fall under the inheritance of His redeemed people.

• Purification Precedes Inheritance: Given the root meaning of refining, the text implies that the path to possessing the inheritance requires passing through the crucible. The exiles of Israel were refined in the crucible of captivity before they could return to possess the land to its furthest borders.

• The Extension of Sovereign Grace: Long before the gospel was systematically preached to the Gentiles, God’s work in Zarephath signaled His grace toward those outside the commonwealth of Israel. The city represents the truth that God's redemptive purposes always had a global, boundary-breaking horizon.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:20, the prophet speaks to the displaced captives of Israel who had suffered loss, humiliation, and exile. While Edom gloated over Jerusalem's destruction, God declares a dramatic reversal of fortunes.

The phrase "as far as Zarephath" defines the northern boundary of the restored kingdom. The northern exiles (the "host of the children of Israel") will not merely return to a cramped, vulnerable territory; they will expand vastly, possessing the entire land of the Canaanites all the way up to Zarephath. This stands in parallel to the exiles of Jerusalem possessing the "cities of the South" (the Negeb). Together, these boundaries paint a picture of complete restoration, extending from the far north to the far south. The nuance is one of absolute triumph, security, and the complete vindication of God’s covenant promises.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• 1 Kings 17:9: "Arise, go to Zarephath, which belongs to Sidon, and dwell there. See, I have commanded a widow there to provide for you."

• 1 Kings 17:10: "So he arose and went to Zarephath. And when he came to the gate of the city, indeed, a widow was there gathering sticks. And he called to her and said, 'Please bring me a little water in a cup, that I may drink.'"

• Obadiah 1:20: "And the captives of this host of the children of Israel Shall possess the land of the Canaanites As far as Zarephath; The captives of Jerusalem who are in Sepharad Shall possess the cities of the South."

• Luke 4:26 (Greek equivalent Sarepta): "But to none of them was Elijah sent except to Sarepta, in the region of Sidon, to a woman who was a widow."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Reversal of Baal's Stronghold: Zarephath was located in the territory of Sidon, the homeland of Queen Jezebel and the epicenter of Baal worship. Baal was worshipped as the god of rain, dew, and fertility. By sending Elijah to Zarephath during a Baal-defying drought to perform miracles of multiplication and resurrection, God demonstrated His absolute supremacy over Baal in Baal’s own territory.

• The New Testament Application: Jesus referenced Zarephath (Sarepta) in His first sermon at Nazareth (Luke 4:25–26). He pointed out that during a great famine in Israel, Elijah was sent not to an Israelite widow, but to a Gentile widow in Zarephath. This highlight of sovereign grace to a foreigner enraged His hearers, reinforcing that Zarephath stands as an enduring symbol of God's sovereign freedom to show mercy to whom He wills.
