ISRAEL - (Yisrâʼêl, Strong's H3478)

Summary:

Yisraʾel is the proper name given by God to the patriarch Jacob, which subsequently became the enduring title for the covenant nation descended from his twelve sons. In Obadiah 1:20, "Israel" identifies the exiled covenant people whom God promises to restore, contrasting their current state as captives with their guaranteed future as possessors of the promised inheritance.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The term yisraʾel originates in Genesis 32:28, where God renames the patriarch Jacob after their wrestling encounter at Peniel. From that point forward, the name transitions from a personal identifier to a corporate one, designating the twelve tribes descended from Jacob's sons. Throughout the Old Testament, the name carries immense historical and theological weight, serving as the primary designation for God’s chosen people. Historically, it was used to describe the united monarchy under Saul, David, and Solomon, and later specifically designated the Northern Kingdom after the nation divided. Prophetically, it frequently encompasses the entirety of God's covenant people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, yisraʾel is categorized grammatically as a singular proper noun (prop sg). The name is traditionally understood to mean "God strives" or "Prince with God," reflecting the patriarch's foundational encounter with Yahweh. In biblical usage, the term functions in three primary ways: as the personal name of Jacob, as the ethnic and political designation for the nation (often phrased as "the children of Israel"), and as the theological title for the covenant community bound to Yahweh.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

The biblical usage of yisraʾel spans several distinct nuances depending on the historical and literary context:

  • Patriarchal: Referring directly to Jacob, often in poetic parallel with his original name.

  • National/Corporate: Referring to the entire ethnic group descended from Jacob, bound by the Mosaic covenant at Sinai.

  • Political: During the divided monarchy, "Israel" frequently distinguished the ten northern tribes from the southern kingdom of "Judah."

  • Theological/Eschatological: In the Prophets, "Israel" often denotes the idealized or restored covenant community that Yahweh will redeem, purify, and establish in the land, regardless of past political divisions.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, the name yisraʾel is inextricably linked to divine election, covenant faithfulness, and inheritance. To be called Israel is to be the recipient of the Abrahamic, Mosaic, and Davidic promises. When the prophets use this name, especially in contexts of judgment or exile, it serves as a reminder that God's covenant is irrevocable. Even when the nation is fractured, disciplined, or exiled from the land, they remain "Israel." The name itself becomes a guarantee of future restoration, as Yahweh's own reputation is tied to the survival and ultimate blessing of the people who bear this name.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:20, the use of yisraʾel is central to the prophet's overarching message of divine justice and covenant restoration. The entire book of Obadiah is built upon the ancient, fraternal conflict between Esau (Edom) and Jacob (Israel). Edom had gloated over and participated in the destruction of Jerusalem.

In this concluding section of the vision, Obadiah describes the reversal of fortunes. He refers to the "captives of this host of the children of Israel." By using their covenant name, the prophet emphasizes that their current condition—exiled, displaced, and captive—does not negate their identity. Because they are Israel, they are the rightful heirs to the promises made to the patriarchs. The text explicitly states that these captives "shall possess the land." The name yisraʾel here functions as a theological anchor: the people whom Edom despised and whom the nations enslaved are still Yahweh's chosen people, and He will personally ensure they reclaim their inheritance.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 39:42 – "According to all that the Lord had commanded Moses, so the children of Israel did all the work."

  • 1 Samuel 17:52 – "Now the men of Israel and Judah arose and shouted, and pursued the Philistines as far as the entrance of the valley and to the gates of Ekron."

  • 2 Kings 17:23 – "...until the Lord removed Israel out of His sight, as He had said by all His servants the prophets. So Israel was carried away from their own land to Assyria, as it is to this day."

  • Isaiah 40:27 – "Why do you say, O Jacob, And speak, O Israel: 'My way is hidden from the Lord, And my just claim is passed over by my God'?"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The prophetic use of "Israel" in Obadiah 1:20 highlights a profound canonical theme: the permanence of God's promises. While the Northern Kingdom (politically called Israel) had been scattered by Assyria, and Judah had been devastated by Babylon, the prophetic vision sees a unified, restored covenant people. The mention of the "children of Israel" alongside the "captives of Jerusalem" demonstrates that God's restorative work encompasses all the descendants of Jacob, bringing the ancient promises of land and inheritance to their appointed fulfillment.
