POSSESS - (yârash, Strong's H3423)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yarash fundamentally means to take possession of, inherit, occupy, or dispossess former occupants. In covenantal contexts, it frequently describes Israel receiving and occupying the land promised to their ancestors. In Obadiah 1:20, yarash describes the complete reversal of Israel's exile, asserting that the displaced captives of God's covenant people will return to claim and occupy their ordained territory. The word contributes to Obadiah's climax by demonstrating that human exile and foreign oppression do not nullify God's land promises, guaranteeing the full restoration of Yahweh's people to their God-given heritage.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root yarash functions prominently within ancient Israel's legal, covenantal, and historical framework. In ancient Near Eastern culture, land ownership was tied to family heritage, tribal boundaries, and divine gift. For Israel, land was not merely real estate; it was an inheritance (nachalah) granted by Yahweh under covenant promise. Thus, yarash frequently denotes the act of taking control of territory assigned by God, often involving the displacement of previous occupants who stood in opposition to Yahweh's purposes.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, yarash refers to taking hold of property or territory so as to make it one's own legal and physical domain. Its semantic range includes:

  • To take possession of or occupy land/property.

  • To inherit or receive a legal share.

  • To dispossess or drive out existing inhabitants.

The primary force of the word centers on the decisive transition of ownership and control, whether through inheritance, victorious expansion, or covenantal restoration.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament, yarash serves as a key theological term in the narrative of Israel's occupation of Canaan. It appears regularly in Deuteronomy and Joshua to describe the command and action of occupying the Promised Land. The term carries two related aspects: the displacement of hostiles who stood in judgment before God, and the positive settling and inheriting of the land by Yahweh's chosen people. In prophetic literature, yarash shifts toward eschatological and restoration contexts, assuring an exiled or oppressed remnant that God will re-establish them in their land.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, yarash underscores divine sovereignty and covenant faithfulness. Humans do not ultimately acquire sacred inheritance by raw power alone; Yahweh grants the land and enables His people to possess it. When Israel lost the land through covenant unfaithfulness and exile, the ongoing promise of future yarash signaled that judgment was not God's final word. The prophetic promise that God's people will yarash their territory demonstrates that God preserves His covenant promises against all worldly powers and historical catastrophes.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:20, the prophet addresses the humiliation of Judah's exile and the opportunistic arrogance of Edom and neighboring nations who rejoiced over Jerusalem's fall. The verse forms part of the closing triumph of Obadiah, where the tables are turned in favor of Yahweh's kingdom.

The target word in Obadiah 1:20 occurs in the Qal stem and imperfect form. The Qal stem expresses the simple action of taking possession, while the imperfect form presents this future possession as an unfolding reality in God's prophetic purpose. The exiles—those who lost everything and were scattered—are designated as the very ones who will take possession of the land stretching from the northern borders near Zarephath to the southern cities of the Negev.

The use of yarash here directly answers the dispossession suffered during the Babylonian invasion. While Edom thought Judah's inheritance was permanently ruined, Obadiah uses yarash to declare that Yahweh's exiles will return to repossess their assigned territory.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Obadiah 1:17 — "But on Mount Zion there shall be deliverance, and there shall be holiness; the house of Jacob shall possess their possessions."

  • Deuteronomy 6:18 — "And you shall do what is right and good in the sight of the LORD, that it may be well with you, and that you may go in and possess the good land which the LORD swore to your fathers..."

  • Leviticus 20:24 — "But I have said to you, 'You shall inherit their land, and I will give it to you to possess it, a land flowing with milk and honey.' I am the LORD your God, who has separated you from the peoples."

  • Isaiah 54:3 — "For you shall expand to the right and to the left, and your descendants will inherit the nations, and make the desolate cities inhabited."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The restoration described in Obadiah 1:20 using yarash echoes the original conquest language of Deuteronomy and Joshua, framing the return from exile as a second conquest or new Exodus. Just as God enabled a fragile, newly freed people to possess the land under Joshua, so He will empower a weak, exiled remnant to repossess their heritage. The ultimate horizon of this possession points to the final victory of Yahweh's kingdom, as stated in the final verse of the prophecy: "And the kingdom shall be the LORD's" (Obadiah 1:21).
