SOUTH - (negeb, Strong's H5045)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun negev fundamentally designates a dry or parched region, specifically referring to the arid southern wilderness district of Judah, and by extension, the cardinal direction "south." In Obadiah 1:20, the word identifies the southernmost desert zone of the promised land, contributing to the prophet's declaration that returning exiles will repossess the entirety of their covenant inheritance, down to its most remote southern settlements.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Geographically, the Negev constitutes the semi-arid transition zone situated between the central Judean hill country to the north and the Sinai wilderness to the south. Historically, this terrain served as both a pastoral frontier and a strategic southern buffer zone for Israel. Because of its dry climate, survival in this region required specialized knowledge of seasonal water sources and cisterns.

In Old Testament history, the Negev was a place of patriarchs, pastoralists, and border conflicts. Abraham and Isaac journeyed and sojourned through this southern zone. Due to its defining geographic location relative to the heartland of Israel, the proper noun negev became the standard Hebrew designation for the cardinal direction south.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, negev derives from an unexpressed root associated with being dry or parched. Standard Hebrew lexicons (such as Brown-Driver-Briggs and the Theological Wordbook of the Old Testament) categorize its meanings along two primary lines:

  • As a geographical proper noun: The Negev, the dry southern district of Palestine located south of Hebron.

  • As a directional noun: The south, southern region, or southern side of a boundary or structure.

In biblical Hebrew, the term moves seamlessly between these two senses. Whether denoting the specific physical desert region or indicating spatial orientation toward the south, negev carries the contextual weight of Israel's southern boundary.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament narrative, prophecy, and apocalyptic literature, negev functions as both a physical landmark and a direction of boundary lines.

In spatial descriptions, such as the construction of the Tabernacle or the allotment of tribal territories, negev establishes the southern perimeter. In historical narratives, it identifies the dry district through which spies scouted the land or where David conducted raids during his time in exile.

In prophetic texts, the "cities of the South" often represent the vulnerability of Judah's outer frontier during judgment, as well as the complete extent of future restoration when God gathers His exiled people back to their land.

4. Theological Implications

The usage of negev carries profound covenantal significance within Old Testament theology:

  • Covenant Boundary and Repossession: God's promise to Abraham included the full extent of the land. When prophetic promises announce the repossession of the Negev, they affirm that no part of the covenant allotment will remain lost or desolate.

  • Reversal of Exile: During the Babylonian invasion, the cities of the Negev were shut up and emptied. The prophetic promise that God's people will inhabit these cities once again demonstrates divine power to transform complete judgment into renewed life and productivity.

  • Fullness of Restoration: By paired geographical scope—from the northern territory down to the dry Negev—Scripture emphasizes that divine restoration is total rather than partial.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:20, the prophet details the geographical reversal that follows divine judgment upon Edom and the nations. Edom had gloated over Jerusalem’s downfall and sought to exploit Israel's vulnerability. Obadiah answers this injustice with a message of divine triumph and full territorial recovery.

Grammatically, negev appears in Obadiah 1:20 as a masculine singular noun. Within the immediate context, Obadiah divides the returning exiles into distinct groups and assigns them their restored inheritances. The exiles from Jerusalem located in Sepharad are designated to possess "the cities of the South (negev)."

This detail is contextualized by the contrast between northern and southern extremes in the verse. While one group possesses the northern region up to Zarephath, another occupies the southern cities of the Negev. By specifically highlighting the negev—the most arid and remote region of Judah's heritage—Obadiah asserts that God's restoration will extend to the absolute edges of the kingdom. The dry, formerly abandoned southern outposts will once again pulsate with covenant life under the sovereignty of Yahweh.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Numbers 13:17 — "Then Moses sent them to spy out the land of Canaan, and said to them, 'Go up this way into the South, and go up to the mountains.'"

  • 1 Samuel 30:14 — "We made a raid upon the South of the Cherethites, upon the territory which belongs to Judah, and upon the South of Caleb; and we burned Ziklag with fire."

  • Jeremiah 13:19 — "The cities of the South shall be shut up, and no one shall open them; Judah shall be carried away captive, all of it; it shall be wholly carried away captive."

  • Jeremiah 32:44 — "Men shall buy fields for money, sign deeds and seal them, and take witnesses, in the land of Benjamin, in the places around Jerusalem, in the cities of Judah, in the cities of the mountains, in the cities of the lowland, and in the cities of the South; for I will cause their captives to return, says the LORD."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A striking thematic connection exists between Jeremiah 13:19, Jeremiah 32:44, and Obadiah 1:20 regarding the "cities of the South." In Jeremiah 13:19, the shutting up of the Negev's cities marked the catastrophic completeness of Judah's exile. However, in Jeremiah 32:44 and Obadiah 1:20, the repopulation of these exact same southern cities becomes the benchmark for God's faithful restoration. What judgment emptied, covenant mercy repopulates. The mention of this dry region highlights that God's redemption reaches even the parched and distant places of His kingdom.
