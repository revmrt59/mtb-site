MOUNT - ( har , Strong's H2022 )

Summary:

In the Old Testament, the Hebrew word har refers to a mountain, hill, or mountainous region, symbolizing stability, strength, and the dwelling place of God or earthly rulers. In Obadiah 1:21, it draws a stark theological and geographical contrast between "Mount Zion" (the place of God's dwelling, covenant, and salvation) and the "mount of Esau" (the rocky, proud stronghold of Edom destined for judgment). Ultimately, the word serves to show that human strongholds will fail, while God's established mountain will endure as the seat of His sovereign kingdom.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near East, mountains (har) were much more than geographical features; they were potent symbols of security, eternity, and spiritual power. Because of their towering height and solid foundations, they were viewed as the pillars of the earth and the places where heaven met earth. Consequently, mountains were frequently chosen as sites for temples, altars, and military fortresses. The rugged terrain of a mountain range provided natural defenses against invading armies, making the inhabitants of mountainous regions feel virtually invincible. In the biblical landscape, the contrast between different mountains often carries profound spiritual significance, representing the clash between the kingdoms of man and the kingdom of God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The Hebrew noun har designates a mountain, a hill, a mountain range, or a mountainous region. It is used to refer to specific, individual peaks—such as Mount Sinai, Mount Hor, or Mount Zion—as well as broader mountainous territories, such as the hill country of Judah or Ephraim. Lexically, the word denotes:

• A physical mountain or hill.

• A mountainous tract of land or highland region.

• A metaphor for stability, permanence, and political or spiritual kingdoms.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, har is used to construct vivid mental and spiritual images:

• Stability and Permanence: Mountains are described as ancient and enduring. Yet, their trembling or melting in the presence of God highlights His absolute supremacy over creation.

• The Seat of Divine Power: God's holy mountain, Zion, is depicted not merely as a physical elevation in Jerusalem, but as the center of His cosmic rule and the source of His law and blessing.

• Human Pride and False Security: The mountainous, rocky territory of Mount Seir (the home of Edom) represents human hubris. Edom believed their high-altitude fortresses made them secure from any enemy, a delusion of safety that God directly confronts.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, har represents the ultimate destiny of all spiritual and earthly authorities. The contrast in Obadiah 1:21 between Mount Zion and the mount of Esau is a macrocosm of the entire biblical narrative. Mount Zion represents the covenant community, the place where God's presence dwells, and the source of salvation. The mount of Esau represents human pride, fleshly self-sufficiency, and hostility toward God's people.

The triumph of Mount Zion over the mount of Esau demonstrates that:

• Human pride and high strongholds will eventually be brought low by divine justice.

• True security and deliverance are found only in the place of God's covenant presence.

• The ultimate end of history is the undisputed sovereignty of God, where all rival earthly kingdoms are subjected to His righteous rule.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:21, the term har is used twice in a deliberate parallel: "Mount Zion" and the "mount of Esau." Edom (Esau) had looked down in pride from their lofty mountain strongholds, mocking the destruction of Jerusalem. However, the prophet declares a reversal of fortunes. "Saviors" or deliverers will ascend Mount Zion, but they will not merely defend Jerusalem; they will go forth to judge and rule over the mount of Esau. This geographical reversal mirrors a spiritual reality: the humble, afflicted people of God on Zion will be exalted, while the proud, self-reliant kingdom of Esau will be brought to ruin. The passage concludes with the declaration that "the kingdom shall be the LORD's," showing that the collapse of Esau's mountain paves the way for the global manifestation of God's reign.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In biblical typology, physical height does not equate to spiritual value. Geographically, Mount Zion is a modest hill compared to the towering peaks of Hermon or the rugged heights of Mount Seir in Edom. Yet, God chose Zion as His dwelling place. This reveals a consistent biblical principle: God rejects the proud, towering heights of human achievement and chooses the humble, lowly things of this world to display His glory and power.
