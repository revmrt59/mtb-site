LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh is the personal, covenant-revealing name of the one true God of Israel. Derived from the verb of existence, it presents God as the self-existent, eternal, and unchangeable One who is actively present with His people to redeem them. In the dramatic climax of Obadiah 1:21, this name guarantees that despite the historical pride of Israel's enemies and the temporary desolation of His people, the ultimate sovereignty, authority, and final kingdom belong exclusively to the faithful, covenant-keeping God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The name Yahweh is deeply rooted in the historical revelation of God to His people. When Moses stood before the burning bush, God revealed His name as "I AM WHO I AM" (Exodus 3:14), which is grammatically linked to the name Yahweh. The visual background of the burning bush—a bush that burned with fire but was not consumed—is the perfect physical representation of this name. It portrays a God who does not need external fuel to exist. Unlike the pagan deities of the ancient Near East, who relied on human sacrifices, temple care, and geographical boundaries to sustain their power, the God of Israel is entirely self-sufficient, self-sustained, and eternally alive.

Because the ancient Israelites held this name in the highest possible esteem, they eventually stopped pronouncing it aloud to avoid taking it in vain. Instead, they substituted the title Adonai (Lord) during public readings. This deep reverence is reflected in modern English translations by rendering the four Hebrew consonants (the Tetragrammaton, YHWH) in capital letters as "LORD."

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Yahweh is the proper noun representing the personal name of the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

• The word signifies "He who is," "He who exists," or "He who brings into being." It denotes absolute self-existence. God has no beginning, no end, and is dependent on nothing outside of Himself.

• Lexically, it is the name that expresses God's relational presence. While Elohim refers to God in His role as the powerful Creator and Governor of the universe, Yahweh is the name used when God enters into an intimate, binding covenant relationship with human beings.

• It carries the core meaning of unchangeable faithfulness. Because He is the eternally existing One, His character, promises, and covenants remain steadfast throughout all generations.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Appearing over 6,800 times in the Old Testament, Yahweh is the most common designation for God. The Old Testament writers often compound this name with various titles to form rich, comforting word pictures of God’s relational character:

• Yahweh-Yireh ("The LORD Will Provide"): Illustrates God as the one who foresees the needs of His people and provides the ultimate sacrifice (Genesis 22:14).

• Yahweh-Nissi ("The LORD Is My Banner"): Depicts God as the rallying point, victory standard, and defender of His people in times of spiritual and physical warfare (Exodus 17:15).

• Yahweh-Raah ("The LORD Is My Shepherd"): Portrays the tender, intimate, and protective care of God over His flock (Psalm 23:1).

• Yahweh-Sabaoth ("The LORD of Hosts"): Displays God as the sovereign commander of the heavenly armies, wielding supreme authority over all cosmic and earthly powers (1 Samuel 1:3).

4. Theological Implications

The theology of Yahweh centers on the doctrine of divine aseity (self-existence) and covenant immutability.

• Divine Aseity: God does not need us, yet He chooses us. His existence is not contingent upon creation, which means His plans cannot be derailed by human failure or demonic opposition.

• Covenant Immutability: When God binds Himself to His people using the name Yahweh, He stakes His very existence on the fulfillment of His promises. If God were to fail to keep His word, He would cease to be Yahweh. Thus, the name is an absolute guarantee of security for the believer.

• The Sovereign King: Because He is the source of all being, all other kingdoms and authorities are derivative and temporary. Only His kingdom is inherent and everlasting.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:21, the prophet declares, "And the kingdom shall be the LORD’s." This statement serves as the triumphant conclusion of the shortest book in the Old Testament. The immediate context of Obadiah is the devastating invasion of Jerusalem and the cruel betrayal of the Judeans by their close relatives, the Edomites (the descendants of Esau). Edom had gloated over Israel’s downfall, standing on the high, rocky, seemingly impregnable fortresses of Mount Seir, mocking the God of Israel.

By concluding the prophecy with the assertion that "the kingdom shall be Yahweh's," Obadiah delivers a powerful, comforting truth to a traumatized and exiled remnant:

• Sovereign Reversal: Though Edom claimed the high ground and boasted in their secure rocks, their pride would be brought low. Mount Zion, though temporarily desolated, would be restored.

• The Vindications of the Covenant: The name Yahweh is invoked here to remind the survivors of Israel that God has not forgotten His covenant with Jacob. Edom's temporary victory did not mean their gods had triumphed over Israel's God.

• The Ultimate Consummation: History does not belong to the empires of Babylon, Edom, or Rome. History belongs to Yahweh. The final word of human history is not the triumph of injustice, but the visible establishment of the direct, undisputed rule of the self-existent Covenant Keeper.

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The New Testament Connection: The New Testament writers, using the Greek Septuagint translation of the Old Testament, translated Yahweh as Kyrios ("Lord"). When the New Testament boldly declares that "Jesus Christ is Lord" (Philippians 2:11), it is directly identifying Jesus of Nazareth with the self-existent Covenant God of Obadiah 1:21.

• The "I AM" Claims of Christ: Jesus consciously and repeatedly applied the essence of the name Yahweh to Himself, most notably in John 8:58: "Before Abraham was, I AM."

• Structural Symmetry in Obadiah: The book begins with a sovereign declaration from "the Lord GOD" (Obadiah 1:1) and ends with the victorious realization that "the kingdom shall be the LORD's" (Obadiah 1:21). The entire prophecy is bookended by the absolute certainty of divine sovereignty.
