KINGDOM - ( malkut , Strong's H4410 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word malkut denotes the reign, royal authority, dominion, or sovereign rule of a king, as well as the actual realm over which that authority is actively exercised. In the prophetic climax of Obadiah 1:21, the term represents the ultimate, visible, and undisputed establishment of Yahweh's global reign over all human empires. This stands in stark contrast to the pride of Edom, promising a final state of cosmic order where human usurpation of power ends and God's righteous sovereignty is fully recognized.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun malkut derives from the root verb malak, which means to reign, to rule, or to become king. In the ancient Near East, a kingdom was not defined primarily by geographic borders drawn on a map, but rather by the active, dynamic sphere of a monarch’s authority. Where the king’s voice was obeyed and his power was executed, his malkut was present. The word carries a dual sense of both the abstract right to rule (sovereign majesty) and the concrete territory or people being ruled (the kingdom). In the context of the book of Obadiah, this word stands as a sharp divine verdict against the proud mountain-fortress kingdom of Edom, which sought to secure its own dominion at the expense of God's covenant people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The lexical definition of malkut encompasses several key nuances:

• Royal power, dominion, or sovereign rule.

• The reign or period of rule of a specific monarch.

• The realm, territory, or empire subject to a king.

• The absolute, universal, and everlasting kingship of God over all creation.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, malkut is used to describe both human kingdoms (such as those of Saul, David, Solomon, Persia, or Babylon) and the divine kingdom of Yahweh. When applied to human rulers, malkut refers to a delegated, finite authority that is subject to rise and fall. In contrast, when the term is applied to God, it is pictured as an unshakeable throne established in the heavens that rules over all creation. It is described using the word picture of a global, eternal dominion that breaks down all rival human powers and stands forever. God's malkut is not passive; it is an active, reigning force that breaks the pride of the nations and vindicates the oppressed.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, malkut represents the supreme theme of the Old Testament: the sovereign kingship of God. This concept forms the foundation of the biblical theology of theocracy. Human kingship was always intended to be a reflection of, and subservient to, the divine King. The prophetic announcement of the coming malkut of Yahweh serves to remind Israel and the nations that earthly empires are temporary. The ultimate goal of redemptive history is the complete subjugation of all rebellious human authority under the benevolent, righteous, and supreme rule of God. This Old Testament hope directly prepares the way for the New Testament announcement of the arrival of the Kingdom of God in the person and ministry of Jesus Christ.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:21, malkut appears at the absolute climax of the prophecy: "And the kingdom shall be the LORD’s." The historical context of Obadiah is one of deep betrayal; Edom (the descendants of Esau) gloated over and participated in the destruction of Jerusalem (the descendants of Jacob). Edom believed their high, rocky dwellings made them invincible. However, Obadiah declares that those who exalted themselves will be brought down, while Mount Zion will be delivered. The saviors or deliverers who ascend Mount Zion to judge Edom do not establish a human empire for Israel's self-aggrandizement. Rather, the climax of their victory is that all earthly dominion is transferred to its rightful Owner. The nuance of malkut here is eschatological and absolute: it marks the end of human misrule and the visible, undisputed manifestation of Yahweh’s sovereign reign over the entire earth.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 103:19: "The LORD has established His throne in heaven, And His kingdom rules over all."

• Psalm 145:11: "They shall speak of the glory of Your kingdom, And talk of Your power,"

• Psalm 145:13: "Your kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, And Your dominion endures throughout all generations."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The promise that "the kingdom shall be the LORD's" in Obadiah 1:21 finds its ultimate New Testament fulfillment in the Book of Revelation. The transition of worldly power to divine rule is celebrated in Revelation 11:15, where the seventh angel sounds, and loud voices in heaven declare that the kingdoms of this world have become the kingdoms of our Lord and of His Christ. For pastors and teachers, this word study offers a profound message of hope: no matter how powerful, proud, or secure wicked human systems may appear today, they are destined to fail. God's kingdom is the only enduring reality, and believers can live in confident submission to that coming, eternal reign.
