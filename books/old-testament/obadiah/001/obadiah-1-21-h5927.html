COME UP - ( 'alah , Strong's H5927 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'alah primarily means to ascend, rise, go up, or come up. In the context of Obadiah 1:21, it describes the triumphant return and ascent of God's appointed deliverers to Mount Zion, signaling the physical and spiritual restoration of Israel, the execution of divine judgment upon their enemies, and the ultimate establishment of Yahweh's sovereign kingdom over all the earth.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The verb 'alah is one of the most common verbs in the Hebrew Bible, occurring over 800 times.

• Its primary physical meaning is to move from a lower to a higher place, to ascend, climb, or go up.

• In ancient Israel, geography and theology were deeply intertwined. Jerusalem, specifically Mount Zion, was topographically elevated compared to the surrounding regions. Traveling to Jerusalem was always described as "going up" ('alah), regardless of the geographical direction from which one approached.

• Beyond simple physical ascent, 'alah carries significant theological weight. It is used for going up to worship at the tabernacle or temple, ascending to offer sacrifices on the altar, and ascending to the presence of God on a mountain.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The basic meaning of 'alah is to ascend, rise, go up, or come up.

• In the active Qal stem, it denotes physical movement upward (such as climbing a mountain, the rising of mist, or scaling a wall), going up to a land or city of higher elevation, or ascending for a specific purpose like battle or worship.

• In the Hiphil (causative) stem, it means to bring up, lead up, or offer up (such as burnt offerings, 'olah, which literally means "that which goes up").

• In Obadiah 1:21, the verb is in the Qal stem (perfect with waw consecutive, we'alu), meaning "and they shall ascend" or "then they shall come up."

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Sacrificial Ascent: The noun 'olah (burnt offering) is derived from this root because the smoke of the offering ascends to God as a soothing aroma.

• Geopolitical Ascent: Military campaigns are frequently described as "going up" to wage war against a city or region.

• Spiritual Pilgrimage: Going to the temple for the annual feasts is a physical and spiritual ascent ('aliyah), celebrating God's dwelling place on His holy mountain.

4. Theological Implications

• The act of "coming up" to Mount Zion represents restoration, victory, and the re-establishment of divine order. Zion is the place of God's presence and sovereign rule.

• When saviors (deliverers) "come up" to Mount Zion, it marks the reversal of Israel's humiliation. Edom had gloated over Jerusalem's downfall, but God's final triumph involves the vindication of His people on His holy hill.

• This ascent is ultimately linked to the establishment of the eschatological kingdom of Yahweh: "And the kingdom shall be the LORD’s." The physical ascent of these leaders mirrors the supreme elevation of Yahweh's authority over all nations, especially Esau (Edom).

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Obadiah 1:21, 'alah is used in the context of the Day of the Lord and the restoration of Israel.

• Edom (the mountains of Esau) had actively participated in and gloated over the destruction of Jerusalem.

• In a dramatic reversal of fortunes, the "saviors" (or deliverers, moshia'im, related to the word for judges or saviors in the book of Judges) do not just defend Jerusalem; they "come up" on Mount Zion to execute judgment on Edom.

• Mount Zion, once defiled and laid low by foreign invaders, becomes the elevated seat of righteous judgment from which the mountains of Esau (representing pride and self-exaltation) are brought low. The movement of "coming up" emphasizes the triumphant enthronement of God's representatives on His holy mountain.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 35:1: "Then God said to Jacob, 'Arise, go up to Bethel and dwell there; and make an altar there to God, who appeared to you when you fled from the face of Esau your brother.'"

• Exodus 19:3: "And Moses went up to God, and the LORD called to him from the mountain, saying, 'Thus you shall say to the house of Jacob, and tell the children of Israel:'"

• Psalm 24:3: "Who may ascend into the hill of the LORD? Or who may stand in His holy place?"

• Isaiah 2:3: "Many people shall come and say, 'Come, and let us go up to the mountain of the LORD, To the house of the God of Jacob; He will teach us His ways, And we shall walk in His paths.' For out of Zion shall go forth the law, And the word of the LORD from Jerusalem."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The term translated "saviors" (moshia'im) in Obadiah 1:21 is a Hiphil participle of yasha. It refers to human instruments of deliverance, reminiscent of the "judges" whom God raised up to deliver Israel from oppression.

• The ascent of these saviors to Mount Zion contrasts sharply with Edom's prideful dwelling "in the clefts of the rock" (Obadiah 1:3). Edom thought they were secure in their high mountain fortresses, but they are brought down, while God's humble people are lifted up and "come up" to the highest spiritual plane of victory.

• Ultimately, this passage points forward to the supreme victory of Christ, who ascended to the heavenly Zion and will return to establish His visible, universal kingdom.
