ESAU - ( 'Esaw , Strong's H6215 )

Summary:

The name "Esau" refers to the twin brother of Jacob, representing the father of the Edomite nation. In the prophetic context of Obadiah 1:21, "the mountains of Esau" serves as a metonymy for the entire nation of Edom, symbolizing human pride, self-sufficiency, and hostility toward God’s covenant people. The judgment of Esau's mountains stands in stark contrast to the restoration of Mount Zion, illustrating the ultimate triumph of God's sovereign kingdom over all rebellious earthly powers.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • Esau is the firstborn son of Isaac and Rebekah, twin brother to Jacob. At birth, he was red and "like a hairy garment all over," leading to his name 'Esaw (related to the idea of being rough or hairy) and his alternate name 'Edom (meaning "red").

  • Esau famously despised his birthright, trading it for a single meal of red stew. This act defined his character in biblical history as a profane, carnal man who valued immediate physical gratification over eternal covenant promises.

  • The descendants of Esau settled in Mount Seir, a rugged, mountainous, and highly defensible region south of the Dead Sea. This geography fostered a spirit of pride and invulnerability. In the prophetic books, especially Obadiah, "Esau" represents the nation of Edom, embodying a perpetual, generational enmity against Israel (Jacob).

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The proper noun 'Esaw designates the historical figure Esau and, by extension, the ethnic group and territory of his descendants.

  • The primary meaning of the name relates to being "hairy" or "rough-skinned," pointing to his physical appearance at birth.

  • In prophetic literature, the term functions as a collective singular noun representing the entire Edomite collective, their cultural identity, and their territorial stronghold ("the mountains of Esau").

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The Mountainous Stronghold: The "mountains of Esau" refers to the high, rocky cliffs of Petra and the surrounding Seir range. This physical elevation mirrored their spiritual pride. They believed they were secure from any invader, crying out, "Who will bring me down to the ground?"

  • The Brother-Enemy: The use of the personal name "Esau" instead of simply the political term "Edom" highlights the fraternal relationship. The conflict between Jacob and Esau is a family tragedy. Edom's betrayal of Judah during the Babylonian invasion was a violation of brotherly covenant.

  • The Fleshly Man: Spiritually, Esau represents the natural, unspiritual man who relies on human wisdom, military strength, and geographic security rather than the grace and promise of God.

4. Theological Implications

  • Sovereign Election and Justice: The historical tension between Jacob and Esau reflects the mystery of divine election. God's judgment of Esau in Obadiah is the righteous outworking of Edom's persistent wickedness, pride, and active malice toward God’s chosen line.

  • The Doom of Pride: Esau's mountain strongholds seemed impregnable to men, but they were utterly vulnerable to God. The destruction of Esau proves that no human security, wealth, or wisdom can withstand divine judgment.

  • The Kingdom of God: The ultimate defeat of Esau's mountains and the exaltation of Mount Zion demonstrate that God's kingdom will triumph over every earthly power that opposes His people.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Obadiah 1:21, "saviors" (or deliverers/liberators) ascend Mount Zion. Their mission is "to judge the mountains of Esau."

  • Mount Zion represents the place of God's presence, grace, and restored remnant. Conversely, the "mountains of Esau" represent the heights of human pride and rebellion.

  • The judgment of Esau is not arbitrary; it is the direct consequence of Edom's violence against "your brother Jacob." The poetic justice is clear: those who stood aloof and rejoiced in Jerusalem's fall will themselves be brought low, and their territory will be ruled from the very mountain they mocked.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 25:25 - "And the first came out red. He was like a hairy garment all over; so they called his name Esau."

  • Malachi 1:3 - "But Esau I have hated, And laid waste his mountains and his heritage For the jackals of the wilderness."

  • Obadiah 1:6 - "Oh, how Esau shall be searched out! How his hidden treasures shall be sought after!"

  • Obadiah 1:8 - "'Will I not in that day,' says the LORD, 'Even destroy the wise men from Edom, And understanding from the mountains of Esau?'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The Herodian Connection: The New Testament continuation of this conflict is seen in the Herods, who were Idumeans (descendants of Esau/Edom). Herod the Great attempted to murder the infant King of the Jews (Jesus), and Herod Antipas mocked Him, continuing the historic animosity of Esau toward Jacob's line.

  • Typology: Esau represents the old man, the flesh, which is restless, violent, and ultimately destined for subjection under the spiritual man (Jacob/Israel), who represents the spirit and the kingdom of God.
