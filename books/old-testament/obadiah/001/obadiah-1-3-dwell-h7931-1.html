DWELL - (shâkan, Strong's H7931)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb shakhan means to settle down, abide, dwell, or take up residence. In Obadiah 1:3, shakhan describes Edom as the one dwelling in the clefts of the rock. The word identifies Edom's settled habitation within high, inaccessible mountain fortresses. This contributes directly to the prophet's argument by exposing how Edom's secure physical dwelling fueled their heart's deception and pride, setting up the contrast with God's sovereign judgment.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root shakhan reflects a foundational concept in the Ancient Near East regarding residence, settlement, and pitch-dwelling. It indicates the act of taking up residence, settling down in a specific locality, or abiding in a place over time. In Old Testament history, the term encompasses both the physical habitation of human beings in cities or lands and the localized dwelling of God among His people in the tabernacle and temple.

In the historical setting of Obadiah, the nation of Edom occupied the rugged, mountainous territory south of the Dead Sea, centered around regions such as Sela and Mount Seir. The natural topography consisted of high sandstone cliffs and narrow canyons, providing formidable natural defenses. Edom relied on this geography for physical security, believing their dwelling place rendered them invincible to enemy assault.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, shakhan carries the core meaning of "to settle down," "to abide," "to dwell," or "to reside." Standard Hebrew lexicons distinguish its basic spatial sense from temporary movement: while generic verbs of motion describe moving toward a place, shakhan emphasizes remaining in that location and making it one's habitation.

The semantic range includes:

  • Settle down or rest in a location (such as a cloud abiding over a mountain or a person residing in a region).

  • Take up permanent or ongoing residence within a dwelling, territory, or fortress.

  • Tabernacle or localized indwelling, used extensively for God causing His presence or name to dwell among Israel.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, shakhan operates across both earthly and divine contexts. In human settings, it describes individuals or groups inhabiting lands, cities, or natural shelters. For example, in Genesis 26:2, God commands Isaac to dwell in the land He would show him. In Jeremiah 49:31, it describes a nation dwelling securely without gates or bars.

In divine contexts, shakhan serves as a key theological term for God's presence. In Deuteronomy 12:11 and Nehemiah 1:9, God chooses a specific place to make His name dwell among His people. This divine habitation is not due to God being contained by physical structures, but represents His covenantal willingness to abide near His redeemed people.

4. Theological Implications

The theological significance of shakhan centers on the distinction between human self-sufficiency and divine sovereignty. When humans make their physical dwelling the ultimate source of security, as Edom did, residence turns into reliance on the creation rather than the Creator. Scripture repeatedly demonstrates that no earthly habitation, regardless of its elevation or natural defenses, can protect a nation from divine righteousness.

Conversely, true security in biblical theology is found where God chooses to dwell. While Edom trusted in their high earthly dwelling, God's promise to Israel was that He would dwell among them, offering genuine covenantal protection. Thus, shakhan highlights the contrast between human strongholds built on pride and the divine dwelling built on covenant grace.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:3, the prophet confronts Edom with the word of Yahweh: "The pride of your heart has deceived you, You who dwell in the clefts of the rock, Whose habitation is high, Who says in his heart, 'Who will bring me down to the ground?'"

The authoritative target morphology for shakhan in Obadiah 1:3 is Stem: Qal, Form: participle, Gender: masculine, Number: singular. In this grammatical construction, the participle functions substantively to characterize Edom by their location: "the one dwelling" or "O dweller."

The context directly connects Edom's physical dwelling with their spiritual deception. The high clefts of the rock provided a high degree of natural safety, which Edom interpreted as total invulnerability. By describing Edom as the dweller in these high places, Obadiah highlights the ground of Edom's hubris. The very next verse (Obadiah 1:4) breaks this illusion: "Though you ascend as high as the eagle, and though you set your nest among the stars, from there I will bring you down, says the LORD." The word shakhan thus establishes the location of Edom's pride, establishing the spatial contrast between Edom's high habitation and Yahweh's power to dislodge them.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 26:2 – "Then the LORD appeared to him and said, 'Do not go down to Egypt; dwell in the land of which I shall tell you.'"

  • Deuteronomy 12:11 – "Then there will be the place where the LORD your God chooses to make His name dwell, there you shall bring all that I command you..."

  • Isaiah 33:16 – "He will dwell on high; His place of defense will be the fortress of rocks; bread will be given him, his water will be sure."

  • Jeremiah 49:31 – "Arise, go up to the wealthy nation that dwells securely, which has neither gates nor bars, dwelling alone, says the LORD."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The spatial imagery in Obadiah 1:3–4 relies heavily on height and depth. Edom's dwelling place in the rock clefts generated an internal monologue of absolute safety: "Who will bring me down to the ground?" Edom assumed that physical altitude equated to ultimate safety.

The irony of the passage is that while Edom viewed their elevated residence as unassailable, Yahweh operates above all earthly heights. The contrast between Edom settling down (shakhan) in high rocks and Yahweh bringing them down demonstrates that earthly fortifications cannot withstand God's judgment.
