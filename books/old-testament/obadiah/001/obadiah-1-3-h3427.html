YASHAB - ( yāšab , Strong's H3427 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yāšab primarily means to sit, remain, dwell, or inhabit. It conveys a sense of permanence, settledness, and established residency rather than a temporary, nomadic tent-dwelling. In the context of Obadiah 1:3, this root undergirds the concept of Edom's high and seemingly impregnable "habitation" (the Hebrew noun šebet, which is a direct nominal derivative of yāšab). Edom relied on the natural, rocky fortresses of Mount Seir, believing their dwelling was so physically secure that no enemy could ever reach them. However, this settled "sitting down" in the heights fostered a false, self-deceiving pride, which God promised to bring down.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient Near Eastern culture, sitting (yāšab) was a posture of authority, rest, and established presence. Kings sat on thrones, judges sat to render decisions, and families dwelt in ancestral lands. Contrast this with nomadic wandering; to yāšab is to settle down, claim a space as one’s own, and find rest from labor or threat.

For Edom, the descendants of Esau, this settled dwelling was carved directly into the red sandstone cliffs of Petra and the rugged clefts of Mount Sela. Their physical geography offered natural military protection, giving their yāšab a nuance of unassailable security. They "sat" on high, looking down on the rest of the world, convinced that their physical home was an eternal, unreachable fortress.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The basic meaning of yāšab is "to sit down," "to remain," "to dwell," or "to inhabit."

• In its active verbal forms, it describes the physical act of sitting (on a throne, on the ground, or at a table) or the state of living in a particular place over a prolonged period.

• As a noun or infinitive construct (such as šebet, the word for "habitation" in Obadiah 1:3), it refers to a seat, place of residence, or lifestyle of dwelling.

• The semantic range spans from physical posture (sitting) to socio-political status (reigning as king) to geographical location (inhabiting a territory).

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Sitting on a Throne (Authority): The word is frequently used for kings sitting on their thrones, representing established rule, judgment, and sovereign authority. When God is described as sitting in the heavens, it paints a picture of His absolute rule over creation.

• Dwelling in Peace (Rest): The term pictures a life of security and blessing, where a family can dwell under their own vine and fig tree without fear of invaders.

• The Sanctuary / Temple: God is said to dwell among His people, specifically in the Holy of Holies or enthroned upon the cherubim, signifying His permanent presence and covenant relationship with Israel.

### 4. Theological Implications

• Sovereignty vs. Human Pride: God is the ultimate Dweller of the heights. When humans attempt to exalt their own dwelling (yāšab) to the heavens, it is an act of idolatrous self-deification. Edom attempted to occupy a height of security that belongs to God alone.

• The Illusion of Earthly Security: Settling down (yāšab) in earthly fortresses leads to spiritual complacency. Edom believed their geographical habitation made them immune to judgment, forgetting that no human fortress is out of God's reach.

• True Dwelling is in Yahweh: True security is not found in high physical places, but in dwelling in the presence of God. While Edom’s high habitation would be brought down, those who dwell in the secret place of the Most High remain forever secure.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:3, the prophet addresses Edom’s pride: "The pride of your heart has deceived you, you who dwell in the clefts of the rock, whose habitation is high." The word "habitation" (šebet) points to Edom's illusion of permanent, unshakeable sovereignty.

They had "seated" themselves in the heights of Sela (Petra), believing no power could dislodge them. The height of their physical habitation mirrored the height of their spiritual pride. By using this root, the prophet highlights that their secure dwelling has become their spiritual snare. God targets this specific "dwelling" to show that no human height can withstand His sovereign summons; He will bring them down from their high seat.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 13:12: "Abram dwelt in the land of Canaan, and Lot dwelt in the cities of the plain and pitched his tent even as far as Sodom."

• Psalm 1:1: "Blessed is the man Who walks not in the counsel of the ungodly, Nor stands in the path of sinners, Nor sits in the seat of the scornful;"

• Psalm 2:4: "He who sits in the heavens shall laugh; The Lord shall hold them in derision."

• Psalm 91:1: "He who dwells in the secret place of the Most High Shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

A powerful contrast exists in Hebrew between šākan (to camp, tabernacle, or temporarily reside) and yāšab (to sit down, settle permanently). In Obadiah 1:3, both concepts are at play. Edom’s temporary camping (šākan) in the rock clefts had become, in their minds, an eternal, unshakeable "enthronement" or permanent habitation (šebet). Historically, this pride was shattered when the Nabataeans eventually drove them out of Petra, proving that only God's habitation is truly eternal and secure.
