HEART - ( leb, Strong's H3820 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word leb refers to the inner man, serving as the command center of a person's thoughts, emotions, will, and choices. In Obadiah 1:3, it highlights the seat of Edom’s pride and self-delusion, illustrating how an arrogant heart blinds people to their true vulnerability before God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient Hebrew thought, the heart was not merely the organ that pumped blood, nor was it solely the seat of fleeting emotions. Instead, the heart represented the central core of human life—the command center of a person's entire existence. It is the place where thoughts are processed, decisions are formulated, desires are cultivated, and one's relationship with God is either embraced or rejected.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun leb designates the inner self or the interior life of a human being. The semantic range of this word includes:

  • The mind and intellect, where plans are devised and logical thoughts occur.

  • The seat of emotions, such as joy, sorrow, courage, fear, and love.

  • The will and volition, where moral decisions, intentions, and choices are made.

  • The spiritual center, where the human spirit interacts with the Spirit of God.

In the context of moral failure, the heart is the fertile soil where sin and pride take root before manifesting in external actions.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament uses leb over 800 times, making it the primary term for the inner life.

  • The Deceitful Heart: The heart possesses an active ability to deceive its owner, convincing a person of false security or false righteousness.

  • The Hardened Heart: Scripture frequently depicts a heart that has grown stubborn, heavy, or unyielding to God's warnings.

  • The Heart Speaking: To "say in one's heart" is a Hebrew idiom for thinking or whispering internally, revealing a person's deepest, unvarnished convictions.

4. Theological Implications

God judges the heart, not just external appearances. While human beings look at outward stature, achievements, or defenses, God searches and evaluates the leb. Pride is fundamentally an inward spiritual disease of the heart before it is an outward posture. When a heart is filled with pride, it attempts to usurp God's position, declaring self-sufficiency and independence. Because the natural heart is prone to self-delusion, true spiritual transformation must begin with a supernatural renewal of the heart.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:3, the prophet addresses Edom, a nation nestled in the highly secure, mountainous region of Sela (Petra). Their physical geography fostered a false sense of absolute security. The phrase "The pride of your heart has deceived you" reveals that Edom's primary problem was not their physical altitude, but their spiritual attitude. Their leb had deceived them into believing they were invincible ("Who will bring me down to the ground?").

The nuance here is the self-deceiving nature of pride. The heart is both the deceiver and the deceived. Edom's heart convinced their mind that their natural defenses made them immune to divine judgment, showing how arrogance distorts rational reality.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Proverbs 4:23 - "Keep your heart with all diligence, For out of it spring the issues of life."

  • Jeremiah 17:9 - "The heart is deceitful above all things, And desperately wicked; Who can know it?"

  • Proverbs 3:5 - "Trust in the LORD with all your heart, And lean not on your own understanding;"

  • Psalm 51:10 - "Create in me a clean heart, O God, And renew a steadfast spirit within me."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • There is a direct contrast in the text between physical height and spiritual pride. Edom lived high in the clefts of the rock, but their heart's pride went even higher, prompting God to bring them down.

  • "Saying in the heart" shows that pride speaks. Even if the arrogant person does not publicize their self-exaltation to others, God hears the internal dialogue of the leb.

  • The cure for a deceived leb is absolute surrender to the truth of God’s Word, which cuts through self-delusion.
