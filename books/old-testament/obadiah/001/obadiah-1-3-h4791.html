MAROM - ( marom , Strong's H4791 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word marom refers to a high place, height, or elevation, carrying both literal physical and deep figurative meanings. Physically, it denotes lofty geographic heights, such as mountain fortresses, steep cliffs, or the heavens. Metaphorically, it represents pride, self-exaltation, and social or spiritual loftiness. In Obadiah 1:3, marom describes Edom's seemingly impregnable mountain stronghold of Sela (Petra), showcasing how physical elevation can foster a false sense of security and spiritual pride, which ultimately invites divine judgment.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word marom derives from the root rum, which means to be high, active, or exalted. In the ancient Near East, physical height was synonymous with safety, power, and divine presence. Cities built on high hills or carved into steep rock faces, like Edom's capital, were incredibly difficult to conquer. This physical reality naturally lent itself to a psychological and spiritual metaphor: to be "high" of heart was to view oneself as invincible, independent of God, and superior to others. Thus, the word picture moves from a secure mountain peak to the haughty, self-exalted heart of a rebel.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term marom refers to height, elevation, or a lofty place. It can denote the literal heavens or sky, a physical mountain or high hill, or a high position of power and status. When used in a moral or spiritual sense, it describes haughtiness, arrogance, and pride. In the context of Obadiah 1:3, it functions as a noun describing the literal geographic height of Edom's dwellings ("whose habitation is high"), while simultaneously acting as a literary pivot to expose their internal arrogance.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Scripture utilizes marom in several distinct ways to convey different aspects of height:

  • The Celestial Heights: It represents the dwelling place of God, the highest heavens, emphasizing His supreme authority and transcendence over creation.

  • Physical Elevation: It describes literal mountains, fortresses, or high places of refuge that offer defense in warfare.

  • Arrogance and Pride: It is used adverbially or substantively to describe those who lift up their eyes or voices in defiance of God, or those who walk with a haughty attitude.

4. Theological Implications

The theological core of marom lies in the contrast between God's rightful height and humanity's usurped height. God is the truly High One, dwelling in the heights of holiness. When human beings attempt to establish their own "high place" of security or pride, they set themselves up as rivals to the Creator. Physical height (like Edom's cliffs) can deceive the human heart into believing it is beyond the reach of divine accountability. However, Scripture repeatedly demonstrates that God brings down the high and lofty to show that He alone is exalted.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:3, marom is used to describe the dwelling of Edom (the descendants of Esau). Edom lived in the rugged, mountainous territory of Mount Seir, with cities like Sela carved directly into high sandstone cliffs. This literal physical height made them feel completely secure from enemy invasions, leading them to ask, "Who will bring me down to the ground?" Obadiah uses marom to expose how their physical topography deformed their theology. Their physical elevation bred a spiritual pride. God responds by warning that even if they make their nest as high as the eagle, He will bring them down.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Psalm 18:16 - "He sent from above [marom], He took me; He drew me out of many waters."

  • Isaiah 24:21 - "It shall come to pass in that day That the LORD will punish on high [marom] the host of exalted ones, And on the earth the kings of the earth."

  • Isaiah 40:26 - "Lift up your eyes on high [marom], And see who has created these things..."

  • Isaiah 57:15 - "For thus says the High and Lofty One Who inhabits eternity, whose name is Holy: 'I dwell in the high [marom] and holy place, With him who has a contrite and humble spirit...'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The irony of marom in Obadiah is juxtaposed with the verb yarad (to bring down) in the very same verse ("Who will bring me down to the ground?"). Edom believed their marom (height) made them immune to yarad (descent). This reveals a universal human struggle: we often turn God's physical blessings and natural protections into sources of self-reliance. Any "high place" we construct—whether it is financial wealth, intellectual achievement, or social status—can become a spiritual trap that blinds us to our radical dependence on God.
