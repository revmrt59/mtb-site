GROUND - ( erets , Strong's H776 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word erets most commonly refers to the earth, a specific land, or the ground. In the context of Obadiah 1:3, it is translated as "ground" and represents the ultimate destination of human pride when confronted by divine judgment. Edom nested itself high in the rocky fortresses of Sela, boasting in its safety and asking who could bring them down. God's answer is that they will be brought low, all the way to the erets—the common, vulnerable ground—shattering their illusion of untouchable security and demonstrating that no earthly heights can withstand the hand of the Almighty.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The physical geography of Edom is central to the word picture here. Edom occupied the rugged mountainous territory of Mount Seir, south of the Dead Sea. Their principal stronghold was Sela (later known as Petra), a city literally carved into high, towering cliffs of red sandstone. The only access to this fortress was through narrow, winding gorges that could easily be defended by a handful of men. This gave the Edomites an incredible sense of military invincibility. They looked down on everyone else, believing they were physically and strategically untouchable. To be brought down to the erets (ground) meant a complete dismantling of this geographical advantage, forcing them down from their lofty, sovereign heights to the common, vulnerable dirt of defeat.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun erets is one of the most frequent words in the Old Testament, occurring over 2,500 times. Its primary semantic range includes:

• The earth as a whole, often contrasted with the heavens.

• A specific land, country, or territory occupied by a nation.

• The physical soil, dirt, or ground.

In Obadiah 1:3, the word carries the third nuance. It functions as the absolute bottom of the spatial spectrum, acting as the direct physical and symbolic opposite to Edom's "high" habitation in the clefts of the rock.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, erets is used to create vivid spatial contrasts:

• The Cosmic Contrast: God sits above the circle of the earth (erets), and its inhabitants are like grasshoppers.

• The Posture of Submission: Falling prostrate to the erets is the universal ancient gesture of worship, fear, or total capitulation before a higher power.

• The Levelling of Pride: When God humbles a proud nation, He does not merely lower them slightly; He casts them down to the erets, bringing their lofty structures and high-minded ambitions into direct contact with the dust.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, the use of erets in Obadiah exposes the futility of human self-exaltation.

• God’s Universal Domain: No earthly height can provide sanctuary from God’s judgment. Edom built high, but God is the Creator of both the heavens and the erets.

• The Law of Spiritual Gravity: Scripture consistently reveals that those who exalt themselves will be brought low. Edom’s pride caused them to forget that they were mere creatures of the earth, subject to the Creator.

• The Illusion of Autonomy: Edom believed their high dwelling made them independent of God and immune to human threats. God’s promise to bring them down to the ground is a reminder that all human security apart from Him is a delusion.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:3, Edom asks a arrogant rhetorical question: "Who will bring me down to the ground?" This was not just military confidence; it was spiritual defiance. They believed their physical elevation translated to absolute sovereignty. God answers this challenge directly in the next verse: "Though you ascend as high as the eagle, and though you set your nest among the stars, from there I will bring you down." The "ground" (erets) is the place of humiliation, defeat, and historical obscurity. By bringing them to the erets, God demonstrates that their lofty fortresses were nothing more than a trap, and that the ground they disdained would become the place of their ultimate subjugation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Ezekiel 28:17 - "Your heart was lifted up because of your beauty; You corrupted your wisdom for the sake of your splendor; I cast you to the ground (erets), I laid you before kings, That they might gaze at you."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Historical fulfillment beautifully illustrates this passage. Despite Sela’s seemingly impregnable defenses, the Nabataeans conquered and dispossessed the Edomites in the late sixth and early fifth centuries BC. Edom was forced out of their mountain strongholds and down into the southern Judean desert (later known as Idumea). They were literally brought down to the erets—losing their high mountain kingdom forever and eventually disappearing from history altogether after the destruction of Jerusalem in AD 70.
