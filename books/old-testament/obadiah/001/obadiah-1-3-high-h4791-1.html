HIGH - (mârôwm, Strong's H4791)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun marom denotes a high place, altitude, elevated location, or exalted position, referring physically to lofty mountain fortresses and cliffs, or cosmically to the heavenly dwelling of God. In Obadiah 1:3, marom describes the inaccessible mountain habitations of Edom, which fostered a false sense of security and self-exaltation. By employing marom, the prophet exposes how Edom's physical height deceived them into believing they were immune to downfall, setting up the divine announcement that Yahweh alone reigns from the ultimate height and will bring down every proud stronghold.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The noun marom is derived from the verbal root rum, which carries the primary sense of being high, elevated, or exalted. In the ancient Near East, topographically elevated terrain was highly valued for military defense and administrative prestige. Natural rock formations, steep ridges, and mountain strongholds offered significant tactical advantages against invading forces.

In the historical setting of the prophecy of Obadiah, the nation of Edom occupied the rugged mountain region of Mount Seir, situated south of the Dead Sea. Their territory was characterized by towering sandstone cliffs, narrow gorges, and high plateau strongholds such as Sela. This physical environment allowed Edom to thrive commercially while remaining naturally fortified against adversaries. The term marom captures both the literal geographical altitude of Edom's cliffside habitations and the psychological posture of invulnerability that this terrain cultivated among its inhabitants.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, marom signifies a "high place," "height," or "exalted position." Its semantic range encompasses several distinct categories of usage across Old Testament literature:

  • Physical heights: Elevated natural features such as cliff tops, mountain peaks, and rugged ridges.

  • Human fortresses and elevated seats: Royal thrones, fortified battlements, or high dwelling places constructed for defense.

  • The transcendent realm of God: The supreme heavenly dwelling place from which Yahweh exercises universal sovereignty and executes justice.

In Obadiah 1:3, the word appears as a common masculine singular noun in the construct state, modifying the noun for "habitation" or "dwelling." In this grammatical construction, marom functions to qualify Edom's settlement as a "dwelling of height" or "lofty habitation," identifying both the literal altitude of their regional stronghold and the exalted status in which they placed their trust.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament Scriptures, marom develops a clear contrast between earthly heights inhabited by humanity and the absolute divine height inhabited by God.

Earthly usages often highlight human attempts to achieve safety, fame, or self-preservation through elevated physical or social positions. When human beings rely on their own marom, the biblical text frequently frames such reliance as vanity or pride. Fortresses situated in high places cannot offer permanent protection when brought under divine evaluation.

In contrast, divine usages portray Yahweh as uniquely residing on marom. God's height is not merely spatial but ontological and moral; He is seated above all creation, governing with righteousness. When God acts from the marom, He judges arrogance, delivers the humble, and establishes His eternal kingdom. Thus, the term frequently carries a polemical nuance: human claims to height are temporary and derivative, whereas divine height is absolute and unshakeable.

4. Theological Implications

The theological significance of marom in prophetic literature centers on the folly of creaturely pride versus the reality of divine sovereignty.

First, physical and circumstantial security easily becomes a spiritual trap. When individuals or nations possess natural advantages, strategic wealth, or high status, they are prone to mistake temporary created elevation for ultimate invulnerability. Edom’s high location fostered an illusion of self-sufficiency, leading them to boast against their neighbors and ignore accountability to God.

Second, God’s true height exposes all false human heights. Scripture consistently asserts that Yahweh alone occupies the supreme marom. Every earthly elevation—whether a physical mountain, an imperial fortress, or personal pride—remains completely subject to His command. The God who dwells on high possesses absolute authority to bring down those who exalt themselves above His truth.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:3, marom functions as a central element in the prophet's indictment of Edom. The text declares: "The pride of your heart has deceived you, You who dwell in the clefts of the rock, Whose habitation is high (marom); You who say in your heart, ‘Who will bring me down to the ground?’"

The immediate context establishes a sharp contrast between human self-assurance and divine reality. The construct form of marom directly describes Edom's habitations. Edom looked down from their cliffside strongholds and concluded that no earthly power could reach them. Their geographic height fed their heart's pride, prompting their arrogant assertion that they could never be brought down.

In the subsequent verse (Obadiah 1:4), Yahweh directly refutes their boast: "Though you soar aloft like the eagle, though your nest is set among the stars, from there I will bring you down." The deliberate interplay between Edom's marom (height) and Yahweh bringing them "down to the ground" underscores the futility of human self-exaltation. Edom believed their lofty geography rendered them sovereign, but Obadiah uses marom to show that their physical elevation served only to prepare them for a more devastating fall under Yahweh's judgment.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Habakkuk 2:9 — "Woe to him who covets evil gain for his house, that he may set his nest on high (marom), that he may be delivered from the power of disaster!"

  • Isaiah 33:16 — "He will dwell on high (marom); his place of defense will be the fortress of rocks; bread will be given him, his water will be sure."

  • Jeremiah 17:12 — "A glorious high (marom) throne from the beginning is the place of our sanctuary."

  • Psalms 92:8 — "But You, LORD, are on high (marom) forevermore."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between Edom's physical height and their sudden overthrow highlights a recurring prophetic theme: physical geography in Scripture often serves as a backdrop for spiritual realities. Edom's capital region was situated hundreds of feet above the surrounding terrain, accessible only through steep, narrow canyons. While ancient military strategists considered such topography impregnable, the prophetic word demonstrates that no physical height can shield a people from divine justice when they abandon righteousness and exalt themselves against God.
