LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh (LORD) is the personal, covenant-keeping name of the God of Israel, signifying His eternal self-existence, sovereign supremacy, and faithfulness to His people. In Obadiah 1:4, this name stands as a dramatic guarantee of judgment against the proud nation of Edom. Though Edom deemed herself safe in her high, rocky fortresses—as if nested among the stars—the absolute sovereignty of Yahweh ensures that no earthly power can escape His decree. It reminds the believer that the self-existent Creator of the universe is actively involved in history to bring down the proud and rescue His covenant people.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The name Yahweh is the sacred Tetragrammaton (YHWH), the most significant and frequent name for God in the Old Testament. Historically, the Jews held this name in such high reverence that they avoided pronouncing it aloud, substituting it with Adonai ("Lord"). When translating the Old Testament into English, translators signaled this specific name by printing "LORD" in all capital letters.

The name is deeply connected to Exodus 3:14, where God reveals Himself to Moses as "I AM WHO I AM." Structurally, the name is a third-person singular form of the Hebrew verb hayah ("to be"). It presents a word picture of a God who is entirely self-sufficient, uncreated, and independent of His creation, yet intimately present and active within human history.

In the historical background of Obadiah, the Edomites (descendants of Esau) lived in Mount Seir, a rugged mountainous region characterized by high cliffs, narrow gorges, and naturally fortified rock structures. This geography bred a spirit of fierce pride and self-reliance. Edom believed their mountain home made them completely secure from invaders. By introducing Yahweh as the one who speaks judgment over them, Obadiah contrasts the temporary, physical high places of Edom with the eternal, spiritual reality of the God who rules over all creation.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name Yahweh designates the self-existent, eternal, supreme God of Israel. It denotes Him as the One who possesses life in Himself, who is uncaused, unchanged, and sovereign over all creation. As the covenant name of God, it expresses His permanent availability, holiness, and absolute commitment to His promises.

The semantic range of the root verb implies:

• Active Existence: He is the God who is dynamically present, not a distant, static force.

• Causation of Being: He is the Creator and Sustainer of everything that exists.

• Unchanging Faithfulness: He remains consistent in His character, covenant commitments, and moral standards across generations.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, Yahweh is used over 6,800 times. It is specifically the name associated with God's redemptive actions, His holiness, and His relational covenant with Israel.

In prophetic literature, the name is frequently paired with ne'um ("says" or "declares"), forming the phrase "says the LORD" (ne'um Yahweh). This is a technical prophetic formula indicating an oracle of absolute authority, acting like a royal seal on a decree.

The word pictures associated with Yahweh emphasize His supreme authority over nature and nations:

• He is the Creator who stretches out the heavens and lays the foundations of the earth.

• He is the divine Warrior who executes justice on behalf of the oppressed.

• He is the Shepherd of Israel, guiding and protecting His flock.

• In the context of Obadiah, He is the supreme gravity that pulls down those who falsely believe they have risen above His reach.

### 4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of Yahweh in Obadiah 1:4 centers on His sovereignty and covenant faithfulness:

• The Limit of Human Pride: Edom's pride led them to believe they could escape judgment. By declaring "says the LORD," the text asserts that human security systems, high fortresses, and technological or military achievements are meaningless before the Creator.

• Covenant Vindicated: Edom had betrayed and plundered their brother nation, Jacob (Judah), during their time of distress. Because Yahweh is Israel's covenant God, an attack on His people is an affront to His name. The name Yahweh guarantees that He will act as the Goel (Redeemer/Avenger) for His people, fulfilling His covenant promise to bless those who bless Abraham's seed and curse those who curse them (Genesis 12:3).

• Absolute Control over History: Yahweh is not a localized deity bound to a specific territory. He has the authority to reach into the highest cliffs of Edom and bring them to ruin, proving that He rules over all nations.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:4, the name Yahweh functions as the ultimate authority that shatters Edom's delusion of safety.

Edom asked arrogantly in verse 3, "Who will bring me down to the ground?" They looked at their high altitude and deemed themselves untouchable. Verse 4 answers this question directly and terrifyingly: "From there I will bring you down,” says the LORD."

The placement of Yahweh at the end of this sentence acts as the signature of absolute certainty. The pronoun "I" in "I will bring you down" refers to Yahweh. Edom was not merely facing a superior military alliance; they were facing the self-existent Creator of the stars among which they fancied their nest to be. The contrast is sharp: Edom's loftiness is a delusion; Yahweh's supremacy is reality.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 15:6 - "And he believed in the LORD, and He accounted it to him for righteousness."

• Exodus 3:15 - "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

• Proverbs 16:5 - "Everyone proud in heart is an abomination to the LORD; Though they join forces, none will go unpunished."

• Isaiah 42:8 - "I am the LORD, that is My name; And My glory I will not give to another, Nor My praise to carved images."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

For the Christian pastor and teacher, the study of Yahweh in Obadiah provides a powerful dual application:

• A Warning to the Self-Sufficient: Modern culture encourages people to build their own "nests among the stars"—whether through financial wealth, academic prestige, or digital influence—believing they are secure from life's storms and divine accountability. The name Yahweh reminds us that all human glory is temporary, and true security is found only in submission to Him.

• Comfort for the Afflicted: When believers face oppression, injustice, or betrayal, they can find refuge in the name Yahweh. It assures us that the covenant-keeping God sees, cares, and will ultimately right every wrong. Our hope does not lie in our own strength to fight back, but in the faithful character of the LORD who brings down the proud and exalts the humble.
