DOWN - ( yarad , Strong's H3381 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yarad literally means to descend or go down, but when used in its causative stem, it means to actively bring down, cast down, or humble. In the context of Obadiah's prophecy against Edom, this word represents the absolute sovereignty of God over human arrogance. The Edomites lived in high mountain fortresses and pridefully asked who could pull them to the earth. God’s response is a swift decree of judgment, declaring that no matter how high they climb, His hand will bring them down. This term serves as a stark biblical reminder that God actively opposes self-exaltation and will inevitably humble those who refuse to submit to His lordship.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root verb yarad is a fundamental Hebrew term describing motion from a higher elevation to a lower one. Geographically, it depicts descending from rugged mountains, traveling from highland regions like Jerusalem or Canaan down to lowlands like Egypt or the Jordan Valley, or sinking into the depths of the sea. Culturally and militarily, high ground represented safety, defensive superiority, and impregnable strength. In contrast, descending or being cast down symbolized vulnerability, military defeat, and submission. To go down also carries the ultimate physical gravity of descending into the grave or the underworld.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb yarad means to descend, go down, or come down. In its basic active form (the Qal stem), it describes routine physical descent, such as stepping down from a chariot, walking down a mountain, or water flowing downward. In its causative form (the Hiphil stem), which is the specific construction used in Obadiah 1:4, it means to cause to descend, bring down, drag down, pull down, or cast down. It carries the force of a powerful external agent actively reducing someone or something from a position of height, authority, and security to a state of lowliness and defeat.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• yarad is employed across the Old Testament in a variety of vivid contexts:

• Natural Descent: The falling of rain, snow, or dew from the heavens to fertilize the earth.

• Bodily Expressions: Tears flowing down a face in deep lamentation or grief.

• Spatial and Territorial Humiliation: The collapse and bringing down of heavily fortified city walls that once stood tall.

• Existential and Final Descent: Going down to the Pit, the grave, or Sheol, depicting the ultimate end of human strength and the onset of death.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, yarad is a key term in the biblical theology of divine judgment and the reversal of human pride. Yahweh is the sovereign God who "comes down" to act in human history—either in mercy to deliver His people, as at the burning bush, or in judgment to disrupt human self-deification, as at the Tower of Babel. The term exposes the futility of human self-exaltation. When created beings attempt to ascend and claim autonomy, God exercises His sovereign prerogative to bring them down. True elevation comes only from God, while self-assertion guarantees a divine descent.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:4, yarad is addressed to the nation of Edom. Nestled in the high, red sandstone cliffs of Mount Seir, the Edomites believed their physical geography made them completely safe from conquest. This geographical height bred an intense spiritual arrogance, prompting them to boast, "Who will bring me down to the ground?" God answers this challenge with absolute certainty. Even if they soar like eagles or build their nests among the stars, God promises, "From there I will bring you down." Here, yarad demonstrates that no human stronghold, natural defense, or political alliance can protect a nation when the Lord decrees its humbling. The very height they trusted in becomes the measure of their fall.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 11:5: "But the LORD came down to see the city and the tower which the sons of men had built."

• Isaiah 14:11: "Your pomp is brought down to Sheol, And the noise of your stringed instruments; The maggot is spread under you, And worms cover you."

• Isaiah 14:15: "Yet you shall be brought down to Sheol, To the lowest depths of the Pit."

• Jeremiah 49:16: "Your fierceness has deceived you, The pride of your heart, O you who dwell in the clefts of the rock, Who hold the height of the hill! Though you make your nest as high as the eagle, I will bring you down from there," says the LORD."

• Amos 9:2: "Though they dig into Sheol, From there My hand shall take them; Though they climb up to heaven, From there I will bring them down;"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The literary structure of Obadiah highlights a profound irony. In verse 3, the proud Edomites ask, "Who will bring me down?" using the verb yarad. They believed the answer was "no one." In verse 4, God answers them with the exact same verb: "I will bring you down." The very word they used to express their untouchable security is turned back upon them as the sentence of their ruin. The sovereign Lord is the answer to their defiant question. This teaches that the heights of human security are illusions; true safety is found not in ascending to elevated self-reliance, but in bowing down before the Lord of hosts.
