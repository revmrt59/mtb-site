STARS - ( kowkab , Strong's H3556 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word kowkab refers to a celestial star or stargroup, representing the luminous, distant, and elevated host of the night sky. In the ancient world, stars symbolized supreme height, permanence, and the boundary of the divine realm. In Obadiah 1:4, God addresses the nation of Edom, which had pridefully nestled itself in the high, rugged rock clefts of Petra, believing it was physically and strategically untouchable. By using kowkab metaphorically, the prophet delivers a devastating critique of human arrogance: even if Edom could ascend to the absolute heights of the cosmos and build its nest among the stars, God's sovereign hand would still reach up and bring them down.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun kowkab derives from an unused root meaning to roll or to shine, evoking the image of round, brilliant, and blazing spheres of light set in the dome of heaven. To the ancient observer, the stars were the highest visible points of creation, completely out of human reach, untouched by earthly conflicts, and firmly under the control of the Creator. To "nest among the stars" is a vivid word picture of ultimate safety, self-exaltation, and absolute independence. Edom’s mountain strongholds in Mount Seir felt as secure as the stars, leading to a false sense of invincibility that bypassed the fear of God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary definition of kowkab is a star. It is used in several distinct ways across the Old Testament scriptures:

  • Literal Stars: The physical, shining heavenly bodies created on the fourth day of creation to rule the night and serve as signs and seasons.

  • Metaphor for Multitude: A standard of measureless abundance, particularly in God's covenant promises to multiply Abraham's descendants.

  • Metaphor for Arrogance: A symbol of supreme height, representing the pride of rulers and empires who seek to elevate themselves above God's authority.

  • Messianic Symbol: A representation of royal splendor, authority, and divine kingship.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament employs kowkab to paint powerful theological pictures:

  • The Creator's Signature: The stars are not independent deities, as Israel's neighbors believed, but created objects that God counts, names, and marshals by His personal power.

  • The Scepter and the Star: In messianic prophecy, a "Star" represents the coming King who will arise out of Jacob to defeat Israel's enemies, linking the luminosity of a star with the glory of the coming Messiah.

  • The Fall of the Arrogant: In passages dealing with judgment on prideful rulers (such as the King of Babylon or the nation of Edom), attempting to scale the heights of the stars is the ultimate expression of rebellion, mimicking the original pride of Satan.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of kowkab in prophetic literature centers on the absolute sovereignty of God over human pride. Stars represent the ultimate limit of human achievement and security. When mankind tries to colonize the "stars" of self-sufficiency, wealth, power, or intellect to escape divine accountability, they commit the sin of Babel. The theological reality is that God is the Creator of the stars; therefore, no height is too high for His judgment, and no human fortress is out of His reach. True security is not found in ascending to the stars, but in humbling oneself before the One who made them.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:4, kowkab is contrasted directly with the "eagle" (nesher). The eagle represents the highest-flying bird, nesting on the sheerest, most inaccessible mountain peaks. Edom's physical geography matched this—their capital was carved into high, red-sandstone canyon walls. But God pushes the hyperbole further: even if they could go beyond the highest cliffs of the eagle and nest among the kowkab—the very stars of heaven—they would not be safe from His decree. The contrast highlights the utter futility of Edom's self-reliance. Their mountain fortress, which seemed as high as the stars to human eyes, was nothing but low ground to the Almighty.

  • Isaiah 14:13 - "For you have said in your heart: 'I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above the stars of God; I will also sit on the mount of the congregation On the farthest sides of the north;'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

For the contemporary believer and pastor, the "stars" represent the modern-day fortresses of security that people build to insulate themselves from vulnerability and from God. These "stars" can take the form of financial wealth, political power, professional achievements, or technological advancements. Just as Edom looked at its high cliffs and felt secure, modern culture looks at its achievements and feels self-sufficient. Obadiah's warning reminds us that any security built apart from God is an illusion. The heights we scale to escape God only make the eventual fall more devastating.
