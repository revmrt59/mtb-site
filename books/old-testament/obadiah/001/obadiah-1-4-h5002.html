SAYS - ( ne'um , Strong's H5002 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun ne'um represents a solemn, authoritative, and prophetic declaration. Translating to "says" or "declares" in English, it is almost exclusively used in the Old Testament to mark the direct, inspired speech of God (ne'um YHWH). Unlike common verbs for speaking, ne'um carries the weight of an oracle, signaling to the hearer that the message originates from the sovereign throne of God and is guaranteed to come to pass. In Obadiah 1:4, it seals the absolute certainty of Edom’s impending judgment despite their proud self-security.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word ne'um derives from a root that suggests whispering, sighing, or groaning, originally carrying the nuance of an intimate whisper or a deep, solemn utterance. In its ancient Near Eastern context, it developed into a technical term for a divine oracle. It is not the standard Hebrew verb for speaking (amar or dabar), but a passive noun denoting "that which is whispered" or "revealed" by a deity to a prophet. Thus, the word picture is one of divine disclosure—God whispering His sovereign decrees into the ear of His messenger to be broadcast to the world with absolute authority.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Ne'um refers to an utterance, declaration, or oracle. It acts as a formal sign-off or introductory stamp of divine authority. The lexical meaning denotes a solemn declaration that cannot be annulled. It is used almost exclusively in the genitive construction ne'um YHWH ("declaration of Yahweh" or "says the LORD"). This phrase functions as a divine seal of authenticity, emphasizing that the words spoken are not the prophet’s own human opinions or predictions, but are the very breath and decree of the living God.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Ne'um occurs over 370 times in the Hebrew Bible, primarily in the prophetic books.

• It acts as a divine punctuation mark, often placed at the end of a prophetic announcement to seal the message, or in the middle of a sentence to remind the listener of the speaker's identity.

• It conveys a sense of inescapable reality; when God utters a ne'um, the decree is legally binding in the cosmic court.

• The word pictures a royal decree from a king's throne room. Just as an ancient monarch's seal finalized an edict, ne'um finalizes a divine prophetic word.

4. Theological Implications

• Divine Authority: The use of ne'um establishes the absolute lordship of Yahweh. He is the master of history whose word shapes reality.

• Infallibility of Prophecy: Because the declaration is a ne'um, its fulfillment is guaranteed. Human pride, military strength, or geographical advantages cannot thwart it.

• The Nature of Revelation: It demonstrates that biblical prophecy is verbally inspired. God speaks specific words to His prophets, transforming them into His mouthpieces.

• Judgment and Mercy: It is used both to seal terrifying judgments and to guarantee beautiful promises of covenant restoration, showing that both God's wrath and His grace are unshakeable.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Obadiah 1:4, the proud nation of Edom believed they were physically and strategically impregnable, dwelling high in the clefts of the rock and nesting "among the stars" like an eagle.

• God challenges this hubris directly: "From there I will bring you down."

• The verse ends with the abrupt and heavy stamp: ne'um YHWH ("says the LORD").

• This placement is highly strategic. It serves as a sudden, sobering reality check. Edom may speak boastfully of their security, but their word is nothing compared to the sovereign ne'um of Yahweh. The divine decree instantly nullifies Edom's security, turning their highest heights into the site of their deepest fall.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 22:16: "and said: 'By Myself I have sworn, says the LORD [ne'um], because you have done this thing, and have not withheld your son, your only son...'"

• Psalm 110:1: "The LORD said [ne'um] to my Lord: 'Sit at My right hand, Till I make Your enemies Your footstool.'"

• Jeremiah 23:24: "'Can anyone hide himself in secret places, So I shall not see him?' says the LORD [ne'um]; 'Do I not fill heaven and earth?' says the LORD [ne'um]."

• Ezekiel 18:32: "'For I have no pleasure in the death of one who dies,' says the Lord GOD [ne'um]. 'Therefore turn and live!'"

• Zechariah 4:6: "So he answered and said to me: 'This is the word of the LORD to Zerubbabel: "Not by might nor by power, but by My Spirit," says the LORD [ne'um] of hosts.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• On rare occasions, ne'um is used of human speakers, but always in a highly elevated, oracle-like context. For example, in Numbers 24:3–4, it is used of Balaam ("the utterance of Balaam"), and in 2 Samuel 23:1, of David ("the utterance of David... the sweet psalmist of Israel"). This demonstrates that even when used of humans, it refers to inspired, visionary, or authoritative speech rather than everyday conversation.

• The Septuagint (LXX) frequently translates ne'um YHWH using legei kyrios ("says the Lord"), a phrase which the New Testament writers carry over to assert the divine authority of Old Testament scriptures (e.g., Romans 12:19, 2 Corinthians 6:17).
