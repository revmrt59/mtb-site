SET - ( śûm , Strong's 7760 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb śûm primarily means "to put, place, set, or establish." In Obadiah 1:4, it describes Edom’s arrogant attempt to secure their dwelling place in the highest, most inaccessible mountain cliffs, metaphorically compared to setting a nest among the stars. It highlights the depth of human self-sufficiency and the illusion of security apart from God. Even the most carefully established and fortified human positions are easily brought down by the sovereign hand of Yahweh.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The verb śûm is a very common Hebrew word occurring over 580 times in the Old Testament. Its concrete, physical background is to physically place an object in a specific location with deliberate action, intention, and establishment. To "set" something is to give it a designated position of permanence or function. In the ancient Near East, setting one's dwelling or "nest" high in the mountains was a literal defense strategy. The Edomites lived in the rugged, red-rock territory of Mount Seir (modern-day southern Jordan). Its steep canyons and high-altitude sandstone cliffs made their capital city, Sela (later Petra), virtually impregnable to ancient armies. This formidable physical landscape shaped their psychological sense of absolute invincibility, leading them to believe they had successfully established a permanent stronghold beyond the reach of any enemy.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The semantic range of śûm is broad but consistently denotes an act of placement or determination. It includes:

• To put, place, or lay down a physical object.

• To establish, ordain, or appoint a person to a position or status (such as appointing a king or leader).

• To make, constitute, or transform someone or something into a specific state (such as turning a city into ruins).

• To fix, determine, or direct one’s attention or mind toward something.

In Obadiah 1:4, the verb functions in its active, physical-locative sense of establishing or positioning an object (specifically a nest) in a highly secure, elevated location.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

The use of śûm in Obadiah 1:4 creates a powerful contrast between human effort and divine sovereignty:

• The Eagle's Nest: The verb is paired with "nest" (qēn). In biblical imagery, the eagle represents the apex predator of the skies, building its nest on inaccessible crags to protect its young from land-bound predators. By setting (śûm) their nest high, Edom is acting out of a desire for absolute immunity from danger.

• The Cosmic Scale: Edom's pride is so vast that the prophet projects their action from the high cliffs of Seir into the heavens: "And though you set your nest among the stars." This hyperbolic image mirrors the cosmic arrogance of the King of Babylon in Isaiah 14:13 ("I will exalt my throne above the stars of God").

• The Divine Counter-Action: While Edom exerts deliberate effort to "set" their secure home, Yahweh responds with effortless authority. The human attempt to "set" is immediately countered by the divine promise to "bring you down."

### 4. Theological Implications

Several critical theological truths emerge from the use of śûm in this context:

• The Illusion of Human Autonomy: Humans possess the capacity to build, secure, and "set" their lives in seemingly unshakeable ways—whether through wealth, military might, or intellect. However, any security established apart from God is a deceptive illusion.

• The Reach of Divine Sovereignty: There is no place in creation outside the jurisdiction of God. Even if humanity could literally "set" its habitation among the stars, God's sovereign hand remains supreme.

• The Principle of Divine Reversal: God actively opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble. Those who attempt to "set" themselves above others in self-exaltation will experience a sudden, divinely initiated descent.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Obadiah is a prophetic indictment against the nation of Edom for their pride and their betrayal of their brother-nation, Judah, during Jerusalem’s destruction. In Obadiah 1:3, the prophet notes: "The pride of your heart has deceived you, you who dwell in the clefts of the rock... Who says in her heart, 'Who will bring me down to the ground?'"

Obadiah 1:4 directly answers this boast. The placement of śûm emphasizes that Edom's security was not an accident; it was a carefully constructed, intentional endeavor. They had deliberately set their lives, their values, and their defenses in defiance of God. The nuance of the word reveals that God does not merely judge passive pride, but active, calculated self-deification.

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

A helpful comparison can be made between śûm and other Hebrew verbs for "placing" or "setting," such as shîth (to put, lay, or make) and yasad (to found or lay a foundation). While shîth often emphasizes the general act of putting something somewhere, śûm carries a stronger sense of deliberate arrangement, designation, and determination. When God śûm (sets) order in the cosmos, it stands forever. When man attempts to śûm (set) his own destiny in opposition to God, it is an exercise in futility.
