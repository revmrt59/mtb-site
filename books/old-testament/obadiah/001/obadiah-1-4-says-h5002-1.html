SAYS - (nᵉʼum, Strong's H5002)

Summary:

The Hebrew word neʾum primarily means an utterance, declaration, or oracle. While frequently translated into English as a verb ("says" or "declares"), it is actually a noun that functions as a formal marker of speech, almost exclusively used to identify a divine decree. In Obadiah 1:4, this word serves as the authoritative signature of Yahweh, sealing the absolute certainty of Edom’s impending judgment despite their perceived invincibility.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the ancient Near East, the authority of a message was entirely dependent upon the authority of the one who sent it. In the Hebrew Scriptures, neʾum functions as a specialized prophetic term used to authenticate a message. It is distinct from the common Hebrew verb for speaking (amar). Instead of merely describing the act of talking, neʾum acts as a formal, often legal or royal, declaration. It is the prophetic equivalent of a king pressing his signet ring into the wax of a royal decree, verifying that the words belong to the sovereign and carry his full backing.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic lexical meaning of neʾum is "utterance," "declaration," or "oracle." Because English translations prioritize readability, it is most often rendered as "says" or "declares" (as in "says the LORD").

Grammatically, the authoritative morphology for this occurrence identifies it as a masculine singular noun in the construct state. The construct state in Hebrew means the noun is grammatically bound to the word that follows it, indicating a relationship of possession or close association. Therefore, the literal rendering is "the utterance of" or "the declaration of." When followed by the divine name, it forms the binding phrase "the utterance of Yahweh."

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

The biblical usage of neʾum is overwhelmingly concentrated in the prophetic books. It is frequently inserted at the end of a sentence, or sometimes parenthetically in the middle of a thought, to continually remind the hearer of the source of the message.

While it is occasionally used to describe the formal oracle of a human being who is speaking under spiritual influence, it is predominantly reserved for the speech of God. It marks a statement as a fixed, unalterable decree. When a prophet uses neʾum, he is stepping out of the way to let the weight of God's own identity validate the truth of the words being spoken.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, neʾum anchors the certainty of biblical prophecy in the character and sovereignty of God. When God makes a promise of salvation or a threat of judgment, the fulfillment of that word does not depend on human circumstances, shifting geopolitical powers, or natural probabilities. By appending "the utterance of the LORD," the text establishes that the Creator of the universe has staked His own reputation on the outcome. It is a profound reminder of the doctrine of divine veracity—God cannot lie, and His declared purposes will inevitably come to pass.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:4, the prophet addresses the nation of Edom, a people who lived in the high, rocky cliffs of Mount Seir. Because of their geography, the Edomites were filled with pride, believing their capital was an impenetrable fortress. God addresses this arrogance directly: "Though you ascend as high as the eagle, And though you set your nest among the stars, From there I will bring you down."

The verse concludes with neʾum—"says the LORD." In this context, the word functions as a devastating reality check against Edom's pride. Edom's security was based on human elevation and natural defenses. By appending this formal divine signature, God shatters their illusion of safety. The construct state binds this decree directly to Yahweh; it is "the utterance of the LORD." It tells Edom, and the reader, that the promise to bring them down is not the wishful thinking of an oppressed Israelite prophet, but the binding, inescapable decree of the Sovereign God. No altitude or fortification can protect a nation when the Almighty has issued a formal declaration of their fall.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Jeremiah 23:29 – "Is not My word like a fire? says the LORD, And like a hammer that breaks the rock in pieces?" Here, the word seals God's declaration about the devastating, purifying power of His own truth against false prophets.

  • Haggai 2:8 – "The silver is Mine, and the gold is Mine, says the LORD of hosts." The term functions as a divine claim of absolute ownership and authority over all earthly resources.

  • Ezekiel 36:23 – "And the nations shall know that I am the LORD, says the Lord GOD, when I am hallowed in you before their eyes." The word guarantees the certainty of God's future restorative work and the vindication of His holy name.

  • Numbers 24:3 – "Then he took up his oracle and said: 'The utterance of Balaam the son of Beor, The utterance of the man whose eyes are opened...'" This is a rare instance where the word is used for a human speaker, marking the formal, inspired nature of Balaam's prophetic oracle.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The placement of neʾum at the very end of Obadiah 1:4 is rhetorically powerful. In Hebrew poetry and prophetic literature, delaying the divine signature until the end of a shocking or definitive statement forces the audience to first absorb the weight of the judgment ("I will bring you down") and then immediately reckon with the inescapable authority of the One who spoke it. It leaves no room for debate or appeal.
