SET - (sûwm, Strong's H7760)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb sum primary denotes the act of placing, setting, putting, or establishing something in a designated location or state. In Obadiah 1:4, the word describes Edom's arrogant attempt to position its dwelling in an impossibly exalted height ("among the stars"). It highlights human self-exaltation and presumed invulnerability, which Yahweh directly confronts and promises to overthrow.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The verb sum (sometimes rendered sim) is a common and versatile Old Testament root expressing spatial placement, functional appointment, or positional determination. In physical contexts, it refers to laying objects, setting boundaries, or positioning people. In relational or official contexts, it can describe appointing leaders, establishing laws, or bringing about a specific condition or state.

In the book of Obadiah, the prophet delivers a message of judgment against the nation of Edom. Geographically, Edom occupied the rugged, mountainous terrain south of the Dead Sea, characterized by steep sandstone cliffs and natural fortresses such as Sela and Petra. This inaccessible topography fostered a deep sense of national security and pride. In Obadiah 1:4, the prophet uses imagery of an eagle building its nest on high cliffs, extending the metaphor to an extreme: Edom attempting to set its habitation among the stars.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, sum means "to put, place, set, establish, appoint, or make." Its primary semantic scope centers on deliberate arrangement or stationing. Depending on the context, the verb carries several distinct nuances:

  • Spatial placement: physically putting or laying an object in a location.

  • Functional designation: setting or appointing someone to an office, role, or purpose.

  • Resulting condition: causing an entity or place to become or be set in a particular state (e.g., making a land desolate).

  • Intentional focus: directing one's heart, eyes, or attention toward something.

In Obadiah 1:4, the verb emphasizes intentional self-placement—the deliberate action of positioning one's abode in a place of perceived absolute safety.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, sum appears across diverse genres, including narrative, law, wisdom, and prophecy. Its theological weight depends heavily on who is performing the action:

  • Human placement: When human beings are the subject, sum often describes daily tasks, administrative appointments, or, as in Obadiah, prideful attempts to secure their own destiny apart from God.

  • Divine placement: When God is the subject, sum expresses His sovereign control over creation, redemptive history, and the rise and fall of nations. God sets the boundaries of the seas, places humanity in purpose, establishes covenants, and sets judgment upon the proud.

The verb highlights that positioning is never ultimate when done by human hands; true authority over human placement belongs solely to Yahweh.

4. Theological Implications

Obadiah 1:4 uses sum to expose the folly of human pride and self-sufficiency. Edom believed that by setting its habitation in high, fortified places, it had secured itself beyond the reach of calamity or divine reckoning.

Theologically, this verse reflects the broader biblical theme of divine reversal regarding human pride. When creatures attempt to exalt themselves—seeking to set their throne or nest alongside the heavens—they trespass upon the unique majesty of God. Yahweh alone possesses the authority to exalt or abase. Human efforts to establish ultimate security through geography, military strength, or self-effort are exposed as illusions when confronted by the living God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:3–4, the prophet constructs a sharp contrast between Edom's self-assessment and Yahweh's sovereign decree. Verse 3 establishes the spiritual root of Edom's condition: "The pride of your heart has deceived you." Edom considered its mountain strongholds impenetrable, asking in self-assurance, "Who will bring me down to the ground?"

Verse 4 answers this rhetorical presumption. Even if Edom were to ascend as high as the eagle and explicitly set its nest among the stars, that self-achieved altitude provides no protection against divine judgment. The verb sum highlights Edom's active, deliberate effort to construct an unassailable life independent of God. The passage establishes that no matter how high human pride attempts to place itself, Yahweh's reach remains absolute: "from there I will bring you down, says the LORD." Edom's self-appointed height simply becomes the distance from which God casts them down.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The imagery of setting a nest on high appears elsewhere in Hebrew prophetic discourse to describe false security. In Habakkuk 2:9, a similar warning is pronounced against one who "sets his nest on high, that he may be delivered from the power of disaster."

By selecting sum in Obadiah 1:4, the text emphasizes the calculated effort involved in human self-fortification. Edom did not merely happen to occupy high ground; they actively relied upon their self-established positioning as a guarantee against calamity. The prophetic message dismantles this delusion, showing that human positioning is always subject to divine disposition.
