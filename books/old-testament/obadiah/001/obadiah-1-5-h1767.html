ENOUGH - ( day , Strong's H1767 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun day denotes sufficiency, abundance, or what is adequate to meet a need or desire. In everyday ancient Near Eastern life, it defined the boundaries of necessity—the point at which hunger is satisfied, a debt is paid, or a task is complete. In Obadiah 1:5, the word serves as a literary foil to expose the absolute, unrestrained devastation coming upon Edom. Unlike typical thieves who steal only until they have "enough" to satisfy their immediate greed, leaving the rest behind, God's judgment on Edom through their enemies will be total and unsparing, leaving absolutely nothing behind.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun day emerges from the practical, agricultural, and domestic realities of ancient Israel. It carries the concrete image of a measure or vessel filled to its proper limit. In a world of scarcity, knowing what constitutes "enough" was vital for survival, commerce, and social harmony. The term visually represents a line of demarcation: the boundary between deficit and excess. It is the point of satisfaction where one's appetite, need, or capacity is fully met. To go beyond day is to enter into waste or greed, while falling short of it is to remain in want.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun day signifies sufficiency, enough, plenty, or a required abundance. It is frequently paired with prepositions to express comparative or temporal relationships:

• Combined with the preposition ke (kedi), it means "according to the sufficiency of" or "as much as is needed."

• Combined with be (bedi), it means "for the sufficiency of" or "for what is enough."

• Combined with min (mide), it functions temporally as "from the frequency of" or "as often as."

At its core, the word expresses a quantitative measure that satisfies a specific demand, standard, or capacity.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

In the Old Testament, day appears in various contexts to illustrate physical and spiritual satisfaction:

• Provision and Sufficiency: It describes the physical abundance of food, resources, or space, such as the manna in the wilderness or the daily provisions for the temple.

• The Limit of Need: It establishes the legal and moral limits of collection, such as the offerings for the construction of the Tabernacle where the people brought "more than enough."

• Comparative Sufficiency: It is used to measure agricultural yield or the capacity of a nation to withstand an adversary.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, day reveals a God of precise and bountiful provision. God does not merely provide a bare minimum; He provides day—exactly what is sufficient, and often in abundance. However, the term also highlights human accountability and the limits of earthly security. When humanity attempts to define "enough" apart from God, they fall into greed or false security. In the prophetic literature, day becomes a tool to measure divine judgment. When God withdraws His hand, even the most self-sufficient nations find that their reserves are not "enough" to save them.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:5, day (appearing with a pronominal suffix as deyam, "their sufficiency" or "enough for them") is used in a rhetorical question to highlight the severity of Edom's impending ruin. Obadiah contrasts the incoming judgment with common human behaviors:

• Ordinary thieves and nighttime robbers operate under a principle of limitation; they steal only what they can carry or what is "enough" to satisfy their immediate desires, leaving the rest of the house intact.

• Grape-gatherers leave gleanings behind, either by oversight or in accordance with agricultural custom, leaving some fruit on the vines.

• In contrast, the judgment of Yahweh against Edom will have no such boundaries. Edom's enemies will not stop when they have "enough." The destruction will be absolute, leaving no remnants, no gleanings, and no survivors. Edom's proud self-sufficiency will be utterly emptied.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Deuteronomy 15:8: "...but you shall open your hand wide to him and willingly lend him sufficient for his need, whatever he needs."

• Proverbs 25:16: "Have you found honey? Eat only as much as you need, Lest you be filled with it and vomit."

• Proverbs 27:27: "Enough goats’ milk for your food, For the food of your household, And the nourishment for your maidservants."

• Jeremiah 49:9: "If grape-gatherers came to you, would they not leave some gleanings? If thieves by night, would they not destroy until they had enough?"

• Malachi 3:10: "...And try Me now in this," Says the LORD of hosts, "If I will not open for you the windows of heaven And pour out for you such blessing That there will not be room enough to receive it."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The concept of day is closely tied to the biblical ethic of contentment. In the New Testament, this translates to the Greek concept of autarkeia (sufficiency, contentment), as seen in 1 Timothy 6:6 ("godliness with contentment is great gain"). Understanding day helps the believer recognize that God’s standard of "enough" is designed to cultivate trust and dependency on Him, rather than on material hoarding.
