CUT OFF - ( damah , Strong's H1820 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb damah refers to being brought to silence, ruined, destroyed, or utterly cut off. In the context of prophetic judgment, it denotes the sudden, shocking, and complete devastation of a nation. Unlike a typical robbery where some possessions are left behind, or a grape harvest where gleanings remain, the judgment of Edom represented by this term is an absolute and irreversible wiping out, leaving nothing but silent desolation.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root idea of damah is closely tied to silence, stillness, and cessation. It paints a visceral picture of a bustling, proud city or nation suddenly reduced to absolute, eerie silence—the silence of death and non-existence. In the ancient Near East, safety and prosperity were marked by the noise of the marketplace, the cries of children, and the songs of harvest. To be damah is to have all life, sound, and activity permanently silenced through sudden, catastrophic ruin. It suggests not just a military defeat, but a complete termination of existence.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb damah (specifically the second root, damah II) means to cease, to be destroyed, to perish, or to be cut off. In the passive stems, such as the Niphal stem used in Obadiah 1:5 (nidmethah), it expresses the state of being ruined, undone, or brought to silence. The semantic range spans from being made silent (by death or ruin) to being utterly destroyed and brought to nothing. It represents an intensive and completed action where the subject is completely silenced and wiped out of existence.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout prophetic literature, damah is used to describe the sudden, tragic downfall of nations that once boasted in their strength.

  • It is used of Moab being laid waste and silenced in a single night.

  • It is used of Samaria’s king being swept away like a broken twig on the surface of rushing water.

  • The word picture is one of sudden, helpless cessation. Just as a speaking voice is instantly silenced when life departs, a nation under this judgment is instantly hused, its pride and boasting cut short by the hand of God.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, damah demonstrates the absolute sovereignty of God over proud human empires. Edom trusted in her high rocky fortresses (the high cliffs of Petra) and her human wisdom, feeling completely secure from any adversary. This word shows that human security apart from God is a fragile illusion. When the Sovereign Lord decrees judgment, even the most impenetrable strongholds are silenced in an instant. It also highlights the finality of divine wrath when a nation crosses the line of God's patience, particularly in their hostile treatment of His covenant people.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:5, the prophet uses a powerful rhetorical comparison to emphasize the extremity of Edom’s ruin. Ordinary thieves only steal what they want or can carry; grape-gatherers always leave gleanings behind, as mandated by natural limits and law. However, God's judgment on Edom will leave absolutely nothing. The parenthetical exclamation "Oh, how you will be cut off!" (ayk nidmethah) is an inserted cry of shock at the sheer completeness of the ruin. The nuance here is absolute plunder; the destruction is so thorough that it defies the natural limits of human greed. Edom is not merely robbed; she is utterly silenced and brought to nothing.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Isaiah 15:1 - "Because in the night Ar of Moab is laid waste and destroyed, Because in the night Kir of Moab is laid waste and destroyed..."

  • Hosea 4:6 - "My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge."

  • Hosea 10:7 - "As for Samaria, her king is cut off Like a twig on the water."

  • Hosea 10:15 - "In a morning the king of Israel shall utterly be cut off."

  • Jeremiah 47:5 - "Baldness has come upon Gaza, Ashkelon is cut off With the remnant of their valley."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Hebrew language contains homonyms for damah. While damah II refers to destruction and silencing, damah I means to be like, resemble, or compare, and damah III means to ponder, plan, or intend. This phonetic overlap sometimes allows for poetic depth in Hebrew prophecy, but in Obadiah 1:5, the context of total devastation makes the meaning of ruin and cessation unmistakable. The Greek translation (LXX) uses aporripto (to throw away, reject, or cast off), underscoring the sudden rejection and disposal of Edom under God's wrath.
