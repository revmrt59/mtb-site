LEFT - ( sha'ar , Strong's H7604 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb sha'ar means to remain, be left over, or leave behind as a remnant. In Obadiah 1:5, it highlights the terrifying completeness of Edom's coming judgment. While night thieves or grape-gatherers naturally leave a small portion behind, God's judgment on Edom will be absolute, leaving absolutely nothing of their wealth, security, or population.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient agrarian Israel, the concept of leaving (sha'ar) a portion of the harvest was deeply woven into daily life and God's covenant law. Farmers and grape-gatherers were commanded by God to leave the gleanings of their fields and vineyards for the poor, the stranger, the fatherless, and the widow. This left-over portion represented mercy, hope, and survival. Even in natural disasters or enemy raids, it was highly unusual for absolutely everything to be taken; a small remnant almost always remained. The word sha'ar evokes the image of these sparse but vital leftover fragments—the few grapes clinging to a vine after the harvest, or the small band of survivors who step out of the ruins of a collapsed city.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb sha'ar is a primitive root primarily meaning to remain, be left behind, survive, or keep back.

  • In the Qal stem, it refers to the state of remaining alive or being left behind as a survivor after a catastrophe.

  • In the Hiphil stem (which is the form used in Obadiah 1:5), the verb becomes causative: to cause to remain, to leave over, to spare, or to leave behind.

  • This verb is the direct linguistic root of the noun she'ar, which means "remnant." It describes the leftover portion of a population or resource that serves as the seed for future restoration.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, sha'ar is used to paint vivid pictures of survival amidst devastation:

  • It describes Noah and his family as the only ones who remained alive after the floodwaters covered the earth.

  • It is used of a tree stripped of its branches, leaving only a bare trunk, or a single signal pole left standing on top of a mountain.

  • It describes the small band of Israelite soldiers left alive after a crushing military defeat.

  • In every case, what is left (sha'ar) represents the absolute minimum required for a new beginning. To have nothing left is to face total annihilation.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, sha'ar is intimately connected to the doctrine of the Remnant. Usually, when God judges His covenant people, He promises to leave a remnant (sha'ar). This shows that His covenant mercy triumphs over judgment; He will not allow His people to be completely wiped out. However, when sha'ar is applied to the wicked or to the unrepentant enemies of God (such as Edom), the theological truth is reversed. For those who set themselves against God and persecute His people, the judgment is absolute. No remnant is spared, no grace-margin is given, and nothing is left. This serves as a solemn reminder of the finality of divine judgment when God's patience is exhausted.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:5, the prophet uses a powerful a fortiori (how much more) argument to emphasize the severity of Edom's impending ruin. He compares God's judgment to two common experiences of loss:

  • First, night thieves. Even the most ruthless thieves only steal "till they have enough." They do not take every single object in a home; they leave the heavy or useless items behind.

  • Second, grape-gatherers. Even the most thorough grape-harvesters leave some gleanings behind, either by accident or in obedience to the law of gleaning.

Yet, when God judges Edom using foreign nations as His instruments, there will be no such limitation. The invaders will search out Esau’s hidden treasures and strip him completely bare. God will not cause even a tiny remnant to be left (sha'ar) for Edom. Their pride in their secure rock fortresses of Petra will fail them completely.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 7:23 - "He destroyed all living things which were on the face of the ground: both man and cattle, creeping thing and bird of the air. They were destroyed from the earth. Only Noah and those who were with him in the ark remained alive."

  • Exodus 10:19 - "And the Lord turned a very strong west wind, which took the locusts away and blew them into the Red Sea. There remained not one locust in all the territory of Egypt."

  • Ruth 1:3 - "Then Elimelech, Naomi's husband, died; and she was left, and her two sons."

  • 1 Kings 19:18 - "Yet I have reserved seven thousand in Israel, all whose knees have not bowed to Baal, and every mouth that has not kissed him."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between Jacob (Israel) and Esau (Edom) is starkly highlighted by this word. While God severely disciplined Jacob, He always left a remnant (sha'ar) of His people to inherit the promises. Edom, however, because of their pride, violence, and malicious joy over Israel's downfall, was denied a remnant. Historically, this prophecy was literally fulfilled. The Nabataeans drove the Edomites from their rocky strongholds, and by the first century AD, they were absorbed into other nations, eventually disappearing from history entirely. While Israel survived the centuries, Edom had nothing left.
