OFF - (dâmâh, Strong's H1820)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb damah primarily signifies to cease, come to an end, be ruined, or be cut off. In the Niphal stem, it carries the passive sense of being brought to silence, destroyed, or thoroughly ruined. In Obadiah 1:5, damah appears as a dramatic parenthetical exclamation ("Oh, how you are cut off!"), underscoring the absolute and inescapable destruction awaiting Edom, contrasting the thoroughness of God's coming judgment with the partial plunder left behind by earthly thieves or grape-gatherers.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The prophecy of Obadiah addresses the proud nation of Edom, descendants of Esau, who rejoiced over and participated in the plunder of Jerusalem during Judah's time of crisis. Trusting in their natural mountain strongholds and human wisdom, Edom believed themselves invincible. Into this context of misplaced confidence, the prophet pronounces divine sentence. The root damah sits within a semantic domain of cessation, silence, and destruction. In prophetic literature, this verb frequently describes the sudden bringing to an end of human boasting, national security, and self-reliant power when confronted by the righteous judgment of God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, damah denotes the action or state of coming to an end, ceasing, being reduced to silence, or perishing completely. In the Niphal stem, the verb functions passively, depicting the subject as being ruined, cut off, or brought to silence by an external power. The term does not merely imply a temporary setback or partial loss; rather, it indicates a finality in which an entity's standing, activity, or existence is terminated.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament literature, damah is employed in contexts of sweeping divine ruin, royal downfall, and spiritual undone-ness:

  • It describes nations or cities brought to sudden devastation and silence under judgment.

  • It depicts kings or dynasties being terminated and swept away like debris on water.

  • It characterizes individuals stripped of their self-righteousness or worldly honor, reduced to helpless silence before God.

  • In wisdom poetry, it compares the high-minded person who lacks spiritual discernment to beasts that perish and cease to be.

4. Theological Implications

The theological significance of damah stems from God's absolute sovereignty over nations and human pride. When God decrees that a person or kingdom is to be "cut off" (damah), human defenses, strategic alliances, and natural geographic advantages are rendered entirely ineffective. Human plunderers operate with limitations, taking only what satisfies their immediate desire, but divine judgment is exhaustive. The term highlights the ultimate vulnerability of any security built apart from covenant faithfulness to the Lord.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:5, the prophet uses a vivid, rhetorical comparison to highlight the severity of Edom's coming judgment. He asks whether common thieves operating at night would not steal only until they had enough, or whether grape-gatherers would not leave behind some gleanings. Before completing the second half of the comparison, Obadiah interrupts his own sentence with an exclamatory outburst: "Oh, how you will be cut off!"

The target occurrence of damah in Obadiah 1:5 is parsed in the Niphal stem, perfect form, 2nd person, masculine, singular. The Niphal stem establishes that Edom is the recipient of this destructive judgment, while the 2nd person masculine singular directly addresses Edom as a unified entity. The choice of damah in this parenthetical clause serves to shatter Edom's illusion of partial survival. Unlike ordinary victims of theft who retain residual possessions, Edom will experience a total stripping, brought to complete silence and ruin by the decree of God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Hosea 4:6 — "My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge. Because you have rejected knowledge, I also will reject you from being priest for Me..."

  • Isaiah 6:5 — "So I said: 'Woe is me, for I am undone! Because I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips; for my eyes have seen the King, The LORD of hosts.'"

  • Psalms 49:20 — "A man who is in honor, yet does not understand, is like the beasts that perish."

  • Hosea 10:7 — "As for Samaria, her king is cut off like a twig on the water."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The parenthetical placement of damah in Obadiah 1:5 creates a literary pause that heightens the emotional weight of the divine oracle. Edom boasted in her high dwellings in the rock crevices (Obadiah 1:3), claiming no one could bring her down. By introducing damah into the middle of the passage's imagery, the text structurally demonstrates that God's judgment pierces directly into the heart of human arrogance, leaving no place for pride or escape.
