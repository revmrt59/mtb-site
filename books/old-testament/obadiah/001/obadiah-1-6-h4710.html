TREASURES - ( matspon , Strong's H4710 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun matspon refers to hidden treasures, secret stores, or well-concealed valuables. Derived from a root meaning to hide or treasure up, it highlights things deliberately kept out of sight for safekeeping. In Obadiah 1:6, it refers to the material wealth and secret strongholds of Edom, which they believed were completely secure in their rocky mountain fortresses. However, God warns that even their most deeply hidden treasures will be thoroughly ransacked and exposed by their enemies. This word serves as a sobering reminder of the futility of seeking security in earthly wealth or secret defenses rather than in the living God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • matspon comes from the primitive root tsaphan, which means to hide, treasure, store up, or conceal.

  • The word picture is that of a valuable possession carefully tucked away in a secret place to protect it from thieves, invaders, or decay.

  • Historically, Edom (the descendants of Esau) lived in the rugged, mountainous territory of Mount Seir. The red sandstone cliffs and deep gorges (such as the area around Petra) provided natural, impregnable fortresses.

  • Because of this terrain, the Edomites believed their wealth and their lives were entirely safe. They utilized deep caves, clefts in the rock, and hidden crevices as literal vaults to store their accumulated riches. The word matspon captures both the physical treasures themselves and the secret, secure places where they were hoarded.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • matspon is a masculine plural noun occurring only once in the Hebrew Bible (a hapax legomenon) in Obadiah 1:6.

  • The lexical definition of matspon is hidden treasures, secret stores, concealed valuables, or hidden recesses.

  • The word signifies stored wealth that is intentionally covered, obscured, or hoarded in secret vaults to prevent detection.

  • It describes treasures that are placed in inaccessible locations for maximum security, representing the ultimate earthly reserves of a nation.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • The use of matspon in Obadiah 1:6 paints a vivid picture of divine scrutiny and total exposure.

  • Obadiah contrasts the thoroughness of God's judgment with that of ordinary thieves. Ordinary thieves only take what they want, and grape-gatherers leave some gleanings. But when God judges Edom, the invaders will strip everything.

  • The word pictures the complete violation of Edom’s ultimate security. The "hidden treasures" they relied upon for their future and their pride are dragged out into the light. What they thought was invisible and untouchable is easily found and plundered.

4. Theological Implications

  • The Futility of Earthly Security: Human beings consistently try to create secure, hidden reserves—whether of wealth, power, or secrets—to protect themselves from vulnerability. Edom's hidden treasures represent self-reliance and false security.

  • Divine Omniscience and Sovereignty: Nothing is hidden from the eyes of God. Human fortresses and secret vaults are completely open to His gaze. God’s judgment penetrates the deepest, most secure recesses of human life.

  • The Illusion of Pride: Pride deceives the human heart into believing that we can hide our assets or ourselves from accountability. Obadiah shows that pride inevitably leads to exposure and downfall.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Obadiah 1:6, the prophet addresses the pride of Edom, who boasted, "Who will bring me down to the ground?" (v. 3).

  • Edom's geography gave them a false sense of absolute safety. They believed their rocky clefts were impenetrable.

  • The nuance of matspon here highlights the irony of their situation. The very things they took pains to hide, thinking no enemy could ever reach them, are "searched out" and "sought after" with microscopic precision.

  • The passive verbs ("searched out," "sought after") emphasize that this is a divine action executed through human instruments. God Himself is the one uncovering their secrets.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

While matspon occurs only once in the Old Testament, its direct root verb tsaphan (Strong's H6845) carries the same core meaning of hiding, storing up, or treasuring. Key uses of this root include:

  • Psalm 119:11 - "Your word I have hidden [tsaphan] in my heart, That I might not sin against You."

  • Proverbs 2:1 - "My son, if you receive my words, And treasure up [tsaphan] my commands within you,"

  • Job 23:12 - "I have not departed from the commandment of His lips; I have treasured [tsaphan] the words of His mouth More than my necessary food."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The historical fulfillment of this prophecy occurred when the Nabataeans, who were allies of Edom, betrayed them. They gained access to these very mountain strongholds and plundered their secure storehouses, proving that human alliances and geographical defenses cannot withstand God's decree.

  • There is a powerful spiritual contrast here: while the world seeks to hide physical treasures (matspon) in rocky vaults that will ultimately fail them, believers are called to hide God's Word (tsaphan) in their hearts (Psalm 119:11) and have their lives "hidden with Christ in God" (Colossians 3:3). One leads to exposure and loss; the other leads to eternal security.
