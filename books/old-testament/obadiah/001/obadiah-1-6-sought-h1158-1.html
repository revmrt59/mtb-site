SOUGHT - (bâʻâh, Strong's H1158)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb baʿah carries the basic lexical meaning of seeking, inquiring, or asking, while in distinct physical contexts it can refer to swelling or boiling. In the passive Niphal stem as used in Obadiah 1:6, the word means "to be sought out" or "to be searched out." In this verse, the word describes the thorough exposure and pillaging of Edom's hidden riches. It contributes to Obadiah's announcement of divine judgment by demonstrating that no concealed treasure or secret stronghold of Edom will escape the exhaustive search of the conquerors commissioned to execute God's decree.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root baʿah appears in classical Hebrew and related ancient Semitic languages primarily as a verb associated with asking, seeking, or requesting. In prophetic and poetic literature, it functions either in contexts of seeking knowledge and making inquiry or in descriptions of physical swelling and boiling.

In the brief prophetic book of Obadiah, the historical setting centers on the judgment declared against the nation of Edom. Situated in the rugged, mountainous territory south of the Dead Sea, Edom felt invulnerable due to its natural rock defenses, strategic high ground, and stored wealth accumulated through trade routes. The nation had proudly rejoiced over the destruction of Jerusalem and participated in plundering Judah. Obadiah 1:6 forms part of God's response to Edom's arrogance, warning that physical hideouts and secret caches will offer no protection when divine judgment comes upon them.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

At its core, baʿah signifies the act of seeking or inquiring. The primary semantic spectrum includes:

  • Qal stem: to seek, ask, inquire, or request; in specific physical contexts, to swell or boil.

  • Niphal stem: to be searched out, to be sought out, or to be exposed.

It is crucial from a lexical perspective to keep the semantic categories distinct. While baʿah can describe physical boiling or swelling in certain Isaiah passages, its use in the passive Niphal stem in Obadiah 1:6 is straightforwardly lexicalized as "to be sought out" or "to be searched out." The depth of Obadiah 1:6 does not depend on importing physical boiling or swelling imagery into the verb, but rather on the comprehensive nature of the search described by the prophetic context.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Old Testament Scriptures, baʿah is used relatively rarely. In active non-physical settings, it describes making inquiry or seeking an answer (such as inquiring of a watchman concerning the night). In vivid physical descriptions, it depicts water boiling over a fire or a structural wall bulging prior to a collapse.

In Obadiah 1:6, where the verb appears in the Niphal stem, perfect form, 3rd person, the nuance is strictly passive and exhaustive: hidden treasures are brought to light through being systematically searched out. In Hebrew poetic structure, baʿah operates in synthetic parallelism with chapas ("ransacked" or "searched out"). Together, these terms convey the utter thoroughness of Edom's defeat—what was painstakingly concealed by human effort is fully sought out and taken away.

4. Theological Implications

The usage of baʿah in Obadiah 1:6 highlights several major canonical themes:

  • The Folly of False Security: Edom trusted in natural defenses, high fortresses, and concealed underground wealth. Scripture repeatedly teaches that human secrecy and physical strongholds cannot shield anyone from divine accountability.

  • God's inescapable Omniscience and Sovereignty: Nothing remains hidden from God. When God decrees judgment, human attempts to hide assets or retreat into darkness are entirely ineffective.

  • Measure-for-Measure Retribution: Edom participated in searching out and plundering Judah during her calamity. Canonically, God repays Edom's cruelty by allowing Edom's own secret treasures to be searched out and stripped away by invaders.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:1–9, the prophet constructs a stark contrast between Edom's self-satisfied pride and God's sovereign sentence. In verse 3, Edom asks in its heart, "Who will bring me down to the ground?" God answers in verse 4 that even if they make their nest among the stars, He will pull them down.

Verse 5 establishes a contrast with typical nighttime thieves or grape-gatherers:

  • Normal thieves take only what they want until they have enough.

  • Grape-gatherers leave gleanings behind.

In verse 6, however, the plunder of Esau (Edom) is shown to be absolute: "How Esau shall be ransacked! How his hidden treasures shall be sought out!" The phrase "hidden treasures" paired with baʿah (in the Niphal stem, perfect form, 3rd person) emphasizes that the invaders will not perform a casual raid. Instead, they will leave no stone unturned, searching out every secret vault and hidden stash. The syntax presents the action as a completed reality in the prophetic scope, emphasizing the certainty and completeness of the coming exposure.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Isaiah 21:12 — "inquire" — Used in the active sense of seeking information from a watchman: "If you will inquire, inquire; return! Come back!"

  • Isaiah 30:13 — "bulge" — Used in a physical sense to describe a swelling defect in a high wall: "a break ready to fall, bulging out in a high wall."

  • Isaiah 64:2 — "boil" — Used in a physical sense to depict the intense action of fire on water: "As fire burns brushwood, as fire causes water to boil."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The parallel term chapas in Obadiah 1:6 means to search, track down, or disguise. The pairing of chapas and baʿah creates a poetic double-emphasis on total discovery.

  • Scripture frequently uses the concept of "searching out" hidden things to describe judgment (e.g., Jeremiah 23:24; Amos 9:2–3). Edom's historical judgment serves as a sober reminder that material wealth and earthly secrecy provide no ultimate refuge against the Lord.
