TREASURES - (mitspun, Strong's H4710)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun mitzpun refers to hidden treasures, secret stores, or concealed riches. In Obadiah 1:6, it highlights the total exposure and plunder of Esau’s (Edom’s) most carefully guarded wealth under divine judgment, demonstrating that no human security or hidden storehouse can withstand the sovereign scrutiny and retribution of God.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The noun mitzpun is derived from the primary Hebrew verbal root tzafan, which means "to hide," "to treasure up," or "to conceal for safekeeping." While the root verb appears across various Old Testament contexts referring to physical concealment or metaphorical treasuring of wisdom, mitzpun represents a specialized nominal derivative signifying the actual hidden places or concealed riches themselves.

Geographically and historically, Edom occupied the mountainous region south of the Dead Sea, characterized by steep sandstone cliffs, narrow gorges (such as the Siq leading into Petra), and high-altitude mountain strongholds. The Edomites utilized these natural, inaccessible rock formations as subterranean vaults and secure caches for the wealth accumulated through international trade routes. The term reflects the psychological and practical reality of Edom's security model: wealth intentionally hidden away in deep, impenetrable places where human plunderers could never reach it.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, mitzpun denotes hidden treasures, concealed stores, or secret hoards. Standard lexical authorities (such as Brown-Driver-Briggs and TWOT) define the term as "hidden treasure" or "secret place of store."

The word does not refer merely to surface wealth or visible assets, but specifically to stored valuables that have been deliberately removed from sight and secured against detection. The masculine plural form in the passage emphasizes the vast collections or multiple hidden caches of wealth that Edom had amassed over centuries of regional commerce and plunder.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Because this specific noun is restricted to this prophetic context within the Hebrew Scriptures, its biblical nuance is defined by its immediate poetic and theological function. The term carries a tone of ironic exposure. Edom believed that its physical geography and secret vaults provided ultimate immunity.

By employing mitzpun, the prophet focuses attention on the extreme measures Edom took to protect its gains—and the absolute failure of those measures when God executes judgment. The nuance is not simply "riches," but "the most secret, prized, and carefully buried reserves."

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of mitzpun rests on the doctrine of divine omniscience and sovereign retribution.

  • The Limits of Human Security: Edom trusted in its geography, allies, wisdom, and hidden reserves. The divine stripping of Edom's mitzpun demonstrates that material wealth, no matter how securely hidden or defended, offers zero protection against divine holy wrath.

  • Divine Omniscience: Human eyes cannot penetrate the dark, secret crevices of the mountains, but nothing is hidden from the eyes of the Lord. God sees into the deepest secret vaults of human self-reliance.

  • Lex Talionis (Eye for an Eye): Edom had stood aloof and even participated in the plundering of Jerusalem's wealth during Israel's calamity. In turn, God declares that Edom’s own secret treasures will be completely searched out and exposed to plunder.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Obadiah 1:5–6 presents a powerful contrast between normal human raiding and God’s thorough judgment:

  • Verse 5 points out that ordinary thieves or grape-gatherers take only what they want or leave gleanings behind. They steal until they have enough, leaving something remaining.

  • Verse 6 stands in sharp contrast: "How Esau shall be searched out! How his hidden treasures shall be sought after!"

The grammar utilizes a masculine plural form for mitzpun, emphasizing the comprehensive nature of the loss. God will leave no stone unturned. The allies and enemies whom God brings against Edom will not merely plunder the visible cities; they will meticulously seek out and empty every single hidden cache (mitzpun). What human thieves would miss, divine judgment will thoroughly strip. The rhetorical exclamations ("How...!") stress the shocking completeness of Edom's ruin.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • This term is unique to this context and does not appear elsewhere in the biblical corpus.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint, or LXX) renders the poetic phrase in Obadiah 1:6 using terms that convey "things that are hidden" or "concealed possessions," preserving the precise idea of deep concealment being exposed.

Furthermore, the prophetic emphasis on searching out hidden things underscores a major motif in prophetic literature: pride causes nations to build false refuges, but God delights in bringing down high places and exposing hidden trust. The plunder of Edom’s hidden treasures served as an enduring warning to the nations that self-sufficiency is a fatal illusion.
