BREAD - (lechem, Strong's H3899)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun lechem basic lexical meaning is "bread," "food," or generic "sustenance." Derived from basic agrarian life where grain products formed the essential staple of daily existence, the term expanded conceptually to encompass complete meals, royal feasts, and God's providential nourishment. Beyond physical nourishment, sharing lechem symbolized covenant binding, hospitality, mutual obligation, and sacred trust between host and guest or allied partners. In Obadiah 1:7, lechem refers to the shared meal that signified table fellowship and covenant loyalty. The prophet uses it to highlight the severe treachery of Edom's trusted allies; those who broke bread with Edom—benefiting from their table and covenant friendship—are the very ones who deceive them and lay a snare beneath them.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In ancient Near Eastern culture, bread was the primary food source necessary for human survival. The production of bread required planting, harvesting, threshing, grinding grain, and baking—a daily effort central to household survival. Consequently, the noun lechem became the foundational term for food in general, representing the essential life-sustaining provision provided by God through creation.

Beyond basic biology, sharing lechem carried deep social, legal, and covenantal obligations in ancient Israel and surrounding nations. To eat bread with someone was not merely a casual act of dining; it established a bond of hospitality, mutual protection, and peace. Eating another person's bread signaled alliance and trust, making betrayal by a table companion one of the most egregious violations of social and covenantal ethics in the biblical world.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, lechem refers primarily to bread or baked grain products. Secondarily, by synecdoche, it denotes general food, sustenance, or provisions required to maintain life.

standard lexical works define lechem according to several basic categories:

  • Physical bread or baked cakes made from wheat, barley, or other grains.

  • General food or provisions, including meat, fruit, and agricultural yield.

  • Sacrificial food offered upon the altar, often described as the food of God's house or sanctuary.

  • Figurative or metaphorical sustenance, such as the yields of wisdom, labor, or deceit.

In grammatical structure, the form in Obadiah 1:7 functions as a singular noun in the absolute state, referring directly to the physical food shared between covenant partners.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament literature, lechem appears across narrative, law, wisdom, and prophetic texts to convey physical, relational, and ceremonial truths.

  • Daily Sustenance and Provision: lechem designates the daily bread necessary to sustain life. Its absence signifies famine, judgment, or spiritual impoverishment, while its abundance reflects divine favor and creation's bounty.

  • Hospitality and Covenant Bonds: Inviting a stranger or neighbor to eat lechem established host obligations and implied peace between parties. Breaking bread together sealed agreements and bound participants in social fellowship.

  • Ceremonial and Cultic Sacredness: In the tabernacle and temple, the "bread of the Presence" (lechem ha-panim) stood continually before the Lord as an ongoing memorial of God's covenant with the twelve tribes of Israel.

  • Wisdom and Moral Metaphor: Wisdom literature uses lechem metaphorically, contrasting the "bread of wickedness" or "bread of deceit" with the wholesome nourishment of wisdom and righteousness.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, lechem demonstrates God's sovereignty as the ultimate provider of human life. Rain, fertility, and grain are gifts from Yahweh, emphasizing humanity's total dependence upon Him for daily existence. Human life is sustained not by human self-sufficiency, but by the creative word and providential care of God.

Furthermore, lechem underscores the sanctity of fellowship and community under God's moral order. Because meal sharing implies peace and covenant obligations, using shared food as a cover for treason or malice directly opposes God's righteous standards. The betrayal of table fellowship is not merely a social offense; it reveals a profound moral corruption that invites divine justice.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:7, the prophet pronounces judgment on the nation of Edom for their pride and their violence against their brother nation, Judah. Edom trusted in her secure, rocky fortifications and her geopolitical alliances with neighboring nations. However, Obadiah announces that Edom's trusted allies will turn against her.

The phrase "those who eat your bread" (lechem) specifically targets Edom's intimate covenant partners and allies. Eating Edom's bread signified that these confederates enjoyed Edom's hospitality, shared in her provisions, and were bound by solemn duties of peace and protection. Grammatically, the noun appears in the singular number and absolute state, serving as the direct object of eating in this poetic clause.

The contextual irony is sharp: the very allies who sat at Edom's table and consumed her lechem become the agents of her ruin. Instead of offering protection, they deceive Edom, force her to her borders, and lay a hidden trap beneath her feet. The word lechem accentuates the treacherous nature of Edom's fall—highlighting that misplaced trust in earthly alliances and compromised table fellowship provides no security against the decree of Yahweh.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Deuteronomy 8:9 – "A land in which you will eat bread without scarcity, in which you will lack nothing in it..."

  • 1 Samuel 21:4 – "And the priest answered David and said, 'There is no common bread on hand; but there is holy bread, if the young men have at least kept themselves from women.'"

  • Ecclesiastes 11:1 – "Cast your bread upon the waters, for you will find it after many days."

  • Ezekiel 18:16 – "Has not oppressed anyone, has not withheld a pledge, nor robbed by violence, but has given his bread to the hungry and covered the naked with a garment..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In biblical prophecy, betrayal by table companions is a vivid motif for deep personal and national treachery. Shared food creates an expectation of goodwill; converting that fellowship into an ambush exposes extreme moral deviousness. In Obadiah 1:7, Edom's false security in her diplomatic pacts is exposed. The allies who shared her lechem actively orchestrated her expulsion, demonstrating that human treaties formed apart from divine righteousness are fragile and deceptive.
