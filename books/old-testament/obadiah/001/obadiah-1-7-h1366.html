BORDER - ( gebul , Strong's H1366 )

Summary:

In Obadiah 1:7, the word gebul refers to a boundary line, frontier, or territory limit. In this prophetic warning, it serves as a powerful symbol of displacement and betrayal. The passage describes how Edom's trusted political allies escorted them to the very edge of their territory, only to abandon them and shut them out. Instead of securing Edom's frontiers, these false friends pushed them to the border, stripping them of their security and leaving them vulnerable to destruction.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The noun gebul derives from an unused root meaning to twist as a rope, reflecting the ancient practice of using measuring cords to mark out land boundaries. In the ancient Near East, a border was not merely an abstract line on a map; it was a physical frontier marked by landmarks, stones, rivers, or mountain ridges. Borders defined national identity, heritage, and the limits of a king's sovereign protection. To be escorted or driven to the gebul meant being cast out of the sphere of safety and belonging into the vulnerable, lawless regions beyond.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term gebul refers to a border, boundary, limit, or the territory enclosed within those boundaries. It denotes the concrete boundary line itself, the landmark that indicates it, or the entire geographic region contained within those limits. In legal and covenantal contexts, it represents the designated inheritance given to a tribe or nation. In poetic and prophetic literature, it signifies the limits of safety, sovereignty, and divine allowance.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, gebul is employed in several distinct ways:

• Territorial Allotments: It is used extensively in the book of Joshua to outline the precise geographic borders of the tribes of Israel, indicating their God-given inheritance.

• Sovereign Limits: It represents the boundaries established by God for the natural world, such as the limits set for the waters of the sea so they do not flood the earth.

• The Boundary Stone: It refers to the physical landmark marking property lines. Moving a neighbor's boundary stone (gebul) was strictly forbidden under covenant law, representing theft and a violation of community trust.

• Defensive Frontiers: It marks the outer edge of a nation's military strength. To stand at the border without defense is to be on the brink of national collapse.

### 4. Theological Implications

Theologically, gebul reminds us that God is the ultimate Sovereign who establishes the boundaries of all nations. He determines the times and the preappointed limits of human dwellings. Edom prided themselves on their high, rocky fortresses, believing their natural borders made them impenetrable to invaders. However, human defenses are useless when God decrees judgment. The theological irony in Obadiah is that those whom Edom trusted to secure their borders became the very instruments of their expulsion. This demonstrates the futility of placing ultimate trust in human alliances and physical geography rather than in the living God.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:7, the phrase "brought you to the border" reveals a deep and painful deception. Edom had entered into confederacies and peace treaties with neighboring nations, expecting mutual protection. Instead, these allies practiced treacherous diplomacy. They escorted Edom's ambassadors or forces to the frontier under the guise of friendship, only to turn them away, expel them from their own land, or deliver them into the hands of their enemies. The gebul here is the threshold of ruin. The very borders Edom thought were secure became the exit point of their sovereignty and the entry point of their destruction.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Joshua 13:23 - "And the border of the children of Reuben was the bank of the Jordan."

• Psalm 104:9 - "You have set a boundary that they may not pass over, That they may not return to cover the earth."

• Psalm 147:14 - "He makes peace in your borders, And fills you with the finest wheat."

• Malachi 1:5 - "Your eyes shall see, and you shall say, 'The LORD is magnified beyond the border of Israel.'"

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

Edom’s geography was uniquely rugged, characterized by the steep cliffs and narrow gorges of Mount Seir. Their borders were naturally defensible, requiring only a small number of soldiers to guard the narrow passes. For Edom to be driven to the gebul means they were forced out of these secure mountain strongholds and into the open, flat plains where their tactical advantages were entirely lost. This physical displacement mirrors their spiritual fall from haughty pride to absolute humiliation.
