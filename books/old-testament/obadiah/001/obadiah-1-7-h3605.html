ALL - ( kol , Strong's H3605 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun kol (translated as "all," "every," or "the whole") expresses totality, completeness, and the absolute entirety of a collective group. In Obadiah 1:7, it underscores the sweeping and absolute scope of Edom’s betrayal. Every single one of Edom's political allies and trusted confederates—without exception—will turn against them, exposing the utter folly of relying on human alliances rather than the sovereign Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word kol derives from a root meaning to complete, perfect, or make whole. It functions as a noun indicating "the whole" or "totality," though it is most frequently used in a construct state with other nouns to mean "all of" or "every." The word picture is one of a closed circle containing everything of a certain class, leaving absolutely nothing outside its boundary. When applied to groups, nations, or alliances, it conveys a sweeping, comprehensive reality where no individual, faction, or subgroup is exempted.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary definition of kol is the entirety of any noun it modifies. Depending on the grammatical context, it indicates:

  • The whole or entirety of a unit (e.g., the whole land, the whole heart).

  • Every individual member of a collective group (e.g., all the men, every living thing).

  • Any or all possible options in a given category.

In Obadiah 1:7, the word functions in the construct state modifying "the men of your covenant" (anshe beritekha), meaning "all the men of your confederacy." It highlights that the betrayal of Edom is not partial, minor, or limited to a few rogue actors; it is comprehensive.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, kol serves to establish absolute boundaries, often emphasizing God's complete ownership ("all the earth is mine") or humanity's universal condition ("all have sinned").

In covenantal and political contexts, "all" signifies either total loyalty or, conversely, absolute abandonment. The word picture in Obadiah 1:7 is that of a besieged man looking around at his inner circle of friends and allies, only to find that every single face has turned hostile. Not one ally remains loyal; the entire network of Edom's defense collapses because the betrayal is one hundred percent complete.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of kol in Obadiah 1:7 rests on the total futility of human confidence. Edom prided themselves on their wisdom, mountain strongholds, and secure political treaties. God demonstrates that when His judgment is unleashed, every earthly support system is completely swept away.

The comprehensive nature of the betrayal ("all the men") reflects the comprehensive scope of divine justice. Edom's complete reliance on human partners results in a complete abandonment by those same partners. This serves as a stark pastoral warning against spiritual adultery, misplaced trust, and relying on worldly structures rather than the living God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:7, Edom is confronted with the shocking reality that "all" the men of their confederacy have driven them to the border. The verse contains three parallel descriptions of Edom's close associates: "all the men in your confederacy," "the men at peace with you," and "those who eat your bread."

The word kol sets the stage for this descending order of intimacy. The betrayal starts with political treaty partners at the borders and reaches to the very table of fellowship. The nuance is that Edom's ruin is complete because their misplaced trust was complete. The comprehensive external betrayal is matched by a comprehensive internal blindness; because they trusted in "all" these allies, they are left with "no one" who has understanding.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the ancient Near East, covenant-making was a sacred act, often sealed with a shared meal. To eat bread with someone (lahmekha) and then set a trap represents the ultimate violation of hospitality and covenant loyalty. The fact that "all" of Edom's allies participated in this treachery highlights the absolute isolation of Edom under the judgment of God. No human wisdom or diplomatic skill could preserve a single alliance once the Lord decreed their downfall.
