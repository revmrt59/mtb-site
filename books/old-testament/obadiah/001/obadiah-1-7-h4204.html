TRAP - ( mazor , Strong's H4204 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word mazor refers to a trap, snare, or net laid secretly to capture an unsuspecting target. Used exclusively in the Old Testament in Obadiah 1:7, it depicts the shocking treachery of Edom's trusted allies who turned against them. Instead of offering mutual support, those who shared Edom's bread secretly laid a trap under them, exploiting their hospitality and trust. This term highlights the destructive nature of deceitful alliances and the ultimate failure of relying on human schemes rather than divine covenant.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• In the ancient Near East, eating bread together was a sacred covenant of peace, loyalty, and mutual protection. To share a table was to guarantee the safety of one's host or guest.

• The word mazor evokes the image of a hunter or fowler stealthily laying a hidden net or trap underneath an animal's path, waiting for the precise moment of vulnerability to snap it shut.

• The contrast is stark: the hands that reached out to receive bread were simultaneously setting a hidden snare beneath the feet of the giver.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The word mazor means a trap, snare, net, or plot.

• A semantic connection exists between this word and mazor (Strong's H4205), which means "wound" or "sore." This indicates that the trap in view is not merely restrictive, but one designed to inflict a binding, painful, and fatal injury.

• In the context of Obadiah 1:7, it designates a treacherous plot or conspiracy, specifically a trap set under someone.

• The Septuagint (LXX) translates this word as enedra, meaning an ambush, plot, or deadly trap.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Mazor functions as a vivid metaphor for betrayal within covenantal relationships. It is not an open declaration of war, but a covert, subterranean deception.

• The "trap" is placed "under you" (tachtecha), indicating that Edom walked blindly over a hazard designed by their closest political and economic partners.

• The word picture emphasizes the complete blindness of the victim. Edom pridefully believed they were secure in their mountain fortresses, completely unaware that the ground beneath their feet had been rigged by their allies to collapse.

4. Theological Implications

• Retributive Justice (Lex Talionis): Edom had stood by and participated in the betrayal and looting of their brother Jacob (Israel). Consequently, God sovereignly decreed that Edom would experience the exact same betrayal from their own allies.

• The Folly of Carnal Security: Edom trusted in their wisdom, alliances, and geographical defenses. God demonstrates that human wisdom cannot foresee or prevent the ruin that comes from misplaced trust.

• Sacredness of Covenant: God takes covenantal loyalty seriously. The violation of the "bread-covenant" is treated as an egregious sin that invites divine judgment.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• Obadiah 1:7 states: "All the men of your confederacy shall force you to the border; the men at peace with you shall deceive you and prevail against you. Those who eat your bread have laid a trap for you."

• The progression of betrayal in this verse is highly structured: first, allies escort them to the border (expulsion); second, peaceful associates deceive them; third, intimate dining partners lay the mazor (trap).

• The nuance here is that the trap was prepared while friendship was still being feigned. The very source of Edom's sustenance and economic pride (their trade routes and treaties) became the mechanism of their downfall.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

Because mazor (H4204) is a hapax legomenon in the Hebrew Bible with the meaning of "trap," its Greek equivalent in the Septuagint (enedra, meaning ambush, plot, or trap) is found in other key passages illustrating this concept:

• Joshua 8:9 - "Joshua therefore sent them out; and they went to lie in ambush, and stayed between Bethel and Ai..."

• Psalm 10:9 - "He lies in wait secretly, as a lion in his den; He lies in wait to catch the poor..."

• Acts 23:16 - "So when Paul's sister's son heard of their ambush, he went and entered the barracks and told Paul."

• Acts 25:3 - "...asking a favor against him, that he would summon him to Jerusalem—while they lay in ambush along the road to kill him."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The concept of a "bread-eater" laying a trap has strong biblical parallels to the betrayal of Jesus by Judas Iscariot. Psalm 41:9 ("My own familiar friend in whom I trusted, who ate my bread, has lifted up his heel against me") uses a similar theological and cultural framework of table-fellowship betrayal, which Jesus directly quotes in John 13:18.

• Historically, this prophecy was fulfilled when the Nabataeans, whom Edom trusted as allies, turned on them, drove them from their rock-hewn fortress of Petra, and seized their territory.
