DECEIVE - ( nasha , Strong's H5377 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb nasha means to lead astray, delude, or utterly deceive. In the context of Obadiah 1:7, it describes the treacherous betrayal of Edom by her trusted allies. Edom, proud of her rocky fortresses, placed absolute confidence in her political and military confederates. However, these very allies actively deceived Edom, exploiting her misplaced trust to bring about her sudden ruin. The word highlights the painful truth that earthly alliances and human security apart from God are ultimately deceptive and fragile.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root nasha carries the primary idea of causing someone to err, leading them off the correct path, or causing them to accept a falsehood as truth. It implies an active, deceptive influence that lures the victim into a false sense of security.

• In the ancient Near East, covenant treaties and alliances (confederacies) were considered sacred. To deceive a covenant partner was not just a diplomatic failure but a heinous act of treachery that violated solemn oaths of peace and mutual defense. The word picture is one of a traveler being deliberately led to a cliff by a guide they trusted implicitly.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The verb nasha occurs in the Hiphil (causative) stem, meaning "to deceive," "to beguile," "to mislead," or "to delude."

• The semantic range includes:

• To mislead or lead astray through false representations.

• To cause someone to believe a lie, resulting in their downfall.

• To cheat or gull someone by taking advantage of their gullibility or trust.

• In Obadiah 1:7, the word is used in the Hiphil perfect tense, indicating a completed, decisive act of deception carried out by Edom's allies.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The word paints a picture of intellectual and spiritual blindness. The deceived person is blinded to reality, walking confidently toward their own destruction because they have believed a falsehood.

• It is the same verb used in Genesis 3:13, where Eve says, "The serpent deceived me, and I ate." The deception involves presenting something harmful as highly beneficial, bypassing the victim's critical judgment.

• In prophetic literature, nasha describes how false prophets, false gods, or a person's own pride can deceive them, creating an illusion of safety where there is only impending judgment.

4. Theological Implications

• Deception is intimately linked to pride. In Obadiah, Edom's pride made her highly susceptible to deception. When a nation or individual trusts in their own wisdom, resources, or political alliances rather than the living God, they open themselves up to being deceived.

• Human covenants and political treaties are inherently unstable when divorced from God's truth. God often allows those who trust in human strength to be deceived and betrayed by the very objects of their misplaced trust, demonstrating the vanity of relying on the creature rather than the Creator.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Obadiah 1:7, Edom (the descendants of Esau) is condemned for her pride and her violence against her brother Jacob (Israel). Edom boasted in her secure, mountainous home (Sela/Petra) and her wise men. She also trusted her international treaties.

• The use of nasha here shows the ironic justice of God. Edom stood by and gloated while Jerusalem was plundered, believing she was safe because of her alliances. God turned those very allies against her. The "men at peace" actively deceived Edom, pretending friendship while plotting her demise. The deception was so thorough that the verse ends with, "No one is aware of it," showing how completely Edom's famed wisdom failed her.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 3:13 - "And the LORD God said to the woman, 'What is this you have done?' The woman said, 'The serpent deceived me, and I ate.'"

• 2 Kings 18:29 - "Thus says the king: 'Do not let Hezekiah deceive you, for he shall not be able to deliver you from his hand;'"

• Jeremiah 37:9 - "Thus says the LORD: 'Do not deceive yourselves, saying, "The Chaldeans will surely depart from us," for they will not depart.'"

• Jeremiah 49:16 - "Your fierceness has deceived you, The pride of your heart, O you who dwell in the clefts of the rock, Who hold the height of the hill! Though you make your nest as high as the eagle, I will bring you down from there,' says the LORD."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The parallel between Obadiah 1:7 and Jeremiah 49:16 is striking. In Jeremiah, Edom's own pride is the agent of deception (nasha). In Obadiah, it is her allies who deceive her (nasha). This shows that external deception and internal self-deception are two sides of the same coin.

• The psychological impact of nasha is total blindness to danger. Edom's wise men, famed throughout the ancient world, were completely blindfolded by this deception, proving that human wisdom apart from God's fear is foolishness.
