LAID - ( śûm , Strong's H7760 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb śûm fundamentally means to put, place, set, or establish. In Obadiah 1:7, it describes the treacherous actions of Edom’s close allies—those who shared their bread—who actively "laid" or "placed" a trap beneath them. This word choice highlights a deliberate, calculated betrayal, contrasting the external appearance of covenant peace with the secret positioning of a destructive snare. It serves as a sober warning of the blindness that pride brings, rendering a nation unable to see the traps laid by its closest companions.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete nature of ancient Hebrew relies heavily on physical actions to convey deep intellectual and spiritual concepts. The verb śûm is an active word of physical placement and arrangement. It is the word used when a builder positions a foundation stone, when a creator sets boundaries for the sea, or when a hunter carefully positions a snare. The background of laying a trap in Obadiah 1:7 is predatory and covert. It evokes the imagery of a hunter who understands the paths and habits of the prey, quietly arranging a snare in the exact spot where the target is sure to step, relying entirely on the prey's trust and ignorance.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary semantic range of śûm includes:

• To put, place, or set an object in a specific location.

• To lay upon, impose, or apply (such as laying hands on someone or imposing a tax).

• To appoint, establish, or direct (such as appointing a leader or establishing a decree).

• To bring about, cause, or make (such as making a city a heap of ruins).

In Obadiah 1:7, śûm is paired with the noun māzôr (variously translated as trap, snare, net, or wound). In this context, the verb means to deliberately set, deploy, or position a hidden trap with the intent to capture, injure, or overthrow.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, śûm is used to illustrate several powerful word pictures:

• The Hand on the Mouth: Placing a hand over the mouth is a gesture of awe, humility, and self-imposed silence in the presence of majesty or judgment.

• The Yoke of Subjugation: Placing a heavy yoke upon the neck of a defeated enemy or subject people.

• The Sovereign Placement of God: God putting humanity in the Garden, placing His name in Jerusalem, or setting His bow in the clouds.

• The Covert Trap: Setting an ambush or positioning a net. This usage emphasizes calculation. The trap is not fallen into by accident; it is positioned with precision by an adversary who knows the terrain of the victim’s life.

### 4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of śûm in Obadiah rests on the contrast between human schemes and divine sovereignty. Edom prided themselves on their wisdom and the natural security of their rocky, mountainous territory. They believed they were safe from invasion. Yet, while they looked outward for enemies, their closest allies were actively laying a trap right under their feet.

Theologically, this reveals:

• The Limits of Human Wisdom: Edom’s vaunted sages could not discern the trap. Human intellect is utterly blind to danger when hardened by pride.

• The Deceptiveness of False Alliances: Alliances built on mutual self-interest rather than covenant faithfulness to God are inherently unstable. The very people Edom trusted became the instruments of their destruction.

• The Irony of Divine Justice: Those who helped plunder Israel (Edom) are plundered by their own confederates. The trap they stood upon was ultimately set under the sovereign allowance of God to humble their pride.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:7, the layout of the betrayal is progressive and devastating. The verse describes three groups: "the men in your confederacy," "the men at peace with you," and "those who eat your bread." In ancient Near Eastern culture, sharing bread was the ultimate seal of covenant friendship, symbolizing mutual protection and loyalty. To eat bread with someone and simultaneously lay (śûm) a trap under them was the ultimate expression of treachery. The placement of the trap directly beneath Edom suggests that the threat was not approaching from the horizon; it was already positioned underneath them, integrated into their daily life and security. Edom was standing on their own ruin without knowing it.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:8 - "The LORD God planted a garden eastward in Eden, and there He put the man whom He had formed." (Demonstrates God's sovereign placement of humanity in a secure, nurtured environment).

• Genesis 22:9 - "...and he bound Isaac his son and laid him on the altar, upon the wood." (Illustrates the solemn, deliberate physical placement of a sacrifice).

• Job 40:4 - "Behold, I am vile; What shall I answer You? I lay my hand over my mouth." (Depicts absolute silence and submission in the presence of the Almighty).

• Isaiah 51:23 - "...And you have laid your back like the ground, And as the street to those who walk over." (Describes the physical posture of utter humiliation and defeat under oppressors).

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a profound parallel between the betrayal of Edom in Obadiah 1:7 and the betrayal of Jesus Christ. Psalm 41:9 states, "Even my close friend in whom I trusted, who ate my bread, has lifted up his heel against me." Jesus quotes this in John 13:18 to describe the betrayal of Judas Iscariot. While Edom’s betrayal led to their deserved destruction due to pride, Christ willingly walked into the trap laid by His companion to secure salvation for His people.
