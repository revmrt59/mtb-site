BROUGHT - ( shalach , Strong's H7971 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb shalach fundamentally means to send, stretch out, or dismiss, carrying a wide range of actions from sending a messenger to driving someone away in expulsion. In the context of Obadiah 1:7, it describes the treacherous betrayal of Edom by their political allies, who "brought" or escorted them to the border, effectively expelling them from their own territory under the guise of diplomatic escort. This word underscores the irony of Edom's misplaced trust; those they counted on for security became the very instruments of their banishment.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete word picture behind shalach is the extension of the hand or the launching of an object, such as an arrow or spear, to make it go to a distant destination. It represents the active motion of directing something or someone away from oneself to fulfill a specific purpose. In ancient Near Eastern culture, sending messengers or representatives was a key aspect of covenant relationships and international diplomacy. To send someone could indicate authority, but in the passive or intensive stems, it often means to forcibly expel, cast out, or escort someone out of a territory.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

In its basic Qal stem, shalach means to stretch out (as a hand), to send (a messenger, message, or agent), or to let go. In the Piel stem, which is the intensive form used in Obadiah 1:7 (shillechu-kha), the meaning intensifies to sending away, dismissing, casting out, letting loose, or expelling. In this specific prophetic context, the Piel verb denotes a forced or deceptive dismissal. The allies did not merely accompany Edom; they escorted or sent them off to the border, actively pushing them out of their land and leaving them vulnerable.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The verb shalach is used in several vivid metaphorical and literal ways throughout the Old Testament:

  • Extension of Power or Judgment: God stretches out His hand to bring plagues, judgments, or deliverance upon a nation.

  • Dismissal or Divorce: The sending away of a wife in divorce or the freeing of a Hebrew slave.

  • Forcible Expulsion: Driving out nations from the Promised Land, or the expulsion of humanity from sacred space.

  • Deceptive Escort: Escorting a former ally to the border under the pretense of friendship, only to lock them out.

4. Theological Implications

  • The Folly of Misplaced Trust: Edom trusted in their political alliances and their geographical security. God uses these very allies to execute His judgment. The allies sending Edom to the border illustrates that human alliances apart from God are fleeting and ultimately destructive.

  • The Sovereignty of God: Though the earthly agents of Edom's expulsion were their duplicitous allies, the ultimate sender was Yahweh. God uses the natural consequences of political treachery to accomplish His divine decree of judgment against pride.

  • Poetic Justice: Edom rejoiced when Judah was sent into exile and captured. In perfect poetic justice, Edom is sent out and expelled from their own borders by their closest friends.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:7, the phrase "have brought you to the border" (ad-haggebul shillechukha) describes a process of polite but firm expulsion. The allies did not openly attack Edom at first. Instead, they acted as friends, escorting them to the border under the pretense of alliance or assistance, only to hand them over to enemies or lock them out of their mountainous stronghold. The nuance of the Piel stem here emphasizes the thoroughness of the displacement. Edom was completely escorted out; there was no turning back. Their trusted partners practiced total deception, turning a diplomatic mission into a deportation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 3:23: "therefore the LORD God sent him out of the garden of Eden to till the ground from which he was taken."

  • Exodus 5:1: "Afterward Moses and Aaron went in and told Pharaoh, 'Thus says the LORD God of Israel: "Let My people go, that they may hold a feast to Me in the wilderness."'"

  • Isaiah 6:8: "Also I heard the voice of the Lord, saying: 'Whom shall I send, And who will go for Us?' Then I said, 'Here am I! Send me.'"

  • Jeremiah 1:7: "But the LORD said to me: 'Do not say, "I am a youth," For you shall go to all to whom I send you, And whatever I command you, you shall speak.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the ancient Near East, treaties required mutual defense and loyalty. By sending Edom to the border, the allies violated the sacred covenant of peace. This historical reality deepens the emotional weight of Obadiah's prophecy. Additionally, the borders Edom sought to expand at Judah's expense became the very limits to which they were forcibly escorted and expelled.
