UNDERSTANDING - ( tebunah , Strong's H8394 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun tebunah refers to the capacity for discernment, insight, and analytical understanding. Derived from a root meaning "to distinguish" or "between," it denotes the intellectual and spiritual ability to perceive the true nature of things, make wise decisions, and navigate life's complexities. In the context of Obadiah 1:7, it describes the sudden, catastrophic loss of political discernment and wisdom among the Edomites, who prided themselves on their wisdom but were utterly blinded and deceived by their closest allies.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

  • The root of tebunah is bin, which fundamentally means to perceive, to discern, or to distinguish between.

  • The word picture represents the ability to separate options, weigh competing facts, and discern truth from falsehood. It is not merely intellectual capacity but a practical, moral, and spiritual skill. It suggests a mental sorting process where one can see "between" things to grasp their true relationship and consequences.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • The noun tebunah refers to the faculty of understanding, intelligence, insight, and discernment.

  • It denotes the act of understanding, the object of understanding (knowledge or skill), or the personification of wisdom itself.

  • In practical terms, it describes the skill of a craftsman, the prudence of a leader, or the spiritual discernment of a righteous person who understands God's ways.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

  • God's Creative Power: Tebunah is used to describe the infinite skill and understanding by which God established the heavens and the earth. It represents the architectural precision of the Creator.

  • Wisdom Literature: In the book of Proverbs, tebunah is frequently paired with chokmah (wisdom) and da'at (knowledge). It acts as a moral guardian, keeping the upright from stumbling into evil.

  • The Blindness of Fools: A fool is defined by a lack of tebunah, preferring to express his own opinion rather than seek objective truth.

4. Theological Implications

  • True understanding is not merely an intellectual achievement; it is a moral attribute that has its source in God.

  • Human wisdom and strategic brilliance are ultimately futile when divorced from God. When a nation or individual walks in pride, God's judgment often manifests as a withdrawal of tebunah, leading to self-destruction through blind folly.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

  • In Obadiah 1:7, tebunah is used to expose the total collapse of Edom's legendary wisdom. Edom, specifically Teman, was famous in the ancient Near East for its wise men and political sagacity.

  • The text states, "No one has understanding in him." Edom's leaders, despite their reputation for shrewdness, fail to see that their own political allies and close friends are actively plotting their destruction.

  • Their pride blinded them to the obvious treachery around them. God used this blindness as a mechanism of judgment, proving that when He determines to bring down a proud nation, He first deprives them of their discernment.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Proverbs 3:19: "The LORD by wisdom founded the earth; By understanding He established the heavens;"

  • Proverbs 18:2: "A fool has no delight in understanding, But in expressing his own heart."

  • Psalm 147:5: "Great is our Lord, and mighty in power; His understanding is infinite."

  • Proverbs 2:11: "Discretion will preserve you; Understanding will keep you,"

  • Proverbs 20:5: "Counsel in the heart of man is like deep water, But a man of understanding will draw it out."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • The contrast between Edom's historical reputation and their sudden ruin is a major theme in prophetic literature. Jeremiah 49:7 asks, "Is wisdom no more in Teman? Has counsel perished from the prudent? Has their wisdom vanished?" Obadiah 1:7-8 answers this with a resounding yes: on that day, God will destroy the wise men from Edom and tebunah from the mount of Esau.

  • This serves as a sobering warning to modern believers and nations alike: relying on intellectual prowess, military alliances, or economic security while ignoring God leads to spiritual blindness and inevitable collapse.
