LAY - (sûwm, Strong's H7760)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb sum (also written sim) carries the fundamental meaning of "to put, place, set, establish, or appoint." Its semantic range extends from the concrete physical act of laying an object in a specific location to the abstract or relational act of appointing a leader, establishing a condition, or setting an ambush. In Obadiah 1:7 ("Those who eat your bread have laid a trap for you"), sum refers to the deliberate, calculated laying or setting of a snare by Edom's trusted confederates. The word highlights the intentional treachery of allies whom Edom trusted, contributing to the prophet's argument that human alliances offer no security against divine judgment and that Edom's misplaced confidence would result in total betrayal.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The root sum is a primary Hebrew verb used throughout the Old Testament to denote purposeful placement or establishment. In the culture and worldview of the ancient Near East, putting or setting something in place was rarely viewed as an accidental or passive occurrence. Setting a table, placing a seal upon a document, establishing a boundary stone, appointing an official, or laying a snare all represented conscious, goal-oriented action.

In the Book of Obadiah, the prophet delivers a oracle of judgment against the nation of Edom. Edom had gloated over the calamity of Judah and participated in its ruin. Confident in its lofty, rock-hewn strongholds and strategic international alliances, Edom considered itself invulnerable. In Obadiah 1:7, the prophet exposes the vanity of Edom's trust: the very confederates with whom Edom made covenants and shared meals were actively setting (sum) a net or trap beneath them.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, sum denotes the act of bringing a person, object, or state into a designated position or relation. Standard Old Testament lexicons categorize its primary definitions into several core concepts:

  • Spatial/Physical: To place, put, or set down an object (e.g., placing food on a table, putting a ring on a finger, or laying hands upon someone).

  • Positional/Functional: To appoint, constitute, or set someone into a role or office (e.g., setting a king over a nation or appointing taskmasters).

  • Situational/Relational: To make or transform something into a specific condition (e.g., making a city a ruin, establishing peace, or setting a trap/ambush).

In Obadiah 1:7, the verb operates in this situational and conspiratorial sense, describing the covert setting of a snare or trap (mazor) beneath an unsuspecting partner.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament literature, sum is employed to express intentionality. Because the verb requires an agent who acts purposefully, its presence points directly to the deliberate intent of the subject.

When God is the subject, sum conveys sovereign ordination—such as establishing His name in Jerusalem, placing words in the mouth of a prophet, or bestowing peace upon His people. When human subjects act righteously, sum describes order, worship, and faithful governance. However, when hostile or deceitful actors are the subject, sum depicts calculated schemes, such as setting an ambush, laying a trap, or positioning troops for battle. The nuance of sum in contexts of conflict is not merely that something exists, but that it was deliberately placed there to achieve a specific outcome.

4. Theological Implications

The usage of sum in Obadiah 1:7 carries significant theological weight regarding divine justice and human self-reliance:

  • The Irony of Divine Retribution: Edom mistreated its brother nation Jacob, seeking to profit from Judah's downfall. In Yahweh's righteous judgment, Edom suffers a corresponding fate: Edom's own allies set (sum) a trap for Edom. The text presents human betrayal as an instrument within God's overarching judgment against pride.

  • The Folly of False Trust: Human covenants and political alliances apart from God are inherently unstable. Edom trusted in human diplomacy and mutual treaties ("men of your alliance," "those who eat your bread"), yet those very partners engineered Edom's downfall.

  • Sovereign Placement versus Human Schemes: While human actors set traps in secret, Yahweh remains the ultimate Lord over human history who exposes hidden schemes and executes justice.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

The immediate context of Obadiah 1:7 multiplies the impact of the verb sum:

  • Relational Context: The verse builds a climax of intimacy—"the men of your confederacy," "the men at peace with you," and "those who eat your bread." In ancient Near Eastern culture, sharing bread sealed a covenant of peace and mutual protection.

  • The Contrast: It is precisely these close companions who "have laid (sum) a trap under you." The placement of the trap was not performed by an open enemy on a battlefield, but covertly engineered under the table of fellowship.

  • Strategic Blindness: The clause concludes, "no understanding in him." Edom's legendary wisdom and political shrewdness failed entirely to detect what its allies had set (sum) beneath its feet. The verb underscores the subtlety of the betrayal and the total failure of human wisdom under divine judgment.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 45:8 – "So now it was not you who sent me here, but God; and He has made me a father to Pharaoh, and lord of all his house." Here sum describes God's sovereign action in placing and appointing Joseph into a position of high authority in Egypt.

  • Exodus 4:15 – "You shall speak to him and put words in his mouth; and I will be with your mouth and with his mouth, and I will teach you what you shall do." The verb depicts the direct placement of divine messages into the mouth of God's chosen spokesman.

  • Numbers 6:26 – "The LORD lift up His countenance upon you, and give you peace." In the Aaronic blessing, sum refers to the bestowal and establishing of Yahweh's peace upon His covenant people.

  • Psalms 89:25 – "I will set his hand also over the sea, and his right hand over the rivers." The verb emphasizes God's sovereign establishment of the Davidic king's authority and territorial dominion.

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Table Fellowship and Treachery: Sharing bread in biblical thought represented a binding bond of loyalty. The act of laying (sum) a trap while sharing bread mirrors the deep grief of covenant betrayal expressed elsewhere in the Old Testament wisdom literature, where intimate friends turn into covert adversaries.

  • Reversal of Wisdom: Edom was famous throughout the ancient world for its wise men and strategic counsel. However, when Yahweh decrees judgment, human wisdom is blinded to the very traps set (sum) in its immediate presence, demonstrating that human intellect cannot bypass God's moral governance of the world.
