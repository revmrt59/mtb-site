WISE - ( chakam , Strong's H2450 )

Summary:

In Obadiah 1:8, "wise" (chakam) refers to the internationally renowned sages and counselors of Edom, particularly Teman, who were celebrated for their practical intellect, political sagacity, and strategic foresight. In the ancient Near East, wisdom was not merely abstract philosophy but the practical skill of navigating life, politics, and warfare successfully. Edom took immense pride in this intellectual heritage, believing their counselors could outmaneuver any threat. God’s judgment, however, exposes the limitations of human wisdom apart from moral and spiritual alignment with Him. On the day of judgment, God promises to utterly confound and destroy Edom's wise men, proving that human intellect is powerless against divine decree.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The root chakam is deeply tied to the ancient Near Eastern concept of skill, mastery, and practical competence. Originally, the term referred to the physical skill of a craftsman, a metalworker, a sailor, or a professional mourner—anyone who possessed specialized knowledge and the ability to execute a task successfully.

When applied to the intellectual and political spheres, chakam came to describe counselors, diplomats, and statesmen who advised kings and guided nations. Edom, and specifically its region of Teman, was famous throughout the ancient world for producing such elite minds. They were the premier geopolitical strategists of their day. The word picture is one of a highly trained, deeply experienced class of intellectuals who built a reputation for impenetrable defense and peerless counsel, only to have their masterfully laid plans instantly dissolved by God's sovereign hand.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The adjective chakam means wise, skillful, clever, or learned. It describes a person who possesses practical wisdom, ethical discernment, or specialized technical capabilities. The semantic range of the word includes:

• Practical, artistic skill in craftsmanship or technical execution.

• Political shrewdness, diplomatic competence, and administrative capability.

• Ethical and religious wisdom characterized by the fear of the Lord and adherence to moral order.

• The worldly, secular cunning of advisors, magicians, and strategists.

In the specific context of Obadiah 1:8, chakam is used substantively ("the wise [men]") to denote the political and military strategists of Edom whose intellect served as the nation's ultimate security blanket.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, chakam appears in various dimensions that enrich its meaning:

• The Master Craftsman: Those who built the Tabernacle and its furnishings were described as having chakam hearts, meaning they possessed the practical, hands-on capability to execute God's designs flawlessly.

• The Political Advisor: Pharaoh and the kings of Assyria relied heavily on their chakam counselors to navigate geopolitical crises and maintain imperial power.

• The Moral Sage of Wisdom Literature: In Proverbs, the chakam is the individual who hears instruction, fears the Lord, and actively avoids the path of folly.

The usage in Obadiah 1:8 stands in stark, ironic contrast to these positive descriptions. Here, the very people who excelled in navigating the horizontal affairs of earth are rendered utterly foolish because they ignored the vertical reality of God's sovereignty. Their "skill" becomes their snare.

### 4. Theological Implications

This text illustrates the theological truth that human wisdom, no matter how highly regarded or historically proven, is entirely inadequate when detached from the fear of God. Edom's wisdom was secular, proud, and self-serving. They watched their brother Jacob fall and gloated, believing their secure mountain strongholds and intellectual superiority made them invulnerable.

Scripture consistently warns that God "catches the wise in their own craftiness." True wisdom begins with the fear of Yahweh. When a nation or an individual relies on intellectual capacity while rebelling against God, that wisdom is exposed as foolishness. Divine judgment acts as a solvent that dissolves human ingenuity.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:8, the destruction of the "wise men" occurs in tandem with the destruction of "understanding from the mount of Esau." By targeting Edom’s chakam, God is systematically dismantling the pillars of Edom’s national confidence.

The Edomites trusted in two main assets: their natural geography (the rocky, impenetrable cliffs of Petra) and their human resources (their brilliant advisors). By destroying the wise men, God deprives the nation of its leadership, leaving them blind, confused, and helpless in the face of disaster. The judgment is highly ironical: the "experts" will have no answers when God acts.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Deuteronomy 4:6 - "Therefore be careful to observe them; for this is your wisdom and your understanding in the sight of the peoples who will hear all these statutes, and say, 'Surely this great nation is a wise and understanding people.'"

• Proverbs 13:20 - "He who walks with wise men will be wise, But the companion of fools will be destroyed."

• Jeremiah 9:23 - "Thus says the LORD: 'Let not the wise man glory in his wisdom, Let not the mighty man glory in his might, Nor let the rich man glory in his riches;'"

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• Historical Fulfillment: Edom was eventually overrun by the Nabataeans. Despite their highly praised intellectual resources, their wise counsel failed to anticipate or prevent their displacement, demonstrating the historical accuracy of Obadiah’s prophecy.

• Pastoral Application: Leaders and believers today must beware of the "Edomite trap"—relying on strategic planning, intellectual credentials, and analytical expertise while neglecting deep prayer, moral integrity, and radical obedience to the Word of God. Strategy without spirituality leads to ruin.
