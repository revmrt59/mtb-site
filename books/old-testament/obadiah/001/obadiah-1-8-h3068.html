LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh is the personal, covenant-keeping name of the God of Israel, denoting His eternal, self-existent, and unchangeable nature. Revealed most fully to Moses at the burning bush, it signifies God's active, faithful presence with His people and His sovereign authority over all of creation. In Obadiah 1:8, this sacred name underscores the absolute certainty of divine judgment against Edom, assuring God's covenant people that their sovereign, promise-keeping Lord is the one executing perfect justice in human history.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The name Yahweh is the tetragrammaton—the sacred four-letter name of God (YHWH) in the Hebrew Bible. Out of deep reverence and fear of taking this name in vain, ancient Jewish readers avoided pronouncing it aloud, substituting the title Adonai ("Lord") in its place. The name is deeply connected to the Hebrew verb hayah, which means "to be" or "to exist." When God revealed this name to Moses in Exodus 3, He declared, "I AM WHO I AM." This presents God not as an abstract philosophical concept, but as a personal, active, and relational Being who enters into binding covenants with His people, initiating redemption and guaranteeing His promises by His very existence.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Yahweh refers to the self-existent, eternal, and unchangeable God. The name carries several core lexical definitions:

• The Self-Existent One: God depends on nothing outside of Himself for His existence, power, or counsel. He is the uncaused Cause of all things.

• The Sovereign Creator: He is the one who causes all things to be, sustaining the universe by His power.

• The Covenant Keeper: The name represents His absolute faithfulness. Because He is the eternal "I AM," He remains completely consistent in His character, His covenants, and His decrees across all generations.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, Yahweh is the specific name used when God acts in grace, deliverance, and intimate relationship with Israel, contrasting with Elohim, which emphasizes His role as the powerful Creator of the universe.

• When God makes covenants, He signs them with the name Yahweh.

• The name is frequently joined with descriptive terms to form compound names that illustrate His active care for His people, such as Yahweh Yireh ("The LORD will provide") or Yahweh Nissi ("The LORD is my banner").

• It serves as a verbal fortress for the righteous, representing safety, ultimate authority, and the ultimate courtroom of cosmic justice.

### 4. Theological Implications

• Absolute Sovereignty: Yahweh is the supreme King who rules over history. No human nation or ruler can thwart His purposes.

• Covenant Security: His commitments to Israel are unbreakable because they are tied to His own name. To break His promise would be to deny His own existence.

• Holiness and Judgment: While Yahweh is compassionate and gracious, His holiness requires Him to judge sin. His judgment on pagan nations is an exercise of His righteous rule, vindicating His people and demonstrating His exclusive claim to deity.

• Exclusive Monotheism: The name establishes Him as the only living God, rendering the idols of the nations utterly impotent and non-existent.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:8, the phrase "says the LORD" (neum Yahweh) functions as a divine signature of absolute authority and certainty. Edom (the descendants of Esau) boasted in their seemingly impregnable mountain strongholds and their renowned worldly wisdom. By invoking the name Yahweh, the prophet contrasts the fleeting, fragile wisdom of Edom with the sovereign, omniscient decree of the Creator. It is Yahweh Himself—the covenant God of Israel—who will personally dismantle, expose, and destroy Edom's wisdom. The use of this name in a prophecy of judgment reassures the suffering Judeans that their covenant God has not forgotten them and remains fully in control of their vindication.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 3:15 - "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

• Psalm 23:1 - "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

• Isaiah 42:8 - "I am the LORD, that is My name; And My glory I will not give to another, Nor My praise to carved images."

• Malachi 3:6 - "For I am the LORD, I do not change; Therefore you are not consumed, O sons of Jacob."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• In the Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament), Yahweh was translated using the Greek word Kyrios ("Lord").

• The New Testament writers directly apply this title Kyrios to Jesus Christ (e.g., Philippians 2:11), thereby declaring that Jesus is indeed Yahweh manifested in human flesh.

• In most English translations, including the NKJV, the use of all capital letters ("LORD") is a specific typographic convention alerting the reader that the original Hebrew text employs the sacred name Yahweh.
