SAYS - ( ne'um , Strong's H5002 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word ne'um represents a solemn, authoritative declaration, oracle, or utterance, almost exclusively used for divine speech. Unlike common verbs for speaking, ne'um is a passive noun that carries the weight of a formal prophetic decree, signaling that the message originates directly from the mouth of God and carries absolute, unalterable certainty. In Obadiah 1:8, it anchors the terrifying promise of Edom's impending judgment in the sovereign, unbreakable decree of Yahweh.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• ne'um is derived from a root meaning to whisper, groan, or utter a low sound. This root suggests an intimate, whispered revelation of God to His prophets, which is then spoken to the world with absolute authority.

• It is not the standard Hebrew verb for speaking (amar or dabar). Instead, it functions as a noun formula—literally "the utterance of"—marking a transition to or climax of divine revelation.

• Historically, this word is used in prophetic formulas to distinguish the prophet's personal thoughts from the sovereign, objective word of God. It acts as a divine seal of authenticity.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The word ne'um means an utterance, declaration, oracle, or whisper. It is used over 370 times in the Old Testament, predominantly in the prophetic books as part of the formula ne'um YHWH ("declaration of Yahweh" or "says the LORD").

• It signifies a formal, binding pronouncement that is divinely inspired, carries absolute authority, and cannot be annulled.

• It is a noun in the construct state, meaning the phrase "says the LORD" is more literally translated as "the decree of the LORD."

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• ne'um is typically positioned at the end or in the middle of a divine pronouncement to seal the message, much like an official signet ring or signature at the bottom of a royal decree.

• It conveys the picture of a royal herald announcing the unalterable decree of a king. When the herald speaks, the words are not his own; they carry the full legal and executive weight of the throne.

• Occasionally, the word is used of human oracles, such as the oracle of Balaam (Numbers 24:3) or the oracle of David (2 Samuel 23:1), where the human speaker is moved by a divine force, emphasizing that the source of the message is beyond human intellect.

4. Theological Implications

• Sovereignty and Authority: Because ne'um is almost exclusively associated with Yahweh, it underscores God's supreme authority over human history. What He decrees is guaranteed to happen.

• Infallibility of Prophecy: The use of ne'um guarantees that the prophecy is not a mere prediction or educated guess, but an unshakeable reality guaranteed by the character of God.

• The Distinction Between Creator and Creation: It reminds the hearer that God's thoughts are not man's thoughts; His decrees are external, objective, and authoritative standards to which all humanity is subject.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Obadiah 1:8, the word ne'um is placed in the context of God's sudden, devastating judgment on Edom. Edom was proud, relying on their strategic high strongholds and their famous "wise men."

• By inserting "says the LORD" (ne'um YHWH) in the middle of this rhetorical question ("Will I not in that day..."), God shatters Edom's security. It is not Obadiah the prophet threatening Edom; it is Yahweh Himself issuing a formal, irreversible decree of destruction.

• Edom's wisdom and security cannot stand against the authoritative decree of the sovereign Creator.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 22:16: "and said: 'By Myself I have sworn, says the LORD, because you have done this thing, and have not withheld your son, your only son—'"

• Jeremiah 31:31: "'Behold, the days are coming, says the LORD, when I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah—'"

• Jeremiah 23:24: "'Can anyone hide himself in secret places, So I shall not see him?' says the LORD; 'Do I not fill heaven and earth?' says the LORD."

• Zechariah 12:4: "'In that day,' says the LORD, 'I will strike every horse with confusion, and its rider with madness; I will open My eyes on the house of Judah, and will strike every horse of the peoples with blindness.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• In the Septuagint (the Greek translation of the Old Testament), ne'um YHWH is frequently translated as legei kyrios ("says the Lord").

• This is the exact phrase used by the apostle Paul in Romans 12:19 ("'Vengeance is Mine, I will repay,' says the Lord") and in 2 Corinthians 6:17. This connects the absolute, binding nature of Old Testament divine decrees directly to the authority of the New Testament scriptures, demonstrating the unified biblical theology of divine speech.
