ESAU - ( Esaw , Strong's H6215 )

Summary:

The name Esau represents the twin brother of Jacob, the firstborn son of Isaac and Rebekah, whose name carries the meaning of "hairy" or "rough." In the prophetic context of Obadiah, Esau functions metonymically for the nation of Edom—his descendants—and the rugged mountainous region they inhabited. In Obadiah 1:8, "the mount of Esau" refers to the high, secure mountain strongholds of Mount Seir, symbolizing Edom's worldly wisdom, political sagacity, pride, and false sense of security, all of which God promises to bring to complete ruin under His sovereign judgment.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The name Esaw is deeply linked to the physical features of Isaac's firstborn son. At birth, he emerged red and covered with hair like a garment, prompting his parents to name him Esau (Genesis 25:25).

• The physical characteristics of Esau foreshadowed the geography of his descendants. The Edomites settled in Mount Seir, a rugged, elevated, and mountainous region south of the Dead Sea. This territory is characterized by steep red sandstone cliffs, narrow, easily defensible gorges, and high plateau fortresses.

• The physical ruggedness of this homeland mirrored the character of Esau and his descendants. They were an independent, hostile, and self-sufficient people who felt completely secure from foreign invasion due to their natural mountain defenses. Over time, "Esau" became synonymous with this high, mountainous terrain and the proud nation that ruled it.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The proper noun Esaw refers to the historical patriarch Esau, the brother of Jacob.

• Lexically, the term extends beyond the individual to represent the entire ethnic group of his descendants, the Edomites, as well as the specific territory they inhabited (Deuteronomy 2:5).

• In poetic and prophetic literature, the word is frequently joined in construct states, such as "the house of Esau" (representing the lineage or nation) and "the mount of Esau" or "mountains of Esau" (representing the high-altitude territory of Mount Seir).

• The designation "mount of Esau" (har Esaw) signifies not just a single peak, but the entire mountainous range that formed the defensive backbone and pride of the Edomite kingdom.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• "The mount of Esau" serves as a vivid word picture for human pride, loftiness, and impenetrable security. The Edomites literally lived in the "clefts of the rock" (Obadiah 1:3), looking down upon their neighbors with disdain.

• In prophetic imagery, the mount of Esau stands in stark, theological contrast to Mount Zion. While Mount Zion represents the humble hill of God's grace, election, and ultimate deliverance, the mount of Esau represents human achievement, physical strength, and self-glorification.

• The pairing of "understanding" with the "mount of Esau" in Obadiah 1:8 highlights another key word picture. The region of Edom was historically famous for its wise men, counselors, and intellectual traditions (such as those associated with Teman). The mount of Esau thus represents the pinnacle of worldly wisdom and strategic planning, which is ultimately futile against God.

4. Theological Implications

• The Illusion of Self-Sufficiency: The primary sin of Edom was pride rooted in their geography. By trusting in the natural fortifications of the mount of Esau, they believed they were beyond the reach of divine judgment. This teaches the theological truth that no human fortress can protect against the hand of God.

• Sovereign Judgment and Election: The division between Jacob and Esau begins in the womb and stretches into eternity. Esau represents the profane, worldly man who despises his spiritual birthright for temporary, physical gratification (Hebrews 12:16). The destruction of the mount of Esau demonstrates that God's covenant promises to Jacob will stand, while those who rely on fleshly heritage will fail.

• The Overthrow of Worldly Wisdom: God's judgment on the wise men of Esau demonstrates that human intellect, when divorced from the fear of the Lord, is foolishness. God confounds the wisdom of the wise to show that true understanding belongs to Him alone.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Obadiah 1:8, "the mount of Esau" is placed in parallel construction with "Edom." This parallelism emphasizes that the judgment is total, affecting both the people ("Edom") and their highly valued geographic strongholds ("mount of Esau").

• The phrase "destroy the wise men from Edom, and understanding from the mount of Esau" targets the intellectual pride of the nation. Edom believed their political alliances and strategic brilliance would save them from disaster.

• By specifying that "understanding" will be destroyed from the mount of Esau, the text stresses that their high-altitude isolation will not preserve their clarity of thought; when God judges a nation, He first deprives its leaders of wisdom and sound judgment.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 36:8: "So Esau dwelt in Mount Seir. Esau is Edom."

• Malachi 1:3: "But Esau I have hated, And laid waste his mountains and his heritage For the jackals of the wilderness."

• Obadiah 1:18: "The house of Jacob shall be a fire, And the house of Joseph a flame; But the house of Esau shall be stubble; They shall kindle them and devour them, And no survivor shall remain of the house of Esau,” For the LORD has spoken."

• Obadiah 1:21: "Then saviors shall come to Mount Zion To judge the mountains of Esau, And the kingdom shall be the LORD’s."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The geographic region once known as the mount of Esau corresponds to the rugged southern desert highlands of modern Jordan. This area includes the famous archaeological site of Petra, where buildings were carved directly into the red sandstone cliffs. This stunning backdrop perfectly illustrates the biblical descriptions of Esau's dwelling places.

• The spiritual tragedy of Esau is that his descendants inherited his worst spiritual traits. Just as Esau acted out of immediate, fleshly impulses and treated his brother Jacob with contempt, his descendants throughout history repeatedly sought to exploit and destroy Israel during times of national crisis.
