UNDERSTANDING - ( tebunah , Strong's H8394 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word tebunah refers to understanding, discernment, and intellectual or administrative skill. Derived from the root bin, which means to separate or distinguish, it describes the mental capacity to analyze a situation, see through its complexities, and make wise, practical decisions. In Obadiah 1:8, it represents the strategic insight, political sagacity, and intellectual superiority for which Edom was famous—a human wisdom that God promises to utterly dismantle in judgment.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The noun tebunah is closely tied to the verbal root bin, which fundamentally means to separate, distinguish, or perceive differences. The physical image behind this root is the act of looking between things to discern their distinct parts. Therefore, tebunah represents analytical insight—the capability to look at a complex matter, distinguish right from wrong, order from chaos, and truth from falsehood, and then act accordingly. It is not passive knowledge, but an active, structuring wisdom that designs, plans, and builds.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Tebunah refers to the faculty of understanding, intelligence, skill, or discernment. It encompasses several shades of meaning:

• The cognitive capacity to understand and act with insight.

• The skill or craftsmanship required to build, organize, or rule.

• Political or strategic wisdom and prudence in administration.

• The ethical discernment to choose the path of righteousness over folly.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

• In the Old Testament, tebunah is frequently personified alongside chokmah (wisdom) and binah (understanding) in the book of Proverbs, where it cries out in the streets to guide humanity.

• It is used to describe the craftsmanship of Bezalel, who was filled with the Spirit of God in "understanding" (tebunah) to design the Tabernacle.

• It describes God's creative masterwork, as He established the heavens by His "understanding" (tebunah).

• In a military and political context, it refers to the clever, strategic planning of nations, which proves useless when confronted by God's sovereignty.

### 4. Theological Implications

True tebunah originates in God and is a gift from Him. It is part of His divine character, reflected in the order and design of creation. When human beings possess tebunah, it is meant to lead them to fear the Lord and walk in obedience. However, when human understanding becomes self-reliant and detached from God, it degenerates into pride. Scripture warns that there is no counsel, wisdom, or understanding (tebunah) that can succeed against the Lord. God routinely frustrates and exposes the bankruptcy of autonomous human intellect to demonstrate that He alone is the source of true insight.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:8, the prophet pronounces judgment on Edom (the descendants of Esau). Edom was renowned in the ancient Near East for its wisdom, particularly the city of Teman, which was famous for producing deep thinkers, counselors, and strategists. The rugged, mountainous terrain of Mount Esau gave the Edomites a false sense of security, believing their physical fortresses and intellectual prowess made them invincible.

God's judgment is targeted specifically at these two pillars of Edomite pride: their wise men and their tebunah (understanding). By destroying their understanding, God blinds their leaders, rendering them incapable of making sound political or military decisions. Their celebrated intellectual superiority becomes their downfall, proving that human discernment apart from God cannot withstand divine judgment.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 31:3: "And I have filled him with the Spirit of God, in wisdom, in understanding, in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship,"

• Psalm 147:5: "Great is our Lord, and mighty in power; His understanding is infinite."

• Proverbs 2:6: "For the LORD gives wisdom; From His mouth come knowledge and understanding;"

• Proverbs 3:19: "The LORD by wisdom founded the earth; By understanding He established the heavens;"

• Proverbs 18:2: "A fool has no delight in understanding, But in expressing his own heart."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between Mount Esau and Mount Zion is central to Obadiah. Mount Esau represents human pride, physical height, and self-sufficient "understanding" (tebunah). Mount Zion represents the dwelling place of God's presence and His sovereign rule. The loss of tebunah is one of the most severe judgments God can bring upon a society or leadership. When God removes discernment, leaders make foolish choices that lead to their own destruction, illustrating that intellectual capacity is a gift of common grace that God can withdraw at any moment.
