SAYS - (nᵉʼum, Strong's H5002)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun neʾum denotes an authoritative declaration, oracle, or solemn utterance. In prophetic discourse, it functions primarily as a technical term identifying the divine source of a message, typically appearing in construct with the divine name as "declaration of Yahweh" (rendered in the NKJV as "says the LORD"). Lexically, the term itself simply designates a spoken oracle or solemn statement rather than an intrinsic concept of unalterable fate. In Obadiah 1:8, neʾum frames God's rhetorical announcement regarding the dismantling of Edom's famous human wisdom, explicitly attributing the coming judgment to Yahweh Himself as the sovereign speaker.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The noun neʾum originates in ancient West Semitic speech patterns where formal, authoritative announcements were introduced by specifying the speaker's authority. Within the Old Testament, neʾum functions as a specialized formulaic noun used almost exclusively to introduce or close divine oracles. Unlike general verbs of speaking, neʾum functions as a solemn attribution, identifying the words spoken as an inspired announcement or oracle. While human figures rarely use it to mark inspired utterances (such as Balaam in Numbers 24:3 or David in 2 Samuel 23:1), its predominant context across the prophetic books is to signal the direct speech of God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, neʾum signifies an "utterance," "oracle," "declaration," or "announcement." Standard Hebrew lexicons (such as BDB and TWOT) define the term as a masculine noun designating a spoken declaration. It is most frequently bound in the construct state to a following proper noun, primarily the divine name (neʾum YHWH), forming the familiar prophetic phrase "declaration of Yahweh" or "says the LORD."

The word itself simply denotes the act of solemn utterance or the content of an oracle; it does not inherently carry theological concepts such as unalterable destiny, inherent irrevocability, or automatic execution. Whatever authority, certainty, or weight the oracle possesses derives from the speaker (YHWH) and the nature of the specific message delivered, rather than from the lexical definition of the noun itself.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament prophetic literature, neʾum serves as a structural and rhetorical marker. Prophets frequently insert neʾum YHWH in the middle of poetic lines or at the conclusion of a oracle stanza. This technique interrupts the flow of human poetic address to remind the audience of the divine origin of the proclamation.

Grammatically, in Obadiah 1:8, the noun is masculine singular in the construct state (neʾum), directly governing the proper noun Yahweh (YHWH). This construct relationship specifies that the utterance belongs to Yahweh. The construct form functions syntactically as an adverbial or parenthetical clause ("is the declaration of Yahweh"), grounding the speech directly in the divine persona.

4. Theological Implications

The theological significance of neʾum lies in the doctrine of divine revelation and sovereign governance:

  • Attribution of Revelation: By appending neʾum to prophetic statements, the biblical text distinguishes divine speech from human speculation or political commentary. The prophet speaks not out of personal malice or nationalistic pride, but as a messenger conveying Yahweh's actual word.

  • Universal Sovereignty: When used in prophecies concerning foreign nations, as in Obadiah, neʾum asserts that Yahweh's authority extends far beyond the borders of Israel. He issues binding declarations over non-covenant nations such as Edom.

  • Grounding for Faith and Fear: For God's people, the phrase neʾum YHWH reassures them that promises and warnings alike originate in the character and power of the Creator.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:8, the prophet addresses the proud nation of Edom. Historically, Edom was famous for its natural mountain defenses and its reputation for wisdom (particularly associated with the region of Teman). Edom felt secure in its strategic position and intellectual self-sufficiency.

Obadiah 1:8 states: "'Will I not in that day,' says the LORD, 'Even destroy the wise men from Edom, and understanding from the mountains of Esau?'"

The insertion of neʾum YHWH ("says the LORD") right after the temporal phrase "in that day" serves a crucial contextual purpose:

1. Divine Initiative vs. Human Wisdom: Edom relied on its famed wise men to navigate geopolitical crises. By inserting neʾum YHWH, the text highlights that the dismantling of Edom's human wisdom is an intentional action announced by God Himself.

2. Irony of Rhetorical Question: The verse opens with a rhetorical question ("Will I not..."). Interjecting "says the LORD" within the question reinforces that Yahweh Himself is questioning Edom's security, emphasizing the total inability of human sagacity to thwart divine judgment.

3. Explanation of Downfall: Edom's impending ruin would not be the result of mere political miscalculation or military fortune; the passage presents it as the direct unfolding of Yahweh's announced word.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Isaiah 43:10 — "'You are My witnesses,' says the LORD, 'And My servant whom I have chosen...'"

Here neʾum solemnizes Yahweh's declaration regarding Israel's covenant role as witnesses to His unique deity before the nations.

  • Jeremiah 23:7 — "'Therefore behold, the days are coming,' says the LORD, 'that they shall no longer say, "As the LORD lives who brought up the children of Israel out of the land of Egypt..."'"

In Jeremiah's prophecy, neʾum introduces a promise of future restoration that will surpass the historical memory of the Exodus.

  • Ezekiel 18:30 — "'Therefore I will judge you, O house of Israel, every one according to his ways,' says the Lord GOD."

Here neʾum grounds a call to individual repentance, attributing the standard of justice directly to Yahweh's authoritative evaluation.

  • Zechariah 2:6 — "'Up, up! Flee from the land of the north,' says the LORD..."

In this post-exilic context, neʾum accompanies a divine directive calling the scattered covenant people to return to Zion.

7. Other Relevant Insights

In prophetic discourse, neʾum often alternates with another common introductory formula, ʾamar YHWH ("thus says the LORD"). While ʾamar uses the standard verbal root for speaking ("to say"), neʾum is a noun ("utterance of"). Consequently, neʾum functions almost like an official seal or signature placed upon a document, frequently positioned within or at the end of a sentence rather than exclusively at the beginning. In Obadiah 1:8, this nominal structure gives the sentence a formal, solemn tone, placing the ultimate focus on Yahweh as the authority behind the predicted event.
