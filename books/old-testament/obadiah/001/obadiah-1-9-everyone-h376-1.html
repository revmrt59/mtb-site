EVERYONE - (ʼîysh, Strong's H376)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun ʾish fundamentally denotes an individual man, a person, a husband, or, when used in a distributive sense, "each one" or "everyone." In Obadiah 1:9, the word is employed distributively in the masculine singular absolute state to communicate the absolute scope of divine judgment against Edom. It underscores that God's sovereign decree of retribution against Mount Esau will not remain a vague corporate threat, but will reach every single individual without exception, resulting in total accountability and cut-off from the community of Nations.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The noun ʾish is one of the most fundamental and frequent nouns in Hebrew literature. It is typically used to distinguish an individual male from a female (ʾissah), or an individual person from humanity collectively (ʾadam). Beyond its primary reference to a male adult, ʾish routinely functions in structural idioms, administrative lists, legal codes, and prophetic declarations to express individual agency or distributive totality ("each person," "every man," or "everyone").

In the prophetic vision of Obadiah, the prophet pronounces Yahweh’s imminent judgment against the nation of Edom (referred to poetically as "Teman" and the "mountains of Esau"). Edom had proudly gloated over Judah's destruction, actively participating in the violence and plundering of Jerusalem. Obadiah 1:9 falls within the section detailing the complete overthrow of Edom’s human confidences: their strategic wisdom, their trusted allies, and their formidable military defenders.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, ʾish possesses a clear primary meaning with several context-dependent nuances across the Old Testament text:

  • An adult male human being, as distinguished from a woman or child.

  • A husband, in relation to a wife.

  • A specific person of distinction, such as a warrior, leader, or champion (e.g., "man of war").

  • A representative or individual member of a broader group.

  • Distributively (often modified by context or syntactical positioning): "each one," "every single man," or "everyone."

In classical Hebrew grammar, when ʾish functions distributively without an accompanying plural noun, it singularizes a collective group, directing attention to every individual constituent within that group. In Obadiah 1:9, it conveys the idea of "every single individual" within the mountainous territory of Esau.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Hebrew narrative, law, and poetry, ʾish serves to move the reader's focus from the general to the particular. While ʾadam emphasizes humanity in its universal or earthly nature, ʾish highlights personal identity, specific action, and individual standing.

In legal literature, ʾish frequently opens statutes to indicate that the law applies to any given individual who commits a specified act. In military contexts, it denotes individual combatants standing in rank or fleeing from battle. In wisdom literature, it categorizes types of individuals (e.g., "a man of understanding," "a wise man"). In prophetic discourse, the distributive use of ʾish ensures that divine promises or divine warnings are not viewed as mere abstractions; they apply directly and inescapably to every individual member of the targeted population.

4. Theological Implications

The distributive use of ʾish in prophetic judgment literature highlights the principle of individual divine accountability within corporate judgment. While nations stand responsible before God for corporate sins—such as Edom's pride and violence against their brother Jacob—God's judgment is precise. Corporate condemnation manifests in the actual judgment of every single person who comprises that prideful community.

Furthermore, the word underscores the total failure of human self-reliance. Edom boasted in its natural mountain fortresses, its legendary sagacity, and its formidable warriors ("mighty men"). By declaring that ʾish will be cut off, the text demonstrates that human strength, wisdom, and geographic security cannot shield a single individual from Yahweh’s righteous sentence.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Obadiah 1:9 states: "Then your mighty men, O Teman, shall be dismayed, to the end that everyone [ʾish] from the mountains of Esau may be cut off by slaughter."

The immediate context sets up a striking contrast between Edom's elite defensive forces and the absolute scope of their impending destruction. Verse 9 opens with "your mighty men" (gibborim), referring to Edom's elite warriors and military champions. These seasoned fighters will be overwhelmed with dismay and terror. The verse then state the intended outcome of this collapse: "to the end that everyone [ʾish]... may be cut off."

Syntactically, the masculine singular absolute noun ʾish functions here distributively. Rather than repeating a plural noun for the population, the singular ʾish emphasizes that judgment will penetrate to the smallest individual unit of Edomite society. No warrior will successfully defend his neighbor, and no individual civilian will escape notice.

The phrase "from the mountains of Esau" grounds this distributive totality in Edom's distinct geographical sanctuary. The high, rocky strongholds that Edom deemed impregnable will afford no shelter to any single person. The word ʾish thus reinforces the absolute thoroughness of God's judgment against Edom's pride: the entire population will be subjected to the sentence of being cut off.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 16:29 – "See! For the LORD has given you the Sabbath; therefore He gives you on the sixth day bread for two days. Let every man [ʾish] remain in his place; let no man go out of his place on the seventh day."

  • Jonah 3:8 – "But let man [ʾish] and beast be covered with sackcloth, and cry mightily to God; yes, let everyone turn from his evil way and from the violence that is in his hands."

  • Jeremiah 17:10 – "I, the LORD, search the heart, I test the mind, even to give every man [ʾish] according to his ways, according to the fruit of his doings."

  • Proverbs 6:27 – "Can a man [ʾish] take fire to his bosom, and his clothes not be burned?"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The declaration in Obadiah 1:9 directly connects to the broader prophetic principle of divine lex talionis ("law of retribution") expressed later in the prophecy: "As you have done, it shall be done to you; your reprisal shall return upon your own head" (Obadiah 1:15). Edom sought the complete annihilation and plunder of Israel's individuals when Jerusalem fell. Consequently, the judgment pronounced against Edom is expressed in identical terms of individual totality through the word ʾish. The destruction Edom gleefully plotted against every individual in Judah becomes the precise sentence handed down to every individual in Edom.
