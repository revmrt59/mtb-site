DISMAYED - ( chathath , Strong's H2865 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb chathath carries the primary physical meaning of being broken, shattered, or cracked, which metaphorically describes a person being filled with terror, dismayed, or broken in spirit. In the context of divine judgment, it denotes the sudden, paralyzing fear and loss of courage that overtakes even the strongest individuals when they face the sovereign wrath of God. In Obadiah 1:9, it depicts the utter collapse of Edom's legendary military confidence and intellectual pride under the weight of God's impending decree.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root meaning of chathath is concrete and physical, referring to the physical shattering or breaking of an object, such as a bow or pieces of ground cracked by drought. When applied to the human psyche, it paints a vivid word picture of a person's inner strength, resolve, or courage being completely fractured. Instead of standing firm and unified, the brave warrior's heart is shattered into pieces, leaving them utterly paralyzed, helpless, and devoid of the capacity to resist.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb chathath means to be broken, shattered, dismayed, terrified, or filled with dread. In its causative forms, it means to terrify, break, or cause dismay. Lexically, it describes the emotional and spiritual state of collapse that occurs when external pressures, threats, or divine interventions overwhelm human defenses. It represents the absolute opposite of being strong, courageous, or secure.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The term chathath is used approximately 50 times in the Old Testament, functioning in several distinct ways:

• Physical Shattering: It describes weapons of war being broken or shattered.

• Psychological Dismay: It frequently occurs alongside commands to "not fear" or "not be dismayed." In these passages, the word highlights the emotional paralysis that hinders faith and action.

• Divine Judgment: When God acts in judgment, He shatters the power of nations and sends a supernatural terror that breaks their collective will to fight.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, chathath demonstrates the futility of human self-sufficiency and pride when confronted by the holiness and sovereignty of God. Humans often build fortresses, accumulate wealth, and rely on intellectual wisdom or military might. However, when God arises in judgment, He easily shatters these false foundations. True security is found only in Yahweh; relying on any other source of strength ultimately leads to a shattered spirit and complete dismay.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Obadiah 1:9, the judgment is directed against Edom, specifically "Teman," a region renowned for its wisdom and elite warriors. Edom had proudly relied on secure mountain strongholds, strategic alliances, and military prowess. When God declares that the mighty men of Teman "shall be dismayed," He is targeting the very source of their security. The passive or internal state of being shattered indicates that their legendary courage will vanish instantly. They will not be able to formulate a defense, leading to their total slaughter and cutting off.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Deuteronomy 1:21: "Look, the Lord your God has set the land before you; go up and possess it, as the Lord God of your fathers has spoken to you; do not fear or be dismayed."

• Joshua 1:9: "Have I not commanded you? Be strong and of good courage; do not be afraid, nor be dismayed, for the Lord your God is with you wherever you go."

• 1 Samuel 2:4: "The bows of the mighty men are broken, And those who stumbled are girded with strength."

• Jeremiah 1:17: "Therefore prepare yourself and arise, And speak to them all that I command you. Do not be dismayed before their faces, Lest I dismay you before them."

• Jeremiah 17:18: "Let them be humiliated who persecute me, But let me not be humiliated. Let them be dismayed, But let me not be dismayed. Bring on them the day of doom, And destroy them with double destruction!"

7. Other Relevant Insights

A key contrast in the Old Testament exists between the dismay experienced by the enemies of God and the command for God's people not to experience it. For the believer, the presence of Yahweh is the antidote to being shattered; hence, God repeatedly commands His servants not to be dismayed because He is with them. For the wicked, however, this dismay is an inescapable consequence of divine judgment. The very strength they boasted of becomes the vessel of their shattered pride.
