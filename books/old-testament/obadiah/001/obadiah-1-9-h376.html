EVERYONE - ( 'ish , Strong's H376 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'ish typically refers to an individual man, husband, or human being, but in specific contexts, it functions as a distributive pronoun meaning "each one," "every man," or "everyone." In Obadiah 1:9, it highlights the absolute, comprehensive nature of God's judgment upon Edom. Rather than a general or partial defeat, the judgment is personal and universal; every single person in the mount of Esau is marked for cutting off, leaving no survivors.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew word 'ish carries a rich background of personal presence, strength, and individual responsibility. While other Hebrew terms for "man" or "human" emphasize physical frailty ('enosh) or dust-formed corporate humanity ('adam), 'ish is historically used to denote a specific person of standing, character, or action. When biblical writers employ this word in a distributive sense, they shift the focus from a massive crowd or a nation down to the single, irreplaceable individual. It paints a picture of a line where every single person is counted, addressed, and dealt with one by one.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary lexical definition of 'ish is an individual male, a husband, or a person of high rank and strength. However, in specific grammatical and contextual settings, the noun functions as a distributive pronoun meaning "each," "every single one," or "everyone." In the context of Obadiah 1:9, the word conveys the absolute totality of the coming judgment. It indicates that the destruction will not merely be a general national defeat, but an individual reckoning that leaves "everyone" cut off, meaning every last person residing in the region.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Standing Soldier: The word often depicts individual men standing in their designated places, whether in battle or in a community. In judgment, this order is shattered as each individual soldier is confronted.

• The Enumerated Household: In ancient Near Eastern culture, census and ownership were counted by the 'ish—the head or representative of each unit. Using 'ish in a negative sense of judgment indicates that the accounting of wrath will be just as meticulous as a census; every single household and individual is marked.

• Individual Agency: The term highlights that every person acts as an individual agent. Therefore, when judgment comes, it targets every individual participant in Edom’s prideful rebellion.

4. Theological Implications

• Personal Accountability: Although God deals with nations historically, He ultimately judges individuals. Edom’s collective sin of pride, violence, and betrayal against Judah is broken down to the level of personal guilt. No one can hide behind national identity or the collective guilt of their people.

• The Limits of Human Defense: Edomites trusted in their high mountain fortresses and corporate strength. The use of "everyone" indicates that human fortifications cannot shield a single soul from divine justice.

• Absolute Justice: God’s execution of justice is precise. It does not sweep away people indiscriminately without a sovereign, individual plan. Every single person who is cut off is done so by divine decree.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Obadiah 1:9, the prophet addresses Teman, a major city and district of Edom renowned for producing wise men and mighty warriors.

• The transition from "mighty men" to "everyone... cut off" is a dramatic literary contrast. The very warriors who were supposed to protect the nation will be so utterly dismayed that they will fail to defend anyone. As a result, every individual ('ish) in the rugged, seemingly impregnable "mount of Esau" will be reached and cut off by slaughter.

• The term emphasizes that the rugged geography of the mountains, which Edom believed would isolate and protect them, will offer zero protection for any single person.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 9:5 - "From the hand of every man's ('ish) brother I will require the life of man." (Emphasizes individual accountability for taking human life).

• Exodus 12:3 - "On the tenth of this month every man ('ish) shall take for himself a lamb, according to the house of his father, a lamb for a household." (Demands personal responsibility in preparing the Passover).

• Joshua 6:20 - "...and the people went up into the city, every man ('ish) straight before him, and they took the city." (Highlights the individual, unified action of the soldiers in taking Jericho).

• Isaiah 53:6 - "All we like sheep have gone astray; We have turned, every one ('ish), to his own way; And the Lord has laid on Him the iniquity of us all." (Illustrates the personal, individual nature of human rebellion and sin).

7. Other Relevant Insights

This term provides a striking pastoral warning against "groupthink" and false security. It is easy for individuals to lose their sense of personal moral responsibility when surrounded by a wealthy, powerful, or proud culture. The judgment of Edom reminds us that God's eyes penetrate through corporate structures, societal pride, and geographic defenses to hold every single individual accountable.
