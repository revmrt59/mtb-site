CUT OFF - ( karat , Strong's H3772 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb karat in Obadiah 1:9 refers to the complete, irreversible elimination and destruction of Edom. Historically renowned for their wisdom and secure mountain fortresses, the Edomites would experience a total judicial cutting off by divine decree. In this prophetic context, the word signifies an absolute end—not merely a temporary military defeat or a season of exile, but a permanent eradication from the stage of history, highlighting the severe consequences of pride and betrayal against God's covenant people.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The literal, concrete meaning of karat is to sever, chop down, or slice through. It is the standard verb used for felling a tree, cutting off a branch, or slicing pieces of meat.

• In the ancient Near East, the word is deeply bound to the practice of covenant-making. In a ceremony known as "cutting a covenant" (karat berit), sacrificial animals were cut in half, and the covenanting parties walked between the pieces. This physical action served as a graphic, self-maledictory oath: "May I be cut in pieces like these animals if I fail to keep my word."

• When applied to people or nations, the word picture shifts from agricultural pruning or covenant ritual to absolute termination. To be "cut off" is to be severed from the source of life, community, and future legacy.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Karat means to cut, cut off, cut down, eliminate, kill, or excommunicate.

• In the passive stems (such as the Niphal stem used in Obadiah 1:9), it means to be destroyed, consumed, cut off, or exterminated.

• The lexical range encompasses:

• The physical destruction of objects (chopping down Asherah poles or trees).

• The judicial execution or banishment of individuals from the covenant community.

• The complete obliteration of a nation or family line so that no remnant or memory remains.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Felled Tree: Just as a grand cedar is cut down and can no longer grow, a nation cut off by God loses its vitality, stature, and future. It is a picture of proud heights brought down to the dust.

• The Severed Branch: Karat depicts a branch severed from the vine or tree trunk. Deprived of its life source, the branch quickly withers, dies, and is gathered for burning.

• The Broken Lineage: In genealogically-driven cultures, to be cut off meant the termination of one’s name and lineage. It is the ultimate tragedy of leaving no descendants to carry on the family name.

4. Theological Implications

• Divine Sovereignty in Judgment: While human armies (the "slaughter" mentioned in the text) serve as the physical agents of destruction, God is the ultimate Author of the cutting off. Judgment is not an accidental turn of geopolitical history; it is a direct, righteous response of a holy God to human sin.

• The Reversal of Grace: For those who reject God's law and abuse His people, the protection of the covenant is completely removed. Instead of being grafted into God's blessings, the rebellious are systematically excised.

• Finality of God's Decree: Unlike God’s discipline of Israel—which is often described as pruning with the promise of a future sprout—the cutting off of Edom is absolute. It demonstrates that while God's mercy is everlasting for His people, His patience with unrepentant pride has a definite end.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Obadiah 1:9, the prophet addresses Teman, a major region of Edom famous for its wisdom, wealth, and military might. God declares that Edom's "mighty men" will be dismayed and paralyzed with fear.

• The ultimate goal of this divine visitation is "to the end that everyone from the mount of Esau may be cut off (karat) by slaughter."

• Edom had stood by and gloated when Jerusalem was plundered, even cutting off Jewish fugitives who tried to escape. Because Edom "cut off" the survivors of Jacob, God declares a poetic, lex talionis justice: Edom itself will be completely "cut off" by slaughter.

• The placement of karat at the climax of verse 9 emphasizes the total failure of Edom’s defenses. Their wisdom, rocky fortresses, and mighty men cannot prevent the absolute extinction of their national identity.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 9:11 – "Thus I establish My covenant with you: Never again shall all flesh be cut off by the waters of the flood; never again shall there be a flood to destroy the earth."

• Genesis 17:14 – "And the uncircumcised male child, who is not circumcised in the flesh of his foreskin, that person shall be cut off from his people; he has broken My covenant."

• Psalm 37:9 – "For evildoers shall be cut off; But those who wait on the LORD, They shall inherit the earth."

• Joel 1:9 – "The grain offering and the drink offering Have been cut off from the house of the LORD; The priests mourn, who minister to the LORD."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• There is a powerful contrast in Scripture between the cutting off of the wicked and the cutting off of the Messiah. In Daniel 9:26, the Messiah is prophesied to be "cut off, but not for Himself." Christ took the ultimate covenant curse of being severed from the land of the living so that believers, who deserve to be cut off for their sins, might be eternally joined to God.

• Historically, the fulfillment of Obadiah's prophecy was complete. By the Malachi era, Edom's heritage was laid waste (Malachi 1:3). By the first century AD, after the destruction of Jerusalem by the Romans, the Edomites (Idumeans) completely disappeared from the pages of history. They were literally cut off forever.
