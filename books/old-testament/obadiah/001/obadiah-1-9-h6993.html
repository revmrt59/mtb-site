SLAUGHTER - ( qetel , Strong's H6993 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun qetel refers to violent execution, carnage, or complete destruction. Occurring as a rare term in the Old Testament, it denotes an absolute, devastating end brought about by divine judgment. In the context of Obadiah 1:9, it depicts the sudden, inescapable, and total eradication of Edom's leadership and population. This term highlights the severe, irreversible consequences of pride, self-sufficiency, and hostility toward God's covenant people.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The noun qetel is derived from the primary verbal root qatal, which means to slay, kill, or destroy. In ancient Semitic contexts, this root carried a stark, decisive connotation of putting an end to life, often in warfare or judicial execution.

• Unlike more common Hebrew words for killing that can imply personal manslaughter, accidental death, or ritual sacrifice, qetel emphasizes the grim reality of mass destruction and violent termination.

• In the ancient Near East, a nation's "mighty men" (its elite warriors and counselors) were its ultimate line of defense. The word picture of these defenders being "cut off by slaughter" conveys the total collapse of military might and the sudden, hopeless ruin of a seemingly secure empire.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Qetel means slaughter, carnage, ruin, or violent death. It points to a systematic, devastating destruction where lives are swept away.

• The semantic range is narrow and highly intense, focusing on the physical act of being put to death in a decisive judgment.

• In the Septuagint (LXX), the Greek translation of the Old Testament, qetel is translated by the noun sphagē, which refers to slaughter, butchery, or being slaughtered like animals. This Greek equivalent reinforces the graphic and absolute nature of the termination described by the prophet.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The noun qetel is used to describe the utter helplessness of those under divine judgment.

• The term evokes the imagery of a harvest or a butchering process where there is no escape, no negotiation, and no survivors among those marked for judgment.

• By associating this noun with the "mount of Esau," the text contrasts the lofty, impregnable mountain fortresses of Edom with the lowliness of a slaughterhouse, illustrating a dramatic and humbling reversal of fortune.

4. Theological Implications

• Divine Retribution: The slaughter of Edom is a direct consequence of their pride and their mistreatment of Judah during Jerusalem's calamity (the law of retaliation). Edom stood by, gloated, and participated in Judah's destruction; therefore, they face their own devastating end.

• The Illusion of Self-Sufficiency: Edom trusted in her geographical defenses (the high rocks of Petra and Teman) and her wise men. God demonstrates that no human security can withstand His judicial decree.

• God's Sovereignty over Nations: The term underscores that God is the ultimate judge of history, possessing the authority and power to completely cut off a nation that opposes His covenant purposes.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Obadiah 1:9, the prophet addresses Teman, a key region of Edom renowned for its wisdom and elite warriors.

• The placement of "by slaughter" (miqqatel) at the very end of the sentence acts as a dramatic climax. It qualifies how the mighty men of Esau will be "cut off." Their end will not be a gradual decline or a peaceful assimilation, but a sudden, violent, and complete destruction.

• The prepositional prefix mi- (derived from min) functions as a marker of cause or means: they are cut off because of or by means of slaughter.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

Because qetel occurs uniquely in the Hebrew text of Obadiah 1:9, its semantic scope is illuminated by the Septuagint (LXX) equivalent sphagē (Strong's G4967), which appears in both the Old and New Testaments to describe sacrificial or violent slaughter:

• Obadiah 1:9 – "Then your mighty men, O Teman, shall be dismayed, to the end that everyone from the mount of Esau may be cut off by slaughter." (Hebrew: qetel)

• Acts 8:32 – "The place in the Scripture which he read was this: 'He was led as a sheep to the slaughter; and as a lamb before its shearer is silent, so He opened not His mouth.'" (Greek: sphagē)

• Romans 8:36 – "As it is written: 'For Your sake we are killed all day long; we are accounted as sheep for the slaughter.'" (Greek: sphagē)

• James 5:5 – "You have lived on the earth in pleasure and luxury; you have fattened your hearts as in a day of slaughter." (Greek: sphagē)

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The connection between the Mount of Esau and "slaughter" stands in stark contrast to Mount Zion, which God promises to deliver and make holy in the latter part of Obadiah's prophecy.

• While Edom faced physical annihilation in history—fulfilled through successive Nabataean and Roman conquests—this passage serves as a permanent warning to all nations that oppose God's people.

• The verbal root qatal is also used in Job 13:15 ("Though He slay me, yet will I trust Him"), demonstrating that the root conveys ultimate, terminal physical power over life and death.
