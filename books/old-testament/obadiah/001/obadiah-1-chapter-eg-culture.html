HEALTH || Trickle Down

Culture Statement

We are healthy when our thoughts, feelings, and desires reflect God’s. When we care for our spiritual, physical, financial, emotional, and interpersonal health, we are empowered to influence our communities for the better. Health flows from the top down and from the inside out.

Living It in Obadiah 1

Living It in Obadiah 1

Obadiah shows how the condition of our heart eventually affects our attitudes, words, and actions. Edom’s trouble began inside: “The pride of your heart has deceived you” (v. 3). When Jerusalem fell, that pride came out through the eyes and the mouth, as Edom gazed with satisfaction, rejoiced, and spoke proudly over Judah’s ruin (v. 12). It then came out through the hands, as Edom entered the gate, looted, and handed over survivors (vv. 13–14). What was in the heart set the direction of everything else.

Health flows from the top down as well as from the inside out. What we allow in our hearts affects more than ourselves, especially when we lead others. Pride, jealousy, and resentment can influence an entire family, ministry, church, or organization. The same is true of humility, love, and spiritual health. So we bring our thoughts and desires to the Holy Spirit early, while they are still small (Proverbs 4:23; Psalm 139:23–24). A flicker of pleasure when someone we compare ourselves with fails, or a quiet decision to keep our distance from a brother in trouble, is a sign that our hearts do not yet reflect God’s. A heart God has healed shows in how we look at people, what we say about them, and what we do for them, and others are blessed by it.

HONOR || Through His Eyes

Culture Statement

Relationships are protected when we ascribe heaven’s value to the people around us. Like Jesus, we choose to interact with people, not as they deserve, but according to their God given value.

Living It in Obadiah 1

Living It in Obadiah 1

Edom looked at Judah in its humiliation and treated its brother with contempt. But God still called Judah “My people” (v. 13). He calls Edom’s victim “your brother Jacob” (v. 10), and Judah was under His discipline for its own sins, yet that discipline did not take away the value He placed on His people. It gave Edom no right to treat them as less. Edom’s wrongs were failures of honor: standing aloof (v. 11), gloating (v. 12), and taking what belonged to another (v. 13).

Honor means treating people not as they deserve but according to their God-given value. That is hardest when someone’s trouble is partly their own doing, such as a moral failure, a lost ministry, or a broken relationship. In those days we can come near, keep the way of restoration open, and refuse to enjoy or take advantage of what has happened. How we act on a person’s worst day shows whether we are seeing them through our own eyes or through His.

EMPOWERMENT || Model and Invite

Culture Statement

Humility is the key that unlocks empowerment at East Gate. Feedback is the doorway through which we mature, as we intentionally submit and embrace opportunities for growth. When we choose to be teachable and correctable, we are empowered to fulfill every good and perfect work.

Living It in Obadiah 1

Living It in Obadiah 1

Pride deceived Edom about its true condition: “The pride of your heart has deceived you” (v. 3). Edom was known for its wisdom (Jeremiah 49:7), yet it failed to recognize its danger (vv. 7–8). Pride can do the same to us. It hides our blind spots, so we feel secure while a problem grows. Humility is the opposite. It makes us willing to be shown what we cannot see in ourselves and to learn from others. “God resists the proud, But gives grace to the humble” (1 Peter 5:5).

At East Gate, humility is the key to empowerment, and leaders go first. We model teachability by asking for correction and receiving it well, and we invite others to speak into our lives. Gifts, experience, and success are real strengths, but they do not remove our blind spots. Feedback is one way we can help discover what pride would like to hide. “The ear that hears the rebukes of life Will abide among the wise” (Proverbs 15:31).

We Await the Return of Christ

Culture Statement

Jesus went to prepare a place for us so that we could be with Him forever. He promised to return for His bride and it is our HONOR to prepare and wait expectantly for Him. Our greatest desire is to be found ready when our Beloved returns.

Living It in Obadiah 1

Living It in Obadiah 1

Obadiah looks ahead to a day when God sets things right. “The day of the LORD upon all the nations is near” (v. 15), and the chapter ends, “The kingdom shall be the LORD’s” (v. 21). The New Testament connects the final day of the Lord with Christ’s return, when He brings judgment on those who oppose God and rest to His people (2 Thessalonians 1:6–10), and it declares that “the kingdoms of this world have become the kingdoms of our Lord and of His Christ” (Revelation 11:15).

Obadiah’s announcement of the day of the LORD reminds us that Christ will return and that we must live ready for Him. Edom was confident and unprepared, sure that no one could bring it down (v. 3). The people of Zion are marked by deliverance and holiness (v. 17), set apart for the LORD. Read prophetically, the saviors of verse 21 point to a generation God raises up to share in His kingdom by Christ’s strength. We wait for His return by holding our own securities loosely, guarding our hearts against pride and cruelty, and pursuing holiness, so that the day we wait for finds us ready.

We Stand with Israel

Culture Statement

We love what the Lord loves. The Lord loves Israel, and we love Israel. We take seriously the Lord’s promise to bless those who bless Israel, and curse those who curse Israel.

Living It in Obadiah 1

Living It in Obadiah 1

Obadiah is a word from the LORD about how a nation treated Israel. Edom is charged with “violence against your brother Jacob” (v. 10), and the LORD calls those it wronged “My people” (v. 13). The judgment follows a plain principle: “As you have done, it shall be done to you” (v. 15). This agrees with the promise to Abraham that God would bless those who bless him and curse the one who curses him (Genesis 12:3), though Obadiah does not quote it.

God’s relationship with Israel runs through the chapter. Even while Judah was under discipline, He called them His people, and He promises deliverance, holiness, and restored possessions for the house of Jacob (vv. 17–20). God keeps His promises to Israel, and His love for them does not end with judgment. Edom did not have to strike a blow to be counted with the attackers. Standing aloof was enough (v. 11). So we do not stay at a distance when Israel is in trouble. Our love for Israel does not require hostility toward other peoples. We stand with Israel because God loves Israel and remains faithful to His promises.
