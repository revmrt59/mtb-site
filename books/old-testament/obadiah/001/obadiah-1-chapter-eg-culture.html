East Gate Bethlehem

EG Culture - Obadiah 1

Mission

Culture Statement

East Gate Exists to Elevate and Empower Sons and Daughters to Be the Place Where Heaven Touches Earth.

Living It in Obadiah 1

Obadiah reminds us that God's kingdom is advancing through humble people who stand with Him, not through those who exalt themselves. As sons and daughters of God, we are called to reflect His heart by walking in humility, defending the vulnerable, and living as citizens of His kingdom until "the kingdom shall be the LORD'S."

Honor

Culture Statement

We Honor God, One Another, and Those God Has Placed in Authority.
Honor recognizes the value God places on every person. It treats people with dignity, humility, and love, building a culture where relationships flourish and people are strengthened.

Living It in Obadiah 1

Edom's greatest failure was dishonoring their own brother. Rather than protecting Judah in its day of distress, they stood by, rejoiced over its suffering, plundered its possessions, and betrayed its survivors. Obadiah calls us to the opposite spirit—to protect rather than exploit, to grieve rather than gloat, and to treat every person according to the value God places on them.

Presence Driven

Culture Statement

We Are Presence Driven.
The Holy Spirit is God Himself dwelling within us. He directs our decisions, shapes our communities, and equips us to supernaturally impact our surroundings. We hear, connect, and obey as He leads. We are Spirit-led and unashamed.

Living It in Obadiah 1

Edom trusted its mountain strongholds, wealth, alliances, wisdom, and military strength. Obadiah exposes how easily what we have can become what we trust. As a Presence Driven people, our confidence is in the Lord Himself. We stay close enough to hear His voice, connect with His heart, and obey His leading rather than allowing our resources, abilities, or circumstances to become our security.

Health

Culture Statement

We Value Health.
Healthy people build healthy families, healthy ministries, and a healthy church. We pursue spiritual, relational, emotional, and personal health so that life flows from us to others.

Living It in Obadiah 1

Obadiah reveals how an unhealthy heart progresses from pride to indifference, then to delight in another's suffering, and finally to open betrayal. Healthy disciples guard their hearts early, refusing to allow bitterness, envy, or pride to take root before they produce destructive actions.

People of the Word

Culture Statement

We Are People of the Word.
We believe God's Word is our final authority. We read it, study it, obey it, and allow it to shape every area of our lives.

Living It in Obadiah 1

Obadiah repeatedly declares, "Thus says the Lord." Edom ignored God's perspective and trusted its own assessment of reality. We cultivate a different culture by allowing God's Word—not our feelings, culture, or circumstances—to define truth, expose pride, and direct our lives.

Forgiveness

Culture Statement

We Choose Forgiveness.
Because Christ has freely forgiven us, we extend forgiveness to others, refusing to live in bitterness or offense. Forgiveness protects unity and keeps our hearts free.

Living It in Obadiah 1

The hostility between Edom and Judah had grown over generations until Edom celebrated Judah's destruction. Obadiah shows the devastating end of long-nurtured resentment. We refuse to allow offenses to become bitterness, choosing instead to keep short accounts and maintain hearts that remain soft before God.

We Await the Return of Christ

Culture Statement

Jesus is coming again to judge the nations, establish His kingdom, and reign forever. We live with hope, holiness, and expectancy as we prepare for His return.

Living It in Obadiah 1

The final movement of Obadiah looks beyond Edom to the Day of the Lord and concludes with the triumphant declaration, "The kingdom shall be the LORD'S." This chapter teaches us to live with an eternal perspective, knowing that every injustice will be made right when Christ establishes His everlasting kingdom.

Unity

Culture Statement

We choose submission to the Holy Spirit and one another over our personal preferences, giftings, and anointings. Our hearts’ desires are to know God and walk in His ways. We understand that the greatest blessings and anointings are found when we are united together in Christ.

Living It in Obadiah 1

Obadiah repeatedly emphasizes that Judah was Edom's brother. Edom and Judah came from two brothers, Esau and Jacob, yet generations of hostility eventually reached the point where Edom could stand by and rejoice when Judah fell. The tragedy is not merely that Edom harmed another nation; division and resentment had grown between brothers until one brother could rejoice when the other fell.

Obadiah shows us where unresolved resentment can lead. We protect unity by refusing to feed offense, competition, resentment, or division and by remembering that the people of God are family.

GENEROSITY: Its Not Ours Anyway

Culture Statement

We recognize that every resource we’ve been given comes from our generous Father. We choose to be just like Dad, and give as He leads.

Living It in Obadiah 1

Edom saw Judah in a time of desperate need and responded by taking rather than giving. They entered Jerusalem's gates, laid hands on their possessions, and took advantage of their brother's weakness. Kingdom generosity moves in the opposite direction. When we encounter people in need, we look for what we can give, how we can help, and how God might use what He has placed in our hands to strengthen someone else.

Communication

Culture Statement

We “real talk” because we desire real relationships built on trust. We have 100% of our conversations, choosing to be authentic, honest, and open with one another. Awkward today, stronger tomorrow.

Living It in Obadiah 1

Obadiah is an extraordinarily direct book. God exposes Edom's pride, deception, gloating, indifference, betrayal, and false security. There is no hiding behind appearances or from God. Pride had deceived Edom so deeply that they could no longer see themselves clearly. God spoke directly to them.

Likewise, we should all desire Real Talk from people who love us and are willing to speak honestly into our lives. It takes humility to receive that kind of truth, especially when others may see something in us that we cannot see ourselves. It is better to face the truth and allow God to change us than to protect an image of ourselves that is not true.
