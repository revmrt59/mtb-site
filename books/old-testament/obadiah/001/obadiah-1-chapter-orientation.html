Morning Chapter Explanation

Obadiah Chapter 1

Chapter 1

Central Message

The prophecy of Obadiah delivers a swift and decisive decree of divine judgment against Edom for its pride, treacherous violence, and rejoicing over Judah's calamity. Through Edom's downfall, Obadiah points to the greater Day of the LORD, when God will judge human arrogance, vindicate His people, and establish the ultimate triumph of His kingdom.

What This Chapter Is About

This chapter unfolds in three movements. God first announces Edom's coming downfall because of its pride (vv. 1–9). He then explains why Edom deserves judgment by exposing its violence and betrayal of Judah (vv. 10–14). Finally, the prophecy widens beyond Edom to the Day of the LORD, when God will judge every nation, restore His people, and establish His everlasting kingdom.

The Heart of the Chapter

Obadiah is about far more than the downfall of one ancient nation. Edom's judgment reveals that God sees and responds to human pride, injustice, and the mistreatment of others, and that history is ultimately moving toward the day when “the kingdom shall be the LORD'S.”

Chapter Sections

Obadiah 1:1–9 — The Pride and Coming Judgment of Edom
God declares that Edom's confidence in its defenses and wisdom will not save it. He will humble the nation and bring it down.

Obadiah 1:10–14 — Edom's Sin Against Judah
Rather than helping their relatives in Judah during Jerusalem's destruction, the Edomites watched with satisfaction, rejoiced over their downfall, looted the city, and captured fleeing survivors.

Obadiah 1:15–21 — The Day of the LORD and Israel's Restoration
God announces that His justice will extend to all nations. Israel will be restored, possess its inheritance, and the chapter concludes with the triumphant declaration, "The kingdom shall be the LORD'S."

Why This Chapter Matters

This chapter shows that God's judgment is never arbitrary. He first announces His verdict, then explains the reasons behind it, and finally reveals the larger purpose of His justice. Obadiah reminds us that God sees every act of pride and betrayal, yet His ultimate purpose is to uphold justice, restore His people, and establish His kingdom.

Key Themes to Watch For

  • False security

  • Betraying your brother

  • God's justice explained

  • Reaping what you sow

  • The widening scope from Edom to all nations

  • Restoration after judgment

  • Pride blinds us to our need for God.

Key Words & Concepts

Edom — The descendants of Esau, Israel's brother nation, who became long-time enemies of Judah. Because Esau consistently chose immediate earthly desires over God's promises, Edom is often seen as illustrating life lived according to the flesh. Throughout Scripture, Edom becomes a picture of life lived according to the flesh. Like Esau, who chose immediate earthly desires over God's promises, Edom trusted human strength, pursued self-interest, and resisted God's purposes. Obadiah also reminds us that God holds us accountable for how we treat our neighbors—and especially our brothers—calling us to stand with them in their time of need rather than turning against them.

Pride — Trusting in one's own strength, position, wisdom, or achievements instead of recognizing dependence upon God.

The Day of the LORD — A time when God intervenes in history to judge evil, vindicate His people, and establish His righteous rule.

Mount Zion — Jerusalem, representing God's dwelling place, His people, and ultimately His kingdom.

Inheritance — The land and promises God gave His people, which He will ultimately restore despite temporary loss.
