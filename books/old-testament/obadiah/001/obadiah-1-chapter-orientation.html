Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 1
Main Idea:

The LORD declares that He will bring proud Edom down for its violence against its brother Jacob, extend that judgment to all the nations in the Day of the LORD, and give deliverance on Mount Zion, where the kingdom shall be His.

Setting:

Edom descended from Esau, Jacob’s brother, and lived in the rugged heights southeast of Judah. The charges in verses 10–14 best fit Edom’s conduct when Babylon destroyed Jerusalem in 586 BC (Psalm 137:7).

Chapter Outline:

  • Obadiah 1:1–4 — The LORD Will Bring Proud Edom Down

  • Obadiah 1:5–9 — Edom Plundered, Betrayed, and Cut Off

  • Obadiah 1:10–14 — Violence Against Your Brother Jacob

  • Obadiah 1:15–16 — The Day of the LORD upon All the Nations

  • Obadiah 1:17–18 — Deliverance and Holiness on Mount Zion

  • Obadiah 1:19–21 — The Kingdom Shall Be the LORD’s

Key Verse:

“For the day of the LORD upon all the nations is near; As you have done, it shall be done to you; Your reprisal shall return upon your own head.” (Obadiah 1:15, NKJV)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Edom thought it could not fall. Its cities sat high among the rocks, and its heart asked, “Who will bring me down to the ground?” (1:3). The LORD answers Edom’s own question: “From there I will bring you down” (1:4). One by one, the things Edom trusted give way. Its hidden treasures are searched out, the allies who ate its bread betray it, its wise men are destroyed, and its warriors are dismayed (1:6–9). The pride that “deceived” Edom (1:3) is matched by the allies who “deceive” it (1:7). The people who fooled themselves end up fooled by their friends.

Then the LORD names the reason: “violence against your brother Jacob” (1:10). Edom was family, and that makes its conduct worse. On the day foreigners entered Jerusalem’s gates, Edom stood at a distance, and the LORD says, “Even you were as one of them” (1:11). The charges then mount, line by line: gazing on Judah’s disaster, rejoicing over it, boasting, entering the gate, seizing the plunder, and standing at the crossroads to cut off and hand over those who escaped (1:12–14). Through all of it the LORD still calls Judah “My people” (1:13). His discipline of Judah gave Edom no right to feed on its suffering.

Verse 15 is the hinge of the book. Edom’s judgment becomes one part of “the day of the LORD upon all the nations,” and the rule is simple: “As you have done, it shall be done to you” (1:15). Edom handed over Judah’s survivors, and no survivor will remain of the house of Esau (1:14, 18). Yet the vision does not end with Edom’s ruin. “But on Mount Zion there shall be deliverance, And there shall be holiness” (1:17). The escape Edom tried to block, God Himself provides. The last word belongs to Him: “the kingdom shall be the LORD’s” (1:21), a promise whose final fulfillment the New Testament celebrates when the kingdoms of this world become the kingdoms of our Lord and of His Christ (Revelation 11:15).

Chapter Sections

Obadiah 1:1–4 — The LORD Will Bring Proud Edom Down

The LORD summons the nations against Edom and exposes the pride that made it feel untouchable in its rocky heights. Though it soars like an eagle and nests among the stars, He will bring it down.

Obadiah 1:5–9 — Edom Plundered, Betrayed, and Cut Off

Edom will be stripped more completely than thieves or grape-gatherers would leave it, and every support it relied on will fail. Its treasures are found, its allies turn on it, and its wise men and the mighty men of Teman cannot save the mountains of Esau.

Obadiah 1:10–14 — Violence Against Your Brother Jacob

The LORD charges Edom with violence against its brother Jacob on the day Jerusalem fell. A repeated “you should not have” (1:12–14) piles charge upon charge, from looking on and gloating to looting and handing over survivors, so that the indictment grows heavier with each line.

Obadiah 1:15–16 — The Day of the LORD upon All the Nations

Edom’s judgment widens to all the nations, governed by the principle that what they have done will return on their own heads. Verse 16 says the nations will drink as “you” drank on the LORD’s holy mountain; whether that first drinking is Edom celebrating or Judah enduring judgment is not settled, but the nations’ coming cup of judgment is clear.

Obadiah 1:17–18 — Deliverance and Holiness on Mount Zion

The vision turns from judgment to Zion, where there will be deliverance and holiness and the house of Jacob will possess what belongs to it. The house of Jacob and the house of Joseph, best understood as the southern and northern tribes together, will be a fire, and the house of Esau stubble.

Obadiah 1:19–21 — The Kingdom Shall Be the LORD’s

God’s people will possess the lands around them on every side; Israel’s captives will possess Canaanite land as far as Zarephath, and Jerusalem’s captives in Sepharad the cities of the South. Saviors will come to Mount Zion to judge the mountains of Esau, and the kingdom shall be the LORD’s.

Why This Chapter Matters

Obadiah exposes false security. Edom had real advantages, among them height, wealth, allies, wisdom and soldiers, but pride turned them into a fortress against God, and the LORD declares that none of them will protect Edom when He acts (1:3–9). The book shows how easily strength becomes a reason to forget Him.

It also shows how seriously God takes cruelty toward those who are suffering. The charges against Edom range from standing aloof and gloating to plundering and handing over survivors, and the LORD holds Edom accountable for every one of them (1:11–14). Finally, Obadiah gives the wronged a place to rest their case. God sees, His judgment is just, and His purpose ends in deliverance and His own kingdom, so His people can leave vengeance with Him (Romans 12:19).

Key Themes to Watch For

Pride and self-deception. Edom’s pride deceived it, and its trusted allies deceived it in turn (1:3–4, 7).

Brotherhood betrayed. The charge is framed as violence against “your brother Jacob” (1:10–14).

The day. “Day” appears ten times in verses 11–14 for Judah’s calamity, building to “the day of the LORD” in verse 15.

Reciprocal justice. What Edom did returns on its own head, from survivors handed over to no survivor left (1:14–15, 18).

Two mountains. The mountains of Esau stand against Mount Zion, where there is deliverance, holiness and the LORD’s kingdom (1:8–9, 17, 19, 21).

Key Words & Concepts

Edom and Esau. Edom is the nation and Esau the ancestor. From verse 6 the prophet calls the nation “Esau,” which keeps its family tie to Jacob in view (1:6, 10).

Teman. A region or town in Edom, used for the nation’s strength and famed wisdom; its exact location is uncertain (1:9).

The day of the LORD. The time when God steps in decisively to judge and to save. In Obadiah it reaches beyond Edom to “all the nations” (1:15).

The cup. “For as you drank on My holy mountain, So shall all the nations drink continually” (1:16). Some read the first drinking as Edom celebrating on Jerusalem’s ruins, others as Judah drinking the cup of God’s discipline. Either way, the nations then drink the cup of His judgment, a familiar prophetic image (Jeremiah 25:15–29).

Deliverance. The Hebrew word for “deliverance” in verse 17 comes from the same root as those who “escaped” in verse 14. What Edom tried to cut off, the LORD provides on Mount Zion.

Saviors. Plural deliverers, like the leaders God raised up to rescue Israel in Judges 3:9, 15. They come to Mount Zion to judge the mountains of Esau, and the text does not name them (1:21). Read prophetically, they point to an end-time generation of empowered, victorious believers who share in the advance and manifestation of God’s kingdom, with Christ as the source of their authority and victory.
