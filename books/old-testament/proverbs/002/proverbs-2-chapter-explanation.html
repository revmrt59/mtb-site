Proverbs 2

Verse-by-Verse
Proverbs 2:1
Proverbs 2:1
NKJV

Proverbs 2:1 My son, if you receive my words, And treasure my commands within you,(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 2:1 My child, listen to what I say,and treasure my commands. (NLT)

Main Idea

The father begins a long if-then appeal with its first condition: the son must receive his words and treasure his commands within, storing them up like something valuable. Wisdom starts with a willingness to take in instruction and keep it, not let it pass by.

Verse Explanation

The father begins a long if-then appeal with its first condition: the son must receive his words and treasure his commands within, storing them up like something valuable. Wisdom starts with a willingness to take in instruction and keep it, not let it pass by.

“My son, if you receive my words,”

The father speaks again with the familiar “My son,” and with the word “if” he begins a long conditional sentence that runs through the whole chapter. The conditions come first (2:1–4), then the results (2:5–11), then the dangers wisdom will rescue the son from (2:12–19), and finally the purpose of it all (2:20–22). The first condition is simple: receive my words. To receive is to accept and take in, the opposite of the refusal Wisdom met in 1:24–25. Everything else in the chapter depends on a son who is willing to listen.

“And treasure my commands within you,”

To treasure is to store something away as valuable, the way a person hides what he does not want to lose. The father’s commands are to be kept within, held in the heart where they can guide the son when the father is not there. The psalmist uses similar language: “Your word I have hidden in my heart, That I might not sin against You” (Psalm 119:11). Wisdom begins with taking instruction in and keeping it, not letting it pass by and fade.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb translated “treasure” means to hide or store up. The same verb appears in 2:7, where the LORD “stores up sound wisdom for the upright,” so the son’s storing of his father’s words is answered by the LORD’s storing of wisdom.

Proverbs 7:1 repeats almost the same line: “My son, keep my words, And treasure my commands within you.”

Proverbs 2:2
Proverbs 2:2
NKJV

Proverbs 2:2 So that you incline your ear to wisdom, And apply your heart to understanding;(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 2:2 Tune your ears to wisdom,and concentrate on understanding. (NLT)

Main Idea

Receiving the father’s words should lead the son to turn his ear toward wisdom and set his heart on understanding. The conditions move inward, from hearing what is said to giving the whole inner person to grasp it.

Verse Explanation

Receiving the father’s words should lead the son to turn his ear toward wisdom and set his heart on understanding. The conditions move inward, from hearing what is said to giving the whole inner person to grasp it.

“So that you incline your ear to wisdom,”

Receiving the father’s words should lead somewhere. To incline the ear is to bend it toward a speaker, the posture of someone who leans in because he does not want to miss a word. The son is not merely to tolerate instruction but to turn toward wisdom with interest and attention.

“And apply your heart to understanding;”

The heart in Proverbs is the inner person: thought, will, and desire together. To apply the heart is to set it, to direct the whole inner life toward understanding. The conditions move inward, from the ear to the heart. Hearing alone is not enough; wisdom must be grasped and wanted from within. The pairing of ear and heart describes a person who both listens and cares about what he hears, the opposite of the simple who drift and the scorners who mock.

Study Notes

None.

Proverbs 2:3
Proverbs 2:3
NKJV

Proverbs 2:3 Yes, if you cry out for discernment, And lift up your voice for understanding,(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 2:3 Cry out for insight,and ask for understanding. (NLT)

Main Idea

The appeal grows more urgent as the son is told to cry out for discernment and lift up his voice for understanding. Wisdom is gained by those who know they lack it and ask for it like someone calling for help.

Verse Explanation

The appeal grows more urgent as the son is told to cry out for discernment and lift up his voice for understanding. Wisdom is gained by those who know they lack it and ask for it like someone calling for help.

“Yes, if you cry out for discernment,”

The word Yes marks an increase in intensity. The son is now to cry out for discernment, calling for it the way a person calls for help. This is the language of need. Wisdom is gained by those who know they lack it and say so. The father’s picture matches the promise of the New Testament: “If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask of God, who gives to all liberally and without reproach” (James 1:5). A person who thinks he already has enough will never cry out.

“And lift up your voice for understanding,”

To lift up the voice is to call aloud, openly and urgently. In chapter 1 Wisdom lifted up her voice in the streets, calling to the simple (1:20). Now the son is told to lift up his voice in return, calling for understanding. The one who answers Wisdom’s call becomes the one who calls for Wisdom. The urgency shows that discernment is worth wanting badly. It is not something a person drifts into while preoccupied with other things.

Study Notes

In Hebrew, “lift up your voice” in 2:3 is the same expression used of Wisdom in 1:20, “She raises her voice in the open squares,” though NKJV renders it differently in the two places.

Proverbs 2:4
Proverbs 2:4
NKJV

Proverbs 2:4 If you seek her as silver, And search for her as for hidden treasures;(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 2:4 Search for them as you would for silver;seek them like hidden treasures. (NLT)

Main Idea

The final condition pictures wisdom as silver and hidden treasure, sought with the effort of a miner digging for buried wealth. Those who search this way are seeking more than good advice, for all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden in Christ (Colossians 2:3).

Verse Explanation

The final condition pictures wisdom as silver and hidden treasure, sought with the effort of a miner digging for buried wealth. Those who search this way are seeking more than good advice, for all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden in Christ (Colossians 2:3).

“If you seek her as silver,”

The father now speaks of wisdom as “her,” the woman who called in the streets in 1:20–33. To seek her as silver is to want her the way people want money and to work for her the way they work to get it. Silver was the common measure of wealth and trade in the ancient world. The comparison is deliberate: if people will spend years and endure hardship to gain silver, wisdom deserves at least the same effort. Proverbs will later say that wisdom is better than silver (3:14; 8:10; 16:16).

“And search for her as for hidden treasures;”

Hidden treasures suggest buried valuables or ore deep in the ground, found only by digging. Job describes miners tunneling into the earth after silver and gold, and then asks, “But where can wisdom be found?” (Job 28:12). Wisdom does not lie on the surface for the casual passerby. It must be searched for with patience and persistence. Read in the light of the New Testament, the treasure sought here is Christ Himself, “in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge” (Colossians 2:3). The search for wisdom becomes the search for Him, and Jesus spoke of the kingdom as “treasure hidden in a field” for which a man gladly sells everything (Matthew 13:44).

Study Notes

Job 28:1–11 describes ancient mining for silver, gold, and gems in vivid detail before asking where wisdom can be found, the same comparison this verse draws in brief.

Proverbs 2:5
Proverbs 2:5
NKJV

Proverbs 2:5 Then you will understand the fear of the LORD, And find the knowledge of God.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 2:5 Then you will understand what it means to fear the LORD,and you will gain knowledge of God. (NLT)

Main Idea

Here the long sentence reaches its result: “Then you will understand the fear of the LORD, And find the knowledge of God.” The search for wisdom ends in a right view of God and in knowing Him personally, which is the treasure the seeker was really after.

Verse Explanation

Here the long sentence reaches its result: “Then you will understand the fear of the LORD, And find the knowledge of God.” The search for wisdom ends in a right view of God and in knowing Him personally, which is the treasure the seeker was really after.

“Then you will understand the fear of the LORD,”

Here the long string of conditions reaches its result. After all the receiving, listening, crying out, and digging, the son will understand the fear of the LORD. This is the motto of 1:7 again. The fear of the LORD is a right view of God, and that is what the search was really for. The son set out looking for wisdom and finds himself standing before God, seeing Him as He is.

“And find the knowledge of God.”

The parallel line goes further. The son will find the knowledge of God, which in Scripture means more than information about God; it means knowing Him personally. The treasure at the end of the search is God Himself. The New Testament describes the same discovery in Christ: God “has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ” (2 Corinthians 4:6). The verse also echoes the LORD’s promise through Jeremiah: “you will seek Me and find Me, when you search for Me with all your heart” (Jeremiah 29:13).

Study Notes

“God” in the second line translates Elohim, the ordinary Hebrew word for God, while “the LORD” in the first line translates the personal name of Israel’s God. The parallel joins the two, so that knowing the LORD and knowing God are one and the same knowledge.

Proverbs 2:6
Proverbs 2:6
NKJV

Proverbs 2:6 For the LORD gives wisdom; From His mouth come knowledge and understanding;(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 2:6 For the LORD grants wisdom!From his mouth come knowledge and understanding. (NLT)

Main Idea

The reason the search succeeds is that the LORD Himself gives wisdom; knowledge and understanding come from His mouth. The son’s diligent seeking does not manufacture wisdom; it brings him to the God who speaks and gives it freely.

Verse Explanation

The reason the search succeeds is that the LORD Himself gives wisdom; knowledge and understanding come from His mouth. The son’s diligent seeking does not manufacture wisdom; it brings him to the God who speaks and gives it freely.

“For the LORD gives wisdom;”

The word For gives the reason the search succeeds. The son’s effort does not manufacture wisdom; the LORD gives it. The two truths stand side by side in this chapter. Wisdom must be sought with the whole heart (2:1–4), and yet when it is found, it is received as a gift. Effort does not earn it, and passivity never finds it. The diligent seeker is brought to the God who gives freely.

“From His mouth come knowledge and understanding;”

Knowledge and understanding come from God’s mouth, which means they come through His word. The father’s words to his son (2:1) point beyond themselves to the words the LORD speaks. Wisdom is not discovered by human ingenuity alone; it is revealed by the God who speaks. This is why the fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge (1:7). The source of true understanding is outside of us, in what God has said, and the son who treasures his father’s words is learning to treasure God’s.

Study Notes

The semicolon at the end of the verse in NKJV shows that the sentence continues into 2:7–8, which describe what the LORD does for the upright.

Proverbs 2:7–11 — The LORD Guards the Upright

Proverbs 2:7
Proverbs 2:7
NKJV

Proverbs 2:7 He stores up sound wisdom for the upright; He is a shield to those who walk uprightly;(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 2:7 He grants a treasure of common sense to the honest.He is a shield to those who walk with integrity. (NLT)

Main Idea

The LORD stores up sound wisdom for the upright, keeping it ready for those who walk with Him, and He is a shield to those whose lives are upright. The God who gives wisdom also protects the people who live by it.

Verse Explanation

The LORD stores up sound wisdom for the upright, keeping it ready for those who walk with Him, and He is a shield to those whose lives are upright. The God who gives wisdom also protects the people who live by it.

“He stores up sound wisdom for the upright;”

The God who gives wisdom also keeps it in store. He stores it up for the upright, holding it ready for those who walk with Him. Sound wisdom is wisdom that works, practical good sense that leads to sound results. The upright are people whose lives run straight before God, without the twisting and hiding the chapter will soon describe in evil men (2:15). The son who stored up his father’s commands (2:1) finds that the LORD has been storing up wisdom for him.

“He is a shield to those who walk uprightly;”

A shield protects the one who carries it from blows he cannot always see coming. The LORD is a shield to those who walk uprightly, those whose conduct has integrity. He told Abram, “I am your shield” (Genesis 15:1), and Proverbs later says, “He is a shield to those who put their trust in Him” (30:5). This describes how God generally deals with those who walk His way. It does not promise that the upright will never suffer harm, but it assures them that they do not walk unprotected.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word translated “sound wisdom” describes wisdom that works, and it can also mean the lasting success that such wisdom produces. The same word appears in 3:21 and 8:14.

ESV and NASB render “walk uprightly” as walk “in integrity.” The Hebrew word behind it means completeness or integrity, a life that is whole and sound.

Proverbs 2:8 He guards the paths of the justand protects those who are faithful to him. (NLT)

Main Idea

He guards the paths of justice and preserves the way of His saints, those who belong to Him and walk faithfully. The picture is of God watching over the road itself, so that those who keep to His way are kept by Him.

Verse Explanation

He guards the paths of justice and preserves the way of His saints, those who belong to Him and walk faithfully. The picture is of God watching over the road itself, so that those who keep to His way are kept by Him.

“He guards the paths of justice,”

The LORD guards the paths of justice. The picture is of God watching over the road itself, keeping the way of right conduct protected. Those who walk on the path of justice walk on ground God has taken responsibility for. This is the road the son is being called to.

“And preserves the way of His saints.”

To preserve is to keep, watch over, and protect. His saints are those who belong to Him and walk faithfully with Him, people marked by loyal love toward God. The word His shows whose they are. They are kept because they are His, and their way is kept because it is His way. Proverbs 2:7–8 thus balances the strong demands of 2:1–4. The son must seek, but he does not seek alone. The God who gives wisdom also shields and keeps those who walk in it.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word translated “saints” comes from the same root as the word for steadfast, loyal love, a root meaning to be kind or loyal. It describes people who are faithful and devoted to the LORD.

Proverbs 2:9
Proverbs 2:9
NKJV

Proverbs 2:9 Then you will understand righteousnessand justice, Equity and every good path.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 2:9 Then you will understand what is right, just, and fair,and you will find the right way to go. (NLT)

Main Idea

A second “Then” follows: the son who seeks wisdom will also understand righteousness, justice, equity, and every good path. Knowing God leads to knowing how to live, and wisdom gives a sense of direction for every choice of the road.

Verse Explanation

A second “Then” follows: the son who seeks wisdom will also understand righteousness, justice, equity, and every good path. Knowing God leads to knowing how to live, and wisdom gives a sense of direction for every choice of the road.

“Then you will understand righteousness and justice,”

A second Then follows, matching the first in 2:5. The first result of seeking wisdom was knowing God; the second is knowing how to live. Righteousness and justice are the very qualities named in the book’s purpose statement (1:3). The son who seeks wisdom will understand what is right and fair, not merely as rules but as a way of seeing that guides his decisions.

“Equity and every good path.”

Equity is straightness, fair dealing without hidden twists. Every good path widens the promise to cover the whole of life. The son will be able to recognize the good road in each choice before him. The order of the two results matters. Knowing God (2:5) comes before knowing how to live (2:9). Right living flows from a right view of God, and when God is seen rightly, the right path becomes clearer.

Study Notes

The three Hebrew nouns behind righteousness, justice, and equity repeat those of 1:3, where NKJV renders the first of them “Justice.”

Proverbs 2:10
Proverbs 2:10
NKJV

Proverbs 2:10 When wisdom enters your heart, And knowledge is pleasant to your soul,(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 2:10 For wisdom will enter your heart,and knowledge will fill you with joy. (NLT)

Main Idea

The father now describes the moment wisdom takes hold: it enters the heart, and knowledge becomes pleasant to the soul. Wisdom has done its work when a person no longer merely knows what is right but has come to love it.

Verse Explanation

The father now describes the moment wisdom takes hold: it enters the heart, and knowledge becomes pleasant to the soul. Wisdom has done its work when a person no longer merely knows what is right but has come to love it.

“When wisdom enters your heart,”

The father now describes the moment wisdom takes hold. It enters the heart, the center of thought, will, and desire. Up to this point the son has been seeking wisdom from outside; now wisdom has come in. What he has been searching for has become part of him.

“And knowledge is pleasant to your soul,”

Knowledge becomes pleasant to the soul. This is the decisive change. The son no longer merely knows what is right; he has come to enjoy it. What was once a duty has become a delight. That change in appetite is what makes wisdom protective. A person who loves what is right is far harder to lure away than someone who only knows the rules. The evil men of 2:14 rejoice in doing evil; the son who has received wisdom finds his pleasure in knowledge of the good. The battle the next verses describe is won or lost at the level of what a person loves.

Study Notes

The Hebrew conjunction that opens 2:10 can mean when or for. NKJV and KJV read When, making 2:10 the setting for the guarding in 2:11; ESV, NASB, and the 1984 NIV read For, linking it back as the reason the son will understand every good path (2:9).

Proverbs 2:11
NKJV

Proverbs 2:11 Discretion will preserve you; Understanding will keep you,(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 2:11 Wise choices will watch over you.Understanding will keep you safe. (NLT)

Main Idea

Once wisdom lives within, discretion will preserve the son and understanding will keep him. The protection described here works from the inside, as good judgment stands guard over a heart that has learned to love what is right.

Verse Explanation

Once wisdom lives within, discretion will preserve the son and understanding will keep him. The protection described here works from the inside, as good judgment stands guard over a heart that has learned to love what is right.

“Discretion will preserve you;”

Once wisdom lives within, discretion stands guard. Discretion is the ability to think ahead and plan with purpose, the same quality the book promised to the young man in 1:4. It will preserve the son, watching over him so that he does not walk blindly into danger.

“Understanding will keep you,”

Understanding will keep him. The two verbs, preserve and keep, are guarding words, the language of a watchman over a city. The protection described here works from the inside. Good judgment, rooted in a heart that loves what is right, recognizes the danger and refuses it. The comma at the end of the verse leads into the dangers themselves (2:12–19). Discretion and understanding will deliver the son from two kinds of people: men who speak twisted things and the woman who flatters.

Study Notes

In Hebrew, the verb translated “preserve” here is the same verb rendered “preserves” in 2:8, and “keep” is the same verb rendered “guards” in 2:8, so the same two guarding words appear in reverse order. The LORD’s guarding of His saints is carried out in part through the discretion and understanding He gives them.

Proverbs 2:12–15 — Deliverance from the Way of Evil Men

Proverbs 2:12
Proverbs 2:12
NKJV

Proverbs 2:12 To deliver you from the way of evil, From the man who speaks perverse things,(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 2:12 Wisdom will save you from evil people,from those whose words are twisted. (NLT)

Main Idea

Wisdom’s first rescue is from the way of evil and from the man who speaks perverse things. The danger comes through twisted speech, words that turn right and wrong upside down and draw others along.

Verse Explanation

Wisdom’s first rescue is from the way of evil and from the man who speaks perverse things. The danger comes through twisted speech, words that turn right and wrong upside down and draw others along.

“To deliver you from the way of evil,”

Now the father names what wisdom will do for the son: deliver him. To deliver is to snatch away, to rescue someone from danger. The first danger is the way of evil, a whole road of life that leads to harm. Wisdom does not only teach the son what is good; it pulls him out of the path of those who are bent on evil.

“From the man who speaks perverse things,”

The danger takes the form of a person, and his weapon is his speech. He speaks perverse things, words that twist and overturn, calling evil good and good evil. This is how the gang in chapter 1 worked: they talked. Twisted speech is dangerous because it changes how a young man sees things before it changes what he does. A son whose heart loves knowledge (2:10) will recognize such talk for what it is and refuse to be drawn in. Proverbs will warn often about the perverse mouth, and the LORD Himself says, “the perverse mouth I hate” (8:13).

Study Notes

The Hebrew word for “perverse things” comes from a verb meaning to turn over or overturn. It describes speech that turns the truth upside down, and Proverbs uses the same word again in 2:14, “the perversity of the wicked.”

KJV renders the phrase “froward things,” an older English word for what is contrary or perverse.

Main Idea

These perverse men have left the paths of uprightness to walk in the ways of darkness. Their condition is the result of a choice, for they once stood on the straight road and abandoned it for a darker one.

Verse Explanation

These perverse men have left the paths of uprightness to walk in the ways of darkness. Their condition is the result of a choice, for they once stood on the straight road and abandoned it for a darker one.

“From those who leave the paths of uprightness”

The single man of 2:12 becomes a group. These people leave the paths of uprightness. Their condition is the result of a choice. They once stood on the straight road, or at least knew where it was, and they walked away from it. Proverbs treats departure from the right way as one of the clearest marks of folly.

“To walk in the ways of darkness;”

They leave the straight paths in order to walk in the ways of darkness. Darkness is the place where people hide what they do and where they cannot see where they are going. The book will later describe the way of the wicked as darkness in which “They do not know what makes them stumble” (4:19). The contrast is between paths of uprightness, where a person sees and is seen, and ways of darkness, where deeds are hidden and the road is unclear. Jesus spoke of the same preference: “men loved darkness rather than light, because their deeds were evil” (John 3:19).

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb for “leave” here is the same verb translated “forsakes” in 2:17. The perverse men forsake the paths of uprightness, and the immoral woman forsakes the companion of her youth.

Worse still, they rejoice in doing evil and delight in the perversity of the wicked. Their sin has moved from action into appetite, so that wrongdoing has become their pleasure.

“Who rejoice in doing evil,”

The description goes deeper. These men are not reluctant sinners who do wrong and then regret it. They rejoice in doing evil. Their sin has moved from action into appetite, so that wrongdoing has become a source of pleasure. This is the reverse of the son whose soul has come to find knowledge pleasant (2:10). Both have learned to love something, and what each one loves decides where he walks.

“And delight in the perversity of the wicked;”

They also delight in the perversity of the wicked, enjoying other people’s twisted ways as well as their own. Taking pleasure in someone else’s wrongdoing shows how far a heart has turned. Paul describes people who not only practice evil “but also approve of those who practice them” (Romans 1:32). A young man who spends time with such people will be pressed to enjoy what they enjoy, which is why wisdom’s rescue matters so much.

Proverbs 2:15

Main Idea

Their ways are crooked and they are devious in their paths, twisting and hiding where they go. The straight path of the upright stands in contrast to their winding roads, which no one can trust or easily follow.

Verse Explanation

Their ways are crooked and they are devious in their paths, twisting and hiding where they go. The straight path of the upright stands in contrast to their winding roads, which no one can trust or easily follow.

“Whose ways are crooked,”

Their ways are crooked, twisted out of line. The picture is a road that bends and doubles back, never going straight. The upright walk paths of uprightness (2:13); these men walk ways that are bent. Crookedness in Proverbs describes moral distortion, a life that departs from what is right and true.

“And who are devious in their paths;”

They are devious in their paths, turning aside and hiding where they go. Their roads wind, and no one can trust them or follow them safely. A young man who joins them would soon lose his own sense of direction. The father’s description of these evil men ends here, and the second danger follows. Both dangers in this chapter work through words and both lead away from the straight road. The son needs wisdom to see the crooked path for what it is before he takes the first step onto it.

Study Notes

ESV, NASB, and the 1984 NIV have “paths” in the first line and “ways” in the second, the reverse of NKJV and KJV. The Hebrew uses two different words for a road or track, and the meaning is the same either way.

Proverbs 2:16–19 — Deliverance from the Immoral Woman

Proverbs 2:16
Proverbs 2:16
NKJV

Proverbs 2:16 To deliver you from the immoral woman, (H802) From the seductress who flatters with her words,(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 2:16 Wisdom will save you from the immoral woman,from the seductive words of the promiscuous woman. (NLT)

Main Idea

Wisdom also rescues the son from the immoral woman, the seductress who flatters with her words, and this is her first appearance in the book. She is an outsider to the young man’s home and to God’s ways, and her danger lies in smooth, flattering speech.

Verse Explanation

Wisdom also rescues the son from the immoral woman, the seductress who flatters with her words, and this is her first appearance in the book. She is an outsider to the young man’s home and to God’s ways, and her danger lies in smooth, flattering speech.

“To deliver you from the immoral woman,”

Wisdom will also rescue the son from a second danger, the immoral woman. This is her first appearance in the book, and she will return at length in chapters 5–7. She is first of all a literal adulterous woman, a wife unfaithful to her husband who draws a young man into sin. The Hebrew words behind immoral woman and seductress describe her as an outsider or stranger. The text presents her as an outsider to the young man’s home and to God’s ways, without settling her nationality. Her danger is not that she is a woman; the young man remains fully responsible for his own steps.

“From the seductress who flatters with her words,”

Her weapon, like the perverse man’s, is speech. She flatters with her words, making them smooth and pleasing. Flattery appeals to a young man’s pride and desire, telling him what he wants to hear. The book will later describe her lips as dripping honey and her mouth as “smoother than oil” (5:3). Both dangers in this chapter speak: one with twisted words and one with flattering words. Wisdom delivers the son by teaching him to hear through the words to what lies behind them.

Study Notes

The two Hebrew words behind “immoral woman” and “seductress” both describe an outsider. The first describes a woman who is a stranger; KJV and NASB render it “strange woman,” and ESV “forbidden woman.” The second is the word for a foreigner; ESV and NASB render it “adulteress.”

Proverbs 7:5 repeats the second line of this verse word for word: “From the seductress who flatters with her words.”

The Hebrew verb behind “flatters” means to make smooth, so her flattery is speech made smooth and easy to accept.

Proverbs 2:17
Proverbs 2:17
NKJV

Proverbs 2:17 Who forsakes the companion of her youth, And forgets the covenant of her God.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 2:17 She has abandoned her husbandand ignores the covenant she made before God. (NLT)

Main Idea

She has walked away from the companion of her youth, the husband of her early years, and “forgets the covenant of her God.” Her unfaithfulness is a broken marriage and a broken promise made before God, so her sin is against both her husband and the LORD.

Verse Explanation

She has walked away from the companion of her youth, the husband of her early years, and “forgets the covenant of her God.” Her unfaithfulness is a broken marriage and a broken promise made before God, so her sin is against both her husband and the LORD.

“Who forsakes the companion of her youth,”

The companion of her youth is her husband, the man she married when she was young. To forsake him is to abandon the most intimate commitment of her life. Her flattery toward the young man is built on unfaithfulness toward her own husband. The word companion carries the sense of a close friend and trusted partner, which makes her betrayal the more serious.

“And forgets the covenant of her God.”

She also forgets the covenant of her God. This most likely refers to the marriage promise she made before God, for marriage in Israel was a solemn commitment witnessed by the LORD. Malachi uses the same language of a wife: “she is your companion And your wife by covenant” (Malachi 2:14). Her sin is therefore against both her husband and the LORD. To forget here is not a lapse of memory but a willful setting aside of what she promised. The young man who joins her becomes a partner in breaking a promise made before God.

Study Notes

Another reading takes “the covenant of her God” (2:17) as the LORD’s covenant with Israel, whose commandments forbid adultery (Exodus 20:14). The two readings overlap, since a marriage vow broken in adultery also breaks God’s commandment.

KJV renders “companion” as “guide.” The Hebrew word describes a close friend or intimate companion, here her husband.

Death here includes physical ruin, but the book’s concern reaches further, to the loss of life in its fullest sense. Proverbs states it plainly elsewhere: the man who commits adultery “destroys his own soul” (6:32).

The Hebrew verb behind “leads down” means to sink, and ESV and NASB say her house “sinks down” to death.

It does not lead back to where a person started, and those who walk it rarely come out the same. The statement is a sober warning about where the road ends and about how hard it is to turn around once a person is on it.

“Nor do they regain the paths of life—”

Nor do they regain the paths of life. The way of life and the way of death have separated, and the one who goes down to her house has left the path of life behind. This warning is meant to keep the young off that road. It is not a ruling that the fallen cannot find mercy. Proverbs itself says, “whoever confesses and forsakes them will have mercy” (28:13), and the gospel declares of people who had lived in sexual sin, “But you were washed, but you were sanctified, but you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus” (1 Corinthians 6:11). The verse warns how deadly the way is; it does not put those who turn from sexual sin beyond God’s grace.

To keep to the paths of righteousness is to stay on them, not wandering off when the road becomes hard or when another voice calls. The perverse men left the paths of uprightness (2:13); the son is to keep to the paths of righteousness. The way of goodness is not a single decision but a continuing walk. Wisdom, once it has entered the heart (2:10), keeps the son moving in the right direction, step after step.

Proverbs 2:21 For the upright will dwell in the land, And the blameless will remain in it;(NKJV)

Proverbs 2:21 For only the godly will live in the land,and those with integrity will remain in it. (NLT)

Main Idea

The upright will dwell in the land and the blameless will remain in it, enjoying the place God gave His people. In Israel’s setting this meant staying secure in the land of promise, a hope Jesus extends to the meek (Matthew 5:5).

Verse Explanation

The upright will dwell in the land and the blameless will remain in it, enjoying the place God gave His people. In Israel’s setting this meant staying secure in the land of promise, a hope Jesus extends to the meek (Matthew 5:5).

“For the upright will dwell in the land,”

The word For gives the reason to keep to the good road: the outcome. The upright will dwell in the land. In Israel’s setting, the land was the place God had given His people, and remaining in it meant enjoying His blessing and presence. Moses had urged Israel to obey “that you may live, and go in and possess the land” (Deuteronomy 4:1). To dwell in the land is to have a secure place under God’s care.

“And the blameless will remain in it;”

The blameless will remain, staying put while others are removed. Blameless describes people whose lives are whole and sound before God, not sinless but upright in heart and conduct. The psalms make the same promise: “The righteous shall inherit the land, And dwell in it forever” (Psalm 37:29). Jesus takes up the hope and widens it: “Blessed are the meek, For they shall inherit the earth” (Matthew 5:5). This verse is not a promise of property or national success; it describes the secure place of the righteous under God.

Study Notes

Jesus’ words in Matthew 5:5 come from Psalm 37:11, “But the meek shall inherit the earth,” a psalm that repeatedly contrasts the righteous who inherit the land with the wicked who are cut off.

The Hebrew word translated “blameless” is the same word rendered “whole” in 1:12.

Main Idea

The wicked, by contrast, will be cut off from the earth and the unfaithful uprooted from it, like plants pulled out by the roots. The chapter ends with the two ways and their two ends, permanence for the upright and removal for the wicked.

Verse Explanation

The wicked, by contrast, will be cut off from the earth and the unfaithful uprooted from it, like plants pulled out by the roots. The chapter ends with the two ways and their two ends, permanence for the upright and removal for the wicked.

“But the wicked will be cut off from the earth,”

The chapter ends with the other side of the contrast. The wicked will be cut off. Where the upright remain, the wicked are removed. The two ways have two ends, and the father wants his son to see the end of each road before he chooses which one to walk.

“And the unfaithful will be uprooted from it.”

The unfaithful are the treacherous, people who break faith, like the woman who forsook her husband and forgot the covenant of her God (2:17). They will be uprooted, pulled out like plants torn from the ground. The image is the reverse of the upright who dwell and remain. Israel’s law had warned that a faithless people would be “plucked from off the land” (Deuteronomy 28:63). The chapter that began with treasuring the father’s words ends with permanence for the upright and removal for the wicked. Proverbs says the same in a single line later: “The righteous will never be removed, But the wicked will not inhabit the earth” (10:30).

Study Notes

The Hebrew word NKJV renders “earth” in this verse is the same word it renders “land” in 2:21. ESV, NASB, and the 1984 NIV render it “land” in both verses, keeping the contrast between those who remain in the land and those cut off from it.

KJV’s margin offers “plucked up” as an alternative to “rooted out” in the second line.
