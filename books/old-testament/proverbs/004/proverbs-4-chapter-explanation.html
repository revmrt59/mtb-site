Proverbs 4

Verse-by-Verse
Proverbs 4:1
Proverbs 4:1
NKJV

Proverbs 4:1 Hear, my children, the instruction of a father, And give attention to know understanding;(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 4:1 My children, listen when your father corrects you.Pay attention and learn good judgment, (NLT)

Main Idea

The father now speaks to “my children,” calling them to hear a father’s instruction and pay attention so they may gain understanding. His appeal widens from one son to all his children, for this teaching is meant for the whole household.

Verse Explanation

The father now speaks to “my children,” calling them to hear a father’s instruction and pay attention so they may gain understanding. His appeal widens from one son to all his children, for this teaching is meant for the whole household.

“Hear, my children, the instruction of a father,”

The father’s appeal widens. Instead of “my son,” he now says “my children,” addressing all his sons, or the whole household of learners. What he offers is the instruction of a father, the training and correction that a father gives because he cares how his children turn out. The phrase sounds almost like a title, as if he were saying: listen to what a father has to teach. His authority rests on his role and on the experience he is about to describe.

“And give attention to know understanding;”

To give attention is to listen closely, the way a person leans in to hear something important. The goal is to know understanding, to gain insight that will guide life. Listening is not passive here. The father asks his children to focus, because understanding does not come to those who hear with half their attention. The father renews this call to listen at 4:10 and again at 4:20.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word behind “children” here is the plural of the word for son, so it may also be rendered sons, as ESV and NASB do. The address shifts back to the singular “my son” in 4:10 and 4:20.

Proverbs 4:2
Proverbs 4:2
NKJV

Proverbs 4:2 For I give you good doctrine: Do not forsake my law.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 4:2 for I am giving you good guidance.Don’t turn away from my instructions. (NLT)

Main Idea

He gives his reason for speaking with confidence: what he offers is good doctrine, sound teaching worth keeping. So he urges them not to forsake his law, since good instruction is only useful if it is held on to.

Verse Explanation

He gives his reason for speaking with confidence: what he offers is good doctrine, sound teaching worth keeping. So he urges them not to forsake his law, since good instruction is only useful if it is held on to.

“For I give you good doctrine:”

The Hebrew word translated doctrine means teaching, what is received and learned. The father speaks with quiet confidence: what he gives is good. He is not offering opinions or untested ideas but sound instruction that has proved itself in life. The word good marks it as worth receiving and worth keeping, in contrast to the twisted talk of perverse men (2:12) and the smooth flattery of the immoral woman (2:16).

“Do not forsake my law.”

Because the teaching is good, the children must not forsake it. Law here, as in 1:8 and 3:1, translates torah, the Hebrew word for teaching or instruction. To forsake is to abandon or let go. Good teaching is only useful if it is held on to. Many people hear good instruction in their youth and drift away from it later. The father warns against exactly that. Receiving wisdom once is not enough; it must be kept for a lifetime.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word rendered “doctrine” is the same word translated “learning” in 1:5. ESV renders it “precepts,” and NASB “sound teaching.”

Proverbs 4:3
Proverbs 4:3
NKJV

Proverbs 4:3 When I was my father's son, Tender and the only one in the sight of my mother,(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 4:3 For I, too, was once my father’s son,tenderly loved as my mother’s only child. (NLT)

Main Idea

The father reaches back to his own boyhood, when he was his father’s son, young and tender, and especially dear in his mother’s eyes. The wisdom he passes on was first given to him in a loving home, so he teaches as one who was once taught.

Verse Explanation

The father reaches back to his own boyhood, when he was his father’s son, young and tender, and especially dear in his mother’s eyes. The wisdom he passes on was first given to him in a loving home, so he teaches as one who was once taught.

“When I was my father’s son,”

The father reaches back to his own childhood. He was once a son too, receiving instruction from his own father. This personal memory gives weight to what he teaches. He is not speaking from above as someone who never needed to learn; he speaks as one who was taught and who now passes on what he received. If the speaker is Solomon, the grandfather would be David, but the text does not name them, and the teaching does not depend on that identification.

“Tender and the only one in the sight of my mother,”

He describes himself as tender, young and impressionable, and as the only one in his mother’s sight. The phrase may mean that he was especially dear to her, uniquely beloved, rather than literally an only child. Solomon, for example, had brothers born to the same mother (1 Chronicles 3:5). Whatever the family situation, the picture is of a home where a child was loved and taught. The wisdom he passes on was first given to him in that setting, and he teaches as one who was once on the receiving end.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word rendered “the only one” is also used of Isaac as Abraham’s “only son” (Genesis 22:2). It can describe a unique or especially beloved child. KJV renders it “only beloved,” and NASB has the only son.

Proverbs 4:4
Proverbs 4:4
NKJV

Proverbs 4:4 He also taught me, and said to me: "Let your heart retain my words; Keep my commands, and live.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 4:4 My father taught me,“Take my words to heart.Follow my commands, and you will live. (NLT)

Main Idea

His own father taught him, and he now repeats those words: “Let your heart retain my words; Keep my commands, and live.” Instruction passes across three generations here, and the grandfather’s teaching ties obedience directly to life.

Verse Explanation

His own father taught him, and he now repeats those words: “Let your heart retain my words; Keep my commands, and live.” Instruction passes across three generations here, and the grandfather’s teaching ties obedience directly to life.

“He also taught me, and said to me:”

His father taught him and said to him, and now he repeats those very words. Instruction passes across three generations here: the grandfather to the father, the father to the children. Wisdom is meant to be handed down. Paul later describes Timothy’s faith in the same way, as faith “which dwelt first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice” (2 Timothy 1:5). The father’s own teaching is simply what he first received.

“Let your heart retain my words;”

The grandfather’s first command is for the heart to retain his words, to hold on to them firmly. The heart, the inner person, is the place where instruction must be kept if it is to guide life. Words that stay only in the ears are soon lost.

“Keep my commands, and live.”

The second command ties obedience directly to life: keep my commands, and live. Keeping God’s ways leads to life, and abandoning them leads toward death. Moses set the same choice before Israel: “I have set before you life and death,” he said, “therefore choose life” (Deuteronomy 30:19). The grandfather’s words show that this teaching is not new. It has been tested across generations and has proved to be the way of life. Those who did not grow up in such a home are not shut out. Wisdom is acquired, not inherited, and the command to get it (4:5) is open to all.

Study Notes

The NKJV opens a quotation at the end of this verse that continues through 4:9. Some interpreters end the grandfather’s words earlier, at 4:4 or 4:5, but NKJV marks the whole of 4:4–9 as his speech.

Proverbs 4:5–9 — Get Wisdom: The Principal Thing

Proverbs 4:5
Proverbs 4:5
NKJV

Proverbs 4:5 Get wisdom! Get understanding! Do not forget, nor turn away from the words of my mouth.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 4:5 Get wisdom; develop good judgment.Don’t forget my words or turn away from them. (NLT)

Main Idea

Still quoting his father, the speaker presses the urgent command, “Get wisdom! Get understanding!” He adds that the son must not forget or turn away from these words, because acquiring wisdom and staying with it go together.

Verse Explanation

Still quoting his father, the speaker presses the urgent command, “Get wisdom! Get understanding!” He adds that the son must not forget or turn away from these words, because acquiring wisdom and staying with it go together.

“Get wisdom! Get understanding!”

The grandfather presses an urgent command. Get wisdom! Get understanding! The Hebrew verb means to acquire, even to buy, and it is the word used for purchasing property or goods. Wisdom is not absorbed by accident; it must be acquired deliberately, as someone sets out to obtain something he wants. Later the book uses the same verb: “Buy the truth, and do not sell it, Also wisdom and instruction and understanding” (23:23). The exclamations convey urgency. This is not optional advice but the first priority of life.

“Do not forget, nor turn away from the words of my mouth.”

Acquiring wisdom and staying with it go together. The son must not forget the words or turn away from them. To turn away is to swerve, to leave the path for another one. The grandfather knows that young people often begin well and then drift. Getting wisdom is the first act; keeping it is the work of a lifetime.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb translated “Get” in this verse and in 4:7 is the ordinary verb for acquiring or buying.

Proverbs 4:6
Proverbs 4:6
NKJV

Proverbs 4:6 Do not forsake her, and she will preserve you; Love her, and she will keep you.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 4:6 Don’t turn your back on wisdom, for she will protect you.Love her, and she will guard you. (NLT)

Main Idea

Wisdom is now spoken of as a person to be loved: do not forsake her, and she will preserve you; love her, and she will keep you. Loyalty to wisdom is answered by wisdom’s protection, and at the layered level this love for Wisdom is love for Christ Himself.

Verse Explanation

Wisdom is now spoken of as a person to be loved: do not forsake her, and she will preserve you; love her, and she will keep you. Loyalty to wisdom is answered by wisdom’s protection, and at the layered level this love for Wisdom is love for Christ Himself.

“Do not forsake her, and she will preserve you;”

Wisdom is now spoken of as a person, a woman to whom the son must be loyal. Do not forsake her, do not abandon her as an unfaithful husband abandons his wife. If the son stays with her, she will preserve him, guarding and protecting him. Loyalty to wisdom is answered by wisdom’s protection. The language prepares for chapters 5–7, where the son will be tempted to forsake Wisdom for another woman.

“Love her, and she will keep you.”

The relationship deepens: love her, and she will keep you. Wisdom is not merely a set of rules to follow but someone to love. The son who loves wisdom will find himself kept by her. This personal language is the poem’s way of describing a heart that has come to treasure wisdom. Read in the light of the New Testament, loving Wisdom is loving Christ Himself, the One in whom “are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge” (Colossians 2:3). Jesus said, “He who has My commandments and keeps them, it is he who loves Me” (John 14:21). The one who loves Him is kept by Him.

Study Notes

Wisdom speaks of the same mutual love in 8:17: “I love those who love me, And those who seek me diligently will find me.”

Proverbs 4:7
Proverbs 4:7
NKJV

Proverbs 4:7 Wisdom is the principal thing; Therefore get wisdom. And in all your getting, get understanding.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 4:7 Getting wisdom is the wisest thing you can do!And whatever else you do, develop good judgment. (NLT)

Main Idea

Wisdom is the principal thing, the first and chief priority, and so the son must get it. Whatever else he gains in life, and whatever it costs him, he must above all get understanding.

Verse Explanation

Wisdom is the principal thing, the first and chief priority, and so the son must get it. Whatever else he gains in life, and whatever it costs him, he must above all get understanding.

“Wisdom is the principal thing;”

The Hebrew word translated principal is the same word used for “beginning” in 1:7, meaning first, chief, or most important. Wisdom is the first thing, the priority above all others. Every other good thing in life depends on having it. A person may gain wealth, skill, or status, but without wisdom he will not know how to use them, and they may become his ruin.

“Therefore get wisdom.”

Because wisdom is first, the son must get it. The logic is simple and direct. If wisdom is the most important thing, then acquiring it is the most important task.

“And in all your getting, get understanding.”

The final line can be read two ways. It may mean that with everything else a person acquires, he must be sure to acquire understanding as well. It may also mean that he should get understanding even at the cost of all he has. Both readings point the same direction. Understanding is worth more than all one’s possessions, and no price is too high to pay for it. Jesus told of a merchant who found “one pearl of great price” and “sold all that he had and bought it” (Matthew 13:46).

Study Notes

ESV and NASB render the first word “beginning,” reading the line as a statement that the beginning of wisdom is to get wisdom.

The Hebrew noun in “all your getting” comes from the same verb as “get,” and can mean acquisitions or possessions.

Proverbs 4:8
Proverbs 4:8
NKJV

Proverbs 4:8 Exalt her, and she will promote you; She will bring you honor, when you embrace her.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 4:8 If you prize wisdom, she will make you great.Embrace her, and she will honor you. (NLT)

Main Idea

The son is to exalt wisdom, holding her in high honor, and she will raise him up; when he holds her close, she will bring him honor. The one who prizes wisdom finds that wisdom gives him a place of dignity in return.

Verse Explanation

The son is to exalt wisdom, holding her in high honor, and she will raise him up; when he holds her close, she will bring him honor. The one who prizes wisdom finds that wisdom gives him a place of dignity in return.

“Exalt her, and she will promote you;”

To exalt her is to hold her in high honor, to prize her above other things. In return, she will promote, lifting the son up. The one who honors wisdom finds that wisdom honors him. The reverse is also true: a person who treats wisdom as unimportant will find himself brought low by his own folly.

“She will bring you honor, when you embrace her.”

She will bring honor when you embrace her. The language of embracing continues the picture of wisdom as a beloved woman. The son is to hold her close, as a husband embraces his wife. The honor she brings is the dignity and respect that come from a life well lived. This is not a promise of worldly promotion, fame, or position. The honor wisdom gives is the kind that lasts, the good name and the inner dignity that belong to the person who walks God’s way.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb rendered “Exalt” appears in this form only here. Elsewhere the same verb describes heaping up a raised road, as in “Build up, build up the highway!” (Isaiah 62:10); here it means to lift her high or prize her. ESV and NASB render it “Prize.”

Verse Explanation

The grandfather’s words end with a picture of reward: wisdom will set an ornament of grace on the son’s head and deliver to him a crown of glory. The life that honors wisdom is crowned with beauty and honor, the kind that does not depend on worldly promotion.

“She will place on your head an ornament of grace;”

The grandfather’s words end with a picture of reward. Wisdom will set on the son’s head an ornament of grace, a garland or wreath of beauty. The image recalls 1:9, where the parents’ instruction is called “a graceful ornament on your head.” What the parents promised, wisdom delivers.

“A crown of glory she will deliver to you.”

A crown of glory is the final honor, the sign of a life that has been crowned with dignity and worth. Crowns were worn by kings and at times of celebration. Wisdom delivers it, gives it as a gift. The life that honors wisdom is crowned with beauty and honor, not the kind that depends on worldly position but the kind that marks a person whose life has been well lived before God. The closing quotation mark in NKJV ends the grandfather’s speech.

Study Notes

KJV’s margin offers an alternative for the second line: she shall compass thee with a crown of glory.

ESV renders the two images as a “graceful garland” and a “beautiful crown.”

Proverbs 4:10–19 — Two Paths

Proverbs 4:10
Proverbs 4:10
NKJV

Proverbs 4:10 Hear, my son, and receive my sayings, And the years of your life will be many.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 4:10 My child, listen to me and do as I say,and you will have a long, good life. (NLT)

Main Idea

The father begins a new appeal, “Hear, my son,” asking him to receive his sayings so that the years of his life will be many. As before, long life is the general fruit of the wise way rather than a guarantee for every case.

Verse Explanation

The father begins a new appeal, “Hear, my son,” asking him to receive his sayings so that the years of his life will be many. As before, long life is the general fruit of the wise way rather than a guarantee for every case.

“Hear, my son, and receive my sayings,”

A new appeal begins with the familiar call to listen. The father speaks again to one son, and he asks him not only to hear but to receive, to take in and accept what he says. His sayings are his words of instruction. The father renews here the call to listen that he made in 4:1, and he will renew it again in 4:20, because nothing can happen until the son listens.

“And the years of your life will be many.”

The reason given is long life. The years of the son’s life will be many. As in 3:2, this is the general fruit of the wise way. The paths the father is about to describe, the path of the wicked and the path of the just, lead to very different ends, and the way of wisdom tends toward life. It is not a guarantee for every case. Some wise and godly people die young, and their early death is no sign that they failed to listen. The verse describes how God’s world generally works, not a promise that can be held against God in a particular loss.

None.

Proverbs 4:11 I have taught you in the way of wisdom; I have led you in right paths.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 4:11 I will teach you wisdom’s waysand lead you in straight paths. (NLT)

Main Idea

Looking back, the father reminds his son that he has already taught him the way of wisdom and led him in right paths. He speaks as a guide who has walked ahead of him and shown him the straight road.

Verse Explanation

Looking back, the father reminds his son that he has already taught him the way of wisdom and led him in right paths. He speaks as a guide who has walked ahead of him and shown him the straight road.

“I have taught you in the way of wisdom;”

The father looks back on what he has already done. He has taught his son in the way of wisdom. The way is the road of life, and the father has been showing him how to walk it. This is not a new beginning but a reminder of instruction already given. The son is not starting from nothing; he has been trained.

“I have led you in right paths.”

He has led his son in right paths, straight roads that go where they should. To lead is to walk ahead and show the way. The father speaks as a guide who has walked the road before his son and shown him where it goes. Right paths are the opposite of the crooked ways of 2:15. The father’s confidence is not pride but responsibility. He has done his part, and now the son must choose whether to keep walking where he has been led.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word rendered “right” here means straightness or uprightness. It comes from the same root as the word for “the upright” in 2:7 and 2:21.

Proverbs 4:12
Proverbs 4:12
NKJV

Proverbs 4:12 When you walk, your steps will not be hindered, And when you run, you will not stumble.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 4:12 When you walk, you won’t be held back;when you run, you won’t stumble. (NLT)

Main Idea

On that road the son’s steps will not be hampered as he walks, and he will not stumble when he runs. The right path gives freedom of movement, so the son can go forward with confidence instead of tripping over his own choices.

Verse Explanation

On that road the son’s steps will not be hampered as he walks, and he will not stumble when he runs. The right path gives freedom of movement, so the son can go forward with confidence instead of tripping over his own choices.

“When you walk, your steps will not be hindered,”

On the right path, the son’s steps will not be hindered. The Hebrew word suggests being cramped or hemmed in, as on a narrow, obstructed road. Wisdom gives freedom of movement. A person who lives by God’s ways is not constantly tangled in the consequences of foolish choices, trapped by debts, lies, broken relationships, or guilt.

“And when you run, you will not stumble.”

Even when he runs, he will not stumble. Running suggests moving quickly, perhaps in a crisis or under pressure. On a clear, straight road, a person can run without tripping. The son who walks in wisdom can go forward with confidence instead of tripping over his own choices. This is the same security promised in the words “your foot will not stumble” (3:23), and it stands in contrast to the wicked who stumble in darkness (4:19).

Study Notes

KJV renders the verb “straitened,” reflecting the Hebrew idea of being narrowed or cramped; ESV renders it “hampered,” and NASB “impeded.”

Proverbs 4:13
Proverbs 4:13
NKJV

Proverbs 4:13 Take firm hold of instruction, do not let go; Keep her, for she is your life.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 4:13 Take hold of my instructions; don’t let them go.Guard them, for they are the key to life. (NLT)

Main Idea

The son must grip instruction firmly and not let go, guarding her because she is his life. Instruction is not a passing lesson to set aside after childhood but a lifeline to hold onto.

Verse Explanation

The son must grip instruction firmly and not let go, guarding her because she is his life. Instruction is not a passing lesson to set aside after childhood but a lifeline to hold onto.

“Take firm hold of instruction, do not let go;”

The son must grip instruction firmly and not let go. Instruction is the discipline and training the father gives. The verbs picture a strong grasp, as a person holds on to a rope or a lifeline. Instruction is not a passing lesson to be set aside after childhood. It must be held on to through every stage of life, especially when temptation pulls against it.

“Keep her, for she is your life.”

Keep her, guard her, for she is your life. The pronoun her suggests that instruction is being spoken of as wisdom herself, the woman the son must love and hold. The reason is the strongest one possible: she is your life. To let go of instruction is to let go of life. The verse sums up the father’s whole appeal. Wisdom is not one good thing among many; it is life itself.

Study Notes

Hebrew nouns are either masculine or feminine in form. The Hebrew noun for “instruction” is masculine, yet the pronoun “her” that refers back to it is feminine. This is a matter of grammatical form, not of instruction having a sex. The feminine probably continues the poem’s portrait of Wisdom as a woman, who has been the subject of 4:5–9.

ESV and NASB render the verb “guard” in place of NKJV’s “Keep.”

Main Idea

A sharp warning follows: do not enter the path of the wicked or walk in the way of evil. The danger begins with the first step onto the wrong road.

Verse Explanation

A sharp warning follows: do not enter the path of the wicked or walk in the way of evil. The danger begins with the first step onto the wrong road.

“Do not enter the path of the wicked,”

A sharp warning follows. Do not enter the path of the wicked. The danger begins with the first step onto the wrong road. The father does not tell the son to walk carefully along the wicked path or to visit it occasionally; he tells him not to set foot on it at all. This echoes his first warning in 1:15: “My son, do not walk in the way with them.”

“And do not walk in the way of evil.”

The parallel line repeats the warning: do not walk in the way of evil. To walk is to continue, to make the path one’s own. Entering and walking describe the beginning and the continuing of a life of wrongdoing. The father wants his son to refuse both. The psalmist’s description of the blessed man follows the same progression: he “walks not in the counsel of the ungodly, Nor stands in the path of sinners” (Psalm 1:1).

Proverbs 4:15

Proverbs 4:15 Avoid it, do not travel on it; Turn away from it and pass on.(NKJV)

Proverbs 4:15 Don’t even think about it; don’t go that way.Turn away and keep moving. (NLT)

Main Idea

Four quick commands pile up: avoid the wicked path, do not travel on it, turn away from it, and pass on. Wisdom does not test how close it can walk to evil but keeps as far from it as possible.

Verse Explanation

Four quick commands pile up: avoid the wicked path, do not travel on it, turn away from it, and pass on. Wisdom does not test how close it can walk to evil but keeps as far from it as possible.

“Avoid it, do not travel on it;”

Four quick commands pile up. Avoid it: steer clear of the wicked path altogether. Do not travel on it: do not even pass along it. The repetition makes the point unmistakable. The father is not giving the son a balanced assessment; he is pleading with him.

“Turn away from it and pass on.”

Turn away from it: if you find yourself near it, change direction. Pass on: keep going, do not linger. Wisdom does not test how close it can walk to evil without falling in. It keeps as far from it as possible. Many people are drawn into sin not by a sudden plunge but by walking near the edge, curious or overconfident. The father’s four commands leave no room for that kind of experimenting. The way to stay off the wrong road is to turn away early and keep moving.

Verse Explanation

The wicked cannot sleep unless they have done evil, and they lie awake until they have made someone fall. Evil has become such a compulsion for them that they are restless until they have done harm.

“For they do not sleep unless they have done evil;”

The word For gives the reason for such strong avoidance: the character of the wicked themselves. They do not sleep unless they have done evil. Wrongdoing has become such a compulsion that they cannot rest until they have done it. The psalmist describes the same kind of person: “He devises wickedness on his bed” (Psalm 36:4). Their evil is not occasional but habitual and driven.

“And their sleep is taken away unless they make someone fall.”

Their sleep is taken away unless they make someone fall. They lie awake until they have harmed another person, causing him to stumble into ruin. This is the exact opposite of the sweet sleep promised to the wise (3:24). The wicked are restless until they have done damage. A young man who walks with them will be drawn into that same restless hunger and will become one of those who make others fall.

Study Notes

ESV and NASB render the second line with the verb “stumble,” where NKJV and KJV have “fall.”

Proverbs 4:17
NKJV

Proverbs 4:17 For they eat the bread of wickedness, And drink the wine of violence.(NKJV)

NLT

Main Idea

Wickedness is their bread and violence their wine. They feed on wrongdoing as their daily food, so that harming others has become what sustains and satisfies them.

Verse Explanation

Wickedness is their bread and violence their wine. They feed on wrongdoing as their daily food, so that harming others has become what sustains and satisfies them.

“For they eat the bread of wickedness,”

Wickedness is their bread, their daily food. The phrase may mean that they feed on wickedness, finding it nourishing and satisfying, or that their bread is obtained by wicked means. Both senses fit. Wrongdoing has become what sustains them, as ordinary as eating a meal. A later saying uses the same picture: “Bread gained by deceit is sweet to a man, But afterward his mouth will be filled with gravel” (20:17).

“And drink the wine of violence.”

Violence is their wine. Wine was the drink of celebration and enjoyment. The wicked drink violence the way others drink wine, with pleasure. Harming others has become what satisfies them. The two lines together picture people whose whole diet is evil. The son is being warned about where the path leads: not merely to occasional wrongdoing, but to a life in which wickedness becomes one’s food and drink.

Proverbs 4:18 The way of the righteous is like the first gleam of dawn,which shines ever brighter until the full light of day. (NLT)

Main Idea

In contrast, “the path of the just is like the shining sun,” growing brighter and brighter until the full light of day. The life of the righteous is pictured as a dawn that keeps increasing in clarity and goodness.

Verse Explanation

In contrast, “the path of the just is like the shining sun,” growing brighter and brighter until the full light of day. The life of the righteous is pictured as a dawn that keeps increasing in clarity and goodness.

“But the path of the just is like the shining sun,”

In contrast to the dark, restless way of the wicked, the path of the just is like the shining sun, the light of dawn. The just are the righteous, those who live rightly before God. Their path is bathed in light. They can see where they are going and walk with clarity and confidence.

“That shines ever brighter unto the perfect day.”

The light shines ever brighter, growing from the first glow of dawn to the full brightness of midday. The perfect day is the day fully established, the sun at its height. The life of the righteous is pictured as a light that keeps increasing in clarity and goodness. This is the clearest statement in the prologue of the two ways that frame the book. The verse fits the way the New Testament describes believers being changed “from glory to glory” (2 Corinthians 3:18). It does not promise a life without darkness or struggle. The righteous still pass through hard seasons, but the direction of their path is toward greater light.

Study Notes

ESV and NASB render the image as the light of dawn, and KJV as the “shining light.” The Hebrew phrase behind “the perfect day” is literally the established day, commonly understood as the full light of noon.

Proverbs 4:18–19 form a pair, setting the light of the righteous against the darkness of the wicked.

Proverbs 4:19 But the way of the wicked is like total darkness.They have no idea what they are stumbling over. (NLT)

Main Idea

The way of the wicked, by contrast, is like deep darkness, and they do not even know what makes them stumble. Their path grows darker, and they fall without understanding why.

Verse Explanation

The way of the wicked, by contrast, is like deep darkness, and they do not even know what makes them stumble. Their path grows darker, and they fall without understanding why.

“The way of the wicked is like darkness;”

The way of the wicked is like darkness, deep and thick. The Hebrew word describes the gloom of night when nothing can be seen. Where the path of the just grows brighter, the way of the wicked grows darker. They live in a moral darkness of their own making, and it deepens as they go.

“They do not know what makes them stumble.”

They do not know what makes them stumble. They fall, again and again, but they cannot see why. Their darkness hides the very things that are tripping them up. Jesus spoke of the same condition: “he who walks in darkness does not know where he is going” (John 12:35). The contrast with 4:18 is complete. The righteous see more and more clearly; the wicked understand less and less. The son must choose which path he will walk, knowing where each one leads.

Study Notes

ESV renders “darkness” as “deep darkness,” reflecting a Hebrew word for thick gloom.

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

Again the father calls, “My son,” urging him to pay attention and incline his ear to his sayings. This final appeal of the chapter begins, like the others, with a call to listen carefully.

“My son, give attention to my words;”

The final section of the chapter begins with a renewed call to listen, as at 4:1 and 4:10. My son, give attention to my words. The father asks for focused attention once more. Repetition is part of good teaching, and the father knows that his son needs to hear the call again and again.

“Incline your ear to my sayings.”

Incline your ear, bend it toward the speaker. The phrase repeats the posture of 2:2, “incline your ear to wisdom.” The father wants his son to lean in and listen closely. What follows is the central charge of the chapter, and he wants the son’s full attention before he gives it.

None.

Proverbs 4:21 Do not let them depart from your eyes; Keep them in the midst of your heart;(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 4:21 Don’t lose sight of them.Let them penetrate deep into your heart, (NLT)

Main Idea

The son is not to let these words depart from his sight but to keep them in the midst of his heart. Instruction must be kept before the eyes and stored deep within, where it can guide him from the inside.

Verse Explanation

The son is not to let these words depart from his sight but to keep them in the midst of his heart. Instruction must be kept before the eyes and stored deep within, where it can guide him from the inside.

“Do not let them depart from your eyes;”

The father’s words must not depart from the son’s eyes. He must keep them in view, looking at them often, keeping them before him as a guide. The same command was given in 3:21 about sound wisdom and discretion. Instruction that is out of sight soon goes out of mind.

“Keep them in the midst of your heart;”

He must keep them in the midst of his heart, deep within, at the center of his inner life. Instruction must be both before the eyes and stored deep in the heart. The eyes represent attention, and the heart represents inner conviction and desire. Wisdom that is kept in both places will guide a person from the inside, which is exactly where the father’s teaching is heading: the heart as the spring of life (4:23).

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb rendered “depart” is the same one used in 3:21, “let them not depart from your eyes.” It means to turn aside or slip away.

Main Idea

The father’s words are life to those who find them and health to all their flesh. Wise instruction brings well-being to the whole person, inner and outer alike.

Verse Explanation

The father’s words are life to those who find them and health to all their flesh. Wise instruction brings well-being to the whole person, inner and outer alike.

“For they are life to those who find them,”

The word For gives the reason to keep the father’s words: they are life. They are life to those who find them. Finding suggests that the words must be sought and discovered, as wisdom must be sought in chapter 2. Those who find them receive life, the full life God intends.

“And health to all their flesh.”

They are also health to all their flesh, the whole body. Wise instruction brings well-being to the whole person, inner and outer alike. A life lived by God’s wisdom is generally a healthier life, free from many of the ravages of sin and folly. As in 3:8, this describes a general blessing, not a promise of healing in every case. Faithful people still face sickness. The point is that God’s words are not burdens that drain life but gifts that give it.

Study Notes

KJV’s margin explains the Hebrew word behind “health” as medicine; ESV renders it “healing.” The word can mean health, healing, or cure.

Proverbs 4:23 Keep your heart with all diligence, For out of it spring the issues of life.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 4:23 Guard your heart above all else,for it determines the course of your life. (NLT)

Main Idea

The chapter reaches its central charge: keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it spring the issues of life. The heart, the inner person of thought, will, and desire, is the spring from which a life flows, so guarding it must come before everything else.

Verse Explanation

The chapter reaches its central charge: keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it spring the issues of life. The heart, the inner person of thought, will, and desire, is the spring from which a life flows, so guarding it must come before everything else.

“Keep your heart with all diligence,”

Here is the central charge of the chapter. Keep your heart with all diligence. The heart in Proverbs is the inner person: thoughts, will, and desire together. To keep it is to guard it as a watchman guards a city. With all diligence means with the greatest care, above every other kind of guarding. A person can protect his money, his reputation, and his possessions, but if he neglects his heart, he has neglected the one thing that matters most.

“For out of it spring the issues of life.”

The reason is that out of the heart spring the issues of life. The issues are the sources or outflows, the places from which a life flows. The heart is the spring from which everything else comes: words, actions, choices, and direction. If the spring is polluted, everything downstream is polluted too. Jesus taught the same: “out of the heart proceed evil thoughts, murders, adulteries” (Matthew 15:19), and “out of the abundance of the heart his mouth speaks” (Luke 6:45). Guarding the heart is not withdrawing from life. It is active watchfulness over what one receives, what one allows to grow, and what one loves. The next verses show what that guarding looks like.

Study Notes

KJV’s margin gives the literal Hebrew phrase as above all keeping. The Hebrew can mean either with every kind of guarding or more than anything else you guard.

ESV and NASB render “issues” as “springs.”

Proverbs 4:24 Put away from you a deceitful mouth, And put perverse lips far from you.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 4:24 Avoid all perverse talk;stay away from corrupt speech. (NLT)

Main Idea

From the heart the father moves to the mouth: put away a deceitful mouth and keep perverse lips far away. What springs from the heart soon comes out in speech, so guarding the heart includes guarding the words.

Verse Explanation

From the heart the father moves to the mouth: put away a deceitful mouth and keep perverse lips far away. What springs from the heart soon comes out in speech, so guarding the heart includes guarding the words.

“Put away from you a deceitful mouth,”

From the heart the father moves outward to the mouth. What springs from the heart soon comes out in speech, so guarding the heart includes guarding the words. A deceitful mouth is crooked speech, words that twist the truth. The son must put it away, remove it from his life entirely.

“And put perverse lips far from you.”

Perverse lips are speech that turns aside from what is right and straight. He must put them far away. The verse recalls the man who speaks perverse things (2:12) and the crooked and devious ways of the wicked (2:15). The son is not only to avoid such people; he must avoid becoming one. Speech reveals what is in the heart, and a guarded heart shows itself in honest, straight words. James observes, “Out of the same mouth proceed blessing and cursing. My brethren, these things ought not to be so” (James 3:10).

Study Notes

The Hebrew word for “deceitful” comes from the same root as “crooked” in 2:15. The word for “perverse” is a noun meaning deviation, built on the same root letters as the verb behind “devious” in 2:15 and “perverse” in 3:32.

KJV’s margin renders the line literally as frowardness of mouth and perverseness of lips.

Main Idea

Next come the eyes, which are to look straight ahead with the gaze fixed on what lies before him. Attention that wanders to every side invites a person off the path, and a steady focus keeps him on it.

Verse Explanation

Next come the eyes, which are to look straight ahead with the gaze fixed on what lies before him. Attention that wanders to every side invites a person off the path, and a steady focus keeps him on it.

“Let your eyes look straight ahead,”

Next come the eyes. They are to look straight ahead, focused on the road in front. Eyes that wander look for what they should not see, and a wandering gaze often leads the feet off the path. Chapters 5–7 will show how the eyes are drawn toward the immoral woman. Job said, “I have made a covenant with my eyes; Why then should I look upon a young woman?” (Job 31:1).

“And your eyelids look right before you.”

The eyelids, here standing for the gaze, are to look right before him, fixed on what lies ahead. The repetition stresses focus. Attention that drifts to every side invites a person off the path, and a steady focus keeps him on it. This is not about never noticing the world around him but about not letting his attention be captured by what would pull him away from the right road.

Study Notes

ESV and NASB render “eyelids” as “gaze,” taking the word as a figure for what a person looks at.

Proverbs 4:26 Ponder the path of your feet, And let all your ways be established.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 4:26 Mark out a straight path for your feet;stay on the safe path. (NLT)

Main Idea

He must ponder the path of his feet, weighing where each step leads, so that all his ways will be established and secure. Careful thought about direction produces a steady life.

Verse Explanation

He must ponder the path of his feet, weighing where each step leads, so that all his ways will be established and secure. Careful thought about direction produces a steady life.

“Ponder the path of your feet,”

The feet come next. Ponder the path of your feet, weigh it, consider carefully where each step leads. The Hebrew verb can also mean to make level or smooth, as a road builder levels a path. Either way, the son must think about his direction. Many people drift into trouble because they never stop to consider where they are going.

“And let all your ways be established.”

Then all his ways will be established, firm and secure. Careful thought about direction produces a steady life. The one who ponders his steps does not lurch from one decision to the next but walks a path that holds firm. The psalmist says, “I thought about my ways, And turned my feet to Your testimonies” (Psalm 119:59).

Study Notes

Hebrews 12:13, “make straight paths for your feet,” draws on the wording of this verse.

The Hebrew verb behind NKJV’s “Ponder” can mean to weigh or consider, or to make level or smooth. The current NIV gives “Make level” as an alternative rendering in its footnote, reflecting that second sense, and KJV’s margin offers ordered aright for the second line.

Proverbs 4:27 Do not turn to the right or the left; Remove your foot from evil.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 4:27 Don’t get sidetracked;keep your feet from following evil. (NLT)

Main Idea

The chapter ends with a final command: do not swerve to the right or the left, and remove your foot from evil. Heart, mouth, eyes, and feet are all brought under wisdom, preparing the son for the warnings that follow in chapters 5–7.

Verse Explanation

The chapter ends with a final command: do not swerve to the right or the left, and remove your foot from evil. Heart, mouth, eyes, and feet are all brought under wisdom, preparing the son for the warnings that follow in chapters 5–7.

“Do not turn to the right or the left;”

The chapter ends with a final command. Do not turn to the right or the left. The phrase recalls Moses’ charge to Israel, “you shall not turn aside to the right hand or to the left” (Deuteronomy 5:32), and the LORD’s word to Joshua (Joshua 1:7). The son must stay on the straight path without swerving. Every turn aside, however small, takes him off the road.

“Remove your foot from evil.”

Remove your foot from evil, step away from it. The chapter that began with a father’s instruction ends with the son’s feet. Heart, mouth, eyes, and feet have all been brought under wisdom. The whole person is to be guarded: what he loves, what he says, what he looks at, and where he walks. This prepares the son for the warnings that follow in chapters 5–7, where his eyes and feet will be drawn toward the immoral woman’s house. A son whose heart is guarded and whose feet are kept on the straight path will be ready.

Study Notes

The Septuagint, the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament, adds two further lines after this verse that do not appear in the Hebrew text. English versions translated from the Hebrew do not include them.
