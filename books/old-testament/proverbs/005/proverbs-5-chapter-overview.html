Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 5
Main Idea:

Proverbs 5 warns a son that the immoral woman’s honey-sweet words lead to a bitter and deadly end, tells him to stay far from her door, and calls him to lasting joy with the wife of his youth, because the LORD sees every path.

Setting:

The father’s appeal follows directly on his charge to keep the heart and guard the feet (4:23–27). It opens a run of warnings about adultery that reaches its fullest picture in chapter 7.

Chapter Outline:

  • Proverbs 5:1–6 — Sweet Lips and a Bitter End

  • Proverbs 5:7–14 — Stay Far from Her Door

  • Proverbs 5:15–20 — Rejoice with the Wife of Your Youth

  • Proverbs 5:21–23 — The LORD Sees, and Sin Entraps

Key Verse:

“For the ways of man are before the eyes of the LORD, And He ponders all his paths.” (Proverbs 5:21)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

In Proverbs 5 a father speaks openly to his son about sex, and he begins with speech. The immoral woman’s lips “drip honey” and her mouth is “smoother than oil”; yet “in the end she is bitter as wormwood, Sharp as a two-edged sword” (5:3–4). The son is shown the whole road before he sets foot on it. Wisdom, the skill of living life God’s way, here means judging a path by where it ends while it still looks pleasant.

The father does more than forbid. At the chapter’s center he celebrates married love in frank and tender language, urging his son to “rejoice with the wife of your youth” and to “always be enraptured with her love” (5:18–19). Desire is God’s good gift, and the surest guard against the counterfeit is delight in the real thing. The longing blessed in verse 19 is wasted in verse 20.

Behind the warning and the blessing stands the LORD. The son is warned against weighing “her path of life” (5:6), and the chapter ends with the LORD, who “ponders all his paths” (5:21). Nothing done in secret escapes Him, and the man who thinks he can manage his sin is “caught in the cords of his sin” (5:22). The God who watches is the God who gave the gift, and His watching is part of His care.

Chapter Sections

Proverbs 5:1–6 — Sweet Lips and a Bitter End

The father asks for his son’s attention before the danger arrives, so that discretion and knowledge will guard the son’s own lips against hers. He sets her smooth speech beside where it leads, a bitter and wounding end and steps that go down to death, on a shifting road whose direction the one drawn to her cannot see.

Proverbs 5:7–14 — Stay Far from Her Door

Speaking now to all his children, the father gives his plainest command: keep far from her and do not even go near her door. He then counts what ignoring him would cost: honor, years, and wealth handed to strangers, a body worn out, and public disgrace for a man who sees too late that his ruin began when he hated instruction.

Proverbs 5:15–20 — Rejoice with the Wife of Your Youth

Through pictures of a cistern, a well, and a fountain, the father teaches that a man’s desire is meant to be satisfied within his own marriage, kept for his wife alone and not poured out in the streets. He blesses married love openly, then asks why a son who can delight in his wife would trade that gift for the arms of a seductress.

Proverbs 5:21–23 — The LORD Sees, and Sin Entraps

Every path a man takes lies open before the LORD, who weighs each one. Sin also springs its own trap, holding the wicked man in the cords of his sin until he dies for lack of the instruction he refused.

Why This Chapter Matters

Proverbs 5 answers sexual temptation with both a fence and a feast. The fence is distance, “do not go near the door of her house” (5:8), and 5:9–14 counts what is lost when it is ignored: honor, years, labor, health, and standing among God’s people. The feast is joy in marriage, so purity is never presented as a grim refusal of desire. Nothing here shames readers who are single, widowed, or in a painful marriage; the warnings guard every reader’s steps.

The chapter also prepares for chapter 7, where the steps into sin are traced in full, and it leaves room for hope. The ruined man of 5:12–14 sees his folly too late, but Proverbs later promises that “whoever confesses and forsakes them will have mercy” (28:13), a mercy given fully in Christ, whose blood cleanses those who have fallen.

Key Themes to Watch For

Sweet beginnings and bitter ends. Sin comes with smooth, honeyed words, but the father shows its end in wormwood, a sword, and death (5:3–5).

Distance as wisdom. The father tells his son to stay away from her door instead of testing his strength up close (5:8; compare 1 Corinthians 6:18).

Refused instruction. The ruined man traces his fall to hating instruction and ignoring his teachers, and the chapter closes with a man who dies “for lack of instruction” (5:12–13, 23).

Joy in married love. Sexual delight within marriage is blessed and meant to be enjoyed, kept for husband and wife alone like water from one’s own well (5:15–19; compare Hebrews 13:4).

The LORD who sees. Every path lies before the LORD, and sin binds the very person who thinks he controls it (5:21–22).

Key Words & Concepts

The immoral woman (5:3, 20). The NKJV also calls her a “seductress” (5:20). She is an outsider to the young man’s home and to God’s ways, a wife who has left “the companion of her youth” (2:17). The warning concerns her choices and the son’s own steps, not women in general.

Wormwood (5:4). A plant known for its bitter taste. The father uses it to show how the sweetness of sin turns bitter at the end.

Hell (5:5). Here the realm of the dead, the grave. “Her steps lay hold of hell” means that her path leads down to death.

The cruel one (5:9). Someone who will show the adulterer no mercy. Whether this is the wronged husband or others who take advantage of him is debated; either way, his best years end up in hostile hands.

Cistern, well, and fountain (5:15–18). In a land where water had to be stored and guarded, a man’s own cistern was precious. The father uses these images for married love, to be enjoyed at home and not spilled as “Streams of water in the streets” (5:16).

Enraptured (5:19, 20). Carried away with delight. The same word describes delight in one’s wife and the folly of delight in a seductress, setting the true gift beside the counterfeit.
