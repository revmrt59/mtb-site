Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 7
Main Idea:

Proverbs 7 urges a son to keep his father’s words within his heart and then shows him a young man devoid of understanding walking step by step into an adulterous woman’s house, the pattern by which anyone enters the path of sin and death.

Setting:

This last and fullest warning about adultery follows chapters 5 and 6 and comes just before Wisdom’s call in chapter 8. The father describes what he saw from the window of his own house.

Chapter Outline:

  • Proverbs 7:1–5 — Treasure My Words

Key Verse:

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Proverbs 7 begins with the father’s words held close. The son is to keep them “as the apple of your eye,” “Write them on the tablet of your heart” (7:2–3), and call wisdom his sister (7:4). Wisdom, the skill of living life God’s way, must be that near, because the danger ahead comes through “the seductress who flatters with her words” (7:5).

Then the father watches from his window as “A young man devoid of understanding” (7:7) takes “the path to her house” (7:8) while evening turns to “the black and dark night” (7:9). The story is first a plain warning against adultery, and the young man answers for every step. Read as the book’s pattern of the path of sin, it also traces how anyone walks into sin: an uncommitted heart, coming close, darkness, opportunity, enticement, a religious excuse (7:14), the promise that no one will know, sin renamed as love, God’s word forgotten, and sudden yielding, “as an ox goes to the slaughter” (7:22).

The road ends at “the chambers of death” (7:27), and “He did not know it would cost his life” (7:23). Paul names that end and its remedy: “the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord” (Romans 6:23).

Chapter Sections

Proverbs 7:1–5 — Treasure My Words

The father asks his son to guard his commands as carefully as the pupil of his eye, keeping them on his fingers and written on his heart. He is to treat wisdom as a sister and understanding as close family, so that a better voice is already at home when the seductress begins to flatter.

Proverbs 7:6–12 — The Simple Young Man Near Her Corner

Looking through his lattice, the father picks out one young man among the simple who wanders near her corner as twilight deepens into night. There a married woman, dressed as a harlot and restless away from her home, meets the man who has placed himself in her path.

Proverbs 7:13–20 — The Seductress’s Invitation

She takes hold of him with a kiss and a bold speech that answers every hesitation: a meal from her peace offerings, flattery that he is the one she came looking for, a perfumed bed, and a husband away until the appointed day. She calls adultery love and uses religion to excuse it, and she never mentions the LORD, who sees every path.

Proverbs 7:21–23 — Like an Ox to the Slaughter

Her flattering words finally carry him, and he follows at once, as unaware as an ox led to slaughter or a bird rushing into a snare. The decision looks sudden, yet it finishes what his earlier steps began, and he does not see the price until it has cost him his life.

Proverbs 7:24–27 — Her House Is the Way to Hell

The father turns from the story to “my children” (7:24) and tells them to guard the heart before the feet ever stray. Strong men have fallen on this road, and her house leads only downward to the chambers of death.

Why This Chapter Matters

Proverbs 7 shows that a fall rarely begins at the moment of yielding. The young man’s ruin started when he wandered near her corner at dusk, so the father’s defense starts there too. The way of escape runs the pattern in reverse: God’s word kept close, distance from the corner, the cost remembered, and a guarded heart (7:25). Since “all who were slain by her were strong men” (7:26), no one is too strong to need this warning.

The chapter also exposes the lies that make sin feel safe: a vow already paid, a husband far away, adultery called love. None of them reckons with the LORD, who “ponders all his paths” (5:21). For those who have fallen, Proverbs promises that “whoever confesses and forsakes them will have mercy” (28:13), and in Christ that mercy cleanses all who turn to Him (1 John 1:9).

Key Themes to Watch For

Words treasured and words that flatter. The father’s words kept in the heart protect the son, while the seductress wins him with enticing speech and flattering lips (7:1–5, 13–21).

Step by step into sin. The young man’s fall moves from drifting near her corner to sudden yielding, each step making the next one easier (7:7–10, 21–22).

Darkness and secrecy. Deepening night and an absent husband promise that no one will see, yet sin’s hidden price still comes due (7:9, 19–20, 23).

Religious cover for sin. Her talk of peace offerings and paid vows wraps adultery in the language of worship and implies her account with God is settled (7:14).

Two houses. Her house descends to death and stands opposite the house Wisdom builds in chapter 9 (7:8, 27; 9:1).

Key Words & Concepts

The apple of your eye (7:2). The pupil, the part of the eye a person protects by instinct. The son is to guard his father’s teaching with that same quick care.

The simple (7:7). People who are uncommitted and easily led. This young man has not set himself against wisdom, yet he has never given himself to it, and drift carries him to her door.

The immoral woman and the seductress (7:5). An outsider to the young man’s home and to God’s ways, a wife whose feet “would not stay at home” (7:11). The father warns about her choices and his son’s own steps; he does not make women in general the danger.

Peace offerings (7:14). Sacrifices that were shared as a meal (Leviticus 7:15–16). She most likely means she has meat at home and her religious duty is done, as though that settled her account with God.

Hell and the chambers of death (7:27). Here hell is the realm of the dead, the grave. The phrase pictures her house as a stairway going down, and Proverbs uses death for the whole ruin that follows sin’s path, not only the end of the body.
