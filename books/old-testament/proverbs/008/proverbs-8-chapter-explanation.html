Verse-by-Verse
Proverbs 8:1
Proverbs 8:1
NKJV

Proverbs 8:1 Does not wisdom cry out, And understanding lift up her voice?(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 8:1 Listen as Wisdom calls out!Hear as understanding raises her voice! (NLT)

Main Idea

The chapter opens with a question that expects a yes: “Does not wisdom cry out”? Wisdom and understanding lift their voice openly, in sharp contrast to the seductress of chapter 7, who whispered her offer in the dark.

Verse Explanation

The chapter opens with a question that expects a yes: “Does not wisdom cry out”? Wisdom and understanding lift their voice openly, in sharp contrast to the seductress of chapter 7, who whispered her offer in the dark.

“Does not wisdom cry out,”

The chapter opens with a question that expects the answer yes. In 8:1–3 the speaker describes Wisdom before she speaks for herself from 8:4 onward. The question comes immediately after chapter 7, which ended at “the chambers of death” (7:27). The seductress spoke in the dark to one young man; now the reader is asked to notice that Wisdom has been calling all along, openly and loudly. No one can claim that her voice was hidden.

“And understanding lift up her voice?”

Understanding stands beside wisdom as her partner, the ability to see into things and grasp what is true. Both raise their voice so that it carries. The poem speaks of wisdom as a woman because the Hebrew word for wisdom is feminine and because the book sets Wisdom opposite the immoral woman of chapters 5–7. The feminine language belongs to the poetry. It does not present Wisdom as a goddess, and it does not make Christ feminine. In the book’s fuller reading, which the New Testament makes possible, the Wisdom who speaks in this chapter is the eternal Son of God, whom Paul calls “Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God” (1 Corinthians 1:24). The chapter first lets her speak in her own poetic portrait, and that portrait must be heard on its own terms before its deeper meaning is drawn out.

Study Notes

8:1–3 describe Wisdom in the third person; from 8:4 to the end of the chapter she speaks in the first person.

Proverbs 8:2
Proverbs 8:2
NKJV

Proverbs 8:2 She takes her stand on the top of the high hill, Beside the way, where the paths meet.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 8:2 On the hilltop along the road,she takes her stand at the crossroads. (NLT)

Main Idea

Wisdom takes her stand on the top of the high hill, beside the road where the paths meet. She places herself where she can be seen and heard, at the very spot where travelers must choose which way to go.

Verse Explanation

Wisdom takes her stand on the top of the high hill, beside the road where the paths meet. She places herself where she can be seen and heard, at the very spot where travelers must choose which way to go.

“She takes her stand on the top of the high hill,”

Wisdom chooses the most visible place in the landscape. On a hilltop she can be seen and heard from far away. The seductress lurked at corners and waited for darkness, as 7:9–12 described; Wisdom stands in the open and in the light.

“Beside the way, where the paths meet.”

She also stands beside the road, at the crossroads, the place where travelers must decide which way to go. That is exactly where the young man of chapter 7 went wrong, when he “took the path to her house” (7:8). Wisdom places herself at the point of decision, before the turn is made and not after the damage is done. The book’s two ways, one leading to life and one to death, meet at this spot, and Wisdom is waiting there.

Proverbs 8:3
Proverbs 8:3
NKJV

Proverbs 8:3 She cries out by the gates, at the entry of the city, At the entrance of the doors:(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 8:3 By the gates at the entrance to the town,on the road leading in, she cries aloud, (NLT)

Main Idea

She also cries out by the city gates and at the entrances of the doors. Wisdom goes where people come and go, so no one can say her call was hidden from them.

Verse Explanation

She also cries out by the city gates and at the entrances of the doors. Wisdom goes where people come and go, so no one can say her call was hidden from them.

“She cries out by the gates, at the entry of the city,”

The city gate was the busiest public place in an ancient town. Elders sat there to judge cases, merchants traded, and everyone coming in or going out passed through. Wisdom calls where the life of the community is carried on, as she did in chapter 1, “At the openings of the gates in the city” (1:21).

“At the entrance of the doors:”

Her call continues “At the entrance of the doors,” the doorways at the city’s entry where people pass in and out. From the hilltop to the crossroads to the city gate, Wisdom takes her stand wherever people come and go, so that no one can say her call was out of reach. The colon at the end of the line introduces her own words.

Proverbs 8:4
Proverbs 8:4
NKJV

Proverbs 8:4 "To you, O men, I call, And my voice is to the sons of men.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 8:4 “I call to you, to all of you!I raise my voice to all people. (NLT)

Main Idea

Wisdom now speaks in her own voice: “To you, O men, I call,” and her voice goes out to the sons of men. Her call is for everyone, and at the deeper level of the book it is the voice of Christ calling all people to Himself.

Verse Explanation

Wisdom now speaks in her own voice: “To you, O men, I call,” and her voice goes out to the sons of men. Her call is for everyone, and at the deeper level of the book it is the voice of Christ calling all people to Himself.

“To you, O men, I call,”

Now Wisdom speaks in the first person, and her speech continues to the end of the chapter. “To you” is direct and personal. “O men” addresses people simply as people. She does not wait for her hearers to become wise, important or religious before she speaks to them.

“And my voice is to the sons of men.”

“The sons of men” means human beings, the whole human race. The phrase returns at the climax of the creation poem, where Wisdom says, “my delight was with the sons of men” (8:31). She calls the human race because she delights in it. In the light of the New Testament this is the voice of Christ calling all people to Himself; Jesus Himself spoke of “the wisdom of God” sending messengers (Luke 11:49). That deeper reading does not erase the plain portrait of a noble woman calling in public. The portrait is the picture through which the deeper meaning is seen.

Proverbs 8:5
Proverbs 8:5
NKJV

Proverbs 8:5 O you simple ones, understand prudence, And you fools, be of an understanding heart.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 8:5 You simple people, use good judgment.You foolish people, show some understanding. (NLT)

Main Idea

She addresses the simple and the fools by name, urging the simple to understand prudence and fools to gain an understanding heart. Wisdom’s offer reaches even those who have so far ignored her, which shows her call is full of hope.

Verse Explanation

She addresses the simple and the fools by name, urging the simple to understand prudence and fools to gain an understanding heart. Wisdom’s offer reaches even those who have so far ignored her, which shows her call is full of hope.

“O you simple ones, understand prudence,”

Wisdom names her hearers. The simple are the uncommitted, easily led and not yet settled in wisdom, like the young man of 7:7. She calls them to understand prudence, the practical good sense that sees danger coming and acts in time. Prudence is exactly what the young man of chapter 7 lacked when he wandered toward her corner.

“And you fools, be of an understanding heart.”

She also calls fools, who in Proverbs are morally deficient and resistant to instruction, not merely slow. She urges them to gain an understanding heart, inner sense and judgment. Wisdom’s offer reaches even those who have so far ignored her, and she begins with the least promising hearers. Her call is full of hope. The heart that the young man of 7:7 lacked is the very thing she offers.

Study Notes

In Hebrew “be of an understanding heart” reads literally ‘understand heart,’ with the same verb Wisdom uses in the first line for “understand prudence.” Here “heart” means inner sense and judgment, which is why the ESV has “learn sense” and the NASB “understand wisdom.”

Proverbs 8:6
Proverbs 8:6
NKJV

Proverbs 8:6 Listen, for I will speak of excellent things, And from the opening of my lips will come right things;(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 8:6 Listen to me! For I have important things to tell you.Everything I say is right, (NLT)

Main Idea

Wisdom asks for a hearing because she will speak excellent things, and right things will come from her lips. Her words are noble and upright, worth stopping to hear.

Verse Explanation

Wisdom asks for a hearing because she will speak excellent things, and right things will come from her lips. Her words are noble and upright, worth stopping to hear.

“Listen, for I will speak of excellent things,”

Wisdom asks for a hearing and gives her reason. “Excellent things” are noble things, words of the highest worth. She does not keep lesser words for lesser people; the simple and the fools of 8:5 are offered her best.

“And from the opening of my lips will come right things;”

When she opens her mouth, what comes out is right, straight and upright. The contrast with the seductress is pointed. She made her words smooth (7:5, 21); Wisdom makes hers straight. Smooth words go down easily and lead astray; straight words may be harder to hear, but they lead to life.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word behind “excellent things” is the plural of a word for a leader or prince, so Wisdom promises princely, noble words; the ESV and NASB render it “noble things.”

Main Idea

Her mouth will speak truth, and wickedness is an abomination to her lips. Truthfulness belongs to her nature, so lying is something she finds detestable.

Verse Explanation

Her mouth will speak truth, and wickedness is an abomination to her lips. Truthfulness belongs to her nature, so lying is something she finds detestable.

“For my mouth will speak truth;”

Wisdom’s speech is true. The Hebrew word for truth carries the sense of what is firm and reliable, what a person can lean on. Her words will hold the weight of a life built on them.

“Wickedness is an abomination to my lips.”

“Abomination” is the word Proverbs usually uses for what is detestable to the LORD, as in “Dishonest scales are an abomination to the LORD” (11:1). Here wickedness is an abomination to Wisdom’s own lips. She shares the LORD’s revulsion at evil. Lying is not merely something she avoids; she finds it detestable, because truthfulness belongs to her nature.

Proverbs 8:8 All the words of my mouth are with righteousness; Nothing crooked or perverse is in them.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 8:8 My advice is wholesome.There is nothing devious or crooked in it. (NLT)

Main Idea

All her words are spoken in righteousness, with nothing crooked or perverse in them. Unlike the twisted speech of evil men and the flattery of the seductress, Wisdom’s words are straight through and through.

Verse Explanation

All her words are spoken in righteousness, with nothing crooked or perverse in them. Unlike the twisted speech of evil men and the flattery of the seductress, Wisdom’s words are straight through and through.

“All the words of my mouth are with righteousness;”

“All” leaves no exceptions. Every word Wisdom speaks is spoken in righteousness, in line with what is right before God. Her hearers do not have to sort through her words to find the trustworthy ones.

“Nothing crooked or perverse is in them.”

“Crooked” means twisted, and “perverse” means bent out of shape. The seductress’s method was all twisting: religion turned into an excuse in 7:14, adultery renamed love in 7:18, secrecy offered as safety in 7:19. Wisdom’s words have no twist in them at all. “All” at the start of the verse and “Nothing” at the start of this line close every gap through which deceit could enter.

Main Idea

To the one who understands, her words are plain, and to those who find knowledge they are right. Wisdom is not obscure to an open heart; those willing to learn find her words clear and true.

“They are all plain to him who understands,”

Wisdom’s words are plain, straightforward and clear, to the one who understands. They are not a puzzle reserved for experts. A heart that is open and willing to learn finds them clear.

“And right to those who find knowledge.”

To those who find knowledge, her words are right, straight and true. The verse does not claim that every word of wisdom is equally easy for everyone; the book itself speaks of “an enigma” and of riddles (1:6). It says that the person who is willing to learn finds her words reliable, and that understanding grows as it is used. Clarity belongs to the willing heart.

Proverbs 8:10 Receive my instruction, and not silver, And knowledge rather than choice gold;(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 8:10 Choose my instruction rather than silver,and knowledge rather than pure gold. (NLT)

Main Idea

Wisdom urges her hearers to receive her instruction instead of silver and knowledge rather than choice gold. Given the choice between wealth and wisdom, the wise choose instruction.

Verse Explanation

Wisdom urges her hearers to receive her instruction instead of silver and knowledge rather than choice gold. Given the choice between wealth and wisdom, the wise choose instruction.

“Receive my instruction, and not silver,”

Wisdom sets her instruction against money and asks her hearers to choose. “Instruction” is the word for discipline and training, including correction. Most people would gladly take silver and avoid correction. Wisdom says the opposite choice is the wise one.

“And knowledge rather than choice gold;”

“Choice gold” is the finest, most refined gold, and even that is worth less than knowledge. The verse does not promise silver and gold as a reward for choosing wisdom. It says that wisdom is worth more than they are, and that given the choice, the wise choose instruction.

Proverbs 8:11

Proverbs 8:11 For wisdom is betterthan rubies, And all the things one may desire cannot be compared with her.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 8:11 For wisdom is far more valuable than rubies.Nothing you desire can compare with it. (NLT)

Main Idea

Wisdom is better than rubies, and nothing a person could desire compares with her. The verse echoes the earlier praise of wisdom (3:15), confirming that she is worth more than anything else a person could pursue.

Verse Explanation

Wisdom is better than rubies, and nothing a person could desire compares with her. The verse echoes the earlier praise of wisdom (3:15), confirming that she is worth more than anything else a person could pursue.

“For wisdom is better than rubies,”

Rubies stand here for the most valuable jewels a person could own, and wisdom is better than them. The line echoes the father’s earlier praise: “She is more precious than rubies” (3:15). What the father said about wisdom in chapter 3, Wisdom now confirms in the middle of her own speech.

“And all the things one may desire cannot be compared with her.”

Nothing a person could want compares with wisdom: not wealth, not pleasure, not the luxuries the seductress described in 7:16–17. The comparison is total. Within Wisdom’s speech the verse speaks of wisdom as “her,” stating a principle before Wisdom resumes speaking as “I” in 8:12.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word translated “rubies” names a precious stone or jewel that cannot be identified with certainty. Hebrew lexicons suggest coral or pearls, and the ESV and NASB render the word “jewels.”

Main Idea

Wisdom introduces herself, “I, wisdom,” saying that she dwells with prudence and finds out knowledge and discretion. Good sense and sound judgment are her constant companions, so where wisdom is, practical skill for living is found as well.

Verse Explanation

Wisdom introduces herself, “I, wisdom,” saying that she dwells with prudence and finds out knowledge and discretion. Good sense and sound judgment are her constant companions, so where wisdom is, practical skill for living is found as well.

“I, wisdom, dwell with prudence,”

Wisdom introduces herself by name: “I, wisdom.” She dwells with prudence; she lives with practical good sense as a constant companion under the same roof. Where wisdom is, sound judgment about real situations is found as well. Wisdom is not theory detached from life. It is skill for living God’s way, and prudence belongs to her household.

“And find out knowledge and discretion.”

She finds out knowledge and discretion, discovering them and bringing them to light. “Discretion” is the ability to plan a wise course and think ahead. Wisdom does not merely possess these things; she seeks them out and supplies them to those who listen. Her words echo the purpose of the whole book, which was written “To give prudence to the simple, To the young man knowledge and discretion” (1:4).

Proverbs 8:13
Proverbs 8:13
NKJV

Proverbs 8:13 The fear of the LORD is to hate evil; (H7451) Pride and arrogance and the evil way And the perverse mouth I hate.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 8:13 All who fear the LORD will hate evil.Therefore, I hate pride and arrogance,corruption and perverse speech. (NLT)

Main Idea

The fear of the LORD is defined here as hating evil, and Wisdom names what she hates: pride, arrogance, the evil way, and the perverse mouth. A right view of God produces a settled hatred of evil, beginning with pride.

Verse Explanation

The fear of the LORD is defined here as hating evil, and Wisdom names what she hates: pride, arrogance, the evil way, and the perverse mouth. A right view of God produces a settled hatred of evil, beginning with pride.

“The fear of the LORD is to hate evil;”

The book’s motto said that “The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge” (1:7). Here the fear of the LORD receives a moral definition: it is to hate evil. The fear of the LORD is a right view of God, seeing Him as He truly is in His holiness and goodness. A person who sees God rightly begins to see evil as God sees it, and to hate it.

“Pride and arrogance and the evil way”

Wisdom names what she hates, and pride comes first, named twice in “Pride and arrogance.” Pride is the direct opposite of fearing God, because it puts self in the place that belongs to God. “The evil way” is a whole course of life given over to wrong. The list recalls the things the LORD hates in 6:16–19, which also begin with pride.

“And the perverse mouth I hate.”

Wisdom also hates the perverse mouth, the twisted and deceitful speech of the worthless man in 6:12. Her hatred matches the LORD’s own. Those who walk with her come to share what she loves and what she hates, and pride is the first thing that has to go.

Main Idea

Verse Explanation

Wisdom claims counsel and sound wisdom as her own, and says she is understanding and has strength. She does not merely give advice; she possesses the insight and power needed to carry it out.

“Counsel is mine, and sound wisdom;”

“Counsel” is advice, the ability to give a sound plan. “Sound wisdom” is practical effectiveness, the kind of wisdom that actually succeeds. Wisdom claims both as her own. She knows what should be done.

“I am understanding, I have strength.”

She does not merely have understanding; she is understanding. And she has strength, the power to carry out what she advises. Many people know what they should do but lack the strength to do it, and others have strength but no counsel. Wisdom has both the plan and the power. Isaiah describes the coming King in similar terms, with “The Spirit of counsel and might” resting on Him (Isaiah 11:2).

Proverbs 8:15
Proverbs 8:15
NKJV

Proverbs 8:15 By me kings reign, And rulers decree justice.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 8:15 Because of me, kings reign,and rulers make just decrees. (NLT)

Main Idea

By Wisdom kings reign and rulers decree what is just. Good government depends on her, and any ruler who governs justly does so through the wisdom God gives.

Verse Explanation

By Wisdom kings reign and rulers decree what is just. Good government depends on her, and any ruler who governs justly does so through the wisdom God gives.

“By me kings reign,”

In the ancient world the king was the highest human authority. Wisdom says that kings reign by her. Every king who rules well does so through a wisdom he did not invent. Wisdom stands above the throne.

“And rulers decree justice.”

Rulers who make just laws do so by wisdom. To “decree” is to issue an ordinance, to set a rule in place, and just laws reflect the order God built into His world. The verse speaks of wisdom’s role in all just government, wherever it is found. It does not make every king in Proverbs a picture of Christ, though in the book’s fuller reading the Wisdom by whom kings reign is the One whom Scripture finally names “KING OF KINGS AND LORD OF LORDS” (Revelation 19:16).

NLT

Proverbs 8:16 Rulers lead with my help,and nobles make righteous judgments. (NLT)

Main Idea

Princes, nobles, and all the judges of the earth also rule by her. Wisdom’s authority reaches every level of leadership, showing that all just rule depends on her.

Verse Explanation

Princes, nobles, and all the judges of the earth also rule by her. Wisdom’s authority reaches every level of leadership, showing that all just rule depends on her.

“By me princes rule, and nobles,”

The list continues down the ranks of authority, princes and nobles. Every level of leadership depends on wisdom.

“All the judges of the earth.”

“All the judges of the earth” extends wisdom’s authority to every land and every court. No ruler anywhere can govern justly apart from the wisdom God gives, whether or not he acknowledges its source. The verse humbles human power. Kings and judges are not the final word; they are accountable to a wisdom above them.

Study Notes

The Hebrew text followed by the ESV and NASB ends 8:16 with the word for “righteousness,” literally ‘all who judge righteousness,’ which lies behind the ESV’s ‘all who govern justly’ and the NASB’s ‘All who judge rightly.’ The NKJV and KJV read “earth,” which reflects a different reading of the Hebrew text at this point. The sense of just rule is the same on either reading.

Proverbs 8:17
Proverbs 8:17
NKJV

Proverbs 8:17 I love those who love me, And those who seek me diligently will find me.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 8:17 “I love all who love me.Those who search will surely find me. (NLT)

Main Idea

Wisdom says, “I love those who love me,” and those who seek her diligently will find her. Love is returned and seeking is rewarded, and in the light of the New Testament these are the words of Christ, who is found by those who seek Him.

Verse Explanation

Wisdom says, “I love those who love me,” and those who seek her diligently will find her. Love is returned and seeking is rewarded, and in the light of the New Testament these are the words of Christ, who is found by those who seek Him.

“I love those who love me,”

Wisdom now speaks of love. She is not a cold principle but one who returns love. In the plain sense of the poem, love for wisdom is met by wisdom’s own favor, and the person who values her receives more of her. In the light of the New Testament these words are heard as Christ’s own. Jesus said, “he who loves Me will be loved by My Father, and I will love him and manifest Myself to him” (John 14:21). His love comes first, and those who answer it find Him making Himself known to them more and more.

“And those who seek me diligently will find me.”

To seek diligently is to seek eagerly and earnestly, with focus. The promise is that such seeking is never in vain. The seductress used the same language for her pursuit of the young man in 7:15, but her seeking ended in death; this seeking ends in finding the one who gives life. Earlier the father said that wisdom must be sought “as silver” and searched for “as for hidden treasures” (2:4). At the book’s deeper level, seeking wisdom with that intensity is seeking Christ, “in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge” (Colossians 2:3).

Study Notes

“Seek me diligently” and “Diligently to seek” in 7:15 translate the same Hebrew verb; the KJV renders it here ‘seek me early.’

Proverbs 8:18
Proverbs 8:18
NKJV

Proverbs 8:18 Riches and honor are with me, Enduring riches and righteousness.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 8:18 I have riches and honor,as well as enduring wealth and justice. (NLT)

Main Idea

Riches and honor are with Wisdom, enduring riches joined with righteousness. Her wealth lasts because it is bound up with right living, unlike riches that fly away (23:5); the saying commends wisdom’s true and lasting goods rather than promising every wise person a fortune.

Verse Explanation

Riches and honor are with Wisdom, enduring riches joined with righteousness. Her wealth lasts because it is bound up with right living, unlike riches that fly away (23:5); the saying commends wisdom’s true and lasting goods rather than promising every wise person a fortune.

“Riches and honor are with me,”

Wisdom now speaks of what she gives. Riches and honor are found with her, in her company. The father said the same of wisdom earlier: “In her left hand riches and honor” (3:16). These are real goods, provision and a good name.

“Enduring riches and righteousness.”

Her riches are enduring, and they are joined to righteousness. Riches gained apart from righteousness do not last, for “riches certainly make themselves wings” (23:5). The riches Wisdom gives last because they belong to a life lived rightly before God. Like the book’s other sayings about blessing, the verse states what is generally true in God’s world and commends wisdom’s lasting goods. It does not promise a fortune to every wise person, and Proverbs elsewhere warns against trusting in riches. Jesus had all wisdom and lived without wealth. The riches Wisdom offers are measured first by righteousness and the favor of God.

Verse Explanation

The line ends with a comma because the sentence continues into 8:21, which states the purpose of her walking there. The riches of 8:18 and the inheritance of 8:21 are found along this road and not apart from it.

Proverbs 8:22

NKJV

Main Idea

Wisdom now speaks of her origin, saying the LORD possessed her at the beginning of His way, before His works of old. Wisdom has belonged to God from the very first, and since this Wisdom is revealed as the eternal Son, the verse must never be read as if the Son had a beginning (John 1:1).

Verse Explanation

Wisdom now speaks of her origin, saying the LORD possessed her at the beginning of His way, before His works of old. Wisdom has belonged to God from the very first, and since this Wisdom is revealed as the eternal Son, the verse must never be read as if the Son had a beginning (John 1:1).

“The LORD possessed me at the beginning of His way,”

Wisdom now speaks of her origin, and the poem moves from her call and her gifts to her place beside the LORD before anything was made. “The LORD possessed me” means that Wisdom belonged to the LORD and was His from the very first. “The beginning of His way” refers to the start of God’s activity in creating the world. God was never without His wisdom; before He made anything, Wisdom was already His.

“Before His works of old.”

“His works of old” are the works of creation, and Wisdom was there before them. The verse is poetry. It does not describe a moment when wisdom began; it declares that wisdom belongs to God from before all His works, as the next verse says plainly, “from everlasting” (8:23). Because the Wisdom who speaks here is revealed in the New Testament as the eternal Son, the verse must never be read as though the Son was created or had a beginning. John writes, “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God” (John 1:1). The Son is not one of God’s works. He is the One through whom they were made.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb rendered “possessed” usually means to get or acquire, and in a few poetic passages it is used of God as the one who brings something into being, as when Genesis 14:19 calls Him “Possessor of heaven and earth.” Translations therefore differ here. The NKJV, ESV, NASB and KJV read “possessed”; the 1984 NIV has ‘brought me forth,’ the NLT “formed me,” and the AMP ‘created and possessed.’

The Septuagint, the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament, rendered the verb with a Greek word meaning “created.” In the fourth century Arius and his followers took the verse in that sense to argue that the Son was a created being. The church rejected that teaching at the Council of Nicaea in AD 325, confessing the Son as ‘begotten, not made.’

“Then I was beside Him as a master craftsman;”

“Then,” during all this work of creation, Wisdom was beside Him. The phrase pictures close partnership, standing at the Creator’s side. “As a master craftsman” presents Wisdom as a skilled worker sharing in God’s work, the architect beside the builder. “And I was daily His delight,”

“Daily His delight” means that day after day God delighted in Wisdom. “Rejoicing always before Him,”

Wisdom rejoiced continually in God’s presence. The delight was mutual: God delighted in Wisdom, and Wisdom rejoiced before God. The picture is of creation coming from the overflow of joy within God, a joy the Son has shared with the Father from eternity.

The Hebrew verb translated “rejoicing” can also mean to laugh or to play; the same verb describes children “Playing in its streets” (Zechariah 8:5). The word pictures Wisdom’s joy before God as free and glad.
