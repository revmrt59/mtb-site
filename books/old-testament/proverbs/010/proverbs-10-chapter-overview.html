Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 10
Main Idea:

Proverbs 10 opens Solomon’s collection of short sayings by carrying the two ways into the home, the harvest field, the purse, and the mouth, showing that the righteous rest on a lasting foundation while the way of the wicked passes away.

Setting:

Wisdom and Folly have just issued their rival invitations (9:1–18). A new heading, “The proverbs of Solomon” (10:1), now turns the book from long appeals to short two-line sayings, most of them sharp contrasts.

Chapter Outline:

  • Proverbs 10:1–5 — A Wise Son and Diligent Hands

  • Proverbs 10:6–11 — Blessing on the Righteous, Violence in the Mouth of the Wicked

  • Proverbs 10:12–21 — Words That Wound and Words That Feed

  • Proverbs 10:22–30 — The Everlasting Foundation of the Righteous

  • Proverbs 10:31–32 — The Mouth of the Righteous Brings Forth Wisdom

Key Verse:

“The mouth of the righteous is a well of life, But violence covers the mouth of the wicked.” (Proverbs 10:11)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

With chapter 10 the father’s long appeals give way to single sayings, and the first one goes straight back into the home: “A wise son makes a glad father, But a foolish son is the grief of his mother” (10:1). The choice between wisdom and folly that filled chapters 1–9 now shows up in ordinary places. A son who sleeps through harvest shames his family (10:5), and the lazy man is like “smoke to the eyes” to those who send him (10:26).

The largest cluster concerns speech. Twice the chapter says that “violence covers the mouth of the wicked” (10:6, 11), words that hide the harm in a heart, while the mouth of the righteous is “a well of life.” Righteous lips are “choice silver” and “feed many” (10:20–21), but hidden hatred, slander, and “the multitude of words” open the door to sin (10:18–19).

Behind the contrasts stands the LORD Himself. He will not let the righteous soul famish (10:3), His blessing makes rich without sorrow (10:22), and His way is “strength for the upright” (10:29). These are general truths about God’s ordered world rather than promises for every case, yet they show plainly that God is not neutral between the two ways. When the whirlwind passes, the wicked are gone, “But the righteous has an everlasting foundation” (10:25).

Chapter Sections

Proverbs 10:1–5 — A Wise Son and Diligent Hands

Under Solomon’s name, the first saying ties a son’s choices to his parents’ gladness or grief, and the sayings that follow show what is at stake: wicked gain cannot rescue from death, the LORD sustains the righteous, and work done or neglected at harvest brings a family plenty or shame. Diligence is commended as wisdom at work, without blaming those who are poor through hardship or inability.

Proverbs 10:6–11 — Blessing on the Righteous, Violence in the Mouth of the Wicked

A repeated line about violence covering the mouth of the wicked frames sayings on how a life is remembered, how it stands, and how it speaks. The teachable heart and the person of integrity walk securely, while the chattering fool and the one who winks to make mischief fall or are found out, and the section closes with the righteous mouth as a well of life.

Proverbs 10:12–21 — Words That Wound and Words That Feed

These sayings weigh the moral value of words and what lies behind them: love refuses to keep a quarrel burning, the wise store up knowledge until it is needed, and restrained lips guard against sin, while hidden hatred, slander, and careless talk bring ruin. Woven among them are reminders that wealth, wages, and the response to correction all lead somewhere, and the section ends with the righteous feeding many by what they say.

Proverbs 10:22–30 — The Everlasting Foundation of the Righteous

The LORD’s blessing, the delights of the fool and the wise, and the fears and hopes of each are traced to their ends: the wicked meet what they dread and vanish like debris in a storm, while the hope of the righteous turns to gladness and they stand on a foundation that lasts. Read as general truths, these sayings describe how the two ways finally end without promising the righteous a life free of loss or explaining every early death.

Proverbs 10:31–32 — The Mouth of the Righteous Brings Forth Wisdom

The chapter closes as it has run, with the mouth. Righteous lips bear wisdom like fruit and know what is fitting to say, while the twisted tongue that knows only what is perverse will be cut out.

Why This Chapter Matters

Proverbs 10 teaches how to read the rest of Solomon’s sayings. Each verse sets two lines side by side, so the reader learns to recognize the two ways of chapters 1–9 in a harvest field, an honest wage, or a single remark. The outcome sayings describe how God’s world generally works and how the two ways finally end, and they are never grounds for blaming the poor, the grieving, or a family that has buried someone young (10:3, 27, 30).

The chapter also gives speech great moral weight. The same mouth can be a well of life or a cover for violence. When Solomon says “love covers all sins” (10:12), and Peter takes up the thought for the church (1 Peter 4:8), love refuses to keep a conflict alive, though it never means hiding abuse or avoiding needed correction.

Key Themes to Watch For

Two ways in daily life. Nearly every verse sets the righteous against the wicked or the wise against the foolish, carrying the two ways into family, work, and money (10:1–5, 15–16, 24–25).

The mouth and the heart. Speech gives life or hides violence, and it reveals what fills the heart (10:6, 11, 13–14, 18–21, 31–32).

Diligence and laziness. The diligent hand and the son who gathers in summer stand against the slack hand, the sleeper in harvest, and the lazy man who frustrates those who depend on him (10:4–5, 26).

The LORD behind the outcomes. God sustains the righteous, gives the blessing that makes rich, and makes His way strength for the upright and destruction for evildoers (10:3, 22, 27, 29).

What lasts. The memory, hope, and foundation of the righteous endure, while the name, expectation, and place of the wicked perish (10:7, 25, 28, 30).

Key Words & Concepts

The proverbs of Solomon (10:1). The heading that begins the first collection of short sayings, which runs to 22:16. Its sayings are grouped by repeated words and subjects, so they reward reading in clusters.

The righteous and the wicked (10:3, 6–7, 24–25, 28–32). Righteousness in Proverbs is a way of life before God, rooted in the fear of the LORD; the wicked walk the opposite way. These two names mark the broad division beneath every other character type in the book.

A prating fool (10:8, 10). One who chatters without listening. The fool in Proverbs is morally deficient rather than slow, and his own talking keeps him from hearing the instruction that would keep him standing.

The lazy man (10:26). The NKJV’s term here for the sluggard first portrayed in 6:6–11. Proverbs distinguishes laziness from inability, illness, or genuine need.

The blessing of the LORD (10:22). Riches that come as God’s gift, without the sorrow that grasping or ill-gotten wealth brings. Some translators render the second line to say that toil adds nothing to it; either way the LORD is the source, and the verse is no formula for wealth.

The fear of the LORD (10:27). A right view of God: terror for those who defy Him and awe for all at His glory, majesty, and holiness, for He is unlike any other. It tends to lengthen life, a general pattern and not a rule that explains every early death.
