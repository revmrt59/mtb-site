Chapter Study

Proverbs 11

Verse-by-Verse
Proverbs 11:1
Proverbs 11:1
NKJV

Proverbs 11:1 Dishonest scales are an abomination to the LORD, But a just weight is His delight.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:1 The LORD detests the use of dishonest scales,but he delights in accurate weights. (NLT)

Main Idea

God cares about the marketplace. Dishonest scales used to cheat a buyer are “an abomination to the LORD,” something He finds detestable, while an honest weight is “His delight,” so ordinary trade is carried on under His eye.

Verse Explanation

God cares about the marketplace. Dishonest scales used to cheat a buyer are “an abomination to the LORD,” something He finds detestable, while an honest weight is “His delight,” so ordinary trade is carried on under His eye.

“Dishonest scales are an abomination to the LORD,”

Trade in Israel ran on balance scales and stone weights. A merchant could cheat by using a lighter stone when he sold and a heavier one when he bought, and the customer would never know. The Law had already forbidden it: “You shall have honest scales, honest weights” (Leviticus 19:36). Proverbs calls such scales “an abomination to the LORD,” something He finds detestable. That is strong language for a market trick, and it is meant to be. God does not treat cheating in business as a small matter, because it wrongs a neighbor in secret and lies about the value of things.

“But a just weight is His delight.”

The contrast is just as strong in the other direction. An honest weight is “His delight.” God is not only offended by fraud; He takes pleasure in honesty. The ordinary work of buying and selling is carried on under His eye, and a fair deal at a market stall pleases Him. This is wisdom as skill for living applied to work and money: doing business in a way God enjoys. The same pairing of abomination and delight returns in 11:20, where it is applied to the heart.

Study Notes

The 1611 King James Version gives the Hebrew of two phrases in its margin. For its “false balance” (the NKJV’s “Dishonest scales”) it notes balances of deceit, and for “a just weight” it notes a perfect stone, that is, a whole, full-weight stone. The Hebrew word for a weight is the ordinary word for a stone, because weights were stones carried in a bag (compare Deuteronomy 25:13, “differing weights, a heavy and a light”).

Proverbs returns to the subject in 16:11 and in 20:10 and 20:23, where “Diverse weights” are again called “an abomination to the LORD.”

Proverbs 11:2
Proverbs 11:2
NKJV

Proverbs 11:2 When pride comes, then comes shame; But with the humble is wisdom.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:2 Pride leads to disgrace,but with humility comes wisdom. (NLT)

Main Idea

Pride comes in, and shame follows close behind it. Wisdom is found with the humble, who know their limits and stay teachable, so humility is the ground where wisdom grows.

Verse Explanation

Pride comes in, and shame follows close behind it. Wisdom is found with the humble, who know their limits and stay teachable, so humility is the ground where wisdom grows.

“When pride comes, then comes shame;”

Pride and shame arrive together, one right behind the other. Pride is an inflated view of oneself that refuses to admit limits or take correction. It promises honor, but it brings the opposite. The proud person overreaches, refuses advice, and sooner or later is exposed and brought low. Proverbs will say it again: “Pride goes before destruction” (16:18).

“But with the humble is wisdom.”

The humble are modest about themselves and know their limits. Because they do not think they know everything, they stay teachable, and so wisdom is found with them. Humility is the ground where wisdom grows. The saying fits the fear of the LORD, which begins with a right view of God and therefore a right view of oneself. Proverbs says elsewhere that the LORD “gives grace to the humble” (3:34). Pride receives shame; humility receives wisdom.

Study Notes

In Hebrew the two key words of the first line sound alike: the word for pride is zadon and the word for shame is qalon. The matching sound ties the two words together, so the ear hears shame arriving with pride just as the line says it does, and the saying becomes easy to remember.

The Hebrew word for “humble” occurs only here in the Old Testament and means modest. A verb from the same root appears in Micah 6:8, “to walk humbly with your God,” where it describes a lowly, modest walk before God. The humble of this verse have that same modesty, which keeps them teachable.

Proverbs 11:3
Proverbs 11:3
NKJV

Proverbs 11:3 The integrity of the upright will guide them, But the perversity of the unfaithful will destroy them.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:3 Honesty guides good people;dishonesty destroys treacherous people. (NLT)

Main Idea

Integrity works like a guide for the upright, keeping them on course when choices are hard. The crooked dealing of the unfaithful does the opposite, leading them toward their own destruction.

Verse Explanation

Integrity works like a guide for the upright, keeping them on course when choices are hard. The crooked dealing of the unfaithful does the opposite, leading them toward their own destruction.

“The integrity of the upright will guide them,”

Integrity is wholeness, being the same person inside and out. For the upright it works like a guide. When choices are hard or confusing, integrity already knows what it will not do, and that keeps them on course. They do not need to calculate every angle, because they are not looking for a way around what is right.

“But the perversity of the unfaithful will destroy them.”

The unfaithful are the treacherous, people who break trust. Their perversity, their crooked and twisted dealing, does the opposite of a guide: it leads them to their own destruction. The same character that drives their choices finally ruins them. The saying shows that a person’s character is not neutral. It either guides or destroys.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word for “perversity” means crookedness or crooked dealing, and in the Old Testament it occurs only here and in 15:4. It stands against “upright,” a word whose basic sense is straight. The ESV renders it “crookedness” and the KJV “perverseness.”

Proverbs 11:4
Proverbs 11:4
NKJV

Proverbs 11:4 Riches do not profit in the day of wrath, But righteousness delivers from death.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:4 Riches won’t help on the day of judgment,but right living can save you from death. (NLT)

Main Idea

Wealth cannot buy anything “in the day of wrath,” when God’s judgment comes. Only righteousness delivers from death, echoing the second line of 10:2 and putting money in its true, limited place.

Verse Explanation

Wealth cannot buy anything “in the day of wrath,” when God’s judgment comes. Only righteousness delivers from death, echoing the second line of 10:2 and putting money in its true, limited place.

“Riches do not profit in the day of wrath,”

“The day of wrath” is the day when God’s judgment comes, whether in a disaster within history or at death. On that day money cannot buy a way out. The prophet Zephaniah said the same thing about the day of the LORD: “Neither their silver nor their gold Shall be able to deliver them In the day of the LORD’s wrath” (Zephaniah 1:18). Wealth can do many things, but it cannot deal with God.

“But righteousness delivers from death.”

The second line repeats 10:2 word for word. Righteousness, living rightly before God, does what riches cannot. Placed in this chapter, the saying begins a cluster of sayings about deliverance (11:4–9). It also puts money in its true, limited place, a theme the chapter will return to at its end: “He who trusts in his riches will fall” (11:28).

Study Notes

The second line is identical to 10:2b in the NKJV; the first line here replaces “Treasures of wickedness profit nothing” with a reference to the day of wrath.

Deliverance links the sayings of this section: “delivers” (11:4), “deliver” (11:6), “is delivered” (11:8), and “will be delivered” (11:9). The Hebrew uses two different verbs for it, one in 11:4 and 11:6 and another in 11:8 and 11:9, so the link is one of thought carried by related words rather than a single repeated word.

Proverbs 11:5
Proverbs 11:5
NKJV

Proverbs 11:5 The righteousness of the blameless will direct his way aright, But the wicked will fall by his own wickedness.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:5 The godly are directed by honesty;the wicked fall beneath their load of sin. (NLT)

Main Idea

A blameless person’s righteousness keeps his road straight and level before him. The wicked man needs no outside enemy to bring him down; he trips over his own wickedness.

Verse Explanation

A blameless person’s righteousness keeps his road straight and level before him. The wicked man needs no outside enemy to bring him down; he trips over his own wickedness.

“The righteousness of the blameless will direct his way aright,”

The blameless person is whole and sincere before God. His righteousness makes his road straight and level, clearing the way in front of him. The picture is of a path without hidden pits and sudden turns. Living rightly does not remove every hardship, but it keeps a person from the self-made obstacles that crooked living creates.

“But the wicked will fall by his own wickedness.”

The wicked man needs no outside enemy. He trips over his own wickedness. The very schemes and habits he uses to get ahead become the obstacles that bring him down. This verse and the next show sin becoming its own trap.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb behind “direct his way aright” means to make straight or level, and it is the same verb used in 3:6, “He shall direct your paths.” The 1611 King James Version points this out with the marginal note “Heb. rectifie,” meaning to make right or straight. The ESV says that righteousness “keeps” his way straight.

Proverbs 11:6
Proverbs 11:6
NKJV

Proverbs 11:6 The righteousness of the upright will deliver them, But the unfaithful will be caught by their lust.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:6 The godliness of good people rescues them;the ambition of treacherous people traps them. (NLT)

Main Idea

Righteousness rescues the upright, while the unfaithful are trapped by their own lust, caught by the very desires they chased. Together with verse 5, the saying shows sin becoming its own snare.

Verse Explanation

Righteousness rescues the upright, while the unfaithful are trapped by their own lust, caught by the very desires they chased. Together with verse 5, the saying shows sin becoming its own snare.

“The righteousness of the upright will deliver them,”

Righteousness rescues those who live by it. This line nearly repeats the thought of 11:5 and 11:4, and it adds the idea of rescue. The upright are protected by the very life they have chosen.

“But the unfaithful will be caught by their lust.”

The unfaithful, the treacherous, are caught like an animal in a trap. What catches them is their own lust, the craving and greed that drove them. They chased their desires, and their desires became the snare. Together with 11:5, this saying shows that sin is self-defeating. God has made His world so that wickedness tends to undo itself.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word for “lust” is the same word translated “desire” in 10:3. It means a craving, usually an evil one, though it can also mean ruin. The ESV says the treacherous are “taken captive” by their lust, and the KJV renders the word “naughtiness” in its older sense of wickedness.

Proverbs 11:7
Proverbs 11:7
NKJV

Proverbs 11:7 When a wicked man dies, his expectation will perish, And the hope of the unjust perishes.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:7 When the wicked die, their hopes die with them,for they rely on their own feeble strength. (NLT)

Main Idea

Death ends everything the wicked man was hoping for. His expectations rested on this life and on his own schemes, so when he dies, his hope perishes with him.

Verse Explanation

Death ends everything the wicked man was hoping for. His expectations rested on this life and on his own schemes, so when he dies, his hope perishes with him.

“When a wicked man dies, his expectation will perish,”

Death ends everything the wicked man was counting on. His expectations were tied to this life, to his schemes, his wealth, and his plans. When he dies, they die with him. Nothing he hoped for reaches past the grave.

“And the hope of the unjust perishes.”

The second line repeats the first in different words, which drives the point home. The hope of the unjust perishes. This verse looks back to 10:28, “the expectation of the wicked will perish,” and shows what that means at the end of a life. By contrast, the hope of the righteous rests on God, which is why it can end in gladness.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word the NKJV renders “the unjust” is a plural form that can come from a noun meaning strength or wealth, or from a similar-looking word meaning wickedness. The NKJV and KJV take it in the second sense. The ESV takes it as the expectation of “wealth,” and the 1984 NIV as what he expected from his “power.” Either way the second line restates the loss of everything the wicked man relied on.

Proverbs 11:8
Proverbs 11:8
NKJV

Proverbs 11:8 The righteous is delivered from trouble, And it comes to the wicked instead.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:8 The godly are rescued from trouble,and it falls on the wicked instead. (NLT)

Main Idea

The righteous person is rescued out of trouble, and the trouble falls on the wicked instead. The saying pictures a reversal like Haman hanged on the gallows he had built for Mordecai (Esther 7:10).

Verse Explanation

The righteous person is rescued out of trouble, and the trouble falls on the wicked instead. The saying pictures a reversal like Haman hanged on the gallows he had built for Mordecai (Esther 7:10).

“The righteous is delivered from trouble,”

The righteous person is rescued out of trouble. The saying does not say the righteous never face trouble; they are delivered from it. God brings them through and out.

“And it comes to the wicked instead.”

The trouble does not disappear. It lands on the wicked in place of the righteous. Scripture tells this kind of reversal more than once. In the book of Esther, the gallows Haman built for Mordecai became his own: “So they hanged Haman on the gallows that he had prepared for Mordecai” (Esther 7:10). The saying reflects God’s justice, which often turns evil back on those who planned it.

Study Notes

The KJV says the wicked comes in his stead, and the ESV that the wicked “walks into” the trouble, both bringing out the idea of one taking the other’s place.

Proverbs 11:9–15 — Neighbors, the City, and Faithful Speech

Proverbs 11:9
Proverbs 11:9
NKJV

Proverbs 11:9 The hypocrite with his mouth destroys his neighbor, But through knowledge the righteous will be delivered.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:9 With their words, the godless destroy their friends,but knowledge will rescue the righteous. (NLT)

Main Idea

The hypocrite uses his mouth to ruin his neighbor with flattery, lies, or slander. The righteous are delivered through knowledge, seeing through his words and escaping the harm he intends.

Verse Explanation

The hypocrite uses his mouth to ruin his neighbor with flattery, lies, or slander. The righteous are delivered through knowledge, seeing through his words and escaping the harm he intends.

“The hypocrite with his mouth destroys his neighbor,”

The hypocrite is the godless person who hides his real character behind a pleasant face. His weapon is his mouth. With flattery, lies, rumors, or false advice, he can ruin the person who lives next to him and trusts him. The damage is real, though no blow is struck. The section opens here because the next several sayings trace how one person’s life and speech reach the people around him.

“But through knowledge the righteous will be delivered.”

The righteous escape “through knowledge.” They know the truth, know God’s ways, and can see through smooth words, so the hypocrite’s mouth does not take them in. Knowledge here is a protection. The deliverance theme of 11:4–8 continues, and this time the rescue comes by discernment.

Study Notes

The ESV and the 1984 NIV render “The hypocrite” as “the godless,” the sense of the Hebrew word, which describes a person estranged from God rather than only a pretender.

Proverbs 11:10
Proverbs 11:10
NKJV

Proverbs 11:10 When it goes well with the righteous, the city rejoices; And when the wicked perish, there is jubilation.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:10 The whole city celebrates when the godly succeed;they shout for joy when the wicked die. (NLT)

Main Idea

A whole city feels the fortunes of the righteous and the wicked. When the righteous prosper the city rejoices, and when the wicked perish people shout for joy, because both kinds of lives touch the common good.

Verse Explanation

A whole city feels the fortunes of the righteous and the wicked. When the righteous prosper the city rejoices, and when the wicked perish people shout for joy, because both kinds of lives touch the common good.

“When it goes well with the righteous, the city rejoices;”

A whole city feels the fortunes of its people. When the righteous prosper, the city is glad, because their success benefits others. They deal honestly, help their neighbors, and use their influence for good. Their well-being spreads to the common good.

“And when the wicked perish, there is jubilation.”

When the wicked fall, people shout for joy. This is not cruelty but relief. The wicked had been a burden on the community through oppression, fraud, and fear, and their removal lifts that weight. Proverbs says the same of rulers: “When the righteous are in authority, the people rejoice; But when a wicked man rules, the people groan” (29:2). Both kinds of lives touch everyone around them.

Study Notes

The word “jubilation” translates a Hebrew word for a ringing shout of joy. The ESV renders it with “shouts.”

Proverbs 11:11
Proverbs 11:11
NKJV

Proverbs 11:11 By the blessing of the upright the city is exalted, But it is overthrown by the mouth of the wicked.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:11 Upright citizens are good for a city and make it prosper,but the talk of the wicked tears it apart. (NLT)

Main Idea

Verse 10 finds its explanation here. The good that the upright bring to a community lifts a city up, while the mouth of the wicked, with its lies and stirring of strife, can overthrow it.

Verse Explanation

Verse 10 finds its explanation here. The good that the upright bring to a community lifts a city up, while the mouth of the wicked, with its lies and stirring of strife, can overthrow it.

“By the blessing of the upright the city is exalted,”

This saying explains the one before it. The upright bring blessing to a community, through their honest work, their wise counsel, their prayers, and the good they do. That blessing lifts a city up and makes it a better place to live. Proverbs says it on a larger scale: “Righteousness exalts a nation” (14:34).

“But it is overthrown by the mouth of the wicked.”

The wicked can tear a city down, and the instrument named here is the mouth. Lies, slander, false promises, and words that stir up strife can break apart what took years to build. This verse continues the concern of 11:9: what the hypocrite’s mouth does to a neighbor, the wicked mouth can do to a whole city.

Study Notes

None.

Proverbs 11:12
Proverbs 11:12
NKJV

Proverbs 11:12 He who is devoid of wisdom despises his neighbor, But a man of understanding holds his peace.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:12 It is foolish to belittle one’s neighbor;a sensible person keeps quiet. (NLT)

Main Idea

Belittling a neighbor shows a lack of wisdom. The man of understanding keeps quiet when he could mock or criticize, guarding both the neighbor’s dignity and the peace between them.

Verse Explanation

Belittling a neighbor shows a lack of wisdom. The man of understanding keeps quiet when he could mock or criticize, guarding both the neighbor’s dignity and the peace between them.

“He who is devoid of wisdom despises his neighbor,”

To despise a neighbor is to look down on him and belittle him, often out loud. Proverbs says this reveals a lack of wisdom, in Hebrew literally a lack of heart. The person who mocks his neighbor shows that he does not understand people, himself, or the value God places on others. He also damages a relationship he will need.

“But a man of understanding holds his peace.”

The man of understanding keeps quiet. He may see the neighbor’s faults, and he may be tempted to criticize, but he holds his tongue. That guards both the neighbor’s dignity and the peace between them. In a chapter concerned with the neighbor and the city, this restraint is part of what keeps a community whole.

Study Notes

The KJV margin gives the literal Hebrew of “devoid of wisdom” as destitute of heart, the expression met earlier at 9:4 and 10:13.

Proverbs 11:13
Proverbs 11:13
NKJV

Proverbs 11:13 A talebearer reveals secrets, But he who is of a faithful spirit conceals a matter.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:13 A gossip goes around telling secrets,but those who are trustworthy can keep a confidence. (NLT)

Main Idea

A talebearer goes around revealing what was told in confidence. Someone with a faithful spirit keeps the matter covered, and trustworthiness with a secret is a true mark of faithfulness.

Verse Explanation

A talebearer goes around revealing what was told in confidence. Someone with a faithful spirit keeps the matter covered, and trustworthiness with a secret is a true mark of faithfulness.

“A talebearer reveals secrets,”

The talebearer is someone who goes around carrying stories from house to house. Whatever is told to him in confidence, he passes on. The Law forbade this: “You shall not go about as a talebearer among your people” (Leviticus 19:16). A talebearer damages trust, reputations, and friendships, and no one is safe confiding in him.

“But he who is of a faithful spirit conceals a matter.”

The person with a faithful spirit is trustworthy deep down. When something is told to him, he keeps it covered. Being reliable with a secret is a true mark of faithfulness. This is not covering up wrongdoing; it is refusing to spread what is private and harmful. Faithfulness in small matters of speech shows a person’s character.

Study Notes

The KJV margin gives the literal Hebrew of “A talebearer” as one who walks about, being a talebearer, which pictures the person going from place to place with his stories. Proverbs repeats the first line in 20:19.

Proverbs 11:14
Proverbs 11:14
NKJV

Proverbs 11:14 Where there is no counsel, the people fall; But in the multitude of counselors there is safety.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:14 Without wise leadership, a nation falls;there is safety in having many advisers. (NLT)

Main Idea

Without wise guidance a people stumble and fall. Safety lies in many counselors, because no single person sees everything, and this holds for nations as well as for families and personal decisions.

Verse Explanation

Without wise guidance a people stumble and fall. Safety lies in many counselors, because no single person sees everything, and this holds for nations as well as for families and personal decisions.

“Where there is no counsel, the people fall;”

Counsel is wise guidance. Without it, a people stumble and fall. The word “people” suggests a nation or community, and the saying fits the city of the surrounding sayings. When leaders act without advice, or when no one is willing to give honest guidance, the whole community suffers for it.

“But in the multitude of counselors there is safety.”

Safety lies in many counselors. No single person sees everything. Several wise advisers can catch what one would miss, test plans, and warn against danger. This is humility in practice, the opposite of the pride that brings shame (11:2). The principle holds for nations, and it holds just as well for families, churches, and personal decisions.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word for “counsel” means direction or guidance, and it is the same word translated “wise counsel” in 1:5. The ESV renders it “guidance.” A people without that guidance has no one to set its course, and so it falls.

Proverbs repeats the thought in 15:22 and 24:6, which ends with the same phrase, “in a multitude of counselors there is safety.”

Proverbs 11:15
Proverbs 11:15
NKJV

Proverbs 11:15 He who is surety for a stranger will suffer, But one who hates being surety is secure.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:15 There’s danger in putting up security for a stranger’s debt;it’s safer not to guarantee another person’s debt. (NLT)

Main Idea

Pledging oneself for a stranger’s debt invites suffering, as the father warned in 6:1–5. Refusing such a pledge keeps a person secure, which is wise counsel about rash commitments rather than a ban on helping others.

Verse Explanation

Pledging oneself for a stranger’s debt invites suffering, as the father warned in 6:1–5. Refusing such a pledge keeps a person secure, which is wise counsel about rash commitments rather than a ban on helping others.

“He who is surety for a stranger will suffer,”

A surety is someone who guarantees another person’s debt, promising to pay if the borrower does not. The father warned his son about this in 6:1–5: “If you have shaken hands in pledge for a stranger” (6:1), get out of it quickly. To do it for a stranger, someone whose character and circumstances you do not know, is to put yourself at the mercy of a person you cannot trust. Such a pledge will bring suffering.

“But one who hates being surety is secure.”

The person who refuses to make such pledges stays secure. This is wise counsel about rash commitments, not a ban on helping people. Proverbs elsewhere commends generosity and kindness to the poor (11:24–25). The point is to avoid putting oneself under an obligation that may destroy one’s household. A wise person can give what he is able to give, rather than signing away what he cannot afford to lose.

Study Notes

The KJV says the surety “shall smart” for it. The 1611 edition notes in its margin that the Hebrew is literally shall be sore broken, and that the Hebrew behind “suretiship” is literally those that strike hands, the gesture that sealed a pledge.

Proverbs 11:16–23 — The Sure Reward of Righteousness

Proverbs 11:16
Proverbs 11:16
NKJV

Proverbs 11:16 A gracious womanretains honor, But ruthless men retain riches.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:16 A gracious woman gains respect,but ruthless men gain only wealth. (NLT)

Main Idea

A gracious woman gains and keeps honor, while ruthless men gain and keep only riches. Both hold on to something, and the comparison leaves no doubt which possession is worth more.

Verse Explanation

A gracious woman gains and keeps honor, while ruthless men gain and keep only riches. Both hold on to something, and the comparison leaves no doubt which possession is worth more.

“A gracious woman retains honor,”

A gracious woman is marked by kindness and charm of character. She gains honor and keeps it. Her honor is not seized; it grows out of the way she treats people, and it stays with her. In a section about what each kind of life finally holds, she stands as the first example.

“But ruthless men retain riches.”

Ruthless men are violent and overbearing. They also hold on to something, but what they hold is only riches, often taken from others by force. The verb is the same in both lines: each grasps and keeps what he has pursued. The comparison leaves no doubt about which possession is worth more. Honor won by kindness outlasts wealth seized by violence, and the next verses explain why.

Study Notes

For “ruthless men” the ESV has “violent men” and the KJV “strong men.”

The Greek Septuagint expands this verse with additional lines that are not in the Hebrew; the NKJV follows the Hebrew.

Proverbs 11:17
Proverbs 11:17
NKJV

Proverbs 11:17 The merciful man does good for his own soul, But he who is cruel troubles his own flesh.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:17 Your kindness will reward you,but your cruelty will destroy you. (NLT)

Main Idea

Mercy blesses the one who shows it, doing good to his own soul. Cruelty works the other way and brings trouble on the cruel person’s own flesh, so the way we treat others comes back on ourselves.

Verse Explanation

Mercy blesses the one who shows it, doing good to his own soul. Cruelty works the other way and brings trouble on the cruel person’s own flesh, so the way we treat others comes back on ourselves.

“The merciful man does good for his own soul,”

The merciful man shows kindness and loyal love to others. The surprise of the saying is that he also benefits himself. Kindness does good to his own soul, his inner life and his whole person. Mercy guards his heart from the bitterness that hardness breeds, and it is pleasing to God. This is the usual way God’s world answers kindness, not a promise of health or companionship. A merciful person may still be ill, lonely, or in distress, and that does not prove a lack of mercy.

“But he who is cruel troubles his own flesh.”

Cruelty works the other way. The cruel man means to hurt others, but he brings trouble on his own body and life. His harshness comes back on him in broken relationships, in enemies, and in the way God’s world answers cruelty. “Soul” and “flesh” together cover the whole person. The way we treat others returns on ourselves.

Study Notes

The ESV reads the verse with the simpler “benefits himself” and “hurts himself.”

Proverbs 11:18
Proverbs 11:18
NKJV

Proverbs 11:18 The wicked man does deceptive work, But he who sows righteousness will have a sure reward.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:18 Evil people get rich for the moment,but the reward of the godly will last. (NLT)

Main Idea

The wicked man’s work is deceptive, and its wages are as unreliable as the way he earned them. The one who sows righteousness has a sure reward, a harvest that will not disappoint him.

Verse Explanation

The wicked man’s work is deceptive, and its wages are as unreliable as the way he earned them. The one who sows righteousness has a sure reward, a harvest that will not disappoint him.

“The wicked man does deceptive work,”

The wicked man’s work is deceptive in two ways. He earns by deceit, and his wages deceive him too. What he gains looks solid but does not last or satisfy. His work rests on lies, and its results are as unreliable as the way he got them.

“But he who sows righteousness will have a sure reward.”

The righteous person is pictured as a farmer who sows. What he plants is righteousness, and his harvest is sure. The reward may take time, as a harvest does, but it will come and it will be real. Paul states the same principle: “whatever a man sows, that he will also reap” (Galatians 6:7). The contrast is between deceptive wages and a sure reward.

Study Notes

The Hebrew words for “deceptive” and “reward” sound alike: sheqer means a lie or deception, and seker means wages or hire. The first line speaks literally of wages of deception and the second of a wage of truth, so the wordplay sets false wages beside true wages. The ESV renders the first line with “deceptive wages.”

Proverbs 11:19
Proverbs 11:19
NKJV

Proverbs 11:19 As righteousness leads to life, So he who pursues evil pursues it to his own death.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:19 Godly people find life;evil people find death. (NLT)

Verse Explanation

Righteousness and evil each lead somewhere. Just as righteousness leads to life, the one who chases evil is running toward his own death, even if he cannot see where the road ends.

“As righteousness leads to life,”

Righteousness is a road, and it leads somewhere: to life. Proverbs has said this many times (10:16; 10:17). Life here means the full life God gives, including length of days and His favor, set against the death that awaits the other road.

“So he who pursues evil pursues it to his own death.”

The other person is chasing evil, running hard after it. He thinks he is chasing pleasure or gain, but what he is really pursuing is his own death. He may not see where the road ends, but the end is fixed. The two lines show that everyone is going somewhere, and that the direction of a life matters more than how fast a person is moving.

Study Notes

The first Hebrew word of the verse can be read as “so” or “thus,” as in the NKJV and KJV, or as a word meaning right, true, or firm, as in the ESV, which speaks of one who is steadfast in righteousness. Either reading sets life against death.

The repetition of “His delight” from 11:1 links honest business and an honest heart as the same kind of life.

“But the posterity of the righteous will be delivered.”

The descendants of the righteous will be delivered. The blessing of a godly life commonly reaches the next generation. Children raised by righteous parents receive an example, teaching, prayer, and often protection from the consequences that wickedness brings on a family. The picture is jarring on purpose: a gold ring in a pig’s snout. Beauty without discretion is just as out of place and wasted, and the principle holds for men as well as women.

“As a ring of gold in a swine’s snout,”

The picture is jarring on purpose. A gold nose ring was a piece of women’s jewelry; when Abraham’s servant found Rebekah, he says, “I put the nose ring on her nose” (Genesis 24:47). Now imagine that ring in the snout of a pig, an unclean animal rooting in the mud. The gold is real and valuable, but it is completely out of place.

“So is a lovely woman who lacks discretion.”

Beauty without discretion is just as out of place and wasted. Discretion is good judgment and moral sense, knowing what is fitting. Without it, outward loveliness is attached to a life that drags it through the dirt. The saying is not an insult to women, and it does not make women the problem. The principle holds for men just as well; honor is no more fitting for a fool (26:1). The book closes by saying that “beauty is passing,” and that the woman who fears the LORD is the one to be praised (31:30).

Study Notes

The Hebrew word for “discretion” means taste, and by extension judgment, a sense of what is fitting. The line says literally that the woman turns aside from taste. The 1611 King James Version, which reads “without discretion,” gives the Hebrew in its margin as “departeth from.” She has the beauty, but she has turned away from the judgment that would make it fitting.

Verse Explanation

Generosity works by a paradox: one person scatters freely and yet gains more, while another holds back more than is right and still ends in poverty. Giving does not drain a life the way hoarding does, though the saying describes a pattern rather than a financial formula.

“There is one who scatters, yet increases more;”

The picture is of a farmer scattering seed, or of a person scattering his goods freely to others. By ordinary arithmetic he should end up with less, yet he “increases more.” Generosity works by a paradox in God’s world. What is given away freely does not drain a life the way people expect. The generous person is enriched in relationships, in trust, and under God’s blessing, and often in material ways as well.

“And there is one who withholds more than is right, But it leads to poverty.”

The other person holds back “more than is right,” keeping what he should have given or paid. He expects to protect his wealth, but his stinginess “leads to poverty.” Hoarding does not secure a life; it shrinks it. The saying describes a pattern of God’s world, not a financial formula. It commends an open hand without promising that every gift will be returned in money.

Study Notes

This verse has three lines in the NKJV rather than the usual two. The ESV says that the one who withholds “suffers want.”

Refreshment given returns to the giver under God’s care, the principle Paul applies to sowing and reaping in giving (2 Corinthians 9:6), though the saying commends generosity without promising a financial return.

“The generous soul will be made rich,”

In Hebrew the generous person is literally a soul of blessing, someone whose life brings blessing to others. That person “will be made rich,” or in the older image, made fat, a picture of abundance and health in a land where food was never guaranteed. The blessing a generous person gives comes back to him.

“And he who waters will also be watered himself.”

In a dry land, water is life. The one who waters others, refreshing them with help, encouragement, and provision, will be refreshed himself. Paul applies the same principle to giving: “he who sows bountifully will also reap bountifully” (2 Corinthians 9:6). The saying commends generosity and describes how God commonly answers it. It does not promise a financial return on every gift, and it should not turn giving into an investment strategy. The point is that generosity does not leave the giver dry.

Study Notes

The KJV says that the liberal soul shall be “made fat,” and its margin gives the literal Hebrew of “liberal soul” as soul of blessing.

Proverbs 11:30
Proverbs 11:30
NKJV

Proverbs 11:30 The fruit of the righteous is a tree of life, And he who wins souls is wise.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:30 The seeds of good deeds become a tree of life;a wise person wins friends. (NLT)

Main Idea

The fruit of a righteous life is a tree of life, giving nourishment and life to others, just as Wisdom herself was called in 3:18; the tree of life is finally restored in Christ (Revelation 22:2). The one who wins souls is wise, gaining people for what is good, and the words rightly apply to bringing others to God.

Verse Explanation

The fruit of a righteous life is a tree of life, giving nourishment and life to others, just as Wisdom herself was called in 3:18; the tree of life is finally restored in Christ (Revelation 22:2). The one who wins souls is wise, gaining people for what is good, and the words rightly apply to bringing others to God.

“The fruit of the righteous is a tree of life,”

The fruit of a righteous life is what it produces: its words, deeds, and influence. That fruit is a tree of life, giving nourishment and life to others. The image goes back to the tree in Eden (Genesis 2:9), and Proverbs has already used it of Wisdom herself: “She is a tree of life to those who take hold of her” (3:18). A righteous life does not feed itself only; it becomes a source of life for those around it.

At the layered level, the tree of life points to Christ. The tree lost in Eden is restored in Him, and the Spirit promises, “To him who overcomes I will give to eat from the tree of life” (Revelation 2:7). The righteous bear life-giving fruit because they are joined to the One who is life.

“And he who wins souls is wise.”

The Hebrew of this line is literally “takes lives” or “takes souls.” Elsewhere the same expression can mean to kill; Proverbs itself says greed “takes away the life of its owners” (1:19). Here it is clearly positive, since the one who does it “is wise.” It is usually understood as winning or gaining people, drawing them to what is good. The wise person, whose life bears fruit like a tree of life, attracts others and wins them to the way of life. The NKJV’s “wins souls” rightly supports applying the words to bringing others to God, an application the rest of Scripture affirms; Daniel 12:3 speaks of “those who turn many to righteousness.” That application is legitimate, but it is secondary; the verse’s first point is that a righteous and wise life draws others toward life.

Study Notes

The KJV margin gives the literal Hebrew of “winneth” as “taketh.” The ESV says the wise man “captures” souls, and the NLT “a wise person wins friends.”

The Greek Septuagint reads the second line very differently, as a statement that the lives of the lawless are taken away before their time. The NKJV follows the Hebrew.

Proverbs 11:31

Proverbs 11:31 If the righteous will be recompensed on the earth, How much more the ungodly and the sinner.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 11:31 If the righteous are rewarded here on earth,what will happen to wicked sinners? (NLT)

Main Idea

The verse argues from the lesser to the greater. If even the righteous are repaid on the earth, including God’s discipline, how much more will the ungodly and the sinner be repaid; Peter draws on the same argument (1 Peter 4:18).

Verse Explanation

The verse argues from the lesser to the greater. If even the righteous are repaid on the earth, including God’s discipline, how much more will the ungodly and the sinner be repaid; Peter draws on the same argument (1 Peter 4:18).

“If the righteous will be recompensed on the earth,”

To be recompensed is to be repaid. “On the earth” means in this life. The verse begins from what the reader already accepts: even the righteous are repaid here, for good and also for wrong. God disciplines those He loves: “For whom the LORD loves He corrects” (3:12). The righteous are not exempt from His dealings with them.

“How much more the ungodly and the sinner.”

The argument moves from the lesser to the greater. If God repays even the righteous, how much more will He repay the ungodly and the sinner. No one should imagine that the wicked will slip through God’s justice. Peter quotes this verse to suffering believers, in the form found in the Greek Septuagint: “If the righteous one is scarcely saved, Where will the ungodly and the sinner appear?” (1 Peter 4:18). The wording differs from the Hebrew, but the argument is the same. Peter draws from it a call to keep trusting God, who judges rightly.

Study Notes

The KJV begins the verse with “Behold,” rendering a Hebrew particle that introduces the first line as a given.

Peter’s quotation follows the Greek translation, which reads “scarcely saved” where the Hebrew has “recompensed on the earth.” The NKJV of each passage translates its own text.
