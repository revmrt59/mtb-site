Proverbs 12

Verse-by-Verse
Proverbs 12:1
Proverbs 12:1
NKJV

Proverbs 12:1 Whoever loves instruction loves knowledge, But he who hates correction is stupid.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:1 To learn, you must love discipline;it is stupid to hate correction. (NLT)

Main Idea

Loving instruction and loving knowledge go together, because no one grows in understanding without being taught and corrected. The one who hates correction makes himself “stupid,” as senseless as a creature that cannot be taught, and he chooses that condition by refusing to listen.

Verse Explanation

Loving instruction and loving knowledge go together, because no one grows in understanding without being taught and corrected. The one who hates correction makes himself “stupid,” as senseless as a creature that cannot be taught, and he chooses that condition by refusing to listen.

“Whoever loves instruction loves knowledge,”

Instruction here is discipline, the training and correction that form a person’s thinking and conduct. Loving it and loving knowledge go together. No one grows in understanding without being taught, and being taught always involves being corrected. The person who truly wants to know will welcome the discipline that brings knowledge, even when it stings.

“But he who hates correction is stupid.”

The word “stupid” is blunt. It describes the dullness of an animal that cannot be trained, an ox or a mule that will not learn. The person who hates correction chooses that condition for himself. His problem is not a lack of intelligence but a refusal to be taught. This is a mark of the fool throughout Proverbs, and it is a danger anyone can fall into. The verse is not an insult to hurl at others but a warning about what happens to a heart that will not listen.

Study Notes

The KJV renders “stupid” as “brutish.” The Hebrew word means brutishness or stupidity, the dullness of a brute beast, which is the picture behind both English words.

The ESV renders “instruction” as “discipline,” which brings out the sense of correction in the word translated “instruction.”

Proverbs 12:2
Proverbs 12:2
NKJV

Proverbs 12:2 A good man obtains favor from the LORD, But a man of wicked intentions He will condemn.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:2 The LORD approves of those who are good,but he condemns those who plan wickedness. (NLT)

Main Idea

The LORD Himself responds to character. A good man receives His favor, while the man who plots wickedness falls under His condemnation, whatever he manages to hide from others.

Verse Explanation

The LORD Himself responds to character. A good man receives His favor, while the man who plots wickedness falls under His condemnation, whatever he manages to hide from others.

“A good man obtains favor from the LORD,”

The LORD Himself responds to character. A good man, one who is kind, upright, and generous, receives His favor. Favor is God’s goodwill and acceptance, His smile on a life. Wisdom promised the same thing in 8:35: “whoever finds me finds life, And obtains favor from the LORD.” Goodness and wisdom lead to the same place.

“But a man of wicked intentions He will condemn.”

The other man plots evil. His schemes may be hidden from people, but not from God, and the LORD will condemn him. The line focuses on intentions, what a person plans in his heart, not only what he manages to do. God judges the inner purposes as well as the outward acts.

Study Notes

The phrase “obtains favor from the LORD” appears in the NKJV of 8:35 and again in 18:22.

Proverbs 12:3
Proverbs 12:3
NKJV

Proverbs 12:3 A man is not established by wickedness, But the root of the righteous cannot be moved.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:3 Wickedness never brings stability,but the godly have deep roots. (NLT)

Main Idea

Wickedness cannot give a person secure footing, however much it seems to prosper. The righteous are like a tree with roots so deep it cannot be moved, and this image of the root returns in verse 12 to frame the section.

Verse Explanation

Wickedness cannot give a person secure footing, however much it seems to prosper. The righteous are like a tree with roots so deep it cannot be moved, and this image of the root returns in verse 12 to frame the section.

“A man is not established by wickedness,”

To be established is to be made firm and secure. Wickedness cannot do that. It may bring quick gain or temporary power, but it never gives a person secure footing. Whatever is built on wickedness rests on nothing that will hold.

“But the root of the righteous cannot be moved.”

The righteous are pictured as a tree with deep roots. Storms may shake the branches, but the root holds. Their stability comes from what they are rooted in: God and His ways. This image of the root returns in 12:12, where it bears fruit, and the two sayings frame the first section of the chapter. It also echoes 10:30, “The righteous will never be removed.”

Study Notes

None.

Proverbs 12:4
Proverbs 12:4
NKJV

Proverbs 12:4 An excellent wifeis the crown of her husband, But she who causes shame is like rottenness in his bones.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:4 A worthy wife is a crown for her husband,but a disgraceful woman is like cancer in his bones. (NLT)

Main Idea

An excellent wife, a woman of strength and worth like the one praised in 31:10–31, is a crown that brings her husband honor. A wife who causes shame works on him like rot in the bones, decay from the inside, and the book holds husbands to the same faithfulness (5:15–19).

Verse Explanation

An excellent wife, a woman of strength and worth like the one praised in 31:10–31, is a crown that brings her husband honor. A wife who causes shame works on him like rot in the bones, decay from the inside, and the book holds husbands to the same faithfulness (5:15–19).

“An excellent wife is the crown of her husband,”

The excellent wife is a woman of strength, worth, and capability. The same description is used of the woman praised at the end of the book, “Who can find a virtuous wife?” (31:10), and of Ruth (Ruth 3:11). Such a wife is her husband’s crown. She brings him honor and dignity, and people see her worth reflected on him, as 31:23 says: “Her husband is known in the gates.”

“But she who causes shame is like rottenness in his bones.”

A wife who brings shame on her husband works on him like rot in the bones, decay from the inside where it cannot be seen but cannot be escaped. The contrast is between honor displayed on the head and corruption hidden in the bones. The saying is not a burden laid on women alone. The book holds husbands to the same faithfulness and delight in their wives (5:15–19), and it says elsewhere that “The wise woman builds her house” (14:1).

Study Notes

The NKJV’s “excellent wife” and the “virtuous wife” of 31:10 render the same Hebrew phrase, a woman of strength or worth, and Boaz uses the same phrase of Ruth in Ruth 3:11. The KJV reads “virtuous woman” here as well.

The Hebrew word for “rottenness” means decay, and in the Old Testament it is always used in a figurative sense. It is related to the verb “rot” in 10:7 and appears again in 14:30, “envy is rottenness to the bones.” Rot in the bones is decay at the deepest level of the body, hidden from view yet weakening a person from within. That is the picture of how a wife who causes shame affects her husband.

Proverbs 12:5
Proverbs 12:5
NKJV

Proverbs 12:5 The thoughts of the righteous are right, But the counsels of the wicked are deceitful.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:5 The plans of the godly are just;the advice of the wicked is treacherous. (NLT)

Main Idea

Character begins in the mind. The thoughts of the righteous are just and fair, while even the counsel the wicked offer is crafted to deceive.

Verse Explanation

Character begins in the mind. The thoughts of the righteous are just and fair, while even the counsel the wicked offer is crafted to deceive.

“The thoughts of the righteous are right,”

Character begins in the mind. The righteous think in just and fair ways. Their plans and intentions are straight before any action is taken. What they do grows out of what they think.

“But the counsels of the wicked are deceitful.”

The wicked also make plans, and they give advice to others. But their counsel is crafted to deceive. It serves their own ends while pretending to help. The verse warns the reader to consider the character of anyone whose advice he takes, because the counsel of the wicked cannot be trusted even when it sounds reasonable.

Study Notes

Where the NKJV says the thoughts of the righteous are “right,” the ESV says they are “just.”

Proverbs 12:6
Proverbs 12:6
NKJV

Proverbs 12:6 The words of the wicked are, "Lie in wait for blood," But the mouth of the upright will deliver them.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:6 The words of the wicked are like a murderous ambush,but the words of the godly save lives. (NLT)

Main Idea

The wicked talk as if planning an ambush: “Lie in wait for blood.” Their words are weapons aimed at others, but the mouth of the upright brings deliverance, using speech to rescue rather than destroy.

Verse Explanation

The wicked talk as if planning an ambush: “Lie in wait for blood.” Their words are weapons aimed at others, but the mouth of the upright brings deliverance, using speech to rescue rather than destroy.

“The words of the wicked are, ‘Lie in wait for blood,’”

The wicked talk like men planning an ambush. Their words are aimed at harming others, whether by violence, false testimony, or slander. The phrase recalls the gang of the first chapter, whose call was, “Let us lie in wait to shed blood” (1:11). The father warned his son against such companions, and here the saying shows their speech for what it is.

“But the mouth of the upright will deliver them.”

The mouth of the upright does the opposite. It rescues. The upright may speak up for the person in danger, give a true testimony that clears the innocent, or warn the one who is about to be trapped. The word “them” most naturally refers to those whom the wicked threaten, and it may include the upright themselves. Speech that could destroy is used to save.

Study Notes

The NLT renders the second line “but the words of the godly save lives,” taking “them” as the intended victims.

Proverbs 12:7
Proverbs 12:7
NKJV

Proverbs 12:7 The wicked are overthrown and are no more, But the house of the righteous will stand.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:7 The wicked die and disappear,but the family of the godly stands firm. (NLT)

Main Idea

When the wicked are overthrown, they are gone and nothing of them remains. The house of the righteous keeps standing, its family and its name secure after the storm has passed.

Verse Explanation

When the wicked are overthrown, they are gone and nothing of them remains. The house of the righteous keeps standing, its family and its name secure after the storm has passed.

“The wicked are overthrown and are no more,”

The wicked are overthrown, turned upside down, and then they are simply gone. Nothing of them remains. The saying is brief and final, like the whirlwind of 10:25 that leaves no trace of the wicked behind it.

“But the house of the righteous will stand.”

The house here includes the family and the household as well as the building. When the storm has passed, the house of the righteous is still standing. The contrast is between being gone and remaining. Jesus told a similar story about two houses, one on the rock and one on the sand (Matthew 7:24–27). Proverbs repeats the thought: “The house of the wicked will be overthrown, But the tent of the upright will flourish” (14:11).

Study Notes

The NLT renders the second line “but the family of the godly stands firm,” bringing out the sense of “house” as household.

Proverbs 12:8
Proverbs 12:8
NKJV

Proverbs 12:8 A man will be commended according to his wisdom, But he who is of a perverse heartwill be despised.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:8 A sensible person wins admiration,but a warped mind is despised. (NLT)

Main Idea

People generally praise a man according to the wisdom he shows. A perverse heart earns contempt, since twisted thinking eventually shows itself to others.

Verse Explanation

People generally praise a man according to the wisdom he shows. A perverse heart earns contempt, since twisted thinking eventually shows itself to others.

“A man will be commended according to his wisdom,”

People generally praise a man according to the wisdom he shows. Good sense, sound judgment, and the skill of handling life well earn respect over time. The saying describes how people usually respond, not a guarantee that every wise person will be honored in his own lifetime.

“But he who is of a perverse heart will be despised.”

A perverse heart is twisted and crooked. The person who has one may hide it for a while, but twisted thinking eventually shows itself, and others come to despise him. The contrast is between the praise that wisdom earns and the contempt that a crooked heart brings on itself.

Study Notes

The 1611 King James Version notes in its margin that the Hebrew behind “of a perverse heart” is literally perverse of heart; the ESV speaks of a “twisted mind.”

Proverbs 12:9
Proverbs 12:9
NKJV

Proverbs 12:9 Better is the one who is slighted but has a servant, Than he who honors himself but lacks bread.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:9 Better to be an ordinary person with a servantthan to be self-important but have no food. (NLT)

Main Idea

Better to be thought little of and still have a servant, with enough to live on, than to put on a show of importance while lacking bread. Real provision matters more than the appearance of status.

Verse Explanation

Better to be thought little of and still have a servant, with enough to live on, than to put on a show of importance while lacking bread. Real provision matters more than the appearance of status.

“Better is the one who is slighted but has a servant,”

This is one of the “better … than” sayings of Proverbs, which compare two situations and say which is preferable. The first person is slighted. People think little of him, and he has no great reputation. But he “has a servant,” which means he has enough to live on and to employ help. His real circumstances are better than his reputation.

“Than he who honors himself but lacks bread.”

The second man honors himself. He puts on a show of importance and acts like a great man, yet he does not have bread to eat. His reputation is a front with nothing behind it. The saying exposes pride that cares about appearances more than substance. It is better to be modest and provided for than to be important in one’s own eyes and hungry.

Study Notes

The ESV describes the second man as one who plays the “great man.”

Proverbs 12:10
Proverbs 12:10
NKJV

Proverbs 12:10 A righteous man regards the life of his animal, But the tender mercies of the wicked are cruel.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:10 The godly care for their animals,but the wicked are always cruel. (NLT)

Main Idea

Righteousness reaches even to how a man treats his animals; he cares about their life and needs (compare Deuteronomy 25:4). The wicked are cruel even in what they call kindness, because a hard heart turns every mercy sour.

Verse Explanation

Righteousness reaches even to how a man treats his animals; he cares about their life and needs (compare Deuteronomy 25:4). The wicked are cruel even in what they call kindness, because a hard heart turns every mercy sour.

“A righteous man regards the life of his animal,”

To regard the life of his animal is to know and care about its needs, its hunger, thirst, rest, and wellbeing. Righteousness reaches even into the stable and the field. The Law expressed the same concern: “You shall not muzzle an ox while it treads out the grain” (Deuteronomy 25:4). A righteous man’s goodness is not only for people who can return it; it extends to creatures that depend on him.

“But the tender mercies of the wicked are cruel.”

The wicked are cruel even in what they call kindness. Their “tender mercies,” whatever compassion they think they show, come out harsh, because a hard heart turns every mercy sour. The saying shows that character goes all the way through a person. How someone treats an animal that cannot speak for itself reveals what kind of person he is.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word behind “tender mercies” means compassion and is related to the word for the womb, the inward parts regarded as the seat of tender feeling. The 1611 King James Version offers “bowels” in its margin as an alternative rendering, the older English way of speaking about deep compassion. The line says that even what the wicked call compassion comes out cruel.

The phrase “regards the life” is literally knows the soul of his animal, using the common Hebrew word for life or soul. To know the animal’s life is to pay attention to what it needs.

Proverbs 12:11
Proverbs 12:11
NKJV

Proverbs 12:11 He who tills his land will be satisfied with bread, But he who follows frivolity is devoid of understanding.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:11 A hard worker has plenty of food,but a person who chases fantasies has no sense. (NLT)

Main Idea

Steady work on one’s own land brings enough bread to be satisfied. Chasing frivolous, empty pursuits instead shows a lack of understanding, because it trades real provision for things that cannot feed anyone.

Verse Explanation

Steady work on one’s own land brings enough bread to be satisfied. Chasing frivolous, empty pursuits instead shows a lack of understanding, because it trades real provision for things that cannot feed anyone.

“He who tills his land will be satisfied with bread,”

The farmer who works his own land steadily, plowing, planting, and tending, will have enough bread to be satisfied. The saying commends diligent, ordinary work. Nothing about it is glamorous, but it feeds a household.

“But he who follows frivolity is devoid of understanding.”

The other person chases frivolity, empty and worthless pursuits, whether get-rich-quick schemes, idle company, or fantasies of easy success. He shows that he lacks understanding; in Hebrew he literally lacks heart, meaning the inner understanding and judgment that the heart stands for in Proverbs. He trades real provision for things that cannot feed anyone. Proverbs repeats the first line in 28:19 and gives the result: the one who follows frivolity “will have poverty enough!”

Study Notes

The KJV reads “vain persons” where the NKJV has “frivolity,” taking the word as worthless people rather than worthless things. The ESV renders it “worthless pursuits.”

Proverbs 12:12
Proverbs 12:12
NKJV

Proverbs 12:12 The wicked covet the catch of evil men, But the root of the righteous yields fruit.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:12 Thieves are jealous of each other’s loot,but the godly are well rooted and bear their own fruit. (NLT)

Main Idea

The wicked covet what other evil men have caught by their schemes, always wanting more plunder. The righteous need no such prey, because their root, first mentioned in verse 3, yields fruit of its own.

Verse Explanation

The wicked covet what other evil men have caught by their schemes, always wanting more plunder. The righteous need no such prey, because their root, first mentioned in verse 3, yields fruit of its own.

“The wicked covet the catch of evil men,”

The wicked look at what other evil men have caught by their schemes, the plunder and gain of crime, and they want it for themselves. Their life is driven by envy of other people’s spoil. The picture is of a hunter or fisherman eyeing another’s catch. It is a life of grasping, never satisfied, always looking at someone else’s net.

“But the root of the righteous yields fruit.”

The righteous do not need anyone else’s plunder. Their root, the one that “cannot be moved” in 12:3, produces fruit of its own. What they have grows from within, from a life rooted in God. The section closes with this picture of a rooted life bearing fruit, set against the restless coveting of the wicked.

Study Notes

The first line is difficult in Hebrew. The word the NKJV renders “catch” is spelled like Hebrew words for a hunting net and for a stronghold, so it can describe either what evil men catch or the place they hold. The 1611 King James Version reads “the net” and gives “the fortresse” in its margin as an alternative, while the ESV renders it “the spoil.” On any of these readings the wicked are looking enviously at what other evil men have.

Proverbs 12:13–23 — Truthful Lips and a Lying Tongue

Proverbs 12:13
Proverbs 12:13
NKJV

Proverbs 12:13 The wicked is ensnared by the transgression of his lips, But the righteous will come through trouble.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:13 The wicked are trapped by their own words,but the godly escape such trouble. (NLT)

Main Idea

The wicked man is trapped by the sins of his own lips, as his lies and boasts become the snare that catches him. The righteous come through trouble and escape, because their words do not turn against them.

Verse Explanation

The wicked man is trapped by the sins of his own lips, as his lies and boasts become the snare that catches him. The righteous come through trouble and escape, because their words do not turn against them.

“The wicked is ensnared by the transgression of his lips,”

The wicked man is caught in a trap, and the trap is his own lips. His lies contradict each other, his boasts expose him, his angry words provoke enemies, and his false testimony is found out. The sins of his speech become the snare that holds him. This saying opens the long cluster on speech in 12:13–23.

“But the righteous will come through trouble.”

The righteous also face trouble, but they come through it and out of it. Their words do not turn against them, because they have not spoken lies that must be defended or covered. Truthful speech is not only right; it is also a protection. The person with nothing to hide in his words has less to fear when trouble comes.

Study Notes

The 1611 King James Version gives the Hebrew of the first line in its margin as the snare of the wicked is in the transgression of lips. The Hebrew word order puts the sin of the lips first, so the emphasis falls on the lips as the place where the trap is set. The ESV says the righteous “escapes” from trouble.

Proverbs 12:14
Proverbs 12:14
NKJV

Proverbs 12:14 A man will be satisfied with good by the fruit of his mouth, And the recompense of a man's hands will be rendered to him.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:14 Wise words bring many benefits,and hard work brings rewards. (NLT)

Main Idea

Words bear fruit as surely as work does. A man’s speech returns to him in good, and the work of his hands is repaid to him, so both mouth and hands are a kind of sowing.

Verse Explanation

Words bear fruit as surely as work does. A man’s speech returns to him in good, and the work of his hands is repaid to him, so both mouth and hands are a kind of sowing.

“A man will be satisfied with good by the fruit of his mouth,”

Words bear fruit as surely as work does. What a person says comes back to him. Good words, true counsel, kind encouragement, and honest testimony produce a harvest of good that satisfies the speaker. The image is of a man eating the produce of what he has planted, and here the planting is done with the mouth.

“And the recompense of a man’s hands will be rendered to him.”

The second line adds the work of the hands. What a man does is repaid to him. Mouth and hands together cover a person’s whole activity, speech and action. Both are a kind of sowing, and both bring a return. This is not a contrast but a pair of matching statements, and it places speech on the same level of importance as work.

Study Notes

Proverbs returns to the phrase “the fruit of his mouth” in 13:2 and 18:20.

Proverbs 12:15
Proverbs 12:15
NKJV

Proverbs 12:15 The way of a fool is right in his own eyes, But he who heeds counsel is wise.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:15 Fools think their own way is right,but the wise listen to others. (NLT)

Main Idea

The fool’s way always looks right to him, and that blindness is exactly what keeps him a fool. The wise man is the one who listens to counsel, willing to let others show him what he cannot see.

Verse Explanation

The fool’s way always looks right to him, and that blindness is exactly what keeps him a fool. The wise man is the one who listens to counsel, willing to let others show him what he cannot see.

“The way of a fool is right in his own eyes,”

The fool’s way always looks right to him. That is the heart of his folly. He does not question his own judgment, so he never sees the need for correction, and so he never changes. Proverbs warns against this blindness repeatedly: “Do not be wise in your own eyes” (3:7). The fool is not lacking in confidence; he is lacking in humility.

“But he who heeds counsel is wise.”

The wise man listens to counsel. He knows that his own eyes can deceive him, so he is willing to let others show him what he cannot see. This ties back to 12:1: the one who loves instruction loves knowledge. The difference between a fool and a wise man is not that one is always right and the other always wrong; it is that one listens and the other does not.

Study Notes

Similar sayings about being right “in his own eyes” appear in 16:2, 21:2, and 26:12.

Proverbs 12:16
Proverbs 12:16
NKJV

Proverbs 12:16 A fool's wrath is known at once, But a prudent man covers shame.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:16 A fool is quick-tempered,but a wise person stays calm when insulted. (NLT)

Main Idea

A fool lets his anger show at once, reacting before he thinks. The prudent man covers an insult or shame, choosing not to make it public and not to let the quarrel grow.

Verse Explanation

A fool lets his anger show at once, reacting before he thinks. The prudent man covers an insult or shame, choosing not to make it public and not to let the quarrel grow.

“A fool’s wrath is known at once,”

The fool cannot hide his anger. When he is offended or insulted, everyone knows about it immediately. He reacts before he thinks, and his temper goes on display. Proverbs says elsewhere, “A fool vents all his feelings, But a wise man holds them back” (29:11).

“But a prudent man covers shame.”

The prudent man covers shame. When he is insulted or slighted, he does not make a scene. He absorbs the offense, chooses not to make it public, and does not let the quarrel grow. This is not weakness but self-control. Proverbs says, “his glory is to overlook a transgression” (19:11). The prudent man’s restraint protects both his own dignity and the peace.

Study Notes

The Hebrew behind “at once” is literally in the day, meaning on the same day. The 1611 King James Version reads “presently” and gives the Hebrew in its margin as in that day. The fool’s anger shows on the very day he is provoked. The ESV says that the prudent man “ignores” an insult.

Proverbs 12:17
Proverbs 12:17
NKJV

Proverbs 12:17 He who speaks truth declares righteousness, (H6664) But a false witness, deceit.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:17 An honest witness tells the truth;a false witness tells lies. (NLT)

Main Idea

Speaking the truth brings what is right into the open, especially when a person gives testimony. A false witness produces only deceit, and in court such a lie can destroy an innocent person.

Verse Explanation

Speaking the truth brings what is right into the open, especially when a person gives testimony. A false witness produces only deceit, and in court such a lie can destroy an innocent person.

“He who speaks truth declares righteousness,”

The setting is likely the law court, where a witness gives testimony. The person who speaks the truth brings what is right into the open. His testimony makes justice possible. Truthful speech serves righteousness by letting the facts be known.

“But a false witness, deceit.”

A false witness produces only deceit. In court, such a lie can destroy an innocent person, and the Law treated it with great seriousness: “You shall not bear false witness against your neighbor” (Exodus 20:16). The LORD also names “A false witness who speaks lies” among the things He hates (6:19). The verse sets two kinds of witnesses side by side and shows what each one brings into the courtroom.

Study Notes

Proverbs returns to the true and false witness in 14:5 and 14:25. The ESV renders “declares righteousness” with the words “honest evidence,” which brings out the courtroom setting.

Proverbs 12:18
Proverbs 12:18
NKJV

Proverbs 12:18 There is one who speaks like the piercings of a sword, But the tongue of the wise promotes health.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:18 Some people make cutting remarks,but the words of the wise bring healing. (NLT)

Main Idea

Some people speak words that cut like sword thrusts, careless and cruel. The tongue of the wise brings healing instead, so the same tongue that can wound can also restore.

Verse Explanation

Some people speak words that cut like sword thrusts, careless and cruel. The tongue of the wise brings healing instead, so the same tongue that can wound can also restore.

“There is one who speaks like the piercings of a sword,”

Some people speak carelessly and rashly, and their words cut like sword thrusts. A thoughtless remark, a cutting criticism, or a cruel joke can wound deeply and leave scars that last long after the words are forgotten by the speaker. The image is vivid on purpose. Words are not harmless.

“But the tongue of the wise promotes health.”

The tongue of the wise brings healing. The same organ that can wound can restore. Wise words comfort, encourage, correct gently, and help a hurting person recover. Proverbs says elsewhere, “Pleasant words are like a honeycomb, Sweetness to the soul and health to the bones” (16:24). The contrast is between the sword and the medicine, and every person chooses which his tongue will be.

Study Notes

The ESV speaks of “rash words” that are like “sword thrusts,” bringing out the idea of speaking rashly. Where the NKJV says the tongue of the wise “promotes health,” the KJV says simply that it “is health.”

Proverbs 12:19
Proverbs 12:19
NKJV

Proverbs 12:19 The truthful lip shall be established forever, But a lying tongue is but for a moment.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:19 Truthful words stand the test of time,but lies are soon exposed. (NLT)

Main Idea

Truth lasts, and the truthful lip will be established forever. A lying tongue may seem to win, but its success lasts only a moment before it is exposed.

Verse Explanation

Truth lasts, and the truthful lip will be established forever. A lying tongue may seem to win, but its success lasts only a moment before it is exposed.

“The truthful lip shall be established forever,”

Truth lasts. The truthful lip will be established, made firm and lasting, forever. Truthful speech does not have to be held up by further invention; it rests on what actually happened. Lies are different, because one lie usually needs another to support it. Truthful people may still have to defend themselves against false accusations, but what they say does not depend on fabrication to survive. The person who speaks truth builds something that endures.

“But a lying tongue is but for a moment.”

A lie may seem to win. It may get a person out of trouble or gain an advantage. But its success lasts only a moment. Lies are exposed, contradicted, or simply collapse under their own weight. The contrast is between forever and a moment, and it shows how short-sighted lying is.

Study Notes

The NLT renders the second line “but lies are soon exposed.”

The Hebrew expression behind “for a moment” uses a verb that lexicons explain as making a twinkling, the time it takes to blink an eye. The lie lasts about that long.

Proverbs 12:20
Proverbs 12:20
NKJV

Proverbs 12:20 Deceit is in the heart of those who devise evil, (H7451) But counselors of peace have joy.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:20 Deceit fills hearts that are plotting evil;joy fills hearts that are planning peace! (NLT)

Main Idea

Those who plot evil carry deceit in their hearts. Those who counsel peace have joy, finding gladness in the good they seek for others.

Verse Explanation

Those who plot evil carry deceit in their hearts. Those who counsel peace have joy, finding gladness in the good they seek for others.

“Deceit is in the heart of those who devise evil,”

Those who plot evil carry deceit in their hearts. Their plans depend on hiding their real purpose. The heart is the source; the scheme is the result. A person who devises evil cannot be honest, because honesty would expose him.

“But counselors of peace have joy.”

The contrast is not quite what the reader expects. Instead of saying that counselors of peace have honesty, the verse says they have joy. Those who give advice that leads to peace, who work to reconcile people and settle quarrels, find gladness in the good they seek for others. Their inner life is marked by joy rather than by the anxious deceit of those who plot evil.

Study Notes

The ESV speaks of those who “plan peace,” which makes the parallel with “devise” in the first line clearer.

Proverbs 12:21
Proverbs 12:21
NKJV

Proverbs 12:21 No grave trouble will overtake the righteous, But the wicked shall be filled with evil.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:21 No harm comes to the godly,but the wicked have their fill of trouble. (NLT)

Main Idea

No grave trouble will finally overtake the righteous, while the wicked are filled with evil of their own making. The saying states a general pattern under God’s rule, not a promise that the righteous never suffer.

Verse Explanation

No grave trouble will finally overtake the righteous, while the wicked are filled with evil of their own making. The saying states a general pattern under God’s rule, not a promise that the righteous never suffer.

“No grave trouble will overtake the righteous,”

The word rendered grave trouble describes real harm or disaster. The saying declares that no such harm will finally overtake the righteous. God watches over them. This is a general pattern under God’s rule, not a promise that the righteous never suffer. Scripture is full of righteous people who suffered, and Proverbs itself knows that the righteous face trouble (12:13). The point is that trouble will not have the last word over them.

“But the wicked shall be filled with evil.”

The wicked, by contrast, are filled with evil, trouble of their own making. They pursued evil, and now they have their fill of it. The contrast is between being kept from harm and being saturated with it.

Study Notes

Where the NKJV has “grave trouble,” the ESV has “No ill” and the KJV “no evil.” The NKJV’s wording narrows the sense to serious harm.

Proverbs 12:22
Proverbs 12:22
NKJV

Proverbs 12:22 Lying lips are an abomination to the LORD, But those who deal truthfully are His delight.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:22 The LORD detests lying lips,but he delights in those who tell the truth. (NLT)

Main Idea

Lying is no small fault in God’s sight: “Lying lips are an abomination to the LORD.” Those who deal truthfully are His delight, the same abomination-and-delight pairing found in 11:1 and 11:20, now applied to speech.

Verse Explanation

Lying is no small fault in God’s sight: “Lying lips are an abomination to the LORD.” Those who deal truthfully are His delight, the same abomination-and-delight pairing found in 11:1 and 11:20, now applied to speech.

“Lying lips are an abomination to the LORD,”

Lying is no small fault in God’s sight. Lying lips are an abomination to Him, something He detests. A “lying tongue” is among the seven things the LORD hates (6:17). God is the God of truth, and lies attack the very thing that makes trust, justice, and community possible.

“But those who deal truthfully are His delight.”

Those who deal truthfully, not only in what they say but in how they act, are His delight. The abomination-and-delight pairing of 11:1 and 11:20 returns, now applied to speech. In 11:1 it was the merchant’s scales, in 11:20 the heart, and here the lips. God cares about honesty in every part of life, and He takes pleasure in people who live truthfully.

Study Notes

The ESV speaks of those who “act faithfully,” bringing out that the Hebrew describes faithful conduct as well as truthful speech.

Proverbs 12:23
Proverbs 12:23
NKJV

Proverbs 12:23 A prudent man conceals knowledge, (H1847) But the heart of fools proclaims foolishness.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:23 The wise don’t make a show of their knowledge,but fools broadcast their foolishness. (NLT)

Main Idea

The prudent man does not display everything he knows but holds his knowledge until it will help. Fools cannot hold back, and their hearts keep broadcasting their foolishness for everyone to hear.

Verse Explanation

The prudent man does not display everything he knows but holds his knowledge until it will help. Fools cannot hold back, and their hearts keep broadcasting their foolishness for everyone to hear.

“A prudent man conceals knowledge,”

The prudent man does not display everything he knows. He holds his knowledge until the right time, when it will help. This is not secrecy or false modesty but discretion. Proverbs said earlier, “Wise people store up knowledge” (10:14). There is “a time to keep silence, And a time to speak” (Ecclesiastes 3:7).

“But the heart of fools proclaims foolishness.”

Fools cannot hold back. Their hearts keep broadcasting their foolishness for everyone to hear. They speak to show off, but what they show is folly. Proverbs puts it another way: “a fool lays open his folly” (13:16). The speech cluster that began with the trap of the wicked man’s lips (12:13) ends here with the difference between a mouth that knows when to stay closed and one that never does.

Study Notes

None.

Proverbs 12:24
Proverbs 12:24
NKJV

Proverbs 12:24 The hand of the diligent will rule, But the lazy man will be put to forced labor.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:24 Work hard and become a leader;be lazy and become a slave. (NLT)

Main Idea

Diligent hands lead to responsibility and rule. The lazy man ends up under forced labor, made to work for others, because he would not discipline himself to work freely.

Verse Explanation

Diligent hands lead to responsibility and rule. The lazy man ends up under forced labor, made to work for others, because he would not discipline himself to work freely.

“The hand of the diligent will rule,”

The diligent person works with care and steady effort, and over time that work leads to responsibility. People trust him with more, and he rises to positions of leadership. Steady, careful work commonly earns trust and responsibility in a household, a business, or a community, though the proverb is not a promise of authority to every diligent person. Proverbs said in 10:4 that “the hand of the diligent makes rich”; here it brings authority.

“But the lazy man will be put to forced labor.”

The lazy man ends up in forced labor, made to work for others under compulsion. The irony is sharp. He would not discipline himself to work freely, so in the end he must work anyway, but for someone else and on someone else’s terms. He is the same lazy man whose portrait Proverbs drew in 6:6–11. The saying describes the outcome of unwillingness, not the hardship of those who cannot work.

Study Notes

The KJV says the slothful shall be “under tribute,” and the 1611 margin gives “deceitfull” as an alternative for “slothfull.” Two Hebrew words with the same spelling lie behind that choice, one meaning slackness and the other deceit. Set against the diligent in this verse, the word means slackness; it is the same word behind the “slack hand” of 10:4.

Proverbs 12:25
Proverbs 12:25
NKJV

Proverbs 12:25 Anxiety in the heart of man causes depression, But a good word makes it glad.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:25 Worry weighs a person down;an encouraging word cheers a person up. (NLT)

Main Idea

Anxiety settles on the heart and weighs it down, the heaviness the NKJV calls “depression.” A good word lifts that burdened heart and makes it glad, so kind and timely speech is real help to a person carrying worry.

Verse Explanation

Anxiety settles on the heart and weighs it down, the heaviness the NKJV calls “depression.” A good word lifts that burdened heart and makes it glad, so kind and timely speech is real help to a person carrying worry.

“Anxiety in the heart of man causes depression,”

Anxiety is worry, the fear and care that settle on a person’s heart. It weighs the heart down and bends it low. The NKJV’s word “depression” describes that heaviness, a heart pressed down by its burden. It is not a medical diagnosis, and the verse does not explain every kind of sorrow or illness. It describes what most people know from experience: worry is heavy, and it pulls a person down.

“But a good word makes it glad.”

A good word lifts that burdened heart and makes it glad. A word of encouragement, comfort, hope, or kindness, spoken at the right time, can change how a person carries his load. Proverbs says, “a word spoken in due season, how good it is!” (15:23). The saying honors the value of encouragement. It does not suggest that a kind word alone will cure deep or lasting depression, or that those who struggle simply lack encouragement or faith. It shows that God cares about the inner life and that kind speech is real help to a person carrying worry.

Study Notes

The KJV says that heaviness in the heart maketh it “stoop,” which keeps the picture of the heart bending low under its weight. The ESV says anxiety “weighs” a person down.

Proverbs 12:26
Proverbs 12:26
NKJV

Proverbs 12:26 The righteous should choose his friends carefully, For the way of the wicked leads them astray.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:26 The godly give good advice to their friends;the wicked lead them astray. (NLT)

Main Idea

The righteous should choose friends with care, because companions influence the path a person walks. The way of the wicked leads people astray, so the wrong friends can pull even a good man off course.

Verse Explanation

The righteous should choose friends with care, because companions influence the path a person walks. The way of the wicked leads people astray, so the wrong friends can pull even a good man off course.

“The righteous should choose his friends carefully,”

The NKJV reads this line as counsel about friendship. The righteous person should be careful about whom he walks with, because companions influence the path a person takes. The father’s very first warning concerned companions: “My son, if sinners entice you, Do not consent” (1:10). Later Proverbs says, “He who walks with wise men will be wise, But the companion of fools will be destroyed” (13:20).

“For the way of the wicked leads them astray.”

The reason is given in the second line. The way of the wicked leads people astray. Wrong friends can pull even a good man off course, and the wicked themselves are misled by the way they have chosen. The verse warns that friendship is never neutral. Choosing friends is part of choosing a path.

Study Notes

The first line is difficult in Hebrew, and translations differ widely because its first word can be taken in two ways. Read as a form of a verb meaning to search out or explore, it gives the NKJV’s “choose his friends carefully,” the 1984 NIV’s rendering about being cautious in friendship, or the ESV and NASB rendering “a guide” to his neighbor. Read as a form of a verb meaning to excel, it gives the KJV’s “more excellent” than his neighbour, with “abundant” in the 1611 margin. All agree in the second line that the way of the wicked leads astray.

Proverbs 12:27
Proverbs 12:27
NKJV

Proverbs 12:27 The lazy man does not roast what he took in hunting, But diligence is man's precious possession.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:27 Lazy people don’t even cook the game they catch,but the diligent make use of everything they find. (NLT)

Main Idea

The lazy man may go hunting but will not bother to roast what he caught, leaving the work unfinished and the meal wasted. Diligence is a precious possession because it carries a task through to the end.

Verse Explanation

The lazy man may go hunting but will not bother to roast what he caught, leaving the work unfinished and the meal wasted. Diligence is a precious possession because it carries a task through to the end.

“The lazy man does not roast what he took in hunting,”

The lazy man may get up the energy to go hunting, and he may even catch something. But then he will not bother to roast it. The work stays unfinished, and the meal is wasted. This fits the portrait of the lazy man that Proverbs began in 6:6–11 and fills out in later sayings: he does not finish what he starts. Proverbs has a similar picture of the lazy man who “buries his hand in the bowl, And will not so much as bring it to his mouth again” (19:24).

“But diligence is man’s precious possession.”

Diligence is a treasure, precious because it carries a task through to the end. It turns a catch into a meal, a crop into a harvest, and a plan into a finished work. The contrast is between effort wasted halfway and effort that finishes. A diligent spirit is worth more than many possessions, because it makes good use of what a person has.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb rendered “roast” occurs only here in the Old Testament, so its meaning cannot be checked against other passages. The NKJV, KJV, ESV, NASB, and the 1984 NIV all take it as roasting: the lazy man does not cook what he caught. Lexicons also give a sense to set in motion or start, which would mean that the lazy man does not even stir up his game, the animals he means to hunt. Either way the lazy man gains nothing from the hunt. The second line is also difficult because its Hebrew word order is unusual; the ESV speaks of the “precious wealth” the diligent man gains, and the KJV of the “substance” of a diligent man as precious.

Together with 12:24 this saying frames the final section with the diligent and the lazy man.

Proverbs 12:28
Proverbs 12:28
NKJV

Proverbs 12:28 In the way of righteousness is life, (H2416) And in its pathway there is no death.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 12:28 The way of the godly leads to life;that path does not lead to death. (NLT)

Main Idea

Life is found in the way of righteousness, and on its pathway there is no death. The certain point is that righteousness leads to life; the second line may also point toward life beyond death, a hope that Scripture as a whole brings fully to light in Christ (2 Timothy 1:10).

Verse Explanation

Life is found in the way of righteousness, and on its pathway there is no death. The certain point is that righteousness leads to life; the second line may also point toward life beyond death, a hope that Scripture as a whole brings fully to light in Christ (2 Timothy 1:10).

“In the way of righteousness is life,”

The chapter closes on the road itself. Life is found in the way of righteousness. This is the certain point of the verse and a summary of everything Proverbs has said about the two ways. Wisdom said, “whoever finds me finds life” (8:35), and the righteous way is where that life is lived. Life here includes length of days, God’s favor, and fullness of living now.

“And in its pathway there is no death.”

The second line restates the first from the opposite side: the pathway of righteousness has no death in it. At the least, this means the righteous way does not lead to the death that awaits the wicked. Some interpreters see more here, an early glimpse of life beyond death. Proverbs does say elsewhere that “the righteous has a refuge in his death” (14:32). That fuller hope is consistent with the whole of Scripture, where Christ “has abolished death and brought life and immortality to light through the gospel” (2 Timothy 1:10). It is a possible extension of the verse in the light of the canon, not something to build a doctrine on from this line alone.

Study Notes

The Hebrew of the second line is very compact; it reads literally, and the way of a path, not death. The NKJV, ESV, NASB, and KJV read it as “no death,” and the NLT as “that path does not lead to death.” The 1984 NIV goes further, rendering the line as a path of immortality, taking “not death” as a single idea.
