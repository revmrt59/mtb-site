Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 14
Main Idea:

Proverbs 14 sets the wise woman who builds her house against the foolish one who tears hers down, warns that a way can seem right and still end in death, and finds in the fear of the LORD both strong confidence and a fountain of life.

Setting:

Solomon’s short contrasting sayings continue (10:1–22:16). The words chapter 13 used for the teaching of the wise, “a fountain of life” (13:14), return here for the fear of the LORD (14:27).

Chapter Outline:

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Proverbs 14 begins with a woman and a house: “The wise woman builds her house, But the foolish pulls it down with her hands” (14:1). Her house recalls the one Wisdom built (9:1) and looks ahead to the woman of valor (31:10–31). Whatever a life builds, a home, a marriage, or a livelihood, carries the signature of the wisdom or folly behind it, and the foolish woman’s ruin is her own work.

The middle of the chapter questions what feels certain. “There is a way that seems right to a man, But its end is the way of death” (14:12), repeated word for word at 16:25. Fools laugh off their guilt (14:9), the simple believe every word (14:15), and a laughing face can hide a sorrowing heart (14:13). Because feelings and appearances mislead, the prudent man “considers well his steps.”

Against that uncertainty the chapter sets something firm: “In the fear of the LORD there is strong confidence” (14:26). The right view of God that turns the wise from evil (14:16) also gives His children refuge, and it is “a fountain of life” (14:27). The same God stands behind the poor: “He who oppresses the poor reproaches his Maker” (14:31). Seeing Him rightly changes how a person treats the neighbor others avoid (14:20–21), and such righteousness “exalts a nation” (14:34).

Chapter Sections

Proverbs 14:1–7 — The Wise Woman Builds Her House

The chapter’s first picture is a house built up or pulled down by the woman who lives in it, and the sayings that follow show wisdom at work in an upright walk that reveals what a person thinks of God, in lips that protect the wise, and in the untidy stable where an ox’s strength brings a larger harvest. The section closes with a scoffer who seeks wisdom without finding it and with the counsel to walk away from a man whose lips carry no knowledge.

Proverbs 14:8–15 — The Way That Seems Right

The prudent man studies his own road, while fools deceive themselves and laugh off their guilt, and at the center stands the warning that a road can feel right and still lead to death. Around it the sayings look inward, to a heart whose bitterness and joy no stranger fully shares, to laughter that can cover sorrow, and to the simple who believe every word while the prudent weigh each step.

Proverbs 14:16–25 — Prudence, Mercy to the Poor, and a True Witness

The wise man fears evil enough to turn from it, while the fool rages ahead in self-confidence, the quick-tempered act rashly, and the simple inherit folly by drifting. The section then speaks honestly about how the poor are shunned, calls despising a neighbor sin, blesses the one who shows mercy, and prizes labor over idle chatter and the true witness whose testimony saves lives.

Proverbs 14:26–27 — The Fear of the LORD Is a Fountain of Life

Two sayings name the fear of the LORD as strong confidence and a refuge for His children, and as a spring that keeps turning a person away from the snares of death. The right view of God that makes the defiant tremble is the same view that gives those who fear Him a safe place to stand.

Proverbs 14:28–35 — Righteousness Exalts a Nation

Sayings about the king frame the last section, measuring a ruler’s honor by the people he governs and his favor by the wisdom of his servants. Between them stand the patient man and the sound heart, the poor whose treatment honors or insults their Maker, the righteous who have refuge even in death, and the righteousness that lifts up a whole nation.

Why This Chapter Matters

Proverbs 14 warns that sincerity cannot certify a road. A person can feel sure, follow what seems right, and still arrive at death (14:12), which is why the chapter keeps calling for a prudence that tests words and watches steps (14:8, 15). Jesus describes the same danger: “broad is the way that leads to destruction” (Matthew 7:13).

The chapter also ties honor toward God to the treatment of the poor. The rich gather friends while the poor man is avoided even by his neighbor (14:20), yet mercy to the needy honors their Maker and oppression insults Him (14:31). Alongside this, the sayings on hidden bitterness and sorrow (14:10, 13) teach a compassion that does not judge a person by the surface.

Key Themes to Watch For

Building and tearing down. The wise build a house that lasts, folly pulls down its own, and the house of the wicked is overthrown while the tent of the upright flourishes (14:1, 11).

The way that seems right. The prudent understand their way and consider their steps, while the simple believe every word and a confident path can still end in death (14:8, 12, 15).

The hidden heart. Bitterness, joy, envy, and sorrow lie deeper than others can see, while wisdom rests quietly in the heart of the one who understands (14:10, 13, 30, 33).

Mercy to the poor. Despising a neighbor is sin, and how a person treats the poor is how he treats their Maker (14:20–21, 31).

Truthful testimony. A faithful witness does not lie, and a true witness delivers souls, while a false one pours out lies (14:5, 25).

Key Words & Concepts

The wise woman (14:1). A woman whose wisdom builds up her household. She echoes Wisdom, who “has built her house” (9:1), and anticipates the woman of valor (31:10–31). The saying calls every reader to examine what he or she is building; it does not blame women for every troubled home.

The scoffer (14:6). The one who will not be taught (9:7–8; 13:1). Here he even goes looking for wisdom, but his contempt for correction keeps him from finding it, while knowledge comes easily to a teachable heart.

The backslider in heart (14:14). A person who has turned away inwardly, whatever his outward life may still look like. He is filled with the fruit of his own ways, while a good man is satisfied from above.

The simple and the prudent (14:15, 18). The simple are uncommitted and easily led, so they believe whatever they hear and inherit folly by drifting. The prudent have practical good sense and are “crowned with knowledge” (14:18), the prudence Proverbs was written to give the simple (1:4).

The fear of the LORD (14:26–27). A right view of God. For those who defy Him it means terror, and for all it means awe at His holiness, His being unlike any other. That same awe makes His children secure, because the God they revere is their refuge.

A refuge in his death (14:32). The righteous have a place of safety in God that death cannot take away. The ancient Greek translation reads the line differently, but the NKJV wording most likely expresses hope that reaches beyond the grave.
