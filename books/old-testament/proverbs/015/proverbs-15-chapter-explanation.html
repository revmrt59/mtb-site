Proverbs 15

Verse-by-Verse
Proverbs 15:1
Proverbs 15:1
NKJV

Proverbs 15:1 A soft answer turns away wrath, But a harsh word stirs up anger.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 15:1 A gentle answer deflects anger,but harsh words make tempers flare. (NLT)

Main Idea

Gentle speech generally calms an angry person, while a harsh word stirs anger up further. Gentleness is wise and usually works, though not always, for Jesus answered gently and still suffered at the hands of angry men (1 Peter 2:23).

Verse Explanation

Gentle speech generally calms an angry person, while a harsh word stirs anger up further. Gentleness is wise and usually works, though not always, for Jesus answered gently and still suffered at the hands of angry men (1 Peter 2:23).

“A soft answer turns away wrath,”

An angry person comes at someone with heat, and the reply can either calm him or inflame him further. A soft answer is gentle and quiet, not weak or evasive, but spoken without returning the heat. Such an answer generally turns wrath aside. Proverbs shows the same quiet strength elsewhere: “a gentle tongue breaks a bone” (25:15). Gentleness takes self-control, and it often accomplishes what force cannot.

“But a harsh word stirs up anger.”

A harsh word, a word meant to wound, stirs anger up and makes a hot situation hotter. The saying describes what generally happens and shows the wise way to answer. It is not a promise that gentleness will always be met with peace. Jesus answered with perfect wisdom and still suffered at the hands of angry men; when struck, He replied calmly, “If I have spoken evil, bear witness of the evil; but if well, why do you strike Me?” (John 18:23). The verse should never be used to tell a person who was mistreated that gentler words would have prevented it. Gentleness is wise, not a guarantee.

Study Notes

The Hebrew behind “harsh word” is literally a word of pain, a word that causes hurt. The same Hebrew word for pain describes toil in 14:23. KJV and YLT render it “grievous.” NASB renders “soft” as “gentle.”

The chapter opens with a cluster of sayings on speech (15:1–2, 4, 7), interrupted by the reminder that the LORD sees everything (15:3).

Proverbs 15:2
Proverbs 15:2
NKJV

Proverbs 15:2 The tongue of the wise uses knowledge rightly, But the mouth of fools pours forth foolishness.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 15:2 The tongue of the wise makes knowledge appealing,but the mouth of a fool belches out foolishness. (NLT)

Main Idea

The wise know how to use what they know, saying the right thing in the right way. Fools simply pour out foolishness as if it could not be held back, with no thought for what would help.

Verse Explanation

The wise know how to use what they know, saying the right thing in the right way. Fools simply pour out foolishness as if it could not be held back, with no thought for what would help.

“The tongue of the wise uses knowledge rightly,”

The wise do not simply possess knowledge; they know how to use it. They say the right thing, in the right way, at the right time, so that what they know actually helps. Knowledge can be used to show off, to win arguments, or to crush others, but in the mouth of the wise it is handled well and made attractive and useful to those who hear.

“But the mouth of fools pours forth foolishness.”

The fool’s mouth pours out foolishness like water from a spring that cannot be stopped. There is no filter, no thought about what would help, only a constant stream. The contrast is not mainly between knowing much and knowing little, but between controlled and uncontrolled speech. The wise man’s knowledge serves; the fool’s words simply gush.

Study Notes

The Hebrew behind “uses knowledge rightly” is literally makes knowledge good: the wise present what they know in a way that helps. ESV renders it “commends knowledge,” and NLT “makes knowledge appealing.”

The same Hebrew verb stands behind “pours forth” here and in 15:28, where “the mouth of the wicked pours forth evil.” Translations picture an uncontrolled flow: ESV has “pour out,” and NASB “spouts.”

Proverbs 15:3
Proverbs 15:3
NKJV

Proverbs 15:3 The eyes of the LORD are in every place, Keeping watch on the evil and the good.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 15:3 The LORD is watching everywhere,keeping his eye on both the evil and the good. (NLT)

Main Idea

Nothing escapes God’s sight: “The eyes of the LORD are in every place.” He keeps watch over the evil and the good alike, which is a warning to the one and a comfort to the other.

Verse Explanation

Nothing escapes God’s sight: “The eyes of the LORD are in every place.” He keeps watch over the evil and the good alike, which is a warning to the one and a comfort to the other.

“The eyes of the LORD are in every place,”

In the middle of sayings about speech comes a reminder that the LORD sees everything. There is no place hidden from Him, no room in a house or corner of a city where His eyes do not reach. Hagar, alone in the wilderness, named the LORD “You-Are-the-God-Who-Sees” (Genesis 16:13). Proverbs states the same truth as a general fact of life: everything a person says and does happens in God’s sight.

“Keeping watch on the evil and the good.”

God does not merely see; He keeps watch, like a watchman on a wall. He watches the evil and the good alike. For the evil, this is a warning: nothing they do is hidden, and they will give account. For the good, it is a comfort: God sees their faithfulness even when no one else does, and He watches over them. Scripture says, “the eyes of the LORD run to and fro throughout the whole earth, to show Himself strong on behalf of those whose heart is loyal to Him” (2 Chronicles 16:9). God’s sight is both a warning and a comfort, depending on how a person lives before it.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb behind “keeping watch” means to watch or look out; KJV renders it “beholding.” The line pictures God’s steady watchfulness, not a passing glance.

Proverbs 15:4
Proverbs 15:4
NKJV

Proverbs 15:4 A wholesome tongue is a tree of life, But perverseness in it breaks the spirit.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 15:4 Gentle words are a tree of life;a deceitful tongue crushes the spirit. (NLT)

Main Idea

Words carry life or injury. A wholesome, healing tongue is a tree of life to those who hear it, the same image used for Wisdom in 3:18, but twisted speech breaks the spirit.

Verse Explanation

Words carry life or injury. A wholesome, healing tongue is a tree of life to those who hear it, the same image used for Wisdom in 3:18, but twisted speech breaks the spirit.

“A wholesome tongue is a tree of life,”

A wholesome tongue is a healing tongue, words that comfort, encourage, and make whole. Such a tongue is a tree of life to those who hear it. The image reaches back to Eden and was first used in Proverbs for Wisdom herself (3:18). Here it describes words that keep giving life and refreshment to others, like a tree that bears fruit season after season.

As at 13:12, the tree of life invites a deeper reading. The tree lost in Eden appears again at the end of Scripture, whose leaves are “for the healing of the nations” (Revelation 22:2), and the words of Christ Himself are “spirit, and they are life” (John 6:63). Healing words among God’s people carry something of that life.

“But perverseness in it breaks the spirit.”

Perverseness in the tongue, twisted, deceitful, or cruel speech, breaks the spirit. Words can wound the inner person as surely as a blow wounds the body. A person whose spirit has been crushed by lies or harsh words may carry that injury for years. The contrast shows how much is at stake in speech: words can give life or do lasting damage.

Study Notes

“Wholesome” translates the Hebrew word for healing; the phrase “a wholesome tongue” reads literally a healing tongue, and YLT has “healed tongue.” The same Hebrew word is translated “health” in 12:18 and 13:17, so a wholesome tongue is one whose words heal.

The Hebrew at the end of the second line is literally a breaking in the spirit. KJV and YLT render it a “breach” in the spirit, the picture of a wall broken through, while NASB says the spirit is crushed.

Proverbs 15:5
Proverbs 15:5
NKJV

Proverbs 15:5 A fool despises his father's instruction, But he who receives correction is prudent.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 15:5 Only a fool despises a parent’s discipline;whoever learns from correction is wise. (NLT)

Main Idea

The fool shows his folly by despising his father’s instruction. The one who accepts correction proves himself prudent, so the way a son receives teaching reveals his heart.

Verse Explanation

The fool shows his folly by despising his father’s instruction. The one who accepts correction proves himself prudent, so the way a son receives teaching reveals his heart.

“A fool despises his father’s instruction,”

The fool shows what he is by how he treats his father’s instruction. He despises it, looking down on it as worthless. The instruction here is the father’s training and correction, the same Hebrew word translated “instruction” in 13:1. A son who despises it cuts himself off from the very thing designed to make him wise. Proverbs set this pattern from its motto: “fools despise wisdom and instruction” (1:7).

“But he who receives correction is prudent.”

The one who receives correction, who keeps it and takes it to heart, is prudent. He has practical good sense, and receiving correction is part of how he got it. The two lines show that the way a son receives teaching reveals his heart. The difference between the fool and the prudent man is not that one is corrected and the other is not; both are corrected. One despises it, and the other receives it.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb behind “receives” means to keep or guard, the same verb translated “regards” in 13:18. The prudent son holds on to correction instead of letting it go. NASB reads “regards,” and ESV “heeds.”

Proverbs 15:6
Proverbs 15:6
NKJV

Proverbs 15:6 In the house of the righteous there is much treasure, But in the revenue of the wicked is trouble.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 15:6 There is treasure in the house of the godly,but the earnings of the wicked bring trouble. (NLT)

Main Idea

Much treasure fills the house of the righteous, the lasting good that God’s blessing brings. The income of the wicked, however large, carries trouble along with it.

Verse Explanation

Much treasure fills the house of the righteous, the lasting good that God’s blessing brings. The income of the wicked, however large, carries trouble along with it.

“In the house of the righteous there is much treasure,”

The house of the righteous holds much treasure, the stored-up resources and lasting good that come from God’s blessing on an upright life. This need not mean great wealth; it is the security and provision of a household where God is honored. “The blessing of the LORD makes one rich, And He adds no sorrow with it” (10:22).

“But in the revenue of the wicked is trouble.”

The income of the wicked may be large, but trouble comes with it. Money gained wickedly brings anxiety, conflict, and ruin into the house. The contrast is not simply between rich and poor. It is between treasure that brings peace and revenue that brings trouble. As with other sayings about wealth, this is a general pattern, not a promise that the righteous will always prosper materially; 15:16 will say that a little with the fear of the LORD is better than great treasure with trouble.

Study Notes

YLT renders “much treasure” as “abundant strength,” drawing out the sense of a household’s stored-up resources rather than money alone.

In English the word “trouble” links 15:6, 15:16, and 15:27, three sayings about gain. The link is in the English and in the theme, not in a single Hebrew word: 15:16, for example, uses a different Hebrew word from 15:6, one that describes turmoil.

Proverbs 15:7
Proverbs 15:7
NKJV

Proverbs 15:7 The lips of the wise disperse knowledge, (H1847) But the heart of the fool does not do so.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 15:7 The lips of the wise give good advice;the heart of a fool has none to give. (NLT)

Main Idea

The lips of the wise scatter knowledge, spreading it to others like seed. The fool’s heart does not do so; it fails to spread sound knowledge that would help others.

Verse Explanation

The lips of the wise scatter knowledge, spreading it to others like seed. The fool’s heart does not do so; it fails to spread sound knowledge that would help others.

“The lips of the wise disperse knowledge,”

The wise scatter knowledge the way a farmer scatters seed. They share it freely, and it spreads to others, taking root and bearing fruit in other lives. Wisdom is not hoarded; it is passed on. The picture matches 15:2, where the tongue of the wise makes knowledge good and useful.

“But the heart of the fool does not do so.”

The fool’s heart does not scatter knowledge. The NKJV says only that he fails to do what the wise do: he does not spread knowledge to others. The second line mentions the heart rather than the lips, which suggests that the problem lies deeper than speech. What a person spreads depends on what is in his heart, and the fool’s heart is not set on knowledge.

Study Notes

The second line ends with two short Hebrew words that ESV renders “not so,” as the NKJV does. YLT renders them “not right,” making the second line a judgment on the fool’s heart itself rather than a statement that it does not spread knowledge.

The Hebrew verb behind “disperse” means to scatter, as YLT renders it, so the wise spread knowledge widely.

Proverbs 15:8–11 — Worship and the Heart Before the LORD

Proverbs 15:8
Proverbs 15:8
NKJV

Proverbs 15:8 The sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination to the LORD, But the prayer of the upright is His delight.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 15:8 The LORD detests the sacrifice of the wicked,but he delights in the prayers of the upright. (NLT)

Main Idea

Religious ritual cannot cover a wicked life, and the offerings of the wicked who cling to their sin are “an abomination to the LORD.” The simple prayer of the upright is His delight, so God looks at the heart behind worship rather than the size of the gift.

Verse Explanation

Religious ritual cannot cover a wicked life, and the offerings of the wicked who cling to their sin are “an abomination to the LORD.” The simple prayer of the upright is His delight, so God looks at the heart behind worship rather than the size of the gift.

“The sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination to the LORD,”

Sacrifices were offered at the temple as acts of worship, thanksgiving, and atonement. Yet when a wicked person brings a sacrifice while clinging to his wickedness, the LORD finds it detestable. Religious ritual cannot cover a life that refuses to turn from sin. The wicked man may hope his offering will buy God’s favor while he goes on as before, but God is not bought. Samuel told Saul, “to obey is better than sacrifice” (1 Samuel 15:22), and Proverbs later adds, “How much more when he brings it with wicked intent!” (21:27).

“But the prayer of the upright is His delight.”

The simple prayer of the upright delights the LORD. No costly animal is mentioned, only prayer, and yet God takes pleasure in it because it comes from a heart that walks with Him. The contrast is between the size of the gift and the heart of the giver. God looks at the heart behind worship. The saying concerns persistent wickedness, not the repentant sinner. God does not refuse the one who comes to Him in brokenness over sin, for “A broken and a contrite heart— These, O God, You will not despise” (Psalm 51:17).

Study Notes

“Abomination” appears three times in this chapter (15:8, 9, 26), each time set against what pleases the LORD.

“His delight” translates a Hebrew word for favor or acceptance, the same word translated “favor” in 14:9. ESV reads “acceptable.” The prayer of the upright is welcomed by God.

Proverbs 15:9
Proverbs 15:9
NKJV

Proverbs 15:9 The way of the wicked is an abomination to the LORD, But He loves him who follows righteousness.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 15:9 The LORD detests the way of the wicked,but he loves those who pursue godliness. (NLT)

Main Idea

The LORD detests not only the worship of the wicked but their whole way of life. He loves the one who pursues righteousness, and His heart is set toward those who seek to do right.

Verse Explanation

The LORD detests not only the worship of the wicked but their whole way of life. He loves the one who pursues righteousness, and His heart is set toward those who seek to do right.

“The way of the wicked is an abomination to the LORD,”

The previous verse spoke of the worship of the wicked; this one widens the view to their whole way of life. The LORD detests not only the sacrifices of the wicked but the path they walk every day. Their worship is unacceptable because their way is unacceptable. Worship and life cannot be separated, and God sees both.

“But He loves him who follows righteousness.”

The LORD loves the one who follows righteousness. The Hebrew verb in this line suggests eager pursuit, chasing after what is right. The person in view is not already perfect; he is someone whose heart is set on doing right and who keeps pursuing it. God’s love is set toward such a person. The pairing with 15:8 shows that what delights God in worship (15:8) and what He loves in life (15:9) belong together: an upright heart that seeks Him.

Study Notes

ESV and NASB render “follows” as “pursues.” The Hebrew verb is the one used in 13:21, where evil “pursues sinners,” the picture of running after something.

Proverbs 15:10
Proverbs 15:10
NKJV

Proverbs 15:10 Harsh discipline is for him who forsakes the way, And he who hates correction will die.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 15:10 Whoever abandons the right path will be severely disciplined;whoever hates correction will die. (NLT)

Main Idea

Harsh discipline awaits the one who leaves the right way. Whoever hates correction will die, because refusing every warning leaves no way back from the road to death.

Verse Explanation

Harsh discipline awaits the one who leaves the right way. Whoever hates correction will die, because refusing every warning leaves no way back from the road to death.

“Harsh discipline is for him who forsakes the way,”

The one who forsakes the way, leaving the right path, will meet harsh discipline. The correction that a person refuses to receive gently will come in a harder form. Life itself, and God behind it, has a way of bringing severe consequences to those who abandon what is right. The Hebrew word behind “discipline” here is the same word used for a father’s instruction in 15:5, but now it comes as painful correction.

“And he who hates correction will die.”

The one who hates correction will die. This is the end of a long road of refusal. If a person rejects every warning, there is nothing left to turn him back from the way of death. Proverbs warns elsewhere, “He who is often rebuked, and hardens his neck, Will suddenly be destroyed, and that without remedy” (29:1). The two lines move from harsh correction to death, showing how serious it is to refuse instruction. Yet the warning itself is an act of mercy, given so that no one has to reach that end.

Study Notes

The Hebrew behind “harsh discipline” is literally bad discipline, using the same word for discipline as a father’s instruction in 15:5. Here it means painful correction. KJV calls the correction “grievous.”

ESV speaks of “severe discipline.”

Proverbs 15:11
Proverbs 15:11
NKJV

Proverbs 15:11 Hell and Destruction are before the LORD; So how much more the hearts of the sons of men.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 15:11 Even Death and Destruction hold no secrets from the LORD.How much more does he know the human heart! (NLT)

Main Idea

Hell and Destruction, the hidden realm of the dead, lie open before the LORD. If He sees into that place, how much more clearly He sees the hearts of human beings (Hebrews 4:13).

Verse Explanation

Hell and Destruction, the hidden realm of the dead, lie open before the LORD. If He sees into that place, how much more clearly He sees the hearts of human beings (Hebrews 4:13).

“Hell and Destruction are before the LORD;”

Hell here translates the Hebrew word Sheol, the realm of the dead, and Destruction translates the Hebrew word Abaddon, a word for destruction that is paired with Sheol as a name for the hidden world of the dead. These are the most hidden places imaginable, beyond the reach of any human eye. Yet they lie open before the LORD. Job said the same: “Sheol is naked before Him, And Destruction has no covering” (Job 26:6). Nothing is hidden from God, not even the place of the dead.

“So how much more the hearts of the sons of men.”

The second line reasons from the greater to the lesser. If even Sheol lies open before God, how much more the human heart. People may hide their thoughts and motives from each other and even from themselves, but not from God. The verse closes a short section on worship and the heart: God rejects worship from a wicked heart (15:8) and loves the one who pursues righteousness (15:9) because He sees every heart. The New Testament says, “all things are naked and open to the eyes of Him to whom we must give account” (Hebrews 4:13).

Study Notes

The Hebrew words are Sheol and Abaddon, as ESV and NASB render them. The 1984 NIV reads “Death” for Sheol. Abaddon also appears in Revelation 9:11 as the name of the angel of the bottomless pit.

The NKJV prints “Destruction” with a capital letter here, as it does in Job 26:6, treating the word like a name.

Proverbs 15:12–19 — A Merry Heart and a Little with the Fear of the LORD

Proverbs 15:12
Proverbs 15:12
NKJV

Proverbs 15:12 A scoffer does not love one who corrects him, Nor will he go to the wise.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 15:12 Mockers hate to be corrected,so they stay away from the wise. (NLT)

Main Idea

The scoffer resents anyone who corrects him and stays away from the wise. He avoids the very people who could help him, which is how his scorn keeps him where he is.

Verse Explanation

The scoffer resents anyone who corrects him and stays away from the wise. He avoids the very people who could help him, which is how his scorn keeps him where he is.

“A scoffer does not love one who corrects him,”

The scoffer resents anyone who corrects him. Correction feels to him like an attack, and he responds with dislike for the person who gave it. The father warned of this in chapter 9: “Do not correct a scoffer, lest he hate you” (9:8). The scoffer is the most stubborn of Proverbs’ fools because his problem lies in the will; he will not be taught.

“Nor will he go to the wise.”

He also stays away from the wise. He will not seek them out or sit under their teaching, because they are exactly the people who might correct him again. In avoiding them he avoids the very help he most needs. His scorn keeps him where he is, not because wisdom is unavailable, but because he will not go where it is found. The saying follows 15:10, where hating correction leads to death, and shows how the scoffer walks that road: by refusing the people God would use to turn him back.

Study Notes

ESV renders the first line in terms of the scoffer’s dislike of being reproved, focusing on the correction rather than the person who gives it. The Hebrew speaks of the one who reproves him, as the NKJV and NASB show.

Proverbs 15:13
Proverbs 15:13
NKJV

Proverbs 15:13 A merry heartmakes a cheerful countenance, But by sorrow of the heart the spirit is broken.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 15:13 A glad heart makes a happy face;a broken heart crushes the spirit. (NLT)

Main Idea

Joy in the heart shows on the face. Sorrow of heart breaks the spirit, so the inner life affects the whole person.

Verse Explanation

Joy in the heart shows on the face. Sorrow of heart breaks the spirit, so the inner life affects the whole person.

“A merry heart makes a cheerful countenance,”

Joy in the heart shows on the face. A glad heart lights up the countenance, and people can see it. Proverbs recognizes how closely the inner life and the outward appearance are connected. A person cannot easily hide what is happening inside, whether joy or grief.

“But by sorrow of the heart the spirit is broken.”

Sorrow of heart breaks the spirit. Grief that sinks deep affects more than the face; it crushes the inner strength a person needs to carry on. Proverbs asks elsewhere, “The spirit of a man will sustain him in sickness, But who can bear a broken spirit?” (18:14). The saying does not blame the sorrowful; it describes what deep sorrow does. Read with 14:10 and 14:13, it calls for compassion toward those whose spirits have been broken.

Study Notes

The two lines are not exactly parallel: the first moves from heart to face, the second from heart to spirit. The contrast suggests that sorrow goes deeper than what appears outwardly.

A related saying appears in 17:22, “A merry heart does good, like medicine, But a broken spirit dries the bones.”

Proverbs 15:14
Proverbs 15:14
NKJV

Proverbs 15:14 The heart of him who has understanding seeks knowledge, (H1847) But the mouth of fools feeds on foolishness.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 15:14 A wise person is hungry for knowledge,while the fool feeds on trash. (NLT)

Main Idea

A person of understanding is hungry for knowledge and keeps seeking it. Fools feed their mouths on foolishness, filling themselves with what cannot nourish.

Verse Explanation

A person of understanding is hungry for knowledge and keeps seeking it. Fools feed their mouths on foolishness, filling themselves with what cannot nourish.

“The heart of him who has understanding seeks knowledge,”

The person of understanding is hungry for more knowledge. Understanding does not make him satisfied with what he already knows; it makes him want to learn more. He keeps seeking, asking, and listening. The more he understands, the more he sees how much there is to learn.

“But the mouth of fools feeds on foolishness.”

Fools feed their mouths on foolishness. The Hebrew verb behind “feeds” pictures animals grazing, steadily eating whatever is in front of them. Fools fill themselves with folly, gossip, and empty talk, things that cannot nourish. The contrast is between a heart that seeks and a mouth that grazes, between hunger for what is true and appetite for what is worthless. What a person feeds on, he becomes.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb behind “feeds” is the one used for shepherding or grazing a flock; here it pictures fools steadily feeding on folly. NLT renders the line “the fool feeds on trash.”

Proverbs 15:15
Proverbs 15:15
NKJV

Proverbs 15:15 All the days of the afflicted are evil, (H7451) But he who is of a merry hearthas a continual feast.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 15:15 For the despondent, every day brings trouble;for the happy heart, life is a continual feast. (NLT)

Main Idea

For the afflicted, every day can feel hard and bitter. The person with a merry heart has a continual feast, because a glad heart can find nourishment even when outward conditions are hard.

Verse Explanation

For the afflicted, every day can feel hard and bitter. The person with a merry heart has a continual feast, because a glad heart can find nourishment even when outward conditions are hard.

“All the days of the afflicted are evil,”

For the afflicted, every day can feel hard and bitter. The afflicted are those who are poor, oppressed, or burdened, and the saying honestly admits that their days are difficult. It does not dismiss their pain or suggest that they are to blame for it.

“But he who is of a merry heart has a continual feast.”

The person with a merry heart has a continual feast. The contrast suggests that the state of the heart affects how a person experiences his days. A glad heart can find nourishment even when outward conditions are hard. This does not mean that a person can simply choose to feel happy, or that the afflicted are at fault for their sorrow. It recognizes that joy in the heart, the kind that comes from trusting God, can make even a hard life feel like a banquet. The next two sayings show where such contentment comes from: the fear of the LORD and love (15:16–17).

Study Notes

The 1984 NIV renders “afflicted” as “oppressed,” and ESV calls the second man “cheerful,” and YLT calls his feast a “perpetual banquet.”

Proverbs 15:16
Proverbs 15:16
NKJV

Proverbs 15:16 Better is a little with the fear of the LORD, Than great treasure with trouble.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 15:16 Better to have little, with fear for the LORD,than to have great treasure and inner turmoil. (NLT)

Main Idea

A little, held in the fear of the LORD, is better than great treasure that comes with turmoil. A right view of God makes a small portion rich, while wealth without Him brings trouble that money cannot settle.

Verse Explanation

A little, held in the fear of the LORD, is better than great treasure that comes with turmoil. A right view of God makes a small portion rich, while wealth without Him brings trouble that money cannot settle.

“Better is a little with the fear of the LORD,”

Proverbs often uses “better than” sayings to set values in order. A little, a small income, a modest home, held in the fear of the LORD, is better than much without it. The fear of the LORD, a right view of God, makes a small portion rich because the person who has it is at peace with God. He knows that what he has comes from God’s hand and that God is enough.

“Than great treasure with trouble.”

Great treasure that comes with trouble is worse. Wealth without the fear of the LORD brings turmoil, anxiety, and conflict that money cannot settle. The saying does not idealize poverty; it compares relationships, not bank accounts. Paul writes, “godliness with contentment is great gain” (1 Timothy 6:6). The question is not how much a person has, but whether he has it with God or without Him.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word behind “trouble” here describes turmoil or agitation; NASB reads “turmoil,” YLT “tumult,” and ESV keeps “trouble.” Great treasure is pictured with constant unrest attached to it.

A similar comparison appears in 16:8, “Better is a little with righteousness, Than vast revenues without justice.”

Proverbs 15:17
Proverbs 15:17
NKJV

Proverbs 15:17 Better is a dinner of herbs where love is, Than a fatted calf with hatred.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 15:17 A bowl of vegetables with someone you loveis better than steak with someone you hate. (NLT)

Main Idea

A simple dish of herbs shared in love is better than a fattened calf served with hatred. The love around the table matters more than what is on it.

Verse Explanation

A simple dish of herbs shared in love is better than a fattened calf served with hatred. The love around the table matters more than what is on it.

“Better is a dinner of herbs where love is,”

A dinner of herbs is a simple meal of vegetables, the food of ordinary people. Where love is present, that simple meal is better than a feast. The people around the table, and the love between them, matter more than what is on it. A home where people love one another can turn a plain supper into something rich.

“Than a fatted calf with hatred.”

A fatted calf was the finest meat available, kept for special occasions and feasts. Yet served with hatred, it is worse than the plainest meal. Bitterness, resentment, and strife spoil even the best food. The “boundary” is the landmark or border stone that marked a family’s property. KJV reads “border,” and ESV speaks of the widow’s “boundaries.”

“The fear of the LORD is the instruction of wisdom,”

“And before honor is humility.”

Humility comes before honor. The fear of the LORD humbles a person, because seeing God rightly shows a person his true size. Honor follows humility, not as a reward that humility earns or as a technique for getting ahead, but as God’s gift to those who bow before Him. “He scorns the scornful, But gives grace to the humble” (3:34). The same line returns at 18:12. This pattern is fulfilled in Christ, who “humbled Himself and became obedient to the point of death,” and whom God “highly exalted” (Philippians 2:8–9).
