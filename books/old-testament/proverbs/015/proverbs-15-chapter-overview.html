Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 15
Main Idea:

Proverbs 15 weighs words that heal against words that wound, sets every heart and every act of worship before the LORD who sees in every place, and closes with the fear of the LORD joined to humility before honor.

Setting:

These sayings end the first part of Solomon’s collection (10:1–15:33), just before chapter 16 turns to the LORD’s rule over human plans. The last verse brings back the book’s motto from 1:7.

Chapter Outline:

  • Proverbs 15:1–7 — Gentle Words and the LORD’s Watchful Eyes

  • Proverbs 15:8–11 — Worship and the Heart Before the LORD

  • Proverbs 15:12–19 — A Merry Heart and a Little with the Fear of the LORD

  • Proverbs 15:20–24 — Counsel, a Timely Word, and the Upward Way

  • Proverbs 15:25–33 — Before Honor Is Humility

Key Verse:

“The fear of the LORD is the instruction of wisdom, And before honor is humility.” (Proverbs 15:33)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Proverbs 15 opens with the tongue. “A soft answer turns away wrath, But a harsh word stirs up anger” (15:1), and “A wholesome tongue is a tree of life” (15:4), an image first given to Wisdom (3:18). Words can heal a person or break his spirit, so the righteous heart “studies how to answer” (15:28).

Among these sayings stands the LORD who sees. “The eyes of the LORD are in every place” (15:3), and if “Hell and Destruction are before the LORD,” no human heart can hide from Him (15:11). He looks past the gift to the worshiper: the sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination to Him, “But the prayer of the upright is His delight” (15:8). His watching warns the wicked and comforts the good: He tears down the house of the proud and guards the widow’s boundary (15:25).

That same God makes a small portion rich. “Better is a little with the fear of the LORD, Than great treasure with trouble” (15:16), and a plain dinner shared in love outweighs a feast with hatred (15:17). The chapter closes by returning to the book’s motto: the fear of the LORD trains a person in wisdom, “And before honor is humility” (15:33). Christ walked that order: “He humbled Himself,” and then “God also has highly exalted Him” (Philippians 2:8–9).

Chapter Sections

Proverbs 15:1–7 — Gentle Words and the LORD’s Watchful Eyes

Speech leads the opening sayings: a gentle reply cools anger, the wise use what they know with skill, and a wholesome tongue gives life while twisted words crush the spirit. In the middle stands the LORD, watching the evil and the good in every place, and the section ends with the wise scattering knowledge like seed while the fool’s heart fails to pass sound knowledge on.

Proverbs 15:8–11 — Worship and the Heart Before the LORD

The LORD rejects both the offerings and the whole way of life of the wicked who cling to their sin, yet the prayer of the upright delights Him and He loves the one who pursues righteousness. Harsh discipline waits for the one who abandons the right way, and because even Hell and Destruction lie open before the LORD, no human heart is hidden from Him.

Proverbs 15:12–19 — A Merry Heart and a Little with the Fear of the LORD

The scoffer stays away from the wise who could correct him, while the person of understanding hungers for knowledge and a glad heart finds a continual feast even in hard days. Two better-than sayings prize a little held in the fear of the LORD and a plain meal shared in love above wealth and feasting that bring turmoil, and the section closes with the angry man who stirs up strife and the lazy man whose road seems choked with thorns.

Proverbs 15:20–24 — Counsel, a Timely Word, and the Upward Way

A wise son gladdens his father, and the man of understanding walks straight while the one without discernment treats folly as fun. Plans stand firm when many counselors are heard, a word given at the right moment does real good, and the wise climb the way of life upward, away from hell below.

Proverbs 15:25–33 — Before Honor Is Humility

The LORD tears down the house of the proud yet sets the widow’s boundary firm, finds the thoughts of the wicked detestable, keeps His distance from them, and hears the prayer of the righteous. Around those sayings come warnings against greed and bribes and a call to weigh an answer before speaking, and the section ends with the ear that welcomes rebuke and with humility that comes before honor.

Why This Chapter Matters

Proverbs 15 shows that the LORD weighs the life behind the worship. He rejects the sacrifice and the way of the wicked (15:8–9) and even their thoughts (15:26), so religious activity cannot cover a life set against Him. The contrast is with persistent wickedness, and the sinner who turns to Him in repentance is not turned away; the LORD “hears the prayer of the righteous” (15:29).

The chapter also gives speech and correction their proper weight. A soft answer usually calms anger, yet it is wisdom, not a guarantee. Jesus answered gently and still suffered at the hands of angry men (1 Peter 2:23), and no one who has been mistreated should be told that gentler words would have prevented it. Receiving rebuke, meanwhile, is a way of caring for one’s own soul (15:32).

Key Themes to Watch For

Words that heal and words that wound. Gentle, timely, and carefully weighed speech brings life, while harsh, twisted, or careless words stir up anger and break the spirit (15:1–2, 4, 7, 23, 28).

The LORD who sees. His eyes are in every place, and the realm of the dead, the human heart, and the thoughts of the wicked all lie open before Him (15:3, 11, 26).

Worship from an upright heart. The LORD rejects the sacrifice and the way of the wicked, but He delights in and hears the prayer of the upright (15:8–9, 29).

Contentment over wealth. A little with the fear of the LORD and a meal shared in love outweigh treasure and feasting that bring trouble, and greed troubles its own household (15:6, 16–17, 27).

Receiving correction. The fool despises his father’s instruction and the scoffer avoids the wise, while the one who heeds rebuke gains understanding and abides among the wise (15:5, 10, 12, 31–32).

Key Words & Concepts

A soft answer (15:1). A gentle, quiet reply. It generally turns away wrath just as a harsh word stirs anger, but the saying describes the way of wisdom; it does not promise that gentleness will always be met with peace.

A tree of life (15:4). The image recalls the tree in Eden and was first given to Wisdom (3:18). Here a wholesome, healing tongue becomes a source of life to those who hear it, and Scripture ends with that tree restored in Christ’s new creation (Revelation 22:2).

Abomination (15:8–9, 26). Something the LORD finds detestable. Each of the three times it appears in this chapter, it stands opposite what pleases Him: the prayer of the upright, the one who follows righteousness, and the pleasant words of the pure.

Hell and Destruction (15:11). Names for the realm of the dead (Sheol and Abaddon in Hebrew). The saying reasons from the greater to the lesser: if even that hidden place lies open to the LORD, so does every human heart.

The way of life winds upward (15:24). The path of the wise climbs away from “hell below,” the opposite of the immoral woman’s house, which descends “to the chambers of death” (7:27). The saying speaks mainly of the direction a life takes; whether it also looks beyond this life is debated.

The fear of the LORD and humility (15:33). The fear of the LORD is a right view of God: terror for those who defy Him and awe for all at His holiness, His being unlike any other. That view is itself the instruction that leads to wisdom, the skill of living life God’s way, and it humbles a person, so that honor comes afterward as God’s gift.
