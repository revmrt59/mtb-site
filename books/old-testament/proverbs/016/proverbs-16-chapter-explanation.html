Proverbs 16

Verse-by-Verse
Proverbs 16:1
Proverbs 16:1
NKJV

Proverbs 16:1 The preparations of the heart belong to man, But the answer of the tongue is from the LORD.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 16:1 We can make our own plans,but the LORD gives the right answer. (NLT)

Main Idea

A new group of sayings opens with the LORD’s rule over human plans. People make their preparations in their hearts, but the answer that finally comes from the tongue is from the LORD, so even what we end up saying lies within His rule.

Verse Explanation

A new group of sayings opens with the LORD’s rule over human plans. People make their preparations in their hearts, but the answer that finally comes from the tongue is from the LORD, so even what we end up saying lies within His rule.

“The preparations of the heart belong to man,”

Proverbs 16 opens a new movement in Solomon’s sayings, and its first word is about the LORD’s rule over human plans. The first line gives people their real part. The preparations of the heart, the arranging and ordering of thoughts and plans, belong to man. People think ahead, set things in order, and decide what they mean to say and do. The saying does not dismiss that work or call it pointless.

“But the answer of the tongue is from the LORD.”

Yet the answer that finally comes from the tongue is from the LORD. A person may prepare his words carefully, but what he actually ends up saying, and the outcome it produces, lies within God’s rule. The line probably includes both the words that are finally spoken and the final verdict on any matter. Either way, the last word belongs to God. Proverbs says the same of all human planning: “There are many plans in a man’s heart, Nevertheless the LORD’s counsel—that will stand” (19:21). The two lines set human responsibility and God’s sovereignty side by side without letting either cancel the other, and the rest of 16:1–9 develops that theme.

Study Notes

ESV and NASB render “preparations” as “plans,” and YLT as “arrangements.” The Hebrew word describes what the heart sets in order ahead of time, the thinking-through that the first line credits to man.

The phrase “from the LORD” in 16:1 returns in 16:33, “its every decision is from the LORD,” so the chapter opens and closes with the same confession.

Proverbs 16:2
Proverbs 16:2
NKJV

Proverbs 16:2 All the ways of a man are pure in his own eyes, But the LORD weighs the spirits.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 16:2 People may be pure in their own eyes,but the LORD examines their motives. (NLT)

Main Idea

People tend to judge their own ways as pure, seeing their motives in the best light. The LORD weighs the spirits, measuring the motives behind the actions, so self-approval is never the final verdict.

Verse Explanation

People tend to judge their own ways as pure, seeing their motives in the best light. The LORD weighs the spirits, measuring the motives behind the actions, so self-approval is never the final verdict.

“All the ways of a man are pure in his own eyes,”

People tend to judge their own ways kindly. A person sees his actions in the best light, explains his motives generously, and finds himself innocent. “Pure in his own eyes” is the verdict nearly everyone gives himself. The saying does not say a man’s ways are pure; it says they look pure to him.

“But the LORD weighs the spirits.”

The LORD weighs the spirits. The picture is of a merchant’s scale, measuring exactly what something is worth. God measures the motives behind the actions, the inner person that a man cannot see clearly in himself. Self-approval is never the final verdict. This is why the fear of the LORD humbles a person: it reminds him that the One who weighs the heart sees what he cannot. Proverbs repeats the thought with slightly different words: “Every way of a man is right in his own eyes, But the LORD weighs the hearts” (21:2).

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb behind “weighs” pictures weighing something out on a scale. NASB renders “spirits” as “motives,” the inner reasons God measures.

KJV and NASB read “clean” for “pure.”

Proverbs 16:3
Proverbs 16:3
NKJV

Proverbs 16:3 Commit your works to the LORD, And your thoughts will be established.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 16:3 Commit your actions to the LORD,and your plans will succeed. (NLT)

Main Idea

Committing one’s works to the LORD means entrusting them to Him and leaving the outcome in His hands. When a person does that, his thoughts and plans are established and made steady, which is God’s steadying of the planner rather than a guarantee that every undertaking will succeed.

Verse Explanation

Committing one’s works to the LORD means entrusting them to Him and leaving the outcome in His hands. When a person does that, his thoughts and plans are established and made steady, which is God’s steadying of the planner rather than a guarantee that every undertaking will succeed.

“Commit your works to the LORD,”

This saying is a command. To commit one’s works to the LORD is to roll them onto Him, to entrust both the work and its outcome to His hands. The person still works; the command assumes real effort and real undertakings. But he no longer carries the weight of the result alone. The psalmist uses the same picture: “Commit your way to the LORD, Trust also in Him, And He shall bring it to pass” (Psalm 37:5).

“And your thoughts will be established.”

When a person entrusts his works to God, his thoughts will be established, made firm and steady. His thoughts here are his plans and purposes. Note what is promised and what is not. The works are committed; the thoughts are established. God steadies the planner and brings his purposes into line with His own. The verse does not promise that every undertaking will succeed as the person intended. It promises that the one who places his works in God’s hands will find his plans set on a firm footing, under the God who directs his steps (16:9).

Study Notes

The Hebrew behind “Commit” is literally “Roll,” as YLT renders it: the picture is of rolling a burden off oneself and onto the LORD.

Proverbs 16:4
Proverbs 16:4
NKJV

Proverbs 16:4 The LORD has made all for Himself, Yes, even the wicked for the day of doom.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 16:4 The LORD has made everything for his own purposes,even the wicked for a day of disaster. (NLT)

Main Idea

Everything the LORD made serves His purpose, and even the wicked do not escape it, for they are headed for the day of doom. The verse affirms that wickedness stays under God’s rule and will meet His judgment, without making God the author of evil, for He has no pleasure in the death of one who dies (Ezekiel 18:32).

Verse Explanation

Everything the LORD made serves His purpose, and even the wicked do not escape it, for they are headed for the day of doom. The verse affirms that wickedness stays under God’s rule and will meet His judgment, without making God the author of evil, for He has no pleasure in the death of one who dies (Ezekiel 18:32).

“The LORD has made all for Himself,”

Everything the LORD made serves His purpose. Nothing in creation stands outside His plan or escapes His rule. The phrase “for Himself” can also be read as for its purpose, meaning that God has fitted each thing for its proper end. Either way, the world is not a random collection of events; God governs it and directs it toward His ends.

“Yes, even the wicked for the day of doom.”

Even the wicked are included. They do not escape God’s purpose, for they are headed for the day of doom, the day of disaster and judgment. The verse affirms that wickedness stays under God’s rule and will meet His judgment. It does not make God the author of evil or say that He delights in the destruction of the wicked. He says plainly, “I have no pleasure in the death of one who dies” (Ezekiel 18:32), and calls the wicked to turn and live. God is good, and His goodness includes His justice. Even the evil that people intend, He can turn toward His good purposes, as Joseph told his brothers: “you meant evil against me; but God meant it for good” (Genesis 50:20). The verse should not be used on its own to build a doctrine of God creating people for destruction.

Study Notes

The Hebrew behind “for Himself” can mean for its answer or for its purpose; the noun is the same one translated “answer” in 16:1. ESV and NASB read “purpose,” while KJV, with NKJV, reads “for himself.”

The Hebrew behind “the day of doom” is literally the day of evil or disaster, as KJV and NASB render it.

Proverbs 16:5
Proverbs 16:5
NKJV

Proverbs 16:5 Everyone proud in heart is an abomination to the LORD; Though they join forces, none will go unpunished.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 16:5 The LORD detests the proud;they will surely be punished. (NLT)

Main Idea

The LORD detests everyone who is proud in heart. Even if the proud join forces to protect one another, none of them will escape punishment, the same assurance 11:21 gave about the wicked.

Verse Explanation

The LORD detests everyone who is proud in heart. Even if the proud join forces to protect one another, none of them will escape punishment, the same assurance 11:21 gave about the wicked.

“Everyone proud in heart is an abomination to the LORD;”

The LORD detests everyone who is proud in heart. The pride in view is not merely a proud look or proud words, but pride rooted in the heart, a settled self-exaltation that has no room for God. Among the seven things the LORD hates, the first is “A proud look” (6:17). Pride is at bottom a wrong view of God and of oneself, which is why the fear of the LORD, a right view of God, exposes it.

“Though they join forces, none will go unpunished.”

The proud may band together, supporting and protecting one another, but none of them will escape punishment. Their numbers cannot shield them from God’s judgment. The line repeats the assurance 11:21 gave about the wicked: “Though they join forces, the wicked will not go unpunished” (11:21). The saying belongs with the chapter’s longer treatment of pride in 16:18–19.

Study Notes

The Hebrew behind “though they join forces” is literally hand to hand, which KJV renders as hand joining in hand. “Will not go unpunished” is literally will not be acquitted or held innocent, as YLT shows with “not acquitted.”

Proverbs 16:6
Proverbs 16:6
NKJV

Proverbs 16:6 In mercy and truth Atonement is provided for iniquity; And by the fear of the LORD one departs from evil.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 16:6 Unfailing love and faithfulness make atonement for sin.By fearing the LORD, people avoid evil. (NLT)

Main Idea

In mercy and truth atonement is provided for iniquity, and the fear of the LORD leads a person to turn away from evil. Atonement is God’s provision, not something human kindness earns, and mercy and truth meet fully in Christ, for “grace and truth came through Jesus Christ” (John 1:17).

Verse Explanation

In mercy and truth atonement is provided for iniquity, and the fear of the LORD leads a person to turn away from evil. Atonement is God’s provision, not something human kindness earns, and mercy and truth meet fully in Christ, for “grace and truth came through Jesus Christ” (John 1:17).

“In mercy and truth Atonement is provided for iniquity;”

This is one of the few places in Proverbs that speaks of atonement. The Hebrew verb comes from the root used for atonement in Israel’s sacrifices, the covering and removing of guilt. In mercy and truth, atonement is provided for iniquity. Mercy and truth are the LORD’s steadfast love and faithfulness, the qualities He shows in keeping His promises. Some read them as the person’s own loyalty and faithfulness, but even then atonement is God’s provision, not something human kindness earns. The psalmist sings, “Mercy and truth have met together” (Psalm 85:10), and in the fullness of time they met fully in Christ, for “grace and truth came through Jesus Christ” (John 1:17). God “made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us” (2 Corinthians 5:21).

“And by the fear of the LORD one departs from evil.”

The second line shows what follows atonement: the fear of the LORD leads a person to turn away from evil. Forgiveness and a changed life belong together. The person whose iniquity has been covered now sees God rightly and turns from what God hates. “Fear the LORD and depart from evil” (3:7). The verse does not make turning from evil the cause of atonement; it makes it the fruit.

Study Notes

ESV renders “mercy and truth” as steadfast love and faithfulness, and NASB as lovingkindness and truth. KJV says iniquity is “purged.” The Hebrew verb behind “atonement is provided” comes from the same root used throughout Leviticus for making atonement; here it is passive, so iniquity is atoned for, without any mention of sacrifice.

The Hebrew verb behind “atonement is provided” in 16:6 also appears in 16:14, where a wise man will “appease” the king’s wrath; there it means to pacify.

Proverbs 16:7
Proverbs 16:7
NKJV

Proverbs 16:7 When a man's ways please the LORD, He makes even his enemies to be at peace with him.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 16:7 When people’s lives please the LORD,even their enemies are at peace with them. (NLT)

Main Idea

When a person’s ways please the LORD, He can turn even enemies into people at peace with him. The LORD’s rule reaches into human relationships, though this describes His gracious working rather than a promise that the godly will never face opposition.

Verse Explanation

When a person’s ways please the LORD, He can turn even enemies into people at peace with him. The LORD’s rule reaches into human relationships, though this describes His gracious working rather than a promise that the godly will never face opposition.

“When a man’s ways please the LORD,”

The saying begins with a person whose ways please the LORD. His life, not just his words or his worship, is acceptable to God. This is the person who has committed his works to the LORD (16:3) and walks in the fear of the LORD (16:6).

“He makes even his enemies to be at peace with him.”

The LORD can turn even enemies into people at peace with such a person. God’s rule reaches into human relationships. He can soften hearts, change circumstances, and bring reconciliation where it seemed impossible. Esau, who had once threatened to kill Jacob, “ran to meet him, and embraced him” (Genesis 33:4). Yet this describes God’s gracious working, not a promise that the godly will never face opposition. Jesus pleased the Father perfectly and still had enemies who crucified Him. The saying shows what God is able to do and often does, not what He must always do.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb behind “makes even his enemies to be at peace” is a causative form, meaning that someone causes the action: the LORD brings about peace. The verb comes from the same root as shalom, the Hebrew word for peace and wholeness. The NKJV’s capitalized “He” shows that the LORD is the one acting, while NLT makes it sound as if the enemies simply become peaceful.

Proverbs 16:8
Proverbs 16:8
NKJV

Proverbs 16:8 Better is a little with righteousness, Than vast revenues without justice.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 16:8 Better to have little, with godliness,than to be rich and dishonest. (NLT)

Main Idea

A little gained with righteousness is better than great income gained without justice. How wealth is gained matters more than how much of it there is.

Verse Explanation

A little gained with righteousness is better than great income gained without justice. How wealth is gained matters more than how much of it there is.

“Better is a little with righteousness,”

A little gained and held with righteousness is better. The saying is another “better than” comparison, like 15:16. It sets a small portion honestly gained against a large one unjustly gained, and it puts the small portion first.

“Than vast revenues without justice.”

Vast revenues without justice are worse. Great income gained by cheating, oppression, or crooked dealing cannot match a little gained rightly. How wealth is gained matters more than how much of it there is. The saying fits the LORD’s rule over human plans: since He weighs the spirits (16:2) and makes all for Himself (16:4), unjust gain has no future, while a little with righteousness has His approval.

Study Notes

The Hebrew behind “without justice” uses the ordinary word for justice or right judgment, the same word translated “judgment” in 16:10. KJV reads “without right.”

Compare 15:16, “Better is a little with the fear of the LORD, Than great treasure with trouble.”

Proverbs 16:9
Proverbs 16:9
NKJV

Proverbs 16:9 A man's heartplans his way, But the LORD directs his steps.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 16:9 We can make our plans,but the LORD determines our steps. (NLT)

Main Idea

Human beings really do plan, and a man’s heart maps out his way. Yet the LORD directs his steps, so planning and trust belong together, and neither cancels the other.

Verse Explanation

Human beings really do plan, and a man’s heart maps out his way. Yet the LORD directs his steps, so planning and trust belong together, and neither cancels the other.

“A man’s heart plans his way,”

Human beings really do plan. A man’s heart thinks through his way, considers options, and maps out a route. Proverbs does not discourage planning; it commends counsel and forethought (15:22). The heart that plans is doing what God made it to do.

“But the LORD directs his steps.”

Yet the LORD directs his steps. The plan is the man’s, but the actual steps are guided and established by God. This does not cancel planning or make it pointless; it places planning within God’s rule. A person makes his plans and then trusts God to guide each step, knowing that God’s direction is better than his own. Jeremiah prayed, “It is not in man who walks to direct his own steps” (Jeremiah 10:23), and Proverbs asks, “How then can a man understand his own way?” (20:24). The verse sums up the section that began with 16:1: people plan, and the LORD rules.

Study Notes

“Directs” translates the same Hebrew verb rendered “established” in 16:3, which means to make firm. ESV reads “establishes.”

The words “heart,” “man,” and “the LORD” appear in both 16:1 and 16:9, framing the opening section on the LORD’s rule over human plans.

Proverbs 16:10–15 — The King’s Righteous Rule

Proverbs 16:10
Proverbs 16:10
NKJV

Proverbs 16:10 Divination is on the lips of the king; His mouth must not transgress in judgment.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 16:10 The king speaks with divine wisdom;he must never judge unfairly. (NLT)

Main Idea

The king’s word carries the weight of an authoritative verdict, so his mouth must not betray justice when he judges. “Divination” here describes the decisive authority of his judgments, not approval of practices God forbids.

Verse Explanation

The king’s word carries the weight of an authoritative verdict, so his mouth must not betray justice when he judges. “Divination” here describes the decisive authority of his judgments, not approval of practices God forbids.

“Divination is on the lips of the king;”

A group of sayings about the king begins here (16:10–15). “Divination” usually names a practice God forbids; Israel was not to have among them “one who practices witchcraft” or one who “interprets omens” (Deuteronomy 18:10). Here the word is used in a striking way to describe the authority of the king’s verdict. When the king judges, his word settles the matter with the weight of a final decision, as if it came from above. The saying gives no approval to divination; it describes how decisive a king’s judgment is.

“His mouth must not transgress in judgment.”

Because the king’s word carries such weight, his mouth must not betray justice. A ruler whose verdicts are final has a heavy responsibility to judge rightly. Solomon himself asked God for “an understanding heart to judge Your people” (1 Kings 3:9), and when he judged wisely, all Israel “saw that the wisdom of God was in him to administer justice” (1 Kings 3:28). The royal sayings describe what an ordinary king ought to be: a judge whose verdicts are just. Isaiah’s picture of the Messiah’s throne, established “with judgment and justice” (Isaiah 9:7), shows that same duty perfectly fulfilled, though not every earthly king in these sayings is a picture of Christ.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word behind “Divination” is the ordinary word for divination. KJV softens it to a “divine sentence,” and ESV reads “An oracle.”

The Hebrew verb form in the second line can express either a statement or an obligation. ESV takes it as a statement, that his mouth does not sin in judgment, while the NKJV’s “must not” and NASB’s “should not” take it as an obligation. The obligation fits the sayings that follow, which assume that kings can fail.

Proverbs 16:11
Proverbs 16:11
NKJV

Proverbs 16:11 Honest weights and scales are the LORD's; All the weights in the bag are His work.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 16:11 The LORD demands accurate scales and balances;he sets the standards for fairness. (NLT)

Main Idea

Honest weights and scales belong to the LORD, and every weight in the merchant’s bag is His work. Fair dealing is not a human invention but part of the LORD’s own order for the world.

Verse Explanation

Honest weights and scales belong to the LORD, and every weight in the merchant’s bag is His work. Fair dealing is not a human invention but part of the LORD’s own order for the world.

“Honest weights and scales are the LORD’s;”

In the marketplace, goods were weighed on a balance scale against stone weights. A dishonest merchant could carry two sets of weights, one for buying and one for selling. The Law commanded, “You shall have honest scales, honest weights” (Leviticus 19:36). This saying goes further: honest weights and scales belong to the LORD. Fair measurement is His.

“All the weights in the bag are His work.”

Every weight in the merchant’s bag is God’s work. Fair dealing is not a human invention or a mere business rule; it is part of the LORD’s own order for the world. Proverbs says elsewhere, “Dishonest scales are an abomination to the LORD, But a just weight is His delight” (11:1). Placed among the royal sayings, this verse reminds rulers that justice in the marketplace is part of the justice they must uphold, because the standards themselves come from God.

Study Notes

The Hebrew behind “the weights in the bag” is literally the stones of the bag, because merchants carried stone weights in a pouch. Deuteronomy 25:13 forbids having “in your bag differing weights.”

ESV and NASB speak of a just balance and scales.

Proverbs 16:12
Proverbs 16:12
NKJV

Proverbs 16:12 It is an abomination for kings to commit wickedness, For a throne is established by righteousness.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 16:12 A king detests wrongdoing,for his rule is built on justice. (NLT)

Main Idea

Kings are to find wickedness detestable, because a throne stands firm only on righteousness. Power that rests on injustice undermines itself.

Verse Explanation

Kings are to find wickedness detestable, because a throne stands firm only on righteousness. Power that rests on injustice undermines itself.

“It is an abomination for kings to commit wickedness,”

Kings are to find wickedness detestable, especially wickedness in their own conduct. A king who does evil disgraces his office. The line can also mean that good kings detest wickedness in others and will not tolerate it in their courts; both senses fit the picture of a ruler who stands firmly against evil.

“For a throne is established by righteousness.”

The reason follows: a throne stands firm only on righteousness. Power that rests on injustice undermines itself, while just rule brings stability. Proverbs repeats the principle: “Take away the wicked from before the king, And his throne will be established in righteousness” (25:5), and “The king who judges the poor with truth, His throne will be established forever” (29:14). A ruler’s security depends not on force but on justice.

Study Notes

The Hebrew of the first line says literally an abomination to kings is doing wickedness. The 1984 NIV takes it of kings detesting wrongdoing in others, while ESV and NASB, with the NKJV, speak of kings themselves doing evil.

“Established” translates the same Hebrew verb used in 16:3 and 16:9, meaning to be made firm.

Proverbs 16:13
Proverbs 16:13
NKJV

Proverbs 16:13 Righteous lips are the delight of kings, And they love him who speaks what is right.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 16:13 The king is pleased with words from righteous lips;he loves those who speak honestly. (NLT)

Main Idea

Good kings delight in righteous lips and love the one who speaks what is right. A wise ruler wants honest counsel, not flattery.

Verse Explanation

Good kings delight in righteous lips and love the one who speaks what is right. A wise ruler wants honest counsel, not flattery.

“Righteous lips are the delight of kings,”

Good kings delight in righteous lips, in advisers and servants who speak honestly. A wise ruler wants to hear the truth, even when it is unwelcome, because his decisions depend on accurate counsel. Flatterers tell him what he wants to hear; righteous lips tell him what he needs to know.

“And they love him who speaks what is right.”

Kings love the one who speaks what is right. The truthful counselor wins the ruler’s affection and trust. The saying describes the ideal king and, by implication, warns kings who prefer flattery. It also encourages those who serve under authority to speak honestly, trusting that a good ruler values the truth.

Study Notes

NASB renders the second line in the passive, that the one who speaks right is loved.

Proverbs 16:14
Proverbs 16:14
NKJV

Proverbs 16:14 As messengers of death is the king's wrath, But a wise man will appease it.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 16:14 The anger of the king is a deadly threat;the wise will try to appease it. (NLT)

Main Idea

The king’s wrath is like a band of messengers sent to bring death. A wise man knows how to calm that anger, using wisdom to turn danger away.

Verse Explanation

The king’s wrath is like a band of messengers sent to bring death. A wise man knows how to calm that anger, using wisdom to turn danger away.

“As messengers of death is the king’s wrath,”

The king’s anger is like messengers sent to bring death. In the ancient world, a king’s wrath could mean execution, and his officers carried out his sentence swiftly. The image warns of the danger of provoking a ruler. Proverbs says elsewhere, “The king’s wrath is like the roaring of a lion” (19:12).

“But a wise man will appease it.”

A wise man knows how to calm the king’s anger. He uses wisdom, patience, and fitting words to turn danger away. The wise man does not flatter or deceive; he speaks with understanding and helps the king see more clearly. The saying shows wisdom at work in the most dangerous setting, turning wrath into peace.

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb behind “appease” is the same one used for atonement in 16:6. Here it has the sense of covering or pacifying anger, which is why KJV and YLT render it “pacify.”

NKJV

Proverbs 16:15 In the light of the king's faceis life, And his favor is like a cloud of the latter rain.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 16:15 When the king smiles, there is life;his favor refreshes like a spring rain. (NLT)

Main Idea

The light of the king’s face, his favor and approval, means life. His favor is like a cloud bringing the latter rain, the spring showers that ripen the harvest, so a righteous ruler’s favor brings refreshing to those under him.

Verse Explanation

The light of the king’s face, his favor and approval, means life. His favor is like a cloud bringing the latter rain, the spring showers that ripen the harvest, so a righteous ruler’s favor brings refreshing to those under him.

“In the light of the king’s face is life,”

The light of the king’s face is his favor and approval, the bright, welcoming look of a ruler pleased with his servant. That favor means life: protection, provision, and opportunity. Where the king’s wrath brings death (16:14), his favor brings life.

“And his favor is like a cloud of the latter rain.”

The king’s favor is like a cloud bringing the latter rain, the spring showers that came before harvest and ripened the crops. God promised Israel “the early rain and the latter rain, that you may gather in your grain” (Deuteronomy 11:14). A righteous ruler’s favor brings refreshing and fruitfulness to those under him. The image of a shining face also recalls the priestly blessing, “The LORD make His face shine upon you” (Numbers 6:25), which places every earthly king’s favor beneath the favor of the King of kings.

Study Notes

The latter rain fell in spring, before the grain harvest. ESV and NASB render it “spring rain.”

A similar comparison appears in 19:12, “his favor is like dew on the grass.”

Proverbs 16:16–24 — Wisdom, Humility, and Pleasant Words

Proverbs 16:16
Proverbs 16:16
NKJV

Proverbs 16:16 How much better to get wisdom than gold! And to get understanding is to be chosen rather than silver.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 16:16 How much better to get wisdom than gold,and good judgment than silver! (NLT)

Main Idea

Getting wisdom is far better than getting gold, and understanding is to be chosen over silver. The verse is an exclamation, pressing the reader to value what lasts above what merely glitters.

Verse Explanation

Getting wisdom is far better than getting gold, and understanding is to be chosen over silver. The verse is an exclamation, pressing the reader to value what lasts above what merely glitters.

“How much better to get wisdom than gold!”

The verse is an exclamation, not a calm comparison. Getting wisdom is far better than getting gold. Gold was the most precious and lasting form of wealth, yet wisdom is worth more. Wisdom herself said, “My fruit is better than gold, yes, than fine gold” (8:19). Gold can buy many things, but it cannot teach a person how to live, and it can be lost in a day.

“And to get understanding is to be chosen rather than silver.”

Understanding is to be chosen over silver. The word “chosen” presses the reader to decide. Between silver and understanding, a person must pick, and the saying urges him to choose what lasts above what merely glitters. The verse follows the royal sayings and reminds kings and commoners alike that wisdom is the true treasure.

Study Notes

The same comparison runs through the opening chapters: “For her proceeds are better than the profits of silver, And her gain than fine gold” (3:14), and “Receive my instruction, and not silver” (8:10).

Proverbs 16:17
Proverbs 16:17

Proverbs 16:17 The highway of the upright is to depart from evil; He who keeps his way preserves his soul.(NKJV)

The upright travel a highway whose direction is away from evil. Keeping to that road guards one’s life, so staying on the right way is itself a form of protection.
