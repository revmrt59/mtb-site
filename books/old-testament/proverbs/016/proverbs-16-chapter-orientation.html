Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 16
Main Idea:

Proverbs 16 places every human plan, word, and decision under the LORD who weighs the spirits and directs each step, and it gathers around that truth sayings on the righteous king, pride before a fall, gracious speech, the troublemaker, and self-control.

Setting:

Chapter 16 opens the second part of Solomon’s collection (16:1–22:16), right after 15:33 joined the fear of the LORD to humility. More sayings now develop one thought across both lines, and the LORD’s rule frames the chapter (16:1, 33).

Chapter Outline:

  • Proverbs 16:1–9 — The LORD Directs Human Plans

  • Proverbs 16:10–15 — The King’s Righteous Rule

  • Proverbs 16:16–24 — Wisdom, Humility, and Pleasant Words

  • Proverbs 16:25–30 — The Way of Death and the Troublemaker

  • Proverbs 16:31–33 — Gray Hair, Self-Control, and the Lot

Key Verse:

“A man’s heart plans his way, But the LORD directs his steps.” (Proverbs 16:9)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Proverbs 16 begins and ends with the same truth. People really do plan, for “The preparations of the heart belong to man” (16:1), yet the answer that comes from the tongue, the steps a man actually takes, and even the lot cast into the lap all belong to the LORD (16:1, 9, 33). He “weighs the spirits” (16:2), measuring motives a person cannot see clearly in himself, and so the wise commit their works to Him (16:3). This opening cluster also reaches what no plan can repair. “In mercy and truth Atonement is provided for iniquity” (16:6); it is God’s provision, and mercy and truth meet fully in Christ, through whom “grace and truth came” (John 1:17).

The rest of the chapter shows the LORD’s rule touching ordinary life. A king’s throne “is established by righteousness” (16:12), and honest scales are the LORD’s own work (16:11). Pride, the heart claiming a place that belongs to God, “goes before destruction” (16:18). Speech follows the heart: pleasant words heal like honey (16:24), while the ungodly man’s lips burn like fire (16:27). The chapter ranks the man “who rules his spirit” above the one who takes a city (16:32).

Chapter Sections

Proverbs 16:1–9 — The LORD Directs Human Plans

The LORD gives the answer of the tongue, weighs motives that look pure to their owner, steadies the thoughts of those who entrust their works to Him, and keeps even the wicked within His purpose for the day of doom. Pride stands condemned, atonement comes in mercy and truth with turning from evil following it, and the section ends where it began, with a man planning his way while the LORD directs his steps.

Proverbs 16:10–15 — The King’s Righteous Rule

Because the king’s verdict carries decisive authority, his mouth must not betray justice, and his throne stands only on righteousness, just as honest scales belong to the LORD. A good king prizes honest speech, a wise man knows how to calm his wrath, and his favor brings life like the spring rain that ripens the harvest.

Proverbs 16:16–24 — Wisdom, Humility, and Pleasant Words

Wisdom is worth more than gold, the upright keep to a highway that leads away from evil, and a humble place among the lowly is better than a share of the proud man’s plunder, since pride runs ahead of a fall. Trust in the LORD brings true happiness, and a wise heart teaches the mouth to speak words that, like a honeycomb, sweeten the soul and strengthen the body.

Proverbs 16:25–30 — The Way of Death and the Troublemaker

The way that only seems right, first heard at 14:12, returns at the head of this section, followed by the laborer whose hunger keeps him working. Then comes a portrait of the troublemaker, who digs up evil, sows strife, parts close friends with whispering, lures his neighbor into a bad way, and gives away his plots with a wink and pursed lips.

Proverbs 16:31–33 — Gray Hair, Self-Control, and the Lot

Gray hair is a crown of glory when it is found in a righteous life, and mastery of one’s own temper outranks the strength that captures a city. The last word returns to the LORD: the lot falls into the lap, yet its every decision is His.

Why This Chapter Matters

Proverbs 16 keeps planning and trust together. No saying here tells anyone to stop preparing; the chapter places his plans under a God who weighs motives and directs steps (16:2, 9). Committing one’s works to the LORD steadies the planner without guaranteeing that every venture succeeds, and the saying about enemies made to be at peace (16:7) describes His gracious working, not a shield against all opposition.

The chapter also exposes pride at its root. The proud heart is an abomination to the LORD (16:5), and its fall is the end of refusing to see itself rightly before Him (16:18). The fear of the LORD turns a person from evil (16:6) and leaves him content to stand “with the lowly” (16:19). Humility is a right view of oneself before God, very different from self-contempt.

Key Themes to Watch For

The LORD’s rule over human plans. People plan, speak, and cast lots, but the outcome and the steps belong to Him (16:1, 3, 9, 33).

Motives weighed. Ways that look pure to their owner are measured by the LORD, who detests a proud heart however many allies it gathers (16:2, 5).

Righteous rule. Kings are to judge justly, hate wickedness, and value honest lips, because a throne rests on righteousness (16:10–15). These sayings speak first of Israel’s kings, and Scripture’s promise of a King who rules “with judgment and justice” forever finds its answer in Christ (Isaiah 9:7).

Pride and humility. Pride is the road to a fall, and a humble spirit among the lowly is worth more than any spoil (16:5, 18–19).

Words that come from the heart. The wise heart teaches the mouth and pleasant words heal, while the ungodly man’s speech burns and the whisperer separates friends (16:21, 23–24, 27–28).

Key Words & Concepts

Weighs the spirits (16:2). The picture is of a merchant’s scale. The LORD measures the motives behind a person’s actions, so self-approval is never the final verdict.

Commit (16:3). The Hebrew verb pictures rolling a burden onto another. To commit one’s works to the LORD is to entrust them and their outcome to Him.

Even the wicked for the day of doom (16:4). Everything the LORD made serves His purpose, and wickedness never escapes His rule or His judgment. The verse does not make God the author of evil, for He has “no pleasure in the death of one who dies” (Ezekiel 18:32).

Mercy and truth (16:6). In Hebrew, hesed and emet: the LORD’s steadfast love and faithfulness, though some read them as a person’s own loyalty and faithfulness. Either way, atonement is God’s provision, and the psalmist sings, “Mercy and truth have met together” (Psalm 85:10).

Divination (16:10). The word usually names a practice God forbids (compare Deuteronomy 18:10). Here it describes the decisive authority of the king’s verdict and gives no approval to divination.

The lot (16:33). Casting lots was a way Israel sought the LORD’s decision (compare Acts 1:26). The saying places even what looks like chance under His rule and gives no approval to gambling.
