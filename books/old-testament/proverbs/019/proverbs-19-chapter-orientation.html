Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 19
Main Idea:

Proverbs 19 sets integrity above wealth and its fickle friends, finds wisdom in patience, a prudent wife, and timely correction, and places every human plan under the LORD’s counsel, while the false witness, the lazy man, and the scoffer reap what their ways deserve.

Setting:

The sayings come from Solomon’s collection that begins at 10:1. Like 18:23, the chapter opens with the poor man, and repeated sayings about the false witness (19:5, 9) and the scoffer (19:25, 29) frame its first and last groups.

Chapter Outline:

  • Proverbs 19:1–9 — Integrity, Fickle Friends, and the False Witness

  • Proverbs 19:10–14 — Discretion, the King’s Favor, and the Household

  • Proverbs 19:15–21 — Pity for the Poor and the LORD’s Counsel

  • Proverbs 19:22–29 — The Fear of the LORD and Judgment for Scoffers

Key Verse:

“There are many plans in a man’s heart, Nevertheless the LORD’s counsel—that will stand.” (Proverbs 19:21)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Proverbs 19 opens with a comparison that defies the world’s way of keeping score: “Better is the poor who walks in his integrity Than one who is perverse in his lips, and is a fool” (19:1). “Wealth makes many friends” (19:4), while the poor man chases his friends “with words, yet they abandon him” (19:7). Twice the chapter warns that “A false witness will not go unpunished” (19:5, 9), and the second time the liar “shall perish.” Folly even turns on God, for a man twists his own way and then “his heart frets against the LORD” (19:3).

The middle sayings move into the home. Discretion makes a man “slow to anger,” and “his glory is to overlook a transgression” (19:11). A foolish son and a quarreling marriage wear a family down, but while houses and riches are inherited, a prudent wife is God’s gift (19:13–14). The LORD takes the poor man’s side so fully that pity shown him is a loan to God, “And He will pay back what he has given” (19:17).

Over every human scheme stands His purpose: “Nevertheless the LORD’s counsel—that will stand” (19:21). The fear of the LORD, a right view of God, “leads to life” (19:23), while the lazy man leaves his hand in the bowl and judgments wait for scoffers (19:24, 29).

Chapter Sections

Proverbs 19:1–9 — Integrity, Fickle Friends, and the False Witness

A poor man who keeps his integrity outranks a fool with a crooked tongue, eager feet without knowledge run into sin, and the man who ruins his own way turns his anger on the LORD. Between two nearly identical warnings that the false witness will not escape, the sayings watch money gather friends and poverty scatter even brothers, and they call the getting of wisdom true love for one’s own soul.

Proverbs 19:10–14 — Discretion, the King’s Favor, and the Household

Comfort and power sit badly on those who lack the wisdom to use them, while a man of discretion shows his glory by letting an offense pass. The king’s anger and favor are set side by side, and then the household appears in both its griefs and its gifts: a foolish son and constant quarreling bring ruin, but a prudent wife comes from the LORD, beyond what any inheritance can supply.

Proverbs 19:15–21 — Pity for the Poor and the LORD’s Counsel

Idleness ends in hunger and carelessness in death, but the one who keeps the commandment guards his life, and kindness to the poor becomes a loan the LORD Himself promises to repay. Parents are urged to correct a son while correction can still turn him, the hot-tempered man is left to his consequences, and the call to receive counsel gives way to the LORD’s purpose, which outlasts every plan people make.

Proverbs 19:22–29 — The Fear of the LORD and Judgment for Scoffers

Kindness and honesty are what make a person worth having, and the fear of the LORD leads to a life of settled contentment. Against it stand the lazy man too idle to feed himself, the son who drives off his parents, the corrupt witness who mocks justice, and the scoffer, whose punishment teaches the simple and for whom judgments are already prepared.

Why This Chapter Matters

Proverbs 19 shows that God sees the people others walk away from. The poor man whose brothers hate him and whose friends desert him (19:7) is the one whose cause the LORD treats as His own (19:17). The repayment rests on God’s faithfulness and is no scheme for profit, just as 19:23 describes the general blessing of fearing Him without promising a life free of trouble.

The chapter also holds human effort and God’s rule together. The son is told to receive instruction, parents to correct a child while there is hope and never to aim at his harm, and the man of great wrath to bear his own consequences (19:18–20). Planning and counsel matter, yet only the LORD’s counsel is certain to stand (19:21). Wise people make their plans and then rest them on His purpose.

Key Themes to Watch For

Integrity over wealth. A poor man who walks honestly is better than a fool with twisted speech or a liar, whatever either may own (19:1, 22).

Friends who follow money. Wealth and gifts draw a crowd, while poverty drives off even brothers; the sayings record this behavior without approving it (19:4, 6–7).

False witnesses. Lies told under oath will be answered, and the corrupt witness who scorns justice feeds on evil (19:5, 9, 28).

Griefs and gifts in the home. A foolish son, a quarrelsome marriage, and a son who shames his parents stand beside a prudent wife and correction given while there is hope (19:13–14, 18, 26).

Listening to counsel. Receiving instruction makes a person wise in his latter days, a rebuke teaches the one who understands, and the son who stops listening begins to stray (19:20, 25, 27).

Key Words & Concepts

Frets against the LORD (19:3). Raging at God over trouble a person’s own folly has caused. Scripture welcomes honest lament, as the Psalms show; this saying exposes the habit of blaming God for the results of one’s own choices.

Overlook a transgression (19:11). Choosing to let a personal offense go rather than nurse it or repay it. Overlooking an offense does not mean tolerating abuse or ignoring sin that must be corrected.

Lends to the LORD (19:17). God counts kindness to the poor as done to Himself (compare 14:31; Matthew 25:40). The One who receives the loan is the One who repays it, so the giver’s confidence rests on His faithfulness.

The fear of the LORD (19:23). A right view of God, which means terror for those who defy Him and awe for everyone at His glory, majesty, and holiness, for He is unlike any other. Here it leads to life and to a settled satisfaction with Him, describing the general blessing of living in reverent trust.

The lazy man (19:15, 24). The NKJV speaks of “Laziness” and “an idle person” in 19:15 and “A lazy man” in 19:24. The man whose hand stays in the bowl uses biting humor to show a laziness that will not finish even feeding itself; the saying concerns unwillingness, not illness or inability.

The scoffer (19:25, 29). The one who will not be taught, because his problem lies in the will. Striking him may not change him, but the simple see it and grow wary, and the judgments prepared for scoffers show that contempt for correction meets correction in harder form.
