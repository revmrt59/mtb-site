Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 21
Main Idea:

Proverbs 21 opens with the king’s heart in the LORD’s hand and closes with deliverance that belongs to Him, and between those frames it shows a God who weighs hearts and prizes justice above sacrifice, warning against pride, haste, and laziness and commending the righteous man who gives and does not spare.

Setting:

The sayings continue Solomon’s collection begun at 10:1. Royal and military pictures appear at its beginning, middle, and end (21:1, 22, 31), and paired sayings on worship (21:3, 27) and the contentious woman (21:9, 19) echo across it.

Chapter Outline:

  • Proverbs 21:1–3 — The LORD Weighs the Hearts

  • Proverbs 21:4–8 — Pride, Haste, and Ill-Gotten Treasure

  • Proverbs 21:9–19 — Justice and the Cry of the Poor

  • Proverbs 21:20–29 — The Righteous Give and Do Not Spare

  • Proverbs 21:30–31 — Deliverance Is of the LORD

Key Verse:

“Every way of a man is right in his own eyes, But the LORD weighs the hearts.” (Proverbs 21:2)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Proverbs 21 begins on the throne and ends on the battlefield, and both scenes belong to the LORD. “The king’s heart is in the hand of the LORD” (21:1), and when the horse is ready for war, “deliverance is of the LORD” (21:31). The throne and the army, the two strongest powers in Solomon’s world, both answer to Him.

Inside that frame the chapter keeps returning to what the LORD sees. A man’s way looks right in his own eyes, “But the LORD weighs the hearts” (21:2). Pride is named as sin in “A haughty look, a proud heart” (21:4), and the proud and haughty man bears the name “Scoffer” (21:24). The wicked man “hardens his face” (21:29), but the righteous God “wisely considers the house of the wicked” (21:12) and overthrows them for their wickedness.

The rest weighs what people do with money and need. Treasure won by a lying tongue is a “fleeting fantasy” (21:6), the lover of pleasure ends poor (21:17), and the lazy man is killed by his own desire because his hands refuse to labor (21:25). Against him stands the righteous man who “gives and does not spare” (21:26), alongside the warning that whoever shuts his ears to the poor “Will also cry himself and not be heard” (21:13).

Chapter Sections

Proverbs 21:1–3 — The LORD Weighs the Hearts

As a farmer turns water into the channel he chooses, the LORD turns the heart of the most powerful ruler, and He looks past the approval every person gives his own way to weigh the heart itself. What pleases Him more than any sacrifice is a life of righteousness and justice.

Proverbs 21:4–8 — Pride, Haste, and Ill-Gotten Treasure

A haughty look, a proud heart, and even the plowing of the wicked are named together as sin, and the careful plans of the diligent are set against the haste that ends in poverty. Treasure won by lying vanishes like a vapor, the violence of the wicked sweeps them away because they refuse justice, and a man’s crooked or upright conduct shows what he is.

Proverbs 21:9–19 — Justice and the Cry of the Poor

Two sayings about a contentious woman bracket the section, picturing a home so full of strife that a corner of the housetop or the wilderness would be easier to live in, a warning that applies to husbands as well as wives. Between them, the punished scoffer becomes a lesson to the simple, the man who shuts his ears to the cry of the poor will one day cry and not be heard, and doing justice is a joy to the just while destruction awaits the workers of iniquity.

Proverbs 21:20–29 — The Righteous Give and Do Not Spare

The wise keep the treasure and oil that a foolish man squanders, and whoever pursues righteousness and mercy finds life and honor along with the righteousness he sought. A wise man brings down the stronghold the mighty trusted in, a guarded tongue keeps the soul from troubles, the proud man earns the name “Scoffer,” and the lazy man craves all day while the righteous man gives without holding back.

Proverbs 21:30–31 — Deliverance Is of the LORD

No wisdom, understanding, or counsel can stand against the LORD. The horse is rightly made ready for battle, yet the victory comes from Him, so preparation is carried out in trust and never in place of it.

Why This Chapter Matters

Proverbs 21 gives God’s people reason to trust Him above every ruler. If the king’s heart is in the LORD’s hand, no throne is beyond His reach, a confidence that fuels prayer for those in authority (1 Timothy 2:1–2) instead of excusing their injustice. The closing verse keeps the same balance in war: the horse is still prepared, but trust rests on the LORD (compare Psalm 20:7).

The chapter also measures worship by the life behind it. The LORD does not set sacrifice aside, yet righteousness and justice please Him more (21:3), and an offering brought “with wicked intent” is an abomination all the more (21:27). It notices as well the man whose neighbor “finds no favor in his eyes” and the one who shuts his ears to the poor (21:10, 13); that warning targets a hard heart, not every unanswered prayer.

Key Themes to Watch For

The LORD over rulers and plans. The king’s heart, every human counsel, and the outcome of battle are in His hand (21:1, 30–31).

Justice above ritual. Righteousness and justice please the LORD more than sacrifice, the wicked who refuse justice are destroyed, and doing justice is a joy to the just (21:3, 7, 15, 27).

Pride and the scoffer. A haughty look and a proud heart are sin, the proud and haughty man is called a scoffer, and the scoffer’s punishment teaches the simple (21:4, 11, 24).

Wealth gained and wasted. Diligent plans lead to plenty, while haste, lies, love of pleasure, and the fool’s squandering end in loss (21:5–6, 17, 20).

The poor and the generous. The ear shut to the poor brings an unheard cry, and the lazy man craves all day while the righteous man gives freely (21:13, 25–26).

Key Words & Concepts

Rivers of water (21:1). Most likely the irrigation channels a farmer directs where he wants the water to go. The picture shows how readily the LORD directs a ruler, as when He “stirred up the spirit of Cyrus king of Persia” (Ezra 1:1).

Contentious woman (21:9, 19). A wife given to quarreling, and in 21:19 to anger as well. The sayings portray the misery of strife in a home, not a joke about wives; 21:9 returns almost word for word in 25:24.

Scoffer (21:11, 24). The scorner who will not be taught, whose problem lies in the will rather than the mind. Proverbs 21:24 shows where his contempt for correction comes from: the proud and haughty man “acts with arrogant pride.”

Ransom (21:18). A price paid to set someone free. The saying most likely means that the trouble the wicked plan for the righteous falls on themselves instead (compare 11:8), though its exact sense is debated. In the gospel God reverses that order in grace, for Christ suffered “the just for the unjust, that He might bring us to God” (1 Peter 3:18).

Abomination (21:27). Something the LORD finds detestable. Here even a sacrifice becomes one when a wicked heart offers it, and it is all the more detestable when brought with wicked intent (compare 15:8).
