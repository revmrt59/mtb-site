Proverbs 24

Verse-by-Verse
Proverbs 24:1
Proverbs 24:1
NKJV

Proverbs 24:1 Do not be envious of evil men, Nor desire to be with them;(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 24:1 Don’t envy evil peopleor desire their company. (NLT)

Main Idea

Do not envy evil men or wish to be in their company. The son is warned against the quiet pull of admiring the wicked and wanting to belong among them.

Verse Explanation

Do not envy evil men or wish to be in their company. The son is warned against the quiet pull of admiring the wicked and wanting to belong among them.

“Do not be envious of evil men,”

The words of the wise return to a danger already named in 23:17. Evil men often seem to do well. They take what they want, answer to no one, and appear free of the restraints the son lives under. Envy is the quiet resentment and longing that such success can stir. The command is to refuse it. Envy of the wicked is dangerous because it slowly changes what a person admires.

“Nor desire to be with them;”

Envy easily turns into a wish to belong. The son is warned not to “desire to be with them,” not to want a place in their company. Proverbs began with the same warning, when sinners invited the son to “Come with us” and share their loot (1:11). What a person admires he soon wants to join, and what he joins he soon becomes. The next verse shows what the son would be joining.

Proverbs 24:2 For their heart devises violence, And their lips talk of troublemaking.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 24:2 For their hearts plot violence,and their words always stir up trouble. (NLT)

Main Idea

Their hearts plot violence, and their lips talk about making trouble. Seen for what they really are, evil men offer nothing worth envying, since everything inside and everything they say aims at harm.

Verse Explanation

Their hearts plot violence, and their lips talk about making trouble. Seen for what they really are, evil men offer nothing worth envying, since everything inside and everything they say aims at harm.

“For their heart devises violence,”

The reason not to envy evil men is what they are like inside. Their “heart devises violence.” Their inner life is busy planning harm to others. What looks like freedom or success from the outside is, at its core, a mind given over to violence.

“And their lips talk of troublemaking.”

Their speech matches their hearts. Their “lips talk of troublemaking,” the schemes and mischief they plan for others. Heart and mouth together are aimed at harm. Seen for what they really are, evil men offer nothing worth envying. The son who looks past their apparent success to the heart and the lips will see that their company is a place of trouble, not freedom. The contrast with the next verse is deliberate: the wicked plan to tear down, while wisdom builds.

Proverbs 24:3
Proverbs 24:3
NKJV

Proverbs 24:3 Through wisdom a house is built, And by understanding it is established;(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 24:3 A house is built by wisdomand becomes strong through good sense. (NLT)

Main Idea

A house is built by wisdom and made secure by understanding. Over against the violence of the wicked, wisdom is pictured as a builder, and the houses of a life, such as a marriage, a family, or a livelihood, show the mark of the wisdom or folly behind them.

Verse Explanation

A house is built by wisdom and made secure by understanding. Over against the violence of the wicked, wisdom is pictured as a builder, and the houses of a life, such as a marriage, a family, or a livelihood, show the mark of the wisdom or folly behind them.

“Through wisdom a house is built,”

Over against the violence of the wicked, wisdom is pictured as a builder. A “house” is first a dwelling, and in Proverbs it also means a household: the family, the life, and the livelihood a person builds. Wisdom, skill for living God’s way, is what builds it. Proverbs uses the same picture of Wisdom herself, who “has built her house” (9:1), and of the wise woman who “builds her house” (14:1).

“And by understanding it is established;”

Building is not enough; the house must stand. “Understanding” makes it “established,” firm and secure, able to last. The houses of a life, such as a marriage, a family, a business, or a ministry, show the mark of the wisdom or folly behind them. What has been built reveals the builder. This is not meant to condemn those whose homes have suffered loss through no fault of their own. It calls for honest examination of what one is building and with what tools.

Study Notes

The three Hebrew words of 24:3–4 for wisdom, understanding, and knowledge are the same three used of the LORD’s own work in creation, and the verb for “established” in 24:3 is the one used there of the heavens: “The LORD by wisdom founded the earth; By understanding He established the heavens; By His knowledge the depths were broken up” (3:19–20). The builder of a household works with the same qualities by which God made the world.

Proverbs 24:4 By knowledge the rooms are filled With all precious and pleasant riches.(NKJV)

Proverbs 24:4 Through knowledge its rooms are filledwith all sorts of precious riches and valuables. (NLT)

Main Idea

Through knowledge the rooms are filled with every kind of precious and pleasant treasure. Wisdom does more than raise the walls; it furnishes the house with what makes it rich and good to live in.

Verse Explanation

Through knowledge the rooms are filled with every kind of precious and pleasant treasure. Wisdom does more than raise the walls; it furnishes the house with what makes it rich and good to live in.

“By knowledge the rooms are filled”

Wisdom does more than raise walls. “By knowledge the rooms are filled.” Knowledge here is practical understanding of how to live, manage, and provide. It furnishes the house so that it becomes a place worth living in.

“With all precious and pleasant riches.”

The rooms are filled “With all precious and pleasant riches.” The riches are both valuable and delightful. In a household, they may include material provision, and they also include the things that make a home rich in the fuller sense: peace, love, good order, and joy. Like other sayings about reward, this describes what wisdom tends to produce under God’s blessing, not a promise of wealth. The picture stands in sharp contrast to the neglected field at the end of the chapter, described in 24:30–31, where a stone wall that was once built has been left to fall and weeds have overrun the ground. Wisdom builds a house and keeps it in good order; neglect lets even what was built fall into ruin.

Proverbs 24:5
Proverbs 24:5
NKJV

Proverbs 24:5 A wise man is strong, Yes, a man of knowledge increases strength;(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 24:5 The wise are mightier than the strong,and those with knowledge grow stronger and stronger. (NLT)

Main Idea

A wise man is strong, and a man of knowledge grows in strength. Wisdom is a kind of power, often greater than physical might.

Verse Explanation

A wise man is strong, and a man of knowledge grows in strength. Wisdom is a kind of power, often greater than physical might.

“A wise man is strong,”

Wisdom is a form of strength. “A wise man is strong.” Strength in the ancient world usually meant physical might or military power, but the wise man has a strength of his own: the ability to see clearly, plan well, and act effectively.

“Yes, a man of knowledge increases strength;”

The second line goes further. “A man of knowledge increases strength.” Knowledge does not merely give a fixed amount of power; it multiplies it. A strong man without wisdom may waste his strength, while a wise man uses whatever strength he has to the best effect. The saying prepares for the next verse, where wisdom proves itself on the battlefield. Ecclesiastes says the same: “Wisdom is better than strength” (Ecclesiastes 9:16).

Study Notes

The Hebrew wording is more vivid than the English. The KJV margin notes that the first line says literally that a wise man is in strength, and that the second says a man of knowledge “strengtheneth might,” that is, he makes his strength greater. The literal wording supports the point of the verse: knowledge does not merely possess strength but builds it up.

NLT

Proverbs 24:6 So don’t go to war without wise guidance;victory depends on having many advisers. (NLT)

Main Idea

Wise guidance lets a person wage his battles, and safety is found where there are many counselors. Strength comes from seeking and heeding sound advice, not from going it alone (compare 11:14).

Verse Explanation

Wise guidance lets a person wage his battles, and safety is found where there are many counselors. Strength comes from seeking and heeding sound advice, not from going it alone (compare 11:14).

“For by wise counsel you will wage your own war,”

The reason wisdom is strength is that wars are won by “wise counsel,” sound guidance and good strategy. A king who plans his campaign carefully, using wise advice, can “wage your own war” successfully. The same is true of any struggle a person faces. Proverbs has already said, “By wise counsel wage war” (20:18).

“And in a multitude of counselors there is safety.”

Wise counsel usually comes from more than one person. “In a multitude of counselors there is safety.” A leader who listens to many wise advisers sees more clearly, catches mistakes, and avoids disaster. This repeats 11:14 almost exactly. Strength comes from seeking and heeding good advice, not from relying only on oneself. Pride that refuses counsel is weakness, however strong it looks.

Study Notes

The second line repeats the second line of 11:14, “But in the multitude of counselors there is safety” (11:14).

Proverbs 24:7–12 — Strength in Adversity and Rescue for the Perishing

Proverbs 24:7
Proverbs 24:7
NKJV

Proverbs 24:7 Wisdom is too lofty for a fool; He does not open his mouth in the gate.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 24:7 Wisdom is too lofty for fools.Among leaders at the city gate, they have nothing to say. (NLT)

Main Idea

Wisdom is out of the fool’s reach, so he has nothing to say at the city gate where matters are decided. When serious affairs are discussed, the fool has no useful word to offer.

Verse Explanation

Wisdom is out of the fool’s reach, so he has nothing to say at the city gate where matters are decided. When serious affairs are discussed, the fool has no useful word to offer.

“Wisdom is too lofty for a fool;”

To the fool, wisdom is “too lofty,” high above him and out of reach. The problem is not that wisdom is hidden. Proverbs shows Wisdom calling openly in the streets in 1:20–21. The fool cannot reach wisdom because he despises it and will not stoop to learn.

“He does not open his mouth in the gate.”

The “gate” of the city was where the elders met, cases were judged, and public matters were decided. When serious affairs are discussed there, the fool “does not open his mouth.” He has nothing useful to say. Elsewhere Proverbs shows the fool talking constantly, but in the place where wisdom counts, he has no word to offer. His silence at the gate exposes his emptiness.

Proverbs 24:9 The devising of foolishness is sin, And the scoffer is an abomination to men.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 24:9 The schemes of a fool are sinful;everyone detests a mocker. (NLT)

Main Idea

Plotting folly is sin, and people find the scoffer detestable. Wrongdoing begins in what is devised before it is done, and the mocker’s contempt makes him repugnant to others.

Verse Explanation

Plotting folly is sin, and people find the scoffer detestable. Wrongdoing begins in what is devised before it is done, and the mocker’s contempt makes him repugnant to others.

“The devising of foolishness is sin,”

The verse goes to the root. Sin is not only in the doing; it is in “The devising of foolishness.” Planning folly, scheming what is wrong, is already sin before any act follows. Proverbs consistently traces conduct back to the heart, and here the plans of the heart are themselves judged.

“And the scoffer is an abomination to men.”

The second line names the scoffer, the proud mocker who will not be corrected. He is “an abomination to men.” Proverbs often speaks of what is an abomination to the LORD, but here it is people who find the scoffer detestable. His constant contempt, mockery, and refusal to be taught make him repugnant even to others. The scoffer does not only offend God; he makes himself unbearable to everyone around him.

Proverbs 24:10
Proverbs 24:10
NKJV

Proverbs 24:10 If you faint in the day of adversity, Your strength is small.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 24:10 If you fail under pressure,your strength is too small. (NLT)

Main Idea

If you lose heart when trouble comes, your strength is small. Adversity reveals how much strength a person really has, and the saying prepares for the hard demand that follows.

Verse Explanation

If you lose heart when trouble comes, your strength is small. Adversity reveals how much strength a person really has, and the saying prepares for the hard demand that follows.

“If you faint in the day of adversity,”

The “day of adversity” is a time of trouble or distress. To “faint” is to lose heart, give up, or collapse under the pressure. The verse imagines the son facing a hard time and failing to hold up.

“Your strength is small.”

The conclusion is blunt: “Your strength is small.” Adversity reveals how much strength a person really has. Strength that holds only when things are easy is not much strength. The saying prepares for the hard command in 24:11–12, where the son must rescue those in danger even when it is costly. Real strength is shown in persevering through difficulty, and for the believer that strength comes from God, who gives power to the weak.

Study Notes

In Hebrew, “adversity” (tsarah) and “small” (tsar) sound alike, and both come from words for narrowness: the day of adversity is a day of tight straits, and strength that gives way in it is narrow strength. The play on words ties the two lines together and sharpens the rebuke, because the narrow place exposes how narrow a person’s strength really is.

Proverbs 24:11
Proverbs 24:11
NKJV

Proverbs 24:11 Deliver those who are drawn toward death, And hold back those stumbling to the slaughter.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 24:11 Rescue those who are unjustly sentenced to die;save them as they stagger to their death. (NLT)

Main Idea

Rescue those who are being dragged toward death, and hold back those staggering toward slaughter. Wisdom does not stand by while others are led to destruction, even when stepping in is costly.

Verse Explanation

Rescue those who are being dragged toward death, and hold back those staggering toward slaughter. Wisdom does not stand by while others are led to destruction, even when stepping in is costly.

“Deliver those who are drawn toward death,”

The command is to rescue. Some people are being “drawn toward death,” dragged or led toward destruction. The verse does not say who they are or why, and the language could include people unjustly condemned, those led away to be killed, and anyone heading toward ruin. The son is told to “Deliver” them, stepping in to save them.

“And hold back those stumbling to the slaughter.”

The second line repeats the command. The son must “hold back,” restrain and protect, “those stumbling to the slaughter.” They are staggering toward a violent death and cannot save themselves. Wisdom does not stand by while others are led to destruction, even when stepping in is costly. This follows directly from 24:10: rescue takes strength, and the person who faints in adversity will not do it. The verse applies broadly to injustice and danger wherever they are found, and it calls for action, not just sympathy.

Proverbs 24:12
NKJV

Proverbs 24:12 If you say, "Surely we did not know this," Does not He who weighs the hearts consider it? He who keeps your soul, does He not know it? And will He not render to each man according to his deeds?(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 24:12 Don’t excuse yourself by saying, “Look, we didn’t know.”For God understands all hearts, and he sees you.He who guards your soul knows you knew.He will repay all people as their actions deserve. (NLT)

Main Idea

If you say, “Surely we did not know this,” God who weighs the heart sees through the excuse. He guards your life, He knows the truth, and He will repay each person according to what he has done, so pleading ignorance does not cover indifference.

Verse Explanation

If you say, “Surely we did not know this,” God who weighs the heart sees through the excuse. He guards your life, He knows the truth, and He will repay each person according to what he has done, so pleading ignorance does not cover indifference.

“If you say, ‘Surely we did not know this,’”

The son might try to excuse himself: “Surely we did not know this.” It is the oldest excuse for doing nothing. We did not know, we did not see, it was not our business. The verse anticipates this plea and answers it.

“Does not He who weighs the hearts consider it?”

God “weighs the hearts” (21:2). He knows whether the excuse is true. He sees whether the son truly did not know or chose not to know. Ignorance that is willful is not innocence.

“He who keeps your soul, does He not know it?”

The same God “keeps your soul,” guarding and preserving your life. The One who watches over the son also watches the son’s response to others in danger. He knows everything.

“And will He not render to each man according to his deeds?”

Finally, God will “render to each man according to his deeds.” He will repay. Indifference to those being dragged toward death is not neutral. It will be judged. Pleading ignorance does not cover indifference before God.

Study Notes

Paul states the same principle in Romans 2:6, “who will render to each one according to his deeds” (Romans 2:6), and Psalm 62:12 says the same of God in different Hebrew words.

Proverbs 24:13–16 — Sweet Wisdom and the Righteous Who Rise Again

Proverbs 24:13
Proverbs 24:13
NKJV

Proverbs 24:13 My son, eat honey because it is good, (H2896) And the honeycomb which is sweet to your taste;(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 24:13 My child, eat honey, for it is good,and the honeycomb is sweet to the taste. (NLT)

Main Idea

My son, eat honey because it is good, and the honeycomb because it is sweet to the taste. The father begins with an everyday pleasure to set up a comparison.

Verse Explanation

My son, eat honey because it is good, and the honeycomb because it is sweet to the taste. The father begins with an everyday pleasure to set up a comparison.

“My son, eat honey because it is good,”

The father turns to an everyday pleasure. Honey was prized for its sweetness, and the father tells the son to “eat honey because it is good.” There is nothing wrong with enjoying it. This sets up a comparison.

“And the honeycomb which is sweet to your taste;”

The honeycomb, honey straight from the comb, was especially prized. It is “sweet to your taste.” The father draws attention to the pleasure of the senses so that the next verse can point beyond it. Just as honey delights the tongue, something else will delight the soul. Proverbs does not treat pleasure as suspect in itself; it uses an ordinary good to point toward a greater one.

Proverbs 24:14
Proverbs 24:14
NKJV

Proverbs 24:14 So shall the knowledge of wisdom be to your soul; If you have found it, there is a prospect, And your hope will not be cut off.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 24:14 In the same way, wisdom is sweet to your soul.If you find it, you will have a bright future,and your hopes will not be cut short. (NLT)

Main Idea

Wisdom is just as sweet and good for the soul, and whoever finds it has a future and a hope that will not be cut off. The pleasure of honey points to a deeper sweetness that also lasts (compare 23:18).

Verse Explanation

Wisdom is just as sweet and good for the soul, and whoever finds it has a future and a hope that will not be cut off. The pleasure of honey points to a deeper sweetness that also lasts (compare 23:18).

“So shall the knowledge of wisdom be to your soul;”

Here is the point of the comparison. As honey is sweet and good to the body, “So shall the knowledge of wisdom be to your soul.” Wisdom is not only useful; it is sweet. The person who comes to know it finds it delightful, nourishing, and satisfying. The psalmist said the same of God’s word: “Sweeter also than honey and the honeycomb” (Psalm 19:10).

“If you have found it, there is a prospect,”

Wisdom also has a future. “If you have found it, there is a prospect,” a hopeful outcome ahead. Honey’s sweetness passes quickly, but wisdom’s good lasts.

“And your hope will not be cut off.”

The promise echoes 23:18 almost word for word. The person who finds wisdom has a hope that “will not be cut off.” It will not be disappointed or destroyed. The wicked have no future, as 24:20 will say, but the one who finds wisdom has a lasting hope under God.

Study Notes

In Hebrew, “prospect” is the same word rendered “hereafter” in 23:18, a word for an end or future outcome, and the final line of the two verses is identical in the NKJV.

Proverbs 24:16
Proverbs 24:16
NKJV

Proverbs 24:16 For a righteous man may fall seven times And rise again, But the wicked shall fall by calamity.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 24:16 The godly may trip seven times, but they will get up again.But one disaster is enough to overthrow the wicked. (NLT)

Main Idea

Though a righteous man falls seven times, he rises again, but the wicked are brought down by calamity. The falls here are troubles and setbacks the righteous suffer, yet under God’s care they are not finally overthrown; the saying gives no excuse for repeated sin.

Verse Explanation

Though a righteous man falls seven times, he rises again, but the wicked are brought down by calamity. The falls here are troubles and setbacks the righteous suffer, yet under God’s care they are not finally overthrown; the saying gives no excuse for repeated sin.

“For a righteous man may fall seven times”

The reason the wicked man’s plans cannot finally succeed is that the righteous cannot be finally defeated. He “may fall seven times.” Seven times means again and again, as often as trouble comes. In context, the falls are the troubles and setbacks the wicked bring on him, attacks and losses that knock him down.

“And rise again,”

Yet he will “rise again.” He is not finally overthrown. The righteous man gets back up, not by his own strength alone, but because God upholds him. Micah expressed the same confidence: “When I fall, I will arise” (Micah 7:8). The saying does not promise that every loss will be quickly restored. The righteous may suffer real and lasting loss in this life, and the rising may be slow or may come only at the end. What the verse promises is that the one God upholds is not finally overthrown. The verse is not an excuse for repeated sin. It is a promise of resilience under God’s care, and where it is applied to failure, repentance remains central.

“But the wicked shall fall by calamity.”

The wicked man, by contrast, “shall fall by calamity.” His fall carries no promise of rising. The righteous may be knocked down many times and still be upheld, but the wicked may fall once and be finished. This is why the wicked man is warned in 24:15: his attacks on the righteous cannot finally prevail, while his own end is ruin.

Study Notes

Psalm 37 uses a similar picture of the righteous: “Though he fall, he shall not be utterly cast down; For the LORD upholds him with His hand” (Psalm 37:24).

Proverbs 24:17–22 — Do Not Gloat or Fret; Fear the LORD

Proverbs 24:17
Proverbs 24:17
NKJV

Proverbs 24:17 Do not rejoice when your enemy falls, And do not let your heart be glad when he stumbles;(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 24:17 Don’t rejoice when your enemies fall;don’t be happy when they stumble. (NLT)

Main Idea

Do not celebrate when your enemy falls, and do not let your heart be glad when he stumbles. Gloating over another’s ruin is forbidden even when that person has been an enemy.

Verse Explanation

Do not celebrate when your enemy falls, and do not let your heart be glad when he stumbles. Gloating over another’s ruin is forbidden even when that person has been an enemy.

“Do not rejoice when your enemy falls,”

The command is surprising in a world where victory over an enemy was celebrated. The son must “not rejoice when your enemy falls.” Even when someone has truly been an enemy, his downfall is not an occasion for glee. Job counted it among the sins he had not committed: “If I have rejoiced at the destruction of him who hated me” (Job 31:29).

“And do not let your heart be glad when he stumbles;”

The second line goes deeper. It is not enough to avoid public celebration; the son must not even “let your heart be glad.” Gloating can happen silently, inside. The command reaches to the private satisfaction a person might feel when someone who hurt him suffers. God cares about the heart’s response to an enemy’s trouble, not only about outward behavior. Proverbs elsewhere calls for kindness to an enemy in need, as in 25:21, and Jesus carried this teaching further when He said, “love your enemies” (Matthew 5:44).

Proverbs 24:18
Proverbs 24:18
NKJV

Proverbs 24:18 Lest the LORD see it, and it displease Him, And He turn away His wrath from him.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 24:18 For the LORD will be displeased with youand will turn his anger away from them. (NLT)

Main Idea

The LORD may see it, be displeased, and turn His anger away from the enemy. The point is the condition of the gloater’s own heart, which offends God, not a technique for making an enemy suffer more.

Verse Explanation

The LORD may see it, be displeased, and turn His anger away from the enemy. The point is the condition of the gloater’s own heart, which offends God, not a technique for making an enemy suffer more.

“Lest the LORD see it, and it displease Him,”

The reason is that the LORD sees the gloating heart, “and it displease Him.” Gloating over another’s ruin is itself a sin. It reveals pride, cruelty, and a heart that has forgotten its own need of mercy. The LORD who weighs the hearts, as 24:12 has just said, sees the satisfaction the son takes in his enemy’s fall.

“And He turn away His wrath from him.”

The result is that God might “turn away His wrath from him,” that is, from the enemy. The point is not a technique for making an enemy suffer more by hiding one’s joy. The point is that gloating offends God so much that He may turn His attention from the enemy’s guilt to the gloater’s. Judgment belongs to God, and the person who celebrates another’s downfall has stepped into a place that is not his. The verse guards the son’s own heart.

Verse Explanation

Do not fret over evildoers or envy the wicked. The son is told again not to let the apparent success of the wicked stir him to worry or jealousy (compare verse 1).

“Do not fret because of evildoers,”

The son is warned again about his response to the wicked. To “fret” is to become agitated, anxious, or angry, burning inside over the apparent success of “evildoers.” The sight of wicked people prospering can stir up resentment and worry in the righteous.

“Nor be envious of the wicked;”
