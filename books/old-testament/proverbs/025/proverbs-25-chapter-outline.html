Proverbs 25 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Proverbs 25 opens the proverbs copied by Hezekiah’s men with sayings about kings and humility, discreet disputes, fitting words, moderation, love for an enemy, and self-rule, many of them vivid comparisons.

Chapter Sections

1. Proverbs 25:1–7 — The Glory of Kings and the Lower Seat

These proverbs of Solomon were copied by the men of Hezekiah. It is God’s glory to conceal a matter and the glory of kings to search it out, and it is better to be invited higher than to be put lower before the prince.

2. Proverbs 25:8–10 — Settle Disputes Discreetly

Do not go hastily to court, but debate the case with your neighbor and do not reveal another’s secret.

3. Proverbs 25:11–15 — A Word Fitly Spoken

A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold in settings of silver, and a wise rebuke to an obedient ear is like gold. A faithful messenger refreshes, and a gentle tongue breaks a bone.

4. Proverbs 25:16–20 — Moderation, False Witness, and the Heavy Heart

Honey and a neighbor’s house are to be enjoyed in moderation. A false witness wounds like a weapon, an unfaithful man fails in trouble, and singing songs to a heavy heart is like taking away a garment in the cold.

5. Proverbs 25:21–22 — Kindness to an Enemy

If an enemy is hungry or thirsty, give him bread and water, for so you will heap coals of fire on his head, and the LORD will reward you.

6. Proverbs 25:23–28 — Good News and Self-Rule

A backbiting tongue brings an angry face, and a contentious woman is worse than a housetop corner. Good news is like cold water to a weary soul, and whoever has no rule over his own spirit is like a city without walls.
