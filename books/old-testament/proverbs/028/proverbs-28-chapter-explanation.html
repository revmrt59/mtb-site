Proverbs 28

Verse-by-Verse
Proverbs 28:1
Proverbs 28:1
NKJV

Proverbs 28:1 The wicked flee when no one pursues, But the righteous are bold as a lion.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 28:1 The wicked run away when no one is chasing them,but the godly are as bold as lions. (NLT)

Main Idea

A guilty conscience makes the wicked run when no one is chasing them, while the righteous stand as bold as a lion. Their boldness is the confidence of a clear conscience before God, and this opening contrast between fear and courage sets the tone for the righteous-and-wicked sayings of chapters 28–29.

Verse Explanation

A guilty conscience makes the wicked run when no one is chasing them, while the righteous stand as bold as a lion. Their boldness is the confidence of a clear conscience before God, and this opening contrast between fear and courage sets the tone for the righteous-and-wicked sayings of chapters 28–29.

“The wicked flee when no one pursues,” The wicked man runs, though no one is chasing him. His guilt makes him afraid. He hears footsteps that are not there and sees threats in shadows. A guilty conscience does not need an actual enemy to fill a person with fear; it supplies its own. The Law warned that the disobedient would “flee when no one pursues” (Leviticus 26:17).

“But the righteous are bold as a lion.” The righteous man stands his ground. Like the lion of 30:30, which does not turn away from any beast, he faces what comes without panic. His boldness is not aggression or arrogance; it is the confidence of a clear conscience before God. He has nothing to hide, so he has nothing to fear from exposure.

This opening saying sets the tone for chapters 28–29, which return to direct contrasts between the righteous and the wicked after the many comparisons of chapters 25–27. It locates the difference first in the inner life: guilt produces fear, and righteousness before God produces courage. The chapter will later show where the guilty can find relief, in confession and mercy (28:13).

Study Notes

Chapters 28–29 contain many sayings in which the two lines contrast the righteous and the wicked, much like 10:1–15:33. Several of them concern rulers and the effect of righteous or wicked leadership on a whole community (28:2, 12, 15–16, 28).

Proverbs 28:2
Proverbs 28:2
NKJV

Proverbs 28:2 Because of the transgression of a land, many are its princes; But by a man of understanding and knowledge Right will be prolonged.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 28:2 When there is moral rot within a nation, its government topples easily.But wise and knowledgeable leaders bring stability. (NLT)

Main Idea

When a land is full of rebellion, its rulers come and go in quick succession, but a man of understanding and knowledge brings lasting order. A nation’s sin breeds instability, while wise leadership allows what is right to endure.

Verse Explanation

When a land is full of rebellion, its rulers come and go in quick succession, but a man of understanding and knowledge brings lasting order. A nation’s sin breeds instability, while wise leadership allows what is right to endure.

“Because of the transgression of a land, many are its princes;” When a nation is full of rebellion against God and His ways, its rulers change rapidly. One leader falls and another rises, through plots, revolts or assassinations. Instability at the top reflects corruption throughout the land. Sin does not stay private; it weakens the whole nation and makes stable government hard to keep.

“But by a man of understanding and knowledge Right will be prolonged.” In contrast, a single wise and knowledgeable leader can bring lasting order. Understanding and knowledge here are moral and practical wisdom, the ability to see what is right and govern accordingly. Under such a man, what is right is prolonged: justice and stability endure.

The saying ties a nation’s stability to its moral condition and to the character of its leaders. It applies to every level of authority, from nations to communities and households, and it describes the general way God has ordered His world rather than predicting the fate of a particular government.

Study Notes

Israel’s northern kingdom illustrates the first line: it saw many changes of dynasty, several by assassination, in its last decades (2 Kings 15). The proverb itself names no nation.

The second line is compressed in Hebrew, and translations render it in different ways. The word the NKJV translates “Right” can mean right or just, and a Hebrew word spelled the same way is the ordinary word for “so,” which is how the NASB reads it. The NKJV’s “Right will be prolonged” and the NASB’s reading both express lasting stability under a wise ruler.

Proverbs 28:3
Proverbs 28:3
NKJV

Proverbs 28:3 A poor man who oppresses the poor Is like a driving rain which leaves no food.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 28:3 A poor person who oppresses the pooris like a pounding rain that destroys the crops. (NLT)

Main Idea

Oppression by a poor man against others who are poor is like a beating rain that flattens the crops instead of watering them. Rain ought to bring food, yet this storm leaves none, and the one who knows poverty from the inside should be the last to exploit it.

Verse Explanation

Oppression by a poor man against others who are poor is like a beating rain that flattens the crops instead of watering them. Rain ought to bring food, yet this storm leaves none, and the one who knows poverty from the inside should be the last to exploit it.

“A poor man who oppresses the poor” Here is a man who knows poverty from the inside, yet he takes advantage of others who are poor. Perhaps he has gained a little power or position, and he uses it to squeeze those beneath him. He should understand their struggles better than anyone, which makes his oppression especially bitter.

“Is like a driving rain which leaves no food.” Rain normally brings food, watering the crops and producing a harvest. But a driving, beating rain flattens the grain and washes away the soil, leaving nothing. The man who should have been a help has become a destroyer.

The saying warns that oppression is not limited to the rich and powerful. Anyone with a little power over others can misuse it, and the poor can be cruel to the poor. Knowing hardship should make a person compassionate. When it does not, the result is like a storm that ruins the very fields it should have watered.

Study Notes

In Hebrew the man in the first line is described with a word meaning “poor,” and the NKJV, ESV, NASB, KJV and NLT translate it that way. The 1984 NIV reads “A ruler” instead.

Proverbs 28:4
Proverbs 28:4
NKJV

Proverbs 28:4 Those who forsake the law praise the wicked, But such as keep the law contend with them.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 28:4 To reject the law is to praise the wicked;to obey the law is to fight them. (NLT)

Main Idea

People who abandon God’s law end up praising the wicked, but those who keep it stand up and oppose them. How a person treats God’s instruction shows in whom he admires and whom he resists.

Verse Explanation

People who abandon God’s law end up praising the wicked, but those who keep it stand up and oppose them. How a person treats God’s instruction shows in whom he admires and whom he resists.

“Those who forsake the law praise the wicked,” The law here is God’s instruction, the teaching He has given His people. Those who abandon it lose their moral bearings. They begin to admire and praise wicked people, approving their success, excusing their conduct, even celebrating them. Turning from God’s instruction changes what a person values.

“But such as keep the law contend with them.” Those who keep God’s law oppose the wicked. They resist them, speak against their ways, and refuse to approve what God condemns. Their loyalty to God’s instruction shows in their stance toward evil.

The saying shows that a person’s response to God’s law reveals itself in whom he admires and whom he resists. Neutrality is not really possible. Keeping God’s instruction produces discernment and courage, while forsaking it leads to confusion about right and wrong. The contending here is not quarrelsomeness; it is faithful resistance to what is evil, the courage of 28:1 put into practice.

Study Notes

The Hebrew word behind “law” is torah, which basically means instruction or teaching. It appears four times in three nearby verses, twice in 28:4 and once each in 28:7 and 28:9. In Proverbs the word can mean a parent’s teaching (1:8) as well as God’s instruction; in these sayings it stands for God’s instruction to His people.

Proverbs 28:5
NKJV

Proverbs 28:5 Evil men do not understand justice, But those who seek the LORD understand all.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 28:5 Evil people don’t understand justice,but those who follow the LORD understand completely. (NLT)

Main Idea

Evil men cannot grasp what justice really is, “But those who seek the LORD understand all.” Moral understanding comes from seeking the LORD Himself, and those who turn from Him lose the ability to see right and wrong clearly.

Verse Explanation

Evil men cannot grasp what justice really is, “But those who seek the LORD understand all.” Moral understanding comes from seeking the LORD Himself, and those who turn from Him lose the ability to see right and wrong clearly.

“Evil men do not understand justice,” Evil men cannot grasp what justice really is. Their own wickedness clouds their moral vision. They may speak of fairness, but they do not truly understand right and wrong because they do not want to. Persistent sin dulls the ability to see clearly.

“But those who seek the LORD understand all.” Those who seek the LORD understand all that matters for living rightly. This is not a claim to unlimited knowledge, as though seekers of God knew every fact. It is moral understanding: the ability to see what is just and what is wise. Such understanding comes from seeking God Himself.

The saying connects moral discernment with relationship to God. People do not discover justice by cleverness alone; it is learned by seeking the One who is just. This fits the book’s foundation: “The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge” (1:7). A right view of God opens the way to a right view of everything else.

Proverbs 28:6
Proverbs 28:6
NKJV

Proverbs 28:6 Better is the poor who walks in his integrity Than one perverse in his ways, though he be rich.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 28:6 Better to be poor and honestthan to be dishonest and rich. (NLT)

Main Idea

A poor man who walks in integrity is better off than a rich man whose ways are crooked. Character outweighs wealth, a truth already stated in 19:1 and repeated here at the start of this collection’s contrasts.

Verse Explanation

A poor man who walks in integrity is better off than a rich man whose ways are crooked. Character outweighs wealth, a truth already stated in 19:1 and repeated here at the start of this collection’s contrasts.

“Better is the poor who walks in his integrity” Integrity means wholeness, a life that is consistent and honest. A poor man who walks in integrity has little money, but he has a clean conscience and a straight path. “Than one perverse in his ways, though he be rich.” A rich man whose ways are crooked has wealth, but his life is twisted. He gains by deceit and lives by double standards.

The saying declares that the first is better. Character outweighs wealth. The poor man of integrity has something money cannot buy, while the crooked rich man has gained the world at the cost of his soul. This repeats the thought of 19:1 nearly word for word in its first line, now set at the head of a collection rich in contrasts between the righteous and the wicked.

The verse does not praise poverty for its own sake, and it does not condemn wealth as such. It compares integrity with crookedness and declares integrity better, whatever the bank balance. A person should choose integrity even if it costs him financially.

Study Notes

“Perverse in his ways” translates a Hebrew phrase in which “ways” has a special form used for things that come in twos. The wording pictures a man trying to walk two ways at once, living a double or two-faced life. The same expression returns in 28:18.

Proverbs 28:7
NKJV

Proverbs 28:7 Whoever keeps the law is a discerning son, But a companion of gluttons shames his father.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 28:7 Young people who obey the law are wise;those with wild friends bring shame to their parents. (NLT)

Main Idea

Keeping God’s law marks a son as truly discerning, but one who runs with gluttons brings shame on his father. The company a son keeps shows whether he has taken his father’s teaching to heart.

Verse Explanation

Keeping God’s law marks a son as truly discerning, but one who runs with gluttons brings shame on his father. The company a son keeps shows whether he has taken his father’s teaching to heart.

“Whoever keeps the law is a discerning son,” A son who keeps God’s instruction proves himself discerning. He shows that he understands what matters and has taken his father’s teaching to heart. The law here again is God’s instruction, which a wise father passes on to his son.

“But a companion of gluttons shames his father.” A son who runs with gluttons, people given to excess, waste and self-indulgence, brings shame on his father. His companions show his values, and his choices reflect badly on the one who raised him. The book has already warned the son, “Do not mix with winebibbers, Or with gluttonous eaters” (23:20).

The saying ties together obedience to God and the company one keeps. A son’s friends reveal whether he has received his father’s teaching. Keeping God’s law and keeping wise company go hand in hand, and both bring honor to a father, as 27:11 has already said.

Proverbs 28:8
Proverbs 28:8
NKJV

Proverbs 28:8 One who increases his possessions by usury and extortion Gathers it for him who will pity the poor.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 28:8 Income from charging high interest rateswill end up in the pocket of someone who is kind to the poor. (NLT)

Main Idea

Wealth piled up by charging interest to the needy and by extortion will not stay with the one who gathered it; it ends up with someone who is kind to the poor. What was taken from the poor finds its way back to their benefit under God’s just rule.

Verse Explanation

Wealth piled up by charging interest to the needy and by extortion will not stay with the one who gathered it; it ends up with someone who is kind to the poor. What was taken from the poor finds its way back to their benefit under God’s just rule.

“One who increases his possessions by usury and extortion” Usury here means charging interest to the needy, and extortion means taking more than is right by pressure or fraud. The Law of Moses forbade Israelites to charge interest on loans to their poor brothers: “you shall not be like a moneylender to him; you shall not charge him interest” (Exodus 22:25). This man builds his wealth by exploiting those who are already struggling.

“Gathers it for him who will pity the poor.” Yet his wealth will not stay with him. In the end it passes to someone who shows kindness to the poor. What was taken from the poor finds its way back to their benefit. The man thinks he is gathering for himself, but he is gathering for someone else.

The saying affirms God’s justice working through the course of events. Proverbs has said something similar: “the wealth of the sinner is stored up for the righteous” (13:22). This is a description of God’s moral order, not a promise that every exploiter will lose his fortune in his lifetime or that wealth will automatically come to believers. It warns that wealth gained by oppression is not secure, and that God sees and cares for the poor.

Study Notes

The Law also forbade interest on food lent to a poor brother (Leviticus 25:36–37). The two Hebrew words behind “usury and extortion” both refer to forms of interest or profit taken from a loan.

Proverbs 28:9
Proverbs 28:9
NKJV

Proverbs 28:9 One who turns away his ear from hearing the law, Even his prayer is an abomination.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 28:9 God detests the prayersof a person who ignores the law. (NLT)

Main Idea

When a man refuses to listen to God’s law, even his prayer becomes an abomination. Prayer cannot cover a life that stubbornly will not hear God; the warning concerns persistent refusal, not the cry of the repentant (compare 15:8).

Verse Explanation

When a man refuses to listen to God’s law, even his prayer becomes an abomination. Prayer cannot cover a life that stubbornly will not hear God; the warning concerns persistent refusal, not the cry of the repentant (compare 15:8).

“One who turns away his ear from hearing the law,” This man refuses to listen to God’s instruction. He turns his ear away deliberately; he does not want to hear what God says about how he should live. This is persistent refusal, a settled unwillingness to be taught or corrected by God.

“Even his prayer is an abomination.” Strikingly, even his prayer is detestable to God. Prayer is meant to be communion with God, but this man wants to speak to God while refusing to hear Him. His prayer becomes an attempt to use God without submitting to Him. Proverbs has said, “The sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination to the LORD, But the prayer of the upright is His delight” (15:8).

The warning concerns a person who keeps on refusing to hear God, not the cry of someone who is turning back. The repentant sinner who comes to God with a broken heart is not rejected; the very next section promises mercy to the one who confesses and forsakes his sins (28:13). What God rejects is religious activity that covers a heart that will not listen. Prayer cannot stand in for obedience.

Proverbs 28:10
NKJV

Proverbs 28:10 Whoever causes the upright to go astray in an evil way, He himself will fall into his own pit; But the blameless will inherit good.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 28:10 Those who lead good people along an evil pathwill fall into their own trap,but the honest will inherit good things. (NLT)

Main Idea

Whoever leads upright people into an evil way will fall into his own pit, while the blameless will inherit good. Leading others astray is a trap that snaps shut on the one who sets it.

Verse Explanation

Whoever leads upright people into an evil way will fall into his own pit, while the blameless will inherit good. Leading others astray is a trap that snaps shut on the one who sets it.

“Whoever causes the upright to go astray in an evil way,” This man deliberately leads good people into wrongdoing. He tempts, deceives or pressures the upright to abandon their integrity. Corrupting others is a serious evil, because it multiplies wickedness and destroys what was good. “He himself will fall into his own pit;” The trap he sets for others catches him instead. The pit he dug for the upright becomes his own grave. Proverbs has already said, “Whoever digs a pit will fall into it” (26:27).

“But the blameless will inherit good.” In contrast, those who remain blameless will receive good as their inheritance. The upright who resist the tempter, or whom God preserves, end up with blessing.

The verse has three lines instead of the usual two. It sets the corrupter’s self-destruction beside the blameless person’s reward, and it warns anyone tempted to lead others into sin that he is digging a pit for himself.

Proverbs 28:11
Proverbs 28:11
NKJV

Proverbs 28:11 The rich man is wise in his own eyes, But the poor who has understanding searches him out.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 28:11 Rich people may think they are wise,but a poor person with discernment can see right through them. (NLT)

Main Idea

Riches can leave a man wise in his own eyes, but a poor man with understanding sees right through him. Wealth breeds self-confidence, yet it cannot hide a person from the discernment of someone truly wise.

Verse Explanation

Riches can leave a man wise in his own eyes, but a poor man with understanding sees right through him. Wealth breeds self-confidence, yet it cannot hide a person from the discernment of someone truly wise.

“The rich man is wise in his own eyes,” Wealth often brings self-confidence. The rich man assumes his success proves his wisdom. He thinks he knows best because he has done well, and he may become closed to correction. “But the poor who has understanding searches him out.” A poor man who has understanding sees through him. He examines the rich man’s claims and recognizes the pride and folly behind his confidence. His lack of wealth does not prevent him from seeing clearly.

The saying shows that wealth and wisdom are not the same. Riches can make a person feel wise without making him so. Meanwhile, understanding can belong to someone without money or status. The verse does not say every poor person is wise or every rich person foolish; it says the poor man who has understanding can see what the self-satisfied rich man cannot see about himself.

The phrase “wise in his own eyes” recalls the warnings of 26:12 and 3:7. Self-assessment based on success is dangerous, and true discernment can come from unexpected places.

Proverbs 28:12
Proverbs 28:12
NKJV

Proverbs 28:12 When the righteous rejoice, there is great glory; But when the wicked arise, men hide themselves.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 28:12 When the godly succeed, everyone is glad.When the wicked take charge, people go into hiding. (NLT)

Main Idea

When the righteous triumph, there is great glory and celebration, but when the wicked rise to power, people go into hiding. The mood of a whole community follows who is in charge, and the second half of this saying returns at the chapter’s end in verse 28.

Verse Explanation

When the righteous triumph, there is great glory and celebration, but when the wicked rise to power, people go into hiding. The mood of a whole community follows who is in charge, and the second half of this saying returns at the chapter’s end in verse 28.

“When the righteous rejoice, there is great glory;” When the righteous triumph, when they are in positions of influence and things go well for them, there is great glory. The whole community celebrates. Justice prevails, people feel safe, and life flourishes. “But when the wicked arise, men hide themselves.” When wicked people rise to power, ordinary people go into hiding. They keep their heads down, avoid attention, and protect themselves from those who now hold power. Fear replaces celebration.

The saying shows that the character of those in charge affects everyone. Righteous leadership brings joy and openness; wicked leadership brings fear and concealment. The mood of a whole community follows who rises to power. Proverbs makes the same observation elsewhere: “When the righteous are in authority, the people rejoice; But when a wicked man rules, the people groan” (29:2).

This verse opens the second section of the chapter, and its second line returns almost word for word in 28:28, where the chapter closes. The two verses frame the chapter’s concern with righteous and wicked rule and its effects on ordinary people.

Study Notes

The Hebrew of the second line says literally that a man is searched for. The KJV text says that a man is hidden, and its margin gives “sought for.” The picture is of people who disappear so completely that they have to be searched out.

Hiding and covering run through the chapter: people hide themselves when the wicked rise (28:12, 28), the sinner covers his sins (28:13), and the stingy man hides his eyes from the poor (28:27).

Proverbs 28:13
Proverbs 28:13
NKJV

Proverbs 28:13 He who covers his sins will not prosper, But whoever confesses and forsakes them will have mercy.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 28:13 People who conceal their sins will not prosper,but if they confess and turn from them, they will receive mercy. (NLT)

Main Idea

Whoever hides his sins will not prosper, but whoever confesses them and turns away from them will receive mercy. This is the book’s plainest word of mercy for the sinner: confessing and forsaking go together, and mercy is God’s gift to the one who stops hiding, a hope fulfilled in the cleansing Christ provides (1 John 1:9).

Verse Explanation

Whoever hides his sins will not prosper, but whoever confesses them and turns away from them will receive mercy. This is the book’s plainest word of mercy for the sinner: confessing and forsaking go together, and mercy is God’s gift to the one who stops hiding, a hope fulfilled in the cleansing Christ provides (1 John 1:9).

“He who covers his sins will not prosper,” To cover sin is to hide it: to deny it, excuse it, or bury it and hope no one finds out. The person who does this will not prosper. Hidden sin does not stay hidden, and it does not leave the sinner at peace. It festers, damages relationships, hardens the heart and keeps the person from God. David described what concealment did to him: “When I kept silent, my bones grew old Through my groaning all the day long” (Psalm 32:3).

“But whoever confesses and forsakes them will have mercy.” The alternative is to confess, to admit the sin honestly before God and, where appropriate, before others, and to forsake it, turning away and leaving it behind. Confession and forsaking go together. Confession without forsaking is empty words, and trying to forsake sin without confessing it leaves it hidden. The one who does both will have mercy. Mercy is God’s gift, not something earned by confessing; confession opens the door to receive what God freely gives. David found it so: “I said, ‘I will confess my transgressions to the LORD,’ And You forgave the iniquity of my sin” (Psalm 32:5).

This is the plainest word of mercy in the book of Proverbs, and it answers the warnings of chapters 5–7 for anyone who has already fallen into sin. It points ahead to the cleansing God provides in Christ: “If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness” (1 John 1:9).

Study Notes

The Hebrew verb translated “covers” is the same one used in 26:26 of hatred covered by deceit. There the covering is exposed before the assembly; here the alternative to covering is confession.

“Will have mercy” translates a Hebrew verb meaning to love deeply or to show tender compassion. The same verb appears twice in Psalm 103:13, “As a father pities his children, So the LORD pities those who fear Him,” which shows the kind of compassion the word describes.

Proverbs 28:14
Proverbs 28:14
NKJV

Proverbs 28:14 Happy is the man who is always reverent, But he who hardens his heart will fall into calamity.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 28:14 Blessed are those who fear to do wrong,but the stubborn are headed for serious trouble. (NLT)

Main Idea

Happy is the person who lives in constant reverence before God, but the one who hardens his heart will fall into disaster. Verse 13 called for confession; now the call is for a lasting fear of the LORD that keeps the heart soft.

Verse Explanation

Happy is the person who lives in constant reverence before God, but the one who hardens his heart will fall into disaster. Verse 13 called for confession; now the call is for a lasting fear of the LORD that keeps the heart soft.

“Happy is the man who is always reverent,” Happy means truly blessed, living in the good condition God gives. The man who is always reverent lives in constant awe of God. This is the fear of the LORD, a right view of God that brings awe at His holiness and keeps a person tender toward Him and careful about sin. Being always reverent means this awe is not occasional; it is a steady part of his life.

“But he who hardens his heart will fall into calamity.” The opposite of reverence is a hardened heart, a heart that resists God, ignores His warnings and grows insensitive to sin. Such a person will fall into calamity. A hard heart leads to ruin, because it refuses the correction and mercy that would have saved it.

After 28:13 called for confession, this verse calls for a lasting reverence that keeps the heart soft. Confession deals with sin once it has been committed; constant reverence guards the heart against hardening in the first place. The two belong together: the confessing sinner receives mercy, and the reverent heart stays open to God.

Study Notes

The Hebrew says literally that the man fears continually, without naming the object; the KJV has “feareth alway.” The ESV supplies “the LORD,” and the NLT reads “fear to do wrong.” The NKJV’s “always reverent” understands the fear as reverence toward God.

Proverbs 28:15
Proverbs 28:15
NKJV

Proverbs 28:15 Like a roaring lion and a charging bear Is a wicked ruler over poor people.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 28:15 A wicked ruler is as dangerous to the pooras a roaring lion or an attacking bear. (NLT)

Main Idea

A wicked ruler over poor people is as dangerous as a roaring lion or a charging bear. Power in wicked hands turns a ruler into a predator against those least able to defend themselves.

Verse Explanation

A wicked ruler over poor people is as dangerous as a roaring lion or a charging bear. Power in wicked hands turns a ruler into a predator against those least able to defend themselves.

“Like a roaring lion and a charging bear” A roaring lion is terrifying and deadly, and a charging bear is just as dangerous. Both pictures show raw, destructive power aimed at the weak. “Is a wicked ruler over poor people.” A wicked ruler is like these beasts. Instead of protecting the poor, he preys on them. The people least able to defend themselves become his victims.

The saying exposes how power in wicked hands turns a ruler into a predator. Authority is meant to protect, especially the vulnerable, but a wicked ruler uses it to devour. The poor suffer most, because they have no resources to resist or escape.

The verse applies to every kind of authority, not only kings. Anyone with power over others, whether in a government, a business, a church or a family, can become a lion or a bear to those under him, and the saying warns against that abuse.

Study Notes

In 28:1 the righteous are “bold as a lion.” Here the lion returns as a picture of a ruler’s cruelty, showing the same image used for courage and for menace.

Proverbs 28:16
Proverbs 28:16
NKJV

Proverbs 28:16 A ruler who lacks understanding is a great oppressor, But he who hates covetousness will prolong his days.(NKJV)

NLT

Proverbs 28:16 A ruler with no understanding will oppress his people,but one who hates corruption will have a long life. (NLT)

Main Idea

Without understanding, a ruler becomes a great oppressor, but one who hates covetousness will prolong his days. Greed lies at the root of oppressive rule, and a leader who refuses it tends to endure.

Verse Explanation

Without understanding, a ruler becomes a great oppressor, but one who hates covetousness will prolong his days. Greed lies at the root of oppressive rule, and a leader who refuses it tends to endure.

“A ruler who lacks understanding is a great oppressor,” A ruler without understanding, without wisdom and moral insight, becomes a great oppressor. Lacking the discernment to govern justly, he resorts to force and exploitation. Folly in leadership produces cruelty.

“But he who hates covetousness will prolong his days.” Covetousness is greed, the desire for unjust gain. A ruler who hates it will prolong his days. He will rule longer and more securely because he does not exploit his people. Moses told Israel to choose leaders who were “men of truth, hating covetousness” (Exodus 18:21).

The two lines together show that greed lies at the root of oppressive rule. The foolish ruler oppresses because he does not understand; the wise ruler endures because he refuses to be greedy. Long rule is described here as the general fruit of just leadership, not a guarantee that every righteous ruler will reign long.

Study Notes

The NKJV’s “covetousness” is rendered “unjust gain” in the ESV and NASB, pointing to profit taken unjustly.

A man weighed down by the guilt of bloodshed will flee toward the pit of death, and no one should try to shield him from justice. His own guilt drives him toward ruin.

“A man burdened with bloodshed will flee into a pit;” This man is weighed down by the guilt of having shed innocent blood. He is a murderer, and his guilt drives him. He will flee toward the pit, the grave. Whether through fear, the pursuit of justice or his own tormented conscience, he is headed for death. The guilt of 28:1, where the wicked flee when no one pursues, is here at its heaviest.

“Let no one help him.” Others should not try to shield him from justice. They should not hide him, protect him or help him escape the consequences of his crime. From Genesis 9:6 onward, God’s law treated the shedding of innocent blood with utmost seriousness, and the community was not to obstruct justice for a murderer.

The saying is stern. It recognizes that some guilt is so grave that those who help the guilty escape justice become partners in the wrong. It does not forbid compassion for the person or prayer for his repentance, and the mercy of 28:13 remains open to anyone who confesses. It forbids obstructing justice.

Study Notes

The ESV and NASB render the first line as the guilty man being a “fugitive” until death. The NKJV, KJV and YLT speak of his fleeing to the pit.
