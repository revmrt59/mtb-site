Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 29
Main Idea:

Proverbs 29 closes Solomon’s proverbs by showing that response to correction decides a person’s end, that a ruler’s regard for truth and the poor decides whether a people rejoice or groan, and that trust in the LORD frees a person from the snare of the fear of man.

Setting:

The proverbs of Solomon copied by Hezekiah’s men (25:1) end here, and Agur’s words follow. These final sayings keep chapter 28’s direct contrasts, with rulers, the poor, and correction returning often.

Chapter Outline:

  • Proverbs 29:1–7 — The Hardened Neck and Righteous Rule

  • Proverbs 29:8–14 — Wise Men, Truthful Rulers, and the Poor

  • Proverbs 29:15–21 — Correction and Revelation

  • Proverbs 29:22–27 — Pride, the Fear of Man, and Trust in the LORD

Key Verse:

“The fear of man brings a snare, But whoever trusts in the LORD shall be safe.” (Proverbs 29:25)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Proverbs 29 begins with a man “who is often rebuked, and hardens his neck” (29:1). His ruin comes suddenly, after many warnings, and it comes “without remedy” (29:1). Wisdom announced the same end to those who refused her call (1:24–31). Yet each rebuke was mercy, for while correction is still spoken there is time to turn (28:13).

Under righteous authority “the people rejoice,” but under a wicked ruler “the people groan” (29:2). Bribes overthrow a land (29:4), and a ruler who listens to lies soon has only wicked servants (29:12). Behind every throne stands the LORD, who “gives light to the eyes” of the poor man and his oppressor alike (29:13). At home, a child left to himself brings shame (29:15), and a people without revelation “cast off restraint” (29:18). That revelation is a word from God, and the same verse joins it to keeping His law.

The chapter ends by looking past rulers to the LORD. “The fear of man brings a snare” (29:25), and “justice for man comes from the LORD” (29:26), whatever favor a ruler gives. The book began with the fear of the LORD as the beginning of knowledge (1:7); here a right view of God frees a person from the fear of people. Solomon’s proverbs then close with the two ways fully opposed (29:27).

Chapter Sections

Proverbs 29:1–7 — The Hardened Neck and Righteous Rule

The section opens with the man who stiffens his neck against repeated rebuke and is broken without remedy, then sets a people’s gladness under righteous rulers beside their groaning under the wicked, and a father’s joy in a son who loves wisdom beside the waste of the companion of harlots. A just king steadies the land while a bribe-taker tears it down, the flatterer spreads a net and the evil man is caught in his own transgression, and the righteous show what they are by knowing the cause of the poor.

Proverbs 29:8–14 — Wise Men, Truthful Rulers, and the Poor

Scoffers inflame a whole city while wise men calm its anger, a dispute with a fool finds no peace whether he rages or laughs, and the fool vents every feeling that the wise man holds back. A ruler who welcomes lies corrupts all his servants, but the LORD gives light to the eyes of the poor man and his oppressor alike, and the king who judges the poor with truth will have an established throne.

Proverbs 29:15–21 — Correction and Revelation

Loving correction, never harshness or abuse, gives a child wisdom and gives his parents rest, while a child left to himself brings shame, and the multiplying of the wicked will end in their fall. At the center, a people without God’s prophetic word throw off restraint while the one who keeps His law is happy, and around that saying stand a servant who understands but will not respond, a hasty talker with less hope than a fool, and a pampered servant who no longer knows his place.

Proverbs 29:22–27 — Pride, the Fear of Man, and Trust in the LORD

Anger stirs up strife and multiplies transgression, pride brings a man low while the humble keep their honor, and the thief’s partner, bound to silence under oath, hates his own life. The fear of man is a snare, but the one who trusts in the LORD is safe, because justice comes from the LORD and not from the ruler whose favor many seek, and the collection ends with the unjust man and the upright an abomination to each other.

Why This Chapter Matters

Proverbs 29 shows that refusing correction is among the most dangerous habits a person can form. The man who hardens his neck (29:1), the servant who understands yet will not respond (29:19), and the man hasty in his words (29:20) stand in more danger than they know, while correction received brings wisdom and rest (29:15, 17).

The chapter also carries God’s concern for the poor into every seat of authority, from a throne to a home. The king who judges the poor with truth has a throne “established forever” (29:14), a saying fulfilled completely only in Christ, of whom Isaiah wrote, “with righteousness He shall judge the poor” (Isaiah 11:4). And 29:18 shows why God’s people need His voice: without His word they throw off restraint, and happiness belongs to the one who keeps what He has already said.

Key Themes to Watch For

Correction received or refused. Repeated rebuke either softens a person or hardens him; loving correction gives wisdom and rest, while the stiff neck and the unresponsive servant head toward ruin (29:1, 15, 17, 19).

Rulers and the people they govern. Justice, bribes, lies, and truthful judgment at the top reach everyone below, and the LORD remains the final source of justice (29:2, 4, 12, 14, 26).

The poor before God. The righteous know the cause of the poor, the LORD gives light to the eyes of poor and oppressor alike, and a king’s throne rests on truthful judgment of the poor (29:7, 13–14).

Restraint in temper and speech. The wise man holds back what the fool vents, the hasty talker has less hope than a fool, and the angry man stirs up strife; the warning is against venting without restraint, not against honest feeling (29:11, 20, 22).

Pride, fear, and trust. Pride brings a man low and the fear of man sets a snare, while humility keeps honor and trust in the LORD brings safety (29:23, 25).

Key Words & Concepts

Hardens his neck (29:1). The picture of a stubborn refusal to bend under correction, the same image the LORD used of Israel at the golden calf: “it is a stiff-necked people!” (Exodus 32:9). The danger lies in the repetition, rebuke after rebuke refused.

Scoffers (29:8). The scorner of earlier chapters, who will not be taught because his problem lies in his will. Here his contempt reaches past himself and sets a whole city aflame.

The rod and rebuke (29:15). Correction by action and by word. Proverbs has in view loving, consistent discipline that reflects the Father’s correction (3:11–12). These sayings never justify harshness, rage, or abuse, and they are a pattern of wisdom rather than a verdict on parents whose children stray.

Revelation (29:18). The NKJV reads “revelation,” where the KJV has “vision.” It means a prophetic word from God, as in Samuel’s boyhood, when “the word of the LORD was rare in those days; there was no widespread revelation” (1 Samuel 3:1). The verse is about hearing and obeying God, not about planning or goal-setting.

The fear of man (29:25). Fear of what others think or can do, which traps a person into compromise. Its answer is trust in the LORD, and “safe” describes security in God, not a promise that harm will never come.

Abomination (29:27). Something detestable. Elsewhere Proverbs names what is an abomination to the LORD; here the righteous and the wicked detest each other’s ways, though the righteous are still called to do good to an enemy (25:21–22).
