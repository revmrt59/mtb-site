Proverbs 30 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Proverbs 30 records the words of Agur, who confesses the limits of human wisdom, asks the name of God and His Son, rests on the purity of God’s word, and prays for neither poverty nor riches, followed by numerical sayings drawn from human life and creation.

Chapter Sections

1. Proverbs 30:1–4 — Agur’s Confession and His Question

Agur confesses that he lacks understanding and the knowledge of the Holy One, and asks who has ascended into heaven and gathered the wind, and what is His name and His Son’s name.

2. Proverbs 30:5–6 — Every Word of God Is Pure

Every word of God is pure, and He is a shield to those who trust Him; no one is to add to His words.

3. Proverbs 30:7–9 — Neither Poverty nor Riches

Agur asks God to remove falsehood from him and give him neither poverty nor riches, lest he be full and deny the LORD or be poor and steal.

4. Proverbs 30:10–14 — A Generation That Curses and Devours

A servant is not to be maligned. Agur describes a generation that curses its parents, is pure in its own eyes, looks down in pride, and devours the poor.

5. Proverbs 30:15–17 — Things Never Satisfied

The leech’s daughters cry give, and the grave, the barren womb, the dry earth, and fire are never satisfied. The eye that mocks a father will be food for the ravens.

6. Proverbs 30:18–20 — Four Ways Too Wonderful and the Adulterous Woman

The way of an eagle, a serpent, a ship, and a man with a virgin are too wonderful to understand. Beside them stands the way of the adulterous woman, who says she has done no wrong.

7. Proverbs 30:21–23 — Four Things the Earth Cannot Bear

The earth cannot bear a servant who reigns, a fool filled with food, a hateful woman married, or a maidservant who replaces her mistress.

8. Proverbs 30:24–28 — Four Small but Wise Creatures

The ants, rock badgers, locusts, and spider are small but exceedingly wise in preparing, sheltering, advancing together, and reaching kings’ palaces.

9. Proverbs 30:29–31 — Four Things Stately in Walk

The lion, the greyhound, the male goat, and a king with his troops are stately in their bearing.

10. Proverbs 30:32–33 — Put Your Hand on Your Mouth

Whoever has exalted himself foolishly or devised evil should put his hand on his mouth, for the forcing of wrath produces strife.
