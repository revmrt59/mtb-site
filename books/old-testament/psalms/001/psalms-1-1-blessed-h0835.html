BLESSED - ( 'esher , Strong's H0835 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'esher represents a state of deep, flourishing well-being, contentment, and divine favor. Often translated as "blessed" or "happy," it does not refer to a fleeting emotional state driven by external circumstances, but rather to the stable, secure condition of a life aligned with God's covenant and wisdom. It is a plural noun of exclamation, literally meaning "O the happinesses of" the person who walks in uprightness.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The word 'esher is derived from the root 'ashar, which carries the primary meaning of "to go straight," "to walk," or "to advance."

• This root paints a vivid geographical and moral word picture. To be "blessed" is to be on the right path, moving forward in the right direction, and staying on a level, secure highway.

• It evokes the image of a traveler who avoids the dangerous, winding, and slippery detours of wickedness, remaining instead on the straight way of God's wisdom. Consequently, blessedness is not a static state of passive luck, but an active, progressive journey of walking in alignment with God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The noun 'esher means happiness, blessedness, or bliss.

• It is used almost exclusively in the masculine plural construct form ('ashrey) as an interjection, which functions as a holy exclamation: "O the happinesses of!" or "How blessed is!"

• It is vital to distinguish 'esher from the Hebrew verb barak (which also translates as "to bless"). While barak represents God actively bestowing favor, life, or power upon a person or object, 'esher describes the objective state of well-being experienced by the person who lives in covenant alignment. Barak is what God does; 'esher is the resulting condition of the person who receives and walks in that divine favor.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Plurality of Joy: Because the word occurs in the plural construct ('ashrey), it denotes a multi-dimensional, overflowing abundance of happiness. It points to a fullness of joys—spiritual, mental, relational, and eternal—rather than a single, isolated blessing.

• Holy Congratulation: The term functions as a congratulatory exclamation. It points to a person and declares, "How highly favored, secure, and envious is the condition of that individual!"

• Stability and Vitality: In Wisdom Literature, the blessed person is depicted as stable and thriving. Rather than being blown about like chaff, the one who possesses 'esher is anchored, productive, and spiritually green.

4. Theological Implications

• Covenantal and Relational: True blessedness is intrinsically tied to a right relationship with God. It cannot be achieved through self-effort, wealth, or worldly status. It is the natural fruit of fearing the Lord, trusting in Him, and obeying His commands.

• Objective Status over Subjective Emotion: Unlike modern concepts of "happiness," which depend on favorable circumstances (happening), 'esher is an objective spiritual status. A person can experience 'esher even in the midst of trials, persecution, or difficulty, because their standing with God remains secure and unchanging.

• The Way of Life: Wisdom theology presents two paths: the path of life and the path of death. The word 'esher is the hallmark of the path of life. It serves as a reminder that God’s instructions are not burdensome rules, but the boundary lines of true human flourishing.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 1:1, 'ashrey serves as the gateway to the entire Book of Psalms, establishing the theme for the worship and life of God's people.

• The psalm opens with this exclamation to capture the reader's attention and point them toward the ideal human life.

• The context presents a stark contrast between the blessed man and the ungodly. The blessed man does not walk, stand, or sit in the sphere of wickedness; instead, he delights in and meditates on the law of the Lord.

• The nuance in Psalm 1:1 is that blessedness is both protective and preventative. By actively delighting in God's word, the believer is preserved from the gradual downward slide into sin. This alignment produces a life like a tree planted by rivers of water, which yields fruit in its season and does not wither.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 1:1 - "Blessed is the man Who walks not in the counsel of the ungodly, Nor stands in the path of sinners, Nor sits in the seat of the scornful;"

• Psalm 32:1 - "Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven, Whose sin is covered."

• Psalm 84:4 - "Blessed are those who dwell in Your house; They will still be praising You."

• Psalm 119:1 - "Blessed are the undefiled in the way, Who walk in the law of the LORD!"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• Connection to the New Testament: In the Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint/LXX), 'esher is translated using the Greek word makarios. This is the exact word Jesus uses in the Beatitudes in Matthew 5:3-11. When Jesus says, "Blessed are the poor in spirit," He is directly tapping into the rich Old Testament heritage of 'esher, redefining true flourishing not by worldly success but by kingdom alignment.

• Grace-Based Flourishing: Although the blessed man is described as doing certain actions (meditating, obeying), this blessedness is ultimately a work of grace. The ability to delight in God’s law and the forgiveness of sins (as in Psalm 32:1) are gifts from God, making 'esher a state of grace-filled joy.
