COUNSEL - ( 'etsah, Strong's H6098 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun 'etsah refers to counsel, advice, purpose, or design, representing the mental direction, worldview, and foundational principles that guide a person's life choices. In Psalm 1:1, it warns against adopting the thinking, philosophy, and lifestyle of those who live independently of God. The path to blessedness begins with guarding the mind, recognizing that the counsel we heed ultimately shapes the character we develop and the destiny we experience.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun 'etsah is derived from the verbal root ya'ats, which means to advise, consult, deliberate, or resolve. In ancient Near Eastern culture, counsel was a highly valued commodity, typically associated with the wisdom of elders, kings, and trusted advisors. It was not merely casual advice but structured, strategic planning designed to guide a nation, an army, or an individual through complex situations. To receive counsel was to submit one's path to the wisdom and direction of another, allowing their perspective to shape one's decisions and ultimate destiny.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word 'etsah denotes advice, guidance, plans, purposes, or a system of thought. It encompasses both the act of giving advice and the content of the advice itself. The semantic range includes:

• Advice or guidance offered by one person to another.

• Deliberate plan, strategy, or policy, especially in political or military contexts.

• The eternal, sovereign, and immutable purpose of God.

• A worldview or way of thinking that governs behavior and moral choices.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

In Scripture, 'etsah is used to contrast the fragile, shifting, and often foolish advice of humans with the unshakeable, perfect, and life-giving counsel of God. Human counsel is repeatedly depicted as futile when it opposes the Lord, while God's counsel stands forever.

• The Trap of Human Wisdom: When humans rely on their own 'etsah apart from God, it leads to confusion and ruin. The counsel of the wicked is deceptive, lacking the fear of the Lord which is the beginning of true wisdom.

• The Stability of Divine Counsel: God's 'etsah is an anchor. It is characterized by absolute truth, immutability, and redemptive purpose.

• The Progressive Walk: In Psalm 1:1, "counsel" is the starting point of a spiritual decline. The progression moves from "walking" in ungodly counsel (thinking/mindset), to "standing" in the path of sinners (behavior/association), to "sitting" in the seat of the scornful (belonging/cynicism). Counsel is the seed from which lifestyle and destiny grow.

4. Theological Implications

• The Priority of the Mind: The battle for the Christian life begins in the mind. How we think determines how we live. By warning against the "counsel of the ungodly," Scripture teaches that the first step away from God is intellectual and philosophical, adopting a godless framework for interpreting reality.

• The Sovereignty of God: Human plans and counsels are subject to God's ultimate decree. No human strategy can succeed against the Lord, and His sovereign counsel always prevails in human history.

• The Source of True Guidance: True 'etsah belongs to Yahweh. He is the ultimate source of counsel, and His words provide the only reliable guide for navigating the complexities of life.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 1:1, 'etsah is paired with the "ungodly" (resha'im, those who live as if God does not exist). The "counsel of the ungodly" refers to the subtle advice, values, priorities, and cultural wisdom of a world system that leaves God out of the equation.

• Walking in this counsel means adopting this godless worldview as one's operational guide for daily living.

• This stands in sharp contrast to verse 2, where the godly person delights in the "law of the LORD" (torah). The psalm presents a binary choice: a life directed by the shifting, unstable 'etsah of the ungodly, or a life rooted in the eternal, life-giving torah of Yahweh.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 33:11: "The counsel of the LORD stands forever, The plans of His heart to all generations."

• Psalm 73:24: "You will guide me with Your counsel, And afterward receive me to glory."

• Proverbs 19:21: "There are many plans in a man's heart, Nevertheless the LORD's counsel—that will stand."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Septuagint (LXX), the Greek translation of the Old Testament, 'etsah in Psalm 1:1 is translated using the noun boule. In the New Testament, boule refers to counsel, purpose, or deliberate decision. It is frequently used to describe God's predetermined redemptive plan (such as in Acts 2:23 and Ephesians 1:11). This connection underscores that the ultimate alternative to the fleeting counsels of this world is the eternal, sovereign plan of God revealed in Jesus Christ.
