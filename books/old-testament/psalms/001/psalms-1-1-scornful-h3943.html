SCORNFUL - ( lēṣ , Strong's H3943 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word lēṣ refers to a scoffer, mocker, or one who treats sacred things with contempt. In wisdom literature, the scornful person represents the peak of active rebellion against God. Unlike the simpleton who is easily led astray, the scorner has made a deliberate choice to reject wisdom, mock truth, and actively recruit others into cynical rebellion.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun lēṣ is derived from the verb lûts, which carries the core meaning of mouth-making, deriding, boasting, or interpreting with a slant. In ancient Near Eastern thought, speech was considered an extension of character. A scorner is not merely quiet in their skepticism; they are vocal. The word picture is of someone who curls their lip, makes a mouth, mocks, and uses biting humor or arrogant rhetoric to tear down what is holy and elevate themselves. It implies a settled posture of cynical superiority that looks down upon the godly and God Himself.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun lēṣ refers to a scoffer, mocker, or arrogant cynic. It describes an individual who is proud, haughty, and deeply resistant to correction. The lexical range of the word includes:

• One who speaks mockingly or derisively of truth and godliness.

• An arrogant person who rejects spiritual and moral authority.

• A cynical troublemaker who actively disrupts peace and promotes division.

In contrast to the "simple" (petî), who is gullible but teachable, the lēṣ is closed-minded, unteachable, and actively hostile to wisdom.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament uses the word lēṣ primarily in Wisdom Literature, particularly in Psalms and Proverbs, to depict a person who has progressed beyond simple folly into active hostility against God.

• The Progressive Descent of Sin: In Psalm 1:1, there is a progressive downward movement: walking in the counsel of the ungodly, standing in the path of sinners, and finally sitting in the seat of the scornful. The "seat" (môšāb) represents a settled, permanent, and authoritative position. To "sit in the seat" of the scornful is to occupy the chair of a teacher or judge of cynicism, exerting a negative influence on others.

• The Mouth of the Arrogant: The scorner uses verbal violence. Their primary weapon is their mouth, which they use to belittle the righteous and mock the fear of the Lord.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, the scornful represents the ultimate stage of hardened unbelief prior to judgment. It is not just a lack of faith, but an aggressive, cynical counter-faith.

• Hardness of Heart: The scorner is morally and spiritually rigid. Reproof only brings hatred out of them, and they are incapable of finding wisdom because their pride blocks their receptivity.

• Divine Reciprocity: God resists the proud. He mocks the mockers, showing that the ultimate end of mockery is divine judgment, where the scorner is given over to their own derision.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 1:1, the "scornful" represents the climax of evil influence. The progression goes from bad to worse:

• Ungodly (rāšā‘): Loose, unanchored, living without God.

• Sinner (ḥaṭṭā’): Actively missing the mark, habitual wrongdoers.

• Scornful (lēṣ): Cynical, mocking, and teaching others to do the same.

The context shows that the blessed man avoids not just the actions of sinners, but the intellectual and philosophical cynicism of the scorner. The "seat" is the place of teaching. The scorner is the anti-teacher, offering a counter-wisdom to the Torah (which is praised in verse 2).

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Proverbs 9:7 - "He who corrects a scoffer gets shame for himself, And he who rebukes a wicked man gets himself blows."

• Proverbs 13:1 - "A wise son heeds his father’s instruction, But a scoffer does not listen to rebuke."

• Proverbs 14:6 - "A scoffer seeks wisdom and does not find it, But knowledge is easy to him who understands."

• Proverbs 22:10 - "Cast out the scoffer, and contention will leave; Yes, strife and reproach will cease."

• Proverbs 24:9 - "The devising of foolishness is sin, And the scoffer is an abomination to men."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Septuagint translates lēṣîm in Psalm 1:1 as loimōn, which literally means "pestilential, plague-like, or infectious." This is a powerful insight: the Greek translators viewed the scorner not just as an individual sinner, but as a contagious spiritual plague that infects the community. The cynical attitude of the scorner spreads easily, destroying faith and community health. In contrast, Christ-like humility is the ultimate antidote to the spirit of the scorner. While the scorner sits in judgment over others, Jesus sat with tax collectors and sinners to heal them, never mocking, but speaking truth in love.
