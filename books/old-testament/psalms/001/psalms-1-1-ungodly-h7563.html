UNGODLY - ( rasha , Strong's H7563 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word rasha refers to those who are actively wicked, living in rebellion against God's law and hostile to His covenant. Rather than representing a passive state of neutrality, being ungodly is an active posture of self-will that disregards God's authority. In biblical wisdom literature, and specifically in Psalm 1:1, the "ungodly" represent those whose thoughts, advice, and lifestyle are detached from the fear of the Lord. Choosing to walk in their counsel marks the subtle, initial step of a believer's downward slide away from a blessed and fruitful life.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The verbal root of rasha relates to being loose, disjointed, or out of proper boundary. It stands in direct contrast to tsaddiq (righteous), which refers to being straight, aligned, or conforming to an established standard. The word picture is one of restlessness, instability, and ethical disarray. Historically and legally, rasha carries a forensic or courtroom background, describing someone who has been tried and found guilty of violating the law. They are chaotic and disjointed because they have fractured their relationship with the Creator, who is the source of all order and peace.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term denotes a person who is guilty of sin, wicked, or acting in a hostile manner toward God and human society. The semantic range spans from legal guilt (being "in the wrong" in a civil dispute) to ethical wickedness, and ultimately to spiritual rebellion against Yahweh. It is the defining characteristic of those who are condemned under divine judgment due to their persistent violation of the covenant. A rasha is not merely someone who makes a mistake, but someone whose life is patterned by a deliberate disregard for God's standards.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Scripture frequently employs vivid word pictures to describe the rasha and their ultimate end:

• The Troubled Sea: They are like the tossing sea that cannot rest, whose waters continually cast up mire and dirt. This illustrates the perpetual inner turmoil and lack of peace that characterizes a life lived apart from God.

• Chaff in the Wind: They are compared to chaff, the weightless husks separated from the wheat, which the wind easily drives away. This emphasizes their lack of spiritual substance, stability, and permanent value.

• Fading Grass: They may temporarily flourish like green grass, but they are destined to be cut down and wither quickly.

• Deep Darkness: Their way is like deep darkness; they stumble constantly and do not even realize what causes their fall.

### 4. Theological Implications

The use of rasha underscores several major theological realities:

• The Reality of Two Paths: Scripture recognizes no moral neutrality. Humanity is divided into two groups: the righteous (tsaddiq) and the ungodly (rasha). One's standing before God dictates one's character, lifestyle, and ultimate destiny.

• Sin as Relational Rebellion: Wickedness is not just breaking abstract rules; it is a declaration of independence from the Sovereign Creator. To be ungodly is to live as though God does not exist, does not see, or does not care.

• The Certainty of Divine Justice: Because God is holy and straight, He cannot tolerate that which is disjointed and wicked. The rasha stands condemned because their life is lived in direct opposition to His holy character, ensuring that they will not stand in the final judgment.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 1:1, "ungodly" (rasha) is placed at the very beginning of a progressive spiritual decline: walking in the counsel of the ungodly, standing in the path of sinners, and finally sitting in the seat of the scornful.

• The "counsel of the ungodly" refers to the worldview, advice, philosophies, and values of those who live independently of God.

• This represents an intellectual and foundational compromise. The downward slide does not begin with an overt, scandalous act of wickedness, but with quietly listening to and adopting the mindsets of those who disregard God.

• The nuance here is subtle and pastoral: the godly person must carefully guard their mind, recognizing that the world's "wise advice" is actually ungodly counsel if it leaves the Creator out of the equation.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 1:4 - "The ungodly are not so, / But are like the chaff which the wind drives away."

• Proverbs 10:25 - "When the whirlwind passes by, the wicked is no more, / But the righteous has an everlasting foundation."

• Isaiah 57:20 - "But the wicked are like the troubled sea, / When it cannot rest, / Whose waters cast up mire and dirt."

• Ezekiel 33:11 - "Say to them: ‘As I live,’ says the Lord God, ‘I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked, but that the wicked turn from his way and live. Turn, turn from your evil ways! For why should you die, O house of Israel?’"

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• In the poetical structure of the Psalms, rasha is the primary antonym used to define what the righteous person is not. By looking at the dark backdrop of the ungodly, the beauty of a life aligned with God's law (torah) shines more brightly.

• The New Testament equivalent to rasha is often asebes (godless, ungodly) or poneros (wicked).

• Pastoral Warning: The counsel of the ungodly is often highly sophisticated, logical, and appealing to human pragmatism. It rarely presents itself as raw evil; instead, it offers "good advice" that simply lacks divine truth, making vigilant discernment essential for the believer.
