DELIGHT - ( chephets , Strong's H2656 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun chephets denotes a deep-seated pleasure, desire, or delight that fully engages a person's will and brings emotional satisfaction. In Psalm 1:2, it describes the defining characteristic of the righteous person: a joyful, voluntary inclination toward the instruction of Yahweh. Rather than viewing God's law as a heavy, restrictive burden, the righteous individual cherishes it as a supreme treasure, which naturally drives continuous, life-giving meditation.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The noun chephets is derived from the root verb chaphets, which carries the primary meaning of bending toward, leaning, or being favorably inclined to something.

• In ancient concrete contexts, the verb could describe a physical bending or leaning, indicating a natural direction of movement.

• Figuratively, the word picture represents a heart that voluntarily and gladly bends toward an object of affection. It represents the polar opposite of stiff-necked rebellion or forced, begrudging compliance. Just as a plant bends toward the sun to find life, the heart of the believer bends toward the Word of God to find joy and sustenance.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The semantic range of chephets includes pleasure, desire, delight, favor, purpose, and that which is precious or valuable.

• It describes an internal state of being highly pleased with something, where the affections of the heart and the choices of the will are fully aligned.

• It can refer to a concrete object of desire, such as valuable goods, precious jewels, or a personal matter of high interest.

• In spiritual contexts, it signifies a deep, personal devotion that transcends mere intellectual agreement, indicating a passionate and satisfying commitment of the soul.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The term is used throughout the Old Testament to describe intense human desire, such as a man's romantic desire for a woman, highlighting the emotional and affectionate depth of the word.

• It is frequently used to describe precious, costly stones or highly desirable worldly possessions. This comparison illustrates that the Word of God is to be viewed as a priceless treasure, far exceeding the value of any earthly wealth.

• The word also denotes God's sovereign pleasure or good will. When used of God, it indicates His absolute, unforced joy in accomplishing His redemptive purposes.

• By using chephets to describe the believer’s relationship with the law, Scripture depicts a heart that finds its ultimate satisfaction, wealth, and pleasure in the revelation of God.

4. Theological Implications

• True godliness is fundamentally a matter of the desires. External conformity to God's commands without inward delight is legalism. The righteous person is not merely characterized by what they avoid (Psalm 1:1), but by what they actively love and pursue (Psalm 1:2).

• The law (torah) is not presented as an oppressive checklist of rules, but as the loving, grace-filled instruction of a Covenant Father. Delight in the law is ultimately delight in the Lawgiver Himself.

• This internal delight is a marker of regenerating grace. While the fallen human heart is hostile to the law of God, the renewed heart finds supreme joy in it, anticipating the New Covenant promise where God's law is written directly on the heart.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 1:2, chephets stands in stark, immediate contrast to the lifestyle of the ungodly described in verse 1. The wicked find their identity, counsel, and belonging in the shifting standards of the world. In contrast, the righteous person finds their anchor and joy in the eternal instruction of the Lord.

• This inward delight serves as the indispensable engine for spiritual discipline. The text links delight with action: "And in His law he meditates day and night." Meditation is not a dry duty; it is the natural, involuntary reflex of delight. A person naturally and continuously ponders what they love most.

• Because the righteous man's delight is anchored in the unchanging Word of Yahweh, his soul remains green, fruitful, and unshakeable, regardless of external circumstances or seasons of drought.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 16:3: "As for the saints who are on the earth, 'They are the excellent ones, in whom is all my delight.'"

• Psalm 111:2: "The works of the LORD are great, Studied by all who have pleasure in them."

• Proverbs 3:15: "She is more precious than rubies, And all the things you may desire cannot be compared with her."

• Isaiah 53:10: "Yet it pleased the LORD to bruise Him; He has put Him to grief. When You make His soul an offering for sin, He shall see His seed, He shall prolong His days, And the pleasure of the LORD shall prosper in His hand."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• In the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint), chephets in Psalm 1:2 is translated using the noun thelema, which means "will," "desire," or "pleasure." This Greek choice emphasizes that the delight of the righteous is not a fleeting, subjective emotion, but a firm, deliberate setting of the human will to agree with and pursue the will of God.

• Ultimately, Jesus Christ is the perfect embodiment of this verse. He lived a life of absolute, unbroken delight in the Father's will and word, demonstrating that true human flourishing is found only in complete submission to God's instruction.
