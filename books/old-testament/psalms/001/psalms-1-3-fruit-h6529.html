FRUIT - ( peri , Strong's H6529 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word peri refers literally to the agricultural harvest or organic produce of a tree or plant, and metaphorically to the natural consequence, reward, or outcome of a person's character and actions. In Psalm 1:3, it depicts the life of the righteous person who, deeply rooted in the instruction of Yahweh, naturally and consistently produces righteous deeds, godly character, and spiritual vitality at the God-appointed time. It represents a life of active, visible, and beneficial output that stems directly from an intimate connection to the source of life.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient agrarian Israel, fruit was not a luxury; it was a matter of life, health, and sustenance. The growth of fruit-bearing trees required cultivated soil, a reliable water source, and defense against drought. A tree yielding fruit was a sign of divine blessing, fertility, and life-giving energy. In contrast, a barren tree or one yielding bad fruit represented judgment, curse, and spiritual decay. The word peri carries this rich agricultural imagery into the moral realm: just as a healthy tree naturally and inevitably produces sweet, nourishing fruit, so a healthy human heart, nourished by God's Word, naturally produces righteous behavior and spiritual maturity.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Peri denotes the physical product of a plant or tree, such as grapes, figs, pomegranates, or olives.

• It signifies the offspring or fruit of the womb (children).

• It expresses the consequence, result, or outcome of human actions, whether good or bad (the "fruit of their doings").

• It refers to the reward, benefit, or profit derived from wisdom, godly labor, or faithful living.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Tree of Life: Wisdom is compared to a tree of life, and its outcome is characterized as the "fruit of the righteous" (Proverbs 11:30), which brings healing, blessing, and vitality to others.

• The Consequence of Choices: Scripture depicts choices as seeds that inevitably grow into a harvest. The wicked are forced to eat the "fruit of their own way" (Proverbs 1:31), meaning they must endure the painful, destructive consequences of their rebellion.

• The Harvest of Lips: The words a person speaks are described as fruit (Proverbs 18:21), demonstrating that speech is the outward expression of the heart's inner condition.

4. Theological Implications

• Organic Growth vs. Mechanical Effort: Fruit-bearing is an organic process, not a mechanical assembly. It cannot be forced or faked; it requires time, deep roots, and nourishment. This illustrates that true holiness and righteousness in the believer's life are the natural outworking of an inner spiritual reality, rather than a legalistic performance.

• Dependence on God: A tree cannot produce fruit on its own; it is entirely dependent on the soil, the water, and the sun. Similarly, the believer's capacity to produce good fruit depends entirely on being rooted in God's presence and His Word.

• Divine Timing: Fruit is not produced instantly or constantly; it has a "season." God develops character and produces maturity according to His sovereign timeline.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 1:3, the righteous man is likened to a tree "planted by the rivers of water." The Hebrew word for "rivers" (palgey-mayim) refers to irrigation canals dug by a gardener to ensure a constant, deliberate supply of water. The fruit produced by this tree is specified as being "in its season." This indicates that the righteous person's life is marked by timely, appropriate, and beautiful spiritual output. The fruit does not appear overnight, nor is it frantically forced. Because the righteous person "meditates day and night" on the Law of the Lord (Psalm 1:2), their soul is constantly drinking from the living water of divine truth. This inner nourishment guarantees that when trials, responsibilities, or seasons of service arrive, they possess the spiritual reserves to act with wisdom, love, patience, and faithfulness.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 1:11: "Then God said, 'Let the earth bring forth grass, the herb that yields seed, and the fruit tree that yields fruit according to its kind, whose seed is in itself, on the earth'; and it was so."

• Proverbs 1:31: "Therefore they shall eat the fruit of their own way, and be filled with their own fancies."

• Proverbs 11:30: "The fruit of the righteous is a tree of life, and he who wins souls is wise."

• Isaiah 3:10: "Say to the righteous that it shall be well with them, for they shall eat the fruit of their doings."

• Jeremiah 17:8: "For he shall be like a tree planted by the waters, which spreads out its roots by the river, and will not fear when heat comes; but its leaf will be green, and will not be anxious in the year of drought, nor will cease from yielding fruit."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Septuagint (LXX) translates peri in Psalm 1:3 using the Greek word karpos. This provides a direct thematic and theological bridge to the New Testament, where Jesus teaches that a tree is known by its karpos (Matthew 7:16-20) and that abiding in Him is the only way to bear "much fruit" (John 15:1-8). Similarly, Paul contrasts the "works of the flesh" with the "fruit of the Spirit" (Galatians 5:22-23), echoing the organic, God-dependent nature of true spiritual growth first illustrated in Psalm 1.
