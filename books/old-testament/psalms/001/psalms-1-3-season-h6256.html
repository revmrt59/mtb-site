SEASON - ( et , Strong's H6256 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word et refers to an appointed time, proper season, or opportune moment. In Psalm 1:3, it describes the timely fruitfulness of the righteous believer who meditates on God's Word. Rather than producing random, erratic, or forced results, the person rooted in Scripture yields spiritual fruit at the exact moment God intends, showcasing a life beautifully aligned with divine timing and sustained by the living water of truth.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concept of et is deeply rooted in the agricultural rhythms of the ancient Near East. Unlike modern Western views of time, which are often linear, mechanical, and measured by ticking clocks, Hebrew thought conceptualized time concretely through activities, events, and natural cycles. A "season" is not merely an abstract block of days but the specific, proper moment for an event to occur—such as the falling of rain, the planting of seed, or the harvesting of crops. It conveys a strong sense of divine order, purpose, and the sovereignty of God over the rhythms of human life and creation.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun et denotes a time, season, epoch, or a designated period. It carries several layers of meaning depending on context:

• An appointed or designated time for an event.

• An opportune, proper, or fit time (often rendered "due season" or "due time").

• A continuous period of time or an era characterized by specific circumstances (e.g., a "time of trouble").

• The semantic range encompasses both specific chronological points and broader qualitative seasons of life.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, et is used to paint vivid pictures of life under God’s design:

• The Agricultural Cycle: It represents the anticipated periods of agricultural success, such as the rain arriving "in its season" to ensure the survival of the nation.

• The Rhythms of Life: It is the central term in the famous catalog of human experiences in Ecclesiastes 3:1–8, demonstrating that every human activity—weeping, laughing, planting, harvesting—has its own designated et under heaven.

• Divine Opportunties: It describes words spoken at the perfect, most helpful moment, compared to apples of gold in settings of silver.

In Psalm 1:3, the word picture is that of a healthy, well-watered tree that does not struggle to survive. Because its roots reach deep irrigation canals, it naturally and predictably yields its crop when the designated harvest season arrives.

4. Theological Implications

• Divine Sovereignty over Growth: Spiritual fruitfulness is not something believers can force, manufacture, or rush through human striving. Fruit matures in God's perfect timing.

• Patience in Varied Seasons: The life of the believer is not a constant, exhausting harvest. There are seasons of planting, seasons of pruning, seasons of waiting, and seasons of visible fruitfulness. Et reassures the believer that periods of quiet growth or hidden preparation are purposeful and valuable.

• The Sufficiency of God’s Word: Constant meditation on Scripture (Psalm 1:2) ensures that when a season of trial, grief, or leadership arrives, the believer has the spiritual resources necessary to respond with mature character, wisdom, and faith.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 1:3, the phrase "brings forth its fruit in its season" (be-itto) highlights the appropriateness and beauty of the believer's life output. This fruit is not premature (which would be sour, bitter, or underdeveloped) nor is it late (which would result in rot or loss).

• The righteous person is contrasted with the wicked, who are like chaff blown away by the wind—rootless, dry, and devoid of any fruitful season.

• The tree's source is external (planted by rivers of water), meaning its ability to produce fruit in its et is entirely dependent on its connection to the water supply. Similarly, the believer's ability to produce spiritual fruit at the right moment depends on their constant, prayerful meditation on the law of the Lord.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Leviticus 26:4 - "then I will give you rain in its season, the land shall yield its produce, and the trees of the field shall yield their fruit."

• Deuteronomy 28:12 - "The LORD will open to you His good treasure, the heavens, to give the rain to your land in its season, and to bless all the work of your hand."

• Psalm 104:27 - "These all wait for You, That You may give them their food in due season."

• Proverbs 15:23 - "A man has joy by the answer of his mouth, And a word spoken in due season, how good it is!"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint) translates et in Psalm 1:3 using the Greek word kairos. In the New Testament, kairos refers to an opportune, decisive, or spiritually significant time, rather than chronos (simple chronological time). This choice of words underscores that the fruit produced by the righteous is spiritually strategic and uniquely suited for the specific moment of need.
