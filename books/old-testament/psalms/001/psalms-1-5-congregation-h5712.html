CONGREGATION - ( 'edah , Strong's H5712 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'edah denotes an appointed assembly, congregation, or community gathered together by designation or covenant. Derived from a root meaning to meet or gather at an appointed time or place, it refers to a structured, recognized group rather than a casual crowd. In Psalm 1:5, it represents the covenant community of the righteous—those who love God’s law and are vindicated by Him—from which the ungodly are excluded at the final judgment.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The noun 'edah is derived from the verbal root ya'ad, which means to appoint, meet, or gather by agreement. Because of this connection, a congregation is not a random, disorganized mob, but a collection of people who have been summoned to a specific place for an agreed-upon purpose. In the cultural context of the ancient Near East, the 'edah represents an official body of citizens or covenant members gathered to make legal decisions, worship, or hear official proclamations.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word 'edah refers to a formal assembly, community, or congregation. It designates a group bound together by a common purpose, covenant relationship, or shared identity. While it can occasionally refer to a family or a specific group of animals, its primary theological usage refers to the collective community of God's covenant people. It represents a structured, organic unity rather than a temporary gathering.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Covenant Community: The term appears over 140 times in the Old Testament, primarily in the books of Exodus, Leviticus, and Numbers. It most frequently identifies the "congregation of Israel" or the "congregation of the LORD." This usage paints a picture of a nation structured around the tabernacle, gathered in obedience to the divine summons.

• Natural Gatherings: On rare occasions, the word describes physical swarms or groups, such as a "swarm of bees" in Judges 14:8, highlighting a dense, unified, and organized group moving with a single impulse.

• Assemblies of the Wicked: The term can also represent a gathering of those aligned against God, such as the rebel group led by Korah in Numbers 16:16, emphasizing that the ungodly also gather and organize in opposition to God's order.

### 4. Theological Implications

• Covenant Identity: Membership in the 'edah is not merely ethnic but spiritual and covenantal. It is the community that is called out, set apart, and holy to the Lord.

• Divine Ordering: Because the root word implies an appointment, the congregation is established by God Himself. He determines its boundaries, its laws, and its membership.

• Separation and Holiness: God's congregation must remain distinct from the surrounding nations. To preserve the holiness of the 'edah, uncleanness and unrepentant sin must be removed from its midst.

• Eschatological Separation: There is a future, final division of humanity. Though the righteous and the ungodly are currently mixed in history, the ultimate destiny of God's people is to dwell as a purified, exclusive assembly in His presence.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 1:5, the psalmist contrasts the destiny of the blessed man with that of the ungodly. The verse declares: "Therefore the ungodly shall not stand in the judgment, Nor sinners in the congregation of the righteous."

• The Congregation of the Righteous: Here, 'edah refers to the company of those who walk according to God's law (Psalm 1:1-2) and are ultimately vindicated by Him. It is the true spiritual remnant of Israel.

• Exclusion of the Ungodly: The ungodly may physically mingle with the righteous in the earthly community, but when God convenes His court of judgment, they will not stand (be vindicated) nor have a place in this assembly. The 'edah of the righteous will be entirely purified of sinners.

• Eternal Security: The righteous are assured that their community is permanent, secure, and protected by God, while the ungodly are like chaff blown away by the wind.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 12:3: "Speak to all the congregation of Israel, saying: 'On the tenth of this month every man shall take for himself a lamb, according to the house of his father, a lamb for a household.'"

• Numbers 27:17: "...who may go out before them and go in before them, who may lead them out and bring them in, that the congregation of the LORD may not be like sheep which have no shepherd."

• Numbers 16:3: "They gathered together against Moses and Aaron, and said to them, 'You take too much upon yourselves, for all the congregation is holy, every one of them, and the LORD is among them. Why then do you exalt yourselves above the assembly of the LORD?'"

• Psalm 111:1: "Praise the LORD! I will praise the LORD with my whole heart, In the assembly of the upright and in the congregation."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• Translation in the Septuagint (LXX): The Greek translators of the Old Testament frequently translated 'edah as synagoge (synagogue), representing the localized, structured assembly of Jewish worshipers. This term stands in parallel with qahal, which was often translated as ekklesia (church). Together, these concepts form the foundation for the New Testament understanding of the local and universal church as the covenant assembly of believers.

• A Warning to the Local Church: While the local church contains both wheat and tares, the true, invisible church—the ultimate "congregation of the righteous"—is preserved by God and will one day be presented to Him without spot or wrinkle, completely free from the presence of the ungodly.
