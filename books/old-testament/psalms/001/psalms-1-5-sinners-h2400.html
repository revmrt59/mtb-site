SINNERS - ( chatta' , Strong's H2400 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word transliterated as chatta' refers to those who are characterized by habitual, willful departures from God’s moral standards. Rooted in the concept of missing a target or failing to reach a destination, it describes not merely an occasional misstep, but a settled lifestyle and identity defined by rebellion against God’s law. In Psalm 1:5, these "sinners" are contrasted with the righteous, illustrating that those who choose a path of persistent rebellion will ultimately have no place or standing in the assembly of God's people when He executes His final, dividing judgment.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The verbal root of chatta' is the verb chata', which originally meant "to miss" or "to miss the mark." It is used in Judges 20:16 to describe skilled slingers who could aim a stone at a hair and "not miss."

• In a moral and spiritual sense, this word picture depicts failing to hit the target of God's holy standard or wandering off the path of His commandments.

• Instead of hitting the bullseye of God's perfect law, the sinner misses the mark.

• As a noun, chatta' transitions from describing an action to defining a person's character or status. A chatta' is an habitual offender, a person whose life is characterized by missing the mark of righteousness.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• chatta' functions as a noun meaning "sinner," "habitual offender," or "one who is devoted to sin."

• It signifies a person who is actively and continuously engaged in sinful behavior, marking an identity of rebellion rather than a singular act of weakness.

• The semantic range includes:

• One who is guilty of crimes or offenses against God or others.

• Those who are habitually wicked or godless.

• Those exposed to condemnation and judgment due to their persistent violation of the covenant.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Old Testament uses this term to draw a sharp contrast between those who fear God and those who disregard His commands.

• It conveys the picture of a traveler who deliberately chooses a wrong path. Instead of walking in the "way of the righteous," the sinner walks in a way that leads to ruin.

• Another word picture is that of a debtor or a criminal standing trial. The sinner is one who has accumulated an unpayable debt of transgression and stands condemned before the divine Judge.

4. Theological Implications

• Sin is not merely an abstract concept; it is deeply personal and relational. chatta' highlights that sin is a failure in relationship to God, a refusal to conform to His character.

• The progression in Psalm 1 (walking in the counsel of the ungodly, standing in the path of sinners, sitting in the seat of the scornful) shows the hardening nature of sin. A "sinner" (chatta') is one who has progressed from merely listening to ungodly advice to actively taking up a stand in the lifestyle of sin.

• The word underscores total human accountability. To be a "sinner" is to be legally and morally guilty before a holy God, requiring atonement and justification.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 1:5, the plural form chatta'im is paired in parallel with the "ungodly" (resha'im).

• The text states that "sinners [shall not stand] in the congregation of the righteous." This indicates a judicial exclusion.

• The "congregation of the righteous" refers to the community of God's people, both historically and ultimately in the final eschatological gathering.

• "Stand" ('amad) in this verse has a legal connotation: to survive a trial, to be acquitted, or to maintain a position of honor. Because of their chosen path, sinners will fail to stand when God's judgment is rendered. They will be separated from the assembly of the redeemed, like chaff blown away by the wind.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 13:13: "But the men of Sodom were exceedingly wicked and sinners against the LORD."

• Psalm 1:1: "Blessed is the man / Who walks not in the counsel of the ungodly, / Nor stands in the path of sinners, / Nor sits in the seat of the scornful;"

• Psalm 25:8: "Good and upright is the LORD; / Therefore He teaches sinners in the way."

• Psalm 104:35: "May sinners be consumed from the earth, / And the wicked be no more. / Bless the LORD, O my soul! / Praise the LORD!"

• Isaiah 33:14: "The sinners in Zion are afraid; / Fearfulness has seized the hypocrites: / 'Who among us shall dwell with the devouring fire? / Who among us shall dwell with everlasting burnings?'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• Contrast with "the ungodly" (rasha): While rasha emphasizes a state of wickedness, hostility to God, and guilt under the law, chatta' emphasizes the practical expression of that wickedness in daily conduct—continually choosing the wrong path and missing God's moral mark.

• In the Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint), chatta' is regularly translated with the Greek noun hamartolos, which carries the identical meaning of "sinner" in the New Testament. This bridges the Old Testament concept of the "sinner" directly to the New Testament's call to repentance and the work of Christ, who came to save sinners (1 Timothy 1:15).
