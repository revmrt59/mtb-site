UNGODLY - ( rasha` , Strong's H7563 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word rasha` refers to those who are actively wicked, guilty, or hostile to God's covenantal standard. In contrast to the righteous who delight in God's law, the ungodly are spiritually unstable, morally lawless, and ultimately face divine condemnation. In Psalm 1:5, they are depicted as unable to withstand the scrutiny of God's holy judgment, leading to their exclusion from the assembly of the righteous.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• In the ancient Near East and the Old Testament world, life was viewed through the lens of covenant relationships. Righteousness (tsaddiq) was defined by faithfulness to those relationships, while ungodliness (rasha`) was defined by a breach of covenant.

• The word rasha` carries a legal background. It depicts someone who has been tried in court and found guilty of an offense. It is the opposite of being declared innocent or righteous.

• The ungodly are not merely passive or neutral bystanders; they actively live in a way that disrupts the moral order established by God. They are characterized by a restless, chaotic lifestyle, much like the churning sea or chaff driven by the wind.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The term rasha` means wicked, criminal, guilty, or lawless. It describes a person who is in the wrong regarding God's standards or human justice.

• The word denotes an ongoing state of hostility toward God's moral law. It refers to someone who is guilty of sin and remains in a state of condemnation because they refuse to submit to divine authority.

• In a legal context, it refers to a guilty party in a lawsuit—someone whose actions have been weighed and found lacking.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Rasha` is frequently contrasted with tsaddiq (righteous) throughout the Wisdom literature, establishing a clear binary in human existence: there are only two paths, two characters, and two destinies.

• Scripture uses the metaphor of chaff (Psalm 1:4) to describe the ungodly. Chaff is weightless, rootless, and useless—completely at the mercy of the wind. This highlights the spiritual instability and ultimate worthlessness of a life lived apart from God.

• Isaiah compares the wicked to the troubled sea that cannot rest, whose waters cast up mire and dirt (Isaiah 57:20). This highlights the internal turmoil and moral pollution of the ungodly life.

4. Theological Implications

• Ungodliness is fundamentally a heart issue before it becomes a behavioral issue. It begins with a refusal to fear, worship, or submit to God.

• The existence of the rasha` demonstrates that humanity is not morally neutral. True wickedness is defined not by human opinion, but by God’s immutable character and law.

• God's holiness requires a final separation between the righteous and the ungodly. While they may share the same physical spaces in this life, their spiritual realities and ultimate destinies are completely opposed.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 1:5, the phrase "Therefore the ungodly shall not stand in the judgment" uses rasha` to show the legal consequence of their chosen path.

• The verb "stand" (qum) means to rise up, endure, or be vindicated. When God convenes His court, the ungodly will have no legal standing, no defense, and no capacity to endure His holy presence.

• Because they chose to "stand" in the path of sinners during their lives (Psalm 1:1), they are justly denied the ability to "stand" in the assembly of the righteous when judgment comes. Their lack of spiritual root system guarantees their collapse.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 1:1 - "Blessed is the man Who walks not in the counsel of the ungodly, Nor stands in the path of sinners, Nor sits in the seat of the scornful;"

• Psalm 1:6 - "For the LORD knows the way of the righteous, But the way of the ungodly shall perish."

• Psalm 3:7 - "Arise, O LORD; Save me, O my God! For You have struck all my enemies on the cheekbone; You have broken the teeth of the ungodly."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• There is a tragic irony in the lifestyle of the ungodly. They often appear prosperous, strong, and deeply rooted in the eyes of the world (Psalm 73). However, the narrative of Scripture consistently pulls back the curtain to reveal their true reality: they are on slippery ground, heading toward sudden destruction.

• The ultimate remedy for the state of rasha` is the justifying grace of God. While the Old Testament warns of the judgment of the ungodly, the New Testament reveals the stunning truth that God "justifies the ungodly" (Romans 4:5) through the substitutionary death of Jesus Christ.
