RIGHTEOUS - ( tsaddiq , Strong's H6662 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word tsaddiq describes someone who is just, upright, and in a right relationship with God, characterized by conformity to His ethical and moral standards. In Psalm 1:6, it refers to those who walk in covenant faithfulness with God, whose lives are marked by spiritual fruitfulness, obedience, and devotion to His instruction, contrastingly set against the wicked whose lives are unstable and ultimately perish.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root ts-d-q relates to a straight line, an objective standard, or a correct weight and measure. In the ancient world, a "just" scale or weight was one that was true, accurate, and conformed to the standard of justice.

• Applied to people, tsaddiq refers to an individual whose character and conduct align perfectly with God's straight and true standard. It is a relational term, emphasizing a life lived in accordance with covenant obligations toward God and fellow human beings.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The adjective tsaddiq means righteous, just, upright, or vindicated. It describes someone who is in the right, guiltless of moral or legal failure, and actively doing what is right.

• It denotes conformity to a moral and ethical norm. Because God is the ultimate Standard, to be tsaddiq is to be right in His sight, reflecting His character in daily living.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Old Testament frequently contrasts the tsaddiq (righteous) with the rasha (wicked). This binary division highlights two distinct directions, characters, and destinies in life.

• The tsaddiq is pictured as a deeply rooted, fruitful tree planted by streams of water, drawing life from an unseen source, remaining green and productive even in times of drought.

• The word also carries a legal sense. A judge declares the innocent party to be tsaddiq (justified/righteous) in court, meaning they are vindicated and free from condemnation.

4. Theological Implications

• Righteousness is fundamentally covenantal and relational. It is not an abstract, self-righteous morality but a posture of humble trust, devotion, and alignment with God.

• True righteousness in humanity is derived from God, who is perfectly tsaddiq in all His ways. Human righteousness is a response to His grace and a reflection of His holy character.

• The tsaddiq is assured of God's active, personal protection. Righteousness is not merely a moral category but a state of being secure under the covenant promises of God, leading to eternal life and ultimate vindication.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 1:6, the tsaddiq are those who "delight in the law of the LORD" and meditate on it continually (Psalm 1:2). Their righteousness is evidenced by their separation from the path of sinners and their active connection to God's truth.

• The text states that "the LORD knows the way of the righteous." The word "knows" (yada) implies intimate, caring, protective watchfulness. God actively guards, approves, and sustains the path of the tsaddiq.

• Unlike the wicked who are like chaff blown away by the wind, the tsaddiq have an enduring foundation and a destiny secured by God Himself.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 6:9 - "This is the genealogy of Noah. Noah was a just man, perfect in his generations. Noah walked with God."

• Psalm 34:15 - "The eyes of the LORD are on the righteous, And His ears are open to their cry."

• Proverbs 10:25 - "When the whirlwind passes by, the wicked is no more, But the righteous has an everlasting foundation."

• Habakkuk 2:4 - "Behold the proud, His soul is not upright in him; But the just shall live by his faith."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament) translates tsaddiq using the Greek word dikaios. This same Greek word is used extensively in the New Testament to describe the righteousness of faith and the believer's standing before God in Christ.

• The plural form in Psalm 1:5 ("the congregation of the righteous") reminds us that the tsaddiq do not stand alone; they belong to an eternal assembly of those vindicated and gathered by God.
