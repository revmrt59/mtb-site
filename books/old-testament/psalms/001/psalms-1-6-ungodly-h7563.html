UNGODLY - ( rasha` , Strong's H7563 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word rasha` refers to those who are actively hostile to God's covenant, characterized by a lifestyle of moral compromise, self-governance, and disregard for divine law. Rather than describing a mere passive lack of religion, it denotes a state of active rebellion, guilt, and lawlessness that stands in direct opposition to the righteous. In Psalm 1:6, the "ungodly" represent those whose life path is self-directed and ultimately leads to ruin, contrasting-sharply with the righteous who are intimately known and sustained by the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root of rasha` carries the fundamental concept of being loose, disjointed, or out of place. It depicts someone who is turbulent, restless, and constantly unstable because they have severed themselves from the anchor of God's moral order.

• Unlike the righteous who are like firmly planted, fruitful trees, the ungodly are spiritually rootless. They resemble chaff, the worthless husk of wheat that is easily carried away by the wind. This word picture conveys a life devoid of substance, weight, and security.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The word rasha` refers to a person who is guilty of sin, acting wickedly, or living in open rebellion against God's standards.

• In legal contexts, it denotes the guilty party in a lawsuit—the one who is in the wrong, in contrast to the innocent or righteous party (tsaddiq).

• The semantic range spans from local civil injustice and ethical failure to cosmic, spiritual rebellion against the Creator. It describes a heart attitude and lifestyle that rejects God's authority and actively harms the community.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Old Testament frequently contrasts rasha` with tsaddiq (righteous), particularly in wisdom literature. This stark classification shows that humanity is divided into two ethical and spiritual categories based on their relationship with God.

• The ungodly are compared to the troubled sea that cannot rest, whose waters cast up mire and dirt. This illustrates the internal turmoil, lack of peace, and moral pollution that characterizes their existence.

• Their path is described as deep darkness; they do not even know what makes them stumble. This stands in opposition to the path of the righteous, which shines ever brighter.

4. Theological Implications

• Theologically, the ungodly are not merely "imperfect" people; they are defined by their status of guilt before God. Their outward actions flow from an inward rejection of God's sovereignty.

• The presence of the ungodly highlights the reality of a moral universe governed by a holy God. Their existence and prosperity are temporary, proving the necessity of divine judgment.

• God's holiness demands that the ungodly cannot endure in the presence of a holy God or within the congregation of the righteous. Their ultimate end is separation from God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 1:6, rasha` is set in final, absolute contrast to the righteous. This verse serves as the climactic conclusion to the entire opening psalm of the Psalter.

• The "way" (derek) of the ungodly represents their lifestyle, decisions, philosophies, and ultimate destiny. While the Lord "knows" (intimately cares for, watches over, and preserves) the way of the righteous, the way of the ungodly "shall perish."

• The context of Psalm 1 shows a progressive hardening of the ungodly: walking in their counsel, standing in their path, and sitting in their seat. By verse 6, this progressive departure from God culminates in their complete destruction. Their "way" leads to a dead end because it lacks the life-giving presence, approval, and sustaining grace of Yahweh.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 1:1 - "Blessed is the man Who walks not in the counsel of the ungodly, Nor stands in the path of sinners, Nor sits in the seat of the scornful;"

• Psalm 1:4 - "The ungodly are not so, But are like the chaff which the wind drives away."

• Psalm 3:7 - "Arise, O LORD; Save me, O my God! For You have struck all my enemies on the cheekbone; You have broken the teeth of the ungodly."

• Psalm 9:17 - "The wicked shall be turned into hell, And all the nations that forget God." (Translating rasha` as "wicked" to emphasize the ultimate destiny of those who forget God).

• Psalm 145:20 - "The LORD preserves all who love Him, But all the wicked He will destroy."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Septuagint translates rasha` in Psalm 1 using the Greek word asebes, which means irreverent, godless, or lacking worshipful fear of God. This reinforces that the core issue of the ungodly is not merely ethical misbehavior but a fundamental lack of reverence for God.

• The Hebrew verb for "perish" ('abad) in Psalm 1:6 denotes ruin, loss, and destruction. This indicates that the path of independent living is inherently self-destructive and subject to God's active wrath. It does not mean annihilation, but rather the total ruin of their purpose, life, and hope.
