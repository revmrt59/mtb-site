GOD - ( 'El , Strong's H410 )

Summary:

The Hebrew name 'El represents God as the ultimate, sovereign Power of the universe—the strong, mighty, and prominent Creator. In Psalm 19:1, this title emphasizes His transcendent majesty and creative power as the heavens continually proclaim His glory. Rather than focusing on His covenant relationship (typically expressed by Yahweh), 'El directs our attention to His infinite capability, supreme authority, and the undeniable testimony of His handiwork visible to all of humanity.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root of 'El is historically connected to the concepts of power, strength, and preeminence. In the ancient Near Eastern context, the word was used generally for "deity" or as the proper name of the chief Canaanite god. However, biblical writers purified and elevated this term, using it exclusively to identify the one true God who stands in stark contrast to powerless, manufactured idols. The ancient pictograph for 'El consists of an ox head (representing strength and leadership) and a shepherd's staff (representing authority and guidance). Thus, the word picture behind the name is that of a strong, towering leader who guides, protects, and possesses supreme, unmatched power.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• 'El is a singular noun meaning "god," "mighty one," or "the true God." It is derived from a root meaning "to be strong" or "to be powerful." While the plural form Elohim emphasizes God's fullness, majesty, and triune complexity, 'El isolates the specific attribute of divine strength and absolute sovereignty. It occurs over 200 times in the Old Testament, predominantly in poetic, prophetic, and wisdom literature where God's ultimate power over nature, history, and human destiny is declared.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The term 'El is frequently joined with descriptive titles to paint vivid portraits of God's character and relationship to His creation:

• El Shaddai (God Almighty) – God as the all-powerful, all-sufficient source of blessing and comfort.

• El Elyon (The Most High God) – God as the supreme ruler who possesses heaven and earth.

• El Olam (The Everlasting God) – God as the eternal, unchangeable foundation of all things.

• El Roi (The God Who Sees) – God as the personal, watchful protector of the suffering.

These combinations illustrate that God’s strength is not a cold, detached force, but an active power deployed on behalf of His people. He is pictured as an immovable rock, a consuming fire, and a shield of salvation.

4. Theological Implications

• Omnipotence: As 'El, God possesses inherent, unlimited power. There is no force in the universe that can resist His will or derail His sovereign plans.

• Transcendence: 'El stands infinitely above His creation. He is not a part of nature; rather, nature exists because He willed it into being.

• Monotheism: The Old Testament uses 'El to champion the truth that there is only one true, living Sovereign. The pagan "gods" (elim) are empty deceptions, whereas 'El is the living God who acts, speaks, and reigns.

• Absolute Sufficiency: Because God is the strong One, He is fully capable of vindicating His holiness, delivering His people from oppression, and keeping every promise He has made.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19:1, David writes, "The heavens declare the glory of God ('El); and the firmament shows His handiwork." The choice of 'El here is deliberate and highly significant. The first half of this psalm (verses 1–6) deals with general revelation—how God reveals Himself to all humanity through the physical universe. David uses 'El because the creation does not speak of covenant law or historical redemption (which are associated with the covenant name Yahweh), but of sheer, majestic power, infinite scale, and glorious order. The vastness of the cosmos, the heat of the sun, and the precision of the stars all broadcast the message that the Creator is the ultimate Power ('El). By contrast, in the second half of the psalm (verses 7–14), where David shifts to special revelation through the written Word, he immediately switches to the personal, covenant name Yahweh (translated "LORD"). The structural genius of the psalm shows that the awesome, powerful 'El of the cosmos is the very same personal, relational Yahweh who speaks to our souls.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 17:1 - "When Abram was ninety-nine years old, the LORD appeared to Abram and said to him, 'I am Almighty God ['El]; walk before Me and be blameless.'"

• Genesis 35:1 - "Then God said to Jacob, 'Arise, go up to Bethel and dwell there; and make an altar there to God ['El], who appeared to you when you fled from the face of Esau your brother.'"

• Psalm 18:2 - "The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer; My God ['El], my strength, in whom I will trust; My shield and the horn of my salvation, my stronghold."

• Isaiah 9:6 - "For unto us a Child is born, Unto us a Son is given; And the government will be on His shoulder. And His name will be called Wonderful, Counselor, Mighty God ['El], Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace."

• Isaiah 43:10 - "'You are My witnesses,' says the LORD, 'And My servant whom I have chosen, That you may know and believe Me, And understand that I am He. Before Me there was no God ['El] formed, Nor shall there be after Me.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The name 'El is embedded in the names of many prominent biblical figures and places, weaving the reality of God's power directly into human history:

• Israel means "he struggles with God" or "God strives."

• Daniel means "God is my judge."

• Elijah means "My God is Yahweh."

• Bethel means "house of God."

• Emmanuel means "God with us."

In the New Testament, the Greek equivalent of 'El is Theos. When Jesus cried out from the cross, "Eloi, Eloi, lama sabachthani?" (Mark 15:34), He was quoting Psalm 22:1, calling upon 'El as His God and His ultimate source of strength in the dark hour of judgment.
