SHOWS - ( nagad , Strong's H5046 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb nagad fundamentally means to make conspicuous, to declare, or to announce. Rather than a quiet or hidden whisper, it conveys a bold, clear, and visible reporting of information. In Psalm 19:1, it describes how the expanse of the sky actively, continuously, and conspicuously puts on display the creative craftsmanship of God, making His glory undeniable to all who look up.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root meaning of nagad is to stand out, to be bold, or to be conspicuous. It carries the physical imagery of placing something directly in front of someone, bringing it into high relief, or stepping forward to make a formal, public announcement. It is not a passive existence; it is an active, front-facing communication. The word evokes the picture of a messenger standing in the public square, raising his voice, or displaying a royal decree so clearly that no one can miss it.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

In the Hiphil stem (the form used in Psalm 19:1), nagad means to declare, announce, report, make known, or expound. It denotes the clear communication of information, truth, or a message that was previously unknown or requires public witness. The lexical range includes:

• To declare or report a matter clearly.

• To make conspicuously manifest or visible.

• To announce or proclaim with authority.

• To explain, interpret, or solve a riddle or mystery.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Cosmic Herald: The word nagad is frequently used in the Old Testament for messengers bringing news (often of battle or significant events), prophets declaring the word of the Lord, and individuals testifying to what they have witnessed. A herald does not create the news; he simply reports it with clarity and boldness. When the creation "shows" God's handiwork, it serves as God's cosmic herald, pointing away from itself to the genius, power, and beauty of the Creator.

• Continuous Broadcast: The use of the participle form (maggid) in Psalm 19:1 emphasizes a continuous, unending action. Day after day, night after night, the sky is perpetually "showing" and broadcasting this divine testimony.

4. Theological Implications

• General Revelation: The use of nagad affirms the doctrine of general revelation. God has not left Himself without witness. The physical universe is a highly visible, public testimony of His existence, wisdom, and eternal power.

• Inexcusability of Humanity: Because the creation "shows" (makes conspicuous) God's handiwork, humanity is without excuse. The message is not hidden in secret codes; it is declared openly.

• The Nature of God’s Glory: God’s glory is not meant to be hidden. He desires to be known, and He has designed the cosmos to be a theater that constantly publishes His attributes.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 19:1, nagad is paired parallel to saphar ("declare" in "The heavens declare the glory of God"). While saphar focuses on recounting, numbering, or telling a story, nagad emphasizes making that story visible, conspicuous, and clear.

• The "firmament" (the vast expanse of the sky, both day and night) acts as the canvas. It does not just exist; it actively "shows" or reports the "handiwork" (the work of His hands) of God. The physical beauty of the sunrise, the complexity of the atmosphere, and the precision of the stars are a continuous broadcast, telling of God's artistic and structural mastery.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 3:11: "And He said, 'Who told [nagad] you that you were naked?'" (Here, it refers to revealing or reporting information that was hidden or private).

• Psalm 97:6: "The heavens declare [nagad] His righteousness, And all the peoples see His glory." (Using the same cosmic context, the heavens make God's moral perfection conspicuous to all).

• Psalm 145:4: "One generation shall praise Your works to another, And shall declare [nagad] Your mighty acts." (An intentional, vocal, and generational transmission of God's deeds).

• Isaiah 42:9: "Behold, the former things have come to pass, And new things I declare [nagad]; Before they spring forth I tell you of them." (God declaring future events to prove His unique sovereignty).

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Greek equivalent often used in the Septuagint (LXX) for nagad is anangello, which means to report, announce, or declare back. In the New Testament, this word is used for the Holy Spirit "declaring" the things of Christ to the believers (John 16:13-15).

• There is a beautiful progression in Psalm 19: first, the wordless, universal sermon of the skies (nagad), followed by the perfect, written, and specific testimony of God’s Law (verses 7-11). The cosmic sermon prepares the heart to receive the written covenant.
