DECLARE - ( saphar , Strong's H5608 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb saphar primarily means to count, measure, or register, but in its intensive form (the Piel stem used in Psalm 19:1), it shifts to meaning "to recount," "to declare," or "to rehearse in detail." In this context, it describes the continuous, active, and detailed proclamation of the physical heavens as they tell of God's majestic glory. Rather than sitting in passive existence, the cosmos acts as an ongoing, energetic narrator of the Creator’s wisdom, power, and beauty.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root saphar is intrinsically connected to the world of writing, counting, and record-keeping. It is the linguistic basis for the Hebrew words for "scribe" (sopher) and "book" (sepher). The underlying imagery is one of careful, systematic, and orderly recording of facts. When applied to the physical creation in Psalm 19, this root paints a vivid picture of the cosmos acting as a celestial scribe or a living book. The universe does not merely exist as a mute monument; it actively and systematically publishes a record of God's greatness, writing His attributes across the expanse of space.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• In the Qal stem, saphar means to count, number, or reckon. It refers to the physical act of enumerating objects, such as counting stars or numbering days.

• In the Piel stem (the intensive grammatical form used in Psalm 19:1), the meaning shifts from merely counting physical items to recounting, declaring, narrating, or rehearsing events, praises, or stories.

• The Piel stem denotes a thorough, systematic, and enthusiastic declaration. It is not a casual mention, but a comprehensive, detailed telling that leaves no part of the story untold.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Living Ledger: Just as a financial ledger or historical document lists every transaction and event with meticulous detail, saphar represents a detailed public testimony. The heavens serve as God's public record, systematically laying out the evidence of His divine nature.

• The Continuous Proclamation: In Psalm 19:1, the verb appears as a Piel participle (mesapperim). In Hebrew grammar, the participle indicates continuous, ongoing action. The heavens are in a constant state of declaring; their testimony never ceases, pauses, or takes a break. It is a perpetual broadcast.

4. Theological Implications

• The Clarity of General Revelation: Creation speaks. Although it lacks human vocal cords, its testimony to the existence, power, and wisdom of God is objective, clear, and mathematically precise.

• The Nature of God's Glory: The subject of this declaration is the kabod (glory) of God—His weightiness, majesty, and supreme excellence. The cosmos points away from itself to its Maker.

• Order and Intelligent Design: Because the root saphar is tied to counting and ordering, it highlights that the universe's mathematical precision and structural integrity are themselves a primary means of declaring God's wisdom. The orderly cosmos reveals an orderly Creator.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19:1, the Piel participle mesapperim ("are declaring") stands in poetic parallelism with the Hiphil participle maggid ("is showing" or "proclaiming"). Together, these terms emphasize the completeness of creation's testimony.

The subsequent verses (verses 2-4) explain that this declaration has "no speech nor language" and "their voice is not heard," yet their "line has gone out through all the earth." The nuance of saphar here is a visual, structural declaration. The silent, orderly movements of the sun, moon, and stars communicate a message so detailed and universal that it transcends human language barriers, leaving all humanity without excuse before God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 15:5 - "Then He brought him outside and said, 'Look now toward heaven, and count [saphar] the stars if you are able to number [saphar] them.' And He said to him, 'So shall your descendants be.'" (Demonstrates the Qal meaning of physical counting).

• Psalm 9:1 - "I will praise You, O LORD, with my whole heart; I will tell of [saphar] all Your marvelous works." (Demonstrates the Piel meaning of detailed testimony).

• Psalm 22:22 - "I will declare [saphar] Your name to my brethren; In the midst of the assembly I will praise You." (Demonstrates public proclamation).

• Psalm 96:3 - "Declare [saphar] His glory among the nations, His wonders among all peoples." (Demonstrates missionary proclamation of God's acts).

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament) translates saphar in Psalm 19:1 using the Greek verb diegeomai. This Greek term means to relate a matter from beginning to end, to describe in full detail, or to conduct a complete narrative. This translation choice confirms that the ancient understanding of the verse was not a vague, general whisper of God's existence, but a thorough, structured, and detailed presentation of His glory.
