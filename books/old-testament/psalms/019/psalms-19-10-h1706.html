HONEY - ( debash , Strong's H1706 )

Summary:

In the Old Testament, honey (debash) represents the height of natural sweetness, nourishment, and abundance, frequently associated with the promised land flowing with "milk and honey." In Psalm 19:10, it is used metaphorically to describe the profound sweetness and life-giving satisfaction found in God's written Word, surpassing even the purest physical delicacies.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near East, honey was the primary sweetener available, highly prized for its rich taste and medicinal, energizing qualities. Unlike modern refined sugar, honey was a natural, raw gift from creation, harvested from wild bee nests in rock crevices and hollow trees, or prepared as a thick, sweet syrup from boiled grapes, dates, and figs. It was a luxury item, a staple of celebratory feasts, and a symbol of God's abundant provision. Because it required no processing to be enjoyed, honey represented pure, unadulterated goodness directly from the hand of the Creator.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The Hebrew noun debash refers to wild bee honey or the concentrated sweet syrup of fruits. Lexically, the term denotes natural sweetness, pleasantness, and nutrient-dense refreshment. It represents that which is highly desirable to the palate, capable of reviving the faint and bringing delight to the senses.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Land of Promise: The phrase "a land flowing with milk and honey" serves as a vivid picture of agricultural wealth, safety, and divine blessing, highlighting a home of luxury, rest, and complete satisfaction.

• Physical and Mental Revitalization: Honey provides immediate physical energy. When Jonathan ate honey in the forest during a military pursuit, his eyes were instantly brightened, illustrating how raw sweetness brings immediate strength and clarity to those exhausted by spiritual or physical warfare.

• Sweetness of Speech: Pleasant words are described as a honeycomb, sweet to the soul and healing to the bones, showing that healthy, God-honoring speech imparts life-giving nourishment to others.

4. Theological Implications

• Delight Over Duty: God’s law is not presented as a dry, heavy burden of rules. Instead, it is a source of supreme joy and spiritual pleasure. True obedience stems from a heart that tastes and sees that the Lord is good.

• Immediate Spiritual Vitality: Just as physical honey revives the exhausted body, digesting the truths of scripture provides immediate strength, joy, and clarity to the weary soul.

• The Sufficiency of Truth: God's Word satisfies the deepest human longings. It fulfills the intellect, the emotions, and the spirit, proving more valuable than the finest earthly treasures and sweeter than the most indulgent earthly pleasures.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19, David extols the perfection, trustworthiness, and purity of God’s revelation. In verse 10, he declares that God's ordinances are "Sweeter also than honey and the honeycomb." The phrase translated "honeycomb" literally refers to the "drippings of the combs" (nopheth tsupim). This is the purest, sweetest honey that flows naturally from the comb without pressure. David is not merely comparing the Word to ordinary honey, but to the finest, most concentrated portion of the honey. The nuance is clear: nothing in the material world can match the exquisite, refreshing, and satisfying nature of God’s truth when it is received and meditated upon.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 3:8: "So I have come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians, and to bring them up from that land to a good and large land, to a land flowing with milk and honey..."

• Psalm 119:103: "How sweet are Your words to my taste, Sweeter than honey to my mouth!"

• Proverbs 24:13: "My son, eat honey because it is good, And the honeycomb which is sweet to your taste;"

• Ezekiel 3:3: "And He said to me, 'Son of man, feed your belly, and fill your stomach with this scroll that I give you.' So I ate, and it was in my mouth like honey in sweetness."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• Manna, the supernatural bread from heaven that sustained Israel in the wilderness, tasted like wafers made with honey (Exodus 16:31). This connected God's daily, miraculous provision with the comforting sweetness of debash.

• While honey was a symbol of divine blessing, it was strictly forbidden from being burned as a grain offering to the Lord (Leviticus 2:11). Because honey ferments easily when heated, it symbolized corruption and human effort to sweeten what God had already designated as holy. However, when consumed by the believer as God’s truth, it remains a pure metaphor for spiritual satisfaction.
