FINE GOLD - ( paz, Strong's H6337 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun paz refers to the highest grade of refined, pure, and brilliant gold available in the ancient world. Distinguished from ordinary gold, paz represents the ultimate standard of material wealth, beauty, and enduring value. In Psalm 19:10, the psalmist uses this precious metal to illustrate the incomparable worth of God's written revelation. God's law, testimonies, statutes, commandments, and judgments are not merely valuable; they vastly surpass the most highly refined and coveted earthly treasure in existence.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• In the ancient Near East, gold was refined through a process of cupellation, where raw ore was melted repeatedly in a furnace to separate precious metals from base metals and impurities. Ordinary gold still contained trace elements of silver, copper, or iron.

• The noun paz represents gold that has gone through the absolute limit of the refining process, leaving it completely pure, highly lustrous, and remarkably soft. It represents the pinnacle of luxury, royalty, and wealth.

• Because of its exceptional purity, paz did not tarnish or degrade. It was reserved for the crowns of kings, the most sacred vessels of the temple, and the most exquisite jewelry. To the ancient Israelite, paz was the physical manifestation of ultimate earthly perfection, glory, and durability.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The noun paz refers to refined gold, pure gold, or high-grade gold.

• The root meaning is tied to the concept of being purified, separated, or shining with a bright, brilliant luster.

• Unlike general terms for gold, paz specifically emphasizes the quality of purity and the rigorous refining process required to produce it. It denotes the most expensive and rare classification of gold available.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Paz is used to describe the crown placed upon the head of the Davidic king, symbolizing divine favor, authority, and the highest honor (Psalm 21:3).

• It describes the physical appearance of the beloved's head and limbs in the Song of Solomon, symbolizing radiant health, peerless beauty, and royal dignity (Song of Solomon 5:11, 15).

• It serves as a metaphor for the preciousness of the sons of Zion, who were once highly valued like refined gold but were later treated as common clay pots due to judgment (Lamentations 4:2).

• It represents the standard of ultimate value that wisdom exceeds, as wisdom cannot be bartered for jewels of paz (Job 28:17).

4. Theological Implications

• The use of paz in Scripture establishes a hierarchy of values. It represents the highest point of horizontal, earthly value only to show that vertical, divine realities vastly exceed it.

• By comparing God's Word to paz, the Holy Spirit demonstrates that divine revelation is pure, untarnished, and free from any defect or error. Just as refined gold contains no dross, the words of the Lord are flawless.

• This comparison reframes human desire. True wealth is not measured by material acquisition but by spiritual alignment with God’s truth. The possession of God's Word yields eternal dividends that outlast the most durable earthly treasures.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 19, David transitions from the general revelation of God in creation to the special revelation of God in His Word. He uses six distinct titles, characteristics, and effects to describe the Scriptures.

• In verse 10, David declares that these divine words are "More to be desired... than gold, Yea, than much fine gold."

• The progression is highly deliberate. God's Word is not just better than ordinary gold; it is better than a massive quantity ("much") of the absolute highest quality gold (paz).

• The context contrasts the temporal, material satisfaction of wealth with the restorative, soul-enriching, and life-giving power of God's testimonies. Material wealth can corrupt, but God's Word enlightens the eyes and warns the soul against danger.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 21:3 - "For You meet him with the blessings of goodness; You set a crown of pure gold on his head."

• Psalm 119:127 - "Therefore I love Your commandments More than gold, yes, than fine gold!"

• Proverbs 8:19 - "My fruit is better than gold, yes, than fine gold, And my revenue than choice silver."

• Isaiah 13:12 - "I will make a mortal more rare than fine gold, A man more than the golden wedge of Ophir."

• Lamentations 4:2 - "The precious sons of Zion, Valuable as fine gold, How they are regarded as clay pots, The work of the hands of the potter!"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The contrast in Psalm 19:10 also involves the sense of taste: "Sweeter also than honey and the honeycomb." This appeals to both the primary drivers of human endeavor: wealth (paz) and pleasure/sustenance (honey). God's Word satisfies both the desire for security and the desire for delight.

• While paz is virtually indestructible by natural elements, it is still a temporal, created substance. In contrast, the "fear of the Lord" (which is synonymous with His Word in Psalm 19:9) "endures forever." The ultimate irony of human greed is the pursuit of paz at the expense of the eternal Word.
