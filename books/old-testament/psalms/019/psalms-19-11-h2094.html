WARNED - ( zahar , Strong's H2094 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb zahar carries a beautiful dual meaning of "shining brightly" and "giving warning or instruction." In Psalm 19:11, used in its passive form, it describes how the servant of God is actively illuminated, cautioned, and guided by the Holy Scriptures. Rather than a harsh threat of punishment, this warning is a protective, merciful light that exposes hidden spiritual dangers, keeping the believer on the path of safety where there is "great reward."

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The root meaning of zahar is to shine, emit light, or be bright. In the ancient Near East, this concept of physical brightness naturally lent itself to a moral and intellectual metaphor: to enlighten, instruct, or warn. To warn someone, in the biblical mind, is to shine a bright light on their path to reveal obstacles or dangers that would otherwise remain hidden in the darkness. It is the spiritual equivalent of a beacon or lighthouse warning ships of a rocky, hazardous shoreline.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

In the Old Testament, zahar occurs in two primary verbal stems:

• Hiphil (Causative Active): To shine, to warn, to admonish, or to teach. In this form, it represents the active casting of light upon a person or situation to make them aware of reality and consequences.

• Niphal (Passive/Reflexive): To be warned, to take warning, or to receive admonition. This is the form utilized in Psalm 19:11. It refers to the internalizing of instruction, where the hearer actively allows the light of God's Word to redirect their steps and guard their life.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Radiance of Wisdom: In its literal sense of physical shining, the word describes the celestial brightness of the heavens and the future glory of those who teach righteousness (Daniel 12:3).

• The Watchman’s Alarm: The word is used extensively in Ezekiel to describe the duty of a watchman. When the watchman blows the trumpet to sound the alarm of an oncoming sword, he is warning the people. Here, the word represents a life-saving, urgent signal that demands immediate action.

• The Light of the Law: Moses is commanded to use this action to teach Israel the statutes and laws, showing them the way they must walk. The law is depicted not as a heavy burden, but as an illuminating guide that prevents the traveler from straying into danger.

### 4. Theological Implications

• Warnings as Divine Grace: Biblical warnings are not expressions of an angry, restrictive Deity, but manifestations of covenant love. God shines the light of His truth on the hazardous realities of sin because He desires the preservation and flourishing of His people.

• The Word as a Watchman: For the believer, the Scriptures function as a permanent, vigilant watchman. The Bible does not merely inform the intellect; it actively safeguards the soul by alerting the conscience to subtle moral and spiritual traps.

• The Responsibility of the Hearer: Because the verb in Psalm 19:11 is passive-reflexive, it underscores that the servant of God must actively receive and submit to the warning. Light is only useful to the one who opens their eyes and adjusts their path accordingly.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19, David transitions from the general revelation of God's glory in creation (vv. 1–6) to the special revelation of God's character in the Scriptures (vv. 7–11). After describing the law of the Lord as perfect, sure, right, pure, clean, and true, David applies these truths personally in verse 11: "Moreover by them Your servant is warned."

The "them" refers directly to the divine judgments and commandments. In the verses that immediately follow, David asks to be cleansed from "secret faults" and kept back from "presumptuous sins." This context reveals the exact nature of the warning: the Word of God acts as a spiritual diagnostic light, exposing hidden faults and prideful inclinations before they can master the believer. The ultimate end of this warning is not restriction, but the "great reward" of spiritual integrity and unbroken fellowship with God.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Ezekiel 3:17: "Son of man, I have made you a watchman for the house of Israel; therefore hear a word from My mouth, and give them warning from Me:"

• Ezekiel 33:3: "...if, when he sees the sword coming upon the land, he blows the trumpet and warns the people,"

• Ecclesiastes 12:12: "And further, my son, be admonished by these. Of making many books there is no end, and much study is wearisome to the flesh."

• Daniel 12:3: "Those who are wise shall shine Like the brightness of the firmament, And those who turn many to righteousness Like the stars forever and ever."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Family Language of the Servant: David refers to himself here as "Your servant." God’s protective warnings are specifically tailored for those who belong to Him. While the law condemns the rebellious, it lovingly warns and guides the servant.

• The Warning-Reward Connection: There is a beautiful paradox in verse 11. David couples "warned" with "great reward." In the economy of God, heeding a warning is not a loss of freedom, but the very key to inheriting the fullness of God's blessings and joy.
