SERVANT - ( 'ebed , Strong's H5650 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun 'ebed refers to a servant, slave, or worshiper who is completely surrendered to the will of a master. Far from suggesting degrading bondage, when used of a believer in relation to Yahweh, it carries a sense of profound honor, intimate relationship, and willing submission. In Psalm 19:11, David identifies himself as God's 'ebed, recognizing that his life is governed by God's instructions (the Torah) which warn, guide, and protect him, leading to great reward.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The noun 'ebed is derived from the verb 'abad, meaning "to work, serve, till, or worship." In ancient Near Eastern culture, a servant's identity, security, and purpose were entirely bound up in their master. While 'ebed can denote physical slavery or political vassalage, its use in religious contexts expresses voluntary, loving loyalty to God. The 'ebed is not an independent agent but one whose life is defined by obedience, carrying out the master's business, and representing the master's authority.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun 'ebed means a servant, slave, laborer, or subordinate. It designates someone in a position of subservience to another, whether a human master, a king, or God Himself. Within the covenant relationship, it functions as a title of humility and devotion, indicating a worshiper who acknowledges the absolute sovereignty of God. It carries a multi-faceted semantic range:

• A slave or servant under physical ownership or obligation.

• A subject or minister serving a human king.

• A loyal worshiper or designated agent of God (such as Moses, Joshua, David, or the prophetic "Servant of the Lord").

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

The term 'ebed is used over 800 times in the Old Testament, making it one of the most common designations for human-divine relationships.

• The Bondservant of Love: In Exodus 21:5–6, a servant who loves his master can choose to remain a servant for life, having his ear pierced with an awl. This provides a vivid word picture of the believer's willing, permanent, and loving submission to Yahweh.

• The Royal Minister: In royal courts, the 'ebed is not a lowly slave but a trusted officer or minister of the king, possessing delegated authority. When David calls himself God's 'ebed, he acknowledges that his royal authority is subordinate to the King of Kings.

• The Instrument of Worship: Because the root 'abad means both "to serve" and "to worship," the 'ebed is one whose daily labor and devotion are integrated into a single offering of worship to God. Servitude to God is the ultimate form of worship.

### 4. Theological Implications

• Submission and Sovereignty: Identifying as an 'ebed requires a total surrender of self-will. It recognizes God as the ultimate Owner, Provider, and Master who has the absolute right to command obedience.

• Identity and Security: A servant's protection and provision are the responsibility of the master. To be the 'ebed of Yahweh means entering into His covenantal protection. The master defends his servant from enemies and provides all necessary sustenance.

• Redemptive Freedom: Ironically, serving God is the path to true human freedom. Freedom in the biblical sense is not autonomy, but being liberated from the bondage of sin to serve the rightful Master. This is illustrated by Israel being redeemed from being servants to Pharaoh to become servants to Yahweh.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19, David transitions from contemplating God's glory in creation (verses 1–6) to praising the perfection of God's written law (verses 7–10). In verse 11, he brings these grand realities down to a deeply personal level: "Moreover by them Your servant is warned, and in keeping them there is great reward."

By calling himself "Your servant," David positions himself as one who stands ready to hear and obey his Master's voice. The "them" refers to the testimonies, statutes, and commandments of Yahweh.

The servant does not view God's law as a heavy, legalistic burden, but as gracious instructions designed for his protection ("warned") and flourishing ("great reward"). David's status as an 'ebed means he does not have the right to define his own truth; he submits to the boundary lines drawn by the Master's Word.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 24:34 - "So he said, 'I am Abraham’s servant.'"

• Joshua 1:1 - "After the death of Moses the servant of the LORD, it came to pass that the LORD spoke to Joshua the son of Nun, Moses’ assistant, saying:"

• Psalm 116:16 - "O LORD, truly I am Your servant; I am Your servant, the son of Your maidservant; You have loosed my bonds."

• Isaiah 42:1 - "Behold! My Servant whom I uphold, My Elect One in whom My soul delights! I have put My Spirit upon Him; He will bring forth justice to the Gentiles."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

The New Testament equivalent of 'ebed is doulos (bondservant/slave). When Paul, James, Peter, and Jude open their epistles by calling themselves "a bondservant of Jesus Christ," they are directly drawing upon the rich Old Testament heritage of the 'ebed Yahweh.

Furthermore, the ultimate fulfillment of the 'ebed is found in Jesus Christ, who took "the form of a bondservant" (Philippians 2:7) and perfectly fulfilled the "Servant of the Lord" prophecies in Isaiah, demonstrating that true greatness is found in humble service and obedience to the Father.
