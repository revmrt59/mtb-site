REWARD - ( eqeb , Strong's H6118 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word eqeb in Psalm 19:11 refers to the consequence, end-result, or recompense that naturally follows an action. Derived from a root word meaning "heel" or "footstep," it paints a vivid picture of something closely following at the heels of another. In the context of God's Word, eqeb reveals that obeying God's truth is not a path of empty duty but one that carries an inevitable, bountiful harvest of spiritual blessing, protection, and life. The "great reward" of keeping God's commandments is both the immediate peace of walking in alignment with the Creator and the ultimate eternal benefit of a life well-pleasing to Him.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun eqeb is deeply connected to the Hebrew root aqab, which means "to take by the heel" or "to supplant." It is directly related to the noun aqeb, which means "heel," "cloven hoof," or "footprint." In the ancient Near Eastern mind, concrete physical terms described abstract spiritual realities. Just as the heel of a foot closely and inevitably follows the sole of the foot with every stride, so does a consequence follow an action. The word picture behind eqeb is that of a result following "at the heels" of the behavior that produced it. When a person walks in obedience to God, the blessing is right behind them, following closely in their footsteps.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, eqeb denotes a consequence, recompense, end, or reward. As a preposition or conjunction, it functions to mean "because of," "on account of," or "consequently." The term does not primarily signify a commercial transaction or a wage earned through legalistic merit. Instead, it refers to the organic, logical, and inevitable outcome of a course of action. It highlights the structural connection between a deed and its moral result. In the context of Scripture, it points to the abundant spiritual and practical benefit that naturally flows from honoring the precepts of Yahweh.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Heel Metaphor: Because eqeb is rooted in the imagery of a heel following a footstep, it indicates that rewards are not detached or arbitrary. God does not hand out rewards like random prizes; rather, the reward is structurally bound to the act of obedience. To walk in God’s law is to walk on a path where blessing is the very next footprint.

• Covenantal Consequence: Throughout the Old Testament, the term is used to introduce the ultimate outcomes of covenantal faithfulness. God promises blessings "because" (eqeb) His servants obeyed His voice. The reward is the relational and physical fruit of a preserved covenant.

• The End-Result: The word is also translated as "the end" or "to the end." This suggests a final, ultimate outcome. The consequence of keeping the commandments is not merely temporary; it has an enduring, ultimate destination that satisfies the soul.

4. Theological Implications

• The Goodness of God's Law: The existence of a "great reward" (eqeb rab) proves that God's commandments are designed for human flourishing. They are not arbitrary restrictions meant to rob humanity of joy. Instead, they are protective guardrails that guide believers into life, joy, and peace.

• Grace and Recompense: While salvation is entirely of grace, God in His kindness has ordered the universe such that obedience yields fruit. The reward is "great" because of God's generous character, not because human obedience is flawless. The reward is an expression of God's delight in those who seek Him.

• Organic Sanctification: Holiness has its own built-in reward. The "heel" of keeping God's word is the immediate transformation of the inner man. To have a clean conscience, enlightened eyes, and a rejoiced heart are the immediate, organic rewards of walking in truth.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19, David contrasts general revelation (the heavens declaring God's glory in verses 1–6) with special revelation (the perfect law of the Lord in verses 7–11). After listing the majestic attributes and qualities of Scripture—it is perfect, sure, right, pure, clean, and true—David concludes by highlighting its highly personal value.

In verse 11, the word eqeb is paired with the Hebrew word rab, meaning "abundant" or "great." David notes that the scriptures warn God’s servant, and in guarding (shamar) them, there is a "great consequence." The contextual nuance of eqeb here is that the reward of keeping the law is the sum total of all the benefits listed in the previous verses: soul restoration, wisdom, heart-rejoicing, spiritual enlightenment, and eternal stability. The reward is not an external trophy; it is the deep, spiritual reality of a life restored and aligned with God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 26:5 - "because Abraham obeyed My voice and kept My charge, My commandments, My statutes, and My laws." (Here, eqeb is translated "because," highlighting the historical consequence of Abraham's obedience).

• Psalm 119:33 - "Teach me, O LORD, the way of Your statutes, And I shall keep it to the end." (Here, eqeb is translated "to the end," reflecting the ultimate, final consequence of a life of devotion).

• Psalm 119:112 - "I have inclined my heart to perform Your statutes Forever, to the very end." (Again, eqeb is translated "to the very end," showing the final, lasting outcome of a dedicated life).

• Proverbs 22:4 - "By humility and the fear of the LORD Are riches and honor and life." (Here, eqeb is translated "By," indicating the inevitable consequences that follow at the heels of humility and the fear of God).

7. Other Relevant Insights

• Contrast with Sakar: The Hebrew word sakar refers to wages, hire, or compensation for labor (often used in a commercial sense). By choosing eqeb in Psalm 19:11 instead of sakar, the psalmist steers clear of a transactional, mercenary view of obedience. We do not put God in our debt as employees earning wages. Rather, we receive the beautiful, relational, and organic consequences (eqeb) of walking in harmony with His perfect design.

• Pastoral Application: Pastors can use this word picture to encourage believers who feel that obedience is difficult or unnoticed. The heel must follow the foot; it is a physical impossibility for it to do otherwise. In the same way, spiritual blessing is spiritually bound to follow the footstep of obedience. Though the "heel" of blessing may not be immediately visible, it is trailing closely behind.
