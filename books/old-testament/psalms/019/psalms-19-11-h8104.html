KEEPING - ( shamar , Strong's H8104 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb shamar means to guard, watch, preserve, or protect. In the context of Psalm 19:11, it refers to the diligent, protective, and obedient observing of God's commands. It is not merely passive compliance, but an active, vigilant safeguarding of God's truth in one's life. Keeping God's word is portrayed not as a burdensome duty, but as a protective boundary that leads to profound spiritual safety and great reward.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The word shamar carries a vivid concrete imagery in ancient Hebrew. It is the action of a shepherd guarding a flock from predators at night, or a watchman standing on a city wall scanning the horizon for danger.

• It evokes the picture of building a protective hedge or fence around something of immense value to prevent it from being lost, damaged, or stolen.

• In the ancient agricultural mindset, to shamar was to exercise vigilant, protective care over territory, property, or people entrusted to one's charge.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The core meaning of shamar is to exercise great care over something. The semantic range includes:

• To guard or watch over (e.g., a garden, a city, or a flock).

• To preserve or keep safe (e.g., keeping one's life or soul).

• To observe or perform diligently (e.g., covenants, commandments, the Sabbath).

• To pay close attention or be on one's guard.

• In the context of God's law, it refers to the careful, intentional preservation of His commands in the heart and the active execution of them in daily life.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Garden Keepers: The word first appears in Genesis 2:15, where God places man in the Garden of Eden to "tend and keep (shamar) it." This establishes shamar as a sacred stewardship—protecting and maintaining what God has sanctified.

• The Priestly Guard: In the Tabernacle and Temple, the Levites were commanded to "keep" (shamar) the charge of the sanctuary, protecting the holy place from defilement.

• The Watchman's Vigilance: The noun form describes a watchman who keeps watch through the night, looking for the dawn.

• These pictures show that "keeping" God’s law is an act of high vigilance, treating God's truth as a sacred sanctuary that must be guarded against foreign defilement and neglect.

4. Theological Implications

• Keeping God's word is fundamentally tied to the covenant relationship. It is the human response of loyalty to God's self-revelation.

• True obedience is protective, not restrictive. By keeping the law, the believer is actually kept by the law. God’s commands are a protective hedge that shields the believer from the destructive consequences of sin.

• Shamar reveals that obedience requires active spiritual warfare. Truth is not maintained automatically; it must be guarded against the eroding influences of a fallen world and a deceitful heart.

• The ultimate "Keeper" is Yahweh Himself, who keeps Israel. When believers keep God's word, they reflect the character of the God who keeps them.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 19:7-11, David extols the perfection, surety, rightness, purity, and truth of God’s word. In verse 11, he writes, "Moreover by them Your servant is warned, And in keeping them there is great reward."

• The context contrasts the warning function of the law with the reward of keeping it. The warning alerts the believer to spiritual danger (like a watchman), and "keeping" (shamar) the statutes is the active response of staying within the safe, guarded path.

• The "great reward" (eqeb rab) is not a transactional payment or merit-based wage, but the organic outcome of living in harmony with God's design. The reward of keeping God's word is the preservation of life, soul-restoration, wisdom, joy, and spiritual sight.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:15 - "Then the Lord God took the man and put him in the garden of Eden to tend and keep it."

• Genesis 4:9 - "Then the Lord said to Cain, 'Where is Abel your brother?' He said, 'I do not know. Am I my brother’s keeper?'"

• Numbers 6:24 - "The Lord bless you and keep you;"

• Psalm 119:9 - "How can a young man cleanse his way? By taking heed [keeping guard] according to Your word."

• Psalm 121:4 - "Behold, He who keeps Israel Shall neither slumber nor sleep."

• Proverbs 4:23 - "Keep your heart with all diligence, For out of it spring the issues of life."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The relationship between God keeping us and us keeping His word is beautifully symmetrical in Scripture. In Psalm 121, the root shamar occurs six times, emphasizing God as our constant Keeper.

• In the New Testament, the Greek equivalent often used in the Septuagint (LXX) for shamar is tereo (to guard, keep, observe). This is the very word Jesus uses in John 14:15: "If you love Me, keep My commandments." Just like shamar, tereo links obedience directly to loving guardianship and covenant loyalty, rather than legalistic drudgery.
