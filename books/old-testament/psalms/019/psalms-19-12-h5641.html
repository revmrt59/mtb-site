SECRET - ( sathar , Strong's H5641 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb sathar means to hide, conceal, or cover. In Psalm 19:12, it appears as a feminine plural participle referring to "hidden things" or "secret faults"—sins that are concealed not only from the eyes of others but even from the sinner's own conscious awareness. This word challenges the believer to recognize the deep, often undetected layers of personal transgression that require the searching light of God's grace and truth for cleansing.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete imagery of sathar involves covering something over to keep it out of sight, or seeking shelter where one cannot be seen. In the ancient Near East, safety and secrecy were closely linked; to be hidden was to be protected or to keep something private. The word is frequently used of hiding from danger, hiding one's face in grief or anger, or concealing plans. In a spiritual sense, when applied to human behavior in Psalm 19:12, it depicts actions, motives, or errors that remain buried beneath the surface, out of the public square and even hidden from the self.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb sathar primarily means to hide, conceal, or cover closely.

• In the active stems, it denotes the action of hiding something or someone from view.

• In the passive Niphal stem (as seen in Psalm 19:12, nistaroth), it means to be hidden, to be concealed, or to hide oneself.

• In the causative Hiphil stem, it means to hide something from sight, to shield, or to cover.

• In Psalm 19:12, the feminine plural participle functions substantively to mean "hidden things," specifically referring to hidden or secret errors, strayings, or faults that escape human observation.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament employs sathar to construct several vivid word pictures:

• Hiding from Divine Presence: Cain laments that he will be hidden (sathar) from the face of God as a fugitive (Genesis 4:14).

• Divine Protection: God hides His people in the secret place (sether, a noun derived from sathar) of His presence to protect them from the plots of men (Psalm 31:20).

• Unseen Sins: In Psalm 19:12, the word picture is that of a secret chamber or a veiled corner of the heart where sins linger, unnoticed by the individual due to spiritual blind spots, ignorance, or self-deception.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of sathar in the context of human sin highlights the limitation of human self-evaluation.

• Omniscience vs. Human Blindness: Humans are limited in their ability to self-examine. We cannot fully see our own fallenness. God, however, sees what is hidden in the dark, exposing our need for His divine searchlight.

• The Nature of Sin: Sin is not merely conscious, deliberate rebellion (which is addressed in the next verse as "presumptuous sins"). Sin also includes unconscious straying, structural blind spots, and inherited corruption that we fail to notice.

• The Necessity of Grace: Because we cannot confess what we do not perceive, we need a broad, cleansing grace that covers even our unrecognized spiritual debts. Cleanliness before God requires a deep purgation that goes deeper than our own self-awareness.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Psalm 19 is divided into two halves: God's revelation in creation (verses 1–6) and God's revelation in His perfect Law (verses 7–11). In verse 12, the psalmist shifts from praising the perfection of the Law to examining his own heart in light of that Law. The perfection of the Law exposes human imperfection.

The question "Who can understand his errors?" implies that the human heart is too complex to fully comprehend its own drift. Therefore, the psalmist cries out, "Cleanse me from secret faults" (nistaroth). These "secret" faults are contrasted with the "presumptuous" (deliberate, defiant) sins in verse 13. The nuance here is of inadvertent, unrecognized, or forgotten sins that still pollute the soul and disrupt fellowship with God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Deuteronomy 29:29 - "The secret things belong to the LORD our God, but those things which are revealed belong to us and to our children forever..."

• Proverbs 22:3 - "A prudent man foresees evil and hides himself, But the simple pass on and are punished."

• Isaiah 45:15 - "Truly You are God, who hide Yourself, O God of Israel, the Savior!"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Septuagint (LXX) translates nistaroth in Psalm 19:12 with the Greek word kryphios, which refers to hidden or inward things. This emphasizes the internal, concealed nature of these faults.

Pastorally, there is a danger in focusing only on known, gross sins while ignoring the subtle, hidden undercurrents of the heart—such as pride, bitterness, or self-righteousness—which often masquerade as virtues or remain hidden from our awareness. True spiritual maturity involves inviting God to search the hidden depths (Psalm 139:23–24).
