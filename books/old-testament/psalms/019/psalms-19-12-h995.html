UNDERSTAND - ( biyn , Strong's H995 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb biyn conveys more than mere intellectual comprehension; it denotes a deep, experiential discernment, the ability to distinguish between options, and the capacity to perceive the true nature of a matter. In Psalm 19:12, this word highlights the profound limitation of human self-awareness. Amidst the perfection of God's law, the psalmist realizes that human beings cannot fully discern, detect, or comprehend the depth of their own errors and hidden sins. True understanding of one's spiritual state requires divine illumination, as our self-perception is naturally clouded by blind spots.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The root of biyn is closely related to the preposition bên, which means "between" or "among." The literal word picture is that of "discerning between" or "distinguishing between" two things. It implies a process of separation, classification, and careful observation. Historically and culturally, it suggests the skill of a craftsman or a judge who looks at a complex situation and separates truth from falsehood, clean from unclean, or wisdom from folly. It is not passive knowing; it is active, analytical perception that sees through outward appearances to the core reality.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meaning of biyn is to discern, perceive, understand, consider, or behave wisely. It ranges from basic mental perception to the teaching of understanding to others. The core lexical definition involves:

• Distinguishing or discriminating between things.

• Perceiving or grasping the deeper meaning of a matter.

• Having insight or understanding.

• Paying close attention or giving heed.

Unlike yada (which often emphasizes relational or experiential knowledge), biyn emphasizes the cognitive and analytical discernment of structure, relationships, and moral distinctions. It is the capacity to put clues together and make a correct assessment.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

In the Old Testament, biyn is frequently paired with wisdom (chokmah) and knowledge (da'at). It is used of Solomon's request for an "understanding heart" to discern (biyn) between good and evil (1 Kings 3:9). It describes the Levites' action of helping the people of Israel understand (biyn) the Law of God during the post-exilic reformation, turning a foreign text into clear, actionable truth (Nehemiah 8:8). The word picture is that of a pathfinder who can distinguish the right path from the numerous deceptive trails, or a winnower who separates the wheat from the chaff.

### 4. Theological Implications

• Human limitation in self-assessment: Biyn applied to oneself reveals that human self-knowledge is highly flawed. Jeremiah 17:9 speaks of the deceitful heart; Psalm 19:12 uses biyn to show that we cannot fully map or understand our own deviations from God's standard.

• The perfection of God's Word vs. human blindness: The first half of Psalm 19 extols the perfection, surety, and purity of God's law. This perfect standard acts as a spiritual mirror, exposing the fact that our sins are often hidden from our own perception.

• The necessity of divine revelation and cleansing: Because we cannot understand our own errors, we must rely on God to cleanse us from "secret faults"—those sins we commit without even realizing them. True spiritual discernment is ultimately a gift from God, not an inherent human capability.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19:12, David asks the rhetorical question, "Who can understand his errors?" The implied answer is "No one." The word "errors" (shegi'ot) refers to inadvertent sins, wanderings, or mistakes made through ignorance or weakness, rather than defiant rebellion (pesha). By using biyn here, David confesses that his capacity to discern and categorize his own moral failings is severely limited. He cannot look into his own soul and accurately trace every deviation from the perfect law of God. This drives the psalmist to structural dependence on Yahweh: since he cannot understand (discern) his errors, he must ask God to cleanse him from those "secret" (unseen, unrecognized) faults.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Nehemiah 8:8 - "So they read distinctly from the book, in the Law of God; and they gave the sense, and helped them to understand the reading."

• Psalm 119:104 - "Through Your precepts I get understanding; Therefore I hate every false way."

• Proverbs 2:5 - "Then you will understand the fear of the LORD, And find the knowledge of God."

• Proverbs 29:19 - "A servant will not be corrected by words; For though he understands, he will not respond."

• Daniel 9:23 - "At the beginning of your supplications the command went out, and I have come to tell you, for you are greatly beloved; therefore consider the matter, and understand the vision."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• The relationship between biyn and spiritual maturity: Discernment is not a passive trait but a cultivated discipline. In the Wisdom Literature, biyn is a goal to be pursued actively through prayer and study of the Scriptures.

• The New Testament parallel: The Greek equivalent of this concept is often diakrisis (discernment) or syniemi (to understand/connect the pieces). Just as the Old Testament believer needed God's help to biyn their heart, the New Testament believer relies on the Holy Spirit to search the deep things of God and convict of sin (1 Corinthians 2:10-11).
