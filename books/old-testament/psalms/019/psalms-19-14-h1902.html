MEDITATION - ( higgayon , Strong's H1902 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun higgayon refers to the internal whispering, deep musing, or quiet murmuring of the heart. Derived from a root meaning to mutter or growl, it describes a continuous, focused contemplation that shapes a person's inner life. In worship, it can also signify the resonant, solemn sound of a stringed instrument. In Psalm 19:14, it represents the quiet, unseen thoughts and motives of the soul, which the psalmist desires to be as pleasing to God as audible praise and sacrificial offerings.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word higgayon derives from the root verb hagah, which means to moan, mutter, growl, or speak. It describes a low, continuous sound, such as a lion growling over its prey or a dove cooing in distress. From this background, higgayon denotes the mental or physical act of repeating thoughts quietly to oneself. It is not an emptying of the mind, but a filling of the mind, mimicking the process of whispering truth to one's own soul.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Higgayon translates as meditation, whispering, plot, or a resounding musical sound. Its semantic range covers the internal monologue of the mind, the quiet murmuring of the mouth, the malicious plotting of enemies, and the resonant tones of stringed instruments. In every use, the word indicates a focused, continuous, and repetitive expression of inner thoughts or feelings.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The term illustrates a beautiful connection between music and thought. Just as a musician coaxes a deep, resonant tone from a harp to create a contemplative atmosphere, so the human heart plays a quiet, continuous melody of thoughts before God. It stands in contrast to passive thinking; it is active intellectual chewing on divine truth, translating knowledge into deep-seated convictions.

4. Theological Implications

God does not merely judge human behavior; He evaluates the hidden whispers of the inner person. The heart is the processing center of human devotion, and its quiet meditations are spiritually active. What a person mutters to themselves in the secret chambers of the mind shapes their spiritual condition and serves as a continuous offering to God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In the context of Psalm 19, David has just detailed the perfection of God’s law, testimonies, statutes, and commandments. Having recognized the searching light of Scripture, he is humbled by his own hidden faults and presumptuous sins. He concludes with a prayer that his external speech ("words of my mouth") and his internal, silent whispering ("meditation of my heart") would be acceptable to God. The word "acceptable" (ratson) refers to a pleasing sacrificial offering. David presents his inner thoughts as a spiritual sacrifice, a sweet-smelling incense rising from a heart perfectly tuned to God's truth.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 9:16 - "The LORD is known by the judgment He executes; The wicked is snared in the work of his own hands. Higgaion. Selah"

• Psalm 92:3 - "On an instrument of ten strings, On the lute, And on the harp, With a solemn sound."

• Lamentations 3:62 - "The lips of my enemies And their whispering against me all the day."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Septuagint translates higgayon in Psalm 19:14 as melete, a Greek word that means practice, exercise, or careful training. This shows that the early translators viewed meditation not as a static state of relaxation, but as a disciplined mental workout. Furthermore, the musical association in other psalms indicates that biblical meditation is meant to be expressive, emotional, and integrated with artistic worship.
