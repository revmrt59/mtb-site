HEART - ( leb , Strong's H3820 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word leb, translated as "heart" in Psalm 19:14, represents the inner core of human personality, encompassing the mind, will, emotions, and moral consciousness. Rather than referring strictly to the physical organ or raw feelings, leb denotes the intentional, reflective center of a person. In Psalm 19:14, the psalmist petitions God that the silent, private meditations of his heart—the unseen thoughts and deepest motivations—be just as pure and acceptable to God as his spoken words.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient Near Eastern and biblical thought, the heart (leb) was understood as the steering wheel of human existence. While modern culture views the heart primarily as the seat of subjective emotions and the brain as the center of intellect, the Hebrew mind made no such division. The leb was the seat of the intellect, the will, the emotions, and the conscience. It was the architectural center of a person's life, where thoughts were formulated, decisions were made, and relationship with God was either nurtured or rejected. It represents the "inner man" in its entirety.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun leb carries a comprehensive range of meanings that describe the internal life:

• The Mind and Intellect: The faculty of thinking, understanding, and processing knowledge. To "speak in the heart" is to think.

• The Will and Volition: The source of intentions, plans, determinations, and decisions.

• The Seat of Emotions: The origin of joy, sorrow, courage, fear, love, and hate.

• The Moral and Spiritual Center: The place where moral character is formed and where God's law is either written or resisted.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Scripture uses leb in several vivid metaphorical ways to describe the spiritual state of humanity:

• The Wellspring of Life: The heart is described as a wellspring or fountain from which all the actions of life flow. What is inside the heart inevitably spills out into a person's behavior.

• The Tablet: The heart is a tablet upon which instructions, loyalty, or sin can be deeply engraved.

• Hard vs. Soft Heart: A stubborn, unyielding spirit is depicted as a "heart of stone," whereas a receptive, responsive spirit is a "heart of flesh."

• The Silent Sanctuary: The heart acts as a private chamber where a person "meditates" or whispers to themselves, formulating their truest desires away from the public eye.

### 4. Theological Implications

The theology of the leb underscores that God's primary concern is with the internal reality of a person rather than external performance.

• The Heart is Deceitful: Because of the Fall, the human heart is inherently sick, deceptive, and beyond human self-cure.

• The Target of Divine Evaluation: Yahweh is uniquely characterized as the one who searches, tests, and weighs the heart. External religious compliance does not deceive Him; He looks directly at the leb.

• The Seat of Regeneration: Spiritual transformation must begin at the heart level. True covenant relationship involves God writing His law on the heart and giving His people a undivided heart to fear Him.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage (Psalm 19:14)

In Psalm 19, David marvels at God’s revelation in the heavens (vv. 1–6) and the perfect, soul-restoring power of God’s written Word (vv. 7–11). Recognizing the purity of God's law, David is humbled and prays for cleansing from hidden faults and willful sins (vv. 12–13).

This culminates in the petition of verse 14: "Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be acceptable in Your sight..." Here, "meditation" (higgayon) refers to the silent, internal whispering or murmuring of thoughts. David pairs the outward "words of my mouth" with the inward "meditation of my heart" (leb). He desires that his private, unspoken thought-life, his motives, and his secret desires—the activity of his leb—be as thoroughly aligned with God's holiness as his public worship. True integrity is the perfect harmony between the mouth's profession and the heart's hidden meditation before the LORD, who is his strength and redeemer.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Proverbs 4:23 - "Keep your heart with all diligence, For out of it spring the issues of life."

• Psalm 51:10 - "Create in me a clean heart, O God, And renew a steadfast spirit within me."

• Joel 2:13 - "So rend your heart, and not your garments; Return to the LORD your God, For He is gracious and merciful, Slow to anger, and of great kindness; And He relents from doing harm."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

A critical pastoral insight is the distinction between biblical self-examination and modern self-actualization. While the modern world encourages people to "follow your heart" (treating the heart's desires as the ultimate moral compass), Scripture warns that the leb must be guided, guarded, and governed by God's Word. Because the heart is the seat of the intellect and will, "guarding" the heart is not merely about managing emotions; it is a call to rigorous cognitive and moral discipline, choosing what we allow our minds to dwell upon.
