STRENGTH - ( tsur , Strong's H6697 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word tsur primarily refers to a massive rock, cliff, or boulder, symbolizing immobility, safety, and enduring stability. When applied to God, as in Psalm 19:14, it conveys His role as an unwavering refuge, a secure foundation, and an unchanging source of strength for the believer. In this closing prayer of Psalm 19, the psalmist appeals to Yahweh not merely as an abstract deity, but as his personal, protective stronghold who enables him to live a life pleasing in His sight.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete imagery of tsur is deeply rooted in the rugged, arid landscape of the ancient Near East. A tsur is not a small stone or pebble, but a sharp, massive rock formation, mountain wall, or towering boulder. In a desert environment, these massive stone structures provided:

• Physical shade from the scorching sun.

• Natural high ground and fortresses for defense against pursuing enemies.

• A firm, immovable foundation that could not be shaken by flash floods or fierce windstorms.

This vivid geological reality is imported into the spiritual realm to describe the character of God. He is not a shifting sand dune or a fragile human shelter; He is the massive, unyielding Rock of His people.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Literal Meaning: A rock, cliff, boulder, or sharp stone.

• Metaphorical Meaning: A refuge, defense, stronghold, or source of ultimate security and strength.

• Theological Meaning: An epithet or title for God, emphasizing His unchangeableness, reliability, covenant faithfulness, and protective power.

• The semantic range spans from physical geological structures to the spiritual reality of God as the origin (the quarry from which a people are hewn) and the active deliverer of His people.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Rock of Salvation: Often paired with deliverance, depicting God as the high ground where the believer is lifted far out of the enemy’s reach.

• The Shadow of a Great Rock: Used to describe refreshing relief, shelter, and preservation in a weary, hostile land.

• The Unshakable Foundation: A secure place to stand when the surrounding earth, circumstances, and human kingdoms fail and shake.

• The Sharp Stone / Flint: Historically used to describe sharp cutting tools (like flint knives), signifying sharpness, decisiveness, and preparation.

### 4. Theological Implications

• The Immutability of God: Rocks do not easily erode, bend, or shift. Identifying God as tsur declares His unchanging character, absolute reliability, and eternal consistency across generations.

• The God of Personal Security: Seeking refuge in the tsur implies human vulnerability. It highlights that human beings are weak and need an external, proactive sanctuary. God does not merely offer strength; He is the Strength.

• Monotheistic Uniqueness: Scripture frequently contrasts the living God as the true Rock with the false "rocks" (gods and protective systems) of the surrounding pagan nations, demonstrating that only Yahweh provides real, enduring security.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19:14, the NKJV translates tsur as "strength" ("O LORD, my strength and my Redeemer").

• This verse serves as the grand climax of Psalm 19. The psalm transitions from the glory of God in creation (verses 1–6) to the perfection of God’s written law (verses 7–11), and finally to a deeply personal plea for inner cleansing and uprightness (verses 12–14).

• By addressing God as "my tsur" (my Rock/Strength), the psalmist confesses that keeping his words and the inner meditations of his heart acceptable to God is impossible through human willpower alone.

• To resist "presumptuous sins" and remain blameless, the believer requires an external, immovable stabilizing force. God is addressed not as a distant judge, but as a personal, protective stronghold who actively holds the believer upright against the erosion of sin.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Deuteronomy 32:4: "He is the Rock, His work is perfect; For all His ways are justice, A God of truth and without injustice; Righteous and upright is He."

• 2 Samuel 22:3: "The God of my strength, in whom I will trust; My shield and the horn of my salvation, My stronghold and my refuge; My Savior, You save me from violence."

• Psalm 18:2: "The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer; My God, my strength, in whom I will trust; My shield and the horn of my salvation, my stronghold."

• Psalm 62:2: "He only is my rock and my salvation; He is my defense; I shall not be greatly moved."

• Isaiah 26:4: "Trust in the LORD forever, For in YAH, the LORD, is everlasting strength."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament) frequently translates tsur in theological contexts using words like boethos (helper) or phylax (guardian/protector), rather than the literal Greek word petra (rock). This indicates that ancient translators understood the term as a functional metaphor for active, personal deliverance and assistance, beautifully justifying the NKJV translation of "strength" in Psalm 19:14.

• In the New Testament, this Old Testament rock imagery culminates in Jesus Christ, who is described as the spiritual Rock (petra) that sustained Israel in the wilderness (1 Corinthians 10:4) and the chief cornerstone upon which the Church is built.
