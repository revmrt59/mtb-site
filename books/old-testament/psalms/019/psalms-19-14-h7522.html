ACCEPTABLE - ( ratson , Strong's H7522 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun ratson denotes pleasure, favor, goodwill, acceptance, or desire. Derived from the verb ratsah (to be pleased with, to accept favorably), it expresses the state of being highly pleasing or delighting to another, particularly to God. In the context of worship and devotion, it refers to that which meets God's holy standards and receives His joyful approval, transitioning from a legal or sacrificial acceptance to an intimate, relational pleasure.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The background of ratson is deeply rooted in both royal court etiquette and the ancient Israelite sacrificial system. In the context of a royal court, ratson carries the imagery of a king's shining face, indicating favor, goodwill, and pleasure rather than anger or rejection. To stand in the ratson of a monarch meant enjoying unchecked access, protection, and delight. In the sacrificial system, ratson describes an offering that is deemed acceptable and pleasing to Yahweh. When a sacrifice was brought according to God's instructions, it was received with ratson—meaning the barrier of sin was covered and the relationship between God and the worshiper was harmoniously restored.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Ratson means goodwill, favor, acceptance, desire, or pleasure. It stems from the root verb ratsah, which means to be pleased with, to find delight in, or to receive favorably. Lexically, the noun defines either the subjective state of favor (God's goodwill toward His people) or the objective state of being acceptable (a gift, prayer, or person that is pleasing to God). It describes that which is well-pleasing because it perfectly aligns with the character and desires of the one receiving it.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Sacrificial Acceptance: Ratson is used frequently in the Levitical law to designate sacrifices that are acceptable to God. The offering must be presented in a way that secures God's favor and pleasure, reflecting a heart of obedience and conformity to His standards.

• Sovereign Favor: The word is used to describe the favor of a earthly king, which brings life and joy, as well as God's sovereign goodwill toward His covenant people, which acts as a shield of protection.

• Personal Desire or Will: It denotes the inner desire, pleasure, or delight of an individual. When applied to God, it refers to His perfect, pleasing will. When applied to humans, it can refer to their self-will (sometimes in a negative, rebellious sense) or their delight in God.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, ratson demonstrates that God is not an impersonal force but a personal Being who experiences delight and pleasure. God is not merely concerned with external conformity; He seeks an affectionate, authentic relationship with His creation. True worship goes beyond ritual correctness to capture the heart of God. Furthermore, acceptance with God is ultimately a gift of His sovereign favor (ratson), yet it calls for a response. Believers are invited to live lives that actively please Him, presenting their thoughts, words, and deeds as living sacrifices that align with His holy character.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19, David transitions from contemplating the majestic glory of God in creation (verses 1–6) and the perfect, soul-restoring holiness of God's written Law (verses 7–11) to a deeply personal petition for inner cleansing (verses 12–14). Recognizing that God's law exposes hidden faults and presumptuous sins, David closes with a prayer: "Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be acceptable (ratson) in Your sight." Here, David uses a sacrificial metaphor. He does not bring a physical animal to the altar; instead, he offers his inner thoughts (meditations) and outward speech (words) as a sacrifice. He prays that his entire inner and outer life might be as spotless, well-pleasing, and acceptable to God as a perfect burnt offering.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 40:8 - "I delight to do Your will, O my God, And Your law is within my heart."

• Proverbs 16:15 - "In the light of the king’s face is life, And his favor is like a cloud of the latter rain."

• Psalm 145:19 - "He will fulfill the desire of those who fear Him; He also will hear their cry and save them."

• Isaiah 60:7 - "All the flocks of Kedar shall be gathered together to you, The rams of Nebaioth shall minister to you; They shall ascend with acceptance on My altar, And I will glorify the house of My glory."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Old Testament concept of ratson shifts beautifully into the New Testament through the Greek equivalent euarestos (well-pleasing, acceptable). In Christ, believers are permanently accepted in the Beloved (Ephesians 1:6). Because of this secure position of favor, New Testament believers are exhorted to present their bodies as "a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable (euarestos) to God, which is your reasonable service" (Romans 12:1). David’s prayer in Psalm 19:14 anticipates this New Covenant reality, where the ultimate altar of worship is the human heart, and the ultimate goal of the believer is to please the Lord in thought, word, and deed.
