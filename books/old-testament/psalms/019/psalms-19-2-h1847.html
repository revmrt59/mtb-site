KNOWLEDGE - ( da'at , Strong's H1847 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun da'at denotes much more than intellectual apprehension or the mere accumulation of facts. Derived from the root verb yada (to know), it represents an experiential, relational, and active understanding. In the context of Psalm 19:2, da'at refers to the ongoing, non-verbal revelation of God’s glory, wisdom, and creative power made manifest through the night sky. This knowledge is not silent speculation but a dynamic, constant declaration of the Creator's character, visible to all humanity and demanding a response of worship.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun da'at is rooted in the biblical concept of experiential knowledge. Unlike the classical Greek concept of knowledge, which emphasizes abstract contemplation and theoretical logic, the Hebrew concept of knowing is intensely practical, relational, and experiential. To know something or someone is to experience them directly, to enter into a relationship with them, and to act in accordance with that reality. It involves the mind, the will, and the heart. In the design of the cosmos, the cyclical patterns of day and night serve as continuous, silent preachers. The night sky acts as a canvas that displays a profound, structured, and deep acquaintance with the majesty of the Creator.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Da'at carries several layers of meaning depending on its literary context:

• Cognitive awareness and perception of truth.

• Experiential acquaintance with a person, covenant, or reality.

• Practical skill, discernment, and wisdom applied to daily life.

• Spiritual and moral discernment, specifically the capacity to distinguish between good and evil, and the personal recognition of Yahweh's authority and character.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

In Hebrew poetry and wisdom literature, da'at is frequently personified and paired with terms like wisdom (chokmah) and understanding (tebunah). It is described as a treasure to be searched out, a shield for the soul, and something sweet to the taste. In Psalm 19:2, the word is positioned in a striking parallel structure. While the day "utters speech" (literally gushes or bubbles forth with vocal praise), the night "reveals knowledge." The Hebrew verb for "reveals" (chavah) means to breathe out, declare, or show. The night sky does not merely exist in cold silence; it actively breathes out a deep, relational, and structured knowledge of God’s wisdom to anyone who looks upward.

4. Theological Implications

• Universal General Revelation: God has written His testimony into the fabric of creation. The da'at revealed by the night sky is accessible to all people, regardless of their language, geography, or culture. This constant communication leaves humanity without excuse regarding the existence and eternal power of God.

• Creation’s Intimate Witness: The use of da'at implies that the universe possesses an intimate "acquaintance" with its Creator. The stars, moon, and galaxies obey His laws and, in doing so, exhibit His mind and character to the earth.

• The Call to Relationship: Because biblical knowledge is relational, the knowledge revealed by creation is not intended to satisfy intellectual curiosity alone. It is designed to evoke wonder, humility, and a desire to know the Creator personally.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Psalm 19 is divided into two distinct movements: God's revelation in the cosmos (verses 1–6) and God's revelation in the written Torah (verses 7–11). In verse 2, the quietness of the night is contrasted with the bright, active energy of the day. The day "pours forth speech" like a rushing stream, while the night, in its solemn grandeur, whispers a deep da'at. This implies that certain aspects of God's character—His vastness, infinite detail, order, and majesty—are best contemplated and received in the quiet, reflective hours of the night. It is a silent sermon that speaks directly to the human spirit.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:9: "And out of the ground the LORD God made every tree grow that is pleasant to the sight and good for food. The tree of life was also in the midst of the garden, and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil."

• Proverbs 1:7: "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge, / But fools despise wisdom and instruction."

• Proverbs 2:6: "For the LORD gives wisdom; / From His mouth come knowledge and understanding;"

• Hosea 4:6: "My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge. / Because you have rejected knowledge, / I also will reject you from being priest for Me; / Because you have forgotten the law of your God, / I also will forget your children."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A clear distinction must be made between the biblical concept of da'at and ancient Gnosticism or modern secular intellectualism. Modernity often treats knowledge as a commodity to be acquired for personal advancement or control, completely separated from morality. In contrast, Scripture teaches that true da'at is inseparable from the fear of the Lord and ethical living. To reject the knowledge of God in creation or Scripture leads to spiritual and moral ruin, as noted in Hosea and Romans. Therefore, the knowledge breathed out by the night sky in Psalm 19:2 is a call to moral alignment and worshipful submission to the Sovereign King of the universe.
