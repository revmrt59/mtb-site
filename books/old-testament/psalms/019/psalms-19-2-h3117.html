DAY - ( yôm , Strong's H3117 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word yôm represents a fundamental unit of time, most commonly referring to the warm, daylight hours or a literal twenty-four-hour cycle. In Psalm 19:2, it is personified alongside "night" as an untiring herald of God's glory. Day after day, the daylight hours pour forth continuous, abundant testimony of the Creator's power and wisdom, establishing a perpetual, silent, yet universal sermon that leaves humanity without excuse.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near Eastern world, the daily cycle of day and night was the most visible and reliable rhythm of existence. The rising of the sun brought light, warmth, and the revival of human activity, contrasting sharply with the cold, dark, and vulnerable hours of the night. For the Hebrew mind, this orderly succession was not a random cosmological accident, but a direct result of God's sovereign design established at creation. The day represents clarity, visibility, and the active unfolding of God's providential care over the earth.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun yôm has a broad semantic range defined by its context:

• Daylight: The period of light from sunrise to sunset, contrasted directly with night (laylah).

• A twenty-four-hour cycle: The complete unit of time, defined in the creation week as consisting of "evening and morning."

• A specific point in time or historical epoch: A designated season, year, or era (such as "the day of the Lord" or "the day of trouble").

• A lifetime: Plural usage (yāmîm) often refers to a person's life span or "years."

In Psalm 19:2, the word refers to the successive, recurring daylight periods that govern human existence.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The psalmist employs a vivid personification in Psalm 19:2: "Day unto day utters speech." Here, yôm is depicted as a member of a cosmic relay team. The phrase "day unto day" (yôm lə-yôm) pictures one day handing off the torch of testimony to the next day. The verb translated "utters" (nāba‘) carries the word picture of a bubbling spring or a fountain gushing forth water. The day does not merely whisper; it gushes with evidence of God's creative genius. Each new sunrise initiates a fresh, overflowing flood of testimony regarding the glory, power, and wisdom of God.

4. Theological Implications

The continuous succession of yôm reveals several major theological truths:

• The Faithfulness of God: The uninterrupted arrival of each day is a physical confirmation of God’s covenant with creation (Jeremiah 33:20). He sustains the universe with absolute reliability.

• General Revelation: God has not left Himself without a witness. The physical creation speaks constantly. This natural revelation is universal, non-verbal, and accessible to every human being on earth, serving as a continuous witness to the Creator.

• Human Responsibility: Because the day continuously "utters speech," humanity is exposed to the reality of the Creator every single day. This constant testimony renders mankind accountable to respond to the true God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Within the structure of Psalm 19, there is a intentional transition from the silent sermon of creation (verses 1-6) to the perfect, vocal instruction of the written Word of God (verses 7-11). In verse 2, the use of yôm emphasizes the constancy of general revelation. While a human preacher must eventually rest and stop speaking, the "day" never stops preaching. The daytime sky—with the sun traversing its path like a bridegroom or a strong runner—declares the majesty of God. The nuance is one of relentless, beautiful, and inescapable proclamation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 1:5: "God called the light Day, and the darkness He called Night. So the evening and the morning were the first day."

• Joshua 1:8: "This Book of the Law shall not depart from your mouth, but you shall meditate in it day and night..."

• Psalm 118:24: "This is the day the Lord has made; We will rejoice and be glad in it."

• Joel 2:11: "The Lord gives voice before His army, For His camp is very great; For strong is the One who executes His word. For the day of the Lord is great and very terrible; Who can endure it?"

7. Other Relevant Insights

A beautiful contrast exists between "day" (yôm) and "night" (laylah) in Psalm 19:2. While the day "utters speech" (representing active, bright, and visible testimony), the night "reveals knowledge" (representing the quiet, deep, and contemplative majesty of the star-lit sky). Together, they form a perfect, twenty-four-hour cycle of praise. Creation has no "off-hours" in its worship of the Creator; the testimony is perpetual.
