UTTERS - ( naba , Strong's H5042 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word naba captures the vivid image of a bubbling spring or a gushing fountain that continuously pours forth water. In Psalm 19:2, this term describes how the creation "utters" or pours out an unstoppable, overflowing testimony of God's glory day after day. Rather than offering a silent or static display, the natural world acts as an active fountain, constantly bubbling over with revelations of its Creator's majesty, power, and wisdom.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete imagery behind naba is rooted in the arid geography of the ancient Near East, where a bubbling spring or natural fountain was a highly prized source of life, energy, and constant renewal. Unlike a stagnant cistern, a spring fed by an underground source cannot help but bubble up and spill over. It is dynamic, unstoppable, and fresh. When applied metaphorically to speech or proclamation, naba represents an irrepressible outpouring of words. The speaker is so full of a message that it bursts forth like water gushing from a newly opened well.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb naba means to bubble up, flow, pour forth, or emit a continuous stream. In its causative (Hiphil) form, which is used in Psalm 19:2, the word transitions from the physical action of water to the verbal action of communication. It means to cause to bubble up, to pour out speech, or to send forth a continuous stream of declaration. It implies an abundant, enthusiastic, and ceaseless flow of expression rather than a measured, hesitant, or dry delivery.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, naba is used to describe both physical and metaphorical gushing:

• Physical Gushing: It describes the constant, refreshing flow of water from a clean source, symbolizing life-giving wisdom.

• Spiritual/Positive Pouring: It denotes the spontaneous bubbling over of praise, worship, and the proclamation of God's acts. The heart of the worshiper becomes a spring that cannot contain its joy.

• Negative Gushing: Conversely, it is used to describe the unchecked, reckless flow of folly or wickedness from the mouths of fools. Just as a spring pours out what is at its source, an evil heart gushes forth corrupt speech.

### 4. Theological Implications

The use of naba in the context of creation reveals several profound truths about how God makes Himself known:

• The Continuous Nature of General Revelation: God's testimony in creation is not a historical event that occurred once and then fell silent. It is a daily, hourly, perpetual fountain. The heavens never stop "bubbling over" with the news of God's glory.

• The Abundance of God's Self-Disclosure: God does not reveal Himself in nature in a hidden, stingy, or obscure manner. The revelation is generous, overflowing, and rich, leaving humanity without excuse.

• The Spontaneous Praise of Creation: The cosmos operates as a cosmic choir. Creation cannot help but declare the handiwork of its Architect; it is hardwired to bubble over with praise.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19:2, the psalmist pairs "day unto day utters (naba) speech" with "night unto night reveals (chavah) knowledge." This parallel structure contrasts the daytime witness with the nighttime witness. While the night quietly whispers and "reveals" the vast, silent depths of the cosmos, the day aggressively and exuberantly "gushes" (naba) with light, color, life, and warmth. The daytime sky pours forth a relentless torrent of speech. This verbal outpouring is universal; though it is a non-verbal voice (v. 3), its "line has gone out through all the earth" (v. 4). The choosing of naba highlights the active, energetic, and irrepressible nature of the daytime testimony.

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

The concept of naba shares a close conceptual and linguistic relationship with the Hebrew word for a prophet (nabi). While etymologically distinct in their direct roots, both words convey the idea of being a conduit through whom a message bubbles up and pours forth. A prophet is not merely someone who speaks their own thoughts, but someone whose mouth overflows with the words of God, much like a spring overflowing with water. For the preacher or teacher, naba serves as a powerful reminder of what biblically faithful preaching should look like: an overflowing stream of truth fed by the deep, internal wellspring of the Holy Spirit and the Word of God.
