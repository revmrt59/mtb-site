SPEECH - ( omer, Strong's H562 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word omer refers to speech, utterance, words, or a saying. In the context of God's creation, it represents the continuous, non-verbal declaration of God's glory poured out by the heavens. Unlike human language, this silent yet powerful "speech" is universal, transcending geographical and linguistic barriers to testify of the Creator's existence, power, and majesty to all mankind.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The root behind the noun omer is the primary Hebrew verb amar, which means "to say," "to speak," or "to command." This root is used thousands of times throughout the Old Testament, most famously in the creation account of Genesis 1, where God speaks the universe into existence. As a noun, omer captures the actual content of what is spoken—an utterance, a word, or a formal declaration. In the ancient Near East, speech was not considered a fleeting sound, but a concrete extension of the speaker's personal power and character. Therefore, the omer of creation is the active, ongoing broadcast of God's own character and wisdom written across the cosmos.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Omer denotes a saying, speech, word, promise, or plan. It represents the communication of a message, whether through audible human words or figurative, non-verbal expressions.

• It refers to spoken words or verbal commands that carry authority.

• It signifies the message or testimony delivered by a speaker.

• In poetic literature, it is used to describe the silent, non-verbal "testimony" of the natural world, which communicates knowledge about God to all observers.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Gushing Fountain of Speech: In Psalm 19:2, omer is paired with the Hebrew verb naba, translated as "utters." This verb literally means to bubble up, pour forth, or gush like a fresh spring of water. The picture is of creation continuously bubbling over with speech. It is an unstoppable, refreshing torrent of divine testimony that never runs dry.

• Silent Proclamation: In Psalm 19:3, omer is contrasted with audible human language. Creation’s omer is a silent voice, yet its message "goes out through all the earth." It is a visual, cosmic language that speaks directly to the human heart without relying on phonetics or translating devices.

• The Sovereign Decree: In Psalm 68:11, omer is the victorious command of God ("The Lord gave the word") that rallies His people and guarantees their triumph over enemies.

### 4. Theological Implications

• General Revelation: The use of omer in Psalm 19 establishes that God has not left Himself without a witness. Creation actively preaches a sermon every second of every day. This continuous "speech" leaves humanity without excuse because the message of God's glory is clearly communicated to all.

• Relentless Communication: The grammatical structure of Psalm 19:2 indicates ongoing, uninterrupted action. Day after day, night after night, the "speech" of creation continues. It is a relentless, faithful witness to God's covenant faithfulness and eternal power.

• The Power of the Word: Just as God's spoken word (amar) originally brought the world into being, the world itself continues to speak (omer) of its Creator. There is an inseparable link between God's voice, His creation, and His written Word.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19, David divides the psalm into two primary revelations of God: General Revelation through creation (verses 1–6) and Special Revelation through the Law (verses 7–11).

• The word omer in verse 2 highlights the continuous, abundant nature of creation's testimony. The daytime sky (sunlight) and the nighttime canopy (stars) take turns in an unending relay race, passing the baton of this divine omer to one another.

• In verse 3, the nuance shifts to clarify that this omer is not characterized by human language or audible voices. It is a "voiceless speech" that is globally understood. It bridges every cultural and linguistic divide, making the knowledge of God universally accessible.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 19:2 – "Day unto day utters speech, And night unto night reveals knowledge."

• Psalm 19:3 – "There is no speech nor language Where their voice is not heard."

• Psalm 68:11 – "The Lord gave the word; Great was the company of those who proclaimed it."

• Job 22:28 – "You will also declare a thing, And it will be established for you; So light will shine on your ways."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• In Romans 10:18, the Apostle Paul quotes the Greek Septuagint translation of Psalm 19:4 ("Their voice has gone out to all the earth") to argue that the gospel message, like the silent speech of creation, must go to the ends of the world.

• There is a beautiful parallel between the silent speech (omer) of creation and the quiet, humble life of a believer. Just as the heavens declare the glory of God without saying a word, our lives can loudly proclaim the reality of Christ through silent, faithful obedience, love, and integrity.
