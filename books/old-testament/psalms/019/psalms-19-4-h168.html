TABERNACLE - ( ohel , Strong's H168 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word ohel fundamentally refers to a tent, nomadic shelter, or temporary dwelling place made of animal skins or woven goat hair. In Israel's history, it represents the simplicity of nomadic life, the sacred place of God's dwelling among His people (the Tabernacle), and metaphorically, any dwelling place or canopy. In Psalm 19:4, it is used metaphorically to describe the "tent" or canopy that God has pitched in the heavens for the sun, highlighting God's absolute sovereignty, creative order, and the solar cycle as a visible witness of His glory.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near East, the ohel (tent) was the primary domestic dwelling for nomadic pastoralists. Constructed from dark goat hair or animal skins stitched together, it was supported by wooden poles, stabilized by ropes, and secured to the ground by wooden pegs. The tent was portable, adaptable, and essential for survival in the harsh wilderness. It symbolized safety, family, rest, and hospitality. Over time, even after Israel settled in permanent stone homes in Canaan, the tent remained deeply embedded in their cultural and religious memory. It recalled the wilderness wanderings and the sacred Ohel Mo'ed (the Tent of Meeting), where Yahweh met with Moses and manifested His glory.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary definition of ohel is a nomadic tent or portable shelter. The semantic range includes:

• A physical tent used for habitation by patriarchs, soldiers, or nomads.

• A home, dwelling, or habitation in general, poetically applied even to permanent structures or cities.

• The sacred Tabernacle of Yahweh, specifically the fabric tent structure that covered the wooden framework of the sanctuary.

• A metaphorical canopy, covering, or dark cloud (such as the heavens stretched out over the earth).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Patriarchal Pilgrim Life: Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob are characterized by living in tents (ohel), highlighting their pilgrim status as strangers and sojourners looking forward to an eternal city.

• The Divine Sanctuary: The ohel of meeting served as the focal point of God's localized presence. It was a portable sanctuary, reminding Israel that God was not bound to a single geographic location but traveled with them.

• The Cosmic Tent: The heavens are frequently described as a tent curtain stretched out by God. In Psalm 19:4, the sky is depicted as a cosmic tent pitched by God specifically to house the sun.

4. Theological Implications

The use of ohel in the Old Testament carries profound theological weight. First, it emphasizes God’s condescension and relational accessibility. He did not demand a towering, immovable stone temple initially; He chose to dwell in a humble, portable tent (ohel) among His wandering people. Second, in a creation context, it establishes God's absolute ownership and architecture of the universe. The heavens are His tent, and the sun—which many pagan nations worshiped as a sovereign deity—is merely a servant housed inside a tent pitched by Yahweh's hand.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19:1-4, David marvels at the silent, universal witness of creation. The heavens declare God's glory, and the night sky proclaims His handiwork. In verse 4, David notes that God has "set a tabernacle [tent] for the sun." The sun does not operate independently, nor is it a self-existent god. It is a tenant in God's universe. God has constructed a dwelling place (the horizon/sky) where the sun rests at night and from which it emerges like a joyful bridegroom in the morning to run its daily course. The use of ohel here strips the sun of its pagan divinity and reduces it to a created element obeying its Creator's design within His cosmic tent.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 12:8 - "And he moved from there to the mountain east of Bethel, and he pitched his tent, having Bethel on the west and Ai on the east; there he built an altar to the LORD and called on the name of the LORD."

• Exodus 33:7 - "Moses took his tent and pitched it outside the camp, far from the camp, and called it the tabernacle of meeting. And it came to pass that everyone who sought the LORD went out to the tabernacle of meeting which was outside the camp."

• Isaiah 40:22 - "It is He who sits above the circle of the earth, And its inhabitants are like grasshoppers, Who stretches out the heavens like a curtain, And spreads them out like a tent to dwell in."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The New Testament picks up the imagery of ohel (translated in the Septuagint as skene). In John 1:14, when the Word became flesh, the Greek text states He "tabernacled" (skenoo) among us, pitching His tent in human flesh. Furthermore, the apostle Paul, who was a tentmaker by trade, compares our mortal bodies to temporary "earthly tents" (skenos) in 2 Corinthians 5:1-4, which will eventually be replaced by an eternal, heavenly building.
