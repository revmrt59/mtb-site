LINE - ( qaw , Strong's H6957 )

Summary:

In Psalm 19:4, the Hebrew word qaw refers to a measuring line or cord used by builders to mark boundaries and lay foundations. Used metaphorically of the heavens, it depicts God's creation as a vast, ordered canvas that defines the boundaries of the known world, effectively "drawing a line" of divine testimony across the globe. This silent, universal proclamation establishes a standard of truth and order, ensuring that no part of the earth is left without a witness of the Creator's glory.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

• qaw refers primarily to a measuring line or a cord used by ancient builders, surveyors, and artisans. In the ancient Near East, a builder stretched out a cord to ensure straightness, establish boundaries, and lay down the exact dimensions of a building. This physical imagery is grounded in construction, craftsmanship, and land distribution. When a boundary or standard is set, the qaw defines what is inside and what is outside, bringing order out of chaos and marking off territory with absolute precision.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun qaw means a measuring line, measuring cord, standard, or rule. It represents a physical tool used for measurement, alignment, and architectural planning. By extension, it denotes a rule of conduct, a standard of measurement, or a boundary line. In certain contexts, it can refer to a musical or vocal chord (sound), though the primary semantic range is rooted in the physical cord of measurement.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Architectural Design and Judgment: The word is frequently used to describe God’s hand in structuring the cosmos or executing judgment. Stretching out a line over a city symbolizes either rebuilding it with precision or measuring it for complete destruction, indicating that God's actions are always measured, deliberate, and perfectly aligned with His standards.

• Instruction and Precept: In prophetic literature, the repetition of the word (qaw la-qaw, "line upon line") describes systematic instruction or, conversely, the babble of those who mock simple, structured teaching. It represents incremental, structured learning.

• Universal Testimony: In Psalm 19:4, the "line" represents the cosmic boundary or the measuring cord of the heavens, marking out their domain and sounding forth their silent testimony to the ends of the earth.

### 4. Theological Implications

Theologically, qaw reveals that God is a God of order, precision, and universal revelation. He does not create or judge haphazardly. The universe is constructed with an intentional "line" of design that serves as a permanent, visible standard of His eternal power and divine nature. Because this line of revelation spans the entire globe, humanity is left without excuse. Furthermore, the use of a measuring line shows that God's standards are absolute, measurable, and sovereignly determined.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19:4, the heavens are personified as silent witnesses declaring the glory of God. The NKJV translates qaw as "line." This "line" is the boundary or sphere of influence that the heavens have measured out. Their silent declaration covers the entire physical territory defined by this cosmic measuring line. The ancient Greek translation (the Septuagint) translated qaw here as phtongos (voice or sound), which the Apostle Paul quotes in Romans 10:18 ("Their voice has gone out to all the earth"). Whether understood as a boundary line defining the reach of their testimony or the resonant vibration of a stretched musical chord, the contextual nuance is clear: the revelation of God in creation has a universal reach, leaving no corner of the earth untouched by its message.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Job 38:5: "Who determined its measurements, if you know? Or who stretched the line upon it?"

• Isaiah 28:10: "For precept must be upon precept, precept upon precept, Line upon line, line upon line, Here a little, there a little."

• Isaiah 34:11: "And He shall stretch out over it The line of confusion and the stones of emptiness."

• Zechariah 1:16: "Therefore thus says the LORD: 'I am returning to Jerusalem with mercy; My house shall be built in it,' says the LORD of hosts, 'And a surveyor’s line shall be stretched out over Jerusalem.'"

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

The connection between the Hebrew qaw (measuring line) and the Greek phtongos (voice or sound) used in the Septuagint and Romans 10:18 is a profound study in biblical theology. A stretched measuring cord, when plucked, produces a distinct sound or tone. Thus, the "line" stretched out across the heavens is not merely a silent boundary, but a vibrating string sending out a constant, resonant testimony of God's creative power. The cosmos functions like a grand musical instrument, continually sounding a silent chord of praise that is "heard" by all who look upon the sky.
