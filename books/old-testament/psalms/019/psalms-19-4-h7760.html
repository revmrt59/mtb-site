SET - ( śûm , Strong's H7760 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb śûm fundamentally means to put, place, set, or establish. In Psalm 19:4, it describes God's sovereign act of establishing a "tabernacle" (or tent) for the sun within the heavens. This term highlights the Creator's supreme authority, demonstrating that the sun is not an independent deity but a created servant positioned by divine decree to fulfill its daily course.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The primitive root śûm is a highly versatile verb occurring over 580 times in the Old Testament. Its primary concrete picture is that of taking an object and deliberately placing, setting, or depositing it in a specific location. It evokes the image of a builder laying a stone, a nomad pitching a tent, or a sovereign setting a crown. It carries a sense of intentionality, order, and authority. The word is not used for accidental or careless placement; it denotes purposeful positioning.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The semantic range of śûm includes:

• To put, place, or set a physical object in a location.

• To appoint, designate, or ordain a person to a position of leadership or responsibility.

• To establish, decree, or make (such as making a name, establishing a law, or setting a boundary).

• To direct one's attention or mind (e.g., "setting" one's heart on something).

In poetic creation texts, the word describes God's cosmic organizing activity—His deliberate placement of natural boundaries, celestial bodies, and physical laws.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, śûm is used in several rich theological contexts:

• The Garden of Eden: God "put" (śûm) the newly formed man into the garden He had planted, establishing humanity's original home and sphere of responsibility (Genesis 2:8).

• Covenantal Protection: God "set" (śûm) a mark on Cain to protect him from vengeance (Genesis 4:15).

• Sovereign Appointment: Pharaoh "set" (śûm) taskmasters over the Israelites (Exodus 1:11), and later, Jethro advised Moses to "place" (śûm) capable men as rulers over the congregation (Exodus 18:21).

• Cosmic Boundaries: Wisdom literature depicts God "assigning" (śûm) boundaries to the sea so that the waters would not transgress His command (Proverbs 8:29).

4. Theological Implications

The use of śûm in the context of creation underscores the absolute sovereignty of Yahweh. In the ancient Near East, neighboring pagan nations worshipped the sun (as the god Ra in Egypt or Shamash in Babylon) as a supreme deity. By stating that God "set" a tent for the sun, the psalmist strips the celestial body of its divine status. The sun is not a god; it is merely a tenant. It dwells in a temporary shelter constructed and positioned by the true, living God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19:4, śûm appears in the perfect tense (śām), denoting a completed, established past action with ongoing, permanent results. The context of the first half of the Psalm is the silent, universal declaration of God's glory through the heavens. In the midst of this vast canvas, God has pitched a tent for the sun. The sun is depicted as a bridegroom coming out of his chamber and a strong man rejoicing to run his course. The sun's path is not random; God has "set" its starting point, its trajectory, and its dwelling place, ensuring that its warmth and light reach the entirety of the earth.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:8 - "The LORD God planted a garden eastward in Eden, and there He put the man whom He had formed."

• Genesis 15:10 - "Then he brought all these to Him and cut them in two, down the middle, and placed each piece opposite the other; but he did not cut the birds in two."

• Proverbs 8:29 - "When He assigned to the sea its limit, So that the waters would not transgress His command, When He marked out the foundations of the earth,"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The metaphor of a "tabernacle" (ohel, tent) for the sun highlights the temporal, contingent nature of the physical creation compared to the eternal nature of God. Tents are easily pitched, moved, and folded up. By "setting" a tent for the sun, God demonstrates that the massive celestial structures of the universe are easily managed and directed by His hand, functioning as simple instruments of His daily grace to humanity.
