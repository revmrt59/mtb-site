WORLD - ( tebel , Strong's H8398 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word tebel refers to the habitable world, emphasizing the earth as an ordered, fertile, and populated sphere designed for human dwelling. Unlike general terms for physical soil or territory, tebel highlights the global arena of human life, civilization, and history. In Psalm 19:4, it underscores the universal reach of God's natural revelation, declaring that the silent sermon of creation extends to every corner of the inhabited earth, leaving all of humanity without excuse and pointing them directly to the Creator.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The word is rooted in the verb yabal, which means to flow, lead, or bring forth. This connects the concept of the world directly to its productivity, watercourses, and fertility. It is not merely a barren rock floating in space, but a beautifully watered, life-yielding home prepared by God for His creatures. The word picture is one of a fruit-bearing, hospitable, and active stage where human life and history unfold under the watchful eye of the Creator.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word designates the inhabited earth, the world of humanity, or the world in its physical, productive entirety. It is frequently paired poetically with erets (earth or land), where erets acts as the broader physical earth and tebel specifies the habitable, populated portion of the globe. The semantic range spans from the physical continents capable of sustaining life to the collective human population dwelling upon them.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

The term is used 36 times in the Hebrew Bible, predominantly in poetic and prophetic literature, such as Psalms and Isaiah. It is used to describe:

• The foundation and establishment of the world by God's wisdom and power.

• The global scope of God's sovereign rule and judgment over all nations.

• The physical stability of the cosmos, which is firmly established and cannot be moved.

• The target of God's universal revelation, love, and righteousness.

### 4. Theological Implications

Theologically, tebel highlights God's sovereignty over the entirety of human existence. The world does not belong to the empires of men or the forces of darkness; the earth and all its inhabitants are the Lord's. Because tebel refers to the inhabited world, it stresses that God's covenantal claims, moral laws, and saving purposes are not localized or tribal, but globally universal. Furthermore, the firm establishment of the tebel demonstrates God's faithfulness in maintaining a stable environment for human life despite human sinfulness.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19:4, the psalmist contemplates the silent, yet thunderous testimony of the heavens. The "line" (or measuring line/sound) of celestial proclamation has gone out through all the "earth" (erets), and their "words" to the end of the "world" (tebel). By using tebel, the psalmist stresses that the message of God's glory is not restricted to a chosen few or a single nation like Israel. It penetrates to the absolute limits of human habitation. Every person living anywhere in the inhabited world is a recipient of this natural revelation.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• 1 Samuel 2:8 - "For the pillars of the earth are the LORD's, And He has set the world upon them."

• Psalm 24:1 - "The earth is the LORD's, and all its fullness, The world and those who dwell therein."

• Psalm 96:10 - "Say among the nations, 'The LORD reigns; The world also is firmly established, It shall not be moved; He shall judge the peoples righteously.'"

• Isaiah 14:17 - "Who made the world as a wilderness And destroyed its cities, Who did not open the house of his prisoners?"

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

The Greek translation of the Old Testament (LXX) frequently translates tebel as oikoumene (the inhabited world) or kosmos (the ordered world). This connection highlights how the New Testament writers conceptualized the global mission of the Gospel. Just as the natural revelation of God's glory reaches the ends of the tebel (quoted by Paul in Romans 10:18), so too must the special revelation of the Gospel of Jesus Christ be carried to all the inhabited earth.
