RUN - ( ruts , Strong's H7323 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb ruts means to run quickly, move swiftly, or rush with decisive energy and purpose. In the context of Psalm 19:5, it portrays the sun not merely drifting across the sky, but eagerly and joyfully sprinting like a champion or a strong hero along an appointed course. This word emphasizes the effortless power, joy, and absolute dependability of God’s creation as it obeys its Creator's design, serving as a vivid visual sermon of God’s glory to all the earth.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• In the ancient Near East, running was associated with messengers, royal bodyguards, elite warriors, and athletic champions.

• To run (ruts) implied purposeful, vigorous, and unhindered movement. It stands in contrast to wandering aimlessly or walking with hesitation.

• The background of Psalm 19:5 sets a striking contrast between the stillness of the night and the sudden, brilliant, energetic burst of the morning sun. The sun is depicted as a bridegroom leaving his chamber and a champion eagerly running a race.

• This word picture captures the sheer vitality of the created order, moving under the precise command of the Creator.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The word ruts is a primary root meaning to run, to hasten, or to move rapidly.

• It denotes rapid physical locomotion, whether by foot or, metaphorically, of celestial bodies.

• The term refers to swiftness, decisive action, and eager movement toward a goal or destination.

• It carries the nuance of strength and stamina, especially when paired with a "strong man" or "champion" (gibbor), indicating an athletic or military-like discipline that delights in the exertion of its power.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Royal Couriers: The noun form of this root (ratsim) refers to the royal runners or bodyguards who carried urgent messages for the king, requiring great speed and physical endurance.

• Eager and Joyful Action: Running represents eagerness. It is used of Abraham running to greet his heavenly visitors, demonstrating urgent hospitality and honor.

• Dynamic Creation: In Psalm 19:5, the word picture is cosmic. The sun is personified as an athlete standing at the starting line, bursting with energy, and rejoicing at the opportunity to run its daily course across the heavens.

4. Theological Implications

• Creation’s Joyful Obedience: Creation does not begrudgingly obey God. The sun "rejoices" to run its path, showing that nature finds its ultimate fulfillment in fulfilling the Creator's design.

• God’s Order and Sovereignty: The path is predetermined. Creation runs on a track laid out by God, demonstrating His supreme order and governance over the universe.

• Unhindered Revelation: The sun's swift and unhindered run ensures that its light and heat reach the ends of the earth. This physical reality mirrors the perfect, pervasive, and life-giving reach of God's written Word, which is celebrated in the second half of the Psalm.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 19:5, ruts is paired with shas (to rejoice) and gibbor (strong man/champion).

• This pairing reveals that the sun's movement is not a chore or a monotonous cycle, but a joyful, triumphant display of strength.

• The "path" (orach) is a well-traveled way or track. The sun knows its track perfectly and does not veer from it. It runs with focused, joyous speed from one horizon to the other, fulfilling its god-given purpose without deviation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 18:2 – "So he lifted his eyes and looked, and behold, three men were standing by him; and when he saw them, he ran from the tent door to meet them, and bowed himself to the ground."

• Proverbs 4:12 – "When you walk, your steps will not be hindered, And when you run, you will not stumble."

• Isaiah 40:31 – "But those who wait on the Lord Shall renew their strength; They shall mount up with wings like eagles, They shall run and not be weary, They shall walk and not faint."

• Joel 2:9 – "They run to and fro in the city, They run on the wall; They climb into the houses, They enter at the windows like a thief."

• Habakkuk 2:2 – "Then the Lord answered me and said: 'Write the vision And make it plain on tablets, That he may run who reads it.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The structural parallel in Psalm 19: The first half of the Psalm (vv. 1-6) describes God’s general revelation in the heavens, culminating in the sun’s glorious run. The second half (vv. 7-11) describes God’s special revelation in the Law (Torah). Just as the sun runs its course perfectly, giving light and heat to all, so the Law of the Lord is perfect, converting the soul and enlightening the eyes of those who walk in it.
