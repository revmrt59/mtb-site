REJOICES - ( sus , Strong's H7797 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb sus denotes an intense, active, and highly visible joy. Unlike quieter terms for gladness, this word represents an exuberant, leaping, or exultant rejoicing that cannot be hidden. In the context of Psalm 19:5, it describes the sun's eager, energetic, and triumphant journey across the heavens, likened to a champion athlete bursting with enthusiasm to run a race. It paints a vivid picture of creation executing its God-given purpose with radiant delight, reflecting the joyful nature of the Creator Himself.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• sus is a primary Hebrew root that graphically captures the physical expression of joy. It carries the distinct sense of leaping, springing, or shining brightly. It represents a joy that is dynamic and externalized, rather than merely internal, quiet, or contemplative. It is the ecstatic joy of a bridegroom on his wedding day, the high-spirited energy of a warrior ready for battle, or the overwhelming gladness of restoration after captivity. The word suggests a rapid, enthusiastic movement, illustrating an eagerness to fulfill a specific task or celebrate a great triumph.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb sus means to rejoice, exult, be glad, or display joy. It describes an active, exultant state of happiness. The semantic range spans from personal, emotional delight to cosmic, figurative rejoicing by creation. It frequently describes God's own joy over His people, the people's joy in salvation, or the personified elements of nature performing their functions with gladness. It is often paired with other Hebrew words for joy, such as samach (to rejoice) and gil (to rejoice/spin around), serving as a climax of emotional expression.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Scriptures employ sus in several vivid contexts:

• The Joy of the Bridegroom: Isaiah 62:5 uses sus to describe a bridegroom rejoicing over his bride, depicting the ultimate expression of delight, commitment, and fresh love.

• The Energy of a Champion: In Psalm 19:5, it portrays a champion athlete who does not reluctantly drag himself to the starting line, but leaps with eagerness to run his race.

• Divine Delight: Deuteronomy 30:9 uses the word to show that God will "again rejoice over you for good," illustrating that God's favor is not grudging but deeply joyful.

• Eschatological Restoration: Isaiah 35:1 describes the wilderness and the solitary place being glad (sus) and blossoming, symbolizing a total reversal of curse into jubilant life.

4. Theological Implications

• Creation is not a cold, mechanical clockwork running on impersonal laws. Under God's sovereign design, creation operates with a spirit of joyful obedience. The sun's daily path is a metaphor for the eager, unhindered fulfillment of God’s will.

• God Himself is a God of intense, active joy. He does not merely tolerate His people; He exults over them.

• Human response to God's revelation should mirror this active, energetic delight. Just as the sun rejoices to run its course, believers should find supreme, energetic joy in walking according to God's law.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19:5, sus describes the sun's daily transit across the sky. David uses two metaphors: a bridegroom leaving his chamber (representing freshness, beauty, and radiant happiness) and a champion athlete rejoicing to run a race (representing strength, focus, and energetic determination). The sun does not move sluggishly or under compulsion; it "rejoices" to run. This personification shows that nature itself delights in obeying the Creator’s decree. The placement of this word right before the psalm shifts to the perfection of God's written law (Psalm 19:7-11) suggests that just as the sun rejoices to fulfill its natural course, the believer should find life and joy in obeying God's testimonies.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Deuteronomy 30:9 - "The LORD will again rejoice over you for good as He rejoiced over your fathers."

• Isaiah 61:10 - "I will greatly rejoice in the LORD, My soul shall be joyful in my God;"

• Isaiah 62:5 - "And as the bridegroom rejoices over the bride, So shall your God rejoice over you."

• Isaiah 65:19 - "I will rejoice in Jerusalem, And joy in My people;"

• Zephaniah 3:17 - "He will rejoice over you with gladness, He will quiet you with His love, He will rejoice over you with singing."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between sus and other words for sadness in the Old Testament emphasizes that true joy is an active force and the antidote to spiritual lethargy. In the Septuagint (LXX), sus is frequently translated by agalliao, a Greek verb meaning to exult, leap for joy, or show extreme gladness (the same word Jesus uses in Matthew 5:12, "Rejoice and be exceedingly glad"). This highlights a thematic bridge to the New Testament, where the joy of salvation causes believers to exult even in trials.
