RISING - ( motsa , Strong's H4161 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word motsa refers to a place or act of going forth, an exit, a source, or a rising. In the context of Psalm 19:6, it describes the daily emergence of the sun as it bursts forth from the eastern horizon to run its course across the heavens. This term captures the regular, sovereignly ordained point of origin for the sun's journey, illustrating the visible, inescapable, and glorious testimony of God's general revelation in creation. Just as the sun's rising is certain and its warmth reaches everywhere, so too is the testimony of God's glory declared to all mankind.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root verb is yatsa, which means "to go out" or "to go forth." The prefix mem transforms this action into a noun of place, time, or action, signifying "the place of going out," "the act of going forth," or "that which comes out." Historically, it paints a vivid picture of emergence. It is used to describe water bursting out from a spring, a word proceeding from someone's mouth, the export of goods from a country, or the rising of the sun from the horizon.

In the ancient Near East, where the sun was frequently worshipped as a deity, the Hebrew scriptures demythologize the sun. It is not a god, but a servant of Yahweh, emerging from its canopy at its appointed "going forth" (motsa) to perform its Creator's bidding.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Motsa carries the primary meaning of an exit, a point of departure, or a source.

• It designates the place where something originates or issues forth, such as a spring of water.

• It denotes the act of going forth or rising, specifically applied to the sun's daily appearance in the east.

• It refers to that which proceeds from the lips, such as a command, promise, or utterance.

• In administrative or geographic contexts, it can refer to a gate, an exit, or a boundary line.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Source of Water: It describes the refreshing bursting forth of a natural spring in the desert, symbolizing life, provision, and divine blessing.

• The Proceeding Word: It describes the solemn, binding utterances that proceed from a person's mouth or the life-giving words that come from the mouth of God.

• The Rising of the Sun: In Psalm 19:6, it represents the precise starting point of the sun's daily arc. It is a visual picture of reliable, orderly, and majestic light breaking through the darkness.

4. Theological Implications

• Sovereign Order: The motsa of the sun demonstrates God's faithful governance of creation. The sun does not rise by its own power or whim; its going forth is governed by a divine decree that has remained unbroken since creation.

• General Revelation: The sun's rising is a universal sermon. It requires no translation; its voice is heard by all people. It declares the power, majesty, and constant care of the Creator.

• The Inescapability of Truth: Just as nothing is hidden from the heat of the sun's rising and circuit, nothing is hidden from the light of God’s truth and judgment. The natural world points directly to the moral order detailed in the second half of Psalm 19.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19, there is a deliberate parallel between the sun's physical circuit (verses 1-6) and the Torah's spiritual perfection (verses 7-11). The "rising" (motsa) of the sun in verse 6 marks the beginning of an all-encompassing journey. The sun starts at one end of the heavens and travels to the other. Its physical reach is complete, leaving nothing untouched by its heat.

This complete physical coverage prepares the reader for the complete spiritual coverage of God's Law in the subsequent verses. Just as the sun's motsa brings light and heat to every corner of the earth, God's perfect law brings light, wisdom, and life to every corner of the human soul.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Hosea 6:3: "Let us know, Let us pursue the knowledge of the LORD. His going forth is established as the morning; He will come to us like the rain, Like the latter and former rain to the earth."

• 2 Kings 2:21: "Then he went out to the source of the water, and cast in the salt there, and said, 'Thus says the LORD: "I have healed this water; from it there shall be no more death or barrenness."'"

• Psalm 107:35: "He turns a wilderness into pools of water, And dry land into water springs."

• Daniel 9:25: "Know therefore and understand, That from the going forth of the command To restore and build Jerusalem Until Messiah the Prince, There shall be seven weeks and sixty-two weeks; The street shall be built again, and the wall, Even in troublesome times."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Messianic Connection: Hosea 6:3 uses this same word to describe the Lord's coming: "His going forth is established as the morning." Just as the rising of the sun is a sure, glorious, and darkness-dispelling reality, so too is the redemption and appearance of Yahweh to His people.

• The Spring of Life: The pairing of motsa with water in places like 2 Kings 2:21 and Psalm 107 highlights God's power to transform dry, lifeless places into flowing sources of sustenance.

• The Reliable Word: In Deuteronomy 8:3, the "proceeding" of God's mouth is what sustains human life, showing that what issues from God is as vital and life-giving as water from a spring or light from the sun.
