HIDDEN - ( satar , Strong's H5641 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word satar means to hide, conceal, or cover, referring to something kept secret, protected, or sheltered from view. In the context of Psalm 19, it describes the inescapable penetration of the sun's heat, serving as a powerful metaphor for the all-revealing, soul-penetrating nature of God's Word and His holy gaze. Just as nothing in physical creation can escape the warmth of the sun, no human heart, motive, or secret sin can remain hidden from the searching light of God’s truth.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete, physical background of satar is rooted in the action of covering something up, putting it out of sight, or seeking a safe shelter. In the ancient Near East, finding a place to hide was a matter of survival—whether seeking refuge in a desert cave to escape a pursuing enemy or finding a shaded canopy to survive the blistering midday heat. The word evokes the image of a secret place, a protective veil, or a barrier that prevents observation. To be hidden is to be placed beyond the reach of human eyes or external forces.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb satar is a primary root meaning to hide, conceal, or keep close. When used in the active voice (such as the Hiphil stem), it means to hide something or someone from view, most notably used when God "hides His face" from His people due to their disobedience. In the passive voice (such as the Niphal stem, which is used in Psalm 19:6), it means to be hidden, to hide oneself, or to be concealed. The term denotes that which is invisible, secret, or completely inaccessible to human perception and understanding.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament employs satar in several vivid contexts:

• Divine Concealment: It frequently describes God hiding His face in times of judgment, leaving humanity to experience the consequences of their choices.

• Human Hiding: It describes people hiding from danger, from enemies, or, futilely, from God Himself.

• The Inescapable Sun: In Psalm 19:6, the passive participle form describes that which is concealed. Even the deepest valleys, thickest walls, or heaviest garments cannot block out the pervasive, life-sustaining, and potentially scorching heat of the sun. The sun's heat penetrates all barriers.

• Secret Faults: The root appears later in Psalm 19:12 to describe "secret faults"—sins that are hidden from the sinner's own awareness or kept secret from other people, yet fully exposed to God.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of satar in relation to God's character is immense. First, it underscores the absolute omniscience of God. Human beings often live under the illusion that they can compartmentalize their lives and hide their thoughts, motives, or actions. However, satar reveals that such concealment is impossible before the Creator.

Second, it highlights the dual nature of God's presence. For the rebellious, the impossibility of being hidden from God brings conviction and judgment; the light of His truth exposes every dark corner. For the righteous, however, this same root offers comfort, as God is praised for hiding His children in the "secret place" (seter, a noun derived from satar) of His presence, sheltering them from the storms of life.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19:1-6, David describes the general revelation of God through the cosmos. The sun is the climax of this heavenly display, running its circuit across the sky with unmatched power. When David writes, "and there is nothing hidden from its heat," he is not merely making a scientific observation about the solar system. He is creating a poetic bridge to the second half of the psalm (verses 7-11), which praises the written Word of God.

Just as the physical sun penetrates every corner of the earth so that nothing is hidden from its heat, the spiritual light of God's Word penetrates every corner of the human heart. Scripture exposes, convicts, warms, and purifies. The contextual nuance of satar here is transitionary: it warns the reader that just as they cannot find a shadow deep enough to escape the sun, they cannot find a secret place deep enough to hide their soul from the searching truth of God’s law.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 4:14 - "Surely You have driven me out this day from the face of the ground; I shall be hidden from Your face; I shall be a fugitive and a vagabond on the earth..."

• Deuteronomy 29:29 - "The secret things belong to the LORD our God, but those things which are revealed belong to us and to our children forever..."

• Psalm 19:12 - "Who can understand his errors? Cleanse me from secret faults."

• Psalm 31:20 - "You shall hide them in the secret place of Your presence From the plots of man..."

• Isaiah 54:8 - "With a little wrath I hid My face from you for a moment; But with everlasting kindness I will have mercy on you..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A beautiful spiritual paradox exists within the root satar. Human beings instinctively try to hide their sins from God, repeating the tragic error of Adam and Eve in the Garden. This type of hiding is utterly futile because nothing is hidden from Him. However, when we confess our sins and abandon our self-made covers, God invites us to hide in Him. The very God whose eyes penetrate all secrets becomes the fortress who hides us from judgment and fear. We stop hiding from God and begin hiding in God.
