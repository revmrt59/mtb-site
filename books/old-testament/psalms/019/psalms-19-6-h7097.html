END - ( qatsah , Strong's H7097 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun qatsah refers to the outermost boundary, extremity, or border of a physical space, object, or concept. In Psalm 19:6, it depicts the vast, all-encompassing reach of the sun's circuit from one end of the heavens to the other. This word highlights the absolute completeness of God's natural revelation, illustrating that His truth and glory penetrate every corner of the earth without exception.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The noun qatsah derives from a root verb meaning to cut off or cut short. This root action implies the drawing of a definitive boundary line—the exact point where an object, territory, or journey is "cut off" because it has reached its absolute limit. In the ancient Near Eastern context, the horizon represented the ultimate boundary of human sight and existence. Therefore, the "ends" of the heavens represented the most remote, absolute limits of the physical universe.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• qatsah means the end, extremity, border, or outermost limit. It is used to specify the physical perimeter of an object, the geographic boundaries of a territory, or the spatial dimensions of the cosmos. The term expresses the concept of maximum reach, finality of scale, and physical completeness. It defines the edge where one reality finishes and another begins.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, qatsah describes both concrete and abstract boundaries:

• Sacred Architecture: It designates the outermost edges or borders of the tabernacle curtains, representing the precise, orderly boundaries established by God's design.

• Geographic Boundaries: It marks the borders of tribal inheritances and land allotments, defining where one dominion stops and another begins.

• Divine Infinity: Figuratively, it refers to the outermost limits of God's ways, which are so vast that human beings can only catch a faint whisper of them.

### 4. Theological Implications

The use of qatsah underscores the comprehensive scope of God's sovereignty and self-disclosure. By establishing physical boundaries or "ends" to the creation, God demonstrates His role as the sovereign Architect who sets limits for the sun and the stars. Furthermore, because the sun's circuit spans from one "end" to the other, God's general revelation is universal. No human being is hidden from its light or warmth. This serves as a physical precursor to the perfect, all-reaching nature of His written Word detailed in the latter half of Psalm 19.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19:6, qatsah emphasizes the inescapable, all-pervasive nature of the sun’s journey across the sky. The psalmist uses the spatial "ends" of heaven to define a complete circuit. The sun starts at one extreme limit and travels to the opposite extreme limit. This physical completeness mirrors the spiritual completeness of God’s law described in verses 7–11. Just as no one escapes the physical heat of the sun at the ends of heaven, no one is outside the moral authority and searching light of God’s truth.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 26:4: "And you shall make loops of blue yarn on the edge of the sister curtain on the selvage of one set, and likewise you shall do on the outer edge of the other curtain of the second set."

• Job 26:14: "Indeed these are the mere edges of His ways, And how small a whisper we hear of Him! But the thunder of His power who can understand?"

• Psalm 19:6: "Its rising is from one end of heaven, And its circuit to the other end; And there is nothing hidden from its heat."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

While human strength, wisdom, and resources have a strict qatsah (a point where they are cut off and fail), God's presence and power exist even at the furthest extremities of creation. There is no geographic or spiritual limit where a believer can wander beyond His sovereign care and grace.
