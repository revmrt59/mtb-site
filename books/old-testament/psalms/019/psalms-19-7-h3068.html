LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh (rendered as "LORD" in all capital letters in most English translations) is the personal, covenantal, and self-revealed name of the true God of Israel. In Psalm 19:7, this sacred name signals a profound shift from the general revelation of God as Creator (El, used in verse 1) to the intimate, relational God of covenant revelation who speaks directly to His people through His law. It represents the self-existent, eternal, and unchanging One who binds Himself to His people in absolute faithfulness, providing perfect instruction that restores the soul and makes the simple wise.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The name Yahweh is the sacred Tetragrammaton, consisting of the four Hebrew consonants YHWH. Out of deep reverence and fear of taking the Lord's name in vain, ancient Jewish readers avoided pronouncing the name aloud, substituting it with the word Adonai (Lord) or Elohim (God). This practice is preserved in modern English translations by printing the word as "LORD" in small capital letters.

The historical background of the name is anchored in God’s self-revelation to Moses at the burning bush in Exodus 3:14, where He declared His name as "I AM WHO I AM." It is a name forged in the context of redemption, deliverance, and covenant-making. It was not a name discovered by human philosophical reflection, but a name graciously gifted by God to His chosen people so they could know Him personally and call upon Him by name.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name Yahweh is the proper name of the God of Israel. It is derived from the active Hebrew verb hayah, which means "to be," "to exist," or "to become." Grammatically, it functions as a third-person singular imperfect form of the verb, meaning "He is," "He will be," or "He active-ly exists."

Lexically, the name signifies absolute self-existence, eternal being, and uninfluenced sovereignty. The LORD is the uncaused Cause of all things, possessing life inherent within Himself. He is the sovereign, self-determining One who acts in history according to His own counsel and character. The name does not merely denote abstract existence, but an active, dynamic, and saving presence—He is the One who is present with His people to deliver and sustain them.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament scriptures, Yahweh is used when emphasizing God's personal relationship with humanity, particularly with the nation of Israel.

• Contrast with Elohim: While Elohim (God) highlights His role as the transcendent Creator, Ruler, and Judge over all nature and nations, Yahweh (LORD) highlights His immanence, His approachability, and His binding covenant oaths.

• The Covenant Partner: The name is constantly coupled with relational terms like "husband," "shepherd," "redeemer," and "savior."

• A Verbal Monument: Yahweh acts as a verbal monument to divine immutability. Because He is the self-existent "I AM," He is entirely unaffected by time, change, or decay. Consequently, His promises are as stable and enduring as His very existence.

### 4. Theological Implications

• Immutability and Faithfulness: Because the LORD is the self-existent One, He does not change. His character, purposes, and covenant promises remain identical yesterday, today, and forever.

• Sovereign Grace: The name Yahweh reminds believers that God’s love and choice of His people are rooted entirely within Himself. He loves because He loves; He saves because He is the saving God.

• Relational Access: The revelation of this name means that God desires to be known, not merely feared as an anonymous cosmic force, but worshiped as a personal, communicative Father.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19, the structure pivots dramatically between verses 6 and 7.

• In verses 1–6, David describes the glory of God revealed in creation (general revelation). In this section, he uses the name El (the strong, powerful Creator). Nature speaks of God's power and handiwork, but it speaks silently and from a distance.

• In verses 7–14, the subject shifts to the written Word of God (special revelation). Instantly, the name of God changes from El to Yahweh. It is repeated six times in verses 7–9 ("the law of the LORD," "the testimony of the LORD," "the statutes of the LORD," etc.).

• The nuance is clear: You can look at the sun, moon, and stars and know there is a powerful Creator (El), but you can only know the personal, covenant-keeping Savior (Yahweh) through His written Word. The law is not a cold set of demands from a distant deity; it is the warm, life-giving, perfect instruction of the covenant-keeping Yahweh, designed to restore the soul of those who belong to Him.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:4 - "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens,"

• Exodus 3:15 - "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

• Psalm 23:1 - "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

• Isaiah 42:8 - "I am the LORD, that is My name; And My glory I will not give to another, Nor My praise to carved images."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

For the Christian preacher and teacher, it is vital to connect Yahweh to the New Testament revelation of Jesus Christ. When Jesus declared "I AM" (Ego Eimi) in the Gospels (such as in John 8:58: "Before Abraham was, I AM"), He was directly claiming the name and identity of Yahweh.

Understanding this connection transforms how we read Psalm 19:7. The law, testimony, and statutes of Yahweh are the very words of Christ. To submit to the scriptures is to submit to the loving, covenant-keeping authority of Jesus, who is Himself the ultimate Word made flesh.
