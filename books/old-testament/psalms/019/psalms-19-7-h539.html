SURE - ( 'aman , Strong's H0539 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'aman carries the fundamental idea of firmness, stability, and reliability. In Psalm 19:7, where the testimony of the Lord is described as "sure," it denotes something that is absolutely dependable, unwavering, and trustworthy. Unlike the shifting sands of human opinion, God’s written testimony provides a solid foundation upon which a person can safely build their life, transforming the simple and naive into those who possess true, godly wisdom.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The root meaning of 'aman is to support, sustain, or be firmly established. Physically, it depicts a pillar that securely holds up a heavy roof, or a nurse carrying and cradle-supporting a vulnerable infant to keep them safe and steady. It represents that which is immovable, solid, and capable of bearing immense weight without bending, breaking, or slipping. From this root, Hebrew derives the familiar word amen, which asserts, "it is reliable," "so be it," or "it is absolutely true."

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

In the Niphal stem (the passive/reflexive verbal stem used in Psalm 19:7), 'aman means to be verified, confirmed, established, faithful, or trustworthy. It describes an object, promise, or person that has been tested by time and trials and has proven completely reliable.

• The Qal stem means to support or nourish.

• The Niphal stem means to be established, faithful, lasting, or verified.

• The Hiphil stem means to stand firm, trust, believe, or have faith.

When applied to God's Word, 'aman declares that the scriptures are not tentative theories but verified realities that cannot fail.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament frequently employs 'aman to draw vivid pictures of durability and trust:

• The Secure Peg: It describes a peg driven deep into a firm wall, capable of holding up heavy vessels without giving way.

• The Enduring Dynasty: It describes a "sure house" or a permanent kingdom built to last across generations.

• The Faithful Witness: It describes a truth-teller who remains steady under cross-examination, refusing to bend the facts.

These word pictures demonstrate that when God speaks, His words act as structural pillars. They do not warp under cultural pressure, nor do they decay over time.

### 4. Theological Implications

The reliability of scripture is an extension of the character of God. Because Yahweh is the unchanging and faithful Covenant God, His spoken and written words must share His nature.

The relationship between 'aman (trustworthy) and he'emin (to believe) is critical: biblical faith is never a blind leap into the dark. True faith is placing one's weight upon that which is already proven 'aman (sure). Humans can only exercise saving faith because God has provided an intellectually and spiritually secure foundation in His revelation.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19:7, David contrasts general revelation (the sun and heavens, which declare God's glory silently) with special revelation (the written Law, which restores the soul).

David uses the term "testimony" ('edut), which refers to God's solemn covenant declarations. By calling this testimony "sure" (ne'emanah), David assures the reader that God's covenant promises are rock-solid.

This is placed in direct contrast to the "simple" (peti). In wisdom literature, the simple person is open-minded to a fault—easily swayed, unstable, naive, and lacking a moral compass. By anchoring their life to the "sure" testimony of God, the simple person receives a solid footing, gaining permanent wisdom and discernment that guards them against deception.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Deuteronomy 7:9: "Therefore know that the LORD your God, He is God, the faithful God who keeps covenant and mercy for a thousand generations with those who love Him and keep His commandments;"

• Psalm 93:5: "Your testimonies are very sure; Holiness adorns Your house, O LORD, forever."

• Psalm 111:7: "The works of His hands are verity and justice; All His precepts are sure."

• Isaiah 55:3: "Incline your ear, and come to Me. Hear, and your soul shall live; And I will make an everlasting covenant with you— The sure mercies of David."

• Proverbs 14:5: "A faithful witness does not lie, But a false witness will utter lies."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Antidote to Anxiety: In a world filled with shifting moral standards and conflicting human philosophies, the believer has an objective point of reference. God's Word does not need to be updated, corrected, or adapted to stay relevant.

• The Foundation of Prayer: When believers pray "Amen" at the conclusion of their prayers, they are appealing to the 'aman nature of God, declaring their absolute conviction that God is faithful to perform what He has promised.
