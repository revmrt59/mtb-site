SIMPLE - ( pethiy , Strong's H6612 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word pethiy describes a person who is naive, gullible, and easily influenced because their mind remains wide open to every passing opinion, wind of doctrine, or temptation. Lacking spiritual discernment and established moral boundaries, the simple person is highly vulnerable to deception and pathologically open-minded. However, unlike the hardened scorner or the stubborn fool, the simple is not yet totally closed to wisdom; they stand at a critical moral crossroads. In Psalm 19:7, the psalmist declares that the "testimony of the LORD" has the supernatural power to transform this uncommitted, easily misled individual into someone who is truly wise, grounded, and discerning.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root of pethiy is the verb pathah, which means to be wide, spacious, or open. The word picture is that of a wide-open door, an unhedged pathway, or a spacious room with no locks. A simple person has no defensive walls, filters, or guardrails on their mind and heart. They are intellectually and morally "roomy," letting anything and everything enter without evaluation. Because they are open-minded to a fault, they are easily persuaded, enticed, or led astray by both good and bad influences. They lack the spiritual maturity to distinguish between truth and error, making them highly susceptible to spiritual and physical danger.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun pethiy refers to a naive, simple, or inexperienced person who lacks wisdom and sound judgment. It denotes someone who is easily enticed, credulous, and lacking in spiritual discernment. The semantic range spans from harmless inexperience to a dangerous, willful gullibility that borders on folly if left unchecked. It is not a reference to intellectual disability, but rather to a moral and spiritual state of being uninstructed, uncommitted, and highly impressionable.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The term pethiy is used predominantly in Old Testament Wisdom Literature, especially in the Book of Proverbs, where they are depicted as being in constant need of instruction yet highly prone to falling into traps.

• The simpleton believes every word, in contrast to the prudent who looks well to his steps (Proverbs 14:15).

• The simple are contrasted with the prudent; while the prudent foresee evil and hide, the simple pass on and are punished (Proverbs 22:3).

• Lady Wisdom appeals directly to the simple to leave their simplistic ways and live, walking in the way of understanding (Proverbs 9:4-6). Conversely, the foolish woman also targets the simple, enticing them with stolen waters and forbidden pleasures (Proverbs 9:13-17).

4. Theological Implications

• Humanity is not born with innate, mature spiritual discernment; we begin life in a state of spiritual simplicity (pethiy), highly vulnerable to deception, sin, and false teachings.

• Simplemindedness is not a neutral or safe state. Scripture treats passive ignorance as a dangerous vulnerability that leads to ruin if not corrected.

• God does not abandon the simple to their vulnerability. He provides His Word (specifically the "testimony of the LORD") as a divine protective boundary and source of wisdom to transform the impressionable into the discerning.

• The transformation from simple to wise is a work of divine revelation. Human philosophy and self-help cannot close the dangerous doors of the naive mind; only the reliable, sure testimonies of God can ground the soul.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19:7, the phrase "making wise the simple" occurs in the context of the perfection and sufficiency of God's written revelation. The "testimony of the LORD" (eduth) refers to His solemn declarations, covenants, and laws. This testimony is described as "sure" (ne'emanah), meaning reliable, faithful, stable, and trustworthy. The contextual contrast is beautiful: the simple person is unstable, easily swayed, and lacks a solid foundation. When the unstable, open-minded "simple" person encounters and submits to the absolutely stable and "sure" testimony of the LORD, they receive a firm foundation. The Word of God closes the vulnerable, open-minded gaps of the simple and replaces their gullibility with divine wisdom.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Proverbs 1:4 - "To give prudence to the simple, To the young man knowledge and discretion"

• Proverbs 14:15 - "The simple believes every word, But the prudent considers well his steps."

• Proverbs 22:3 - "A prudent man foresees evil and hides himself, But the simple pass on and are punished."

• Psalm 119:130 - "The entrance of Your words gives light; It gives understanding to the simple."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Septuagint (LXX) translates pethiy in Psalm 19:7 using the Greek word nepios, which means an infant, babe, or untutored child. This highlights the child-like vulnerability of the simple. In the New Testament, believers are exhorted not to remain nepios (infants) tossed to and fro by every wind of doctrine, but to grow up into spiritual maturity (Ephesians 4:14). This directly aligns with the Old Testament concept of the pethiy needing the stabilizing power of God's Word.
