LAW - ( torah , Strong's H8451 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word torah goes far beyond a mere list of legal demands, rules, or cold regulations. It carries the rich, warm meaning of "instruction," "teaching," or "guidance." In the Old Testament, particularly in the wisdom literature of Psalm 19:7, torah refers to God's loving, authoritative instruction given to His covenant people to guide them in the path of life and righteousness. It is not a burdensome yoke, but a life-giving map of reality, revealing the very character of Yahweh and pointing humanity toward His perfect will.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word torah is derived from the verbal root yarah, which means "to throw," "to shoot," or "to cast." In its physical applications, yarah describes an archer shooting an arrow at a target or a person pointing a finger to show the way. Therefore, the word picture behind torah is that of "hitting the mark" or "pointing out the direction." God’s law is His divine finger pointing out the path of life, or His arrow flying straight to the target of truth. It provides humanity with a reliable moral compass, ensuring we do not wander aimlessly or miss the mark of our created purpose.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Torah refers broadly to instruction, teaching, direction, or law. While often translated as "law" in English Bibles, it encompasses the entire body of divine revelation, doctrine, and ethical teaching. Its primary nuances include:

• Instruction or teaching given by a parent to a child.

• Authoritative instruction or decisions given by priests or prophets.

• The collective body of Mosaic legislation (the Pentateuch).

• The written Word of God as a whole, serving as the ultimate guide for faith and practice.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Torah is used over 220 times in the Hebrew Bible. In wisdom literature, torah is frequently paired with metaphors of light, life, and sweetness:

• Proverbs 6:23 pairs torah with light: "For the commandment is a lamp, And the law is a light; Reproofs of instruction are the way of life."

• It is described as a "fountain of life" (Proverbs 13:14), providing vital nourishment and refreshment to the weary traveler.

• It is compared to gold and honey (Psalm 19:10), emphasizing its ultimate value and inherent sweetness to those who receive it.

4. Theological Implications

• Torah is fundamentally relational. It is grounded in the covenant relationship between Yahweh and His people. God does not issue commands from a distant, cold throne; He instructs His children as a loving Father.

• Torah reveals God's character. Because the law reflects the Lawgiver, torah shows us that God is holy, just, good, and loving. To study the torah is to seek the mind and heart of God.

• Torah is restorative, not restrictive. While human laws often seek to restrict behavior to maintain order, God's torah is designed to restore, convert, and give life to the human soul. It provides the framework for human flourishing.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19, David divides the revelation of God into two parts: general revelation through creation (verses 1–6) and special revelation through God's written Word (verses 7–11). In verse 7, David shifts from speaking of the generic "God" (El) of creation to the personal, covenant name of "the LORD" (Yahweh). The phrase "the law of the LORD is perfect, converting the soul" uses torah to introduce this covenantal revelation. Here, torah refers to the entirety of God's revealed instruction. The context contrasts the silent, distant witness of the heavens with the articulate, intimate, and life-changing witness of the torah. While the sun warms the physical earth, the torah warms, revives, and restores the inner man, bringing a spiritually dead or wandering soul back to its proper path.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Joshua 1:8 - "This Book of the Law shall not depart from your mouth, but you shall meditate in it day and night, that you may observe to do according to all that is written in it."

• Psalm 1:2 - "But his delight is in the law of the LORD, And in His law he meditates day and night."

• Psalm 119:1 - "Blessed are the undefiled in the way, Who walk in the law of the LORD!"

• Proverbs 13:14 - "The law of the wise is a fountain of life, To turn one away from the snares of death."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint) almost always translates torah with the Greek word nomos, which carries a much stronger legalistic and judicial connotation of "statute" or "rule." This translation choice heavily influenced subsequent Western theology, sometimes leading to a flattened view of "law" as purely legalism or a system of earning salvation. Recovering the original Hebrew sense of torah as parental "instruction" helps believers appreciate how the law and grace are not inherently opposed, but are parts of the same continuous history of God's self-revelation. Jesus did not come to abolish the torah, but to fill it to the brim, showing its ultimate fulfillment in His life, death, and teachings.
