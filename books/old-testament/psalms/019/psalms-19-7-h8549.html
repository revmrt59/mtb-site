PERFECT - ( tamim , Strong's H8549 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word tamim signifies completeness, wholeness, soundness, and integrity. In agricultural and sacrificial contexts, it describes an animal that is "unblemished" or free of defect. Applied to the Word of God in Psalm 19:7, it reveals that the Scriptures are flawless, all-sufficient, and entirely whole. God's law lacks nothing; it is completely reliable and uniquely capable of renewing, restoring, and transforming the entire human soul.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The physical and concrete background of tamim lies in the ancient sacrificial system. For an offering to be acceptable to God, it had to be completely intact, healthy, and free from any physical defect, disease, or spot. An animal with a broken limb, a blind eye, or a festering sore could not be offered.

• This imagery transitions beautifully into the moral and ethical realm. To be tamim is to live a life of undivided integrity, where there is no gap between one's profession and practice. It is the picture of a building that is structurally sound, a path that is entirely straight and unhindered, or a vessel that is completely leak-free and whole.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The word tamim derives from the verbal root tamam, which means to be finished, completed, or consumed.

• The primary lexical meaning of tamim is complete, whole, sound, unimpaired, or blameless. It denotes that which is entire and meets the full standard of its intended design.

• In a physical sense, it means unblemished, healthy, or whole.

• In a moral and ethical sense, it translates as upright, blameless, or possessing integrity. It does not imply absolute sinless perfection in the modern philosophical sense, but rather a heart that is undivided, sincere, and fully committed to God.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Sacrificial Integrity: The word is used extensively in Leviticus and Numbers to describe the physical quality required of sacrificial animals. The lamb must be tamim—physically perfect and free of blemish.

• Personal Character: It describes individuals whose hearts are completely devoted to God. Noah is described as tamim among his contemporaries, indicating his moral wholeness in a corrupt generation.

• The Ways of God: God's own character and actions are described with this word. His way is tamim—completely perfect, flawless, and without error.

4. Theological Implications

• Divine Sufficiency: Because the law of the Lord is tamim, it is entirely sufficient for the spiritual life. It does not require human supplements, philosophical updates, or secular adjustments. It is a complete guide for faith and practice.

• Reflecting the Creator: The perfection of the Word is a direct reflection of the perfection of its Author. A flawless God produces a flawless revelation.

• Holiness as Wholeness: Holiness is not merely the avoidance of bad behavior; it is the presence of wholeness. God calls His people to a complete, undivided devotion that mirrors His own wholeness.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 19:7, the Psalmist David shifts from the silent, general revelation of God in creation (verses 1-6) to the vocal, special revelation of God in His Word (verses 7-11).

• The term torah (law/instruction) is paired with tamim (perfect/complete). In this context, tamim contrasts with the broken, incomplete, and shifting opinions of human philosophy.

• The immediate result of this perfect law is "converting the soul" (or reviving, restoring, and bringing back the soul). Because the law of God is whole, flawless, and healthy, it has the supernatural capacity to bring wholeness to a fractured, broken human soul. It leaves no area of the human heart untouched or unhealed.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 6:9: "This is the genealogy of Noah. Noah was a just man, perfect in his generations. Noah walked with God."

• Genesis 17:1: "When Abram was ninety-nine years old, the Lord appeared to Abram and said to him, 'I am Almighty God; walk before Me and be blameless.'"

• Exodus 12:5: "Your lamb shall be without blemish, a male of the first year. You may take it from the sheep or from the goats."

• Psalm 18:30: "As for God, His way is perfect; The word of the Lord is proven; He is a shield to all who trust in Him."

• Psalm 119:1: "Blessed are the undefiled in the way, Who walk in the law of the Lord!"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• In the Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament), tamim is often translated with the Greek words teleios (mature, complete) or amomos (blameless, without spot).

• This connects directly to the New Testament description of Jesus Christ in 1 Peter 1:19 as a lamb "without blemish and without spot" (amomos). Jesus is the ultimate fulfillment of the tamim sacrifice, whose perfect life and death offer complete restoration.

• For the pastor and teacher, this word underscores the doctrine of the inerrancy and sufficiency of Scripture. If the Bible is tamim, it is free from error, fully complete, and totally trustworthy to guide, correct, and mature the believer.
