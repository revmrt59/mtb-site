PURE - ( barah , Strong's H1249 )

Summary:

The Hebrew adjective barah (the feminine form of bar) denotes that which is clean, clear, polished, and entirely free from impurities or foreign mixtures. In Psalm 19:8, it describes the commandment of the Lord as flawless, radiant, and unadulterated by human error or compromise. This divine purity does not merely refer to moral perfection but also to a dazzling clarity that penetrates spiritual darkness, bringing light and discernment to the eyes of the believer.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The word barah derives from a root verb that carries the primary physical concept of selecting, sorting, cleansing, or polishing.

• It is closely tied to ancient agricultural and metallurgical processes, such as winnowing grain to separate the wheat from the chaff, or refining precious metals in a furnace to purge away the dross.

• When applied as an adjective, it depicts something that has been thoroughly purged of any defilement, leaving behind only that which is genuine, bright, and utterly clean.

• In the ancient world, a "pure" object was one that was unmixed, shining with its natural luster, and free from any cheapening additives or deceptive mixtures.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• In its literal and concrete sense, the word means clear, bright, or clean.

• Semantically, it ranges from physical cleanliness (such as washed, clean hands) to celestial brightness (such as the dazzling, unobstructed light of the sun).

• In ethical and spiritual contexts, it signifies moral purity, sincerity of heart, and freedom from deceit, hypocrisy, or moral stain.

• Unlike other Hebrew terms for purity that focus primarily on ritual or ceremonial cleanness, this word emphasizes intrinsic quality—being unalloyed, uncompromised, and thoroughly refined.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Scriptures employ this word family to paint vivid pictures of absolute clarity and integrity:

• The Brightness of the Sun: The term captures the idea of blinding, radiant, and unstained beauty, comparable to the sun in a cloudless sky.

• Clean Hands: It represents actions that are entirely free from the guilt of sin, extortion, violence, or idolatry.

• A Pure Heart: It describes a heart that is undivided, sincere, and single-minded in its devotion to God, entirely free from duplicity.

• Refined Doctrine: It is used to describe teachings or declarations that are claimed to be free from error, compromise, or falsehood.

4. Theological Implications

• The purity of God's commandment reflects the very nature of God Himself. Because God is holy and true, His words are not a mixture of truth and error, nor do they contain any hidden, deceptive motives.

• Human laws and philosophies are always compromised by cultural bias, selfish ambition, or limited understanding. In contrast, the written Word of God is thoroughly refined, possessing no dross, no defect, and no moral compromise.

• The theological significance lies in the absolute reliability of divine revelation. Because the Word is pure, it can be trusted implicitly to guide the soul without leading it astray. It stands as an absolute, immutable standard of truth.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 19, David uses a series of six synonyms for God's Word (law, testimony, statutes, commandment, fear, judgments) to show its multi-faceted perfection.

• In verse 8, "the commandment of the Lord is pure," the focus is on its clarity and moral perfection. Because the commandment is barah (clear, radiant, unmixed), its immediate effect is "enlightening the eyes."

• Just as physical light heals the eyes and reveals the path ahead, the pristine clarity of God's Word dispels the darkness of human ignorance and moral confusion.

• There are no gray areas, deceptive loopholes, or confusing shadows in God's commands; they shine with a brilliant, guiding light that makes the path of righteousness unmistakably clear to the humble seeker.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Job 11:4 - "For you have said, 'My doctrine is pure, And I am clean in your eyes.'"

• Psalm 24:4 - "He who has clean hands and a pure heart, Who has not lifted up his soul to an idol, Nor sworn deceitfully."

• Psalm 73:1 - "Truly God is good to Israel, To such as are pure in heart."

• Song of Solomon 6:10 - "Who is she but looks forth as the morning, Fair as the moon, Clear as the sun, Awesome as an army with banners?"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The connection between purity and sight is a recurring biblical theme that bridges the Old and New Testaments.

• Jesus echoes this concept in the Beatitudes, stating that the "pure in heart" will "see God" (Matthew 5:8).

• In Psalm 19:8, the purity of the commandment performs a similar visual work: it clears the spiritual vision of the believer.

• When we submit to God's pure commandments, the "dross" of worldly thinking and self-deception is removed from our spiritual eyes, allowing us to see reality, sin, and God Himself with absolute clarity.
