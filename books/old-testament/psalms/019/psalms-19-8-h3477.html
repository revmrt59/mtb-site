RIGHT - ( yashar , Strong's H3477 )

Summary:

The Hebrew adjective yashar fundamentally denotes that which is straight, level, smooth, or right. In a moral and spiritual sense, it describes paths, actions, and character traits that align perfectly with God's standard of holiness and truth. In Psalm 19:8, it characterizes the divine "statutes" as straight and dependable guideposts that lead the traveler safely through life, resulting in a heart filled with genuine joy because they align with ultimate reality.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near East, travel was difficult and dangerous. Roads were rarely paved, and paths were often winding, rocky, steep, and treacherous. To find a path that was yashar—straight, level, and free of obstacles—was a traveler’s greatest relief. A straight road meant safety, directness, and a guaranteed destination, whereas a crooked road meant exhaustion, disorientation, and vulnerability to ambush.

Using this concrete imagery, the Hebrew mind understood spiritual life as a journey along a path. Human wisdom and sin make the path crooked, bumpy, and confusing. In contrast, God’s Word provides a path that is perfectly straight, level, and safe. When we walk according to His statutes, we are walking on a highway prepared by God, free from the hidden traps of deceit and moral compromise.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The adjective yashar is derived from the verbal root yashar, which means to be straight, smooth, right, or pleasing. Lexically, the adjective carries three primary dimensions of meaning:

• Physical: Straight, level, or even (used of paths, roads, or even legs).

• Ethical/Moral: Upright, just, virtuous, and honest. It describes a person or an action that conforms to an objective standard of rightness.

• Relational: Pleasing or right in the estimation of someone, most importantly in the eyes of God.

In the context of divine revelation, yashar defines the absolute integrity of God's Word. It is not crooked or twisted; it contains no hidden agendas, double standards, or misleading directions. It is straightforward and reliable.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, yashar is used to paint vivid spiritual pictures:

• The Upright Man: A person of integrity is often described as yashar (upright). This is someone who walks a straight line morally, refusing to veer to the left or to the right into compromise.

• Straight Paths: God is depicted as the one who makes the believer’s paths straight or level, removing the obstacles of sin and temptation that cause stumbling.

• The Danger of Self-Sovereignty: The term is central to the warning against subjective morality. In the book of Judges, the tragedy of Israel was that "everyone did what was right (yashar) in his own eyes." This contrast demonstrates that when human beings determine their own "straightness," they inevitably bend the line to suit their selfish desires. True straightness is defined only by God.

4. Theological Implications

The theological significance of yashar is rooted in the character of God Himself. God is described as upright and just; therefore, everything that proceeds from Him shares this quality.

• Objective Truth: The statutes of God are not relative. They do not bend to cultural whims, historical eras, or personal preferences. They are straight lines in a crooked world.

• The Basis of Divine Judgment: Because God's standards are straight, they serve as the ultimate plumb line. Anything that does not align with His yashar standards is crooked (aqash) and stands condemned.

• God’s Faithfulness: Because His Word is straight, we can trust His promises. He does not speak with a double tongue. His directions are clear, and His destination is secure.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19, David transitions from the general revelation of God in creation (verses 1-6) to the special revelation of God in His Word (verses 7-11). In verse 8, David writes: "The statutes of the Lord are right, rejoicing the heart."

The Hebrew word for "statutes" (piqqudim) refers to God's detailed guidelines, precepts, or life-directions. By calling these detailed guidelines yashar (right/straight), David is showing that God's specific instructions for daily living are not designed to restrict us, but to guide us safely.

Because these statutes are straight, they "rejoice the heart." Wandering in a wilderness of moral confusion and subjective choices is exhausting and anxiety-inducing. Finding the straight highway of God's truth brings an immediate sense of relief, security, and joy. The heart rejoices because it no longer has to guess the way; the path is clearly marked and level.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Deuteronomy 12:8 - "You shall not at all do as we are doing here today—every man doing whatever is right in his own eyes—"

• Job 1:1 - "There was a man in the land of Uz, whose name was Job; and that man was blameless and upright, and one who feared God and shunned evil."

• Psalm 33:4 - "For the word of the Lord is right, And all His work is done in truth."

• Proverbs 14:12 - "There is a way that seems right to a man, But its end is the way of death."

• Micah 7:2 - "The faithful man has perished from the earth, And there is no one upright among men."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament), yashar is frequently translated using the Greek word euthys (straight) or dikaios (righteous, just). This linguistic connection reinforces that moral righteousness is intrinsically linked to keeping a straight, unswerving course.

In the New Testament, this imagery is fulfilled in the ministry of John the Baptist, who came crying in the wilderness: "Make straight (euthys) the paths of the Lord." The coming of the Messiah demands that the crooked ways of human hearts be made straight to receive the King.
