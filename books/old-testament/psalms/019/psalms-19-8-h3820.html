HEART - ( leb, Strong's H3820 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word leb refers to the inner man, encompassing the mind, will, emotions, and moral character. In Psalm 19:8, it represents the center of human personality and affection, which finds deep, abiding joy when aligned with the righteous statutes of the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient Hebrew thought, the heart was not viewed merely as a physical pump or strictly as the emotional center of a person. Instead, the heart (leb) was understood as the "control room" of human life. It was the seat of the intellect, the source of decisions, the wellspring of emotions, and the foundation of moral character. The physical organ was associated with life itself, and metaphorically, the leb was the core of a person’s existence. To speak of the heart was to speak of the entire inner life of a human being.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term leb has a broad semantic range that describes the inner person.

• Cognitive/Intellectual: It refers to the mind, thoughts, understanding, and reflection. It is where a person thinks, plans, and reasons.

• Volitional: It refers to the will, choices, intentions, and determinations of a person.

• Affective/Emotional: It includes the range of human emotions, such as joy, fear, sorrow, courage, and love.

• Moral/Spiritual: It is the seat of moral conscience, integrity, and one's relationship with God.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The word leb is used extensively in the Old Testament to depict various spiritual and mental states:

• A "divided heart" (Hosea 10:2) describes double-mindedness or lack of total commitment.

• A "hard heart" (Exodus 4:21) describes a stubborn, unreceptive, and rebellious attitude toward God.

• A "circumcised heart" (Deuteronomy 10:16) represents an inner life that is consecrated and receptive to God's covenant.

• An "upright heart" (Psalm 119:7) refers to a person of moral integrity whose inner life aligns with God’s standards.

• The "heart of the sea" (Exodus 15:8) is a spatial metaphor meaning the very center or deepest part of the ocean.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, the leb is the primary arena where God interacts with humanity. God does not judge by outward appearances but searches and tests the heart. Because the natural human heart is corrupt and deceitful since the Fall, spiritual transformation must begin at the heart level. True religion is never merely external ritual; it is a matter of the heart. True obedience, worship, and faith must spring from a heart that has been renewed and cleansed by God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19:8, David writes, "The statutes of the Lord are right, rejoicing the heart." The "statutes" (piqqudim) refer to God's detailed directions for daily living. When these divine guidelines are received and digested by the leb—the seat of the intellect, will, and emotions—the result is profound joy (samach). This is not a superficial emotional high, but a deep, structural satisfaction that stabilizes the entire inner man. Because God's statutes are perfectly aligned with reality and righteousness, they bring order, peace, and delight to the believer's core identity and decision-making center.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Proverbs 3:5 - "Trust in the Lord with all your heart, And lean not on your own understanding;"

• Proverbs 4:23 - "Keep your heart with all diligence, For out of it spring the issues of life."

• Psalm 51:10 - "Create in me a clean heart, O God, And renew a steadfast spirit within me."

• Psalm 119:11 - "Your word I have hidden in my heart, That I might not sin against You."

• Jeremiah 17:9 - "The heart is deceitful above all things, And desperately wicked; Who can know it?"

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint), leb is consistently translated as kardia. This direct linguistic bridge connects the Old Testament concept of the heart to the New Testament's description of the inner life. Additionally, the structure of Psalm 19:8 pairs "rejoicing the heart" with "enlightening the eyes." This pairing indicates a progression: when the inner person (the heart) is filled with the joy of God's truth, it clarifies the outer person’s perception (the eyes) to see the world from God's perspective.
