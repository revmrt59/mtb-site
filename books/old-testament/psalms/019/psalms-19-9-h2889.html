CLEAN - ( tahor , Strong's H2889 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word tahor denotes that which is physically, ceremonially, or morally clean, pure, and uncontaminated. In the Old Testament, it describes physical purity (such as refined gold), ritual cleanliness required for worship, and ethical spotlessness before God. In Psalm 19:9, it characterizes "the fear of the LORD" as utterly free from defect, compromise, or corruption, explaining why it "endures forever" and remains a trustworthy guide for life.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root verb taher signifies the act of cleansing, purifying, or shining. In ancient Near Eastern metallurgy, tahor describes metals, particularly gold, that have been refined in the fire until all dross, slag, and impurities are completely purged away. In the ritual realm of ancient Israel, the word depicts the state of a person, vessel, or space that is fit to enter the presence of a holy God, completely free from the defilement of decay, disease, or pagan associations. The word picture is one of brightness, unclouded clarity, and absolute integrity.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word tahor refers to a state of being uncontaminated, unmixed, or unpolluted. Its semantic range covers three distinct areas:

• Physical/Material Purity: Refined, unalloyed materials (such as the "pure gold" used in the tabernacle furnishings).

• Ritual/Levitical Purity: Cleanness that permits participation in worship and community life, free from ceremonial defilement.

• Moral/Ethical Purity: Spiritual integrity, single-mindedness, and freedom from moral corruption or hypocrisy.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament uses tahor to paint vivid pictures of holiness:

• The Refiner’s Gold: Used extensively in Exodus to describe the furniture of the Tabernacle. The lampstand, the table of showbread, and the mercy seat were all constructed of "pure gold," denoting absolute quality and value, suitable for the dwelling place of God.

• The Healthy Leper: When a diseased person was cleansed, they were declared tahor, signifying the restoration of vitality, health, and community fellowship.

• The Clean Heart: In devotional literature, it describes a heart that is undivided and completely aligned with God's character.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of tahor is significant in several areas:

• The Character of God: Because God is holy, everything associated with Him must be tahor. It establishes a sharp boundary between the sacred and the profane.

• The Nature of Worship: Worship requires purification. To approach the living God, one must be cleansed from both external defilement and internal sin.

• The Permanence of the Divine: That which is tahor is not subject to the decay or corruption of the fallen world. Because it contains no seeds of corruption, it is inherently enduring.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19:9, tahor modifies "the fear of the LORD." This is a unique and striking pairing. Usually, tahor describes objects, animals, or people. Here, it describes a spiritual disposition—the reverent, worshipful awe of God. Within the context of Psalm 19, which praises God's law, "the fear of the LORD" is synonymous with true religion and living according to God's revealed instruction. Describing this fear as tahor means it is free from any corrupt motives, pagan superstitions, or self-serving hypocrisy. It is a pure devotion. Because this fear is completely clean and unpolluted, it "endures forever." It does not wear out, decay, or become obsolete like the compromised systems of the world.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 7:2 - "You shall take with you seven each of every clean beast, a male and his female; two each of beasts that are unclean, a male and his female;"

• Exodus 25:11 - "And you shall overlay it with pure gold, inside and out you shall overlay it, and shall make on it a molding of gold all around."

• Psalm 51:10 - "Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a steadfast spirit within me."

• Proverbs 22:11 - "He who loves purity of heart and has grace on his lips, the king will be his friend."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The word tahor stands in direct contrast to tame (unclean/defiled). In the ancient world, the clean (tahor) state was the baseline required for life in God’s camp. To be tahor is to be fit for life, whereas to be tame is associated with death, decay, and exclusion. By labeling the fear of the Lord as tahor, the psalmist indicates that true reverence for God is life-giving and preserves the believer from the moral decay that leads to spiritual death.
