LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh (translated as "LORD" in capital letters) is the personal, covenant name of the God of Israel. It represents Him not merely as an abstract Creator, but as the self-existent, eternal, and faithful God who binds Himself to His people. In Psalm 19:9, this sacred name is paired with "the fear of the LORD" and "the judgments of the LORD," emphasizing that true worship, reverence, and obedience flow from a personal, covenantal relationship with the living God, rather than a generic or distant deity.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The name Yahweh is the Tetragrammaton, the four-letter Hebrew name (YHWH) that is the most sacred designation for God in the Old Testament. Historically, the name is derived from the Hebrew verb hayah, which means "to be" or "to exist." This name was explicitly revealed to Moses at the burning bush in Exodus 3:14, where God declared His name as "I AM WHO I AM."

Because of the extreme reverence Jewish scribes and teachers had for this name, they avoided pronouncing it aloud to prevent taking it in vain, substituting it with the title Adonai (Lord) during public reading. This practice is preserved in modern English translations by rendering the name in capital letters as "LORD." The background of this word communicates absolute self-existence, eternal presence, and unwavering consistency. It is the name of the God who is always present, active in history, and intimately involved with His people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name denotes the self-existent, eternal, and unchanging God who is the source of all life and being. The semantic core of the word signifies "the One who is" or "the One who causes to be." Unlike Elohim, which is a generic title for God as the powerful Creator of the universe, Yahweh is a proper noun. It designates the personal God of revelation, redemption, and covenant faithfulness. It implies absolute independence; God does not depend on anyone or anything else for His existence, yet He voluntarily enters into binding relationships with His creation.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Yahweh occurs over 6,800 times in the Old Testament, far outnumbering any other name or title for God. It is used consistently in Scripture to depict God in relational and covenantal terms:

• The Covenant Partner: It is the name used when God makes promises to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

• The Redeemer: It is the name associated with the deliverance of Israel from Egypt (Exodus 20:2).

• The Shepherd: In the Psalms, Yahweh is depicted as a personal protector and guide (Psalm 23:1).

• The Contrast in Psalm 19: In the first six verses of Psalm 19, God is referred to as El (God as the powerful Creator) as the heavens declare His glory. However, in verses 7–14, as the psalmist shifts to describe the intimate, life-giving Word of God, the name shifts to Yahweh (LORD), which is repeated seven times. This structural shift shows that while nature reveals a powerful Creator (El), only Scripture reveals a personal, covenant-keeping God (Yahweh).

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of Yahweh is immense, carrying several critical truths:

• Immutability: Because Yahweh is the self-existent One, He does not change. His character, purposes, and promises remain identical yesterday, today, and forever.

• Aseity: God is entirely self-sufficient. He does not need humanity, yet He chooses to love and redeem humanity.

• Grace and Covenant: The name is inseparable from God’s gracious initiative to bind Himself to sinful people by covenant. It guarantees that He will never abandon His people or fail to fulfill His promises.

• Holiness and Authority: Because Yahweh is the ultimate reality, His words, laws, and judgments carry supreme, absolute authority.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19:9, the name Yahweh appears twice: "The fear of the LORD is clean, enduring forever; The judgments of the LORD are true and righteous altogether."

• The Fear of Yahweh: The "fear" (reverence and awe) described here is not a terror of an unpredictable deity, but a holy, cleansing awe of Yahweh, the faithful covenant God. This fear is "clean" and "enduring forever" because Yahweh Himself is eternally holy, pure, and unchanging.

• The Judgments of Yahweh: The "judgments" (judicial decisions and moral verdicts) are not arbitrary rules of a distant cosmic judge. They are the righteous declarations of Yahweh, the personal God who loves righteousness. They are "true and righteous altogether" because they reflect the perfect, reliable character of the Covenant Lord.

• Relational Obedience: By using Yahweh in this verse, David reminds the reader that submitting to God's law and fearing Him is not a cold, legalistic duty. It is the natural, joyful response of a person in a loving covenant relationship with the living God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:4 - "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens,"

• Exodus 3:15 - "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

• Psalm 23:1 - "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

• Proverbs 1:7 - "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge, But fools despise wisdom and instruction."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the New Testament, the Greek word Kyrios is used to translate Yahweh from the Hebrew Scriptures. When the New Testament writers declare that "Jesus Christ is Lord" (Philippians 2:11) or apply Old Testament passages about Yahweh directly to Jesus (such as Joel 2:32 in Romans 10:13), they are making a profound theological claim: Jesus of Nazareth is Yahweh manifest in the flesh, the eternal, covenant-keeping God of Israel who has come to save His people.
