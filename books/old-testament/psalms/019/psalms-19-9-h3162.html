ALTOGETHER - ( yachad , Strong's H3162 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word yachad signifies oneness, complete unity, and collective harmony. In Psalm 19:9, it qualifies the "judgments of the LORD," asserting that God's ordinances are not only individually true but also "righteous altogether." This indicates that God's truth forms a seamless, perfectly integrated, and cohesive whole, entirely free from internal contradiction, moral compromise, or deficiency.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root verb of yachad means to join, unite, or bind together. The noun and adverbial forms paint a vivid picture of diverse elements being brought into absolute alignment, agreement, or proximity. It evokes the image of a community living in perfect harmony, a well-constructed building where every stone fits snugly, or a beautiful tapestry where individual threads work together to create a single, breathtaking portrait. It suggests that nothing in God's economy is isolated or fragmented; instead, all parts are synchronized to function as a unified whole.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, yachad functions as an adverb meaning "together," "alike," "at the same time," or "wholly." It communicates the idea of unity in place, time, or quality. When used to describe characteristics or actions, it emphasizes that all the components in view share the exact same nature or are operating in total unison. There is no division, half-heartedness, or exception; the quality is possessed universally and simultaneously by every element.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, yachad is used to describe physical gathering and spiritual unity. It describes brothers dwelling "together" in peace, armies marching "as one man," and nature rejoicing in unified praise. In poetic literature, it is often employed to show that different entities are affected in the exact same way, such as when the rich and the poor, or the wise and the fool, lie down "alike" in the dust. The word underscores the absence of favoritism, discrepancy, or division.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, yachad points to the absolute integrity, consistency, and non-contradictory nature of God’s character and His written Word. Human philosophy and legal systems are frequently fragmented, requiring constant amendments to correct internal conflicts. In contrast, God’s truth is integrated. His holiness does not compete with His love, and His mercy does not compromise His justice. Every law, precept, and historical narrative in Scripture works in concert to reveal the singular, harmonious glory of God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 19:9, David declares, "The judgments of the LORD are true and righteous altogether." The "judgments" (mishpatim) are God's formal judicial decisions, His verdicts, and His governmental decrees. David asserts that these judgments are "true" (emet—stable, reliable, and faithful) and "righteous" (tsadequ—just, straight, and conforming to an absolute standard of holiness). The word yachad ("altogether") guarantees that this righteousness is not patchy or selective. Every single decree of God matches every other decree in perfect moral purity. If you look at one commandment, it is righteous; if you look at the entire counsel of God "together," it forms an unbroken, harmonious wall of absolute righteousness.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 133:1 - "Behold, how good and how pleasant it is / For brethren to dwell together in unity!"

• Psalm 34:3 - "Oh, magnify the LORD with me, / And let us exalt His name together."

• Job 21:26 - "They lie down alike in the dust, / And worms cover them."

• Isaiah 11:7 - "The cow and the bear shall graze; / Their young ones shall lie down together; / And the lion shall eat straw like the ox."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Because God's Word is righteous "altogether," believers cannot treat Scripture like a buffet, selecting some parts to obey while discarding others. To reject one of God's righteous judgments is to disrupt the perfect harmony of the whole. This Old Testament concept anticipates the New Testament truth in James 2:10, which states that stumbling in one point of the law makes one guilty of all. The entire body of divine revelation must be embraced, studied, and lived out as a unified, life-giving whole.
