FEAR - ( yirah , Strong's H3374 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun yirah in Psalm 19:9 refers to the reverent, awe-filled worship and obedience due to God. Far from a paralyzing terror, this "fear of the Lord" is a purifying, life-giving attitude of the heart that recognizes God’s supreme holiness and majestic authority. In the context of Psalm 19, it functions as a synonym for the Word of God itself—specifically, the response of worship that Scripture produces in the believer. It is described as "clean" (pure, free from defilement) and "enduring forever," establishing a permanent, unshakeable foundation for human life.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root verb yare indicates the physical and emotional reaction of trembling or shrinking back in the presence of something immense, powerful, or dangerous. In the ancient world, fear was the default response to erratic, hostile deities who demanded constant appeasement. However, Scripture transforms this concept. When applied to Yahweh, the covenant God of Israel, yirah becomes a response of holy awe, deep respect, and affectionate devotion. It is a posture of heart that trembles at His greatness while rejoicing in His goodness, drawing the worshiper closer in intimacy rather than driving them away in terror.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun yirah possesses a range of meaning including fear, dread, terror, reverence, awe, and piety. In its theological usage, particularly in the phrase "the fear of the Lord" (yirat YHWH), it represents the core of true biblical religion. It is the human subjective response to the objective revelation of God. It signifies a profound reverence that manifests as worship, obedience, and ethical living. It is the inner attitude of the soul that acknowledges God's absolute sovereignty and moral purity, resulting in a lifestyle that hates evil and loves righteousness.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

In Scripture, yirah is used in both secular and spiritual contexts:

• Secular dread: It can describe the terror experienced by humans during military invasion or natural disasters.

• Moral boundary: It acts as an internal guardrail, preventing believers from sinning because they realize they are always in the presence of the holy God.

• A fountain of life: Wisdom literature depicts the fear of the Lord as a life-giving source that protects a person from the traps of death, bringing security and peace rather than anxiety.

4. Theological Implications

The fear of the Lord is the starting point of all spiritual vitality. It is the beginning of wisdom and knowledge, meaning that no one can truly understand reality or live successfully in God's world without a fundamental reverence for the Creator. Yirah balances the intimacy of covenant relationship with the majesty of God's holiness. It prevents the believer from treating God with casual familiarity, ensuring that grace is never cheapened and mercy is received with profound gratitude. It links doctrine with devotion, showing that true knowledge of God must culminate in the worship of God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage (Psalm 19:9)

In Psalm 19:7-9, David uses six parallel terms to describe the excellence of God's written revelation: law (torah), testimony (edut), statutes (piqqudim), commandment (mitzvah), fear (yirah), and judgments (mishpatim).

• The substitution of the effect ("fear") for the cause (the Word of God) is a profound literary and spiritual device. The written Word is so closely identified with the worshipful fear it produces that they are called by the same name.

• This fear is described as "clean" (tahor), meaning free from ceremonial impurity, moral corruption, or foreign alloy. Pagan worship rituals of the ancient Near East were often morally degrading and physically defiling. By contrast, the worship of Yahweh is pure, cleansing the heart of the worshiper.

• It is also described as "enduring forever." Unlike human laws, cultural fads, or earthly kingdoms that decay, the true worship of God inspired by His eternal Word remains constant and relevant in every generation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 20:11: "And Abraham said, 'Because I thought, surely the fear of God is not in this place; and they will kill me on account of my wife.'"

• Proverbs 1:7: "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge, But fools despise wisdom and instruction."

• Proverbs 14:27: "The fear of the LORD is a fountain of life, To turn one away from the snares of death."

• Isaiah 11:3: "His delight will be in the fear of the LORD, And He shall not judge by the sight of His eyes, Nor decide by the hearing of His ears."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The ultimate fulfillment of yirah is found in the person of Jesus Christ. Isaiah 11:2-3 prophesied that the Messiah would be filled with the Spirit of the fear of the Lord and would actually "delight" in this fear. For Jesus, perfect obedience and total reverence toward His Father were not a burden, but a source of deep joy. This demonstrates that the fear of the Lord is not the opposite of joy, but its very foundation. When believers walk in the fear of the Lord, they are walking in the footsteps of Christ.
