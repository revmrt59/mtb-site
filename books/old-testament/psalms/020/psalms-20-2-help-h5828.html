HELP - ( ezer , Strong's H5828 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun ezer refers to vital, life-saving assistance, rescue, or strength provided to someone in a position of vulnerability or need. Far from denoting a subordinate or weak assistant, ezer represents an indispensable, strong helper, most frequently used in the Old Testament to describe God Himself as the sovereign deliverer and shield of His people. In Psalm 20:2, it signifies the powerful, sustaining aid sent from the sanctuary of God to preserve and strengthen His anointed king in the day of battle.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun ezer derives from a root associated with rescue, strength, and saving power. It carries strong military and covenantal overtones, suggesting an intervention by a stronger party on behalf of a weaker one who is facing an overwhelming crisis. In ancient Near Eastern culture, help was not merely a polite gesture but a critical, life-altering deliverance on the battlefield. The word conveys the picture of a shield, a stronghold, or a reinforcing army that arrives just in time to turn the tide of battle and prevent total destruction.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word ezer means aid, support, helper, or deliverance. It denotes one who provides the necessary strength, protection, or resources that the recipient completely lacks. The lexical range spans military reinforcement, divine deliverance in times of national distress, and essential companion partnership. It implies an active, powerful presence that steps into a crisis to secure victory or survival, rather than a passive onlooker or a menial servant.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• ezer occurs 21 times in the Hebrew Bible.

• The first occurrence is in Genesis 2:18, where God designs woman to be a "helper comparable to him" (ezer kenegdo). In this context, the helper is not a subordinate assistant, but an essential partner who supplies what is lacking in the man, enabling humanity to fulfill the divine mandate.

• The vast majority of its occurrences refer to God Himself as Israel's helper in times of desperate military or existential crisis. God is pictured as a shield and a sword (Deuteronomy 33:29) and as a defender who rides across the heavens to bring aid (Deuteronomy 33:26).

• The term is frequently paired with military terms like "shield" (magen) and "deliverer" (palat), highlighting that the "helper" is a powerful warrior standing between the vulnerable and their adversaries.

4. Theological Implications

• God as ezer reveals His deeply relational and intervening character. He is not a distant, unconcerned deity, but a covenant-keeping God who actively enters into human struggles.

• Seeking ezer requires an admission of human limitation and helplessness. It is an act of faith that relinquishes self-reliance to rely entirely on the sovereign power of Yahweh.

• Because God is the ultimate ezer, needing help is not a sign of spiritual failure or shame, but an opportunity for God to manifest His strength and faithfulness on behalf of those who trust Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 20:2, David writes a liturgical prayer for the congregation of Israel to pray over the king before he leads the army into battle. The petition, "May He send you help from the sanctuary," locates the source of this help directly in the dwelling place of God. The "sanctuary" (qodesh) and "Zion" refer to the place of the Ark of the Covenant, the earthly footstool of God's throne. The nuance here is that the king’s victory does not depend on the size of his army, but on transcendent, supernatural reinforcements dispatched from the very presence of God. This divine ezer is the spiritual fuel that will sustain the king's physical strength and guarantee victory over superior forces.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:18: "And the LORD God said, 'It is not good that man should be alone; I will make him a helper comparable to him.'"

• Deuteronomy 33:29: "Happy are you, O Israel! Who is like you, a people saved by the LORD, The shield of your help And the sword of your majesty!"

• Psalm 33:20: "Our soul waits for the LORD; He is our help and our shield."

• Psalm 121:2: "My help comes from the LORD, Who made heaven and earth."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The proper name Eliezer, meaning "God is my help," directly incorporates this noun. Moses named his son Eliezer because "the God of my father was my help, and delivered me from the sword of Pharaoh" (Exodus 18:4).

• In the Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament), ezer is translated using the Greek word boetheia, which carries the vivid imagery of running to the aid of someone crying out in distress. It is the same word used in Hebrews 4:16, inviting believers to draw near to the throne of grace to "find grace to help in time of need."
