SANCTUARY - ( qodesh, Strong's H6944 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word qodesh refers to that which is set apart, sacred, and consecrated to God. In Psalm 20:2, translated as "sanctuary," it points specifically to the tabernacle or temple as the focal point of God's holy presence among His covenant people. It represents the spiritual place of security from which divine help, strength, and answers to prayer flow to the believer in times of trouble.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root idea of qodesh is separation, cutting off, or setting apart from the common, profane, or everyday use for dedicated service to God.

• It is not merely a physical structure but a designated sphere of absolute holiness where God's localized glory dwells.

• In Israel's historical context, the sanctuary (initially the portable Tabernacle in the wilderness, later the stone Temple in Jerusalem) was the visual and spiritual center of the covenant community, representing God dwelling in the midst of His people.

• To speak of the qodesh is to contrast the sacred realm of God's presence with the common, mundane, and often hostile world outside.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The word qodesh denotes holiness, sacredness, a holy thing, or a holy place.

• When applied to a physical location, it refers to a consecrated sanctuary, the holy place of God's worship, or the inner sanctum of the temple.

• The term signifies something reserved exclusively for the divine, marked by purity, majesty, and absolute distinctness from the secular world.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The word is used extensively across the Old Testament to describe holy items (sacrifices, garments, vessels) and holy spaces (the Holy Place, the Most Holy Place).

• It creates a spatial word picture: a boundary line exists between the mundane world and the sacred space where God reveals Himself. To enter or seek help from the qodesh is to approach the throne of the King who is distinct from and elevated above all creation.

• It also depicts a refuge. While the world is chaotic, contaminated, and dangerous, the sanctuary is stable, secure, pure, and untainted by human failure. It is the place where God's holiness becomes a protective shield for His people.

4. Theological Implications

• Holiness is God’s essential nature, and the sanctuary is the earthly embassy of that holiness.

• God's help is not generic or detached; it is covenantal, originating from the very place where He meets His people through blood atonement, sacrifice, and intercessory prayer.

• The sanctuary demonstrates that access to a holy God is mediated. In the Old Testament, this was through priests and sacrifices. In the New Testament, this is fulfilled in Jesus Christ, who is our ultimate sanctuary and High Priest, allowing believers to boldly approach the throne of grace.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• Psalm 20 is a royal psalm, serving as a prayer for the king (David) before he goes out to battle.

• In Psalm 20:2, "May He send you help from the sanctuary," the word is paired poetically with "Zion." Zion is the physical hill in Jerusalem, but the "sanctuary" (qodesh) represents the spiritual reality of God's localized dwelling.

• The nuance here is that military victory does not ultimately depend on chariots, horses, or human strategy, but on divine intervention originating from the place of covenant relationship. Seeking help "from the sanctuary" means relying on the promises, power, and presence of God, who hears the cries of His anointed from His holy dwelling.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 15:11 - "Who is like You, O LORD, among the gods? / Who is like You, glorious in holiness, / Fearful in praises, doing wonders?"

• Psalm 63:2 - "So I have looked for You in the sanctuary, / To see Your power and Your glory."

• Psalm 150:1 - "Praise the LORD! / Praise God in His sanctuary; / Praise Him in His mighty firmament!"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• In the ancient Near East, pagan gods were often thought to be localized and restricted to their temples. While Israel knew the heavens could not contain God, they uniquely understood the sanctuary as the place where God chose to place His name and meet with His people out of sheer grace.

• The progression of the sanctuary in redemptive history moves from a portable tent (Tabernacle) to a stone temple, to the person of Jesus Christ who tabernacled among us, to the local church body as the temple of the Holy Spirit, and finally to the New Jerusalem where there is no physical temple because the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are its temple.
