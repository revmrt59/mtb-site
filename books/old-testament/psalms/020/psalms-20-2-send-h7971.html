SEND - ( shalach , Strong's H7971 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb shalach primarily means to stretch out, extend, direct, or dispatch a messenger or object to a specific destination with a clear purpose. In the context of Psalm 20:2, it expresses a passionate petition for God to actively dispatch His divine, covenantal assistance directly from His holy dwelling place to His people in their hour of trouble. This is not a passive wish, but a plea for an intentional, targeted deployment of divine power and grace, assuring the believer that help is actively sent by a sovereign God who hears prayer.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root concept of shalach involves stretching out the hand or letting loose.

• In ancient Near Eastern contexts, it describes extending one's hand to reap, sending forth a shoot or branch from a vine, or releasing a messenger on an urgent royal mission.

• It carries the strong implication of agency and authority. The sender possesses the position and resources to address a need, and the "sent" entity carries the authority of the sender to execute a specific task. The word picture is one of active extension—God reaching out from His heavenly throne or earthly sanctuary to intervene directly in human affairs.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The verb shalach occurs over 800 times in the Hebrew Bible, demonstrating its foundational role in describing divine and human agency.

• In its basic Qal stem, it means to send, direct, stretch out, or dispatch.

• In the Piel stem, it intensifies to mean sending away, letting go, or dismissing.

• In the Hiphil stem, it indicates casting or throwing.

• In Psalm 20:2, the verb is used in the Qal imperfect jussive form ("May He send"), expressing an earnest, prayerful desire for God to actively commission His help (ezer) as a personalized messenger or force sent directly to the petitioner.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Sent Messenger: Often used for royal decrees where a king sends a messenger with authority. The messenger does not act on his own but carries the full weight and authority of the sender.

• The Extended Hand: Used of stretching out the hand to touch, deliver, or strike. When God sends help, He is metaphorically stretching His own hand of power into the believer's situation.

• The Released Arrow: Just as an archer shoots or sends an arrow directly to its target, God’s sent help is targeted, swift, and hits its mark perfectly. It does not wander aimlessly; it is sent with sovereign precision.

4. Theological Implications

• Divine Initiative: Salvation, deliverance, and daily help are not self-generated. True help must be sent from outside of the human condition. God is the sovereign Sender, the ultimate source of rescue.

• Covenantal Faithfulness: God sends help because of His covenant relationship with His people. He does not remain distant or indifferent to the cries of His anointed.

• Theology of Mission: The concept of shalach lays the Old Testament groundwork for the sending of the prophets, the sending of the Holy Spirit, and ultimately the sending of the Messiah, Jesus Christ, who is the sent One who came to save.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 20:2, the setting is a corporate prayer for the king before he goes out to battle. The people pray: "May He send you help from the sanctuary, and strengthen you out of Zion."

• The sanctuary (qodesh) refers to the tabernacle or temple, the earthly place where God's localized presence dwelt among His people.

• The "help" (ezer) sent from the sanctuary is not abstract; it is covenantal protection and military deliverance. By praying that God "sends" this help, the psalmist acknowledges that true victory does not depend on human chariots or horses, but on the active, dispatched grace of Yahweh.

• The sanctuary is the source of this help, proving that God's holy presence is the fountainhead of all spiritual and physical strength.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 8:7 - "and he sent forth a raven, which kept going to and fro until the waters had dried up from the earth."

• Exodus 3:10 - "Come now, therefore, and I will send you to Pharaoh that you may bring My people, the children of Israel, out of Egypt."

• Psalm 43:3 - "Oh, send out Your light and Your truth! Let them lead me; Let them bring me to Your holy hill And to Your tabernacle."

• Psalm 107:20 - "He sent His word and healed them, And delivered them from their destructions."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Greek Septuagint translates shalach in Psalm 20:2 using the verb exapostello, which means to send forth or dispatch with an official commission.

• This Greek term carries strong administrative and authoritative weight. It is the same root used in the New Testament when God "sent forth His Son" (Galatians 4:4) and "sent forth the Spirit of His Son" (Galatians 4:6) into our hearts.

• The Old Testament petition for God to "send help" from His sanctuary finds its ultimate, historic fulfillment in the incarnation of Christ and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit, who are sent to be our ultimate Help and Comforter.
