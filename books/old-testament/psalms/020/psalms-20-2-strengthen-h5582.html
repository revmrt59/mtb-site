STRENGTHEN - ( sa'ad, Strong's H5582 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb sa'ad primarily means to support, sustain, or uphold. In Psalm 20:2, it describes the active, sustaining strength that flows from God's presence in Zion to uphold His people in times of trouble. Unlike temporary bursts of energy, this word conveys a deep, structural reinforcement—like a pillar holding up a building or a nourishing meal reviving a weary soul. It is a divine upholding that prevents the believer from collapsing under the weight of adversity.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The concrete, physical background of sa'ad involves supporting something that is weak, failing, or otherwise unable to stand on its own.

• In ancient Near Eastern culture, the word was heavily associated with nourishment. To "support" the heart meant to eat a meal, providing the physical body with the structural energy required to continue a journey or perform labor.

• Additionally, it carries an architectural connotation of establishing, propping up, or securing a structure with pillars so that it remains immoveable under pressure.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The verb sa'ad means to sustain, support, uphold, establish, or strengthen.

• In the Qal stem, it refers to the direct action of upholding someone physically, emotionally, or spiritually.

• In the context of divine action, it refers to God actively intervening to secure a person’s standing, ensuring they do not slip, fall, or fail under testing.

• It is not merely the impartation of raw power, but the personal, continuous application of God's stabilizing hand.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Physical Sustenance: The word is used for refreshing the body with food, showing that strength is imported from an outside source to keep the physical frame from failing (Genesis 18:5; Judges 19:5).

• Healing and Recovery: It represents God sustaining a person during times of extreme physical vulnerability, such as holding them up on a bed of sickness so they do not succumb to illness (Psalm 41:3).

• Spiritual and Moral Preservation: It depicts God holding up a believer's steps so they can walk in obedience and safety without falling into sin or ruin (Psalm 119:117).

4. Theological Implications

• Human Frailty vs. Divine Sufficiency: The need for sa'ad highlights human limitation. Humans are not self-sustaining; they require a continuous supply of divine grace to remain upright.

• The Source of Strength: True strength is localized in God's presence. In the Old Testament, this was represented by the sanctuary and Zion. Power to endure does not come from self-determination but from covenant fellowship with Yahweh.

• The King's Dependence: Even the anointed king of Israel, possessing military might, cannot stand without being structurally upheld by God. This strips away all reliance on human chariots and horses.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 20:2, the congregation offers a prayer for King David as he prepares for battle.

• The verse employs poetic parallelism: "May He send you help (ezer) from the sanctuary, And strengthen (sa'ad) you out of Zion."

• While "help" (ezer) refers to God's active assistance or rescue, "strengthen" (sa'ad) refers to the internal fortitude and structural support given to the king to withstand the battle.

• The strength is sent "out of Zion," meaning it is spiritual, covenantal, and relational, flowing directly from the place where God dwells among His people. It is a promise that God will be the king's ultimate support beam when the battle rages.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 18:5 - "And I will bring a morsel of bread, that you may refresh your hearts."

• Psalm 18:35 - "You have also given me the shield of Your salvation; Your right hand has held me up, Your gentleness has made me great."

• Psalm 41:3 - "The LORD will strengthen him on his bed of illness; You will sustain him on his sickbed."

• Psalm 119:117 - "Hold me up, and I shall be safe, And I shall observe Your statutes continually."

• Proverbs 20:28 - "Mercy and truth preserve the king, And by clemency he upholds his throne."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• There is a vital link between sa'ad and the ministry of Jesus Christ. In His humanity, Christ experienced the ultimate need for divine upholding, particularly in Gethsemane and on the cross, where He was sustained to accomplish redemption.

• For the Christian, this word serves as a pastoral comfort. When believers feel they are on the verge of collapsing under spiritual, emotional, or physical trials, they can pray Psalm 20:2, knowing that God's sustaining grace (sa'ad) is available to structurally support them through the Holy Spirit.
