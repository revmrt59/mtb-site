HEART - ( lebab , Strong's H3824 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word lebab represents the innermost center of human existence, encompassing the mind, emotions, will, and moral character. Far more than the physical organ or mere feelings, it is the seat of decision-making, desire, and spiritual devotion. In Psalm 20:4, it denotes the king's deep-seated desires, plans, and purposes, which are aligned with God's will and are therefore worthy of divine fulfillment.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near Eastern world, the heart was viewed as the command center of the entire human being. While modern Western thought separates the head (intellect) from the heart (emotions), biblical Hebrew combines them. The lebab is where thoughts are processed, decisions are forged, plans are formulated, and spiritual commitments are made. It is the workshop of human volition and the seat of moral character. To speak of the heart is to speak of the real person at their deepest, most authentic level.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The lexical definition of lebab covers the inner man, the mind, the will, the emotions, and the spiritual core.

• It refers to the seat of the intellect, understanding, knowledge, and reflection.

• It designates the source of emotions, including courage, fear, joy, sorrow, and love.

• It is the spring of volition, intention, purpose, and moral determination.

• It is the internal mirror of a person’s relationship with God, whether wholehearted, divided, or hardened.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament employs lebab in several figurative ways to illustrate the state of a person's inner life:

• The Wise Heart: A heart that possesses discernment, understanding, and the ability to process truth.

• The Whole or Perfect Heart: A heart characterized by undivided loyalty and integrity, completely devoted to the Lord.

• The Melted or Faint Heart: A graphic depiction of fear, anxiety, and a total loss of courage in the face of adversity.

• The Clean Heart: An inner life purified from sin and deceit, aligned with God's holiness.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, the lebab is the primary arena of God’s interaction with humanity. External actions are secondary; God looks directly at the heart. He searches it, tests it, and knows its secrets. True covenant relationship requires a circumcision of the heart, meaning a radical internal cutting away of sin and self-will. Because the unregenerate heart is prone to deceit, spiritual transformation must begin within the lebab through the renewing power of God's Spirit.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Psalm 20 is a royal liturgy, a prayer of the congregation for the king as he prepares to lead Israel into battle. In Psalm 20:4 ("May He grant you according to your heart's desire, and fulfill all your purpose"), the "heart's desire" refers to the king's strategic intentions and prayers for victory, security, and the preservation of God's covenant people. This is not a blanket promise for the fulfillment of selfish, carnal whims. Because the king of Israel is the Lord's anointed, his lebab is ideally aligned with God's desires. The congregation prays that God would bring to pass the noble, faith-filled purposes that have been conceived in the king's heart.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Deuteronomy 6:5: "You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your strength."

• Psalm 24:4: "He who has clean hands and a pure heart, Who has not lifted up his soul to an idol, Nor sworn deceitfully."

• Psalm 73:1: "Truly God is good to Israel, To such as are pure in heart."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A closely related word is leb (Strong's H3820), which is a shorter spelling with virtually identical meaning. Together, leb and lebab occur over 800 times in the Old Testament, making the "heart" the most common anthropological term in the Hebrew Scriptures. This high frequency underscores that biblical faith is never merely external or ritualistic; it is an intensely personal relationship that must capture the core of human identity.
