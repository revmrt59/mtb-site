FULFILL - ( male' , Strong's H4390 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb māle' fundamentally means to fill, overflow, or bring to completion. In Psalm 20:4, it describes God abundantly satisfying, completing, and bringing to full realization the king's plans and purposes. Rather than a superficial granting of wishes, it denotes a sovereign, divine action where God pours His grace, power, and success into human endeavors that align with His will, leaving no void or lack.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete background of māle' is spatial and physical. It represents filling an empty vessel with water, a land being populated with people, or a hand being filled with a sacrifice or weapon. It carries the vivid image of something empty or incomplete being packed to capacity, leaving no room for deficiency. In a pastoral sense, when God fulfills a plan, He does not merely permit it to happen; He fills it with His presence, substance, and success, transforming a human blueprint into a divine reality.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb māle' has a broad semantic range across its various stems:

• In the Qal stem, it means to be full, to fill, or to overflow.

• In the Piel stem (which is used in Psalm 20:4), it is causative and intensive, meaning to fill up, complete, satisfy, accomplish, or perform.

• It refers to the completion of a period of time, the filling of a space, the satisfying of a desire, or the accomplishment of a promise or petition.

• In the context of petitions and plans, it means to execute or bring to perfect completion, ensuring that the intended outcome is fully realized.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Filling physical space: Waters filling the seas or the glory of the Lord filling the tabernacle.

• Consecration: "Filling the hand" is the standard Hebrew idiom for ordaining a priest or dedicating someone to service, symbolizing being fully equipped and authorized.

• Fulfilling time: Serving a set number of years or reaching the end of a pregnancy.

• Satisfying soul/desire: Bringing satisfying completion to deep-seated longings or plans.

4. Theological Implications

• God is both the source and the agent of true fulfillment. Left to themselves, human plans remain empty and unfulfilled vessels.

• Divine fulfillment requires alignment with God's character. God does not "fill" selfish or wicked desires; He fulfills purposes that honor Him.

• This term highlights God's sovereignty and faithfulness. When God promises or ordains a path, He is faithful to bring it to its designed end, filling it with His power.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 20:4, the psalmist offers a prayer of blessing for the king as he prepares for battle: "May He grant you according to your heart’s desire, And fulfill all your purpose." The "purpose" refers to the king's military plans and strategies for the defense of God's covenant people. By asking God to fulfill these plans, the psalmist acknowledges that human strategic planning is empty without divine filling. The king may design the vessels, but only Yahweh can fill them with victory and success. It shifts the confidence of the army from horse and chariot to the active, filling hand of God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 1:28 - "Then God blessed them, and God said to them, 'Be fruitful and multiply; fill the earth and subdue it...'"

• Exodus 40:34 - "Then the cloud covered the tabernacle of meeting, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle."

• Psalm 20:5 - "We will rejoice in your salvation, and in the name of our God we will set up our banners! May the LORD fulfill all your petitions."

• 1 Kings 8:15 - "And he said: 'Blessed be the LORD God of Israel, who spoke with His mouth to my father David, and with His hand has fulfilled it...'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint) translates māle' in Psalm 20:4 using the verb pleroo. This is the same Greek word used in the New Testament to describe Jesus "fulfilling" the Law and the Prophets (Matthew 5:17) and believers being "filled" with the Holy Spirit (Ephesians 5:18). This links the Old Testament concept of God filling human plans with the New Testament reality of God filling believers with His own life and Spirit to accomplish His eternal purposes.
