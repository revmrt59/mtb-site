PURPOSE - ( etsah , Strong's H6098 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word etsah refers to counsel, advice, deliberate planning, or a well-conceived strategy. In Psalm 20:4, it denotes the king's military plans and godly designs for battle. Rather than representing a vague wish or self-centered ambition, it points to a structured, prayerfully considered plan aligned with God's will. God is petitioned to bring these strategic plans to successful completion, illustrating how human planning and divine execution cooperate when aligned with God's covenant purposes.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun etsah is derived from the primary root verb ya'ats, which means to advise, consult, deliberate, or resolve. In the ancient Near East, a king's etsah was not a sudden, impulsive desire, but the concrete output of formal consultation with wise counselors, military strategists, and priests. It carries the physical imagery of a firm, woven-together counsel that provides a solid foundation for action. To formulate etsah is to design a blueprint; it is the mental and strategic architecture that precedes any successful endeavor.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The semantic range of etsah spans several related concepts:

• Counsel or advice given by one person to another.

• Deliberate plan, purpose, or strategic design formed in the mind.

• The capacity for planning, wisdom, or prudence itself.

• The divine decrees and ultimate counsel of God, which stand immutable throughout history.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Scripture uses etsah in three primary domains:

• Human Advice and Consultation: The guidance shared between individuals, such as the famous strategic counsel of Ahithophel or the wisdom of elders.

• Human Strategy and Resolve: The internal plans formulated by individuals, especially leaders, to achieve a specific goal. The word picture is that of a deep well; a person’s inner plans are like deep waters that require understanding to draw out.

• Divine Determination: The absolute, sovereign plan of Yahweh. While human etsah can fail or be thwarted when it opposes God, the etsah of Yahweh is perfect, eternal, and completely unbreakable.

4. Theological Implications

The relationship between human planning and divine sovereignty is central to the theology of etsah. Scripture teaches that human beings are responsible to plan and exercise wisdom, but they must do so in submission to the Creator.

• Human planning is contingent; it only succeeds if it aligns with God's overarching decree.

• True wisdom begins with receiving God’s counsel. When human etsah is birthed out of a relationship with God, it reflects His righteousness and receives His blessing.

• The ultimate expression of etsah resides in the Messiah. He is the "Wonderful Counselor" who possesses the "Spirit of counsel and might," perfectly executing the redemption plan of God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 20:4, the congregation offers a liturgical prayer for King David as he prepares to lead Israel’s army into battle: "May He grant you according to your heart's desire, and fulfill all your purpose."

• The "purpose" (etsah) here is specifically the king's military strategy and campaign plans to defend the covenant nation.

• The prayer assumes that the king’s heart is aligned with Yahweh. This is not a blank check for selfish ambition, but a prayer for the success of plans made in faith, accompanied by sacrifices and prayers (verses 1–3).

• By asking God to "fulfill" (male) the king's etsah, the passage acknowledges that even the most brilliant human strategy is completely dependent on divine intervention for its success.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 33:11: "The counsel of the LORD stands forever, The plans of His heart to all generations."

• Proverbs 19:21: "There are many plans in a man's heart, Nevertheless the LORD's counsel—that will stand."

• Proverbs 20:5: "Counsel in the heart of man is like deep water, But a man of understanding will draw it out."

• Isaiah 46:10: "Declaring the end from the beginning, And from ancient times things that are not yet done, Saying, 'My counsel shall stand, And I will do all My pleasure,'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• Contrast of Counsel: Scripture frequently contrasts the etsah of the godly with the etsah of the wicked. Psalm 1:1 begins by blessing the man who "walks not in the counsel of the ungodly." While the counsel of the wicked is doomed to perish, the plans of those who rely on God are established.

• The Messianic Connection: In the New Testament, the Greek equivalent for etsah is often boule (purpose, counsel, decree). Acts 2:23 declares that Jesus was delivered up by the "determined purpose (boule) and foreknowledge of God," showing that the ultimate etsah of God was fulfilled through the cross.
