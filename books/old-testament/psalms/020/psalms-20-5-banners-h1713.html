BANNERS - ( dagal , Strong's H1713 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word dagal refers to the act of setting up, displaying, or marching under a banner, standard, or military signal. In the ancient Near East, banners represented identity, victory, and the protective presence of a sovereign ruler or commander. In the context of Psalm 20:5, raising banners in the name of God is an act of defiant faith, public allegiance, and triumphant celebration. It signals to both ally and enemy that the victory belongs to Yahweh, and that His people find their security and glory solely under His divine authority.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient world, military banners and standards were far more than decorative flags. They were rallying points used to organize troops, signal movements, and maintain order in the chaos of battle. A banner was typically a pole topped with a distinctive symbol, fabric, or emblem representing a tribe, a king, or a patron deity. When an army set up its banners, it was an assertion of ownership, presence, and readiness for battle. To display a banner was to declare whose territory was being claimed and under whose authority the soldiers fought.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb dagal means to set up a banner, to display a standard, or to be distinguished by a banner. In its passive forms, the word carries the sense of being outstanding, preeminent, or highly visible, much like a banner raised high above a crowd. The semantic range centers on public visibility, alignment, and military organization. It is closely related to the noun degel (Strong's H1711), which refers to the physical banner or standard itself.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Military Order and Majesty: The word evokes the image of a disciplined, formidable army advancing with all its colors and standards flying high. This represents strength, confidence, and unstoppable progression.

• Distinctive Preeminence: Just as a banner towers over the ranks of soldiers, the passive participle of dagal describes someone who stands out as singularly beautiful, prominent, or distinguished above all others.

• Covenant Assembly: The wilderness encampment of Israel utilized banners to group the tribes around the Tabernacle, symbolizing that God was the center of their life and the standard under which they marched.

4. Theological Implications

• Yahweh as Commander: Setting up banners in the name of God acknowledges that the Lord is the true Commander of His people. The battle is His, and the victory is credited to His name.

• Bold Public Confession: Flying a banner is an open, unapologetic declaration of allegiance. It shows that God's people do not hide their faith or their source of help; they display it proudly for the world to see.

• Preemptive Praise: Setting up banners before the final outcome is secured demonstrates absolute trust in God's promises. It is praise in advance, confident that the Lord will bring the petition to pass.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 20:5, the setting is a liturgical prayer for the king before he goes out to battle. The congregation expresses unshakeable confidence in God’s salvation. The phrase "in the name of our God we will set up our banners" stands in stark contrast to the military self-reliance of Israel's enemies. While pagan nations rely on the tangible strength of chariots and horses (v. 7), Israel's standard is the invisible yet omnipotent character of Yahweh. Raising these banners is a declaration of spiritual warfare, stating that Israel's military endeavor is entirely dependent upon and dedicated to the honor of God’s covenant name.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Song of Solomon 5:10 - "My beloved is white and ruddy, Chief among ten thousand." (Here, the passive participle dagul is translated as "Chief," describing one who stands out prominently like an elevated banner).

• Song of Solomon 6:4 - "O my love, you are as beautiful as Tirzah, Lovely as Jerusalem, Awesome as an army with banners!" (The participle nidgalot describes the striking, awe-inspiring sight of an army marching with its standards unfurled).

• Song of Solomon 6:10 - "Who is she but she who looks forth as the morning, Fair as the moon, Clear as the sun, Awesome as an army with banners?" (The same verbal form is used to depict formidable, majestic, and breathtaking beauty).

7. Other Relevant Insights

In ancient warfare, capturing an enemy's banner signaled total defeat and humiliation, while keeping one's banner raised high indicated that the line had held and victory was secured. When believers raise their banners in the name of God, they are claiming an undefeated position. This concept relates spiritually to the title Yahweh Nissi ("The LORD Is My Banner") in Exodus 17:15, where Moses built an altar after the defeat of the Amalekites, signifying that the Lord Himself is the rallying point and victorious banner of His people.
