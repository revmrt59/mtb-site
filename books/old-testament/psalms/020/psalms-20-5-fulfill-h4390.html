FULFILL - ( male', Strong's H4390 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb male' primarily means to fill to overflowing, or to bring to completion and fullness. In the context of Psalm 20:5, it carries the sense of fully granting, satisfying, or bringing to complete realization the petitions of the king. It portrays God not merely answering a prayer partially, but filling it up to the brim, leaving no lack or deficit in the deliverance and provision He grants to His anointed.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete, physical background of male' is rooted in ancient agricultural and domestic life. It is the visual image of filling a dry skin or clay vessel with water until it can hold no more, or filling a basket with the autumn harvest. The word evokes a sense of abundance, completion, and spatial saturation, such as a house filled with smoke or the earth filled with the glory of the Lord. In military and royal contexts, filling the hand of someone meant equipping them with authority or consecrating them for service. When applied to petitions or desires, the word picture is that of an empty vessel—representing a deep human need or request—being poured into by the hand of God until it overflows with His gracious answers.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The root verb male' means to fill, to be full, to consecrate, or to complete. In its active causative stem (the Piel form, which is used in Psalm 20:5 as yemalle'), the verb means to satisfy, accomplish, fulfill, or fully perform. It goes beyond the basic idea of "answering" a request; it signifies the exhaustive execution of a plan or the complete satisfaction of a desire. When God is the subject of this verb, it denotes His sovereign capacity to translate human prayers into physical, historical, and spiritual reality without leaving any part of the request unfinished.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, the word is used in several vivid ways:

• Physical Abundance: It describes a land overflowing with milk and honey, or vessels filled with oil and water.

• Time and Seasons: It refers to the completion of a set period, such as a pregnancy or the completion of seventy years of exile.

• Consecration: To "fill the hand" (mille’ yad) is the standard Hebrew idiom for ordaining or consecrating a priest for sacred service.

• Accomplishment of Plans: It is used of fulfilling vows or bringing words of prophecy to pass, showing that God's spoken word never falls empty but is always packed with substantive action.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, male' reveals that Yahweh is the God of fullness, in contrast to the empty and vain idols of the ancient Near East. God is not a stingy giver; He does not deal in partial answers or half-hearted victories. When He acts on behalf of His people, He does so to the point of overflowing satisfaction. Furthermore, the capacity to male' (fulfill) petitions rests entirely on God's sovereignty. The psalm shifts the focus of battle from human chariots and horses to the active, filling power of God’s name. This points forward to the ultimate fulfillment found in Christ, who fills all things in all places, and in whom the fullness of the Godhead dwells bodily.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Psalm 20 is a royal psalm of petition, sung by the congregation as the king prepares to lead the nation into battle. It is an appeal for divine favor and protection. In verse 4, the people pray, "May He grant you according to your heart’s desire, And fulfill all your purpose." In verse 5, they repeat the refrain with holy confidence: "May the LORD fulfill all your petitions." The placement of male' in this context emphasizes that the king's military plans and prayers are entirely dependent on Yahweh's divine completion. If the LORD does not "fill up" these plans, they will remain empty shells, leading to defeat. The congregation's joy and the raising of their banners are tied to the absolute certainty that God will saturate the king's request with His saving power.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 25:24: "So when her days were fulfilled for her to give birth, indeed there were twins in her womb."

• Exodus 28:41: "So you shall put them on Aaron your brother and on his sons with him. You shall anoint them, consecrate them, and sanctify them, that they may minister to Me as priests." (The Hebrew word translated "consecrate" is literally "fill their hand").

• 1 Kings 1:14: "There, while you are still talking with the king, I also will come in after you and confirm your words."

• Psalm 20:4: "May He grant you according to your heart's desire, And fulfill all your purpose."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Septuagint, male' in Psalm 20:5 is translated using the Greek verb pleroo. This is the exact same word used in the New Testament to describe Jesus Christ "fulfilling" the Law and the Prophets (Matthew 5:17), and the Holy Spirit "filling" believers (Ephesians 5:18). Consequently, the congregation’s prayer for the earthly king in Psalm 20:5 finds its ultimate, grandest fulfillment in the Great King, Jesus Christ, whose every petition was perfectly fulfilled by the Father, and who now fills His Church with every spiritual blessing.
