LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh (translated as "LORD" in all capital letters in the NKJV) is the personal, covenantal, and self-existent name of the God of Israel. Derived from the Hebrew verb meaning "to be," it highlights His eternal self-sufficiency, His unchangeable faithfulness, and His active, saving presence among His people. In Psalm 20:5, invoking Yahweh is not a generic appeal to a distant deity, but a covenantal plea to the sovereign King who has bound Himself to His people by promise, guaranteeing that their prayers are heard and their battles are won through His faithful character.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The name Yahweh is the Tetragrammaton, the sacred four-letter name of God (YHWH).

• It is derived from the archaic Hebrew verb hayah, which means "to be" or "to become." This refers directly to God's self-revelation to Moses at the burning bush: "I AM WHO I AM."

• The word picture behind the name is one of absolute, independent existence. Every created thing derives its life from something else, but Yahweh has life in Himself. He is the uncaused Cause, the eternal, and the self-sufficient One.

• Culturally, the name was deemed so sacred by ancient Israel that they refrained from pronouncing it aloud to avoid taking it in vain. Instead, they substituted the title Adonai (Lord) during public reading. This deep reverence emphasizes that the name represents the very presence, holiness, and majesty of God Himself.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Yahweh is the proper noun for the one true God, occurring over 6,800 times in the Old Testament, making it the most frequent designation for God in Scripture.

• The lexical meaning denotes the eternal, unchangeable, and active God of covenant. While Elohim emphasizes God's power as Creator and Ruler over all nations, Yahweh designates His relational, covenantal intimacy with Israel.

• The name signifies "He who is" or "He who brings into being." It points to God as the active agent in history who initiates relationships, makes promises, and brings those promises to pass.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The name is uniquely associated with God's redemptive actions. Whenever God delivers, rescues, or covenants with His people, Yahweh is the name used.

• In the Old Testament, this name is often combined with other terms to form descriptive titles that act as word pictures of His care, such as Yahweh-Jireh (The LORD Will Provide), Yahweh-Tsidkenu (The LORD Our Righteousness), and Yahweh-Nissi (The LORD Is My Banner).

• In Psalm 20:5, there is a direct conceptual link to this latter title: "And in the name of our God we will set up our banners!" Invoking the name of Yahweh is equivalent to raising a flag of victory, claiming His presence as the rallying point and source of triumph for the army.

4. Theological Implications

• Immutability: Because He is Yahweh, He does not change. His character, purposes, and love remain constant from age to age. This gives His people absolute security in prayer.

• Covenant Faithfulness: The name is the ultimate guarantee of God's promises. He is bound by His own name to fulfill His word. If Yahweh failed to keep a promise, He would cease to be Yahweh.

• Sovereign Grace: The name reminds the believer that God's favor is not earned. Just as He sovereignly chose Abraham and redeemed Israel from Egypt based on His own character, He acts on behalf of His people today out of His self-determined grace.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• Psalm 20 is a royal liturgy, a prayer offered by the congregation for the king as he prepares to lead Israel into battle.

• In Psalm 20:5, "May the LORD fulfill all your petitions," the petition is anchored in the character of Yahweh. The petition is not a hopeful wish directed into a void, but a confident appeal to the covenant Partner who has bound His own glory to the survival and victory of the Davidic king.

• The immediate context contrasts human resources with divine resources. In verse 7, some trust in chariots and horses, but Israel trusts "in the name of the LORD our God." Calling upon Yahweh in verse 5 is an act of defiance against the overwhelming physical power of pagan nations, declaring that the covenant name of God is more powerful than any military force.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:4 - "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens,"

• Exodus 3:15 - "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

• Psalm 23:1 - "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

• Proverbs 18:10 - "The name of the LORD is a strong tower; The righteous run to it and are safe."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• In the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint), Yahweh was translated using the Greek word Kurios (Lord). This same word Kurios is used extensively in the New Testament.

• When the New Testament writers proclaim "Jesus is Lord" (Romans 10:9; Philippians 2:11), they are intentionally applying the Septuagint’s translation of Yahweh to Jesus Christ. To confess Jesus as Kurios is to identify Him as the very same covenant-keeping, self-existent God who revealed Himself to Moses and who answers the prayers of His people in Psalm 20.
