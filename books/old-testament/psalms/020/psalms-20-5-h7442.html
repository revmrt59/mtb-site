REJOICE - ( ranan , Strong's H7442 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word ranan describes a loud, vibrant cry—a ringing shout of joy, praise, or even a shrill cry of distress, though most frequently it designates an exuberant, vocal expression of triumph and gladness. In Psalm 20:5, it captures the congregation's anticipatory shout of victory in God's saving power, demonstrating that biblical rejoicing is not merely a quiet, internal feeling but a visible, audible, and communal declaration of trust in God's deliverance.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• ranan carries the primary physical concept of producing a shrill, piercing, or ringing sound. It represents a voice raised to its highest pitch. Unlike words for joy that focus on internal gladness of heart, ranan is fundamentally vocal and audible. It is the cry that bursts forth when the heart can no longer contain its emotion. In ancient Israelite culture, this shouting was common in two main contexts: the field of battle (a war cry of triumph) and the temple courts (a shout of praise during worship). It paints a picture of a people so captivated by God's deliverance that their joy physically overflows into a resounding, triumphant shout.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb ranan means to shout for joy, cry out, or give a ringing cry. Depending on the stem, it signifies:

• To emit a loud, ringing cry of joy or praise.

• To exult, rejoice, or sing aloud.

• In rare contexts, to cry out in grief or lamentation (such as crying out in the night in Lamentations 2:19).

The predominant usage in the Old Testament, particularly in the Psalms, is the joyous shout of worship and victory. It is a highly active, public, and energetic form of praise.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• ranan is frequently paired with other terms of joy, music, and victory. It is often translated as "sing aloud," "shout for joy," or "rejoice." The word pictures associated with ranan include:

• Nature personified: The wilderness, the trees of the forest, and the hills are described as "shouting for joy" at the presence and reign of God (Psalm 96:12; Isaiah 44:23).

• The Victorious Army: Soldiers shouting in triumph after a battle has been won.

• The Temple Choir/Congregation: Worshipers raising a collective, thunderous shout of praise to God as King and Deliverer.

4. Theological Implications

First, biblical joy is active and vocal. It is not a passive or silent emotion; it demands physical expression through singing and shouting. Second, our joy is deeply relational and theological. The object of ranan is almost always Yahweh, His character, His creation, or His salvation. It is a response to who God is and what He has done. Third, shouting for joy is an act of faith. In many passages, the shout of praise is offered before the visible deliverance is fully realized, serving as an audible demonstration of absolute trust in God's covenant promises.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 20:5, the setting is a liturgical prayer for the king before he goes out to battle. The congregation declares, "We will rejoice (ranan) in your salvation." Here, ranan is a collective vow of praise. The people are so confident in God's impending victory for their king that they promise to raise a ringing shout of triumph. This rejoicing is paired with "setting up our banners" in the name of God. Just as a victorious army raises its flags and shouts on the battlefield, the people of God celebrate His saving work. The rejoicing is not in human military might, chariots, or horses (as verse 7 warns), but specifically "in your salvation"—the deliverance wrought by Yahweh.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Leviticus 9:24 - "and fire came out from before the LORD and consumed the burnt offering and the fat on the altar. When all the people saw it, they shouted and fell on their faces."

• Psalm 5:11 - "But let all those rejoice who put their trust in You; Let them ever shout for joy, because You defend them; Let those also who love Your name Be joyful in You."

• Psalm 35:27 - "Let them shout for joy and be glad, Who favor my righteous cause; And let them say continually, 'Let the LORD be magnified, Who has pleasure in the prosperity of His servant.'"

• Isaiah 12:6 - "Cry out and shout, O inhabitant of Zion, For great is the Holy One of Israel in your midst!"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between ranan and quiet contemplation highlights the diversity of biblical worship. While there is a time for silence before the Lord, there is also an imperative command to shout aloud. Pastors can use this word to encourage believers to overcome reserve and self-consciousness in worship, recognizing that vocal, enthusiastic praise is a biblical standard for celebrating God’s victories. In the New Testament, the equivalent spiritual posture is often captured by the Greek word agalliao, which means to exult or leap for joy, expressing the same exuberant, overflowing gladness in response to God's salvation.
