ANOINTED - ( mashiach , Strong's H4899 )

Summary:

The Hebrew term mashiach refers to an individual consecrated and set apart for a specific divine office—such as a king, priest, or prophet—through the ceremonial pouring or smearing of oil. In Psalm 20:6, "His anointed" refers contextually to the Davidic king, whom God has chosen, empowered, and promised to deliver. This concept serves as the foundational Old Testament bedrock for the New Testament realization of Jesus as the Messiah (the Christ), the ultimately anointed King-Priest who secures salvation for His people.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near East, anointing was not a cosmetic act but a profound legal and spiritual ceremony. The ritual of pouring specially prepared oil over the head of an individual signified a transition from the common to the sacred. Oil, a symbol of abundance, joy, and the life-giving presence of the Holy Spirit, represented the divine empowerment necessary to fulfill a God-given task.

To be the "anointed" one meant that God had personally chosen, authorized, and equipped the individual for service. The person was no longer acting under their own authority; they became Yahweh’s representative on earth. In Israel's history, this ritual was primarily reserved for the high priest and the king, marking them as holy and set apart.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun mashiach translates directly as "anointed one." It is derived from the verbal root mashach, which means to smear, rub, or paint with oil.

The semantic range of mashiach includes:

• An anointed king (the most frequent usage, particularly referring to Saul, David, or the Davidic dynasty).

• An anointed priest (specifically the high priest, who bore the spiritual burdens of the nation).

• The Messiah (the ultimate, eschatological Deliverer promised to Israel).

In the Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint), mashiach is translated as Christos, which is the direct source of the New Testament title "Christ."

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

The physical application of oil in the Old Testament provides a powerful word picture of spiritual reality. When oil was poured on the head, it ran down the face, collar, and garments, symbolizing a total covering and immersion in God’s provision.

This act established a protective boundary around the individual. Because the king was the "Lord's anointed," his life was treated as sacrosanct. David repeatedly refused to harm King Saul, despite Saul's unjust pursuit of him, because Saul was Yahweh's mashiach. The term depicts someone who is shielded by divine ownership; to touch the anointed one was to assault the authority of the God who anointed him.

### 4. Theological Implications

Theologically, mashiach links the offices of king, priest, and prophet, which find their perfect culmination in Jesus of Nazareth.

First, the anointed king was God’s vice-regent. Israel was a theocracy, meaning the human king did not possess absolute sovereignty; he was a servant-king under Yahweh.

Second, the destiny of the nation was directly bound to the spiritual and physical condition of the mashiach. If the king was faithful, the nation experienced blessing; if the king was rescued, the nation was saved.

Third, the concept of the mashiach points forward to a future, perfect King from the line of David who would not merely reign temporarily, but would rule eternally, fully anointed with the Holy Spirit and power.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 20, a liturgical prayer offered by the congregation before the king went out to battle, the focus is on divine deliverance rather than human military might.

In verse 6, the psalm shifts from corporate petition ("May the Lord answer you...") to personal, prophetic certainty: "Now I know that the LORD saves His anointed."

The contextual nuance of mashiach here is the absolute security of the Davidic king under the covenant faithfulness of Yahweh. The king’s battle is God's battle. The "saving strength of His right hand" guarantees that the mashiach will not be abandoned on the battlefield. Because the king is God's anointed, his victory is assured, which in turn secures peace and salvation for the entire covenant community.

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

Unlike neighboring pagan nations where kings declared themselves to be gods, Israel's mashiach was strictly human and answerable to God's law. The requirement of anointing served as a constant reminder to the king of his dependence on God.

The theological connection between the mashiach and the Holy Spirit is explicitly demonstrated in 1 Samuel 16:13, where the physical anointing of David was immediately accompanied by the rushing of the Spirit of the Lord upon him. This establishes that the physical oil was an outward sign of an inward, spiritual endowment for leadership.
