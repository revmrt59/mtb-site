ANSWER - ( 'anah , Strong's H6030 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'anah carries the primary meaning of responding, replying, or answering. When used of God, it denotes His active, powerful, and faithful intervention in response to human prayer, demonstrating His relational closeness and sovereign control. In Psalm 20:6, it highlights the assurance that God does not remain silent to the cries of His anointed King, but responds with decisive, saving action from His holy heaven.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root 'anah conveys the basic verbal or physical concept of responding to an action, statement, or cry. It is fundamentally a relational word, presupposing a prior communication. In the Ancient Near East, where gods were often viewed as distant, capricious, or unresponsive, the God of Israel is depicted as a personal deity who hears and responds. The word picture of 'anah is that of a dialogical relationship. When God's people cry out in distress, He does not remain passive. The cry is answered by a corresponding divine movement. Thus, the background of the word is deeply tied to the covenant relationship: God binds Himself to His people, making Himself accessible and responsive to their voices.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb means to answer, respond, speak up, or testify. In contexts of prayer and divine-human communication, it goes beyond a mere verbal reply to mean granting a request or intervening dynamically on behalf of the petitioner.

• To answer or reply to a specific verbal address.

• To testify or witness in a judicial setting (to respond to a legal charge).

• To respond favorably to a prayer, which involves a physical act of deliverance or salvation.

• To sing responsively or shout in triumph.

In the context of divine activity, the word indicates a hearing that results in action; God's "answering" is synonymous with His saving intervention.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, the word is used in several vivid contexts:

• The God who Answers by Fire: In the confrontation on Mount Carmel, the true God is identified as "the God who answers by fire." The false god Baal cannot answer, remaining silent and powerless, while Yahweh answers with a visible, consuming demonstration of His presence.

• Responding from the Sanctuary: In the Psalms, the petitioner often cries out from the depths of despair, and God answers from His holy hill or sanctuary. The word picture contrasts the lowliness of the human cry with the majestic, transcendent height from which the divine answer descends.

• The Answer of Deliverance: Often, to "answer" is to bring the petitioner out of a tight or distressing place into a spacious place of safety. The answer is not a set of words, but a historical event of rescue.

4. Theological Implications

The theology of 'anah reveals several key truths about the character of God:

• God is Relational: God is not an impersonal force or a silent watchmaker. He participates in a dynamic relationship with His creation. He speaks, listens, and responds.

• The Efficacy of Prayer: Prayer is not a psychological exercise. The reality of 'anah proves that prayer moves the hand of God. Human petitions are met with real, divine responses.

• The Contrast with Idols: Scriptural polemics often mock idols because they "have mouths, but they do not speak" and cannot answer in the day of trouble. Yahweh's ability to answer is proof of His exclusive deity.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Psalm 20 is a royal psalm, a prayer of the congregation for the Davidic king as he prepares to go out to battle. The psalm begins as a petition: "May the LORD answer you in the day of trouble" (v. 1). However, in verse 6, there is a dramatic shift from petition to absolute certainty: "Now I know that the LORD saves His anointed; He will answer him from His holy heaven..."

The nuance of 'anah here is the transition from plea to promise. The congregation has praying confidence that when the king cries out on the battlefield, the response will come not from earthly allies, chariots, or horses, but from "His holy heaven." Furthermore, the "answer" is specifically defined as "the saving strength of His right hand." God's answer to His anointed is victory over the enemy.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 20:1: "May the LORD answer you in the day of trouble; May the name of the God of Jacob defend you;"

• Psalm 91:15: "He shall call upon Me, and I will answer him; I will be with him in trouble; I will deliver him and honor him."

• Isaiah 58:9: "Then you shall call, and the LORD will answer; You shall cry, and He will say, ‘Here I am.’"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• Messianic Fulfillment: The term "His anointed" (mashiach) in verse 6 ultimately points to Jesus Christ, the ultimate Davidic King. During His earthly ministry, and most intensely in the Garden of Gethsemane and on the cross, the Son cried out to the Father. God answered Him—not by sparing Him from death, but by raising Him from the dead. The resurrection is God’s ultimate, definitive "answer" of saving strength.

• The Heavenly Sanctuary: The answer comes "from His holy heaven." This reminds the believer that the ultimate source of help and victory is transcendent. Earthly crises cannot block or limit the power of the divine response, which descends from the throne of the universe.
