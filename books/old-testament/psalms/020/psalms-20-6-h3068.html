LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh (translated as "LORD" in all capital letters) is the personal, covenant-keeping name of the God of Israel. Revealed uniquely to Moses at the burning bush, it denotes God's self-existence, eternal presence, and unwavering faithfulness to His promises. In the context of Psalm 20:6, this sacred name serves as the anchor for Israel's confidence. It is not a generic title for deity, but the specific name of the covenant Partner who actively intervenes, saves His anointed king, and guarantees victory for His people.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The name Yahweh is intimately connected to the Hebrew verb hayah, which means "to be" or "to become." When God revealed this name to Moses in Exodus 3:14, He explained it as "I AM WHO I AM."

• Unlike generic titles for God (such as Elohim, which emphasizes His power as Creator), Yahweh is His personal, proper name.

• In ancient Near Eastern culture, a name was not merely a label but a representation of a person's character, authority, and presence. To know God's personal name was to be invited into a relationship of mutual commitment and access.

• The visual representation of this name in Hebrew scrolls—the Tetragrammaton (YHWH)—was considered so holy by later Jewish tradition that they avoided pronouncing it aloud to prevent taking it in vain, substituting it with Adonai (Lord). English translations preserve this distinction by rendering YHWH as "LORD" in all capital letters.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Yahweh designates the self-existent, eternal, and unchangeable God.

• The name signifies "He who is" or "He who brings into being." It points to God's absolute independence; He relies on nothing outside of Himself for His existence, power, or counsel.

• It expresses His immanence and active presence with His people. He is the God who is present to save, deliver, and uphold His covenant obligations.

• The semantic core of the name is relational faithfulness, marking Him as the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, who remembers His covenant forever.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Rock of Covenant Security: The name Yahweh is frequently paired with military, relational, and protective imagery (such as rock, shield, and fortress) to show that His eternal nature is the believer's defense.

• The Present Deliverer: Throughout the Old Testament, the name is invoked when God's people are in distress, shifting their focus from their overwhelming circumstances to His unchanging character.

• The Breath of Life: Because the letters Y-H-W-H consist of soft, breathing consonants, ancient writers noted that the name itself mimics the sound of breath, symbolizing that He is the very source of life and vitality.

4. Theological Implications

• Sovereignty and Self-Sufficiency: Because Yahweh is self-existent, He is completely sovereign over history, nations, and individual lives.

• Unconditional Faithfulness: God's covenant promises do not depend on human strength but on His own character. He cannot deny Himself; therefore, He cannot fail His covenant partners.

• The Grounding of Faith: Faith is not an abstract belief in a higher power; it is trust in a specific Person who has bound Himself to His people by His name.

• Redemption: The name Yahweh is always linked to deliverance. He is not a distant observer but an active Savior who steps into human history to redeem.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 20:6, the name Yahweh occurs in a liturgy of prayer and confidence before battle.

• The psalmist writes, "Now I know that the LORD saves His anointed." The confidence of victory is not in chariots or horses, but in the character of the covenant God.

• The use of Yahweh here highlights the relationship between God and "His anointed" (the king). Because the king is God's representative, Yahweh is covenantally obligated to answer him from His holy heaven.

• The transition from petition ("May the LORD answer you," verse 1) to certainty ("Now I know...") rests entirely on the unshakeable nature of Yahweh. His name is the guarantee that prayer is answered not with empty words, but with "the saving strength of His right hand."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 3:15: "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

• Psalm 23:1: "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

• Proverbs 18:10: "The name of the LORD is a strong tower; The righteous run into it and are safe."

• Isaiah 42:8: "I am the LORD, that is My name; And My glory I will not give to another, Nor My praise to carved images."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Connection to Jesus: In the New Testament, the Greek equivalent of Yahweh (used in the Septuagint to translate YHWH) is Kurios. Jesus applies the divine "I AM" (Ego Eimi) statements to Himself in the Gospels (such as John 8:58), claiming the very identity of the covenant God of Israel.

• The Name as a Refuge: To "remember the name of the LORD" (Psalm 20:7) means to rely on His demonstrated character rather than earthly resources. In spiritual warfare, our primary weapon is the character and promises of God.
