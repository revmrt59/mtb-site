RIGHT HAND - ( yamin , Strong's H3225 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word yamin primarily denotes the right hand or right side, symbolizing power, authority, favor, and salvation in the Old Testament. In the context of Psalm 20:6, it represents Yahweh's active, personal, and irresistible sovereign power deployed to rescue His anointed king, assuring the covenant community of ultimate victory not through human military might, but through divine intervention.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near East, the right hand was culturally recognized as the hand of strength, action, and covenant oath. It was the hand used to wield weapons of war, bestow the primary patriarchal blessing, and signify the ultimate position of honor at a host's or king's side. To stand at someone's right hand was to offer protection as a shield-bearer in battle. Consequently, the right hand represents the primary instrument of a person’s skill, power, and active presence.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Literally, yamin refers to the right hand or the right side of the physical body. By geographical extension, because ancient orientation was based on facing the rising sun in the east, yamin also designates the direction of the south. Metaphorically, yamin denotes supreme power, absolute authority, active protection, sovereign deliverance, and the place of highest honor and favor.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Instrument of Battle and Victory: The right hand is the fighting hand, representing the active execution of judgment or deliverance against enemies.

• The Position of Honor: Sitting at the right hand of a ruler indicates sharing in that ruler's authority, prestige, and administrative power.

• The Place of Security: Having the Lord at one's right hand or being held by His right hand represents a state of absolute safety and divine advocacy.

• The Bestowal of Blessing: The right hand was specifically extended to impart the primary, double-portion blessing of the firstborn.

4. Theological Implications

The "right hand" of God is a vital anthropomorphism used by biblical writers to describe the active, personal, and visible exercise of His omnipotence in human history. God does not remain passive or distant; He reaches out His right hand to save.

• It is inextricably linked with salvation and redemption, particularly echoing the Exodus event where God delivered Israel with a mighty hand.

• It guarantees the preservation of His covenant people, assuring them that their ultimate destiny is held by His unshakeable grasp.

• It carries messianic significance, pointing forward to the ultimate Ruler who sits at the right hand of the Majesty on high, executing both salvation and final judgment.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 20:6, the setting is a liturgical prayer of the congregation for the king as he prepares to face an impending military conflict. The surrounding verses contrast the fragile, earthly confidence of the enemy ("some trust in chariots, and some in horses") with Israel’s trust in the name of the Lord.

The transition in verse 6 ("Now I know...") marks a shift from petition to absolute, prophetic certainty. The psalmist realizes that the king's victory does not depend on the physical hands of his soldiers, but on the "saving strength of His [God's] right hand." Here, yamin emphasizes that God’s own personal, sovereign power is actively engaged to deliver His anointed. It is a supernatural strength that makes human armaments irrelevant.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 15:6: "Your right hand, O LORD, has become glorious in power; Your right hand, O LORD, has dashed the enemy in pieces."

• Psalm 16:11: "You will show me the path of life; In Your presence is fullness of joy; At Your right hand are pleasures forevermore."

• Psalm 110:1: "The LORD said to my Lord, 'Sit at My right hand, Till I make Your enemies Your footstool.'"

• Isaiah 41:10: "Fear not, for I am with you; Be not dismayed, for I am your God. I will strengthen you, Yes, I will help you, I will uphold you with My righteous right hand."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• Directional Orientation: Because yamin represents the south, biblical geography frequently uses the term to define boundaries and territories relative to the land of promise, demonstrating how deeply integrated the physical right hand was to their spatial understanding of the world.

• Contrast of Hands: While the left hand is occasionally associated with stealth or secondary honor, the right hand is consistently elevated as the primary instrument of divine covenantal faithfulness and ultimate power.
