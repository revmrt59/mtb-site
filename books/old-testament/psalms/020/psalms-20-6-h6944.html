HOLY - ( qodesh , Strong's H6944 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun qodesh denotes apartness, sacredness, and consecration. It refers to that which is set apart from the common, profane, or ordinary and dedicated entirely to God, or represents God's own unique, transcendent purity. In Psalm 20:6, "holy" describes God's heavenly dwelling place (misheme qodesho—literally, "the heavens of His holiness"), emphasizing that God answers prayers from a realm of absolute purity, sovereign power, and uncompromised authority.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete idea behind qodesh is separation, withdrawal, or a marked boundary. In the ancient world, a clear distinction existed between the sacred (qodesh) and the profane or common (chol). To be "holy" is to be cut off from ordinary, mundane use and reserved exclusively for the divine. When applied to places, objects, or people, it indicates they have been claimed by God, brought into His sphere of ownership, and must be treated with utmost reverence. The word picture is that of a sacred boundary line—a sanctuary perimeter—that protects the pure from the defilement of the common world.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• qodesh is a masculine noun meaning:

• Holiness, sacredness, or saintliness.

• A holy thing, sanctuary, or holy place.

• Consecration or a state of dedication.

When describing God, qodesh refers to His infinite, majestic, and ethical purity. He is completely distinct from His creation and utterly untainted by evil. In Psalm 20:6, the word functions as an attributive genitive ("heavens of His holiness"), which is rightly translated in the NKJV as "His holy heaven." This indicates that heaven is God's unique, unassailable, and perfectly pure sanctuary.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, qodesh is used to describe:

• Holy Spaces: The inner sanctuary of the Tabernacle and Temple is called the "Holy of Holies" (qodesh ha-qodashim), the most sacred place on earth where God's presence dwelt.

• Holy Objects: The vessels, altars, and priestly garments (bigde qodesh) were set apart solely for God's service; using them for ordinary purposes was a desecration.

• Holy Time: The Sabbath and sacred feasts were marked off from the ordinary workweek as holy days of rest and worship.

• A Holy Nation: Israel was called to be a "holy kingdom," separated from pagan practices to live in covenant faithfulness to Yahweh.

### 4. Theological Implications

The holiness of God is the foundational reality of His being. It is not merely one attribute among many; it is the qualifyng characteristic of all His attributes. His love is holy love, His justice is holy justice, and His power is holy power.

• Because God is holy, He cannot tolerate sin or injustice.

• The "holiness" of heaven ensures that His sovereign rule is free from the corruption, weakness, and decay that plague earthly rulers and nations.

• Salvation is not a compromise of God's holiness, but a demonstration of it. God saves His people in a way that fully satisfies His holy justice.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Psalm 20 is a liturgical prayer for the king before he enters battle. In verse 6, the mood shifts from petition to absolute assurance: "Now I know that the LORD saves His anointed."

• The psalmist declares that God "will answer him from His holy heaven."

• This reference to "holy heaven" stands in stark contrast to the earthly battlefield. While earthly armies rely on visible, corruptible resources like "chariots and horses" (v. 7), the king's help comes from a transcendent, unassailable sanctuary.

• The holiness of heaven guarantees that God's deliverance is righteous, unalterable, and victorious. He does not answer from a place of human limitation, but from the throne of absolute purity and power.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Leviticus 10:10 - "...that you may distinguish between holy and unholy, and between unclean and clean;"

• Exodus 15:11 - "Who is like You, O LORD, among the gods? Who is like You, glorious in holiness, fearful in praises, doing wonders?"

• Psalm 2:6 - "Yet I have set My King On My holy hill of Zion."

• Psalm 29:2 - "Give unto the LORD the glory due to His name; Worship the LORD in the beauty of holiness."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament), qodesh is frequently translated by the Greek noun hagiasmos (holiness/sanctification) or the adjective hagios (holy/set apart). This direct link bridges the Old Testament concept of holiness with the New Testament understanding of believers as "saints" (hagioi). Through the sacrifice of Jesus Christ, believers are not merely declared righteous; they are set apart as God's holy temple, called to live lives that reflect the transcendent purity of the "holy heaven" from which their salvation comes.
