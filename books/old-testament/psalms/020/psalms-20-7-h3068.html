LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The covenant name of God, Yahweh, represents His eternal self-existence, unchangeable faithfulness, and intimate relationship with His people. In Psalm 20:7, "the name of the LORD" is set in stark contrast to the fleeting security of military might, reminding believers that ultimate victory and security lie not in human resources, but in the active, personal presence of the sovereign Covenant Keeper.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The name Yahweh is the deeply personal, relational name of Israel’s God. It is represented in Hebrew by the four consonants YHWH, known as the Tetragrammaton. Unlike generic terms for deity, this unique name was revealed to Moses at the burning bush in Exodus 3, directly connected to God’s historical intervention to deliver His people from Egypt. It is not an abstract concept of existence, but a dynamic promise of active, personal presence: "I will be with you." Historically, while pagan nations called upon regional deities using generic titles, Israel was privileged to call upon the specific, personal name of the self-existent Creator.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name is derived from the Hebrew verb root hayah (or the older form hawah), which means "to be," "to exist," or "to become." Lexically, it functions as a third-person singular causative or active verb form, signifying "He who is," "He who brings into being," or "He who is ever-present." It denotes absolute self-existence (aseity)—the reality that God depends on nothing else for His existence, while all creation depends entirely on Him. He is the eternal, unchanging One who exists past, present, and future.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Occurring over 6,800 times in the Old Testament, this is the most frequent designation for God. It is distinct from Elohim, which emphasizes God’s role as the transcendent Creator and Ruler of the universe. Yahweh is the name used in contexts of covenant-making, sacrifice, redemption, and personal relationship. The scriptures often link this name to vivid word pictures:

• A Strong Tower: Proving a place of safety and refuge to the righteous (Proverbs 18:10).

• A Shepherd: Portraying tender care, guidance, and daily provision (Psalm 23:1).

• A Warrior: Demonstrating sovereign power over the enemies of His people (Exodus 15:3).

### 4. Theological Implications

• Immutability: Because God is Yahweh, He does not change. His character, purposes, and promises remain completely constant across generations.

• Covenant Faithfulness: The name is the absolute guarantee of God’s promises. He binds Himself to His people; even when they are unfaithful, He remains faithful because He cannot deny His name.

• Self-Sufficiency and Sovereignty: He does not need human help, sacrifices, or resources. He is complete in Himself, yet He graciously chooses to relate to, love, and deliver His people.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 20:7, David contrasts two competing objects of human trust: "Some trust in chariots, and some in horses; but we will remember the name of the LORD our God." In the ancient Near East, Egypt and other empires relied heavily on advanced military hardware—chariots and horses—as the ultimate measure of national security. For Israel, relying on such physical resources was a form of idolatry that displaced God.

The nuance of "the name of the LORD" here refers to the active presence, character, and saving power of Yahweh. To "remember" (Hebrew zakar) His name means more than cognitive recall; it means to call upon, boast in, and rely on His proven character. By invoking Yahweh, David declares that Israel's true defense is not found in military assets, but in the personal, covenant-keeping God who has pledged to defend His people.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 3:15: "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

• Psalm 23:1: "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

• Proverbs 18:10: "The name of the LORD is a strong tower; The righteous run to it and are safe."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

Out of deep reverence for the sacred name, ancient Jewish scribes and readers avoided pronouncing the Tetragrammaton aloud, substituting it with the Hebrew word Adonai ("my Lord") or HaShem ("the Name"). This practice is reflected in modern English translations, including the NKJV, which render YHWH in capital letters as "LORD."

Furthermore, this covenant identity finds its ultimate fulfillment in the New Testament person of Jesus Christ. When Jesus declares "I AM" (ego eimi) in passages like John 8:58, He directly claims the covenant identity of Yahweh, indicating that the personal, self-existent God of the Old Testament is now present in the flesh to redeem His people.
