UPRIGHT - ( 'odad , Strong's H5749 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'odad in the reflexive Hitpalel stem means to stand upright, to be restored, or to be established. In Psalm 20:8, it describes the resilient posture of God's people who, unlike their enemies who collapse under trials, are lifted up, sustained, and kept standing by the power of Yahweh. It conveys more than just physical posture; it reflects a spiritual reality of divine restoration, encouragement, and enduring strength that comes from trusting in the name of the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root behind 'odad has the primary sense of surrounding, restoring, or holding up. It paints a vivid picture of someone who has been weakened, bent over, or threatened with collapse, but who is surrounded with support and lifted back to a standing position. In ancient warfare, physical posture reflected victory or defeat. To "bow down and fall" represents utter defeat, vulnerability, and collapse, while to "rise and stand upright" is the posture of the victorious survivor who has been sustained by a power greater than their own.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb 'odad (occurring primarily in the Pilel and Hitpalel stems) means to restore, relieve, support, or hold up. In the Hitpalel stem used in Psalm 20:8 (nit'odad), the word carries a reflexive and intensive meaning: "we stood upright," "we restored ourselves," or "we were gathered together to stand firm." It denotes a state of being firmly established, revitalized, and kept upright. The core meaning is not merely ethical uprightness (which is often expressed by other Hebrew roots like yashar), but physical and spiritual stability, resilience, and restoration from a state of vulnerability.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The root is used to describe God’s active intervention on behalf of the vulnerable. When used of the oppressed, it means to relieve or sustain them in their distress.

• The word picture is that of a collapsing structure or a falling person being braced, propped up, and restored to its proper, upright alignment.

• In a military or covenantal context, it describes the rallying of troops who, though shaken by the initial onslaught of the enemy, find their footing, stand their ground, and are restored to a position of strength.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of 'odad rests on the source of the believer’s stability. The contrast in Psalm 20 is between those who trust in earthly military power ("chariots" and "horses") and those who trust in "the name of the LORD our God." True stability and the ability to stand upright in times of crisis are not self-generated. They are the direct result of divine preservation. God is the ultimate source of restoration; He lifts up the humble and preserves the vulnerable, ensuring that while the wicked collapse under the weight of their own self-reliance, the righteous are sustained and established.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 20:8, 'odad is contrasted sharply with "bowed down" (kara') and "fallen" (naphal). The enemies of God's king trust in visible, physical strength (chariots and horses), yet they collapse and fall. Conversely, God's people, who have no such physical advantages, "rise" (qum) and "stand upright" ('odad). The tense of the verb indicates a completed, enduring state: because of their trust in Yahweh, they have been raised up and now stand firm, unshaken by the battle. The nuance here is one of triumphant resilience and divine vindication.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 146:9: "The LORD watches over the strangers; He relieves the fatherless and widow; But the way of the wicked He turns upside down."

• Psalm 147:6: "The LORD lifts up the humble; He casts the wicked down to the ground."

7. Other Relevant Insights

It is vital for the expositor to distinguish 'odad from yashar (the standard Hebrew word for moral uprightness or straightness). While yashar deals with character and integrity, 'odad deals with support, recovery, and standing firm against collapse. The Hitpalel form in Psalm 20:8 also reflects a communal aspect. Together, under the banner of Yahweh's name, the community of faith encourages one another and stands resiliently upright, secure in the victory God has provided.
