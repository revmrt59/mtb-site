ANSWER - ( anah , Strong's H6030 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word anah primarily means to respond, answer, or testify. In the context of prayer, it refers to God's active, relational response to the cries of His people. Far from a mere verbal reply, God's "answering" denotes His powerful intervention, deliverance, and faithful presence in times of trouble, demonstrating His covenant commitment to those who call upon Him.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root anah carries the foundational concept of responding to a stimulus or initiating a counter-action. It is a deeply relational word, implying a connection between the one who speaks or cries out and the one who listens and responds. In the ancient Near Eastern context, a king "answering" his subjects was an act of royal favor. It indicated that the sovereign had heard their petition and was now exercising his power to grant justice, protection, or deliverance.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb anah occurs over 300 times in the Hebrew Bible. Its primary lexical definitions include:

• To answer, respond, or reply to a question or statement.

• To testify or bear witness in a legal assembly or court.

• To respond to a cry for help, particularly of God responding to human prayer.

• To sing responsively or shout in response.

In the context of Psalm 20:9, the word denotes a favorable, saving response from a sovereign ruler to his dependent people.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Active Listener: anah is frequently paired with verbs of crying out, calling, or praying (such as qara). The word picture is not of a distant deity passively receiving information, but of an attentive, active Sovereign who hears and immediately moves into action.

• The Legal Witness: In judicial settings, it refers to giving testimony (testifying), where the witness responds to the truth of a situation.

• Responsive Worship: In liturgical settings, it describes responsive singing, where one group sings and another answers, creating a harmonious dialogue.

4. Theological Implications

• A Covenantal God: God's willingness to anah (answer) is a cornerstone of covenant theology. It proves that Yahweh is not like the silent, unresponsive idols of the nations.

• Relational Accessibility: An "answering" God is a personal, relational God. It reveals His accessibility and His willingness to bind Himself to the needs of His covenant people.

• Answering as Deliverance: In the Psalms, God's answer is often synonymous with salvation. When God answers, the circumstances of the sufferer change; the answer is realized through divine intervention.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Psalm 20 is a royal psalm, a prayer for the king prior to battle. In Psalm 20:9, there is a deliberate parallel and potential wordplay. The congregation cries out, "Save, LORD! May the King answer us when we call."

• The "King" here ultimately refers to Yahweh Himself, the true King of Israel, though it also reflects on the earthly Davidic king who represents God's rule.

• The plea for the King to "answer" (anah) is a cry for divine intervention in battle. It is a petition for the ultimate Sovereign to mobilize His heavenly resources on behalf of His people when they call out in the day of trouble. This matches the opening petition of the Psalm: "May the LORD answer you in the day of trouble" (Psalm 20:1).

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 20:1 - "May the LORD answer you in the day of trouble; May the name of the God of Jacob defend you;"

• Psalm 22:21 - "Save Me from the lion’s mouth And from the horns of the wild oxen! You have answered Me."

• Psalm 91:15 - "He shall call upon Me, and I will answer him; I will be with him in trouble; I will deliver him and honor him."

• Isaiah 65:24 - "It shall come to pass That before they call, I will answer; And while they are still speaking, I will hear."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between Yahweh who answers and false gods who cannot answer is a major theme in Israel's historical and prophetic literature. On Mount Carmel, the prophets of Baal called on his name, "but there was no voice; no one answered" (1 Kings 18:26). Yahweh, however, answered by fire, proving His active existence and covenant love. In the New Testament, this concept of God hearing and answering finds its fulfillment in Jesus Christ, who assures believers that the Father hears them always.
