LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh is the personal, covenantal, and self-revealed name of the God of Israel. It denotes His eternal self-existence, absolute independence, and immutable faithfulness to His promises. In Psalm 20:9, this sacred name is invoked as a desperate, yet highly confident, cry for deliverance, positioning the sovereign Creator as the ultimate Defender of His covenant people.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The name Yahweh, often referred to as the Tetragrammaton (the four-letter name YHWH), is the most sacred name of God in the Old Testament. Historically, the Jewish people held this name in such high esteem and holy dread that they avoided pronouncing it aloud to prevent taking it in vain, substituting the word Adonai (Lord) during public reading. The word is deeply intertwined with God's self-revelation to Moses at the burning bush in Exodus 3:14, where He declared, "I AM WHO I AM." Structurally, it is derived from the Hebrew verb of being, hayah. It paints a picture of a God who is not a passive force, but an actively present, dynamically involved Person who exists eternally in and of Himself. He is the Source of all being, beholden to none, and always present with His people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Yahweh means "the Self-Existent One" or "He who is." It is the active, future-present form of the verb "to be." It signifies:

• Absolute self-existence: He has no beginning, no end, and depends on nothing outside of Himself for His existence.

• Unchanging faithfulness: He is the same yesterday, today, and forever, making His covenant promises utterly reliable.

• Covenant presence: He is the God who is intimately near, actively present to save, redeem, and sustain His people.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

In English translations of the Old Testament, Yahweh is historically represented by the word "LORD" in all capital letters to distinguish it from Adonai (translated as "Lord" in standard title case) and Elohim (translated as "God").

• Elohim speaks of God's power, majesty, and role as Creator of the universe.

• Yahweh speaks of His personal relationship, intimacy, and covenant bond with humanity.

While Elohim created the heavens and the earth, it was Yahweh Elohim (the LORD God) who breathed life into Adam, planted the garden, and walked with humanity in the cool of the day. The word functions as a relational anchor; whenever Israel is in distress, they call upon Yahweh, the God who binds Himself to His people by a solemn oath.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, Yahweh is the name of grace and redemption. It teaches the absolute immutability of God. Because He is the self-existent One, His character, purposes, and love do not fluctuate. He does not grow weary, nor does He adapt His standards to changing cultural tides. Furthermore, Yahweh is the God of hesed (steadfast love and covenant loyalty). He is the One who initiated the covenant with Abraham, rescued Israel from Egypt, and promised an everlasting kingdom to David. The name guarantees that God will fulfill every word He has spoken, as His very identity is staked on His faithfulness.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Psalm 20 is a liturgical prayer offered by the congregation of Israel on behalf of the king as he prepares to go out to battle. The people acknowledge that victory does not depend on military strength, declaring in verse 7, "Some trust in chariots, and some in horses; but we will remember the name of the LORD our God."

This build-up culminates in the climactic petition of verse 9: "Save, LORD! May the King answer us when we call."

By invoking Yahweh here, the psalmist contrasts earthly rulers and resources with the ultimate Sovereign. The Hebrew word order links the cry for salvation (hoshiah) directly with the name Yahweh. It is a recognition that the earthly king's victory is entirely dependent on the heavenly King's favor. The nuance is one of absolute surrender and total trust; Israel enters the battlefield resting not on their strategic advantages, but on the character, power, and historical faithfulness of their covenant-keeping God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:4: "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens,"

• Exodus 3:15: "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you." This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations.'"

• Psalm 23:1: "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

• Proverbs 18:10: "The name of the LORD is a strong tower; The righteous run to it and are safe."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The redemptive character of Yahweh is beautifully expanded in the Old Testament through the compound names of God. When His people faced specific crises, He revealed Himself as:

• Yahweh Yireh: The LORD Will Provide (Genesis 22:14)

• Yahweh Nissi: The LORD Is My Banner (Exodus 17:15)

• Yahweh Shalom: The LORD Is Peace (Judges 6:24)

• Yahweh Raah: The LORD Is My Shepherd (Psalm 23:1)

• Yahweh Tsidkenu: The LORD Our Righteousness (Jeremiah 23:6)

In the New Testament, Jesus claims this divine identity for Himself. When He declares, "Before Abraham was, I AM" (John 8:58), He uses the Greek equivalent of Yahweh's self-revelation. Thus, the Jesus who saves His people from their sins is the very same Yahweh who was invoked to save the king in Psalm 20:9.
