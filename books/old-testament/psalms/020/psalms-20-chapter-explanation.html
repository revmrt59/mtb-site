Study Psalms 19

Verse-by-Verse
Psalm 20:1

NKJV

NKJV

Psalms 20:1 To the Chief Musician. A Psalm of David. May the LORD answer you in the day of trouble; May the name of the God of Jacob defend you;(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Psalm 20:1 In times of trouble, may the LORD answer your cry.May the name of the God of Jacob keep you safe from all harm. (NLT)

Main Idea

David records the people's prayer that God would hear, protect, and deliver His anointed king whenever he faces trouble.

Verse Explanation

"May the LORD answer you" - The psalm begins as a prayer. The people are asking the LORD to hear and respond to the king's cry. In the Bible, for God to "answer" means more than simply hearing words-it means responding with His presence, help, and power.

"in the day of trouble" - This refers to a time of distress, danger, or crisis. In the immediate context, the king is preparing for battle, but the phrase applies to any circumstance where God's people face needs beyond their own strength. Trouble is assumed to be part of life; the focus is not on avoiding it but on where we turn when it comes.

"May the name of the God of Jacob" - When the Bible speaks of God's 'name,' it means far more than the word people call Him. It refers to who He is-His character, His power, His authority, and His proven faithfulness to do what He has promised . - Calling Him 'the God of Jacob' reminds the people how God cared for Jacob throughout his life. Though Jacob often failed and struggled, God remained faithful to His promises,. He protected him, guided him, and kept every promise He had made. The people are asking that the same faithful God, who has proven Himself faithful throughout Israel's history, would now help their king

"defend you" - The Hebrew word carries the idea of setting someone in a high, secure place beyond the reach of danger. The prayer is not that the king would never face battle, but that God Himself would be his protector and preserve him from defeat according to His will.

Remark(s)

Before Israel's king went into battle, the people prayed for him. They understood that victory would not ultimately depend on strategy, soldiers, or weapons, but on the Lord's help. Every significant work of God should likewise begin with prayer, acknowledging our dependence on Him before we take our first step.

Psalm 20:2

NKJV

NKJV

Psalms 20:2 May He send you help from the sanctuary, And strengthen you out of Zion;(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Psalm 20:2 May he send you help from his sanctuaryand strengthen you from Jerusalem. (NLT)

Main Idea

The people pray that God will strengthen and sustain the king from His dwelling place as he prepares for battle.

Verse Explanation

"May He send you help from the sanctuary" refers to God's help coming from His holy dwelling place. In David's day, the sanctuary was where God's presence was uniquely manifested among His people. The prayer recognizes that the king's greatest source of help is not his army or his military skill, but the Lord Himself.

"And strengthen you out of Zion" continues the same thought. Zion was the hill in Jerusalem where the tabernacle had been brought and where God's presence was associated with His people. To be strengthened from Zion means to receive God's power, support, and encouragement. The people are asking that the God who dwells among them would personally sustain their king for the battle ahead. - Together, these two phrases emphasize that true strength comes from God's presence. Before a single sword is drawn, the people acknowledge that victory depends upon the Lord acting on behalf of His anointed king.

Remark(s)

The people do not merely pray that the king would be courageous; they pray that God Himself would be his source of help and strength. Throughout Scripture, God's people are strongest when they recognize that their sufficiency comes from Him rather than from themselves.

Psalm 20:3

NKJV

NKJV

Psalms 20:3 May He remember all your offerings, And accept your burnt sacrifice. Selah(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Psalm 20:3 May he remember all your giftsand look favorably on your burnt offerings. Interlude (NLT)

Main Idea

The people pray that God will receive the king's worship and look with favor on the sacrifices he has offered before going into battle.

Verse Explanation

Before asking God for victory, the king first sought God in worship. The people pray, "May He remember all your offerings," asking God to look with favor on every sacrifice the king had presented to Him. In the Old Testament, sacrifices expressed worship, thanksgiving, repentance, and dependence upon God. They were an outward expression of a heart seeking the Lord.

The prayer continues, "And accept your burnt sacrifice." The burnt offering was completely consumed on the altar, symbolizing wholehearted devotion and surrender to God. By asking God to accept the sacrifice, the people are praying that He would be pleased with the king's worship and respond graciously to his prayers.

The word "Selah" invites the reader to pause and reflect. Before moving on to pray for victory, the psalm lingers on an important truth: the king's relationship with God comes before the battle. His greatest preparation is not military strategy but approaching the Lord with a heart of worship and dependence.

Remark(s)

The order of the psalm is significant. Worship comes before warfare. Before asking God to bless the work of his hands, the king first offers himself to God in worship and dependence.

Psalm 20:4

NKJV

NKJV

Psalms 20:4 May He grant you according to your heart's desire, And fulfill all your purpose.(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Psalm 20:4 May he grant your heart’s desiresand make all your plans succeed. (NLT)

Main Idea

The people pray that God will accomplish the righteous desires and purposes He has placed in the king's heart.

Verse Explanation

"May He grant you according to your heart's desire" is not a promise that God fulfills every personal wish. In the context of this psalm, the king has come before God in worship and is preparing to lead God's people into battle. His desire is to carry out the responsibility God has given him. The prayer is that God would bring those godly desires to completion.

"And fulfill all your purpose" refers to the king's plans and responsibilities as God's anointed ruler. The people are asking that everything God has called him to accomplish would succeed according to His will. Their confidence is not in the king's ability but in God's power to establish the work He has entrusted to him. - Together, these requests recognize that success comes when our desires and plans are aligned with God's purposes. The people are asking God to prosper the mission He has given His king.

Remark(s)

This verse is often quoted as though it promises God will grant whatever we want. In its context, however, it is a prayer that God would accomplish His purposes through a leader who has first sought Him in worship and dependence. The emphasis is not on personal ambition but on fulfilling God's calling.

Psalm 20:5

NKJV

NKJV

Psalms 20:5 We will rejoice in your salvation, And in the name of our God we will set up our banners! May the LORD fulfill all your petitions.(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Psalm 20:5 May we shout for joy when we hear of your victoryand raise a victory banner in the name of our God.May the LORD answer all your prayers. (NLT)

Main Idea

The people anticipate God's victory with confidence and look forward to celebrating what He will accomplish for their king.

Verse Explanation

"We will rejoice in your salvation" expresses confident faith that God will answer their prayers. Although the battle has not yet taken place, the people already look ahead to celebrating God's deliverance. Here, "salvation" refers to God's rescue and victory over the enemy rather than eternal salvation.

"And in the name of our God we will set up our banners!" Banners were military standards carried by armies and raised after a victory. By saying they will raise their banners "in the name of our God," the people declare that any victory belongs to the Lord. Their celebration will honor Him rather than human strength or military skill.

"May the LORD fulfill all your petitions." The verse concludes by returning to prayer. After expressing confidence in God's coming victory, the people ask Him once more to answer every request the king has brought before Him. Their faith does not replace prayer-it strengthens it.

Remark(s)

Faith allows the people to rejoice before the battle is won because their confidence rests in God rather than in the outcome they can see. Their expectation of victory grows out of prayer, worship, and trust in the Lord, not self-confidence.

Psalm 20:6

NKJV

NKJV

Psalms 20:6 Now I know that the LORD saves His anointed; He will answer him from His holy heaven With the saving strength of His right hand.(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Psalm 20:6 Now I know that the LORD rescues his anointed king.He will answer him from his holy heavenand rescue him by his great power. (NLT)

Main Idea

Confidence replaces prayer as the people declare that the Lord will save and give victory to His anointed king.

Verse Explanation

The psalm now shifts from asking God to act to expressing confidence that He will. "Now I know" marks a turning point. The prayers of verses 1-5 become assurance that God has heard and will answer.

"The LORD saves His anointed" refers first to Israel's king, whom God had chosen and anointed to lead His people. The people are confident that because God appointed the king for this task, He will also help and preserve him as he carries out that responsibility.

"He will answer him from His holy heaven" expands the thought from God's earthly dwelling place in Zion (verse 2) to His heavenly throne. God is not limited to a place on earth; He rules from heaven and hears the prayers of His people wherever they are.

"With the saving strength of His right hand" speaks of God's mighty power. Throughout the Old Testament, God's "right hand" is a picture of His strength, authority, and ability to accomplish what no one else can. The people are confident that God Himself will act powerfully to bring deliverance and victory.

Remark(s)

Faith often begins with prayer but does not remain uncertain. After seeking the Lord, the people move from asking, "May God help," to declaring, "Now I know the Lord will save." Biblical faith rests not in wishful thinking but in confidence that God is able to accomplish His purposes.

Psalm 20:7

NKJV

NKJV

Psalms 20:7 Some trust in chariots, and some in horses; But we will remember the name of the LORD our God.(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Psalm 20:7 Some nations boast of their chariots and horses,but we boast in the name of the LORD our God. (NLT)

Main Idea

God's people place their confidence in the Lord rather than in human strength or military power.

Verse Explanation

"Some trust in chariots, and some in horses" describes the confidence of the surrounding nations. In the ancient world, chariots and horses represented the most powerful military technology available. A nation with many horses and chariots appeared strong, secure, and difficult to defeat. Israel's enemies measured victory by the size and strength of their armies.

"But we will remember the name of the LORD our God." In contrast, God's people place their confidence in the Lord. To "remember" His name means more than simply thinking about God. It means calling to mind who He has revealed Himself to be-His power, His wisdom, His faithfulness, and the many times He has delivered His people in the past. Their confidence rests in God Himself rather than in what they can see or possess. - The verse does not suggest that preparation or resources are unimportant. Rather, it teaches that they must never become the source of our confidence. Human strength has limits, but the Lord never does.

Remark(s)

Every generation is tempted to trust in its own "chariots and horses." Today they may look like money, education, influence, technology, experience, or personal ability. These things have value, but they can never replace wholehearted trust in the Lord, who alone gives lasting victory.

Psalm 20:8

NKJV

NKJV

Psalms 20:8 They have bowed down and fallen; But we have risen and stand upright.(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Psalm 20:8 Those nations will fall down and collapse,but we will rise up and stand firm. (NLT)

Main Idea

Those who trust in human strength ultimately fall, but those who trust in the Lord are upheld and remain secure.

Verse Explanation

"They have bowed down and fallen" describes the outcome of those who placed their confidence in chariots and horses. What once appeared strong and unstoppable has collapsed. The verse highlights the temporary nature of human strength when it is relied upon apart from God.

"But we have risen and stand upright." In contrast, God's people remain standing because their confidence is in the Lord. To "stand upright" speaks of stability, endurance, and victory after the conflict. While others are brought low, those who trust God are upheld by His strength. - This verse completes the contrast begun in verse 7. The difference is not that one group had better resources, but that they placed their confidence in different places. One trusted human power that eventually failed; the other trusted the Lord, who proved faithful.

Remark(s)

The true test of where we place our confidence is often seen only after the battle is over. Human strength may appear impressive for a time, but only those whom God upholds remain standing in the end.

Psalm 20:9

NKJV

NKJV

Psalms 20:9 Save, LORD! May the King answer us when we call.(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Psalm 20:9 Give victory to our king, O LORD!Answer our cry for help. (NLT)

Main Idea

The psalm closes with a final prayer asking the Lord to save the king and answer His people whenever they call upon Him.

Verse Explanation

"Save, LORD!" is the concluding cry of the congregation. Although the people have expressed confidence that God will give victory, they end where they began-with prayer. Their confidence in God does not make prayer unnecessary; it gives them even greater reason to call upon Him.

"May the King answer us when we call." Most translations understand "the King" to refer to the LORD Himself, the true King over Israel. While David is Israel's earthly king, the people recognize that the ultimate authority and source of deliverance is God. They ask that whenever they call upon Him, He would hear and answer their prayers. - The psalm therefore begins and ends with dependence on the Lord. Every request for protection, strength, and victory is directed to the One who reigns over all kings and nations.

Remark(s)

Psalm 20 opens with God's people praying before the battle and closes with them still depending on Him. Confidence in God's promises never replaces prayer-it encourages us to keep calling upon the One who alone can save.
