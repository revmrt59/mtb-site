Morning Chapter Explanation

Psalms Chapter 20

Chapter 20

Author

David. The superscription identifies this as "A Psalm of David." As Israel's anointed king, David often wrote from personal experience, but many of his psalms were also intended for corporate worship.

Occasion

While the exact historical event is unknown, Psalm 20 appears to have been written before the king went into battle. The people gather to pray for God's protection, guidance, and victory over His anointed ruler. It naturally pairs with Psalm 21, which is a thanksgiving after God grants the victory. Together they form a "before and after" set of royal psalms.

Kind of Psalm

Royal Psalm • Prayer for Victory • Psalm of Trust

  • Royal Psalm – Focuses on God's anointed king and his role in leading God's people.

  • Prayer for Victory – A corporate prayer asking God to defend, strengthen, and grant success before battle.

  • Psalm of Trust – Moves from petition (aspecific earnest request to God) to confident assurance that God hears and saves those who trust Him.

A Psalm of Trust is essentially a Psalm of Faith, though "Psalm of Trust" is the standard term used by biblical scholars. There is a subtle distinction. Faith is the broader biblical concept of believing God and relying on Him. Trust is faith expressed in a specific situation, especially during danger, uncertainty, or need.

Central Message

Psalm 20 is a prayer for God's help, protection, and victory over His anointed king as he prepares for battle. The psalm teaches that true confidence does not rest in military strength or human resources but in the Lord, who hears, saves, and gives victory to those who trust Him.

What This Chapter Is About

Psalm 20 is a congregational prayer offered before a battle. The people ask God to hear the king's prayers, defend him, accept his worship, strengthen him for the coming conflict, and fulfill His purposes through him. As the psalm progresses, confidence replaces petition because the people are persuaded that God will answer. It concludes with a final plea for God to save the king and answer His people whenever they call. The movement is from prayer to confidence to assurance.

The Heart of the Chapter

Psalm 20 shows that the decisive issue in any battle is not the strength we can see, but the God in whom we trust. Before the king enters the battlefield, God's people gather in prayer, looking to the LORD rather than human strength and declaring that their confidence rests in His name.

Chapter Sections

Psalm 20:1–5 — A Prayer for God's Help and Blessing

The people ask God to hear the king in the day of trouble, protect him, strengthen him from Zion, accept his sacrifices, fulfill his plans, and grant victory so they may rejoice together.

Psalm 20:6–8 — Confidence That God Gives Victory

Faith rises as the people declare that the Lord saves His anointed. While nations trust in military power, God's people trust in His name and know He alone gives lasting victory.

Psalm 20:9 — A Final Cry for God's Salvation

The psalm ends with one final prayer asking the Lord to save the king and answer His people whenever they call upon Him.

Why This Chapter Matters

Psalm 20 teaches one of the Bible's greatest lessons about faith: where we place our confidence determines how we face life's battles. Every generation is tempted to rely on human strength, wealth, influence, ability, or careful planning. God reminds His people that lasting victory comes from Him alone. The psalm also shows the importance of praying for those whom God has placed in leadership. Under the New Covenant, believers recognize Jesus Christ as God's ultimate Anointed King, whose victory secures our salvation and whose reign gives us confidence in every spiritual battle.

Key Themes to Watch For

  • Prayer before action rather than independence.

  • God's protection in times of trouble.

  • The importance of praying for godly leaders.

  • Confidence in God's faithfulness.

  • Trust in God's name rather than human strength.

  • Victory that comes from the Lord.

  • Joyful worship after answered prayer.

Key Words & Concepts

  • The Name of the LORD — God's revealed character, power, authority, and faithful presence with His people.

  • His Anointed — Originally Israel's king, ultimately pointing to Jesus Christ, God's perfect Anointed King (Messiah).

  • Zion — The place of God's dwelling among His people and the source of His help and blessing.

  • Chariots and Horses — Symbols of military strength, human power, and earthly security.

  • Remember the Name of the LORD — To place complete confidence in God's character, promises, and power rather than human resources.

  • Save — God's deliverance, protection, and victory over every enemy.
