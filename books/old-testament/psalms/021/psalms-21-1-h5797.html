STRENGTH - ( oz , Strong's H5797 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word oz fundamentally denotes strength, might, power, or a place of safety and refuge. In Psalm 21:1, it describes the divine power and might of God in which the king rejoices and finds his security. Rather than relying on human military might, the believer's ultimate strength is found exclusively in the person and provision of Yahweh, leading to both triumph and profound trust.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root idea of oz relates to being strong, bold, or secure, often functioning metaphorically as a stronghold or a fortress.

- In the ancient Near Eastern context, a king's strength was typically measured by his chariots, horses, and armies; however, the Psalm radically redirects this dependence toward the invisible, unfailing power of God.

- It pictures a physical or spiritual bulwark—a raised, inaccessible place where a person is lifted above the reach of enemies.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Oz refers to physical power, human strength, or God’s supreme might.

- It also carries the secondary nuance of a refuge, sanctuary, or strong tower that provides protection.

- Lexically, it is frequently paired with concepts of glory, majesty, and salvation, bridging the gap between raw power and saving grace.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- Used broadly in the Old Testament to describe both human vigor and divine omnipotence.

- Often employed in poetic literature (such as the Psalms) as an attribute of God that He graciously shares or exercises on behalf of His people.

- Functions as an exclamation of praise when believers ascribe their victories not to themselves, but to the robust power of the Almighty.

4. Theological Implications

- The strength belonging to God is limitless, eternal, and entirely dependable, contrasting sharply with the frailty of human flesh.

- Salvation and divine strength are intimately linked; God’s power is actively deployed to rescue and sustain those who trust in Him.

- True spiritual leadership recognizes that earthly authority is entirely derivative, dependent upon the daily sustenance of divine power.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 21:1 ("The king shall have joy in Your strength, O Lord..."), oz refers specifically to Yahweh's dynamic, victorious power manifested on behalf of the anointed king.

- The joy of the king is not derived from his own conquests or political prowess, but directly from the divine enablement and triumph supplied by God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Exodus 15:2: "The Lord is my strength and song, and He has become my salvation..."

- Psalm 29:11: "The Lord will give strength to His people; the Lord will bless His people with peace."

- Psalm 46:1: "God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble."

- Isaiah 49:5: "...and My God shall be my strength."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The Septuagint (LXX) frequently translates oz using words related to power (dynamis) or strength (ischys), which the New Testament later applies to the supernatural power of the Holy Spirit at work in believers.

- Recognizing God as our oz transforms anxiety into praise, shifting the focus from our perceived weaknesses to His inexhaustible resources.
