OFFSPRING - ( periy , Strong's H6529 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word periy fundamentally means fruit, produce, or offspring, serving as a powerful biological and agricultural metaphor that connects human progeny directly to the harvest of the earth. In Psalm 21:10, it describes the physical descendants of the wicked who oppose God's anointed king, portraying them as doomed fruit that will be swept away from the earth. The term bridges physical lineage with moral consequence, demonstrating that a person's legacy is intimately tied to their standing before God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root imagery of periy is botanical, drawing from the natural cycle of trees and plants yielding fruit.

- In ancient agrarian Israel, fruit was the visible, tangible evidence of a plant's life, health, and value.

- Applying this agricultural term to human descendants portrays children not merely as individuals, but as the "produce" or "harvest" generated by a family line.

- Just as corrupted trees produce bad fruit that is discarded, unrighteous generations bear the natural consequence of their ancestors' hostility toward God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Primary definitions include fruit, produce, harvest, and reward.

- Extended definitions include offspring, descendants, posterity, and progeny.

- It denotes that which is born of man, beast, or tree as a natural result or yield of life.

- The word emphasizes continuation, legacy, and the harvest of one's actions and lineage.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- Used frequently of the literal fruit of the ground (Genesis 4:3) and the trees of the garden (Genesis 3:2).

- Frequently applied to the womb, denoting children as the "fruit of the womb" (Psalm 127:3, Deuteronomy 28:4), highlighting that children are a gift cultivated and given by God.

- Applied metaphorically to the moral consequences of human behavior, where people "eat the fruit of their doings" (Isaiah 3:10, Proverbs 1:31).

- In martial or judicial contexts, as in Psalm 21:10, it represents the complete extirpation of a hostile legacy, removing both the root (the wicked individual) and the branch (the offspring).

4. Theological Implications

- Generational solidarity: Scripture often views families and households as interconnected units under divine blessing or judgment.

- Sovereignty over life and legacy: God holds the power to establish a righteous legacy or to cut off the memory and offspring of the wicked.

- The contrast between eternal and temporary lines: While the wicked seed is swept away, the righteous inherit enduring generational promises through God's steadfast love.

- Anticipation of ultimate justice: The total removal of the offspring of God's enemies points forward to the final purification of the kingdom when all opposition to the Messiah is eradicated.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- Psalm 21 is a royal psalm of thanksgiving and triumph, celebrating God's strength given to the Davidic king.

- Verses 8-12 turn sharply toward the enemies of the king, outlining the certain destruction of those who plot evil against God's anointed.

- In verse 10, the psalmist declares: "Their offspring You shall destroy from the earth, and their descendants from among the sons of men."

- Here, periy emphasizes the total eradication of the enemy's future; their lineage will yield no more fruit, securing the permanent victory and safety of God's king.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Psalm 127:3 - "Behold, children are a heritage from the Lord, the fruit of the womb is a reward."

- Isaiah 3:10 - "Say to the righteous, that it shall be well with them, for they shall eat the fruit of their doings."

- Micah 6:7 - "Will the Lord be pleased with thousands of rams, ten thousand rivers of oil? Shall I give my firstborn for my transgression, the fruit of my body for the sin of my soul?"

- Genesis 3:3 - "But of the fruit of the tree which is in the midst of the garden, God has said, 'You shall not eat it, nor shall you touch it, lest you die.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The LXX typically translates periy in this family of meanings with karpos (fruit), maintaining the agricultural metaphor across testaments.

- The imagery of cutting off offspring underscores the ancient dread of having one's name and lineage blotted out from the land of the living.

- In prophetic literature, the restoration of God's people is frequently described in terms of flourishing periy (fruit), pointing to spiritual revival and abundant blessing (Isaiah 37:31, Ezekiel 36:30).
