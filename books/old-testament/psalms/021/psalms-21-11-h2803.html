DEVISED - ( chashab , Strong's H2803 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb chashab carries the primary meaning of thinking, planning, calculating, or inventing. In the context of Psalm 21:11, it denotes the deliberate, highly calculated plotting of evil by the enemies of the king (and ultimately, of God). It implies a cognitive process where one mentally weaves together a malicious scheme, much like a weaver designs a complex pattern. Though these enemies meticulously engineer and fabricate their rebellion, the psalmist emphasizes their ultimate powerlessness; their calculated plots are entirely futile against God's sovereign protection of His anointed.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete, physical root of chashab is closely tied to the craft of weaving or accounting. In the ancient world, a choseb was a highly skilled weaver or artisan who carefully designed intricate patterns, calculating how each thread would interlace with the others to form a cohesive fabric. This imagery was carried over into the mental realm: to "devise" is to weave together thoughts, ideas, and plans. When used negatively, as in Psalm 21:11, it presents a vivid word picture of the wicked acting as malicious craftsmen, painstakingly weaving a net of conspiracy or fabricating a complex trap to ensnare their target.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb chashab occurs over 120 times in the Old Testament and possesses a rich semantic range:

• To calculate or reckon: Determining numerical values, financial accounts, or years (e.g., calculating the price of a sale).

• To value or esteem: Regarding or holding someone in a specific status (e.g., being "esteemed" as a friend or an enemy).

• To plan or invent: Creating artistic designs or fabricating military engines.

• To plot or scheme: Intentionally formulating a course of action with malicious intent.

In the Qal stem, when paired with an object of harm (such as "evil" or "a plot"), the word means to forge, concoct, or deliberately design a harmful strategy.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The dual nature of chashab reveals the capacity of the human mind. On one hand, the word is used to describe the beautiful, divinely inspired craftwork of the tabernacle, where skilled artisans "devised" artistic designs in gold, silver, and blue thread (Exodus 35:32, 35). On the other hand, it describes the dark, calculated industry of human sin.

• Artistic Weaving: The intentional interlocking of threads to create a beautiful tapestry.

• Mental Plotting: The interlocking of lies, timing, and resources to execute a wicked plan.

• Divine Accounting: The merciful act of God "reckoning" or "imputing" righteousness to a believer, rather than counting their sins against them (Genesis 15:6).

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, chashab highlights the intentionality of human sin. Sin is not merely an accidental slip or a passive yielding to temptation; it is often "devised"—rationally calculated, structured, and premeditated in the quiet chambers of the heart.

Furthermore, the word highlights the contrast between human schemes and divine sovereignty. Humans are free to devise plans, but God determines the outcome. The plans of the wicked are subject to the veto power of the Almighty. While Joseph's brothers "meant" (chashab) evil against him, God "meant" (chashab) it for good (Genesis 50:20). Ultimately, God's sovereign plan overlays and redirects the malicious designs of humanity to accomplish His own glory.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Psalm 21 is a royal psalm celebrating the victory, strength, and salvation that Yahweh grants to His anointed king. In verse 11, the psalmist exposes the internal motivation of the enemy: "For they intended evil against You; they devised (chashab) a plot."

The context emphasizes the sheer futility of their mental energy. The enemies spent time, intellect, and resourcefulness to "weave" a perfect strategy to overthrow the king (and by extension, Yahweh Himself). However, the verse ends with a sharp, ironic twist: "which they are not able to perform." The "woven" plot is easily shredded by God. The nuance here is pastoral and encouraging: no matter how sophisticated, well-funded, or intellectually formidable the schemes of the enemy appear, they are utterly impotent when positioned against the sovereign decree of God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 15:6 – "And he believed in the LORD, and He accounted it to him for righteousness."

• Genesis 50:20 – "But as for you, you meant evil against me; but God meant it for good, to bring it to pass as it is this day, to save many people alive."

• Psalm 35:20 – "For they do not speak peace, But they devise deceitful matters Against those who are quiet in the land."

• Micah 2:1 – "Woe to those who devise iniquity, And work out evil on their beds! At morning light they practice it, Because it is in the power of their hand."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the New Testament, the Greek equivalent of chashab is logizomai, which means to reckon, calculate, or impute. This Greek term is famously used by Paul in Romans 4 to explain the doctrine of justification by faith, quoting Genesis 15:6. This linguistic connection shows that the same cognitive process used by humans to plot evil (chashab) is mirrored in the divine courtroom, where God graciously "calculates" or "reckons" the believer as righteous in Christ, refusing to "impute" or "reckon" their trespasses against them.
