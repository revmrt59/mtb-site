SCHEME - ( mezimmah , Strong's H4209 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun mezimmah in Psalm 21:11 refers to a planned course of action, often carrying the negative connotation of a wicked device, plot, or malicious scheme concocted against the righteous. While the root idea pertains to discretion, planning, or purpose, in moral contexts it frequently describes the calculated machinations of the ungodly that ultimately fail before the sovereign power of God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The term derives from a root connected to thinking, plotting, and devising a plan.

- In the ancient Near Eastern wisdom and poetic literature, human planning is a neutral faculty that becomes morally good or evil depending on the heart behind it. When driven by a rebellious heart against God or His anointed, the planning becomes a hostile siege or weapon.

- The immediate background of Psalm 21 is a royal psalm of thanksgiving for victory, where the king acknowledges God's deliverance and anticipates the total defeat of all opposition.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Mezimmah primarily means a plan, purpose, discretion, or device.

- In its negative application, it signifies a wicked plot, mischievous device, or malicious scheme.

- It denotes deliberate, calculated intent rather than a sudden, accidental impulse; it is evil that has been thought through and organized.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The word paints a picture of individuals sitting down to carefully architect opposition, plotting how to undermine, trap, or destroy others.

- It highlights the intellectual dimension of sin—that wickedness is frequently strategic, organized, and intentional.

- It stands in stark contrast to the divine counsels, showing puny human strategies pitting themselves against the unshakeable decrees of Almighty God.

4. Theological Implications

- Human autonomy often manifests as rebellion, where men use their God-given capacity for intellect and planning to devise evil.

- Psalm 21:11 explicitly states that these wicked schemes "they are not able to perform." This underscores the biblical theology of divine sovereignty: human beings may plot freely, but they possess no ultimate sovereignty to execute plans that contradict the will of God.

- The futility of human plotting against God's king highlights the security of those who trust in the Lord.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 21:11, the wicked have "intended [literally, inclined] evil against" the king and "devised a plot" (mezimmah).

- The poetic structure demonstrates that despite the coordinated, malicious nature of their attack, their malice crashes against the impenetrable wall of God's judgment and protection.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Job 21:16 - "Indeed their prosperity is not in their hand; the counsel of the wicked is far from me." (Note: While translated "counsel" here in some versions, the conceptual parallel of human planning appears frequently in wisdom literature.)

- Proverbs 12:5 - "The thoughts of the righteous are right, but the counsels of the wicked are deceitful."

- Proverbs 24:9 - "A foolish devriving is sin, and the scoffer is an abomination to men."

- Jeremiah 23:20 - "The anger of the Lord will not turn back until He has executed and performed the thoughts of His heart." (Using the root concept of divine purpose versus human schemes.)

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The ultimate fulfillment of wicked scheming failing against God's anointed is seen at the cross of Christ, where human and demonic powers plotted the death of Jesus, only for God to use that very event to accomplish eternal redemption (Acts 4:27-28).

- Believers can take comfort when facing malicious opposition, knowing that human plots have a strict ceiling imposed by God's sovereign rule.
