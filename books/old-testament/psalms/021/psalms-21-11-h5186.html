INTENDED - ( natah , Strong's H5186 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb natah carries the core meaning of stretching out, bending, or inclining. In Psalm 21:11, it describes the calculated, deliberate plotting of the wicked against the righteous, where they bend their bows and direct their malicious schemes toward a specific target. The word reveals both the physical action of aiming a weapon and the moral posture of a heart inclined toward evil.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root verb fundamentally pictures the stretching out of a tent curtain, the leaning of a wall, or the inclining of a hand or heart.

- When applied to warfare or hunting, it evokes the physical act of drawing and aiming a bow, bending the flexible wood to direct a deadly projectile toward a target.

- Culturally, it underscores intentionality; an archer does not shoot blindly, but carefully sights the mark before releasing the arrow.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Natah spans a semantic range that includes to stretch out, spread out, pitch (a tent), bend, incline, turn aside, or thrust.

- In the context of human planning or scheming, it signifies framing, devising, or directing evil intentions toward someone.

- It highlights a purposeful turning of one's will and energy toward a specific objective, whether that objective is righteous or wicked.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The physical imagery of bending or stretching appears when someone inclines an ear to listen or stretches out a hand to help.

- In negative contexts, as in Psalm 21:11, the wicked "stretch out" or "incline" their evil plots, laying a trap with deliberate forethought.

- The word illustrates that sin is rarely accidental; it involves a calculated turning aside from the path of righteousness to take aim at the innocent.

4. Theological Implications

- Psalm 21 is a royal psalm of thanksgiving for victory, acknowledging that God protects the king. Verse 11 contrasts human malice with divine sovereignty.

- Although the wicked meticulously plan and target the righteous ("they intended evil"), their malice ultimately shatters against the sovereign power of God.

- Human intention (natah) is fully exposed before the omniscient God, who easily overturns the best-laid schemes of rebellious people.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 21:11, the ESV and NKJV translate the phrase to show that the wicked devised or intended evil against the Lord's anointed.

- The context emphasizes the futility of opposing God; the wicked can aim their weapons and plot in secret, but they "are not able to perform it" because God intercepts their arrows.

- Here, natah highlights human accountability and malice: the wicked take deliberate aim, yet their hostility is utterly powerless against divine protection.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Genesis 12:8: "And he moved from there to the mountain east of Bethel, and he pitched [natah] his tent with Bethel on the west and Ai on the east..."

- Exodus 10:13: "So Moses stretched out [natah] his rod over the land of Egypt, and the Lord brought an east wind on the land all that day and all that night..."

- 1 Kings 8:58: "that He may incline [natah] our hearts to Himself, to walk in all His ways, and to keep His commandments..."

- Proverbs 4:5: "Get wisdom! Get understanding! Do not forget, nor turn away [natah] from the words of my mouth."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The same Hebrew root is used both for human plotting (evil intent) and divine grace (inclining the heart toward obedience or stretching out the hand to save).

- This dual usage reminds the believer that intentions matter deeply to God; human beings are constantly directing their hearts either toward rebellion or toward submission to His will.
