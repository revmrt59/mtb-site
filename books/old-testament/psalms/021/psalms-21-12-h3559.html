READY - ( kuwn , Strong's H3559 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word kuwn carries the foundational idea of being firmly established, prepared, or made ready. In Psalm 21:12, it describes the divine positioning of arrows upon the bowstring, aimed directly at the faces of God's adversaries. This vivid imagery portrays the absolute certainty of divine judgment, emphasizing that God's justice is neither delayed by surprise nor prone to missing its mark.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root verb kuwn fundamentally means to be stable, erect, established, or prepared.

- In ancient warfare, the archer must carefully set the arrow upon the string, drawing it back with deliberate tension and precise aim.

- The picture is one of calculated readiness—the bow is drawn, the arrow is fitted, and the posture of the archer is steadfast and immovable.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- To set up, establish, fix, or make firm.

- To prepare, make ready, or arrange with intentionality.

- To direct or make stable so that success or execution is certain.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- Establishing physical foundations, such as mountains or thrones (Psalm 24:2; 93:2).

- Preparing a physical banquet or making provisions (Genesis 43:25).

- Directing the heart to seek God or moral steadfastness (Ezra 7:10; Psalm 78:8).

- Tactical military readiness, as seen in the preparation of weapons for battle (Psalm 7:13; 21:12).

4. Theological Implications

- God's judgment against unrepentant evil is not impulsive or chaotic; it is deliberate, calculated, and securely prepared.

- When the wicked oppose the Lord, they step directly into the path of arrows that are already notched and aimed by divine sovereignty.

- The same attribute that brings terror to the rebellious brings immense comfort to the righteous: God has likewise established and prepared salvation, grace, and eternal dwelling places for His people (Psalm 57:7; John 14:2).

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 21, King David rejoices in the strength and victory granted by God to the earthly king, which ultimately points forward to the absolute reign of the Messiah.

- Verse 12 reveals the turning point where the enemies of God attempt to devise evil plots (v. 11), but God flips their schemes back upon them.

- By making His arrows "ready" upon the string against their faces, God demonstrates that human rebellion is utterly futile before the precision of divine retribution.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Psalm 7:13 - "He also has prepared for Himself instruments of death; He makes His arrows into fiery shafts."

- Psalm 57:7 - "My heart is steadfast, O God, my heart is steadfast; I will sing and give praise."

- Psalm 119:90 - "Your faithfulness endures to all generations; You established the earth, and it abides."

- Proverbs 4:18 - "But the path of the just is like the shining sun, that shines ever brighter unto the perfect day."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The grammatical form in Psalm 21:12 indicates a causative or active state—God Himself is the One actively bringing the weapon to the point of readiness.

- The mention of the "faces" implies inescapable confrontation; the adversary will look directly upon the judgment meant to humble them.
