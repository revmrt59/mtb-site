ARROWS - ( chets , Strong's H4340 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word chets primarily denotes a physical arrow shot from a bow, serving frequently throughout the Old Testament as a vivid metaphor for divine judgment, sudden calamity, or the malicious words of the wicked. In Psalm 21:12, the term vividly pictures the decisive, unerring accuracy of God's judgment directed against the faces of His unrepentant enemies, emphasizing that no human rebellion can evade or deflect the righteous wrath of the Almighty.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root concept of chets is rooted in ancient warfare and hunting, specifically the projectile weapon launched from a bow (qesheth).

- In the ancient Near East, the bow and arrow represented long-range destruction, striking swiftly and fatally before the target could defend themselves.

- The imagery naturally transitioned in biblical poetry to describe invisible, sudden, and piercing acts of judgment or affliction sent from heaven.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Chets translates to arrow, shaft, or missile.

- It can refer literally to the feathered wooden shafts used by archers in battle (1 Samuel 20:36).

- It is frequently employed metaphorically to describe divine retribution, bitter words, or sudden terrors (Deuteronomy 32:23; Psalm 64:3; Lamentations 3:12).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The word is often coupled with verbs of shooting, making ready, or targeting, creating a mental picture of a mighty archer drawing back a bowstring with deliberate, focused intent.

- When applied to God, it portrays Him as the divine Marksman whose aim is infallible; His arrows do not miss their mark.

- When applied to the wicked, it describes deceitful speech launched secretly against the upright (Psalm 64:3).

4. Theological Implications

- God's justice is deliberate, prepared, and inescapable. Just as an archer takes aim before releasing a shaft, the Lord sovereignly positions His judgment against persistent evil.

- The phrase "make them ready" (kuwn, to establish or prepare) indicates that divine judgment is not a random accident of fate, but a settled decree.

- The vulnerability of humanity before God is underscored; human armor and strength are futile when the Creator draws the bowstring.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- Psalm 21 is a royal psalm of thanksgiving for victory and divine blessing bestowed upon the king.

- Verse 12 shifts to the certainty of God's triumph over all His adversaries: "Therefore you will make them turn their back; you will aim your arrows at their faces."

- "Turning their back" implies ignominious flight and retreat, while aiming at the "faces" denotes personal confrontation, inescapable accountability, and utter humiliation before the King.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Deuteronomy 32:23 - "I will heap disasters on them; I will spend My arrows on them."

- Psalm 64:3 - "Who sharpen their tongue like a sword, and bend their bows to shoot their arrows—bitter words."

- Lamentations 3:12 - "He has bent His bow and set me up as a target for the arrow."

- Habakkuk 3:11 - "The sun and moon stood still in their habitation; at the light of Your arrows they went, at the shining of Your glittering spear."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The imagery of God's arrows also appears in contexts of spiritual warfare and protection, where the Lord defends His people and restrains the onslaught of the enemy.

- In prophetic literature, the shining of the Lord's arrows is linked to His glorious and terrifying epiphany in history and final judgment.
