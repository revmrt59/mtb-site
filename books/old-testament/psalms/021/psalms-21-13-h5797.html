STRENGTH - ( oz , Strong's H5797 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word oz conveys raw, foundational power, might, and strength, primarily used in the Old Testament to describe the unstoppable might of God. In Psalm 21:13, it points to the glorious, triumphant power of the Lord that overcomes all opposition, inviting His people to praise Him not merely for what He does, but for who He is in His sovereign might.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• Rooted in a primitive root meaning to be strong, bold, or prominent.

• Often associated with physical security, a fortress, or a stronghold where one finds protection.

• Frequently contrasted with human frailty, highlighting that true, enduring power belongs exclusively to God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Might, strength, power, or force.

• Strong security or refuge (as in a fortified place).

• Splendor, majesty, or glory, particularly as an attribute radiating from God's presence and action.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Used of human physical or political power, though most frequently employed regarding divine omnipotence.

• Serves as an architectural metaphor for a stronghold or place of safety.

• Often linked with the ark of God, representing His dwelling place and the tangible manifestation of His presence and power among His people.

4. Theological Implications

• God is the exclusive source and author of all true strength; human beings possess no independent might that can rival or sustain them apart from Him.

• Divine strength is never exercised in a vacuum; it is consistently tied to His covenant faithfulness, salvation, and the defense of His people.

• The ultimate expression of God's oz is seen in His victory over His enemies, establishing His eternal kingdom and eliciting joyful worship from the redeemed.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 21:13, the congregation responds to the king's victory with a collective resolution: "Be exalted, O Lord, in Your own strength!"

• Here, oz refers to God's overt, manifest power displayed in salvation and judgment.

• The verse pivots from celebrating earthly royal triumph to magnifying the ultimate, divine Sovereign, acknowledging that all human strength derives from and must submit to His glorious might.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 15:2 - "The Lord is my strength and song, and He has become my salvation..."

• Psalm 29:11 - "The Lord will give strength to His people; the Lord will bless His people with peace."

• Psalm 46:1 - "God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble."

• Isaiah 12:2 - "Behold, God is my salvation, I will trust and not be afraid; 'For Yah, the Lord, is my strength and song; He also has become my salvation.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The LXX frequently translates oz with words related to power (dynamis), might (ischys), or glory (doxa), bridging the Old Testament concept of God's active strength with New Testament declarations of Christ as the power of God.

• Recognizing God as oz transforms anxiety into worship, shifting the believer's gaze from overwhelming circumstances to an unconquerable God.
