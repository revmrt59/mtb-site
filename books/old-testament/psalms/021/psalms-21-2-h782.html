REQUEST - ( she'alah , Strong's H782 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word she'alah found in Psalm 21:2 refers to an earnest petition, asking, or specific request made to a higher authority, ultimately highlighting the intimate relationship between the believer and God where human desire meets divine generosity.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• Rooted in the verb sha'al, meaning to ask, inquire, or request.

• Paints a picture of a petitioner coming before a king with an open hand and an honest heart, asking for a specific provision or favor.

• In the ancient Near Eastern context, bringing a she'alah before a monarch required protocol, yet the psalm presents God as a King who not only invites the petition but eagerly anticipates granting it.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Defined as a petition, request, or thing asked for.

• Represents the spoken or unexpressed desire of the heart brought into petition before God.

• Emphasizes the relational aspect of prayer, where dependency is acknowledged and the petitioned party holds the power to grant or withhold the request.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Used across the Old Testament for everyday requests between humans as well as solemn petitions directed toward God.

• Often connected with prayer, fasting, and deep emotional plea.

• Illustrates the alignment of human desire with divine will, where what is asked (she'alah) corresponds to what God delights to provide.

4. Theological Implications

• Demonstrates God's attentiveness and responsiveness to the prayers of His people.

• Highlights divine grace: God anticipates the king's request before it is even fully articulated.

• Reminds the believer that prayer is not merely informing an ignorant God, but humbly submitting human desires to a sovereign, loving Provider.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 21:2, David rejoices that God has given him his heart's desire and has not withheld the she'alah (request) of his lips.

• The context is royal thanksgiving for victory and sustained blessing, acknowledging that the king's success flows directly from God's gracious response to his petition.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• 1 Samuel 1:17 - "Then Eli answered and said, 'Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition which you have asked of Him.'"

• 1 Samuel 1:27 - "For this child I prayed, and the Lord has granted me my petition which I asked of Him."

• Esther 5:7 - "Then Esther answered and said, 'My petition and request is...'"

• Psalm 106:15 - "And He gave them their request, but sent leanness into their soul."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The shift from the "desire of the heart" to the "request of the lips" shows that true prayer involves both internal longing and honest verbal expression before God.

• Contrast is seen in Scripture where requests can be granted out of judgment (as in the wilderness) versus grace and fellowship (as in the Psalms).
