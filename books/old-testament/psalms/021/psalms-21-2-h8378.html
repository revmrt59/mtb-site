DESIRE - ( taavah , Strong's H8378 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun taavah refers to an earnest longing, a deep-seated craving, or a heartfelt petition of the soul. In Psalm 21:2, it speaks of the heartfelt petition and inward aspirations that a king or believer places before God, which the Lord graciously grants.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root verb avah means to incline, covet, or long for intensely.

- Taavah takes this inward inclination and gives it substance as the actual object of desire, craving, or petition.

- In the ancient Near Eastern context, a king's desires were often executed by his own military might or administrative power. However, Psalm 21 reframes the royal desire as something wholly dependent upon the grace and provision of Yahweh.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Defined lexically as a longing, desire, appetite, or strong inclination.

- It can carry a negative connotation when directed toward forbidden things (such as lust or ungodly craving, as in Numbers 11:4), but it frequently carries a positive or neutral meaning when describing human longings that God meets, or the legitimate petitions placed before Him.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The word often paints a picture of an appetite that reaches out for satisfaction, much like physical hunger or thirst applied to the emotional and spiritual realm.

- It represents the inner posture of the will where a person acknowledges their lack and fervently reaches out for fulfillment.

4. Theological Implications

- God is intimately acquainted with the deepest longings of His people.

- True spiritual fulfillment does not come from self-generated ambition, but from God answering the righteous petitions of those who trust Him.

- The verse highlights the sweet communion between God and His anointed, where human desire aligns with divine generosity.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 21:2, the psalmist declares of the king: "You have given him his heart's desire, and have not withheld the request of his lips."

- Here, taavah (“heart’s desire”) is paired in poetic parallelism with “the request of his lips,” showing that the inward, unspoken longings of the king's heart find complete expression and fulfillment in God's gracious response.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Numbers 11:4: "Now the mixed multitude who were among them yielded to intense desire; so the children of Israel also wept again and said: 'Who will give us meat to eat?'"

- Proverbs 10:24: "The fear of the wicked, it will come upon him, and the desire of the righteous will be granted."

- Psalm 106:14: "But lusted exceedingly in the wilderness, and tested God in the desert."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- When the human heart is disciplined by righteousness, its deepest longings increasingly mirror the heart of God, making the granting of those desires an expression of divine fellowship rather than mere indulgence.
