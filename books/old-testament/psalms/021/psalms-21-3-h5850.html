CROWN - ( 'atar , Strong's H5850 )

Summary:

In Psalms 21:3, the Hebrew term translated as "crown" describes the act of bestowing royal honor, victory, and divine favor. Rather than merely denoting an inanimate object placed upon a head, the word carries the dynamic concept of surrounding, protecting, and crowning someone with unmerited dignity and blessing. In the context of Psalm 21, it speaks of God going before the king with blessings of goodness and setting a crown of pure gold upon his head, symbolizing both historical kingship and pointing forward to the ultimate, eternal triumph of the Messiah.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root verb behind this noun form means to surround, encompass, or encircle. In ancient warfare and royal settings, a crown was not just an ornament of office; it was a visible manifestation of honor, victory, and divine appointment.

- In the ancient Near East, kings viewed their authority as divinely granted. The imagery of being crowned by God implies that true sovereignty, protection, and honor originate from the heavenly realm.

- The physical act of crowning in Israelite culture marked public validation, accession to the throne, and the transfer or recognition of authority and blessing.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- The root 'atar primarily signifies to encompass or surround, and by extension, to crown or bestow a crown.

- It is used both as a verb (to surround or to crown) and as a noun (a crown, diadem, or encircling honor).

- Lexically, the word combines the spatial idea of being completely surrounded or encircled with the relational idea of honor, glory, and exaltation.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The verbal and nominal forms of this root create a vivid mental picture of God placing a protective and honorable perimeter around His people or His anointed king.

- Just as a wall encircles a city to protect it, or a garment wraps around the body, God's favor and the symbol of the crown encircle the recipient with grace.

- It depicts a posture of divine initiative: God takes the first step by meeting the king with rich blessings and placing the emblem of victory upon his head.

4. Theological Implications

- In covenant theology, the king of Israel was viewed as God's vice-regent. Therefore, when God crowns the king, He is establishing His own rule and goodness through human agency.

- The concept shifts ultimate credit for victory and majesty away from human achievement and places it squarely upon divine grace ("You meet him with the blessings of goodness").

- It anticipates the Messianic theology of the Psalms, where earthly kingship falls short, but the ultimate Son of David is crowned with glory and honor by the Father (Psalm 8:5, Hebrews 2:9).

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- Psalm 21 is a royal psalm of thanksgiving and confidence, likely used to celebrate a king's victory in battle or his accession, offering praise to God for His deliverance.

- Verse 3 states: "For You meet him with the blessings of goodness; You set a crown of pure gold upon his head."

- Here, the "crown" is a gift from God. The king did not merely seize it by military might; God met him proactively with goodness and bestowed the crown upon him. The focus is on divine enablement and unearned royal dignity.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Psalm 103:4 - "Who redeems your life from destruction, who crowns you with lovingkindness and tender mercies."

- Proverbs 14:18 - "The simple inherit folly, but the prudent are crowned with knowledge."

- Isaiah 28:5 - "In that day the Lord of hosts will be for a crown of glory and a diadem of beauty to the remnant of His people."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The Septuagint (LXX) often translates related concepts using words associated with victory and honor (stephanos or diadema), linking earthly coronation with ultimate spiritual triumph.

- The progression in Psalm 21 moves from strength and salvation (verses 1-2) to divine enrichment and crowning (verse 3), demonstrating that God's ultimate goal for His anointed is not merely survival in battle, but flourishing under His honored favor.
