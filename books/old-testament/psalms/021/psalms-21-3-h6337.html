GOLD - ( paz , Strong's H6337 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word paz refers to refined, pure gold of the highest quality, symbolizing absolute worth, splendor, and enduring value. In Psalm 21:3, it describes the crown of pure gold placed upon the king's head, representing the rich blessings, honor, and majesty bestowed by God upon His anointed.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root idea behind the term relates to the process of refining, pressing, or squeezing out impurities.

- It pictures metal that has passed through the fire until all dross has been removed, leaving only what is unalloyed and brilliant.

- In the ancient Near East, gold was the ultimate standard of wealth, kingship, and divine favor, frequently associated with royalty and sacred temple vessels.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Paz specifically denotes refined, solid, or pure gold.

- It differs from standard words for gold by emphasizing its extreme purity and brilliance.

- Lexically, it conveys the ideas of radiant splendor, permanence, and supreme worth.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The word is often used poetically in the Old Testament to contrast the unmatched value of divine wisdom and God's word with earthly riches.

- It creates a vivid mental image of unblemished brightness and unassailable durability.

- When applied to crowns or ornaments, it visually underscores the dignity, glory, and unblemished honor of the recipient.

4. Theological Implications

- The best of human wealth—refined gold—serves merely as a faint metaphor for the surpassing glory of God's attributes, His laws, and His gracious gifts to His people.

- When God bestows a crown of paz upon His king, it signifies that the honor comes directly from the heavenly treasury, marked by divine purity and permanence.

- It points forward to the ultimate, undefiled rule of the Messianic King, whose kingdom is established in absolute righteousness and glory.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- Psalm 21 is a royal psalm of thanksgiving for victory and divine blessing bestowed upon the king.

- Psalm 21:3 states: "For You meet him with the blessings of goodness; You set a crown of pure gold upon his head."

- Here, the pure gold crown is not merely a trophy of human conquest, but a gracious gift from God ("You set"), highlighting that the king's supreme dignity and honor flow directly from the hand of the Lord.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Job 28:17: "The gold and crystal cannot equal it, nor can it be exchanged for jewelry of fine gold." (paz)

- Psalm 19:10: "More to be desired are they than gold, yea, than much fine gold; sweeter also than honey and the honeycomb." (paz)

- Lamentations 4:1: "How the gold has become dim! How the fine gold changed! The stones of the sanctuary are poured out at the head of every street." (paz)

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The pairing of divine goodness with the crown of pure gold in Psalm 21 suggests that God's blessings impart both spiritual well-being and visible honor.

- The use of this specific term elevates the royal office as a reflection of divine order and splendor under God's sovereign care.
