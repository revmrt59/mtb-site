HONOR - ( kabod , Strong's H1935 )

Summary:

In Psalm 21:5, the word "honor" translates kabod, a rich biblical term fundamentally meaning "weight," "heavy," and by extension, "glory," "splendor," or "dignity." When applied to the king in this passage, it points to the majestic splendor and royal dignity conferred upon him by God Himself, marking him as the recipient of divine favor and victorious blessing.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root verb kabad carries the literal physical sense of being heavy or burdensome.

- In ancient Hebrew thought, that which is heavy carries substance, value, importance, and reality, as opposed to what is light, superficial, or worthless.

- Over time, the noun form kabod came to represent the visible manifestation of a person's weight in society—their honor, glory, majesty, and reputation.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Splendor: The outward display of majesty and magnificence.

- Glory: The luminous or impressive character associated with high status.

- Dignity / Honor: The rightful respect, exalted position, and esteemed standing bestowed upon someone.

- In secular contexts, it could refer to wealth or material substance, but in theological and royal contexts, it denotes esteemed position and divine radiance.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The word appears frequently in the Old Testament, often describing the radiant glory of God (Psalm 19:1, "The heavens declare the glory of God").

- When used regarding human beings, it often describes royal majesty, stately bearing, or the exalted status granted by divine grace.

- It pictures a person clothed not merely in garments of authority, but wrapped in the weighty reality of divine approval and societal reverence.

4. Theological Implications

- True honor is not ultimately self-made or merely secured by human political maneuvering; it is a divine bestowal.

- In Psalm 21, the king’s victory and subsequent honor come entirely from the Lord's salvation, pointing ultimately to the supreme honor and glory given by the Father to the Messianic King.

- Human dignity and royal splendor find their true source and anchor in relationship with God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- Psalm 21 is a royal thanksgiving psalm celebrating God's deliverance and blessing upon the king.

- Verse 5 states: "His glory is great in Your salvation; honor and majesty You have laid upon him."

- Here, "honor" is something actively "laid upon" the king by God, emphasizing that his exalted status is a direct result of God’s saving intervention and sovereign grace.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Psalm 8:5: "For You have made him a little lower than the angels, and You have crowned him with glory and honor (kabod)."

- Psalm 45:3: "Gird your sword upon your thigh, O Mighty One, with your glory and your majesty (kabod)."

- Proverbs 25:2: "It is the glory (kabod) of God to conceal a matter, but the honor (kabod) of kings is to search out a matter."

- Isaiah 22:23: "I will fasten him as a peg in a secure place, and he will become a glorious throne to his father's house. They will hang on him all the glory (kabod) of his father's house."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The Septuagint (LXX) often translates kabod using words like doxa (glory) or timē (honor, value), bridging the Old Testament concept of substantial weight into New Testament discussions of divine and human dignity.

- The connection between salvation and honor in Psalm 21:5 reminds believers that God's rescue always elevates the redeemed into a position of secure standing and purpose.
