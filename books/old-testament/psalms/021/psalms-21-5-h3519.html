GLORY - ( kabod , Strong's H3519 )

Summary:

In the Old Testament, the Hebrew word kabod refers to weight, gravity, or splendor. When applied to God, it represents the heavy, magnificent reality of His divine essence, majesty, and holiness. When applied to the king in Psalm 21:5, it denotes the reflected splendor, honor, and royal dignity bestowed upon him by Yahweh as a result of divine deliverance. It is not an inherent weightiness of the human king, but a derived weightiness granted by God's saving power.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun kabod derives from the root verb kabad, which means "to be heavy" or "to be weighty." In ancient Semitic culture, physical weight was directly associated with value, wealth, authority, and influence. A person of high standing, such as a wealthy patriarch or a powerful king, was described as "heavy" because of their accumulated assets, influence, and societal importance. Conversely, someone of no significance was described as "light" or "worthless" (from the root qalal). Thus, the word picture behind kabod is that of an imposing, substantial reality that demands respect, commands attention, and carries immense moral and spiritual weight.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Kabod carries several distinct nuances depending on the context:

• Abundance and Wealth: The material substance, riches, and possessions that make a person influential (e.g., Genesis 31:1).

• Reputation and Honor: The high esteem, respect, and dignity accorded to an individual by others.

• Splendor and Majesty: The visible, external manifestation of beauty, power, and authority, particularly associated with royalty and the temple.

• The Divine Presence: The radiant, blinding brightness of God's manifest presence, often accompanied by clouds, fire, and smoke, revealing His absolute holiness and power to humanity.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament employs kabod to illustrate the contrast between human transience and divine permanence:

• The Weight of Wealth: Abraham is described as "very rich" (literally, "very heavy") in livestock, silver, and gold, showcasing how material kabod translates to earthly standing.

• The Chariot of Fire: The glory of God is depicted as a heavy, consuming presence. When the kabod of Yahweh filled the Tabernacle (Exodus 40:34) and the Temple (1 Kings 8:11), the priests could not stand to minister because of the sheer spiritual density and holiness of the divine presence.

• The Reflected Crown: Humans were crowned with kabod at creation (Psalm 8:5), acting as God's images to reflect His weighty character to the rest of the created order.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, kabod establishes that God alone possesses absolute, underived weightiness. All human glory is secondary, fragile, and temporary, like a fading flower. True honor for humanity is found only in relationship with God. When God saves, delivers, and exalts His people, He imparts a portion of His kabod to them. This is ultimately fulfilled in Jesus Christ, who is the "brightness of His glory and the express image of His person" (Hebrews 1:3). Through Christ, believers are transformed from "glory to glory" (2 Corinthians 3:18), receiving a weight of glory that far outweighs temporal afflictions.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 21:5, David writes of the king: "His glory is great in Your salvation; Honor and majesty You have placed upon him."

• The immediate context is a celebration of the king’s victory and preservation through Yahweh’s strength.

• The king’s kabod (glory) is explicitly tied to God's yeshua (salvation). David is not boasting of his own military prowess or innate majesty. Instead, he acknowledges that his royal dignity and weightiness are gifts from God.

• The parallel terms "honor" (hod) and "majesty" (hadar) are royal garments that God has "placed upon him." The king is clothed in a reflected splendor. He is weighty and respected among the nations because the God of Israel has stood by him and delivered him.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 33:18: "And he said, 'Please, show me Your glory.'"

• Psalm 19:1: "The heavens declare the glory of God; And the firmament shows His handiwork."

• Isaiah 6:3: "And one cried to another and said: 'Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts; The whole earth is full of His glory!'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Septuagint (LXX), the Greek translators almost exclusively translated kabod using the word doxa. In classical Greek, doxa meant "opinion," "conjecture," or "reputation" (what others think of you). However, when used to translate kabod, doxa was infused with the Hebrew concept of weight, splendor, power, and the visible manifestation of God. This linguistic transformation prepared the New Testament writers to use doxa to describe the physical incarnation of Jesus Christ, in whom the full weight (kabod) of God dwelt bodily (John 1:14).
