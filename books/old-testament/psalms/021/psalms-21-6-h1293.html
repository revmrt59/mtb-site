BLESSED - ( berakah , Strong's H1293 )

Summary:

The Hebrew term berakah fundamentally denotes an endowment of divine favor, a state of prosperity, or a pronouncement of well-being that originates from God. In Psalm 21:6, it represents the ultimate realization of royal joy and eternal favor bestowed upon the king by God's very presence. Rather than a mere wish or abstract concept, it is a tangible, life-giving reality imparted by the Creator, securing lasting honor, gladness, and steadfast security for the recipient.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root verb from which berakah derives is barach, which carries the primary physical action of kneeling. In ancient cultural practice, to kneel before a superior was to render homage, but in a theological context, it signifies the posture of receiving divine bounty or God kneeling down in grace to bestow gifts upon His people.

- In the ancient Near East, royal blessings were often thought to be mediated through deities to earthly monarchs to ensure military victory, agricultural fertility, and dynastic stability. In Scripture, however, the source of this blessing is exclusively Yahweh, the covenant God of Israel, who alone possesses the power to bless.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Berakah translates to "blessing," "source of blessing," or "benefit."

- Lexically, it signifies the transference of divine favor that results in material, spiritual, or relational flourishing.

- It encompasses both the active bestowal of divine goodness and the resulting state of blessedness experienced by the recipient.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The word is frequently used in three distinct ways across the Old Testament: as a material benefit or gift (Genesis 33:11), as a formal spoken blessing or invocation (Genesis 27:38), and as a covenantal state of ultimate well-being and favor from God (Psalm 21:6).

- In poetry and wisdom literature, berakah serves as an antidote to the curse, reversing the effects of the Fall by restoring the divine-human relationship and establishing peace (shalom).

4. Theological Implications

- Divine blessing is entirely unearned and flows directly from God's character and covenant faithfulness (hesed).

- In Psalm 21, the blessing bestowed upon the king points typologically beyond David to the ultimate Messianic King, Jesus Christ, who receives the supreme blessing of eternal life and resurrection joy from the Father.

- True blessedness is found not in self-generated achievement, but in being perpetually set in the presence of God, where fullness of joy resides.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- Psalm 21 is a royal psalm of thanksgiving and confidence, celebrating the victory and enduring strength God has given to the king.

- In verse 6, the text reads, "For You have made him most blessed forever; You have made him exceedingly glad with Your countenance." Here, berakah (translated "most blessed") is amplified by the phrase "forever" and paired with the gladness of God's face.

- The nuance is that God has permanently crowned the king with His favor, establishing him as an eternal monument of divine grace and joy.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Genesis 12:2 - "I will make you a great nation; I will bless you and make your name great; and you shall be a blessing."

- Psalm 24:5 - "He shall receive blessing from the Lord, and righteousness from the God of his salvation."

- Proverbs 10:6 - "Blessings are on the head of the righteous, but violence covers the mouth of the wicked."

- Joel 2:14 - "Who knows if He will turn and relent, and leave a blessing behind Him—a grain offering and a drink offering for the Lord your God?"

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The plural form of related words is sometimes used to denote abundant or overflowing blessings, emphasizing that God's grace toward His anointed is never meager.

- The concept of being made a blessing in Scripture always moves outward: those who receive berakah from God are intended to be conduits of God's grace to the surrounding world.
