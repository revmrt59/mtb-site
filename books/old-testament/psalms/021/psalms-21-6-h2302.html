GLAD - ( chah-lawḥ , Strong's H2302 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word chah-lawḥ carries the primary meaning of being joyful, rejoicing, or making glad, often expressing an outward manifestation of an inner state of gladness. In Psalms 21:6, it describes the profound, exceeding joy experienced by the king in the very presence of God, flowing directly from divine blessing, victory, and fellowship.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root verb fundamentally communicates the physical and emotional expression of joy, often tied to a radiant countenance or an uplifted spirit.

- In ancient Near Eastern royal contexts, a king's gladness was frequently tied to military triumph or wealth, but in the Psalms, true gladness is radically re-centered upon the living God and His enduring favor.

- The term evokes the dynamic posture of someone whose heart has been lifted out of despair or uncertainty and placed into a secure, celebratory reality by the hand of God Himself.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- To rejoice, be glad, be joyful, or make glad.

- It spans both the internal posture of happiness and the external expression of celebration.

- It denotes an intense, settled state of joy rather than a fleeting, superficial emotion.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The word is often paired with physical or emotional exuberance, capturing a whole-person response to divine goodness.

- It describes the contagious joy of a community or an individual recognizing that God has intervened graciously on their behalf.

- It contrasts sharply with mourning or anxiety, serving as a bright focal point of spiritual restoration and divine approval.

4. Theological Implications

- True gladness is never self-generated in biblical theology; it is consistently rooted in God's presence, salvation, and covenant faithfulness.

- For the believer, ultimate joy is found not merely in the gifts God gives, but in the unhindered fellowship of His countenance.

- The gladness of the king in Psalm 21 foreshadows the ultimate joy and exaltation of the Messianic King, whose supreme delight is found in unbroken communion with the Father.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 21:6, the king rejoices because God has bestowed upon him eternal blessings and made him exceedingly glad with His presence.

- The phrase "made him most blessed forever" is intimately connected to being "made him exceeding glad with Your presence" (or literally, "making him glad with joy in Your presence").

- The gladness here is relational and face-to-face; the king's supreme delight is the immediate nearness and favor of God.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Psalm 31:7 - "I will be glad and rejoice in Your mercy, For You have considered my trouble, You have known my soul in adversities."

- Psalm 68:3 - "Pero let the righteous be glad; Let them rejoice before God: Yes, let them rejoice exceedingly."

- Psalm 90:14 - "Oh, satisfy us early with Your mercy, That we may rejoice and be glad all our days!"

- Joel 2:23 - "Be glad then, you children of Zion, And rejoice in the Lord your God; For He has given you the former rain faithfully, And He will cause the rain to come down for you—"

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The grammatical structure in various passages often highlights God as the active agent who causes His people to be glad, emphasizing grace over human effort.

- Joy and gladness function as a spiritual weapon and testimony in the Psalms, testifying to the reliability of God's promises even before every earthly trial is resolved.
