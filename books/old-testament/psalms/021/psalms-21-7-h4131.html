MOVED - ( môt , Strong's H4131 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb môt refers to slipping, shaking, tottering, or falling. In Psalm 21:7, it describes the complete stability and security of the king who trusts in Yahweh. While the world, kingdoms, and human strength are inherently unstable and subject to being violently shaken, the person who rests in the covenant-mercy of the Most High is anchored to an immovable foundation. To "not be moved" is to be preserved from ultimate ruin and displacement, remaining steadfast and secure amidst life's greatest storms.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root môt paints a vivid physical picture of losing one's footing. It describes a foot slipping on a wet or loose path, a foundation cracking and shifting, or a mountain wall crumbling into the sea. In the mountainous terrain of ancient Israel, a slip of the foot on a steep, rocky pass could be fatal. Consequently, walking with a steady, "unmoved" step was the ultimate metaphor for safety, uprightness, and divine protection. To be "moved" is to have your secure position compromised, leading to collapse, vulnerability, and defeat.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb môt means to totter, shake, slip, slide, stagger, or be overthrown. In the Niphal (passive) stem used in Psalm 21:7 (yimmôt), it expresses the state of being shaken, dislodged, or made to totter. The semantic range includes:

• Physical shaking or trembling (such as the earth or mountains).

• Ethical or spiritual wavering (slipping into sin or compromise).

• Political and military instability (kingdoms being shaken or overthrown).

• Experiencing ruin, failure, or being overcome by enemies.

In Psalm 21:7, the passive construction indicates that the king is kept from shaking by an external, supreme power; he is actively secured by the Lord so that no circumstance can dislodge him.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament employs môt to contrast the temporary, shaky nature of the created order with the permanence of God and His promises.

• The Earth and Mountains: Creation seems permanent, yet mountains "slip" (môt) into the sea (Psalm 46:2). In contrast, God established the earth so that it "cannot be moved" (Psalm 104:5), showing His absolute control over chaos.

• The Nations: Kingdoms and empires totter and fall (môt) when God utters His voice (Psalm 46:6).

• The Physical Foot: The slipping of a foot represents the beginning of disaster or defeat (Deuteronomy 32:35; Psalm 38:16).

• The Righteous: In contrast to the wicked who will be cast down, the righteous are promised that God will never allow them to be permanently shaken or moved (Psalm 55:22).

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of môt lies in the contrast between human frailty and divine stability. Left to themselves, human beings—even kings—are highly unstable, easily shaken by fear, sickness, war, and sin. True immovability is an attribute of God alone. When a believer is described as "unmoved," it is because they have participated in the stability of God Himself. By anchoring their life to the Lord, His unshakeable character becomes their protection. This security is not based on the believer's personal strength, but on the unyielding nature of the Rock to which they are bound.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 21:7, the stability of the king is highlighted: "For the king trusts in the LORD, / And through the mercy of the Most High he shall not be moved."

The verse reveals a beautiful theological synergy:

• Human Action: The king actively "trusts" (batah—clings to, relies upon) Yahweh.

• Divine Action: The basis of this security is the "mercy" (chesed—unfailing covenant-love) of the "Most High" (Elyon—the supreme Ruler over all earthly powers).

Because the king trusts in the supreme Sovereign whose covenant love never fails, the king is kept in a state of absolute stability. The attacks of his enemies (detailed in verses 8-12) will fail because the king's position is secured by God. He cannot be overthrown, displaced, or ruined.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 16:8 - "I have set the LORD always before me; Because He is at my right hand I shall not be moved."

• Psalm 46:5 - "God is in the midst of her, she shall not be moved; God shall help her, just at the break of dawn."

• Psalm 62:2 - "He only is my rock and my salvation; He is my defense; I shall not be greatly moved."

• Psalm 125:1 - "Those who trust in the LORD Are like Mount Zion, Which cannot be moved, but abides forever."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Greek Septuagint (LXX), môt is translated by the verb saleuo (σαλεύω), which means to shake, agitate, or cause to waver. This same Greek word is used in the New Testament to describe the shaking of heaven and earth so that "those things which cannot be shaken may remain" (Hebrews 12:27). Ultimately, the only truly "unmoved" King is Jesus Christ, the Son of David. His kingdom is the "kingdom which cannot be shaken" (Hebrews 12:28). Through union with Him, believers share in this eternal, immovable standing.
