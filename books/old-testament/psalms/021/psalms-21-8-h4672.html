FIND - ( matsa' , Strong's H4672 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb matsa' primarily means "to reach," "to find," or "to overtake," conveying the sense of encountering something, often after a search or by divine providence. In Psalm 21:8, it describes the inescapable certainty with which God's hand will reach, apprehend, and overtake His adversaries. Rather than a casual discovery, the term here implies a decisive act of divine justice where the wicked cannot hide or escape the judgment of God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root verb paints a picture of extending the hand to reach a target, coming upon an object, or arriving at a destination.

- In ancient contexts, it frequently describes hunters tracking prey or armies overtaking retreating enemies, emphasizing the certainty of the pursuit.

- Applied to God, it highlights His omnipresence and sovereign ability to track down human motives, actions, and accountability.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Matsa' signifies to find, attain, meet, or acquire.

- In active and causative stems, it carries the nuance of causing to meet, overtaking, or seizing.

- It denotes not merely stumbling upon something by accident, but successfully attaining or reaching an intended object or person.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- Used frequently of finding physical objects, lost items, or provisions (e.g., Genesis 2:20; Genesis 26:12).

- Used of finding divine grace, favor, or wisdom (e.g., Proverbs 3:13, Proverbs 8:35).

- Used in judicial and hostile contexts of sin, guilt, or enemies being "found out" and overtaken by consequence or punishment (e.g., Genesis 44:16, Numbers 32:23).

4. Theological Implications

- God’s justice is inescapable; humanity cannot hide from the penetrating gaze and reach of the Almighty.

- The same word used for finding divine mercy is also used for finding divine judgment, illustrating that human posture toward God determines whether His hand reaches out to save or to condemn.

- Security belongs to the righteous who find refuge in God, while terror awaits the wicked whose sin ultimately "finds" them.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 21:8, King David celebrates God's past victories and expresses total confidence in future triumphs.

- "Your hand will find all your enemies" assures the reader that opposition to God is futile.

- The enemies may scheme, scatter, or hide, but God's hand will extend with unerring precision to reach and subdue them completely.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Genesis 44:16 - Then Judah said, "What shall we say to my lord? What shall we speak? Or how shall we clear ourselves? God has found out the iniquity of your servants; here we are, my lord's slaves, both we and he also with whom the cup was found."

- Numbers 32:23 - But if you do not do so, then take note, you have sinned against the Lord; and be sure your sin will find you out.

- Proverbs 8:35 - "For whoever finds me finds life, and obtains favor from the Lord."

- Isaiah 41:12 - "You shall seek them and not find them—those who contended with you. Those who war against you shall be as nothing, as a nonexistent thing."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The grammatical form in Psalm 21:8 emphasizes certainty and futurity under divine sovereignty.

- It bridges the gap between human rebellion and divine retribution, reminding believers that history moves toward the ultimate vindication of God's righteous rule.
