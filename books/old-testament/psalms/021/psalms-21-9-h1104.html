SWALLOW - ( bala' , Strong's H1104 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb bala' fundamentally means to engulf, swallow down, or consume entirely, leaving no trace behind. In the context of Psalm 21:9, it depicts the absolute, swift, and irreversible destruction of the wicked by God's righteous wrath. Rather than a slow process of decay, this word portrays a sudden and complete vanishing, much like the earth opening up to swallow rebels or a fire consuming its fuel instantly.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The physical action behind bala' is the act of swallowing food or liquid, drawing it into the throat so that it disappears from sight and is assimilated or destroyed. In ancient Near Eastern imagery, the grave (Sheol), death, and predatory beasts are often personified as having wide-open mouths ready to swallow the living. This term captures the terrifying finality of being utterly engulfed and removed from the land of the living. It suggests an active, aggressive eating up that leaves nothing behind, turning what was once prominent and visible into absolute nothingness.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meaning of bala' is to swallow, gulp down, consume, or engulf. In its literal sense, it refers to the physiological process of swallowing food or drink. Figuratively, it denotes total destruction, ruin, or the complete obliteration of an object, person, or nation. In the intensive stem (Piel), which is used in Psalm 21:9, the action is intensified, meaning to completely swallow up, ruin, or wipe out without a trace. It represents a judgment that is sudden, overwhelming, and leaves no remnant.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Swallowing Earth: The most famous historical word picture of bala' is found in the rebellion of Korah, Dathan, and Abiram, where the earth opened its mouth and swallowed them alive. This was a direct, visible act of divine judgment.

• The Devouring Enemy: Enemies of Israel are frequently described as wishing to swallow them up alive, like Sheol, highlighting their ravenous desire to completely destroy the covenant people.

• The Great Fish: In the narrative of Jonah, the lord prepared a great fish to swallow Jonah, showing the word's literal application where God uses the natural order to accomplish His sovereign purposes.

• The Destruction of Death: In a glorious redemptive reversal, the word is used to describe the ultimate destruction of death itself, which has long swallowed humanity but will one day be swallowed up in victory.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, bala' demonstrates that God's final judgment is not merely disciplinary or partial; for those who persistently oppose Him, it is total and consuming. When God swallows His enemies, it signifies that His judgment leaves no survivors, no remnants, and no room for escape. It contrasts the temporary pride of wicked men with the absolute, consuming sovereignty of God. The wicked may puff themselves up and threaten to swallow the righteous, but they are ultimately vulnerable to being swallowed up by the divine decree.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 21:9, David speaks of the destiny of the King's enemies: "The LORD shall swallow them up in His wrath, and the fire shall devour them." The poetic parallelism links "swallow them up" with "fire shall devour them." Both verbs denote complete consumption. The nuance here is the active, personal intervention of Yahweh. The enemies are not just defeated in battle; they are consumed by the personal, fiery wrath of God. The image of the "fiery oven" reinforces this; just as an oven quickly consumes dry wood, Yahweh's wrath completely swallows the wicked, leaving nothing but ash. It emphasizes that opposition to God’s anointed King is a fatal endeavor.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Numbers 16:32: "and the earth opened its mouth and swallowed them up, with their households and all the men with Korah, with all their goods."

• Job 20:15: "He swallows down riches And vomits them up again; God casts them out of his belly."

• Psalm 124:3: "Then they would have swallowed us alive, When their wrath was kindled against us;"

• Isaiah 25:8: "He will swallow up death forever, And the Lord GOD will wipe away tears from all faces; The rebuke of His people He will take away from all the earth; For the LORD has spoken."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament) uses the Greek verb katapino (to drink down, swallow, or consume) to translate bala' in Psalm 21:9 (numbered as Psalm 20:10 in the Greek text). This is the same Greek word used in the New Testament to describe the devil seeking "whom he may devour" (1 Peter 5:8) and the ultimate victory where "Death is swallowed up in victory" (1 Corinthians 15:54). This linguistic connection highlights the thematic continuity from the Old Testament judgments to the final New Testament victories over sin, death, and the devil.
