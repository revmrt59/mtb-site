TROUBLE - ( tsarah , Strong's H6869 )

Summary:

The Hebrew term tsarah describes a state of intense distress, narrow straits, or severe affliction that hems a person in on every side. In Psalm 22:11, the psalmist cries out from a place of overwhelming pressure where help feels distant. The word powerfully captures the human experience of feeling trapped by circumstances, yet it ultimately points the believer to God as the only refuge when all earthly exits are closed.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root idea behind tsarah relates to narrowness, tightness, or being squeezed into a restricted space.

- Culturally and geographically, it evokes the imagery of a traveler trapped in a narrow gorge or pass with enemies closing in, leaving no room to maneuver.

- It represents life closing in with suffocating pressure, where the horizon shrinks and the individual feels entirely cornered by trial, grief, or opposition.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Tsarah translates to trouble, distress, tribulation, anguish, or adversity.

- It denotes a state of acute trouble arising from external circumstances or internal turmoil.

- Lexical sources define it as a time of pressing need or narrow straits, contrasting with the wide, open spaces of deliverance and freedom that God provides.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The word is frequently used in the Old Testament to describe national crises, military sieges, personal grief, and spiritual despair.

- It paints a vivid picture of claustrophobic affliction—situations where a person is pressed hard by sorrow or suffering and can find no relief on their own strength.

- It often appears in tandem with cries for rescue, emphasizing that the one experiencing tsarah is looking desperately for an external deliverer.

4. Theological Implications

- In biblical theology, seasons of tsarah are not evidence of God’s total abandonment, but often the very crucible where faith is refined and desperate prayer is birthed.

- While human strength fails in the "narrow place," God is revealed as the One who brings His people out into a broad, spacious place of safety (Psalm 18:19).

- The presence of trouble drives the dependent believer directly to the throne of divine grace, transforming a place of suffering into a place of encounter with God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 22:11, the suffering servant cries, "Be not far from Me, for trouble (tsarah) is near; for there is none to help."

- Here, the trouble is characterized by its terrifying proximity—it is near—while human help is entirely absent.

- The psalmist experiences the ultimate narrowness of rejection, mocking enemies, and physical agony, making his plea for God's nearness all the more urgent and poignant.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Psalm 50:15 - "Call upon Me in the day of trouble (tsarah); I will deliver you, and you shall glorify Me."

- Genesis 35:3 - "And let us arise and go up to Bethel; and I will make an altar there to God, who answered me in the day of my distress (tsarah), and has been with me in the way which I have gone."

- Nahum 1:7 - "The Lord is good, a stronghold in the day of trouble (tsarah); and He knows those who trust in Him."

- Isaiah 30:20 - "And though the Lord gives you the bread of adversity and the water of affliction, yet your teachers will not be moved into a corner anymore, but your eyes shall see your teachers."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The linguistic link between tsarah and words for "adversary" or "enemy" (tsar) highlights that trouble often comes packaged in human opposition or relational hostility.

- Recognizing the meaning of tsarah deepens our appreciation for passages that speak of God rescuing us from "distress," reminding us that salvation is not merely forgiveness of sins, but rescue from the suffocating traps of a fallen world.
