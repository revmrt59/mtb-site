LION - ( 'aryeh , Strong's H738 )

Summary:

In biblical Hebrew, the word 'aryeh refers to the lion, the apex predator of the ancient Near East, symbolizing majesty, unmatched strength, and terrifying destructive power. In the context of Psalm 22:13, the roaring and tearing lion represents the fierce, merciless enemies who surround the suffering Psalmist (and prophetically, the suffering Messiah), seeking to utterly devour and destroy his life.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The lion ('aryeh) was a well-known and feared animal in ancient Israel, roaming the Jordan valley, forested areas, and deserts. It stood at the top of the animal hierarchy, representing undisputed power, courage, and lethal force. Its roar was associated with terror, signaling an imminent, inescapable attack. In ancient Near Eastern iconography, lions were symbols of royalty and military conquest, often used by kings to demonstrate their own power or to depict their ruthless suppression of enemies.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term 'aryeh is the primary Hebrew word for a fully grown, mature male lion. It denotes a creature characterized by its strength, ferocity, and predatory nature. The root meaning is associated with gathering, plucking, or tearing away, reflecting the lion's method of seizing and tearing its prey. It is used both literally of the wild beast and metaphorically to describe powerful leaders, fierce enemies, or even God Himself in His execution of judgment.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament uses the lion in several vivid word pictures:

• Ruthless Power: Describing violent, lawless men or hostile nations invading Israel.

• Majestic Authority: Symbolizing the tribe of Judah (the "lion of the tribe of Judah") and the ultimate Messianic King.

• The Wrath of God: God is sometimes depicted as a lion that tears and goes away, emphasizing the irresistibility of His judgment.

• The Devil / Demonic Forces: While more explicit in the New Testament (1 Peter 5:8), the Old Testament often uses lions to picture demonic oppression and spiritual adversaries.

4. Theological Implications

The lion's presence in Scripture highlights the reality of a fallen world filled with active, destructive forces. When applied to the enemies of God's people, it reminds believers of their helplessness apart from divine intervention. A human cannot stand against a roaring lion; salvation must come from the Lord. Prophetically, the lion in Psalm 22 points to the spiritual and physical ferocity of the forces of darkness gathered against Jesus Christ on the cross. The ultimate "Lion" (Judah's King) allowed Himself to be surrounded and seemingly defeated by these metaphorical lions to deliver His people from the power of the grave.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 22:13, the Psalmist cries out: "They gape at me with their mouths, Like a raging and roaring lion." The plural subject "They" refers to the hostile mockers and persecutors (likened also to the "bulls of Bashan" in verse 12). The "gaping mouth" of the lion is a terrifying image of imminent destruction. The enemies do not merely threaten; they are poised to consume. The modifiers "raging" (tearing, ripping) and "roaring" emphasize both the physical violence and the psychological terror inflicted upon the sufferer. This directly mirrors the scene at Calvary, where the religious leaders and Roman soldiers mocked Jesus, expressing their voracious hostility as He hung on the cross, seeking to swallow up His life and legacy.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 49:9: "Judah is a lion’s whelp; From the prey, my son, you have gone up. He bows down, he lies down as a lion; And as a lion, who shall rouse him?"

• Proverbs 28:15: "Like a roaring lion and a charging bear Is a wicked ruler over poor people."

• Isaiah 31:4: "For thus the LORD has spoken to me: 'As a lion roars, And a young lion over his prey (When a multitude of shepherds is called against him, He will not be afraid of their voice Nor be humbled by their noise), So the LORD of hosts will come down To fight for Mount Zion and for its hill.'"

• Amos 3:8: "A lion has roared! Who will not fear? The Lord GOD has spoken! Who can but prophesy?"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Hebrew text of Psalm 22 contains a famous and highly debated wordplay in verse 16: "They pierced my hands and my feet." In the Masoretic Text, this reads ka'ari ("like a lion [my hands and my feet]"), whereas the Septuagint (LXX) translates it as "they dug/pierced" (oryxan). The contextual link between the lions of verse 13 and the debated "like a lion" of verse 16 underscores the intense poetic theme of being hunted and torn by wild, predatory beasts.
