WAX - ( dônag , Strong's H1749 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word dônag refers to beeswax, a substance known for its solid state at normal temperatures but rapid softening and melting when exposed to heat. In the Old Testament, it is used metaphorically to describe extreme vulnerability, utter helplessness, and the dissolving of human strength or courage in the presence of intense suffering or divine judgment. In Psalm 22:14, it represents the complete collapse of the sufferer's inner fortitude under the heat of severe trial.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

Beeswax was a common and familiar substance in the ancient Near East. It was used for sealing documents, creating casting molds for metalworking (the lost-wax process), and coating writing tablets. The primary characteristic of wax in biblical imagery is its physical instability when heated. While it appears solid and durable under normal conditions, the application of heat causes it to lose its form, strength, and structure instantly. Unlike stone or metals that require specialized furnace temperatures to melt, wax melts easily, turning from a solid into a useless, shape-lost liquid.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun dônag refers specifically to wax or beeswax. It denotes a substance that liquefies and dissolves under heat or fire. In every biblical occurrence, the word functions as a comparative term (a simile) to illustrate melting, dissolving, or vanishing. It speaks of a rapid transition from a state of strength and stability to one of complete weakness and formlessness.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The word dônag appears only four times in the Hebrew Bible, always carrying a vivid physical-to-spiritual word picture:

• The melting of creation: It describes the physical world dissolving before the presence of the Lord. Mountains, which represent the most stable and immovable structures on earth, melt like soft wax before the majesty of God.

• The destruction of the wicked: It pictures the judgment of the ungodly, who have no inherent stability or strength to withstand the presence of God. They vanish as quickly and completely as wax melts when exposed to open flame.

• The dissolving of internal strength: It describes the subjective experience of extreme suffering, where a person’s inner resolve, courage, and vitality completely dissolve, leaving them unable to stand.

4. Theological Implications

This word highlights the inherent fragility of humanity when contrasted with divine power or subjected to severe trial. It underscores the biblical truth that human strength is an illusion; when exposed to the heat of affliction or the holiness of God, human resolve liquefies.

Theologically, because Psalm 22 is a premier Messianic psalm, this word provides deep insight into the substitutionary suffering of Jesus Christ. It reveals that the Savior did not suffer with stoic insensitivity. Rather, He experienced the full weight of physical, emotional, and spiritual exhaustion. His human will and emotional strength were subjected to the absolute limit, experiencing the agonizing "melting" of His inner being under the heat of divine judgment for sin.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 22:14, the psalmist describes a state of total systemic collapse using three vivid metaphors:

• "I am poured out like water" (loss of physical stability and cohesion).

• "All my bones are out of joint" (loss of structural framework and support).

• "My heart is like wax; It has melted within me" (loss of internal courage, life, and resolve).

The "heart" (lēb) in Hebrew thought is the seat of the intellect, will, emotions, and courage. To say the heart has become "like wax" and "melted within me" means the very center of the sufferer’s life force and courage has liquefied. Under the burning heat of physical agony and spiritual abandonment, the sufferer’s internal fortitude has completely dissolved, leaving him utterly helpless and unable to endure on his own.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 68:2 - "As smoke is driven away, So drive them away; As wax melts before the fire, So let the wicked perish at the presence of God."

• Psalm 97:5 - "The hills melt like wax at the presence of the LORD, At the presence of the Lord of the whole earth."

• Micah 1:4 - "The mountains will melt under Him, And the valleys will split Like wax before the fire, Like waters poured down a steep place."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In physical crucifixion, the body undergoes extreme dehydration, rapid heart failure, and severe traumatic shock. The metaphor of the heart melting like wax within the chest cavity correlates with the physical sensation of pericardial effusion (the accumulation of fluid around the heart) and the overwhelming feeling of internal dissolution that accompanies slow suffocation and circulatory collapse.
