WATER - ( mayim , Strong's H4325 )

Summary:

In Psalm 22:14, the word "water" (mayim) describes a state of utter physical, emotional, and spiritual exhaustion. To be "poured out like water" is a vivid metaphor for losing all strength, stability, and vital life force. In the arid climate of the ancient Near East, water was essential for life, but once spilled onto the dry ground, it was lost forever, unable to be gathered up again. In this messianic context, the term represents absolute helplessness, vulnerability, and the draining of the sufferer’s life, foreshadowing the profound physical and spiritual emptying of Jesus Christ during His crucifixion.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew word mayim is a dual noun, occurring exclusively in the plural form. In the ancient Near East, water was highly prized as the primary source of life, cleansing, and sustenance. However, because of its fluid and formless nature, water also served as a vivid picture of instability and weakness when poured out. Spilled water cannot hold its shape; it spreads across the dirt, sinks into the dust, and quickly evaporates. To be "poured out like water" is to have one's inner strength, resolve, and physical frame completely collapse under intense pressure, leaving the individual entirely helpless and unable to stand.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun mayim primarily refers to physical water, whether from springs, rain, seas, wells, or tears. In figurative biblical contexts, it carries several distinct nuances depending on the setting:

• Instability and weakness: Describing a person or nation whose courage has completely dissolved.

• Overwhelming trouble: Depicting chaotic or deep waters that threaten to drown the sufferer.

• Abundance and blessing: Representing the refreshing presence of God's Spirit or the satisfaction of spiritual thirst.

• In the specific context of Psalm 22:14, mayim is used in a comparative simile ("like water") to denote the absolute loss of physical and structural integrity, symbolizing a body and soul drained of all vital force.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Loss of Courage: When the hearts of the Israelites melted after the defeat at Ai, they "became like water" (Joshua 7:5). The solid resolve of the nation turned into a fluid, trembling state of fear.

• Spilled Life: Abigail's wise woman of Tekoa described human mortality by stating we "are like water spilled on the ground, which cannot be gathered up again" (2 Samuel 14:14). This highlights the irreversible nature of physical death and human frailty.

• The Sacrificial Outpouring: In times of deep repentance, Israel poured out water before the Lord (1 Samuel 7:6) as a physical representation of pouring out their very souls in humility and brokenness.

4. Theological Implications

Psalm 22 is the preeminent messianic psalm of the crucifixion, quoted directly by Jesus on the cross. The description of being "poured out like water" carries profound Christological weight:

• The Physical Reality of Crucifixion: The phrase points to the extreme dehydration, loss of blood volume, and cardiovascular collapse associated with crucifixion. It reflects the moment when Jesus' physical strength was entirely spent.

• The Kenosis (Emptying): This passage provides an Old Testament picture of Christ's self-emptying. He did not holding onto His lives or rights, but poured Himself out to the point of death to secure salvation for His people.

• The Flow of Blood and Water: It anticipates the literal piercing of Christ's side, from which flowed blood and water (John 19:34), proving that His life had been fully poured out as a sacrificial offering.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 22:14, "poured out like water" is paired with two parallel phrases: "all my bones are out of joint" and "my heart is like wax; it has melted within me." This poetic parallelism emphasizes the complete breakdown of the sufferer's physical and psychological frame:

• Bones represent the skeletal structure that allows a person to stand upright with dignity and strength.

• The Heart represents the center of courage, will, and emotional stability.

• Water represents the fluid, helpless state of the body when both structure (bones) and internal courage (heart) have completely failed. The sufferer is unable to resist the gravity of his suffering.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Joshua 7:5 - "therefore the hearts of the people melted and became like water."

• 2 Samuel 14:14 - "For we must by all means die, and are like water spilled on the ground, which cannot be gathered up again."

• Psalm 22:14 - "I am poured out like water, And all my bones are out of joint; My heart is like wax; It has melted within me."

• Psalm 69:1 - "Save me, O God! For the waters have come up to my neck."

7. Other Relevant Insights

For the pastor and teacher, this word study highlights the profound vulnerability Jesus chose to experience. He did not face the cross with a stoic, unfeeling rigidity. Instead, He allowed Himself to be genuinely "poured out" in utter weakness. In counseling and preaching, this demonstrates that Christ deeply understands the experience of those who feel they have nothing left to give, whose strength is completely dried up, and who feel as though they are dissolving under the weight of trial.
