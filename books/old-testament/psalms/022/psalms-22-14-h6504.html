OUT OF JOINT - ( parad , Strong's H6504 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb parad primarily means to divide, separate, or scatter. In Psalm 22:14, it is used in the Hithpael (reflexive) stem to describe a state of physical dissolution where the bones of the sufferer are pulled apart or dislocated. This graphic imagery portrays the ultimate loss of physical strength, structural integrity, and emotional stability, functioning as a profound prophetic description of the Messiah's suffering on the cross.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root meaning of parad relates to the act of dividing or parting things that are naturally joined together. In the ancient agrarian world, it described physical separations, such as rivers branching out in different directions, paths diverging, or herdsmen parting ways to pasture their flocks in different regions.

In the reflexive Hithpael stem used in Psalm 22:14 (hitpar'du), the separation occurs internally or to oneself. The word picture is one of complete disintegration from within. Instead of a strong, unified skeletal frame holding the body upright, the bones act as though they have lost their connective tissues, leaving the body in a collapsed, structurally ruined state.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb parad means to separate, divide, or disperse. Depending on the grammatical stem, its nuances include:

• Qal / Niphal: To divide, separate oneself, or be separated from others (as with people or nations parting).

• Piel / Hiphil: To cause a separation, make a division, or sever a relationship.

• Hithpael: To be divided, separated, disjoined, or scattered.

In Psalm 22:14, the passive/reflexive Hithpael describes the bones undergoing a painful separation or dislocation. The skeletal frame—the very emblem of physical strength and human posture—is rendered completely dysfunctional, separated at the joints.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, parad describes various types of separation:

• Geographical and Relational Separation: It depicts Abraham and Lot parting company to avoid strife, where the physical separation represents a division of families and destinies.

• Agricultural and Natural Dispersion: It describes the scattering of seeds or the parting of waters.

• Military and Societal Scattering: It portrays the scattering of enemies or wicked doers under divine judgment, leaving them isolated and vulnerable.

• Physical Disintegration: In Job and the Psalms, it is used metaphorically for bones or bodily strength being utterly loosened, representing a terrifying loss of vitality and control.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of parad in Psalm 22:14 is deeply tied to the suffering of the Messiah:

• The Physical Reality of the Crucifixion: Written centuries before crucifixion was practiced, this psalm details the physiological consequences of hanging from a cross. The constant downward pull of gravity on a suspended body causes the joints of the shoulders, elbows, and wrists to stretch, subluxate, and eventually dislocate.

• The Loss of All Human Strength: In Hebrew anthropology, bones represent the ultimate physical power, health, and support of a person. For the bones to be "out of joint" means the sufferer is deprived of all self-reliance and strength, forced to rely entirely upon God for sustenance.

• Spiritual Dereliction: The physical separation of the bones mirrors the deep spiritual separation felt by the sufferer. Just as the bones are disjointed from one another, the sufferer feels separated from the presence and favor of God ("My God, my God, why have You forsaken me?").

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 22:14, the phrase "all my bones are out of joint" is part of a tri-fold description of absolute human collapse:

1. "I am poured out like water" (loss of form, stability, and control).

2. "All my bones are out of joint" (loss of skeletal structure, dislocation, and physical frame).

3. "My heart is like wax; it has melted within me" (loss of inner courage, life-force, and emotional resolve).

The context is one of intense physical torture combined with deep spiritual agony. The dislocation of the bones represents a state where the sufferer can no longer stand up, resist his attackers, or hold himself together.

7. Other Relevant Insights

A striking theological contrast exists between the disjointing of Christ’s bones and the breaking of His bones. While Psalm 22:14 prophesies that His bones would be stretched and pulled "out of joint" (dislocated) by the physical trauma of the crucifixion, Psalm 34:20 promises, "He guards all his bones; not one of them is broken." This was fulfilled precisely during the crucifixion when the Roman soldiers broke the legs of the two thieves to hasten death but spared Jesus’ legs because He was already dead (John 19:32-36). The skeletal structure was dislocated (parad), but never broken.
