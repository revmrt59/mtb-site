CLINGS - ( dabaq , Strong's H1692 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb dabaq conveys the idea of sticking, cleaving, or being tightly joined to something so that separation becomes nearly impossible. In Psalm 22:15, it describes extreme physical exhaustion and dehydration, where the psalmist's tongue clings to the roof of his mouth. Beyond this picture of severe distress, the word powerfully illustrates relational loyalty, marital union, and the unwavering devotion of a believer clinging to God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root idea of dabaq is gluing, soldering, or welding two separate materials together until they function as one.

- In ancient nomadic and agricultural life, it described the way wet clay adheres to a potter's wheel, or how armor plates overlap and lock together in battle.

- In Psalm 22:15, the metaphor shifts to an agonizing physical reality: the severe dehydration of extreme suffering causes the tongue to stick to the dry palate, rendering speech impossible.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- To cleave, cling, stick, or stay close.

- To pursue hard after or overtake someone.

- To be joined, united, or fastened firmly to another person or object.

- The semantic range spans from grim physical adhesion (such as skin clinging to bones) to deep emotional and spiritual commitment (such as a soul clinging to God).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- Physical Adhesion: Used of skin sticking to bones during emaciation (Job 19:20) and the tongue sticking to the roof of the mouth in thirst (Psalm 22:15, Lamentations 4:4).

- Relational Devotion: Used of Ruth refusing to leave Naomi, declaring that she will "cling" to her (Ruth 1:14).

- Marital Union: Used in Genesis 2:24 of a man leaving his parents and "cleaving" to his wife.

- Spiritual Loyalty: Used of Israel commanded to "fear the Lord your God and serve Him, and to hold fast [cling] to Him" (Deuteronomy 10:20).

4. Theological Implications

- In Psalm 22, the word underscores the brutal physical cost of suffering, pointing forward typologically to the crucifixion of Christ, who cried out in thirst.

- Theologically, dabaq highlights the intimate, unbreakable bond God desires with His people. Just as physical weakness forces total dependence, spiritual maturity involves cleaving exclusively to the Lord.

- It teaches that steadfast love is not merely an emotion, but a binding commitment that refuses to let go.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- Psalm 22 is a Messianic psalm of profound lament and eventual vindication.

- In verse 15, the psalmist says, "My strength is dried up like a potsherd, and my tongue clings to my jaws; You have brought me to the dust of death."

- Here, dabaq emphasizes absolute helplessness. The sufferer has reached the end of human endurance, stripped of all moisture, strength, and ability to defend himself, anticipating the physical agony endured by the Savior on the cross.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Genesis 2:24: "Therefore a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined [dabaq] to his wife, and they shall become one flesh."

- Ruth 1:14: "Then they wept aloud again; and Orpah kissed her mother-in-law, but Ruth clung [dabaq] to her."

- Deuteronomy 10:20: "You shall fear the Lord your God; you shall serve Him, and to Him you shall hold fast [dabaq], and take oaths in His name."

- Job 19:20: "My bone clings [dabaq] to my skin and to my flesh, and I have escaped by the skin of my teeth."

- Psalm 63:8: "My soul follows close [dabaq] behind You; Your right hand upholds me."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The Septuagint (LXX) often translates dabaq using words like kollao (to glue or join closely) or proskollao, reinforcing the imagery of an inseparable union.

- In prophetic literature, God uses this term to describe how He intended Israel to cling to Him just as a loincloth clings to the waist of a man, symbolizing intimacy and usefulness (Jeremiah 13:11).
