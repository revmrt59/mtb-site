STRENGTH - ( koach , Strong's H3581 )

Summary:

In Psalm 22:15, the word koach describes the complete draining of physical and vital force, portraying a state of utter human exhaustion where even the fundamental capability to endure is gone. This term captures both bodily vigor and the inner resolve required to sustain life, highlighting the profound weakness and vulnerability experienced by the sufferer.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root idea of koach is physical might, power, or capability, often likened to the strength found in the muscles of a human or the brute force of an animal.

• In ancient agrarian and pastoral cultures, this word frequently referred to the working capacity of beasts of burden or the productive yield of the earth.

• When applied to humanity, it represents the biological and functional capacity to labor, stand, and resist physical collapse.

• In the context of poetic literature, it vividly paints the picture of a strong pillar cracking under unbearable weight until no structural integrity remains.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Refers primarily to strength, power, might, or vigor, whether physical, mental, or material.

• Encompasses the underlying reserve of energy that enables a person to act, endure, or produce.

• Denotes capability, effectiveness, and the vital force necessary for sustaining life and activity.

• Can occasionally extend to wealth or substance, viewed as the output of one's labor and capability.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Described metaphorically as something that can be "dried up" or exhausted by extreme distress, illness, or sorrow.

• Often contrasted with human frailty, emphasizing that mortal vigor is fleeting and entirely dependent on the Creator.

• Used in various contexts to denote the raw power of God, the vitality of youth, or the failing capacity of the elderly and sick.

4. Theological Implications

• Human koach is ultimately fragile and finite, pointing to the absolute necessity of divine sustenance for survival.

• The complete loss of physical strength in prophetic passages like Psalm 22 mirrors the substitutionary suffering of the Messiah, who bore our weaknesses.

• True spiritual endurance does not rely on human capability, for God gives power to the faint and increases koach to those who have no might.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 22:15, the psalmist declares, "My strength is dried up like a potsherd, and my tongue clings to my jaws."

• Here, koach signifies the total evaporation of bodily moisture and vital energy, leaving the sufferer parched, brittle, and lifeless like a broken piece of baked clay.

• The nuance is one of absolute depletion and agonizing helplessness, setting the stage for a desperate cry for divine deliverance.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Job 6:11 - "What is my strength, that I should hope? And what is my end, that I should prolong my life?"

• Isaiah 40:29 - "He gives power to the weak, and to those who have no might He increases strength."

• Jeremiah 10:12 - "He has made the earth by His power, He has established the world by His wisdom, and has stretched out the heavens at His discretion."

• Daniel 10:8 - "Therefore I was left alone when I saw this great vision, and no strength remained in me; for my vigor was turned to frailty in me, and I retained no strength."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The drying up of strength like a potsherd (baked pottery) vividly illustrates dehydration and fever, where internal fluids and vigor are entirely consumed by suffering.

• This specific term bridges the gap between physical health and existential despair, showing how deeply bodily trauma affects the whole person.

• Recognizing the absolute failure of koach is the necessary turning point in the psalm where human self-reliance ends and divine rescue becomes the sole focus.
