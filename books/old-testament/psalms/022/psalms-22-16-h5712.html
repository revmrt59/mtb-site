CONGREGATION - ( edah , Strong's H5712 )

Summary:

The Hebrew term edah primarily denotes a designated gathering, assembly, or company of people, frequently used to describe the community of Israel united by covenant. In Psalms 22:16, it refers to the hostile assembly of evildoers surrounding the suffering Messiah, highlighting the corporate and public nature of His rejection and crucifixion.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• Root derivation points to the idea of appointing, meeting together, or fixing a designated time and place for an assembly.

• Originally used in the Pentateuch to describe the entire congregation of the children of Israel, gathered together as a corporate legal and religious body before God.

• Over time, the word expanded to describe any distinct gathering of people, whether righteous or wicked, organized for a specific collective purpose.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Edah signifies a company, assembly, congregation, or swarm.

• It emphasizes a designated group brought together by common identity, purpose, or circumstance.

• In legal and covenantal contexts, it represents the community acting as a collective whole.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The assembly of Israel gathered at the wilderness tabernacle or Sinai.

• A body of leaders or elders making judicial decisions for the nation.

• In darker contexts, it describes a band, mob, or conspiracy of wicked individuals gathered to oppose God or His anointed.

4. Theological Implications

• Corporate Solidarity: The presence of the edah in Psalm 22 underscores that Christ's suffering was not merely a private tragedy, but a public, corporate rejection by the established human community.

• Covenantal Reversal: The community that once constituted God's holy assembly is depicted here standing against the holy Substitute, foreshadowing the trial before the Jewish council and the crowd shouting for crucifixion.

• Substitutionary Isolation: The Messiah stands utterly alone surrounded by a hostile edah, bearing the alienation so that believers might be gathered into the true, redeemed assembly of God (Hebrews 2:12).

5. Contextual Nuance in "Pessaim 22:16"

• "For dogs have surrounded Me; the assembly [edah] of the wicked has enclosed Me. They pierced My hands and My feet."

• Here, the word does not describe the reverent people of God, but a pack-like coalition of adversaries closing in for the kill.

• The term emphasizes the organized, collective hostility directed against the innocent sufferer, pointing prophetically to the Sanhedrin, Roman soldiers, and jeering crowd at Calvary.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 12:3 - "Speak to all the congregation [edah] of Israel, saying: 'On the tenth day of this month every man shall take for himself a lamb...'"

• Numbers 14:5 - "Then Moses and Aaron fell on their faces before all the assembly of the congregation [edah] of the children of Israel."

• Psalm 1:5 - "Therefore the ungodly shall not stand in the judgment, nor sinners in the congregation [edah] of the righteous."

• Psalm 74:2 - "Remember Your congregation [edah], which You have purchased of old, the tribe of Your inheritance, which You have redeemed..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• While edah emphasizes the objective grouping or gathering of people, a related word, qahal, often emphasizes the act of calling them together for a religious purpose. However, in many poetic and historical texts, the two terms are used fluidly.

• The transition of Psalm 22 from the hostile edah in verse 16 to the praising congregation in verse 22 ("In the midst of the assembly [qahal] I will praise You") marks the turning point from crucifixion to resurrection victory.
