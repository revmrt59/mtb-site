DIVIDE - ( chalaq , Strong's H2505 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb chalaq means to divide, share, distribute, or apportion. In its literal sense, it refers to the physical act of splitting property, land, or spoil among multiple parties. In the prophetic and deeply personal context of Psalm 22:18, chalaq describes the callous, systematic distribution of a dying man's garments by his hostile adversaries. This action signifies complete exposure, public humiliation, and the premature claiming of his possessions as if he were already dead. This specific act was fulfilled with literal, historical precision during the crucifixion of Jesus Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root chalaq fundamentally conveys the idea of smoothness, which historically connects to the ancient practice of casting smooth stones or pebbles (lots) to divide property, land, or inheritances. From this concrete action of casting smooth stones, the word expanded to denote the actual distribution or apportionment of land, spoils of war, or inherited goods. It paints a picture of dividing a whole into distinct, measured portions so that each participant receives a share. In ancient Near Eastern culture, dividing an individual's personal garments while they were still alive or dying was a highly degrading act, symbolizing the total stripping away of their dignity, legal standing, and personal identity.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

In the Qal stem, chalaq means to share, divide, or receive a portion. In the Pi'el stem (the intensive form used in Psalm 22:18), the verb means to distribute widely, scatter, or divide up thoroughly among a group. The semantic range of chalaq includes:

• Distributing land or inheritance among heirs.

• Apportioning plunder or spoils of war among victors.

• Dividing duties or classes of people, such as organizing the Levitical priesthood into divisions.

• The closely related noun form, cheleq, refers to the specific portion, share, or territory allocated to someone.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Spoils of War: A frequent imagery of chalaq is victorious soldiers dividing the plunder of a defeated enemy. This highlights the absolute defeat of the victim; they are powerless to stop the victors from taking everything they own.

• Inheritance: It is used of dividing the Promised Land among the tribes of Israel, transforming the act of division into a sign of covenant fulfillment and divine provision.

• Smoothness: A related homonymic root chalaq means to be smooth or slippery, often used metaphorically for flattering, deceitful speech. While conceptually distinct, the smooth stones used for casting lots bridge the semantic development of these Hebrew roots.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, the act of dividing garments in Psalm 22:18 underscores the depths of the Messiah's humiliation. In the Old Testament, garments represented a person's dignity, status, and social identity. To be stripped and have one’s clothing divided is to be reduced to absolute destitution, treated not as a human being with rights, but as a carcass to be plundered. This prophetic picture directly anticipates the crucifixion, where Christ was stripped of everything earthly, demonstrating His voluntary self-emptying for the redemption of humanity. It shows that God's sovereign plan accounts for even the smallest, most cruel details of human malice, turning them into undeniable proofs of messianic fulfillment.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 22:18, David writes, "They divide (chalaq) my garments among them." The verb is in the Pi'el imperfect, suggesting an active, ongoing, or intensive process of distribution. The enemies do not merely take the garments; they systematically partition them. The parallel line, "And for my clothing they cast lots," clarifies the method of division. The Roman soldiers at the cross of Jesus fulfilled this precisely. They divided His outer garments into four parts (one for each soldier), but because His tunic was seamless, they decided not to tear (divide) it, but instead cast lots for it. The Hebrew parallel structure in Psalm 22:18 beautifully captures both aspects of this historical event: the literal dividing of the garments and the casting of lots for the unique clothing item.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 49:27 - "Benjamin is a ravenous wolf; In the morning he shall devour the prey, And at night he shall divide the spoil."

• Joshua 18:10 - "Then Joshua cast lots for them in Shiloh before the LORD, and there Joshua divided the land to the children of Israel according to their divisions."

• 1 Chronicles 24:3 - "With the help of Zadok of the sons of Eleazar, and Ahimelech of the sons of Ithamar, David divided them according to the systematic duties of their service."

• Isaiah 53:12 - "Therefore I will divide Him a portion with the great, And He shall divide the spoil with the strong, Because He poured out His soul unto death, And He was numbered with the transgressors..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Greek Septuagint (LXX) translates chalaq in Psalm 22:18 (Psalm 21:19 in the LXX numbering) using the verb diamerizo, which means to divide up, distribute, or partition. This exact Greek verb is used by the Gospel writers in describing the Roman soldiers dividing Jesus' garments at the foot of the cross. This linguistic link across both testaments provides an airtight hermeneutical bridge, demonstrating that the New Testament writers did not merely see a vague thematic connection, but a literal, word-for-word fulfillment of the Old Testament scriptures.
