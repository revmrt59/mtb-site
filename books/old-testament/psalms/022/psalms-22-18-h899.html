GARMENTS - ( beged , Strong's H899 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word beged primarily refers to a covering, cloak, or garment, but in biblical usage, it also frequently carries the secondary, moral connotation of treachery or unfaithfulness. In Psalm 22:18, it points specifically to the outer clothing stripped from the suffering petitioner. Prophetically, this mundane act of cruelty reaches its ultimate historical fulfillment at the cross of Jesus Christ, where Roman soldiers divided His outer garments and cast lots for His tunic, marking the profound humiliation and total stripping of the Messiah.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root underlying beged suggests a cover or concealment, representing the outer layer worn by an individual in the ancient Near East.

- In ancient culture, a person's outer garment was a vital possession, often serving as a blanket at night or a primary marker of social standing.

- To strip someone of their garments was an act of supreme degradation, vulnerability, and domination, reducing the victim to absolute helplessness.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Beged encompasses clothing, apparel, raiment, or vestments of various kinds.

- It frequently denotes outer garments that are visible to others, as distinguished from inner clothing.

- Beyond the physical object, the same consonantal root and related noun forms are widely used metaphorically in the Old Testament for treachery, faithlessness, or dealing deceitfully (e.g., treacherous dealings).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- Physical Raiment: It is used for everyday clothing, royal robes, priestly vestments, and festive attire.

- Metaphorical Coverings: It frequently illustrates spiritual realities, such as the garments of salvation or the filthy rags of human righteousness.

- The concept of clothing in Scripture often symbolizes character, status, or positional standing before God and man.

4. Theological Implications

- Stripping of Garments: In prophetic texts like Psalm 22, the removal of garments portrays the stripping away of human dignity and the exposure of the righteous sufferer to public shame.

- The Reversal of Shame: While humanity stripped Christ of His physical garments, God provides the "garments of salvation" and the "robe of righteousness" to cover the spiritual nakedness of sinners.

- Substitutionary Suffering: The literal division of Jesus' garments at the crucifixion underscores the historical accuracy of Psalm 22 and highlights the humiliation endured by the Savior in our place.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 22:18, the psalmist cries out in deep distress, describing how his enemies look upon him and divide his garments (beged) among themselves, casting lots for his clothing.

- The context is one of extreme abandonment, mockery, and the final stages of persecution where the victim is treated as already dead or discarded.

- Applied to the Messiah, this verse transitions from Davidic or corporate lament to precise prophetic foreshadowing, perfectly enacted by the Roman guards at Golgotha.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Genesis 39:12 - "And she caught him by his garment (beged), saying, 'Lie with me.' But he left his garment in her hand, and fled and ran outside."

- Joshua 7:21 - "When I saw among the spoils a goodly Babylonian mantle... then I coveted them and took them..." (Note: rendered as mantle/garment contextually).

- Isaiah 61:10 - "I will greatly rejoice in the Lord, my soul shall be joyful in my God; for He has clothed me with the garments (beged) of salvation, He has covered me with the robe of righteousness..."

- Zechariah 3:4 - "Then He answered and spoke to those who stood before Him, saying, 'Take away the filthy garments from him.' And to him He said, 'See, I have removed your iniquity from you, and I will clothe you with rich robes.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The vivid imagery of dividing garments highlights the callousness of the persecutors, who view the sufferer's misery merely as an opportunity for personal gain.

- In the New Testament Gospels, John explicitly notes the fulfillment of Psalm 22:18 when the soldiers divided Christ's garments into four parts and cast lots for His seamless tunic (John 19:23-24).
