SILENT - ( dum , Strong's H1747 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun dum describes an absolute state of stillness, speechlessness, or silence, often representing the heavy, agonizing quietness of unanswered prayer or unexpressed grief. In Psalm 22:2, it captures the raw human experience of divine hiddenness, where the sufferer cries out to God by day and night, yet receives no immediate verbal reply, plunging the soul into a profound and unsettling silence.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root word is connected to the ideas of being still, dumb, or motionless.

- In ancient Near Eastern laments, the silence of God was often terrifying, as it felt like abandonment by the divine patron or king.

- Culturally, speech implies relationship, attention, and intervention; silence implies distance, unresponsiveness, or judgment.

- In the context of Psalm 22—a Messianic psalm of intense suffering—the silence becomes a canvas upon which the agony of the cross and the weight of separation are painted.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Dum primarily denotes stillness, silence, or a state of being speechless.

- It can function as a substantive meaning "silence" or an adverbial state of being "silent" or "kept in silence."

- It stands in direct contrast to speech, vocalization, or the answering voice of God.

- It highlights an absence of sound, specifically the absence of a comforting or delivering word from heaven.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The term paints a picture of a man holding his breath, waiting for a word that does not come.

- It conveys both physical quietness and the emotional exhaustion of crying out until no more words are left.

- It suggests a helpless suspension of movement and sound in the face of overwhelming distress.

4. Theological Implications

- The silence of God is one of the deepest trials of faith. It tests whether the believer trusts the character of God when His voice is withheld.

- Even in His silence, God is the ultimate audience to whom the silent lament is directed, maintaining the relationship despite the lack of reciprocal sound.

- This word points forward to the ultimate silent endurance of Christ, who did not open His mouth against His accusers and experienced the ultimate earthly silence of God during the crucifixion.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 22:2, David—and prophetically Christ—cries out, "O my God, I cry in the daytime, but You do not hear; and in the night season, and am not silent." (Note: The NKJV translates the final phrase with the negative/state, reflecting the unceasing nature of the cry or the inability to find rest).

- The dum here emphasizes the agonizing paradox: the sufferer speaks unceasingly, but the heavens remain wrapped in absolute, unbroken stillness.

- It underscores the profound human feeling of being forsaken, which forms the necessary backdrop for the eventual triumphant praise at the end of the psalm.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Psalm 39:2 - "I was mute with silence (dum), I held my peace even from good; and my sorrow was stirred up."

- Psalm 62:1 - "Truly my soul silently (dum) waits for God; from Him comes my salvation." (Using the related adverbial form of trustful stillness).

- Habakkuk 2:20 - "But the Lord is in His holy temple. Let all the earth keep silence (dum) before Him."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The transition from the painful silence of Psalm 22:2 to the resounding praise of Psalm 22:22-25 demonstrates that God's temporary silence is never permanent abandonment.

- Pastors often use this concept to minister to those experiencing spiritual dryness or unanswered prayer, assuring them that crying out into the silence is a valid expression of biblical faith.
