CRY - ( qara', Strong's H7121 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb qara' in Psalm 22:2 represents a desperate, vocal plea for help addressed to God in the darkest hour of suffering. Far from a passive or quiet meditation, it denotes calling out loudly and urgently, expecting an attentive response from a sovereign covenant partner.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root verb qara' fundamentally carries the physical idea of calling out, shouting, or proclaiming with the human voice.

• In ancient Hebrew culture, calling out to someone implied acknowledging their presence, authority, and ability to intervene in a crisis where the caller was entirely helpless.

• It often describes the vocalization of deep distress, such as a person trapped, wounded, or overwhelmed by enemies, turning their face toward the heavens for rescue.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Qara' means to call, cry, call out, proclaim, or summon.

• It encompasses both verbal utterance (making a sound with the voice) and relational summons (calling upon a person by name to draw near).

• When directed toward God, it functions as a prayer of petition, a desperate appeal rooted in covenant relationship.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The word can describe a herald making an official proclamation to a crowd.

• It frequently depicts a worshipper or sufferer lifting their voice in prayer, refusing to remain silent under affliction.

• It evokes the mental image of a child shouting for a protective father when danger suddenly appears.

4. Theological Implications

• Crying out to God in Scripture assumes that God hears, cares, and has the power to act on behalf of the suffering.

• This word bridges human weakness and divine sovereignty; the mortal voice reaches upward, initiating divine engagement.

• It forms the bedrock of biblical lament—believers do not hide their pain from God, but voice it honestly and intensely to Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 22:2, the psalmist writes, "O my God, I cry in the daytime, but You do not hear; and in the night season, and am not silent."

• Here, qara' expresses the agonizing persistence of unanswered prayer during prolonged suffering.

• The continuous crying highlights the deep emotional and physical exhaustion of the speaker, who maintains a posture of appeal even when heaven feels silent.

• This specific verse finds its ultimate fulfillment on the cross, where Jesus echoes the opening of this psalm, crying out to the Father during His absolute abandonment for our sins.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 34:6 - "This poor man cried out, and the Lord heard him, and saved him out of all his troubles."

• Psalm 55:16 - "As for me, I will call upon God, and the Lord shall save me."

• Jeremiah 33:3 - "'Call to Me, and I will answer you, and show you great and mighty things, which you do not know.'"

• Joel 2:32 - "And it shall come to pass that whoever calls on the name of the Lord shall be saved."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• Qara' is used over 700 times in the Hebrew Bible, demonstrating its central place in biblical vocabulary regarding prayer and human-divine interaction.

• The intensity of qara' underscores that biblical faith is deeply expressive, vocal, and honest about human suffering rather than stoic or detached.
