SWORD - ( chereb , Strong's H2719 )

Summary:

The Hebrew term chereb primarily denotes a sword, dagger, or cutting weapon of warfare and execution, symbolizing violent threat, divine judgment, and severe distress. In Psalms 22:20, the psalmist uses this imagery to depict the lethal, terrifying hostility of wicked adversaries closing in for destruction, driving the petitioner to cry out for divine rescue.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root behind chereb relates to drought, dryness, or a cutting action, picturing an instrument that lays waste or consumes life like a drought upon the land.

- In the ancient Near East, the sword was the quintessential weapon of close combat, representing finality, power, and inescapable danger.

- When applied metaphorically or literally in the Psalms, it vividly captures the terror of a violent, premature death at the hands of merciless enemies.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Chereb refers to a sword, dagger, knife, or axe used for cutting, slaying, and warfare.

- It frequently serves as a metonymy for war, bloodshed, violence, and judicial punishment.

- In existential contexts, it describes the imminent threat of mortal danger and destruction facing the righteous sufferer.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The word is often paired with verbs of sharpness or drawing, emphasizing the readiness of the adversary to strike.

- It can represent the devastating consequences of human rebellion or divine judgment, but in lament literature, it highlights the vulnerability of the innocent under attack.

- The imagery shifts seamlessly from a literal bronze or iron weapon in the hand of a soldier to an all-encompassing picture of deadly peril.

4. Theological Implications

- The threat of the chereb underscores the reality of human malice and the fallen condition of a world where the strong prey upon the weak.

- It magnifies the desperate dependence of the believer upon God as the ultimate protector and deliverer when human shields fail.

- It points forward to the ultimate deliverance from spiritual enemies, wherein God alone rescues the soul from the power of darkness.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 22:20 ("Deliver My life from the sword, My precious life from the power of the dog"), the sword represents the lethal, tearing violence of the psalmist's tormentors.

- Surrounded by hostile mockers likened to wild animals (bulls, lions, dogs), the psalmist feels the cold steel of imminent execution pressed against his life.

- The plea is an urgent appeal for God to intervene precisely at the point of greatest vulnerability, rescuing the "only one" or "precious life" from final destruction.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Genesis 3:24: "So He drove out the man; and He placed cherubim at the east of the garden of Eden, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to guard the way to the tree of life."

- Exodus 15:9: "The enemy said, 'I will pursue, I will overtake, I will divide the spoil; my desire shall be satisfied on them; I will draw my sword, my hand shall destroy them.'"

- Psalm 17:13: "Arise, O Lord, confront him, cast him down; deliver my life from the wicked with Your sword."

- Ephesians 6:17 (LXX/NT conceptual parallel): "And take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The transition from physical weapons in the Old Testament to spiritual armor in the New Testament retains the concept of the sword as an instrument of decisive power, whether for destruction or spiritual defense.

- In Psalm 22, the movement from the cry of abandonment to eventual praise demonstrates that God answers the plea to deliver the soul from the sword.
