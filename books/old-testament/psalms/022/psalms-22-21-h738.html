LION - ( aryeh , Strong's H738 )

Summary:

The Hebrew term aryeh refers to the king of beasts, symbolizing formidable strength, predatory danger, and terrifying power. In the context of Psalm 22:21, the lion serves as a vivid metaphor for the violent, merciless hostility of the psalmist's enemies and the terrifying abyss of suffering, which ultimately yields to miraculous divine deliverance.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- Aryeh derives from a root associated with plucking, tearing, or collecting prey, vividly portraying the destructive behavior of a mature predator.

- In the ancient Near East, lions roamed the wilderness and scrublands of Palestine, presenting a constant, lethal threat to shepherds, travelers, and livestock.

- The animal's immense physical strength, stealthy approach, and razor-sharp teeth made it the ultimate emblem of ruthless, inescapable danger.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Aryeh primarily denotes a lion, whether young or fully grown, male or female, depending on contextual modifiers.

- Semantically, the word frequently functions as a metaphor for fierce adversaries, cruel rulers, or impending destruction.

- It captures both the majestic ferocity of the beast and the helpless terror experienced by its intended prey.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The term is frequently used in the Old Testament to describe the relentless attacks of wicked men who lie in wait to devour the righteous.

- It also paints a picture of divine judgment, where God Himself acts with the terrifying power of a lion against disobedient nations or individuals.

- In triumphant contexts, the imagery shifts to security and victory, contrasting the destructive power of earthly enemies with the sovereign protection of the Lord.

4. Theological Implications

- The lion represents the terrifying reality of sin, death, and human hostility at their most brutal extremes, threatening to overwhelm the believer.

- In messianic passages like Psalm 22, the imagery points directly to the profound agony and cruel mockery faced by the Suffering Servant during His crucifixion.

- The ultimate theological resolution of this terrifying metaphor is found not in the strength of the prey, but in the intervening grace and salvation of God, who rescues the helpless from the jaws of death.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 22:21, David cries out from the depths of abandonment and physical torture: "Save me from the lion's mouth; / For You have answered me from the horns of the unicorns [wild oxen]."

- Here, the lion represents the vicious, closing jaws of executioners and mockers who surround the sufferer like a pack of beasts ready to tear him apart.

- The plea acknowledges total human helplessness and casts the soul entirely upon God's mercy, shifting abruptly from despair to confident praise upon divine rescue.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Judges 14:14: "So he said to them: 'Out of the eater came something to eat, / And out of the strong came something sweet.' Now for three days they could not explain the riddle."

- Psalm 17:12: "He is like a lion that is eager to tear his prey, / And like a young lion lurking in secret places."

- Proverbs 28:1: "The wicked flee when no one pursues, / But the righteous are bold as a lion."

- Isaiah 38:13: "I considered until morning, / That like a lion / He breaks all my bones; / From day until night You make an end of me."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The transition in Psalm 22:21 from the plea regarding the lion to the immediate assurance of an answer marks the psychological and spiritual turning point of the entire psalm.

- Biblical writers frequently utilized familiar elements of the Judean wilderness—such as lions and wild oxen—to ground abstract spiritual warfare in stark, physical reality.
