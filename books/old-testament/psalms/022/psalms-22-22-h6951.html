CONGREGATION - ( qahal , Strong's H6951 )

Summary:

The Hebrew term qahal refers to an assembled body of people gathered for a specific purpose, most often the religious assembly of Israel before God. In Psalm 22:22, the psalmist shifts from deep personal suffering to public praise, declaring an intention to recount God's name to his "brethren" in the midst of the great congregation. This pivotal verse bridges individual lament and corporate worship, finding ultimate fulfillment in the New Testament as Christ gathers His redeemed people to praise the Father.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• Rooted in a verb meaning to call, summon, or gather together.

• Visualizes an intentional assembly called out of their individual lives to form a cohesive, unified body.

• Frequently used in the Old Testament for the national assembly of Israel gathered at the tabernacle or temple for worship, covenant renewal, or solemn occasions.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Assembly, congregation, multitude, company.

• Denotes an organized gathering of people united by a common identity, purpose, or calling.

• Emphasizes the corporate solidarity of the people of God standing together in His presence.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Can refer to a secular gathering or a crowd of evildoers, but its predominant use is sacred and theological.

• Represents Israel functioning as the covenant community, acknowledging their dependence on Yahweh.

• Captures the transition from isolation and abandonment (as felt in the early verses of Psalm 22) to fellowship, warmth, and shared worship.

4. Theological Implications

• Salvation is never merely private; it draws the believer into a community of faith.

• God is not only the Savior of the individual soul but the focal point around which His entire people gather.

• Anticipates the New Testament reality of the church, where Christ—the speaker of Psalm 22:22—leads His brethren in the worship of God (Hebrews 2:12).

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 22:1-21, the sufferer is profoundly isolated, mocked by enemies, and seemingly forsaken by God.

• Verse 22 marks the great turning point ("I will declare Your name to My brethren; in the midst of the assembly I will praise You"), moving from the darkness of the cross to the dawn of resurrection vindication.

• The "congregation" here is the gathered family of God who will hear the testimony of God's deliverance and join in worship.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 28:3 - "May God Almighty bless you, and make you fruitful and multiply you, that you may be an assembly of peoples."

• Leviticus 4:13 - "Now if the whole congregation of Israel sins unintentionally..."

• Deuteronomy 31:30 - "Then Moses spoke in the hearing of all the assembly of Israel the words of this song until they were ended."

• Psalm 40:9 - "I have proclaimed the good news of righteousness in the great assembly; indeed, I do not restrain my lips, O Lord, You yourselves know."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Septuagint translates qahal in Psalm 22:22 with the Greek word ekklesia (assembly, church).

• Hebrews 2:12 directly applies Psalm 22:22 to Jesus Christ, identifying believers as His "brethren" whom He unashamedly leads in praise to the Father.
