FEAR - ( yare , Strong's H3373 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word yare (adjective) refers to those who are "fearing" or "reverent" toward God. In the Old Testament, it describes a profound, holy awe and deep-seated reverence that leads to worship, obedience, and turning away from evil, rather than a paralyzing terror. In Psalm 22:23, it characterizes the community of true worshipers who, having witnessed God’s deliverance of the afflicted Sufferer, are called to erupt in praise, glory, and lifelong devotion.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root yr' is the primary Old Testament term used to express the concept of fear. It encompasses a wide spectrum of meaning, from physical dread of danger, war, and death to the highest expression of religious piety.

• In the ancient Near East, the fear of pagan deities was driven by their capricious, unpredictable, and often hostile nature. In stark contrast, the biblical "fear of Yahweh" was grounded in His covenant faithfulness, holiness, justice, and mercy.

• The word picture of yare when directed toward God is not of a slave cowering before a tyrannical master, but of a child standing in breathless awe of a majestic, loving, and holy father, or a subject recognizing the absolute authority of a righteous and powerful king.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Yare is an adjective meaning "fearing," "reverent," or "afraid."

• When used as a substantive plural (yire'ey YHWH), it translates as "those who fear the LORD."

• The semantic range of this word includes:

• Reverential awe and respect directed toward God.

• Dread or terror in the face of physical danger, divine judgment, or superior power.

• The subjective attitude of piety, worship, and ethical obedience that characterizes a true covenant partner of Yahweh.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Yare is frequently used to designate a specific class of people: the faithful remnant of Israel. "Those who fear the LORD" are contrasted with the wicked who have "no fear of God before his eyes" (Psalm 36:1).

• This reverence is closely paired with practical, visible actions of worship. To fear God is to walk in His ways, love Him, serve Him, and keep His commandments (Deuteronomy 10:12).

• In wisdom literature, this attitude is the starting point for all true knowledge and moral living: "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom" (Proverbs 9:10). It is a life-giving attitude that brings security, wisdom, and rest, shielding a person from the paralyzing fear of human threats.

4. Theological Implications

• The fear of God is the Old Testament equivalent of saving faith, saving discipleship, or devotion. It is the appropriate, rational response of a finite, sinful creature to the infinite, holy, and merciful Creator.

• Holy fear and intimate love are not mutually exclusive in Hebrew theology; they are two sides of the same coin. Love without fear degenerates into presumption and cheap grace; fear without love degenerates into terror and alienation.

• True fear of God drives out all other fears. When a person fears God rightly, they no longer need to fear the wilderness, enemies, or the future.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• Psalm 22 is a deeply messianic psalm of lament and triumph. Verses 1–21 contain the agonizing cries of the suffering King, while verses 22–31 record His dramatic deliverance and the subsequent explosion of global worship.

• In verse 23, the delivered Sufferer addresses the congregation: "You who fear the LORD, praise Him!" The adjective yare here identifies the true spiritual assembly (qahal), those who recognize God’s supreme authority and holiness.

• Having witnessed the dramatic rescue of the afflicted Sufferer (v. 24), these "fearers of the LORD" are called to lead the way in public praise (halal) and glorification (kabad). Their fear of God is not a source of dread, but the engine of their corporate praise and gladness.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 22:12: "And He said, 'Do not lay your hand on the lad, or do anything to him; for now I know that you fear God, since you have not withheld your son, your only son, from Me.'"

• Psalm 15:4: "In whose eyes a vile person is despised, But he honors those who fear the LORD; He who swears to his own hurt and does not change;"

• Psalm 115:11: "You who fear the LORD, trust in the LORD; He is their help and their shield."

• Malachi 3:16: "Then those who fear the LORD spoke to one another, And the LORD listened and heard them; So a book of remembrance was written before Him For those who fear the LORD And who meditate on His name."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• Yare is contrasted with the dread of pagan nations. While pagans are terrified of celestial omens or local deities (Jeremiah 10:2), Israel is commanded to fear Yahweh alone, which uniquely liberates them from earthly anxieties.

• The New Testament equivalent of yare is often found in the Greek verb phobeomai and noun phobos. Specifically, the "God-fearers" (phoboumenoi ton theon) in the book of Acts (e.g., Acts 13:26) were Gentiles who, drawn by the ethical monotheism of Israel, worshiped the God of Abraham in reverent awe.
