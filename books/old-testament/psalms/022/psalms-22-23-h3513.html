GLORIFY - ( kabad , Strong's H3513 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb kabad fundamentally conveys weight, heaviness, and consequently, honor, glory, or abundance. In Psalm 22:23, when the psalmist calls upon those who fear the Lord to "glorify Him," the term is used in the hiphil (causative) stem, meaning to treat someone as weighty, significant, or supremely important. Rather than manufacturing glory that belongs to God, the believer's role is to recognize, honor, and publicly declare the substantial majesty and worth of God. This worship is the natural, joyful response to divine deliverance.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root of kabad carries the literal physical sense of being heavy, burdensome, or substantial (as in heavy eyes, a heavy heart, or heavy wealth).

- In ancient Hebrew culture, that which is heavy carries substance, value, and influence, whereas that which is light is trivial, worthless, or insignificant.

- To "glorify" God, therefore, is to refuse to treat Him as light or trivial; it is to acknowledge the immense, undeniable weight of His character, holiness, and acts.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Kabad means to be heavy, to be honored, to be glorious, or to make heavy.

- When applied to God, it signifies honoring, ascribing glory, or treating with the highest reverence and awe due to His supreme station and moral weight.

- It involves both an internal posture of profound respect and an external expression of praise and exaltation.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- Material Wealth: The word is sometimes used to describe heavy riches or abundance (e.g., Genesis 13:2, where Abram was very heavy/rich in livestock).

- Hardness: It can describe a hardened or stubborn heart (e.g., Exodus 8:15, where Pharaoh made his heart heavy).

- Honor and Respect: Most frequently in the religious sphere, it denotes honoring parents (Exodus 20:12) or honoring God through worship, obedience, and praise.

- When believers "glorify" God, they lift Him up as the heavyweight champion of their lives, acknowledging that nothing else compares to His substance.

4. Theological Implications

- God’s glory is intrinsic; He possesses infinite weight and majesty whether humans acknowledge it or not.

- Human glorification of God is declarative and doxological; we magnify Him in the sense that a telescope magnifies a distant star—making it appear in its true, massive proportions to our vision and to those around us.

- True worship is an accurate theological assessment of God's supreme value compared to the fleeting, weightless vanities of the world.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- Psalm 22 moves dramatically from the depths of abandonment ("My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?") to the heights of worldwide praise.

- Verse 22 begins the pivot: "I will declare Your name to My brethren; in the midst of the assembly I will praise You."

- Verse 23 issues the specific call: "You who fear the Lord, praise Him! All you descendants of Jacob, glorify Him, and fear Him, all you offspring of Israel!"

- Here, kabad is the corporate response of the redeemed community. Because God has not despised nor abhorred the affliction of the afflicted, but has heard His cry, the entire covenant community is summoned to treat God with the highest, heaviest honor.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Exodus 20:12 - "Honor [kabad] your father and your mother, that your days may be long upon the land which the Lord your God is giving you."

- Leviticus 10:3 - "And Moses said to Aaron, 'This is what the Lord spoke, saying: "By those who come near Me I must be regarded as holy [kabad], and before all the people I must be glorified [kabad]."' "

- Isaiah 24:15 - "Therefore glorify [kabad] the Lord in the dawning light, the name of the Lord God of Israel in the coastlands of the sea."

- Proverbs 3:9 - "Honor [kabad] the Lord with your possessions, and with the firstfruits of all your increase."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The New Testament equivalent (doxazo) shares a similar theological trajectory, moving from opinion or estimation to ascribing supreme honor and manifest splendor to God.

- In both Testaments, glorifying God is not merely a matter of emotional sentiment, but of whole-souled allegiance and public testimony.
