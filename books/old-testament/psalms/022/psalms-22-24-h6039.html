AFFLICTION - ( oniy , Strong's H6039 )

Summary:

The Hebrew term oniy describes a state of profound distress, lowliness, and suffering—both physical and emotional—often accompanied by oppression or poverty. In the context of Psalm 22:24, it highlights the agonizing, desolate suffering of the sufferer who feels forsaken by God, yet serves as the foundation for divine attention, sympathy, and ultimate deliverance. God does not despise or hide His face from the oniy of the afflicted, but hears when they cry out to Him.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root verb anah carries the core idea of being bent down, humbled, or brought low by external circumstances, adversity, or mistreatment.

- In ancient Near Eastern contexts, the afflicted or lowly person (ani) was often completely devoid of human protection, rendering them entirely dependent on divine intervention.

- The term paints a vivid picture of a life weighed down by heavy burdens, sorrow, and grief, where one is brought to the very dust of the earth.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Oniy signifies affliction, misery, poverty, trouble, and the state of being humbled or oppressed.

- It denotes not merely difficult circumstances, but the internal and external weight of suffering that humbles a person.

- The word encompasses distress that arises from opposition, sickness, bereavement, or economic destitution.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- Used frequently throughout the Old Testament to describe the bitter trials of individuals and the corporate suffering of the nation of Israel.

- It captures the reality of slavery in Egypt, the grief of barrenness, and the anguish of physical illness.

- Despite the crushing weight of the experience, the word often transitions in Scripture from a description of misery to a place of desperate, faith-filled dependence upon God.

4. Theological Implications

- God maintains a special, attentive posture toward those experiencing oniy; He is neither indifferent nor callous to human suffering.

- Suffering often strips away human self-sufficiency, producing a holy humility that positions the believer to receive God's mercy and grace.

- In the prophetic and messianic scope of Psalm 22, the affliction of the Servant of the Lord is the precursor to global worship and divine redemption.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 22:24, the psalmist transitions from deep lament and agonizing isolation to triumphant praise because God "has not despised nor abhorred the affliction of the afflicted."

- The specific nuance here is vindication after deep suffering; the Lord's face was not permanently hidden from the one enduring agonizing oniy.

- It assures every believer that God recognizes, validates, and responds to the cries born out of deep personal misery.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Genesis 29:32 - "The LORD has looked on my affliction; now therefore, my husband will love me."

- Deuteronomy 26:7 - "Then we cried out to the LORD God of our fathers, and the LORD heard our voice and looked on our affliction and our labor and our oppression."

- 2 Kings 14:26 - "For the LORD saw that the affliction of Israel was very bitter."

- Psalm 119:150 - "They draw near who follow after wickedness; they are far from Your law." (Note: Contextually parallel in theme of distress, though oniy specifically highlights the state of misery in verses like Psalm 119:92: "Unless Your law had been my delight, then I would have perished in my affliction.")

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The pairing in Psalm 22:24 of God hearing the cry of the afflicted demonstrates that prayer is the proper response to deep oniy.

- The word bridges the gap between human weakness and divine compassion, showing that God's strength is most powerfully displayed in the context of human extremity.
