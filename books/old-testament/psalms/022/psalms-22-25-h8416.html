PRAISE - ( tehillah , Strong's H8416 )

Summary:

The Hebrew term tehillah in Psalm 22:25 moves beyond mere vocal affirmation to describe a song or act of public, corporate worship that celebrates the glorious character, mighty acts, and saving intervention of God. In the context of a suffering Savior who has been delivered, it represents the vital transition from agonizing lament to joyful testimony within the assembly of God's people.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- Derived from the root verb halal, meaning to shine, to flash forth, or to boast and make a show of something.

- The suffix changes the verb action into a concrete noun, representing the actual song, hymn, or public expression that results from recognizing God's glory.

- In ancient Israelite culture, public assembly (kahal) was the primary arena for declaring God's goodness, ensuring that personal deliverance became communal encouragement.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Defined primarily as a praise, song of praise, or laudation.

- Can refer to the content of the praise (the song or words spoken) or the act of praising itself.

- Frequently associated with corporate settings where God's renown is proclaimed among His people.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- Used in the Old Testament to describe the spontaneous or formal songs of God's people in response to His deliverance.

- Pictures a believer standing unashamedly before the congregation to boast not in themselves, but entirely in the character and rescuing power of Yahweh.

- Serves as the garment of worship exchanged for the spirit of heaviness (Isaiah 61:3).

4. Theological Implications

- Praise is never performed in a vacuum; it is inherently relational and communal, meant to direct the focus of the entire community toward God.

- In the prophetic scope of Psalm 22, the praise offered in the great congregation connects the personal deliverance of the Messiah to the ultimate worship of all the ends of the earth.

- True biblical praise acknowledges utter dependence on God for rescue and testifies faithfully to His righteousness before others.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 22:25, the speaker has transitioned from the depths of abandonment ("My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?") to miraculous deliverance.

- The vow to offer tehillah ("My praise shall be of You in the great assembly") is the fulfillment of a pledge made during suffering, turning personal rescue into public testimony.

- It emphasizes that God's salvation demands a corporate witness, ensuring that the congregation hears of His faithfulness.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Psalm 22:3 - "But You are holy, enthroned in the praises of Israel."

- Psalm 40:3 - "He has put a new song in my mouth—praise to our God; many will see it and fear, and will trust in the Lord."

- Isaiah 61:3 - "To console those who mourn in Zion, to give them beauty for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning, the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness, that they may be called trees of righteousness, the planting of the Lord, that He may be glorified."

- Jeremiah 17:14 - "Heal me, O Lord, and I shall be healed; save me, and I shall be saved, for You are my praise."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The word bridges the gap between private gratitude and public witness, reminding believers that personal miracles carry a corporate responsibility.

- It forms the focal point of God's dwelling place, as God is said to inhabit the tehillah (praises) of His people (Psalm 22:3).
