SEEK - ( darash , Strong's H1875 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb darash means to search out, inquire of, require, or demand. In a spiritual context, it refers to an earnest, intentional pursuit of God through worship, prayer, and covenant obedience. In Psalm 22:26, it describes those who actively seek a relationship with Yahweh, culminating in joyful praise, communal celebration, and deep spiritual satisfaction.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The concrete, historical root of darash carries the physical concept of treading, trampling, or beating a path with one's feet.

• It evokes the image of a well-worn path created by repeatedly traveling to a specific destination, such as a sanctuary, a prophet, or a place of counsel.

• Spiritually, this word picture depicts a life characterized by a habit of turning to God. It is not a casual or occasional glance, but the deliberate, persistent action of wearing down a path to the presence of God through regular prayer, worship, and reliance.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The verb primarily signifies to seek with care, investigate, or inquire.

• The semantic range includes:

• Inquiring of God for direction, revelation, or an oracle.

• Studying, searching, or investigating a matter carefully (such as searching out the Law of God).

• Demanding or requiring accountability (such as God requiring a reckoning for human blood).

• Seeking the peace, welfare, or prosperity of a person or a city.

• Unlike words that denote a simple or passive look, darash demands the engagement of the heart, mind, and will in a focused quest for a specific object or person.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Occurring more than 160 times in the Old Testament, darash is frequently contrasted with baqash (H1245). While both can be translated as "seek," baqash often emphasizes the external search for a missing object or person, whereas darash carries a stronger internal, intellectual, or moral focus.

• The word pictures associated with darash include:

• The investigator carefully searching out facts to render justice.

• The desperate citizen beating a path to the palace gates to consult the king.

• The worshiper regularly ascending the mountain of the Lord to seek divine counsel.

4. Theological Implications

• Seeking God (darash) is the defining characteristic of the righteous covenant partner. It stands in stark contrast to the wicked, who in their pride do not seek God (Psalm 10:4).

• Seeking God is intimately connected to seeking His commandments. True search for God cannot be separated from a desire to obey His revealed will.

• God promises a reciprocal relationship to those who seek Him. He is not a distant deity hiding from His people; rather, He promises that if His people seek Him with all their heart, He will be found by them.

• Conversely, God is the one who "requires" (darash) justice and loyalty. He examines the hearts of humanity, demanding accountability for sin and covenant unfaithfulness.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• Psalm 22 is a Messianic psalm of lament that transitions dramatically from agonizing suffering (vv. 1-21) to triumphant thanksgiving and global worship (vv. 22-31).

• In Psalm 22:26, the context is a communal sacrificial feast celebrating God’s deliverance: "The poor shall eat and be satisfied; Those who seek Him will praise the LORD."

• "Those who seek Him" are equated with the "poor" or "afflicted" ('anawim). These are the humble of the earth who recognize their desperate need for God and refuse to look to self, idols, or worldly powers for rescue.

• In this context, seeking Yahweh means turning to Him in the midst of suffering, trusting in His ultimate deliverance, and joining the congregation in celebrating His faithfulness. The result of this seeking is absolute satisfaction and eternal joy ("Let your heart live forever!").

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• 1 Chronicles 28:9: "...for the LORD searches all hearts and understands all the intent of the thoughts. If you seek Him, He will be found by you; but if you forsake Him, He will cast you off forever."

• Psalm 119:10: "With my whole heart I have sought You; Oh, let me not wander from Your commandments!"

• Amos 5:4: "For thus says the LORD to the house of Israel: 'Seek Me and live;'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• In the Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament), darash is most frequently translated by the compound Greek verb ekzeteo.

• The prefix ek- in ekzeteo acts as an intensifier, meaning to seek out eagerly, exhaustively, or with earnestness.

• This translation underscores that the "seekers" in Psalm 22:26 are not half-hearted or passive. Their pursuit of God is an intense, wholehearted endeavor born out of deep spiritual thirst.
