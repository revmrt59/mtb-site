HEART - ( lebab , Strong's H3824 )

Summary:

In biblical Hebrew, the heart (lebab) is not merely the organ that pumps blood or the seat of fleeting emotions; it is the innermost center of a person's existence, encompassing the mind, will, emotions, and spiritual vitality. In Psalm 22:26, the blessing "Let your heart live forever" is a declaration of ultimate, unending satisfaction and spiritual revival for the humble and those who seek the Lord, pointing to the eternal life and joy found in covenant fellowship with God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near East and the Old Testament, the heart was viewed as the central command post of the human body and soul. It represents the subterranean depths of human personality. Unlike modern Western thought, which separates the head (intellect) from the heart (emotion), Hebrew thought integrated both into the lebab. It is the place where decisions are made, where thoughts are processed, where emotions are felt, and where a person relates to God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term lebab refers to the inner man, the mind, the will, the heart, or the soul. It denotes:

• The seat of emotions (such as joy, sorrow, fear, and courage).

• The seat of intellect and understanding (reflection, memory, and discernment).

• The seat of the will and decisions (intentions, plans, and moral choices).

• The center of spiritual life (faith, devotion, or rebellion against God).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• A "double heart" (leb va-leb) indicates deceit, hypocrisy, or divided loyalty.

• A "broken heart" (nishberei-leb) represents profound grief, contrition, and humility before God.

• To "lay something to heart" means to consider it deeply and let it guide one's conduct.

• In Psalm 22:26, the heart "living" represents the restoration of strength, hope, and joy. A faint or dying heart is one crushed by despair, but a living heart is fully alive, resilient, and rejoicing in God's presence.

4. Theological Implications

The heart is the primary arena of God’s dealings with humanity. God does not look merely at outward appearances but searches the lebab. Spiritual renewal is described as receiving a "new heart." In the context of worship and covenant, the heart is where true praise and seeking God originate. The eternal life of the heart in Psalm 22:26 is not merely biological survival but the enduring spiritual vitality that comes from being united with the living God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Psalm 22 moves from the agonizing cry of abandonment ("My God, my God, why have You forsaken me?") to a triumphant song of deliverance and worldwide worship. In verse 26, the context is a communal thanksgiving feast. The "poor" (the humble, afflicted, and dependent on God) eat of this sacrificial feast and are fully satisfied. The blessing "Let your heart live forever!" is addressed to these humble seekers of God. While a physical meal sustains the body temporarily, this covenant fellowship sustains the lebab eternally. Their inner being is infused with a divine vitality that death cannot conquer.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 20:5 - "Did he not say to me, 'She is my sister'? And she, even she herself said, 'He is my brother.' In the integrity of my heart and innocence of my hands I have done this."

• Deuteronomy 6:5 - "You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your strength."

• 1 Kings 8:61 - "Let your heart therefore be loyal to the LORD our God, to walk in His statutes and keep His commandments, as at this day."

• Psalm 73:26 - "My flesh and my heart fail; But God is the strength of my heart and my portion forever."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The phrase "Let your heart live forever" echoes the ancient royal salute "O king, live forever." However, while a king's physical life eventually ends, the humble seeker's inner life is granted true, eternal vitality through God's redemptive work. This verse contains a subtle messianic and Eucharistic undertone. The suffering servant of Psalm 22 provides a feast of satisfaction for the humble. The satisfaction of the heart points forward to the spiritual nourishment believers receive through Christ, whose sacrifice brings eternal life to the hearts of all who feast on His grace.
