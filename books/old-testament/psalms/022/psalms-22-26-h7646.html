SATISFIED - ( sabea , Strong's H7646 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word sabea conveys the deep physical and spiritual reality of being fully filled, satiated, and content. In Psalm 22:26, it describes the profound heart-response of the afflicted who look to the Lord, finding their deepest longings completely met in Him. Rather than experiencing empty hunger or superficial relief, those who seek God are abundantly filled, leading directly to a life of perpetual praise and worship.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root verb fundamentally relates to eating and drinking to the point of complete physical gratification.

- In the agrarian and pastoral culture of the Old Testament, experiencing true sabea meant having enough bread, water, and life-sustenance to remove all gnawing hunger and anxiety about survival.

- From this physical bedrock, the language naturally expands to describe the soul's capacity to be filled by God, recognizing that human existence has spiritual hungers that only the Creator can quiet.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- To be full, satisfied, satiated, or having enough.

- To be filled with food, goods, years, or spiritual experiences to the point of complete contentment.

- It signifies an end to lack, striving, and spiritual emptiness.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- A banquet table: The word often paints the picture of a host providing an abundant feast where guests eat until they desire nothing more.

- A dry and thirsty land: It contrasts the barrenness of the wilderness with the overflowing provision of God's grace and presence.

- The satisfied soul is one that ceases its frantic searching because it has found the ultimate source of life and sustenance.

4. Theological Implications

- Human hearts are created with an infinite capacity that only God can fill; any attempt to find sabea apart from Him results in spiritual starvation.

- True satisfaction is not merely a psychological state of positive thinking, but a theological reality grounded in the character and provision of God.

- In the economy of grace, God specifically directs His fullness toward the humble and afflicted—those who recognize their utter dependence upon Him rather than self-sufficient pride.

5. Contextual Nuance in "Psalms 22:26"

- Psalm 22 moves sharply from the agonizing cry of abandonment on the cross ("My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?") to the triumphant declaration of global redemption and fellowship.

- Verse 26 states: "The poor shall eat and be satisfied; those who seek Him will praise the Lord. Let your heart live forever!"

- Here, sabea is the direct result of the "poor" (the humble, afflicted remnant) feasting upon the spiritual provisions won through suffering.

- The satisfaction is inextricably linked to worship ("shall praise the Lord"), showing that when God fills a human soul, the natural overflow is adoration rather than selfish hoarding.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Psalm 23:5: "You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies; You anoint my head with oil; My cup runs over." (Implies the experiential reality of sabea through divine hospitality).

- Psalm 103:5: "Who satisfies your mouth with good things, so that your youth is renewed like the eagle’s."

- Psalm 107:9: "For He satisfies the longing soul, and fills the hungry soul with goodness."

- Jeremiah 31:14: "I will satiate the soul of the priests with abundance, and My people shall be satisfied with My goodness, says the Lord."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The grammatical form in Psalm 22:26 indicates a collective, assured outcome: the humble will unfailingly be satisfied.

- In biblical theology, satisfaction never breeds laziness or complacency; instead, as seen in this verse, being filled by God energizes the heart for vibrant, eternal praise ("Let your heart live forever!").
