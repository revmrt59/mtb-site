PROSPEROUS - ( deshe , Strong's H1879 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word rendered "prosperous" in Psalm 22:29 carries the fundamental imagery of fresh, green vegetation thriving under the life-giving moisture of rain or dew. Far from promising material wealth or modern financial success, the biblical concept points to spiritual vitality, flourishing life, and ultimate sustenance provided entirely by God. In the climactic worship scene of Psalm 22, those who are "prosperous" are the spiritually satisfied who feast upon the provision of the Lord and respond with humble, enduring worship.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The term originates from the natural phenomenon of tender grass, fresh herbage, and green plants shooting up after a refreshing rain in an arid land.

- In the ancient Near East, green growth represented the clear difference between life and death, barrenness and divine favor.

- The word evokes the visual of a pasture fully capable of sustaining and satisfying hungry flocks, shifting the focus from human striving to divine provision.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- The root verb means to sprout, to shoot forth, or to grow green.

- As a noun or adjective, it refers to fresh grass, young growth, or things that are green and flourishing.

- The semantic range covers both literal plant life and the metaphorical flourishing of human life, vigor, and abundance.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- Used poetically to describe the lush condition of meadows and pastures watered by God's rain.

- Applied to human prosperity not as an end in itself, but as a visible sign of life sustained by divine grace.

- Contrasts sharply with dryness, fading, and decay, portraying the soul that is deeply nourished rather than withered by spiritual famine.

4. Theological Implications

- True prosperity in Scripture is inextricably linked to covenant relationship with God and dependence upon His provision.

- The flourishing of humanity—especially in the context of redemption—is entirely dependent on the suffering and exaltation of the Servant, as foreshadowed in Psalm 22.

- Spiritual satisfaction precedes true worship; only those who have been spiritually fed by God can bow before Him in genuine adoration.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 22:29, the English text reads: "All the prosperous of the earth shall eat and worship; all those who go down to the dust shall bow before Him..."

- Here, the "prosperous" are those who experience the rich, life-giving bounty of God's salvation, contrasting with those on the brink of death ("those who go down to the dust").

- The act of eating refers to partaking in the sacrificial feast of fellowship and thanksgiving, demonstrating that the beneficiaries of the Messiah's suffering are fully satisfied and compelled to worship.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Job 8:12 - "While it is yet green and not cut down, it withers before any other herb."

- Psalm 37:2 - "For they shall soon be cut down like the grass, and wither as the green herb."

- Proverbs 27:25 - "When the hay is removed, and the tender grass shows itself, and the herbs of the mountains are gathered in."

- Isaiah 15:6 - "For the waters of Nimrim will be desolate, for the green grass has withered away, the grass fails, there is no green thing."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The transition in Psalm 22 from deep suffering (verses 1-21) to universal worship (verses 27-31) highlights that the fruit of the cross includes a flourishing, satisfied people from every nation.

- The imagery bridges physical hunger and spiritual satiation, reminding the worshipper that every good gift of sustenance originates from the Creator.
