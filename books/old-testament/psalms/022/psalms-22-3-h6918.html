HOLY - ( qadosh , Strong's H6918 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word qadosh denotes that which is set apart, distinct, and utterly unique. In Psalm 22:3, it serves as a profound theological anchor. As the psalmist cries out in the agony of feeling forsaken by God, he immediately declares God's holiness. This affirmation establishes that even in times of deep suffering and apparent divine silence, God remains flawless in His character, righteous in His actions, and worthy of praise. His holiness is the guarantee of His faithfulness.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root qdš carries the primary physical concept of cutting, dividing, or separating. It denotes a withdrawal from common, ordinary, or profane use to be dedicated to a sacred purpose. The word picture is one of a boundary line drawn between the ordinary world and the sacred sphere. When applied to God, qadosh describes His absolute distinctness from His creation. He is "cut off" or infinitely elevated above everything else. He is completely unique, peerless, and incomparable.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Qadosh refers to that which is holy, sacred, set apart, transcendent, and morally pure.

• Ontological Holiness: God is uniquely transcendent. He is sovereign, uncreated, and infinitely distinct from all created things. He is in a class by Himself.

• Ethical Holiness: God is absolutely pure, free from any spot, blemish, or moral imperfection. He is completely light, and in Him is no darkness at all.

• Derived Holiness: Objects, places, days, and people are designated as qadosh because they are set apart for God's exclusive service and ownership.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, qadosh is used in several ways:

• The Holy One of Israel: This title, used extensively in Isaiah, emphasizes God's supreme majesty and His covenant relationship with His people.

• Sacred Spaces and Objects: Mount Sinai, the Tabernacle, the Temple, and the vessels used in worship are qadosh because they are reserved for God's presence and service.

• A Set-Apart People: Israel was called to be a qadosh nation, reflecting God's distinct character by living according to His law, separated from the pagan practices of the surrounding nations.

4. Theological Implications

Holiness is not merely one attribute among many; it is the modifier of all God's attributes. His love is a holy love; His wrath is a holy wrath; His justice is a holy justice. God's holiness is the foundation of His moral law and the standard for His people. Because God is qadosh, He cannot tolerate sin, yet His holiness also guarantees that He will be entirely faithful to His covenant promises. He cannot lie, fail, or act contrary to His perfect character.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 22:3, the context is one of extreme lament and physical and spiritual suffering. The psalm begins with the agonizing cry, "My God, my God, why have You forsaken me?" (v. 1). Yet, in the very next breath, the psalmist declares, "But You are holy, enthroned in the praises of Israel" (v. 3).

• Vindication of God: Even while suffering under the silence of God, the psalmist refuses to charge God with wrongdoing. Declaring God to be qadosh is an act of trust, affirming that God's ways are perfect, even when they are painful and incomprehensible.

• The Throne of Praise: God is "enthroned" (yosheb) in the praises of His people. His transcendent holiness does not make Him distant or indifferent; rather, He dwells intimately in the midst of His people’s worship.

• Messianic Foreshadowing: As a prophetic picture of Christ on the cross, this verse reveals the ultimate tension of the atonement: the Holy One of Israel must turn His face away from the one bearing the sin of the world, yet His holiness is the very reason why this sacrifice is perfect and acceptable.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Leviticus 19:2 - "Speak to all the congregation of the children of Israel, and say to them: ‘You shall be holy, for I the LORD your God am holy.’"

• Isaiah 6:3 - "And one cried to another and said: 'Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts; The whole earth is full of His glory!'"

• Isaiah 43:15 - "I am the LORD, your Holy One, The Creator of Israel, your King."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Hebrew grammar, the repetition of a word expresses emphasis, and a threefold repetition (the trisagion in Isaiah 6:3) represents the superlative degree. God is not merely holy; He is the thrice-holy God. This highlights that holiness is the very essence of His divine being. In Psalm 22, this transcendent purity contrasts with the "worm" status of the suffering psalmist (v. 6), magnifying the grace of a holy God who condescends to hear the cries of the afflicted.
