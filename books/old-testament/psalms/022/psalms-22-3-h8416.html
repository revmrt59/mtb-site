PRAISES - ( tehillah , Strong's H8416 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun tehillah in Psalm 22:3 represents the corporate and individual song of adoration, glorification, and heartfelt commendation offered to God, rooted deeply in His holy character rather than temporary circumstances.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- Derived from the root verb halal, from which the universal exclamation "Hallelujah" (praise Yah) is formed.

- Literally pictures a public, shining celebration, boasting, or joyful clamor regarding the greatness and majesty of someone worthy of honor.

- In the ancient Near Eastern and biblical context, offering a tehillah is an act of stepping outside of oneself to magnify the reputation, splendor, and righteous deeds of the Almighty.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Refers to praise, song of praise, laudation, or a hymn of glory.

- Denotes both the act of praising and the designated song or utterance through which praise is expressed.

- Specifically describes adoration that is fittingly directed toward God because of who He is in His holiness and what He has done in redemption and history.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- Used frequently throughout the Psalms to describe the verbalized worship of God's people.

- Often paired with concepts of temple worship, public assembly, and joyous proclamation before the nations.

- Functions as a garment or attitude put on by the believer in exchange for heaviness (Isaiah 61:3), illustrating praise as a spiritual posture that displaces despair.

4. Theological Implications

- Psalm 22:3 declares that God "inhabits" or is "enthroned upon" the tehillah of His people, indicating that God's presence uniquely manifests where His character is adored and celebrated.

- Connects God's holiness directly to the worship of His people; even amid suffering and feeling forsaken (as seen in the broader context of Psalm 22), the believer recognizes that God remains holy and entirely worthy of praise.

- Shifts the focus of faith from human pain to divine sovereignty, anchoring trust in the unchanging nature of God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 22:3 ("But You are holy, enthroned in the praises of Israel"), the psalmist (traditionally David) is experiencing deep anguish, feelings of abandonment, and mocking enemies.

- Despite the absence of immediate deliverance, the psalmist anchors his soul in the character of God, remembering that Israel's history is marked by God dwelling amid the worship of His people.

- The use of praises here serves as an act of defiant faith, choosing to enthrone God in worship even while surrounded by distress.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Exodus 15:11: "Who is like You, glorious in holiness, fearful in praises, doing wonders?"

- Deuteronomy 10:21: "He is your praise, and He is your God, who has done for you these great and awesome things which your eyes have seen."

- Psalm 40:3: "He has put a new song in my mouth—praise to our God; many will see it and fear, and will trust in the Lord."

- Isaiah 61:3: "To console those who mourn in Zion, to give them beauty for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning, the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness; that they may be called trees of righteousness, the planting of the Lord, that He may be glorified."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The grammatical structure in Psalm 22:3 portrays God as sitting enthroned upon, riding upon, or inhabiting the praises offered by His covenant people.

- It teaches a profound pastoral truth: when we cannot trace God's hand, we can still trust His heart by offering the sacrifice of praise, creating a spiritual sanctuary where God's presence is powerfully known.
