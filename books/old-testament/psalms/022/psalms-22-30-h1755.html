GENERATION - ( dow , Strong's H1755 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun dow primarily denotes a period of time, an age, or the people who live during a specific era, particularly a succession of family descendants. In Psalm 22:30, it points to the unbroken lineage of worshippers who will hear the testimony of God's saving acts and declare His righteousness to those yet unborn, ensuring that the covenant community endures across time.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root idea of dow involves the movement of time, likened to a cycle, a revolution of age, or a dwelling-place and period.

- Culturally, in the ancient Near East and especially within Israel, lineage and generational continuity were vital to preserving the memory of God's deeds and maintaining covenant faithfulness.

- The term visually captures the passing of a torch from fathers to sons, creating a living bridge of faith that spans across centuries.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Refers to an age, a generation of men, or a specific period of historical time.

- Denotes contemporaries—those living at the same time—as well as successive lineage descendants down through history (posterity).

- Used to describe the general moral character of a group living in a specific era (e.g., "a wicked and perverse generation"), though in Psalm 22 it carries a distinctly positive, redemptive tone of faithful descendants.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The word frequently evokes the imagery of a continuous stream of human lives flowing from one era into the next, never drying up because God continually raises up a people for Himself.

- It emphasizes corporate identity; individuals belong to a larger story that began long before their birth and will continue long after their death.

4. Theological Implications

- God’s faithfulness and salvation are never limited to a single moment in history; they are designed to be transmitted dynamically across successive generations.

- The survival and spiritual vitality of the church or covenant community depend on older generations intentionally declaring God's righteousness to the generations following.

- Psalm 22 moves from deep suffering (the crucifixion imagery of vv. 1-21) to worldwide praise and generational legacy (vv. 22-31), showing that Christ's sufferings secure a lasting posterity.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 22:30 ("A posterity shall serve Him. It will be recounted of the Lord to the next generation"), dow (translated here as "generation" in parallel with "posterity") signifies the future offspring who will inherit the spiritual fruits of the Messiah's vindication.

- The focus is on active, ongoing worship: the coming generation will not merely exist; they will actively serve the Lord and hear the ongoing story of His redemptive acts.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Psalm 24:6 - "This is Jacob, the generation of those who seek Him, who seek Your face."

- Psalm 78:4 - "We will not hide them from their children, telling to the generation to come the praises of the Lord, and His strength and His wonderful works that He has done."

- Psalm 145:4 - "One generation shall praise Your works to another, and shall declare Your mighty acts."

- Joel 1:3 - "Tell your children about it, let your children tell their children, and their children another generation."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The transmission of faith from one dow to the next is framed in Scripture not as a natural, automatic inheritance, but as a deliberate act of testimony, teaching, and proclamation.

- This word reminds believers of their stewardship in time: we belong to God for our brief span, and our primary duty is to hand off the knowledge of His righteousness to those who follow.
