POSTERITY - ( zera , Strong's H2233 )

Summary:

In Psalm 22:30, the word translated "posterity" (zera) refers to a future lineage, offspring, or spiritual descendants who will serve the Lord as a direct result of the Messiah’s suffering and vindication. It represents a continuous, faithful generation that carries forward the knowledge of God’s righteousness to those yet unborn, ensuring that the legacy of His redemptive triumph is never forgotten.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew noun zera is derived from the agricultural verb zara, meaning "to sow" or "to scatter seed." In the ancient agrarian world, seed was the very essence of life, potential, sustenance, and hope. A seed sown in the soil represents a hidden but certain future harvest. This concrete physical image naturally translates into human genealogy, denoting physical semen, offspring, descendants, and lineage. In the ancient Near East, securing a physical posterity was paramount; it represented the survival of the family name, the preservation of inheritance, and the fulfillment of covenant promises. When applied metaphorically or spiritually, the word pictures a vital, growing, and continuous line of faithful followers who spring from a common source.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun zera possesses a rich semantic range:

• Seed of plants (agricultural seed sown for crops).

• Offspring, descendants, posterity, or children (human lineage).

• Semen (the physical seed of copulation).

• Family, class, or nation (figurative grouping of those sharing a common heritage).

In Psalm 22:30, zera carries the specific nuance of a future generation of descendants. This is a collective singular noun, which allows it to refer either to a single prominent descendant (a Messianic Seed) or to a massive corporate body of offspring (a spiritual lineage) who serve Yahweh.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Seed of the Woman: In Genesis 3:15, zera is used to describe the "Seed of the woman" who will eventually crush the head of the serpent. This establishes a key Messianic trajectory for the word.

• The Covenant Seed: In the Abrahamic Covenant (Genesis 12:7; 15:5), God promises to multiply Abraham's zera like the dust of the earth and the stars of heaven. This refers to physical Israel, but ultimately points to a spiritual offspring of faith.

• The Royal Seed: God promises to establish the zera of David forever (Psalm 89:4), a promise that culminates in the eternal reign of Jesus Christ.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, zera connects the suffering of the Messiah directly to the birth of the global Church. Psalm 22 begins with the agonizing cry of abandonment ("My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?") but transitions in verse 22 to a triumphal declaration of praise and global dominion. The "posterity" that serves Him in verse 30 is the direct result of this suffering and vindication. Just as a grain of wheat must fall into the ground and die to produce much fruit, the Savior's death produced a vast spiritual posterity. This highlights the doctrine of covenantal faithfulness; God preserves a remnant, a holy seed, through whom His name and truth are perpetually declared.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 22:30, the psalmist declares, "A posterity (zera) shall serve Him. It will be recounted of the Lord to the next generation." This comes immediately after the declaration that the ends of the world shall remember and turn to the Lord (v. 27). The immediate context shows that this posterity is not limited to physical Israel. It represents a new, international community of faith born from the Messiah’s victory. The nuance here is one of continuous, active service ("shall serve Him") and faithful transmission ("It will be recounted"). This posterity does not merely exist; they actively preserve and proclaim the saving work of God to the succeeding generation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 3:15: "And I will put enmity Between you and the woman, And between your seed (zera) and her Seed (zera); He shall bruise your head, And you shall bruise His heel."

• Genesis 12:7: "Then the Lord appeared to Abram and said, 'To your descendants (zera) I will give this land.' And there he built an altar to the Lord, who had appeared to him."

• Genesis 15:5: "Then He brought him outside and said, 'Look now toward heaven, and count the stars if you are able to number them.' And He said to him, 'So shall your descendants (zera) be.'"

• Psalm 89:4: "'Your seed (zera) I will establish forever, And build up your throne to all generations.'"

• Isaiah 53:10: "Yet it pleased the Lord to bruise Him; He has put Him to grief. When You make His soul an offering for sin, He shall see His seed (zera), He shall prolong His days, And the pleasure of the Lord shall prosper in His hand."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A remarkable parallel exists between Psalm 22:30 and Isaiah 53:10. Both are premier Messianic passages detailing the intense suffering of the Servant. In Isaiah 53:10, after the Servant's soul is made "an offering for sin," the text promises that "He shall see His seed (zera)." The suffering of the Savior is not in vain; it guarantees a spiritual family. In the New Testament, this concept is fulfilled as believers are described as being "born again, not of corruptible seed but incorruptible, through the word of God which lives and abides forever" (1 Peter 1:23).
