RIGHTEOUSNESS - ( tsedaqah , Strong's H6666 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word tsedaqah refers to conformity to an ethical or moral standard, specifically the righteous character, covenant faithfulness, and saving acts of God. In Psalm 22:31, it denotes God’s faithful vindication and deliverance of His suffering servant, a demonstration of divine justice and saving power that must be declared to generations yet unborn.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root verb tsadaq relates to being straight, right, or meeting an established standard.

• In ancient Near Eastern contexts, this concept was often illustrated by a straight measuring line or accurate scales used in the marketplace. To be righteous was to be physically, morally, and legally "straight."

• Unlike abstract Greek philosophical ideas of virtue, Hebrew righteousness is inherently relational. It describes a person who fulfills the moral obligations of a relationship, whether with other human beings or with God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The noun tsedaqah means justice, rightness, moral rectitude, and righteousness.

• When applied to God, it represents His absolute moral perfection, His judicial fairness, and His covenant loyalty.

• In poetic and prophetic literature, tsedaqah is frequently used as a synonym for salvation, deliverance, and vindication. It refers to God actively stepping in to set things right for His oppressed people.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• It is used to describe honest mercantile scales and weights, representing absolute fairness and truth in daily life (Deuteronomy 25:15).

• It is portrayed as a protective breastplate worn by God as He marches out to rescue His people (Isaiah 59:17).

• It is described as a garment or robe that wraps the believer, representing a status of honor and acceptance bestowed by God (Isaiah 61:10).

4. Theological Implications

• Righteousness is grounded in the unchanging nature of God. He does not merely conform to a standard of righteousness; He is the standard.

• Because God is righteous, He is faithful to His covenant promises. His tsedaqah guarantees that He will not abandon His people or leave injustice unanswered.

• Human righteousness is fundamentally deficient because of the Fall. Therefore, true righteousness must be credited or accounted to a person by God on the basis of faith, laying the groundwork for the New Testament doctrine of justification by faith.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• Psalm 22 is a messianic psalm of David that begins with the agonizing cry of abandonment ("My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?") and ends with universal praise and victory.

• In Psalm 22:31, "His righteousness" refers to God's active, historical vindication of the Sufferer. God did not turn His face away forever; He heard, answered, and delivered.

• This act of deliverance is so monumental that it cannot be kept silent. It must be declared to "a people who will be born"—future generations who did not witness the event but will live in the reality of its accomplishment.

• The declaration of His righteousness concludes with the phrase, "That He has done this," which serves as the Old Testament equivalent to Christ’s final triumph cry on the cross: "It is finished."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 15:6 - "And he believed in the LORD, and He accounted it to him for righteousness."

• Psalm 24:5 - "He shall receive blessing from the LORD, And righteousness from the God of his salvation."

• Psalm 103:17 - "But the mercy of the LORD is from everlasting to everlasting on those who fear Him, And His righteousness to children's children,"

• Isaiah 61:10 - "I will greatly rejoice in the LORD, My soul shall be joyful in my God; For He has clothed me with the garments of salvation, He has covered me with the robe of righteousness,"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament) translates tsedaqah in Psalm 22:31 as dikaiosyne. This is the exact Greek word used by the Apostle Paul in the Epistle to the Romans to explain the gospel as the revelation of the "righteousness of God" (Romans 1:17).

• The transition in Psalm 22 from verse 21 to verse 22 marks the transition from death to resurrection. The declaration of God's tsedaqah in verse 31 is the preaching of the resurrected Christ and the finished work of redemption to the ends of the earth.
