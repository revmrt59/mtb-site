RIDICULE - ( la'ag , Strong's H3932 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb la'ag conveys the act of mocking, deriding, or speaking with scornful contempt, often accompanied by facial expressions or gestures of disbelief. In Psalm 22:7, it describes the cruel public mockery hurled against the suffering psalmist, a scene prophetically fulfilled in the derision Jesus faced on the cross.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root idea of la'ag carries the physical and vocal posture of looking down on someone with arrogant amusement. It is the sound of derisive laughter and the motion of wagging the head in disbelief or contempt.

- In the ancient Near East, public shaming was a powerful weapon used to isolate the vulnerable, implying that the victim's suffering was proof of divine abandonment.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- La'ag means to mock, deride, ridicule, or scoff.

- It involves verbal insults combined with an attitude of superiority, treating someone's plight as foolish, hopeless, or deserving of scorn.

- The term highlights the painful disparity between the humility of the sufferer and the arrogant pride of the mockers.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The word often paints a vivid picture of observers shaking their heads, pointing fingers, and jeering at those who are down.

- It frequently appears in wisdom literature and the Psalms to depict the wicked mocking the righteous, contrasting the foolish arrogance of the scoffer with the steadfast trust of the godly.

4. Theological Implications

- When the righteous suffer, the ungodly often mistake temporal affliction for permanent rejection by God, leading to cruel ridicule.

- The mockery endured by the faithful points forward to the ultimate rejection and derision suffered by the Messiah, transforming human shame into the arena of divine redemption.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 22:7, the sufferer is utterly isolated, surrounded by enemies who "all those who see me ridicule me; they shoot out the lip, they shake the head."

- Here, la'ag captures both the internal contempt and the outward, theatrical gestures of derision directed at one whose faith in God is put to the extreme test.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Psalm 2:4 - "He who sits in the heavens shall laugh; the Lord shall hold them in derision (la'ag)."

- Proverbs 1:26 - "I also will laugh at your calamity; I will mock (la'ag) when your terror comes."

- 2 Kings 19:21 - "This is the word which the Lord has spoken concerning him: 'The virgin, the daughter of Zion, has despised you, laughed you to scorn (la'ag); the daughter of Jerusalem has shaken her head behind your back!'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The specific physical gestures mentioned alongside la'ag in Psalm 22:7—shooting out the lip and shaking the head—find direct historical fulfillment in the Gospels during the crucifixion of Jesus (Matthew 27:39; Mark 15:29).

- The progression of the word in Scripture demonstrates that human ridicule against God's anointed will ultimately be met with divine laughter and judgment.
