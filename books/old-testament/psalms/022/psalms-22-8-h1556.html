TRUSTED - ( galal , Strong's H1556 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word translated "trusted" in Psalm 22:8 is galal, which literally means "to roll" or "to roll away." In a metaphorical and spiritual sense, it denotes rolling one's heavy burdens, cares, or entire life pathway onto the Lord, signifying complete, helpless, and active reliance upon Him. In the context of Psalm 22:8, this deep trust is mockingly weaponized by enemies who ridicule the suffering speaker for committing his life so entirely to God's care.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The physical imagery behind galal is that of rolling a massive stone or a heavy burden. In the ancient Near East, travelers carried heavy packs on their backs. When the weight became too great, they would roll the burden off their own shoulders and onto a beast of burden or a stronger companion. Spiritually, galal presents trust not as an abstract feeling, but as a physical transfer of weight. To trust God is to roll the crushing weight of one's life, anxieties, and destiny off oneself and onto the sovereign shoulders of Yahweh.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary lexical meaning of galal is to roll, roll away, roll down, or commit. When used in relation to human action toward God, it functions as a figurative idiom for trust, surrender, and commitment. To "roll oneself" or "roll one's way" upon the Lord means to transfer all responsibility for one's safekeeping and ultimate outcome to Him. It implies a total abandonment of self-reliance, acknowledging that the burden is too heavy for human strength to bear.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The verb galal occurs in several distinct contexts throughout the Old Testament:

• Literal Rolling: It describes rolling physical objects, such as rolling a great stone over the mouth of a cave or away from a well.

• Rolling Away Obstacles/Reproach: It is used to describe God rolling away a spiritual or psychological burden, such as rolling away the reproach of slavery and shame from Israel.

• Metaphorical Commitment: It is used as a command to "roll" one’s life path or works onto the Lord, which the NKJV translates as "commit."

4. Theological Implications

The theological depth of galal lies in its portrayal of faith as active relinquishment.

• Human Limitation: It assumes that human beings are not designed to carry the heavy burdens of existence, suffering, or destiny. Self-reliance is exposed as a crushing weight.

• Active Surrender: Biblical trust is more than intellectual assent; it is an active, decisive rolling over of one's concerns to God. Once rolled onto Him, the burden is no longer carried by the believer.

• Messianic Fulfillment: Psalm 22 is highly Messianic. The mockery directed at the suffering servant ("He trusted in the LORD...") represents the ultimate test of faith. On the cross, Jesus Christ perfectly embodied galal by rolling the weight of His spirit into the hands of the Father, despite the mocking voices of those who watched Him suffer.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 22:8, the enemies of the psalmist use this word mockingly: "He trusted in the LORD, let Him rescue Him." The Hebrew grammar here can be understood as a sarcastic command or a mocking observation: "Roll it onto Yahweh!" They mock the sufferer's lifestyle of total dependence. The irony is profound: the mockers see this complete transfer of care as a sign of weakness and folly, yet it is the very definition of saving faith. Christ's enemies at the cross quoted these exact words, demonstrating that the ultimate expression of galal was crucified, yet vindicated through the resurrection.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 29:10 - "And it came to pass, when Jacob saw Rachel the daughter of Laban his mother’s brother, and the sheep of Laban his mother’s brother, that Jacob went near and rolled the stone from the well’s mouth, and watered the flock of Laban his mother’s brother."

• Joshua 5:9 - "Then the LORD said to Joshua, 'This day I have rolled away the reproach of Egypt from you.' Therefore the name of the place is called Gilgal to this day."

• Psalm 37:5 - "Commit [literally: Roll] your way to the LORD, Trust also in Him, And He shall bring it to pass."

• Proverbs 16:3 - "Commit [literally: Roll] your works to the LORD, And your thoughts will be established."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the New Testament, the Greek equivalent concept of casting our cares upon God is found in 1 Peter 5:7 ("casting all your care upon Him, for He cares for you"). The Greek verb epiripto means to throw or cast upon, which beautifully mirrors the Hebrew concept of galal. Both languages emphasize that the believer is not meant to hold onto the heavy anxieties of life but must actively transfer them to God.
