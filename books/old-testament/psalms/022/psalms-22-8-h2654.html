DELIGHTS - ( chaphets , Strong's H2654 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb chaphets speaks of a deep-seated desire, pleasure, and emotional inclination toward someone or something. In the context of Psalm 22:8, it highlights the intimate relationship of favor and pleasure that God is assumed to have with His righteous sufferer. While mockers used this term as a sarcastic weapon during the crucifixion, it ultimately points to the profound truth of God's genuine and unwavering delight in His Messiah, which was fully vindicated through the resurrection.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root of chaphets conveys the physical action of bending, inclining, or leaning toward something. It paints a picture of someone whose heart and attention are completely drawn to a specific object or person. Rather than expressing a cold, intellectual choice, the word communicates a warm, emotional, and affectionate movement of the will. To delight in something is to bend one's steps and desires toward it, finding deep satisfaction and joy in its presence.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term primarily means to take pleasure in, to delight in, to favor, or to be pleased with. In other contexts, it can be translated as "to desire," "to wish," or "to please." It describes an internal state of satisfaction that leads to outward favor. When applied to God, it indicates His sovereign pleasure and loving-kindness toward His covenant people, showing that His actions on their behalf are not done out of begrudging duty, but out of genuine delight.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, this word appears in various contexts to illustrate deep-seated desire:

• Human affection and marriage: It describes a man's romantic desire and delight in a woman.

• Divine sovereignty: It emphasizes that God does whatever pleases Him in the heavens and on the earth, indicating that His sovereign acts are expressions of His divine pleasure.

• Moral alignment: It contrasts those who delight in wickedness with those who delight in the law of God and in doing His will.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, chaphets reveals that God is not a distant, impassive deity, but a personal God who experiences delight. His relationship with His servants is marked by affectionate pleasure. Furthermore, this word carries immense Messianic weight. It is the very word used to express God's supreme pleasure in His Son. At Jesus' baptism and transfiguration, the Father declared, "This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased." This New Testament declaration is the spiritual equivalent of the Hebrew chaphets—God’s ultimate delight rests upon the Messiah.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 22:8, the psalm of the suffering righteous man (and prophetically, the suffering Messiah), the mockers surround the sufferer and hurl insults. They say, "He trusted in the LORD, let Him rescue Him; Let Him deliver Him, since He delights in Him!" The mockers use the reality of God's delight as a tool for psychological torture. They argue that if God truly took pleasure in this sufferer, He would intervene instantly to stop the pain. This creates a powerful tension: the reality of God's genuine delight in His Son is juxtaposed with the apparent abandonment of the cross. The ultimate resolution of the psalm, and of the gospel, is that God's delight was not disproven by the suffering; rather, the suffering was the path to the ultimate vindication of that delight in the resurrection.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Numbers 14:8 - "If the LORD delights in us, then He will bring us into this land and give it to us, ‘a land which flows with milk and honey’;"

• Psalm 18:19 - "He also brought me out into a broad place; He delivered me because He delighted in me."

• Psalm 40:8 - "I delight to do Your will, O my God, And Your law is within my heart."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint), chaphets in Psalm 22:8 is translated using the verb thelo ("to wish, will, or take pleasure in"). This directly connects to the fulfillment in Matthew 27:43, where the religious leaders mock Jesus on the cross using these exact words: "He trusted in God; let Him deliver Him now if He will have Him; for He said, ‘I am the Son of God.’" The mockers unknowingly quoted Psalm 22, confirming Jesus as the ultimate Righteous Sufferer in whom the Father eternally delights.
