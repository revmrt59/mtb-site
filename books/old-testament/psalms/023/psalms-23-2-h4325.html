WATERS - ( mayim , Strong's H4325 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word mayim (waters) in Psalm 23:2 paints a vivid pastoral picture of absolute provision and tranquility. Far from the terrifying, chaotic deeps often depicted in Scripture, the "still waters" represent the gentle, life-giving refreshment that the Good Shepherd provides to restore the weary soul of His sheep.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- In the arid, sun-scorched terrain of the ancient Near East, water was the absolute difference between life and death for a flock of sheep.

- Sheep are notoriously fearful of rushing, turbulent water; they will refuse to drink from a loud, roaring stream. A shepherd had to find or create tranquil pools where the flock could drink safely without being swept away by a sudden flash flood.

- The phrase "still waters" translates literally to "waters of rest" or "resting waters," pointing directly to the shepherd's care in leading his flock to a place of utter peace and physical renewal.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Mayim is a plural noun that occurs over 500 times in the Old Testament, denoting water in various states: rivers, springs, rain, and the deep.

- In its plural form, it often emphasizes abundance, vastness, or the life-sustaining properties of water in a dry land.

- Semantically, it spans from physical hydration and cleansing to profound metaphors of spiritual sustenance, chaos, and divine judgment.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- Physical Refreshment: Used frequently for literal drinking water that quenches physical thirst (Genesis 21:19).

- Spiritual Sustenance: Used metaphorically for the life-giving word and presence of God that satisfies the deepest longings of the human heart (Isaiah 55:1).

- Overwhelming Trials: Used as a metaphor for deep troubles, sorrows, or persecutions that threaten to drown the believer (Psalm 69:1-2).

4. Theological Implications

- God is presented as the ultimate Provider who does not merely keep His people alive, but leads them into places of deep rest and spiritual restoration.

- The provision of water is consistently tied to covenant faithfulness; God provides water in the wilderness to remind His people that He is the source of all life.

- The imagery anticipates the ministry of the Messiah, who offers "living water" that permanently satisfies spiritual thirst (John 4:10, 7:37-38).

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 23:2, "waters" are qualified by the adjective menuchot (resting/quiet).

- The focus is not on the vastness or power of the water, but on its gentleness and safety.

- The Good Shepherd leads His sheep here not to overwhelm them, but to quiet their anxieties, restore their souls, and supply every need in an environment of complete security.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Psalm 23:2: "He leads me beside the still waters."

- Psalm 46:3: "Though its waters roar and be troubled, though the mountains shake with its swelling."

- Isaiah 43:2: "When you pass through the waters, I will be with you; and through the rivers, they shall not overflow you."

- Jeremiah 2:13: "For My people have committed two evils: they have forsaken Me, the fountain of living waters, and hewed themselves cisterns—broken cisterns that can hold no water."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- Sheep actually obtain a significant portion of the moisture they need from the dew on the grass in the early morning, which makes the shepherd's early guidance crucial.

- The Hebrew mindset viewed water not merely as a chemical compound, but as a dynamic gift from the hand of God, directly dependent on His meteorological and providential control.
