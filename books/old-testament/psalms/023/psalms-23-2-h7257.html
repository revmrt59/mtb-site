LIE DOWN - ( rabats , Strong's H7257 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb rabats (H7257) translated "lie down" in Psalm 23:2 paints a vivid and comforting pastoral picture of a sheep settling into safety, deep contentment, and absolute rest under the watchful eye of the shepherd. It denotes more than simply sleeping; it describes a posture of total relaxation made possible only when fear, hunger, and danger have been completely removed by the caretaker.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root verb vividly captures the physical action of a four-legged animal folding its legs and settling its body flat upon the ground.

- In the agrarian and pastoral setting of the ancient Near East, sheep are famously nervous, skittish animals. They will not lie down unless four specific conditions are met: they must be free from fear of predators, free from friction with other sheep in the flock, free from tormenting pests like flies or parasites, and free from hunger.

- The shepherd must actively provide these conditions; therefore, the animal's act of lying down is a direct reflection of the shepherd's diligent care and provision.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Rabats primarily means to lie down, crouch, stretch out, or repose.

- It is frequently used in biblical Hebrew for animals resting in their pastures, lairs, or folds.

- The term carries an inherent sense of quietness, security, and settledness, distinguishing it from sudden collapsing, falling, or lying down in exhaustion or defeat.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The word often conveys a sense of peaceful habitation, such as flocks resting securely in a green pasture or wild animals resting safely in their native habitats.

- It can also be used metaphorically for rest and peace that comes from divine presence and protection.

- In prophetic imagery, rabats frequently illustrates the ultimate peace of the messianic kingdom, where natural predators and prey dwell together without fear.

4. Theological Implications

- Spiritual rest is never self-generated; it is always the gracious gift of the Good Shepherd. Believers cannot manufacture true inner quietness while surrounded by the spiritual "predators" and anxieties of life; God must create the environment of safety.

- The rest described by this word anticipates the ultimate spiritual security found in Christ, who invites weary souls to find true rest in Him (Matthew 11:28-29).

- True worship and trust involve surrendering our need for constant vigilance, resting completely in the sovereign protection of our Maker.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 23:2, "He makes me to lie down in green pastures," the causative grammatical stem (Hiphil) emphasizes that the Shepherd actively causes or guides the sheep into this posture of rest.

- The sheep does not force itself down; the Shepherd arranges the pasture, removes the threats, and gently leads the flock until they willingly and safely settle into the lush grass.

- This highlights both the sovereignty of God's care and the profound vulnerability of the believer who relies entirely upon Him for peace amidst a troubled world.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Isaiah 11:6 - "The wolf also shall dwell with the lamb, The leopard shall lie down with the young goat, The calf and the young lion and the fatling together; And a little child shall lead them."

- Ezekiel 34:15 - "I will feed My flock, and I will make them lie down, says the Lord GOD."

- Zephaniah 3:13 - "The remnant of Israel shall not do unrighteousness And speak lies, Nor shall a deceitful tongue be found in their mouth; For they shall feed their flocks and lie down, And no one shall make them afraid."

- Job 11:19 - "You would also lie down, and no one would make you afraid; Yes, many would court your favor."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The related noun form (rebetz) refers to a resting place or a fold where flocks settle down for the night.

- In ancient poetry and prophecy, the word often evokes a sanctuary of safety, contrasting sharply with the barren, dangerous wilderness where rest is impossible.
