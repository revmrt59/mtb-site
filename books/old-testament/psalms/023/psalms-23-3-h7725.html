RESTORES - ( shûb , Strong's H7725 )

Summary:

In Psalm 23:3, the word restores (from the Hebrew root shûb) portrays the Good Shepherd's tender work of reviving, refreshing, and bringing back the fainting or wandering soul. This term goes beyond simple physical repair; it points to the restoration of spiritual vitality, peace, and alignment with God. When a believer is weary, cast down, or prone to wander, the Shepherd actively intervenes to renew their life-force and guide them back into a right relationship with Himself.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The Hebrew verb shûb is a primary root meaning "to turn back" or "to return."

• In a pastoral or shepherding context, this word carries a rich, practical word picture. Sheep can easily become "cast"—a technical term for a sheep that has rolled over onto its back and cannot get up on its own. Left in this position, a cast sheep will quickly bloat and die. The shepherd must actively search for the cast sheep, gently roll it over, reassure it, and restore its circulation so it can stand again.

• Additionally, sheep are prone to wandering aimlessly away from the flock. The shepherd uses his staff to rescue and bring back the straying sheep, returning them to safety.

• The physical restoration of the sheep maps directly to the spiritual renewal of the believer. God rescues His people when they are emotionally exhausted, spiritually "cast down," or drifting away.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The basic, root meaning of shûb is to turn back, return, go back, or bring back. It is a highly versatile verb used to describe both physical movement and spiritual transformation.

• In Psalm 23:3, the verb appears in the Polel stem. This specific grammatical form emphasizes an intensive, repetitive, or causative action. Rather than a simple one-time return, it means to actively restore, refresh, recreate, or thoroughly bring back.

• The object of this restoration is the nephesh (soul), which refers to the throat, the breath, the seat of vitality, or the entire living being. Therefore, "He restores my soul" means the Lord brings back the failing life-force, reviving the inner man and refreshing the weary spirit.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Spiritual Repentance: Shûb is the standard Old Testament verb used for turning away from sin and returning to God (often translated as "repent" or "return").

• Physical and Emotional Refreshment: It is used of a person dying of thirst or exhaustion whose spirit is revived when they finally receive nourishment or water.

• Restoration of Fortune: The word describes God returning His people from captivity and restoring their blessings, lands, and joy.

4. Theological Implications

• Divine Initiative: The sheep does not restore itself. The verb is active, and God is the subject. Spiritual restoration is entirely a work of divine grace. God actively seeks out, revives, and reinstates His people.

• Holistic Care: God does not just care for the spiritual "soul" in an abstract sense; He restores the whole person—emotional strength, mental peace, and physical endurance.

• Covenant Faithfulness: The Shepherd's work of restoration is tied to His covenant commitment. He restores the soul so that the believer can continue walking in the "paths of righteousness" for the sake of His holy name.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 23:1-2, the Psalmist describes the ideal conditions of green pastures and still waters. However, even in green pastures, sheep can wander, stumble, or become weary.

• Verse 3 acknowledges this reality. The transition from resting by still waters to being restored indicates that believers will face times of exhaustion, spiritual dryness, or failure.

• The continuous nature of the Polel imperfect verb shows that God's restoration is an ongoing ministry. He does not abandon His sheep when they falter; He continually revives them and redirects their steps.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 19:7 - "The law of the LORD is perfect, converting the soul; The testimony of the LORD is sure, making wise the simple;" (Here, "converting"—from shûb—means reviving or restoring the soul through Scripture).

• Psalm 80:3 - "Restore us, O God; Cause Your face to shine, And we shall be saved!" (The cry for national and spiritual restoration).

• Ruth 4:15 - "And may he be to you a restorer of life and a nourisher of your old age..." (Used of Naomi's grandson restoring her joy and purpose).

• Lamentations 1:11 - "All her people sigh, they seek bread; They have given their valuables for food to restore life." (Using food to bring back physical strength and vitality).

7. Other Relevant Insights

• This Old Testament concept of the Shepherd restoring the wandering sheep is vividly fulfilled in the New Testament. In Luke 15:4-7, Jesus tells the parable of the shepherd who leaves the ninety-nine to search for the one lost sheep until he finds it, laying it on his shoulders and bringing it home.

• In John 21, we see a practical example of Jesus restoring a "cast" soul when He gently reinstates Peter after his three-fold denial, reviving his calling and returning him to the path of service.
