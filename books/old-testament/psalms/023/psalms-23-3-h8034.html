NAME - ( shem , Strong's H8034 )

Summary:

In the Old Testament, a person's name is not merely a label but a revelation of their character, authority, and reputation. In Psalm 23:3, when the Lord leads His sheep "for His name's sake," it means He acts in perfect alignment with His covenant character and guarantees their guidance based on His own divine reputation. God’s commitment to lead His people along righteous paths is rooted in His unshakeable faithfulness to His own glory.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient Near Eastern culture, a name (shem) was deeply connected to a person's existence, identity, and destiny. To have no name was to be non-existent or insignificant. Parents often named their children to reflect their circumstances, physical traits, or a prophetic hope for their future. When applied to God, His name represents His self-revelation. It is the sensory and intellectual access point to His invisible reality. To know God's name is to know His character, His past actions of redemption, and His future promises.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The Hebrew noun shem primarily means "name," but its semantic range extends to "reputation," "fame," "renown," "glory," and "memorial." It denotes the individual identity and the manifestation of a person's character. In relation to God, shem is the sum of His revealed attributes. It functions as a synonym for His presence, power, and glory. When God acts, He acts in accordance with His shem—His established reputation as the holy, sovereign, and merciful Covenant Keeper.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The word shem appears over 800 times in the Old Testament, illustrating several rich concepts:

• Ownership and Authority: To call one’s name over a city, nation, or person indicates ownership and protection. God placed His name upon Israel, marking them as His treasured possession.

• A Strong Tower: The name of Yahweh is pictured as a physical place of safety, a fortified stronghold where the righteous can run and find refuge.

• The Seat of Presence: God chose to put His name in the Tabernacle and later the Temple. While the heavens cannot contain Him, He chose to make His presence accessible through His name.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of shem in the Old Testament is staggering. God’s name is holy and must not be taken in vain. More importantly, God is fiercely protective of His name. When Israel sinned and deserved destruction, God repeatedly spared them "for His name's sake"—meaning He would not allow the surrounding nations to mock His power or call His covenant faithfulness into question. Ultimately, God's actions in salvation and judgment are designed to make His name known throughout the earth, establishing His supreme renown.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 23:3, David writes, "He leads me in the paths of righteousness For His name's sake." This is the ultimate guarantee of the believer's security. A shepherd's reputation in the ancient world depended on his ability to care for his flock. If a shepherd lost his sheep to predators, allowed them to starve, or let them wander off cliffs, his reputation (shem) as a competent shepherd was ruined.

By declaring that Yahweh leads us "for His name's sake," David shifts the ultimate burden of our spiritual success from us to God. God restores our souls and guides us along the right paths not because we are worthy, but because His own character and covenant faithfulness are on the line. He will not fail to guide and protect us, because doing so would compromise His own holy reputation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 8:1 - "O LORD, our Lord, How excellent is Your name in all the earth, Who have set Your glory above the heavens!"

• Psalm 25:11 - "For Your name’s sake, O LORD, Pardon my iniquity, for it is great."

• Psalm 31:3 - "For You are my rock and my fortress; Therefore, for Your name’s sake, Lead me and guide me."

• Proverbs 18:10 - "The name of the LORD is a strong tower; The righteous run to it and are safe."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the New Testament, this Old Testament theology of the divine name reaches its climax in the person of Jesus Christ. Jesus is the ultimate manifestation of God's shem. To pray "in Jesus' name" is not a magical formula to get what we want; rather, it means praying in alignment with His character, His authority, and His reputation. Just as the Father led David "for His name's sake," Jesus, the Good Shepherd, protects and guides His church today to display His own glory and faithfulness to the world.
