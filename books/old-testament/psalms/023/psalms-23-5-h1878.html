ANOINT - ( dashen , Strong's H1878 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb dashen in Psalm 23:5 carries the primary meaning of "to make fat," "to drench," or "to enrich." While translated as "anoint" in this context, it is distinct from the ceremonial anointing (mashach) used for kings and priests. In the Ancient Near East, a host refreshed an honored guest by pouring rich, fragrant oil over their head to soothe dry skin, cool the body, and symbolize joy. In Psalm 23:5, God is depicted as a lavish Host who does not merely supply survival needs but abundantly satisfies, refreshes, and honors His guest, transforming a weary traveler into an esteemed seat-holder at His table.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root meaning of dashen centers on "fatness," which represented prosperity, health, and divine blessing in agricultural societies. In the ancient world, fatness was a desirable state indicating fertility, abundance, and the absence of lack.

• In a shepherd's context, oil was a practical tool used to soothe sheep, protecting them from dry skin, insect bites, and minor wounds.

• In a banquet context, which Psalm 23 transitions to in verse 5, a gracious host welcomed travelers by pouring olive oil mixed with sweet spices over their heads. This gesture cooled the skin after a long journey under the Middle Eastern sun, masked travel odors, and signaled that the guest was highly valued.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The verb dashen means to grow fat, to become prosperous, or to be saturated.

• In the intensive active form (Piel) used in Psalm 23:5 (dishanta), it means to make fat, to enrich, to drench with oil, or to satisfy abundantly.

• It describes a state of being thoroughly moisturized or saturated with oil, indicating a physical manifestation of inner joy, vitality, and honor.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Dashen is used to describe the spiritual and physical prosperity of the righteous, whose souls are "made fat" or deeply satisfied by God's goodness.

• It is used in the context of tabernacle sacrifices to describe clearing away the fat-saturated ashes from the altar, demonstrating that God accepted the richness of the offering.

• The word picture in Psalm 23:5 is one of lavish pouring rather than a symbolic dab. The Shepherd-Host drenches the head of the guest, symbolizing a superabundant bestowing of grace, health, and joy that prepares the guest for the overflowing cup that immediately follows.

4. Theological Implications

• Divine Favor and Honor: To have one's head anointed by God is to receive His public approval and favor. In the presence of enemies, God validates His servant, demonstrating that they are under His protection and care.

• The Abundant Grace of God: God is not a stingy provider. The use of dashen emphasizes that He provides beyond basic survival to the point of luxury, comfort, and deep soul-satisfaction.

• Sustaining Power in Affliction: The anointing takes place in the presence of enemies. This shows that God's spiritual refreshment and inner joy are not dependent on the absence of trouble, but are given in the midst of it.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 23:5, the metaphor shifts from a shepherd tending sheep in the wilderness to a wealthy host entertaining a guest in a secure banquet hall.

• While adversaries look on helplessly, God honors the psalmist. The verb dashen indicates that God treats the psalmist not as a fugitive or a beggar, but as a guest of high status.

• The oil is not for a priestly office or kingly coronation (which would use the Hebrew mashach), but for personal refreshment, comfort, and festive joy. This distinction highlights intimacy and personal relationship rather than official duty.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Proverbs 11:25: "The generous soul will be made rich, And he who waters will also be watered himself."

• Proverbs 13:4: "The soul of a lazy man desires, and has nothing; But the soul of the diligent shall be made rich."

• Proverbs 28:25: "He who is of a proud heart stirs up strife, But he who trusts in the Lord will be prospered."

• Psalm 20:3: "Remember all your offerings, And accept your burnt sacrifice. Selah"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• Contrast with mashach: Mashach (H4886) is the root of Mashiach (Messiah) and is used for official consecration to a ministry or office. Dashen focuses on the subjective experience of being enriched, satisfied, and refreshed by God's abundance.

• The connection between oil and the Holy Spirit is a consistent biblical theme. Oil softens what is hard, heals what is broken, and lights what is dark. The dashen of the head represents the Holy Spirit's work of pouring out joy and comfort upon the believer.
