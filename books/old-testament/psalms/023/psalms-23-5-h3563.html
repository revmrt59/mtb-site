CUP - ( kôs , Strong's H3563 )

Summary:

In Psalm 23:5, the "cup" represents a person's life experiences, portion, or destiny allocated by God. While a cup can symbolize divine judgment or sorrow in other Old Testament contexts, here it denotes overflowing blessing, abundant provision, and lavish divine hospitality. The image of a cup running over depicts a host who does not merely meet basic needs but provides with unmeasured generosity, signifying the believer's secure, joyful, and fully satisfied portion in the presence of the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The literal cup in the ancient Near East was a common household drinking vessel, typically made of clay, earthenware, or metal for wealthier households. In a cultural context marked by arid climates and scarce resources, a cup of clean water or wine was a symbol of life, refreshment, and hospitality.

In ancient hospitality customs, a host showed favor to a guest by keeping their cup filled. To let a guest’s cup run dry was a sign of neglect or a hint that it was time to leave. Conversely, filling a guest's cup to the very brim—and even allowing it to spill over—was a profound demonstration of honor, welcome, and boundless abundance. It signaled to the guest that they were not a burden, but a highly valued and cherished member of the household.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Literally, the noun refers to a drinking vessel or cup. Metaphorically, it represents a person's lot, portion, or share in life, whether that portion consists of blessing or adversity.

In the conceptual framework of the Hebrew Scriptures, God is the one who pours out and hands this cup to humanity. The contents of the cup represent what God has appointed for an individual or nation to experience. In Psalm 23:5, the word is used metaphorically to describe a portion of overwhelming goodness, saturation, and divine favor.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament employs the cup metaphor in two distinct directions:

• The Cup of Blessing and Salvation: This represents joy, fellowship with God, and His abundant provision. It is the portion of those who trust in Yahweh. To receive this cup is to experience the fullness of God's covenant loyalty and care.

• The Cup of Wrath and Judgment: This represents the divine retribution poured out upon the wicked. In this word picture, the wicked are forced to drink the dregs of a cup filled with the wine of God's fury, leading to staggering and ruin.

By presenting the cup in Psalm 23:5 as one that "runs over," the writer emphasizes the extreme opposite of judgment. It is a picture of absolute saturation with the goodness of God.

4. Theological Implications

The theological depth of the overflowing cup centers on the character of God as a lavish Provider. God is not a stingy or reluctant giver. His grace is not measured out with cautious calculation; it is poured out to the point of excess.

This imagery connects deeply with the broader biblical theology of redemption. Ultimately, the believer can enjoy the cup of blessing and salvation because another drank the cup of suffering and wrath. Jesus Christ accepted the cup of divine judgment in the Garden of Gethsemane and on the cross, taking the cup of fury so that His people might eternally inherit a cup that overflows with life and favor.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 23, the literary imagery transitions in verse 5 from a Shepherd guiding His sheep in the wilderness to a wealthy and protective Host throwing a grand banquet for His guest. The setting changes from the rugged outdoors to the safety of a banqueting tent.

The Host prepares a table in the presence of enemies, anoints the guest's head with oil, and fills the cup to overflowing. The presence of enemies suggests danger and hostility, yet the guest is perfectly secure. The overflowing cup indicates that the hostility of enemies cannot diminish or dry up the peace, joy, and provision that God provides. The Hebrew term for "runs over" is revayah, which means saturation, thoroughly wet, or wealthy abundance, reinforcing that the believer's spiritual resources in God are exhaustless.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 16:5 - "O LORD, You are the portion of my inheritance and my cup; You maintain my lot."

• Psalm 75:8 - "For in the hand of the LORD there is a cup, And the wine is red; It is fully mixed, and He pours it out; Surely its dregs shall all the wicked of the earth Drain and drink them down."

• Psalm 116:13 - "I will take up the cup of salvation, And call upon the name of the LORD."

• Isaiah 51:17 - "Awake, awake! Stand up, O Jerusalem, You who have drunk at the hand of the LORD The cup of His fury; You have drunk the dregs of the cup of trembling, And drained it out."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between the "cup of salvation" and the "cup of His fury" highlights the sovereignty of God over human destiny. Every human being eventually drinks from a cup handed to them by God. For the righteous, who find their refuge in the Lord, the cup is filled with salvation and runs over with mercy and goodness that will follow them all the days of their lives.
