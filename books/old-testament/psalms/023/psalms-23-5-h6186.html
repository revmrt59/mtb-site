PREPARE - ( arak , Strong's H6186 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb arak means to arrange, set in order, or lay out in a deliberate, systematic manner. In Psalm 23:5, it portrays Yahweh not merely as a basic provider, but as a generous, meticulous Host who calmly and intentionally prepares a lavish banquet table for His guest. This act of preparation demonstrates absolute security, honor, and abundance directly in the sight of hostile forces, showing that the believer's peace is guaranteed by the sovereign order of God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The primary physical concept of arak is the action of lining things up, arranging items in a row, or putting things in their proper place. It conjures up the image of someone working with order, precision, and intent. In ancient Israelite culture, this word was used in everyday, military, and ceremonial life. It described a priest carefully stacking wood on the sacrificial altar, a general drawing up a battle line of soldiers, or a priest arranging the twelve loaves of showbread on the golden table in the Tabernacle. In every case, arak stands in direct opposition to chaos, haste, or confusion. It is the work of deliberate design.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb arak means to arrange, set in order, equip, or prepare. The semantic range of the word includes:

• To set in order or draw up (such as a battle array or legal arguments).

• To arrange in a row or stack (such as wood, bread, or utensils).

• To compare or estimate (putting items in order to evaluate their relative value).

• To furnish or prepare (specifically a table for a meal).

In the context of a feast, arak refers to the complete setting of a table—arranging the dishes, food, and utensils in an orderly, honorable, and welcoming fashion.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament utilizes arak to paint several vivid pictures of order and preparation:

• The Altar and Sacrifice: In Genesis 22:9, Abraham "laid the wood in order" (arak) on the altar before binding Isaac. The Levitical priesthood was continually commanded to "arrange" the wood and the pieces of the offering on the altar fire (Leviticus 1:7).

• The Tabernacle Bread: The "Table of Showbread" was a central feature of the sanctuary. Every Sabbath, the priests were to "set it in order" (arak) before the Lord (Leviticus 24:8), symbolizing Israel's constant communion and covenant relationship with God.

• The Battle Array: In military contexts, arak describes army commanders drawing up their troops in battle lines, ready for conflict (Genesis 14:8).

• The Ordered Prayer: In Job and the Psalms, arak is used for organizing one's thoughts or legal arguments before God (Job 32:14) and arranging one's prayers in an orderly fashion in the morning (Psalm 5:3).

4. Theological Implications

The use of arak reveals a profound truth about the character of God: He is a God of order, covenant faithfulness, and deliberate care. When God prepares something for His people, it is never a last-minute, panicked reaction.

• Sovereignty over Chaos: While the world or enemies present a scene of hostility and chaos, God responds with systematic, quiet order.

• Covenant Communion: Just as the showbread was set in order (arak) before Yahweh as a sign of continuous covenant communion, God arranging (arak) a table for the psalmist signifies an invitation to deep, covenant fellowship. God Himself serves as the Host, welcoming the believer into His immediate presence.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 23:5, the metaphor of the poem shifts beautifully from Yahweh as the Shepherd in the wilderness to Yahweh as the Host in His tent or palace. The phrase "You prepare a table before me" uses arak to highlight the Host’s absolute calmness and total authority.

• The Contrast of the Enemies: The preparation of the table happens "in the presence of my enemies." Normally, the proximity of enemies produces anxiety, rushed movements, and defensive posturing. Yet, God does not hand the psalmist a hurried ration pack to eat on the run. Instead, He takes the time to arrange (arak) a formal, multi-course banquet.

• A Statement of Security: By arranging the table in plain view of the adversaries, God demonstrates His absolute supremacy. The enemies are forced to stand as helpless spectators, unable to disrupt the feast, because the Sovereign Host has secured the perimeter. The orderly table is a monument of victory and divine protection.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 22:9: "And they came to the place which God had told him of; and Abraham built an altar there, and laid the wood in order, and bound Isaac his son, and laid him on the altar upon the wood."

• Leviticus 24:8: "Every Sabbath he shall set it in order before the LORD continually, being taken from the children of Israel by an everlasting covenant."

• Psalm 5:3: "My voice You shall hear in the morning, O LORD; In the morning I will direct it to You, and I will look up." (Here, "direct" translates arak, meaning to arrange prayer like a morning sacrifice).

• Proverbs 9:2: "She has slaughtered her meat, She has mixed her wine, She has also furnished her table." (Here, "furnished" translates arak, depicting Wisdom arranging her banquet).

7. Other Relevant Insights

In ancient Near Eastern hospitality, to "arrange a table" for someone was an act of high honor, establishing a covenant of peace between the host and the guest. Once a guest ate from the host's table, the host was bound by honor to protect that guest with his very life. Therefore, when Yahweh prepares (arak) this table, He is legally and relationally binding Himself to the psalmist's ongoing protection and survival.
