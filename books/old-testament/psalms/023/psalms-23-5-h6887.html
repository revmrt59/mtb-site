ENEMIES - ( tsar , Strong's H6887 )

Summary:

In Psalm 23:5, the word translated as "enemies" refers to adversaries, foes, or those who press in and cause distress. When David declares that God prepares a table before him in the presence of his enemies, the term captures the reality of relentless opposition and hostility. Yet, the broader biblical picture reveals God’s sovereign power to sustain and honor His people right in the middle of active conflict, turning places of danger into places of divine fellowship and provision.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root verb behind this noun relates to the idea of being narrow, cramped, or tightly constricted.

- Culturally and agriculturally, the ancient shepherd often led his flock through narrow, rocky passes where predators and hostile forces could easily lie in wait.

- The literal imagery is that of being squeezed or pressed hard by an adversary, experiencing pressure from every side with no apparent room to maneuver.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- The noun tsar (or tsar-ah) fundamentally means adversary, enemy, foe, or trouble.

- It denotes someone who exhibits hostility, acts as an opponent, or brings narrow straits and affliction into the life of another.

- It bridges the physical reality of a military or personal opponent with the existential feeling of being cornered and distressed.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The word is frequently used in the Psalms to describe those who maliciously seek the downfall of the righteous through slander, violence, or betrayal.

- It paints a vivid portrait of pressure; the enemy is not merely a distant observer, but one who actively hems the believer in, seeking to restrict their freedom and peace.

- In Psalm 23:5, the presence of the tsar contrasts sharply with the abundance of the cup and the hospitality of the host, highlighting divine protection amid active danger.

4. Theological Implications

- The presence of enemies does not indicate the absence of the Shepherd; rather, the Shepherd proves His faithfulness precisely where the opposition is greatest.

- God's provision in the presence of adversaries demonstrates that ultimate security comes from divine favor, not from the temporary absence of trouble.

- Believers are reminded that earthly hostility cannot annul heavenly hospitality; God provides dignity, sustenance, and honor even while foes look on in frustration.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- Psalm 23 shifts imagery in verse 5 from a pastoral green pasture to a royal banquet hall, where God acts as a generous host.

- Even though the adversaries are actively present, watching and waiting, the believer sits calmly at the table, partaking of an anointed feast.

- The focus rests entirely upon the security provided by the Host, rendering the threats of the enemy utterly powerless to disrupt the fellowship.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Psalm 27:2 - "When the wicked came against me To eat up my flesh, My opponents and foes (tsar), They stumbled and fell."

- Psalm 31:15 - "My times are in Your hand; Deliver me from the hand of my enemies (tsar), And from those who persecute me."

- Psalm 119:157 - "Many are my persecutors and my enemies (tsar), Yet I do not turn from Your testimonies."

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The grammatical form used in Psalm 23:5 emphasizes active, living opposition, showing that the danger is real, current, and perceived by the psalmist.

- The contrast between the menacing pressure of the enemy and the relaxed posture of the guest eating a meal emphasizes the peace that passes human understanding.
