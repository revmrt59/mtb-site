RUNS OVER - ( revayah , Strong's H7310 )

Summary:

The phrase "runs over" translates a rich Hebrew noun that signifies saturation, abundance, and overflowing fulfillment. In Psalm 23:5, David pictures the Lord as a generous host whose provision for His people is not merely adequate, but so overflowing that it spills out beyond measure, symbolizing unmerited favor, deep joy, and total security in the presence of God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The word revayah comes from a root verb (ravah) meaning to drink one's fill, to be satisfied, or to be saturated.

- In ancient Middle Eastern hospitality, a host showed honor to a guest by filling their cup to the brim. When the cup literally overflowed, it communicated that the host's generosity was boundless and that the guest lacked nothing.

- The imagery shifts slightly in Psalm 23 from a shepherd guiding his flock to a host welcoming an honored guest at a banquet table, highlighting the intimacy and lavish care of God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Revayah (noun) means abundance, saturation, or overflowing plenty.

- It denotes a state of being completely satisfied or filled to the utmost limit, leaving no room for lack or thirst.

- Unlike a cup that is merely filled, this cup is in a continuous state of running over, emphasizing an active, ongoing outpouring of divine blessing.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- The term is used to describe the absolute satisfaction that comes from God's provision.

- It paints a vivid mental picture of a goblet filled with wine so full that the liquid spills over the rim, wetting the sides and representing a surplus of joy and blessing.

- It conveys emotional and spiritual abundance: the soul that is deeply satisfied in God experiences a joy that cannot be contained within normal boundaries.

4. Theological Implications

- God's grace and provision are never stingy; He gives abundantly beyond what we ask or think.

- The overflowing cup points forward to the spiritual abundance found in the New Covenant through Christ, where believers receive "of His fullness, and grace for grace" (John 1:16).

- True contentment is not found in external circumstances, but in the reality that the Lord Himself is our portion, resulting in a life that overflows with thanksgiving.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 23:5, "My cup runs over" stands in stark contrast to the enemies mentioned in the first half of the verse ("You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies"). Even in the presence of conflict and danger, God grants a peace and provision so profound that the believer's experience is defined by triumph and joy rather than fear.

- The overflowing cup is the climax of the shepherd-host's care, following the restored soul, the paths of righteousness, the valley of the shadow of death, and the comforting rod and staff.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Psalm 66:12 - "You have caused men to ride over our heads; We went through fire and through water; But You brought us out to rich [revayah] fulfillment." (Note: translated as "rich fulfillment" or abundance, capturing the sense of being brought out into a place of complete satisfaction).

- Isaiah 34:5 - "For My sword shall be bathed in heaven; Indeed it shall come down on Edom, and on the people of My curse, for judgment." (Using the verbal root to describe a sword saturated or drenched in blood).

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint) translates this phrase with a word meaning "how excellent [or intoxicating] it is," emphasizing the sheer delight of the experience.

- The verse does not say "Your cup runs over," but "My cup," showing the deeply personal nature of God's blessing in the life of the believer.
