TABLE - ( shulchan , Strong's H7979 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word shulchan refers to a table, spread, or place of dining, shifting the imagery of Psalm 23 from a shepherd guiding his sheep in the wilderness to a generous host welcoming an honored guest into his home. To have a table prepared by God signifies covenant relationship, intimate fellowship, provision, and absolute security. In the ancient Near East, eating at someone's table established a bond of loyalty and mutual protection, meaning that God Himself guarantees the safety and honor of the psalmist even when surrounded by adversaries.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

Historically, shulchan is derived from a root meaning to stretch out or send forth. In early nomadic Semitic culture, a "table" was not always a wooden piece of furniture with legs. Instead, it was often a circular skin, mat, or leather sheet spread out on the ground to hold a meal. As Israel transitioned to settled life, the term came to represent formal wooden tables, such as those used in royal palaces or the Tabernacle. In both contexts, the word picture represents a designated space of communal sharing, covenantal peace, and sustenance.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun shulchan denotes a table or a spread used for eating. It describes:

• A physical table used for ordinary daily meals in a home.

• The royal dining table of a king, signifying high status, favor, and political alliance.

• The sacred Table of Showbread (or "Table of the Presence") in the Tabernacle and Temple, where twelve loaves of bread were set before the Lord continually.

• Metaphorically, the spiritual provision of fellowship and nourishment provided by God.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

In the Old Testament, shulchan is used over 70 times, carrying deep relational weight. To "eat at the table" of a king was the highest honor, representing adoption, reconciliation, and provision. A prominent example is Mephibosheth, the crippled son of Jonathan, whom King David brought to his own table to eat "as one of the king's sons." Conversely, a polluted or despised table symbolized a broken covenant and spiritual rebellion, as seen in the post-exilic prophetic warnings where priests offered defiled food on the altar, treating the "table of the Lord" with contempt.

4. Theological Implications

The table represents the ultimate expression of hospitality and covenant in biblical theology. To share a meal was to share life. In Psalm 23:5, God does not merely feed the psalmist from a distance; He invites him to sit down as an intimate companion. This denotes:

• Reconciliation: The guest is no longer an outsider or an enemy, but a friend of the Host.

• Royal Favor: The believer is treated like royalty, receiving the finest portions from the King's own kitchen.

• Covenant Security: By hosting the meal, God binds Himself to the protection of His guest. To harm the guest would be an assault on the Host's own honor.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 23:5, the setting of the "table" is remarkably paradoxical: "in the presence of my enemies." Ordinarily, one does not sit down to a leisurely, multi-course feast in the middle of a battlefield or while surrounded by hostile forces. In times of danger, soldiers eat in haste, on their guard. However, God prepares a table—a term implying careful, orderly arrangement and deliberate abundance—right in front of those who wish harm to the psalmist. The enemies are rendered powerless, forced to stand at the perimeter and watch as God honors, feeds, and protects His servant. It demonstrates that our peace and security are not dependent on the absence of conflict, but on the presence of the Host.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 25:23 - "You shall also make a table of acacia wood; two cubits shall be its length, a cubit its width, and a cubit and a half its height."

• 2 Samuel 9:7 - "So David said to him, 'Do not fear, for I will surely show you kindness for Jonathan your father's sake, and will restore to you all the land of Saul your grandfather; and you shall eat bread at my table continually.'"

• Malachi 1:7 - "You offer defiled food on My altar, but say, 'In what way have we defiled You?' By saying, 'The table of the Lord is contemptible.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The concept of the shulchan reaches its ultimate fulfillment in the New Testament. Jesus Christ, the Good Shepherd, spent much of His ministry dining with sinners, tax collectors, and His disciples, serving as the ultimate Host. This Old Testament word picture points directly to:

• The Lord's Supper (1 Corinthians 10:21), where believers participate in the "table of the Lord."

• The promise of future fellowship in the kingdom of God, where believers will "eat and drink at My table" (Luke 22:30).

• The Marriage Supper of the Lamb (Revelation 19:9), the final, eternal table prepared for all who belong to the Shepherd.
