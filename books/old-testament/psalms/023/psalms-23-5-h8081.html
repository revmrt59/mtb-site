OIL - ( shemen , Strong's H8081 )

Summary:

The Hebrew term shemen primarily denotes olive oil, which served as a staple commodity in the ancient Near East for food, light, medicine, and ceremonial consecration. In Psalm 23:5, David uses the rich imagery of anointing the head with oil to picture God's abundant hospitality, honor, and comforting presence bestowed upon His people even in the presence of enemies.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- Derived from a root meaning to shine, gleam, or be fat, shemen points to the glistening appearance of freshly applied olive oil.

- In ancient nomadic and pastoral life, olive oil was blended with aromatic spices and poured over guests as a supreme gesture of welcome, honor, and refreshment after a dusty journey.

- Shepherds routinely applied a mixture of oil and sulfur to the heads of sheep to protect them from insects, parasites, and the abrasions caused by thick brambles.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Shemen refers literally to oil, fat, or ointment.

- It encompasses physical liquid pressed from olives, rich food products, and perfumed cosmetic or medicinal unguents.

- Metaphorically, it conveys ideas of richness, abundance, gladness, and divine favor.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- Culinary and Medicinal: Used for cooking, healing wounds, and soothing dry or damaged skin (Isaiah 1:6, Luke 10:34).

- Cosmetic and Social: Applied to the face and hair for daily grooming and festive occasions to bring a healthy glow and pleasant fragrance (Ruth 3:3, Psalm 104:15).

- Consecration: Poured generously over priests, prophets, and kings to signify the empowering and anointing presence of the Holy Spirit for service (Exodus 29:7, 1 Samuel 16:13).

4. Theological Implications

- The anointing oil is a tangible sign of divine selection, joy, and the indwelling Holy Spirit.

- God’s provision of oil demonstrates His generous, overflowing grace—not merely supplying bare necessities, but bestowing royal dignity and comfort upon the believer.

- Spiritual refreshing comes directly from the Good Shepherd, who heals our wounds and gladdens our hearts in times of trial.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Psalm 23:5 ("You anoint my head with oil"), the setting shifts from a green pasture to the banquet table of a generous host.

- The anointing signifies that the believer is welcomed not as an unwelcome intruder, but as an honored guest receiving the highest respect.

- The overflowing cup and the anointed head together portray a life sustained and celebrated by God, transcending earthly hostility.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Psalm 45:7: "You love righteousness and hate wickedness; Therefore God, Your God, has anointed You With the oil of gladness more than Your companions."

- Psalm 104:15: "And wine that makes glad the heart of man, Oil to make his face shine, and bread which strengthens man’s heart."

- Amos 6:6: "Who drink wine from bowls, And anoint themselves with the best ointments, But are not grieved for the affliction of Joseph."

- Micah 6:7: "Will the Lord be pleased with thousands of rams, Ten thousand rivers of oil? Shall I give my firstborn for my transgression, The fruit of my body for the sin of my soul?"

7. Other Relevant Insights

- Olive oil required pressing, crushing, and refining through heavy labor, reminding believers that spiritual fruitfulness and comfort often emerge through trials.

- The contrast between the sparse wilderness and the overflowing horn of plenty highlights the dramatic transformation God brings to the life of His dependent children.
