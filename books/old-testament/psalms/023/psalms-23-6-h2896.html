GOODNESS - ( tôb , Strong's H2896 )

Summary:

In Psalm 23:6, "goodness" (ṭôb) refers to the active, tangible, and abundant benevolence of God. Rather than a passive attribute, it is a dynamic force of God's character that actively pursues the believer. Paired with "mercy" (ḥesed), it represents the covenant-keeping God of Israel providing all that is beneficial, beautiful, and life-giving for His sheep.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew root ṭ-b carries the primary idea of being pleasant, delightful, beautiful, useful, or morally excellent. It encompasses both aesthetic beauty and functional excellence. In the pastoral context of the ancient Near East, "goodness" for a flock meant green pastures, clean water, safety from predators, and a competent shepherd. It is the sensory experience of a well-ordered, flourishing life under the care of a benevolent provider.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The Hebrew word ṭôb functions as an adjective, noun, and adverb. In its noun form, it denotes prosperity, well-being, beauty, gladness, and moral excellence. It signifies that which is beneficial, agreeable, and right, standing in direct contrast to that which is evil or harmful (ra'). It represents the absolute standard of God's character and His generous actions toward His creation.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Creation Alignment: ṭôb is first used in Genesis 1, where God pronounces His creation "good." This establishes the word as the perfect alignment of creation with God’s design and purpose.

• The Land of Promise: It describes the "good land" of Canaan (Exodus 3:8), rich with resources, nourishment, and rest.

• The Pursuing Hound of Heaven: The word picture in Psalm 23:6 is striking because of the verb it pairs with: "shall follow" (rādap̄). This is a strong verb that almost always means "to pursue," "to chase," or "to persecute" in a hostile sense, such as an army chasing its enemies. David flips this military term on its head. Instead of enemies or judgment hunting the psalmist, God's active goodness and loyal love chase him down relentlessly.

4. Theological Implications

• The Character of God: God is the source and definition of all goodness. His goodness is not an abstract concept but is historically demonstrated through covenant faithfulness.

• Sovereign Provision: Because God is good, His actions are inherently good. The believer's life is framed not by random fate, hostile forces, or luck, but by the relentless pursuit of God's beneficial provisions.

• Covenant Security: The pairing of ṭôb (goodness) and ḥesed (steadfast love/mercy) summarizes the entire covenant relationship. Goodness supplies our needs; mercy forgives our failures.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 23, David walks through green pastures, still waters, paths of righteousness, and the valley of the shadow of death. In verse 6, looking back at his life and forward to eternity, David concludes with "Surely" ('aḵ—an expression of absolute certainty). While David’s enemies pursued him throughout his life (such as Saul and Absalom), he declares that the true, ultimate pursuers of his life are God's goodness and mercy. The Shepherd does not just lead from the front; His attributes guard the flock from behind, ensuring no sheep is left behind to wander or perish.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 34:8: "Oh, taste and see that the LORD is good; Blessed is the man who trusts in Him!"

• Psalm 86:5: "For You, Lord, are good, and ready to forgive, And abundant in mercy to all those who call upon You."

• Psalm 100:5: "For the LORD is good; His mercy is everlasting, And His truth endures to all generations."

• Psalm 145:9: "The LORD is good to all, And His tender mercies are over all His works."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament), the translators often rendered ṭôb as agathos (morally good, beneficial) or chrestos (kind, useful). In Psalm 23:6 (Psalm 22:6 in the Septuagint text), the translation emphasizes the kindness and mercy of God following the believer. This highlights that God's goodness is not merely a passive quality to be admired, but an active, kind, and useful grace that meets the practical needs of daily life.
