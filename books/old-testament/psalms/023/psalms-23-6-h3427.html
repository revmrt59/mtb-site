DWELL - ( yashab , Strong's H3427 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yashab fundamentally means to sit down, remain, abide, or dwell. It denotes a transition from wandering or journeying to settling down in a state of rest, security, and permanent residence. In Psalm 23:6, this word expresses the psalmist's supreme confidence and longing not just for a temporary visit, but for a permanent, life-long, and eternal dwelling in the immediate presence and household of Yahweh. It highlights the ultimate destination of the believer: transitioning from a sheep guided along a dangerous path to an honored family member residing forever in the Shepherd’s home.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete, physical background of yashab is the act of sitting down. In ancient Near Eastern culture, to sit down in a place was to establish a presence, claim a position, or find rest. It stands in contrast to walking, running, or wandering. When a nomadic traveler yashab, they ceased their journey, pitched their tent, and settled into a home. The word also carries connotations of sitting on a throne, depicting authority, stability, and established rule. Therefore, the word picture behind "dwelling" is one of settled peace, permanent belonging, and secure rest in a established place.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Yashab possesses a rich semantic range that adapts to its context:

• To sit or sit down: Refers to the physical posture of sitting on the ground, a seat, or a throne.

• To remain, stay, or tarry: Indicates staying in a location for a period of time rather than passing through.

• To dwell or inhabit: Refers to making a place one's permanent home or residence.

• To sit enthroned: Used frequently of kings, judges, and God Himself sitting in sovereign authority.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, yashab is used to paint several vivid pictures:

• The Enthroned King: It describes God sitting enthroned over the flood or in the heavens, highlighting His uncontested sovereignty and peace.

• The Settled Family: It portrays family members sitting around a table or dwelling together in a shared inheritance, emphasizing covenant community and intimacy.

• The Sanctuary: It describes residing in the tabernacle or temple, which represents living in direct communion with the manifest presence of God.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, yashab touches on the ultimate goal of God's redemptive plan: covenant fellowship. God does not merely want to rescue His people; He wants to dwell with them, and He wants them to dwell with Him. This word represents the shift from the wilderness wanderings to the Promised Land—a place of rest. In the Psalms, dwelling in God's house is the highest expression of joy, safety, and spiritual satisfaction. It implies that the believer is no longer an outsider or a servant, but a child of the household who has a permanent seat at the Father's table.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 23:6, David declares, "And I will dwell in the house of the LORD Forever." The context of Psalm 23 transitions from a sheep following a shepherd in the wilderness (verses 1-4) to a guest enjoying a banquet in the host's secure house (verses 5-6).

While the Masoretic text utilizes a verbal form (wesabtî) that some grammatical traditions connect to shub ("to return"), the ancient translations (such as the Septuagint) and the immediate parallel of "the house of the LORD" strongly support the meaning of yashab ("to dwell"). David is expressing a double truth: he will return to the sanctuary of God continually, and he will make his permanent, eternal home there. The phrase "house of the LORD" refers to the place of God's presence. To "dwell" there means to live in uninterrupted communion with God, protected by His covenant love, for the remainder of his earthly days and stretching into eternity.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 13:12 – "Abram dwelt in the land of Canaan, and Lot dwelt in the cities of the plain and pitched his tent even as far as Sodom."

• Psalm 1:1 – "Blessed is the man Who walks not in the counsel of the ungodly, Nor stands in the path of sinners, Nor sits in the seat of the scornful;"

• Psalm 2:4 – "He who sits in the heavens shall laugh; The Lord shall hold them in derision."

• Psalm 91:1 – "He who dwells in the secret place of the Most High Shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty."

• Psalm 133:1 – "Behold, how good and how pleasant it is For brethren to dwell together in unity!"

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a beautiful wordplay in the Hebrew text of Psalm 23:6. The word translated "dwell" (wesabtî) sounds almost identical to the Hebrew word for "return" (shub). This intentional ambiguity suggests that the believer's life is a constant cycle of "returning" to the Lord's presence and "dwelling" there. Every time the trials of life scatter us, the Good Shepherd brings us back to sit down in His presence. Furthermore, the phrase "forever" is literally "for length of days," pointing to a life filled with God's presence today, which naturally flows into eternal dwelling in the life to come.
