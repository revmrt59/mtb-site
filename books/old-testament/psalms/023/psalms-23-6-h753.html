FOREVER - ( 'orek , Strong's H753 )

Summary:

In Psalm 23:6, the English word "forever" translates the Hebrew idiomatic phrase le'orek yamim, which literally means "for length of days." The key word 'orek refers to physical length, extension, or duration. When paired with "days" (yamim), it denotes a prolonged, uninterrupted period of time, stretching throughout the span of one's earthly life and expanding into the boundless horizon of eternity in the presence of God. It expresses the psalmist's deep assurance of permanent communion with the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root verb 'arak means "to be long" or "to prolong." 'Orek is the masculine noun derived from this root, representing "length" or "duration." In the ancient Near East, spatial and temporal concepts were closely linked. To measure "length" ('orek) was to measure physical distance, but when applied to time, it measured the unfolding journey of life. The phrase "length of days" was a standard idiom for a long, blessed life, but in the context of Yahweh's covenant presence, it transcends mere biological survival, pointing to an unbroken, eternal relationship with God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Physical Dimensions: 'Orek frequently refers to physical length, such as the dimensions of Noah's Ark, the Tabernacle curtains, or the temple structures.

• Temporal Duration: When applied to time, the word signifies duration, lifetime, or future extension.

• The Idiomatic Compound: Combined as le'orek yamim (literally "to the length of days"), the expression functions as an idiom meaning "forever," "for all time," or "throughout a long life." While some Old Testament contexts use the phrase to denote longevity on earth, the sanctuary setting of Psalm 23 elevates its meaning to represent permanent, unending residence in God's presence.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Path of Life: The word picture behind 'orek is one of walking down a path that does not end; the "length of days" is a road that stretches beyond the visible horizon into the eternal sanctuary.

• Wisdom's Reward: In the Wisdom literature, "length of days" is personified as a gift held in the right hand of wisdom, symbolizing a life that is full, rich, and prolonged under divine favor.

• The King's Decree: It is also used in royal contexts where kings are granted "length of days" to secure their dynasties, a temporal blessing that foreshadows the eternal reign of the Messiah.

4. Theological Implications

• Eternal Security: The believer's fellowship with God is not temporary or conditional. The Shepherd who guides through the "valley of the shadow of death" ensures safe arrival and permanent residence in His house.

• Covenant Continuity: The phrase underscores the Hebrew concept of chesed (unfailing covenant love). Because God's mercy is everlasting, the believer's dwelling in His presence must match that duration.

• The Heavenly Sanctuary: While "the house of the LORD" initially referred to the Tabernacle or Temple, the theological trajectory of the text points to the ultimate dwelling place of God, where His people will dwell in unbroken fellowship eternally.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Psalm 23:6, David contrasts "all the days of my life" (temporal earthly existence) in the first half of the verse with "forever" (le'orek yamim, length of days) in the second half.

• This structure sets up a beautiful progression: God's goodness and mercy pursue the believer through every temporal earthly day, culminating in an eternal home where time is no longer measured by fleeting days but by the infinite "length of days" in the Father's house.

• The verb "dwell" (which can also be translated "return" or "settle") combined with le'orek yamim indicates a permanent homecoming. The guest does not merely visit; the sheep remains in the Shepherd's home permanently.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Deuteronomy 30:20: "...that you may love the LORD your God, that you may obey His voice, and that you may cling to Him, for He is your life and the length of your days; and that you may dwell in the land which the LORD swore to your fathers..."

• Psalm 21:4: "He asked life from You, and You gave it to him— Length of days forever and ever."

• Proverbs 3:16: "Length of days is in her right hand, In her left hand riches and honor."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Greek Septuagint (LXX) translates le'orek yamim in Psalm 23:6 as eis makrotēta hēmerōn ("into length of days").

• This Hebrew idiom avoids a cold, abstract term for "eternity" (like some philosophical systems), choosing instead a warm, relational term. It portrays eternity not as the absence of time, but as an endless succession of days filled with the life-giving presence of the Lord.

• In John 14:1-3, Jesus mirrors this imagery when He promises to prepare a place in His "Father's house" so that His disciples may be where He is, fulfilling the ultimate hope of Psalm 23:6.
