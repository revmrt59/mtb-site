PEOPLES - (ʼummah, Strong's H523)

peoples - ( ummah , H0523 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun ummah refers to a tribe, clan, people group, or nation bound together by shared lineage or heritage. In Psalm 117:1, the word appears in the plural form to call every distinct Gentile ethnic group and nation across the globe to join in praising the LORD for His enduring merciful kindness and truth.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

In the Old Testament, human society is described through various social groupings, ranging from immediate families and extended clans to entire nations. The word ummah designates a distinct tribe, clan, or people group linked by common ancestral descent, culture, or geographic origin. While other common terms often emphasize political statehood or massive population, this word highlights tribal identity and corporate ethnic groups.

In the poetic structure of the Psalms, ummah often pairs with other national descriptions to embrace foreign populations outside Israel. In Psalm 117:1, it works alongside the general term for foreign nations to ensure that no tribal entity, culture, or language group is omitted from the call to worship the living God.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word can refer to a tribe, a family clan, a distinct ethnic group, or a foreign nation.

When applied to family lines, it points to extended tribal divisions within a larger ethnic group. When used in broad poetic contexts, it designates the distinct national and ethnic groups of the world. In Psalm 117:1, the plural form refers to all the non-Israelite tribes and peoples of the earth, addressing them directly as subjects who owe praise to the creator God.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

The word appears in both historical narrative and Old Testament poetry. In early historical accounts, it describes tribal structures and leaders. For example, it describes the Ishmaelites and Midianites, where it marks tribal units descended from a common chieftain or ancestral head.

In the Psalms, the word transitions from describing specific patriarchal clans to encompassing all foreign ethnic groups. In poetic song, the word emphasizes that Israel's God is not merely a regional deity, but the rightful sovereign over every ethnic community on earth. Whether foreign groups oppose God's people or are summoned to praise Him, the word marks them as distinct human groups under God's ultimate reign.

4. Theological Implications

The call in Psalm 117 for all "peoples" to praise the LORD reveals the global scope of God's redemptive plan. Although God chose Israel as His covenant nation, His salvation was never intended to remain strictly within Israel's physical borders. From the beginning, God's goal was to bring blessing to every family and ethnic line on earth.

The Apostle Paul quotes Psalm 117:1 in Romans 15:11 to prove that the inclusion of Gentiles into the church was explicitly foretold in the Old Testament Scriptures. By inviting every ethnic group to praise God for His merciful kindness and truth, the passage grounds global mission in the character of God Himself. God's mercy is broad enough to cover every language and tribe, and His truth endures forever for every nation that trusts in Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Psalm 117 is the shortest chapter in the entire Bible, yet its vision is as vast as the human race. The psalm consists of a direct command to worship in verse 1, followed by the theological foundation for that worship in verse 2.

Verse 1 uses poetic parallelism to issue a universal invitation: "Praise the LORD, all you Gentiles! Laud Him, all you peoples!" The first line addresses the nations broadly, while the second line uses ummah in the plural ("peoples") to address every specific tribal and cultural group within those nations. The surrounding context shows that worship is not limited to Israel; every human community is called to exalt the LORD. The reason given in verse 2—God's overwhelming lovingkindness and faithful truth—provides the universal gospel truth that unites all diverse ethnic groups in one voice of praise.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 25:16 — "These were the sons of Ishmael... twelve princes according to their nations."

  • Numbers 25:15 — "Zur; he was head of the people of a tribal family in Midian; a leader of a people."

  • Psalms 57:9 — "I will praise You, O Lord, among the peoples; I will sing to You among the nations."

  • Psalms 149:7 — "To execute vengeance on the nations, and punishments on the peoples."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Understanding ummah as a term for ethnic clans helps illuminate the worship of the New Testament church. In the Book of Revelation, the redeemed choir surrounding the throne of God is described as coming from every tribe, tongue, people, and nation. The OT background of calling every tribal clan (ummah) to praise the LORD finds its ultimate fulfillment in the gathered, multi-ethnic church praising the Lamb who was slain.
