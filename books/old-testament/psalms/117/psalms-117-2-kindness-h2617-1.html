KINDNESS - (chêçêd, Strong's H2617)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun hesed refers lexically to kindness, favor, loyal affection, or merciful devotion. Within biblical narrative and poetry, it frequently designates the faithful commitment shown within personal relationships and covenantal bonds. In Psalm 117:2, hesed functions as the primary rationale for universal praise, asserting that God’s steadfast covenant loyalty toward His people is overwhelmingly powerful, providing even the Gentile nations a compelling reason to extol YHWH.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The noun hesed is one of the foundational terms in Old Testament theology, appearing frequently in narrative, wisdom literature, and prophetic texts. Etymologically, it denotes an active expression of goodness or devotion that maintains a relationship. In Ancient Near Eastern social contexts, acts of hesed were often rendered between family members, allies, or rulers and subjects, where one party extended kindness or assistance beyond mere obligation.

In the Hebrew Bible, hesed moves beyond cultural customs of reciprocal hospitality to express God's loyal love and steadfast commitment to His people. While human expressions of hesed can be temporary or contingent, God's hesed is grounded in His divine character and promises, making it a reliable foundation for Israel's hope and worship.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, hesed carries a semantic range that includes kindness, loyal love, favor, mercy, and devotion. Standard Hebrew lexicons (such as Brown-Driver-Briggs and TWOT) define hesed as goodness, kindness, or loyal affection.

While the core lexical meaning is simply "kindness" or "favor," its rich biblical application often elevates the term to describe covenantal faithfulness. Lexicographers distinguish between the basic lexical layer—an act or disposition of kindness and favor—and the theological layer, where context shapes the word to describe God's unwavering, covenant-bound mercy toward His people.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Hebrew Scriptures, hesed appears in a wide variety of settings to depict relational loyalty and divine favor:

  • Relational Loyalty between Humans: Human beings show hesed to one another through acts of loyalty, rescue, or kindness that honor shared commitments or family ties.

  • Redemptive Deliverance: God exhibits hesed by intervening to save His people from bondage, enemies, or distress, demonstrating that His mercy is active rather than passive.

  • Covenant Faithfulness: In covenantal contexts, hesed highlights God's unyielding devotion to His solemn promises despite the failures of human partners.

  • Enduring Quality: The term often pairs with "truth" or "faithfulness" ('emet), emphasizing that divine kindness is not a fleeting emotion but an unshakeable reality.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, hesed reveals the character of YHWH as a God who chooses to bind Himself to a people in gracious love. His hesed is not earned by human merit; rather, it originates in His sovereign grace.

In canonical context, hesed provides the ultimate assurance for God's redeemed community. Because God's hesed persists through judgment and trial, believers can approach Him with confidence, knowing His mercy is renewed and unshakeable. Furthermore, in the broader redemptive history, God's hesed extends beyond the boundaries of Israel to embrace all nations, fulfilling the Abrahamic promise that all families of the earth would be blessed through His mercy.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 117—the shortest psalm in the Psalter—the psalmist issues a universal call for all nations and peoples to praise YHWH (v. 1). Verse 2 provides the twin grounds for this universal summons: God's hesed ("kindness") and His 'emet ("truth" or "faithfulness").

The target occurrence of hesed in Psalm 117:2 is grammatically a noun in the singular number and common gender. Syntax and context combine to emphasize the magnitude of this divine attribute: the psalmist declares that God's kindness "is great toward us." The verb paired with hesed here signifies being strong, overwhelming, or prevailing.

Contextually, the phrase "toward us" refers directly to God's covenant people, yet this manifestation of divine loyalty is set as the reason for Gentile nations ("all you Gentiles... all you peoples") to burst into praise. The passage reveals that God's faithful devotion to His covenant community is not an exclusive privilege to be hoarded, but a public demonstration of His glorious character meant to draw all nations into worship. By witnessing God's prevailing hesed, the nations learn who YHWH is and are invited to share in His praise.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 15:13 – "You in Your mercy have led forth the people whom You have redeemed; You have guided them in Your strength to Your holy habitation."

  • 1 Kings 3:6 – "And Solomon said: 'You have shown Your servant David my father great kindness, because he walked before You in truth, in righteousness, and in uprightness of heart with You...'"

  • Psalms 89:2 – "For I have said, 'Mercy shall be built up forever; Your faithfulness You shall establish in the very heavens.'"

  • Psalms 136:1 – "Oh, give thanks to the LORD, for He is good! For His mercy endures forever."

  • Isaiah 16:5 – "In mercy the throne will be established; and One will sit on it in truth, in the tabernacle of David, judging and seeking justice and hastening righteousness."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Paul's letter to the Romans (Romans 15:8–11), the apostle explicitly quotes Psalm 117:1 to demonstrate that the inclusion of the Gentiles into the church was always part of God's redemptive plan. Paul argues that Christ came to confirm the promises made to the patriarchs so that the Gentiles might glorify God for His mercy. Thus, the prevailing hesed celebrated in Psalm 117:2 finds its ultimate realization in the Gospel, where Jew and Gentile alike are united in praising God for His redeeming grace through Jesus Christ.
