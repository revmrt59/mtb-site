LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

Lexically, YHWH (transliterated as Yahweh) is the proper personal name of the God of Israel, derived from a root verb signifying existence or presence ("to be"). In Psalm 117:2, this covenant name identifies the divine subject whose enduring truth and faithfulness (ʾĕmet) ground the psalm's universal call for all gentiles and nations to praise Him. By attributing everlasting reliability to Yahweh, the author demonstrates that Israel's covenant God is the rightful object of worldwide worship because His personal character guarantees His enduring promises to all peoples.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

YHWH is the unshared, personal name of the God of Israel. In classical Hebrew, the four consonants (the Tetragrammaton) are closely associated with the root hāyāh (or its older variant hāwāh), meaning "to be" or "to exist." When God revealed His name to Moses at the burning bush, He connected this name with the phrase ʾehyeh ʾăšer ʾehyeh ("I AM WHO I AM"), establishing that His name denotes dynamic existence, presence, and active self-manifestation.

In the canonical narrative, YHWH is not merely an abstract title like ʾĔlōhîm ("God") or ʾĂdōnāy ("Lord, Master"). Rather, YHWH is the specific personal name by which the Creator entered into covenant relationship with Abraham and his descendants. Out of reverent awe and to avoid taking the divine name in vain, Jewish tradition substituted the spoken word ʾĂdōnāy whenever YHWH appeared in the text, a tradition reflected in English translations by printing the title as "LORD" in small capital letters.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, YHWH functions as a proper noun designating the one true God who revealed Himself to Israel.

  • Lexical sense: Proper personal name of the God of Israel ("Yahweh").

  • Etymological connection: Derived from the verb root signifying "to be" or "to exist," denoting One who actively exists and reveals Himself in history.

Care must be taken to distinguish between the lexical definition of the proper name itself and the broader canonical and systematic theology attached to God's character across Scripture. Lexically, the word identifies a specific divine Person; the full depth of His eternal nature, immutability, and covenant faithfulness is revealed through His historical actions, direct declarations, and literary contexts.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Old Testament, YHWH distinguishes the true God from the regional deities of surrounding nations. While pagan nations possessed local gods with distinct names, Israel's God revealed Himself as YHWH, the absolute sovereign over all creation and all history.

The usage of YHWH frequently highlights His personal agency, covenant initiatives, and moral character. It appears in narrative texts describing creation and covenant formation, in prophetic declarations of judgment and redemption, and in poetic worship. When paired with divine attributes, the name YHWH serves as the anchor for Israel's assurance that God's character is consistent, trustworthy, and unchanging.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, the personal name YHWH stands at the center of biblical revelation. By making His proper name known, God demonstrated His willingness to enter into intimate, reliable covenantal relationships with human beings.

Furthermore, YHWH is inherently connected to God's faithfulness to His word. Because YHWH is the living God whose existence is independent of creation, His promises carry absolute certainty. In canonical context, the character revealed in the name YHWH reaches its fullest expression in Jesus Christ, through whom God's ultimate salvation and covenant faithfulness are extended beyond Israel to all nations of the earth.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 117:2, YHWH appears in the short two-verse psalm that forms a concise liturgical call to worship. Verse 1 commands all gentiles and nations to praise YHWH. Verse 2 provides the twin grounds for this universal summons: "For His merciful kindness is great toward us, and the truth of the LORD [YHWH] endures forever."

The grammatical function of YHWH in this verse identifies the exact source of enduring truth (ʾĕmet). The parallelism pairs God's covenant mercy (ḥesed) with the enduring faithfulness (ʾĕmet) of YHWH. The author uses the personal name YHWH to emphasize that the universal worship demanded of all nations is not offered to a vague, distant deity, but to the specific, self-revealed God who bound Himself to His word. The truth of YHWH is declared to be everlasting, establishing that His commitment to His covenant purposes will never fail or expire. Consequently, the call for global praise in Psalm 117 rests securely on the unchanging reliability of YHWH.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 – "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens."

  • Exodus 13:5 – "And it shall be, when the LORD has brought you into the land of the Canaanites... which He swore to your fathers to give you... that you shall keep this service in this month."

  • Psalms 24:10 – "Who is this King of glory? The LORD of hosts, He is the King of glory."

  • Isaiah 43:16 – "Thus says the LORD, who makes a way in the sea and a path through the mighty waters."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The authoritative grammatical classification for YHWH in Psalm 117:2 identifies it as a non-inflectional proper noun. This grammatical reality underscores that YHWH operates as an unalterable personal name rather than a descriptive adjective or inflected verbal form.

The canonical significance of Psalm 117 and the name YHWH is explicitly highlighted in the New Testament. In Romans 15:11, the Apostle Paul cites Psalm 117:1 to demonstrate that the inclusion of the gentiles in God's salvation plan was always part of God's covenant intent. The eternal truth of YHWH declared in Psalm 117:2 finds its ultimate fulfillment as gentiles alongside Jews glorify the one true God for His mercy in Jesus Christ.
