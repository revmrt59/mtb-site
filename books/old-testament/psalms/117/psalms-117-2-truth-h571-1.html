TRUTH - (ʼemeth, Strong's H571)

Summary:

The Hebrew noun 'emet denotes firmness, trustworthiness, constancy, reliability, and objective truth. It describes that which is stable, dependable, and true to reality or promise. In Psalm 117:2, 'emet refers to YHWH's unshakeable covenant reliability and absolute truthfulness, which endures forever. Paired with God's merciful kindness (chesed), it provides the foundational ground for the psalm's command that all nations and gentiles praise YHWH.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The noun 'emet is a foundational covenant term in Old Testament theology. Lexically, standard Hebrew references such as Brown-Driver-Briggs (BDB) and the Theological Wordbook of the Old Testament (TWOT) connect 'emet to concepts of stability, certainty, and fidelity.

In ancient Israelite thought, truth was not merely an abstract philosophical category concerning factual statements; it was deeply relational and ethical. Something that possesses 'emet is reliable, valid, and consistent over time. When applied to speech, 'emet signifies truthfulness as opposed to falsehood; when applied to character or covenant relationships, it signifies trustworthiness, faithfulness, and dependability.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, 'emet carries a semantic range encompassing:

  • Firmness, stability, and permanence.

  • Trustworthiness, dependability, and faithfulness in conduct or character.

  • Truth, correctness, and conformity to fact in speech, judgment, or revelation.

The term establishes a contrast with that which is deceptive, transient, or false. While English translations often render 'emet as either "truth" or "faithfulness" depending on context, the underlying Hebrew noun integrates both dimensions: God's truth is inherently faithful, and His faithfulness is completely true.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across Old Testament literature, 'emet functions in several distinct nuances:

  • Divine Attribute: It frequently characterizes God's nature, indicating that YHWH is utterly dependable, incapable of deceit, and unwavering in His commitments.

  • Relational Integrity: In human interactions, 'emet characterizes trustworthy individuals, honest witnesses, and leaders who act with integrity rather than dishonesty.

  • Veracity of Speech and Word: It describes words, judgments, laws, or decrees that accord with reality and divine standard.

  • Pairing with Chesed: 'emet frequently appears alongside chesed ("lovingkindness," "mercy," or "covenant loyalty"). Together, chesed and 'emet form a essential pair describing God's covenantal stance toward His people—loving commitment backed by unyielding reliability.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, 'emet anchors the revelation of God's character. In divine revelation, YHWH reveals Himself as abounding in chesed and 'emet. Because God is 'emet, His promises do not fail, His word is an absolute standard of reality, and His judgments are entirely righteous.

This attribute guarantees that God's redemptive plans are secure across generations. For God's people, divine 'emet provides an unshakeable foundation for faith. Faith is not blind optimism; it is reliance upon the proven, enduring reality of God's character and word.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Psalm 117 is a concise hymn calling all nations and peoples to praise YHWH. Verse 2 provides the explicit ground for this universal summons: "For His merciful kindness is great toward us, and the truth of the LORD endures forever."

In Psalm 117:2, the target word 'emet is a feminine singular noun. The syntax directly links 'emet with the phrase "endures forever." The context highlights two attributes of YHWH: His covenant love (chesed) and His enduring reliability ('emet).

Here, 'emet emphasizes that YHWH's truthfulness and covenant fidelity are not temporary, localized, or subject to decay. By declaring that His 'emet extends forever, the psalmist demonstrates why praise to YHWH must extend to all Gentile nations. The eternal stability of God's truth ensures that His salvation and promises are reliable for all people groups throughout all ages.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 24:27 — "And he said, 'Blessed be the LORD God of my master Abraham, who has not forsaken His mercy and His truth toward my master.'"

  • Exodus 18:21 — "Moreover you shall provide out of all the people able men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness..."

  • Psalms 91:4 — "He shall cover you with His feathers, and under His wings you shall take refuge; His truth shall be your shield and buckler."

  • Psalms 119:142 — "Your righteousness is an everlasting righteousness, and Your law is truth."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The universal scope of Psalm 117:2—grounded in God's eternal 'emet—is confirmed in the New Testament when the Apostle Paul quotes Psalm 117:1 in Romans 15:11. Paul cites this psalm to demonstrate that God's covenant plan always intended for the Gentiles to rejoice in God's mercy alongside Israel. God's 'emet guarantees that His ancient redemptive promises reach their absolute fulfillment in Jesus Christ.
