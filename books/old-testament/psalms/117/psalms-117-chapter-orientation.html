Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Psalm 117
The Core:

All nations are called to praise the LORD for His great love and enduring faithfulness.

The Context:

An Israelite song of worship inviting all nations to praise God.

The Structure:

  • Verse 1: The worldwide call to praise.

  • Verse 2: The reason for praise.

The Key Verse:

“For His merciful kindness is great toward us, And the truth of the LORD endures forever.” (v. 2, NKJV)

Go Deeper

Author

Psalm 117 has no superscription, and Scripture does not identify its author. It belongs to Book Five of the Psalms (107–150) and to the Hallel (Psalms 113–118), a collection of praise psalms. Some Hebrew manuscripts join Psalm 117 to a neighboring psalm, but its original authorship and circumstances remain unknown.

Occasion

The original occasion is unknown, but the psalm became part of Israel's worship. Jewish tradition records that the Hallel was sung at major feasts, including Passover. The hymn Jesus and His disciples sang before going to the Mount of Olives was most likely from this collection (Matthew 26:30; Mark 14:26). If so, Jesus sang Psalm 117 on the night He was betrayed.

Psalm Type

A hymn of praise with a worldwide invitation. Verse 1 calls the nations to worship, and verse 2 gives the reason: God's great love and enduring faithfulness. The psalm ends with another call to praise the LORD.

The Heart of the Chapter

God's love for one nation was always meant to reach every nation. God promised Abraham that all the families of the earth would be blessed through him (Genesis 12:3). Now Israel calls those very nations to praise the LORD for His great kindness toward His people. Through His faithfulness to Israel, God was making Himself known to the world.

The two qualities in verse 2, merciful kindness and truth, recall how God revealed Himself to Moses: “abounding in goodness and truth” (Exodus 34:6). His love is not a passing feeling, and His faithfulness does not change with time.

Paul quotes Psalm 117 in Romans 15:11, showing that God's purpose always included the Gentiles. Christ came to confirm God's promises to Israel so that the Gentiles might also glorify Him for His mercy (Romans 15:8–9). In Jesus, who came “full of grace and truth” (John 1:14), God's love and faithfulness find their fullest expression. The invitation of Psalm 117 ultimately reaches toward the multitude from every nation worshiping before God's throne (Revelation 7:9–10).

Chapter Sections

Psalm 117:1–2 — Praise the LORD, All You Nations

The psalm forms one complete unit. Verse 1 calls every nation to praise the LORD. Verse 2 explains why: His merciful kindness is great, and His truth endures forever.

Why This Chapter Matters

God's heart for the nations is not a New Testament afterthought. Long before the church was born, Israel was singing an invitation for the Gentiles to worship the LORD. Believers from the nations who praise Him today are answering that ancient call.

True praise has reasons. Worship does not depend on our mood or circumstances but on who God is and what He has done. His love is steadfast, and His faithfulness never fails. The God who kept His promises to Israel is the same God in whom we place our hope.

Key Themes to Watch For

The nations called to worship. Every people is invited to praise the LORD (v. 1; Romans 15:11).

God's steadfast love. His merciful kindness toward His people is great and prevailing (v. 2; Psalm 103:11).

God's enduring faithfulness. His truth and promises remain dependable forever (v. 2; Psalm 100:5).

Israel's blessing reaches the nations. God's faithfulness to Israel reveals His heart and purposes for the whole world (vv. 1–2; Genesis 12:3).

Key Words & Concepts

Praise the LORD (hallelu Yah). The familiar “Hallelujah,” a command to praise Yah, a shortened form of the LORD's personal name.

Gentiles and peoples (goyim, ummim). Nations and peoples, particularly those beyond Israel in this setting. The invitation to worship extends to every people group.

Laud (shabach). To praise, commend, or extol, often expressing praise aloud.

Merciful kindness (chesed). God's steadfast, faithful love and enduring kindness toward His people. His love is loyal and dependable.

Is great (gabar). To be strong, mighty, or to prevail. God's merciful kindness is not weak or uncertain; it is mighty and prevailing.

Truth (emet). Faithfulness, firmness, and reliability. God is utterly trustworthy, and what He promises stands forever.
