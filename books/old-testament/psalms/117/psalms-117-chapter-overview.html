Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Psalm 117
The Core:

Psalm 117 calls every nation to praise the LORD because His merciful kindness toward His people is great and His truth endures forever.

The Context:

The shortest psalm in the Bible belongs to the Hallel (Psalms 113–118), a group of praise psalms used in Israel’s worship. In two verses it moves from a worldwide summons to the reason for it and back to praise.

The Structure:

  • Psalm 117:1 — A Call to All Nations to Praise the LORD

  • Psalm 117:2 — His Merciful Kindness and Enduring Truth

The Key Verse:

“For His merciful kindness is great toward us, And the truth of the LORD endures forever. Praise the LORD!” (Psalm 117:2, NKJV)

Go Deeper

Author

Psalm 117 has no title, and Scripture does not name its author. It stands in Book Five of the Psalms (Psalms 107–150) among the Hallel psalms. Some Hebrew manuscripts join it to a neighboring psalm, but it has long been read as a complete song of its own.

Occasion

The psalm names no historical event, and none should be assigned to it. Jewish tradition records that the Hallel psalms were sung at the great feasts, including Passover, so the hymn Jesus and His disciples sang before going to the Mount of Olives was most likely drawn from this collection (Matthew 26:30).

Psalm Type

A hymn of praise in its simplest form. Verse 1 issues the call, verse 2 gives the reason with the word “For,” and the closing “Praise the LORD!” (117:2) returns to the call.

The Heart of the Chapter

Israel’s shortest song has the widest reach. “Praise the LORD, all you Gentiles! Laud Him, all you peoples!” (117:1). Israel does not ask the nations to admire its God from a distance; it calls them to worship Him. God’s purpose for the whole world was being sung in Israel’s own hymnbook.

The reason is what God has shown Israel: “His merciful kindness is great toward us” (117:2). The word for “is great” carries the sense of strength that prevails, so God’s love is pictured as a force that overcomes. His “truth,” His faithfulness, “endures forever” (117:2). The pair recalls how the LORD described Himself to Moses as “abounding in goodness and truth” (Exodus 34:6). Israel’s testimony to that steady love becomes the nations’ reason to sing.

Paul quotes the psalm’s first verse in Romans 15:11 to show that Gentiles glorifying God for His mercy was part of God’s plan from the start, fulfilled in Christ, who came to confirm the promises made to the fathers (Romans 15:8–9). Throughout, the psalm grounds praise in who God is.

Chapter Sections

Psalm 117:1 — A Call to All Nations to Praise the LORD

The psalm opens with two parallel commands that reach beyond Israel to every Gentile nation and every people. To “praise” and to “laud” the LORD both mean to speak His worth aloud.

Psalm 117:2 — His Merciful Kindness and Enduring Truth

The word “For” gives the reason for the call: the LORD’s merciful kindness toward His people is strong and His truth lasts forever. The closing “Praise the LORD!” frames the psalm, ending where it began.

Why This Chapter Matters

Psalm 117 shows that God’s heart for the nations is old. When He called Abraham, He promised blessing to all the families of the earth (Genesis 12:3), and here Israel is already inviting those families to worship. Believers from the nations who praise God today are answering a call Israel sang centuries before Christ.

The psalm also shows that worship has a foundation. The nations are told to praise because God’s love prevails and His faithfulness does not wear out. Praise built on His character can continue when circumstances change, because He does not.

Key Themes to Watch For

The nations called to worship. Every Gentile nation and people is summoned to praise the LORD (117:1; Romans 15:11).

Israel’s testimony. The mercy shown “toward us” becomes the reason the nations should praise (117:2).

Love that prevails. God’s merciful kindness is great, strong enough to overcome (117:2).

Faithfulness that lasts. The truth of the LORD endures forever (117:2; Psalm 100:5).

Key Words & Concepts

Gentiles and peoples. Hebrew goyim and ummim, the nations beyond Israel. The two terms together leave no people out (117:1).

Laud. To praise or commend aloud, a word of spoken celebration (117:1).

Merciful kindness. Hebrew hesed, God’s steadfast, faithful love toward His people, loyal and dependable (117:2).

Is great. Hebrew gavar, to be strong or to prevail. God’s love is not fragile; it wins out (117:2).

Truth. Hebrew emet, firmness and reliability. It speaks of God’s faithfulness to His word as well as the accuracy of what He says (117:2).

Praise the LORD. Hebrew hallelu-Yah, the familiar Hallelujah, a call to praise Yah, a short form of the LORD’s personal name (117:2).
