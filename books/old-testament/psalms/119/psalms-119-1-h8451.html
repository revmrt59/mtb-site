Summary:

LAW - ( torah , Strong’s [8451])

Summary:

Direction, instruction, or teaching. In the context of Psalm 119:1, it refers to the totality of God’s revealed will, serving as the authoritative guide for life and conduct that points the way to righteousness.

Full Study:

1. Hebrew Roots / Derivation

Derived from the verb yarah (Strong’s [3384]), which means "to throw," "to shoot," or "to cast."

It specifically carries the sense of "pointing out" or "hitting a mark," as an archer aims an arrow, signifying a direction that is precise and intentional.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Instruction, direction, teaching, or law.

While often translated as "law" in a legal or judicial sense, the Hebrew mind viewed torah as "divine instruction."

It refers to the body of prophetic and priestly teaching, specifically the Pentateuch (the first five books of the Bible), but also the entire scope of God's revealed truth.

3. Usage in Hebrew Culture & Word Pictures

- The Archer’s Aim: From its root yarah, it pictured a teacher "shooting" information into the mind of a student or a guide "pointing out" the right path to a traveler.

- The Parent’s Instruction: It was used for the guidance a father or mother gave to a child (Proverbs 1:8), representing a "necessary" (urgent) framework for a successful life.

- The Rain or Dew: It carried the idea of "watering" or "showering" (related to the word for early rain, yoreh), suggesting that God’s law "refreshes" and "nourishes" the soul of the one who walks in it.

4. Theological Implications

The torah is the external expression of God’s internal character; it is the standard of "holiness" to which His people are called.

It is not merely a list of prohibitions but a gift of grace that reveals how to live in "blessedness" and "peace."

Theologically, the law provides the "boundaries" that prevent a life from becoming "unprofitable" or "unfruitful" (Titus 3:14).

5. Contextual Nuance in Psalm 119:1

In the NKJV of Psalm 119:1, it states, "Blessed are the undefiled in the way, who walk in the law (torah) of the LORD."

The torah is presented as the environment or the "track" on which the believer "walks."

To walk "in" the law implies a lifestyle that is fully immersed in and directed by God’s instruction, where every step is governed by the "faithful saying" of His Word.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Exodus 13:9: “...that the LORD’S law (torah) may be in your mouth;”

- Joshua 1:8: “This Book of the Law (torah) shall not depart from your mouth...”

- Psalm 1:2: “But his delight is in the law (torah) of the LORD...”

- Psalm 19:7: “The law (torah) of the LORD is perfect, converting the soul;”

- Proverbs 13:14: “The law (torah) of the wise is a fountain of life...”

- Isaiah 2:3: “...For out of Zion shall go forth the law (torah), and the word of the LORD from Jerusalem.”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that "blessedness" is tied to "instruction-following"; it is the "profit" (Titus 3:8) of a life aligned with God.

It suggests that the "undefiled" way is only possible when one is "diligent" (Titus 3:12) to study and "maintain" (Titus 3:14) the requirements of the torah.

By walking in the law, the believer ensures they do not "lack anything" (Titus 3:13) in their spiritual equipment for life.
