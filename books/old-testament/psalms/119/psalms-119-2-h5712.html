Summary:

TESTIMONIES - ( edah , Strong’s [5713])

Summary:

Solemn declarations or witnesses. It refers to the laws and precepts of God as evidence of His will and character, serving as a constant reminder of the covenantal obligations between God and His people.

Full Study:

  1. Hebrew Roots / Derivation

Derived from the verb ud (Strong’s [5749]), which means "to return," "to repeat," or "to warn."

It carries the primary idea of "witnessing" or "protesting" by repeating a statement or a warning, signifying a fixed and reliable affirmation of truth.

  1. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Testimony, witness, or precept.

In the Old Testament, it refers to the statues or decrees that act as "witnesses" to the terms of God’s covenant.

It signifies a truth that God has "testified" or "solemnly declared" to be His standard, leaving no doubt as to His expectations for human conduct.

  1. Usage in Hebrew Culture & Word Pictures

    • The Legal Deposition: In Hebrew culture, edah was used for a witness's testimony in a court of law—a permanent and binding record of what was seen or known to be true.

    • The Boundary Stone: It pictured a physical marker or pillar that "testified" to a contract or a boundary between two parties, serving as an unmoving witness that could not be ignored.

    • The Ark of the Testimony: It specifically refers to the Tablets of the Law placed inside the Ark, which stood in the Holy of Holies as the "testimony" of the agreement between God and Israel.

  2. Theological Implications

The testimonies of God are a "witness" against sin and a "witness" for righteousness. They reflect the unchanging character of the Testifier.

To keep His testimonies is to live in agreement with the "faithful saying" that God’s way is the only way to "blessedness."

They provide the "warnings" (from the root ud) necessary to prevent the believer from becoming "unfruitful" or "unprofitable."

  1. Contextual Nuance in Psalm 119:2

In the NKJV of Psalm 119:2, it states, "Blessed are those who keep His testimonies (edah), who seek Him with the whole heart!"

Keeping the testimonies is inseparable from "seeking Him." This shows that the Word of God is not a dead list of rules but a living witness that points to the Person of God.

The "blessedness" comes not just from mechanical obedience but from a heart that values the "testimony" because it loves the one who gave it.

  1. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

    • Psalm 19:7: “...The testimony (edut—related form) of the LORD is sure, making wise the simple;”

    • Psalm 25:10: “All the paths of the LORD are mercy and truth, to such as keep His covenant and His testimonies (edah).”

    • Psalm 119:24: “Your testimonies (edah) also are my delight and my counselors.”

    • Psalm 119:46: “I will speak of Your testimonies (edah) also before kings, and will not be ashamed.”

    • Psalm 119:111: “Your testimonies (edah) I have taken as a heritage forever, for they are the rejoicing of my heart.”

  2. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that God’s Word is a "repeated" truth; it is a "faithful saying" that remains relevant across all generations.

It suggests that a person who rejects the testimonies is essentially calling God a "false witness," whereas the one who keeps them "maintains" (Titus 3:14) the honor of God’s name.

By delighting in His testimonies, the believer finds "profitable" counsel that directs them through "urgent needs."
