Summary:

STATUTES - ( choq , Strong’s [2706])

Summary:

Something prescribed, engraved, or enacted. It describes the permanent and unalterable decrees of God that function as fixed boundaries for human behavior, representing the inscribed will of the Lawgiver.

Full Study:

1. Hebrew Roots / Derivation

Derived from the verb chaqaq (Strong’s [2710]), which means "to cut," "to engrave," "to inscribe," or "to decree."

It carries the physical image of a law being carved into a stone tablet or a metal surface, signifying a command that is durable, permanent, and intended to be lasting.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

A statute, ordinance, limit, or something prescribed.

In the Old Testament, it refers to the specific "customs" or "enactments" that God has established as a rule of life.

It signifies a boundary that has been "etched" into the fabric of reality, which must not be crossed or ignored by the faithful.

3. Usage in Hebrew Culture & Word Pictures

- The Engraved Tablet: In ancient Near Eastern culture, important laws were "cut" into stone or clay to ensure they would remain unchanged for generations, representing the "stability" of the legal order.

- The Fixed Horizon: It pictured the "limit" or "boundary" (choq) that God set for the sea or the celestial bodies, showing that just as the physical world has laws, so does the moral world.

- The Appointed Portion: It was used for a "prescribed allowance" of food or a "task" assigned to a laborer, signifying that God’s statutes are the "portion" of duty assigned to His people.

4. Theological Implications

Statutes are the "engraved" expressions of God’s sovereignty; they are not suggestions but settled determinations.

They represent the "unchanging" nature of God's requirements in a world of shifting opinions and "unprofitable" disputes (Titus 3:9).

Theological focus is on "stability"—the believer’s life is "established" (verse 5) only when it is anchored to these permanent decrees.

5. Contextual Nuance in Psalm 119:5

In the NKJV of Psalm 119:5, the psalmist cries out, "Oh, that my ways were directed to keep Your statutes (chuqqim)!"

This reflects a heart that recognizes the "permanence" of God’s laws and the "instability" of his own ways.

The desire for "direction" is a plea for the Holy Spirit to align the traveler's path with the "carved" lines of God's requirements, ensuring he does not deviate from the "faithful saying" of the Word.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Genesis 26:5: “...because Abraham obeyed My voice and kept My charge, My commandments, My statutes (chuqqim), and My laws.”

- Exodus 18:16: “...and I make known the statutes (chuqqim) of God and His laws.”

- Psalm 2:7: “I will declare the decree (choq): The LORD has said to Me, ‘You are My Son...’”

- Proverbs 8:29: “When He assigned to the sea its limit (choq), so that the waters would not transgress His command,”

- Ezekiel 36:27: “I will put My Spirit within you and cause you to walk in My statutes (chuqqim), and you will keep My judgments and do them.”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that "blessedness" (verse 1) is found in the "maintenance" (Titus 3:14) of boundaries that God has already "inscribed."

It suggests that the "way" (verse 1) is not something we invent, but something we "keep" by adhering to the "appointed" limits of the LORD.

By longing to keep His statutes, the believer demonstrates they are "watching" (Mark 13:37) and "taking heed" (Mark 13:33) to the permanent marks of divine truth.
