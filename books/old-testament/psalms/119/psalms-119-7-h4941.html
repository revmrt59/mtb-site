Summary:

JUDGMENTS - ( mishpat , Strong’s [4941])

Summary:

A judicial verdict, formal decree, or ordinance. It refers to the legal and moral decisions handed down by God as the Supreme Judge, which define what is right, just, and proper in every circumstance of life.

Full Study:

1. Hebrew Roots / Derivation

Derived from the verb shaphat (Strong’s [8199]), which means "to judge," "to govern," "to decide," or "to act as a lawgiver."

The root suggests the exercise of authority in resolving a dispute or establishing a rule of order, moving from the act of judging to the final "result" or "decision."

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Judgment, justice, ordinance, or custom.

It signifies a verdict pronounced judicially, whether favorable or unfavorable, and includes the formal decree that follows a legal proceeding.

In the Old Testament, it describes the "rights" or "due" of an individual, as well as the universal standards of justice that God has established for all creation.

3. Usage in Hebrew Culture & Word Pictures

- The Courtroom Verdict: In Hebrew culture, mishpat was the final word of the judge at the city gate, bringing a clear and authoritative end to a case and restoring social order.

- The King’s Decree: It pictured the executive "order" of a sovereign that organized the life and behavior of the citizens, representing the administrative side of God’s rule.

- The Measuring Line: It was used as a "picture" of the "proper way" or "custom" for doing things—such as the "plan" (mishpat) for building the tabernacle—signifying that God’s judgments are the blueprints for a correct life.

4. Theological Implications

Judgments reflect the "righteousness" of God’s character applied to human conduct; they are the practical applications of His "law" (torah) to specific situations.

They demonstrate that God is not a passive observer but an active Governor who "decides" what is profitable and what is destructive.

Theological focus is on "discernment"—the believer learns God's judgments to gain the ability to "see" life through the lens of divine justice and moral truth.

5. Contextual Nuance in Psalm 119:7

In the NKJV of Psalm 119:7, it states, "I will praise You with uprightness of heart, when I learn Your righteous judgments (mishpat)."

The psalmist links "learning" (lamad—training) these judgments to the ability to "praise" with an upright heart.

This reveals that true worship is not merely emotional but is grounded in an understanding of God’s judicial decrees; as the heart is "straightened" by God’s verdicts, the lips are opened to offer "upright" praise.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Genesis 18:25: “...Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right (mishpat)?”

- Exodus 21:1: “Now these are the judgments (mishpat) which you shall set before them:”

- Psalm 19:9: “...The judgments (mishpat) of the LORD are true and righteous altogether.”

- Psalm 119:160: “The entirety of Your word is truth, and every one of Your righteous judgments (mishpat) endures forever.”

- Micah 6:8: “...And what does the LORD require of you but to do justly (mishpat)...”

7. Other Relevant Insights

This word highlights that "blessedness" (verse 1) is found in a life that is "regulated" by the verdicts of the Almighty.

It suggests that a "watchful" (Mark 13:37) heart must be a "judging" heart—one that can discern the "difference" between the holy and the profane based on God's decrees.

By learning these judgments, the believer ensures their "maintenance" of good works (Titus 3:14) is performed according to the "righteous" standard that avoids all "shame" (verse 6).
