LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

Lexically, the proper noun Yahweh (rendered as "Lord" in small capital letters in the NKJV) is the personal, covenant name of the God of Israel. In Psalm 134:1, the name identifies the specific divine recipient of Israel's worship, identifying the God who redeemed Israel and established a covenant relationship with them as the exclusive object of the night ministers' praise in the sanctuary.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The term Yahweh is the formal personal name of Israel's God. In Old Testament redemptive history, God revealed this personal name to Moses at the burning bush, distinguishing Himself from generic titles for God (Elohim) and terms of lordship (Adonai). In ancient scribal tradition, out of intense reverence for the divine name, Jewish readers substituted the vocalization of Adonai when reading the sacred text aloud. English translations such as the NKJV reflect this tradition by rendering Yahweh as "Lord" using small capital letters. In the context of Psalm 134—the final song in the collection known as the Songs of Ascents—the pilgrim worshipers direct a call to corporate praise toward the levitical priests and attendants serving during the night hours in Jerusalem.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, Yahweh functions as a proper noun designating the one true God who revealed Himself to Israel. Unlike descriptive titles that denote role or rank (such as "King" or "Master"), Yahweh is a proper personal name. While biblical narrative grounds the revelation of the name in God's active self-existence and presence ("I AM WHO I AM"), the primary lexical function of the word itself is identification: it marks out the personal God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob in distinction from foreign idols or abstract deity.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Hebrew Scriptures, Yahweh appears as the primary divine name in covenant contexts across diverse literary genres:

  • Narrative Literature: The name marks God's personal interventions in history, His mighty acts of deliverance, and His covenant promises to His chosen people.

  • Legal Texts: The divine name establishes the moral authority of the law, grounding obedience in God's identity as Redeemer.

  • Poetic and Worship Texts: In the Psalms, Yahweh is invoked as the recipient of prayer, thanksgiving, and adoration, highlighting the intimate, personal bond between God and His worshipers.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, the personal name Yahweh shifts biblical religion from general cosmic monotheism to relational, revealed covenant faith:

  • Covenant Faithfulness: The name specifies that God is personally committed to His promises and to the preservation of His people.

  • Revealed Self-Identity: Israel did not invent God's identity; God initiated fellowship by revealing His personal name.

  • Unique Sovereignty: The name distinguishes the Creator of heaven and earth from all false deities, establishing Him as the absolute Lord of history.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Psalm 134:1, the divine name Lord ("Yahweh") appears twice in rapid succession: "Behold, bless the Lord, all you servants of the Lord, who by night stand in the house of the Lord!"

  • Syntactically, Yahweh defines both the object of worship ("bless the Lord") and the master whom the temple ministers serve ("servants of the Lord").

  • Contextually, as worshipers prepare to depart from Jerusalem, they address the levitical guards and priests who maintain the sanctuary worship through the night hours.

  • The repetition of Yahweh underscores that even hidden, late-night routine duties in the sanctuary are acts of worship directed directly to Israel's covenant God. The term anchors the call to worship in God's revealed identity rather than human emotional state.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 — "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the Lord God made the earth and the heavens."

  • Exodus 13:5 — "And it shall be, when the Lord brings you into the land of the Canaanites... which He swore to your fathers to give you... that you shall keep this service in this month."

  • Psalms 24:10 — "Who is this King of glory? The Lord of hosts, He is the King of glory."

  • Psalms 103:20 — "Bless the Lord, you His angels, who excel in strength, who do His word, heeding the voice of His word."

  • Isaiah 43:16 — "Thus says the Lord, who makes a way in the sea and a path through the mighty waters..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the worship of ancient Israel, invoking the Lord (Yahweh) reminded the community that genuine praise is a response to divine initiative. The night ministers in Psalm 134:1 were not performing hollow ritual duties to an unknown god; they were standing watch in the presence of the God who had bound Himself to Israel by covenant.
