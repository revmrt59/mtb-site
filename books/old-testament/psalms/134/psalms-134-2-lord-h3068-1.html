LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

The proper noun Yahweh (YHWH) is the sacred personal name of the God of Israel. Derived historically from the verb connection meaning "to be" or "to exist," it designates God not merely by a generic title of divinity or lordship, but by His personal name revealed in covenant relationship with His people.

In Psalm 134:2, the word identifies the specific covenant God who is the sole object of priestly worship within the sanctuary. By invoking Yahweh, the psalmist directs the night watchmen in the temple to lift up their hands not in generic religious devotion, but in explicit praise to the God who dwells among Israel and bound Himself to them by covenant.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

The term Yahweh (represented in English Bibles as "LORD" or "Lord") is the personal name of God in the Old Testament. Historically, Jewish tradition treated this tetragrammaton (the four-letter name YHWH) with deep reverence, avoiding its oral pronunciation to prevent taking the divine name in vain. In public reading, the title Adonai ("Lord") was spoken in place of the sacred name.

Unlike descriptive titles such as Elohim (which highlights God's power as Creator and Supreme Being) or Adonai (which emphasizes mastership and authority), Yahweh is a proper personal noun. It distinguishes the God of Israel from all false gods of the surrounding nations. The name was prominently revealed in redemptive history when God met Moses at the burning bush, establishing a foundational revelation of His identity tied to His dynamic presence with His people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, Yahweh is a proper noun designating the one true God of Israel. Grammatically, the target occurrence in Psalm 134:2 functions as a proper name.

Etymologically, the name is associated with the Hebrew verb hāyāh, meaning "to be" or "to exist." While the base lexical meaning of the proper noun is simply the divine name itself, canonical usage connects the name to God's continuous, active existence and self-determined presence. Lexical precision requires distinguishing between the bare lexical category of the proper noun and the expanded theological truths that Scripture connects to the one who bears the name.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Across the Hebrew Scriptures, Yahweh is used across every genre—narrative, law, poetry, wisdom, and prophecy.

In narrative accounts such as Genesis 2:4, the divine name is linked with Elohim to emphasize that the supreme God of creation is the very same personal God who interacts directly with humanity. In covenant contexts, such as Exodus 13:5, the name marks God's faithful performance of promises sworn to the patriarchs. In the Psalms, Yahweh is the recipient of liturgical praise, prayer, and trust, frequently portrayed as King, Shepherd, Deliverer, and Sanctuary Dweller. In prophetic literature, such as Isaiah 43:16 and Jeremiah 33:1, the name underscores God's absolute sovereignty over creation and history as He speaks authoritative declarations to His people.

4. Theological Implications

While the lexical definition of Yahweh is the proper name of Israel's God, the canonical presentation of this name carries immense theological weight:

  • Self-Existence and Uncreated Being: The root connection to hāyāh highlights that God depends on nothing outside Himself for His existence. He is the living source of all reality.

  • Covenant Faithfulness: Yahweh is uniquely the covenant name of God. When God enters into covenant with Abraham, Israel, and David, He places His personal name on the line, pledging His unyielding loyalty to His promises.

  • Holiness and Presence: The divine name is inextricably linked to God's holy presence, particularly where He chooses to cause His name to dwell, such as the tabernacle and the Jerusalem temple.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Psalm 134 serves as the final song in the collection known as the Songs of Ascents (Psalms 120–134). These psalms accompanied pilgrims journeying up to Jerusalem for the sacred feasts.

Psalm 134:2 records the direct charge: "Lift up your hands in the sanctuary, and bless the LORD." The ones addressed are the Levitical night watchmen and priests who stood in the house of God during the night hours (v. 1).

In this context, the presence of the divine name Yahweh is central to the passage's argument:

  • Object of Liturgical Praise: The priests do not lift their hands into empty space or toward a generalized deity; they raise their hands in the sanctuary specifically to Yahweh.

  • Covenant Reciprocity: The psalm forms a beautiful dialogue anchored by the name Yahweh. In verses 1–2, the servants bless Yahweh in His sanctuary. In verse 3, the psalm concludes with the blessing reversed: "The LORD who made heaven and earth bless you out of Zion!" The same covenant God who receives sanctuary praise is the God who dispenses cosmic blessing upon His people.

  • Sanctuary Identity: The sanctuary is holy because it is the dwelling place associated with Yahweh. To bless Yahweh in the sanctuary is to acknowledge His enthronement among His redeemed people.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 — "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens."

  • Psalms 24:10 — "Who is this King of glory? The LORD of hosts, He is the King of glory."

  • Psalms 103:20 — "Bless the LORD, you His angels, who excel in strength, who do His word, heeding the voice of His word."

  • Isaiah 43:16 — "Thus says the LORD, who makes a way in the sea and a path through the mighty waters..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The structure of Psalm 134 creates a striking thematic bookend for the entire Songs of Ascents collection. As pilgrims prepare to depart Jerusalem, they leave the temple in the care of the night ministers. The repeated invocation of Yahweh three times across the three verses of Psalm 134 compresses the entire theology of Israel's worship into a brief liturgically rich climax: human servants praising the covenant God in His earthly dwelling, and the almighty Creator-God pronouncing blessing upon His people from Zion.
