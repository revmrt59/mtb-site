LORD - (YHWH, Strong's H3068)

Summary:

The proper personal name YHWH (rendered in English Bibles as "LORD") serves as the sacred, distinctive name of the God of Israel. Lexically, the term functions as a proper noun identifying the divine person who revealed Himself to Israel and established covenant with them. In Psalm 134:3, YHWH identifies the personal source of priestly blessing extended to the pilgrim worshiper. By linking YHWH with the description "who made heaven and earth" and noting that this blessing comes "from Zion," the psalmist demonstrates that the covenant God of Israel, who manifests His personal presence at His sanctuary in Zion, possesses infinite cosmic power as the Creator of all things.

Full Study:

1. Word Background & Setting

Psalm 134 stands as the final psalm in the collection known as the Songs of Ascents (Psalms 120–134). These songs were sung by Israelite pilgrims traveling to Jerusalem for the major annual feasts. Psalm 134 represents a litany or dialogue between the worshipers and the sanctuary ministers: verses 1–2 call upon the Levitical night watch to praise God, while verse 3 issues a parting benediction upon the worshiper.

In the surrounding ancient Near Eastern culture, nations worshiped various regional deities. In contrast, Israel's worship was addressed specifically to YHWH, the personal name revealed in God's redemptive history. Grammatically, for this target occurrence in Psalm 134:3, the authoritative morphological classification designates the word strictly as a proper noun (`HNpt`).

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

  • Lexical Meaning (Layer 1): Lexically, YHWH is the proper personal name of the God of Israel. Unlike generic terms for deity such as elohim ("god" or "God"), YHWH functions as a personal identity.

  • Etymological Background: Lexical scholarship connects the name to the Hebrew root hayah ("to be"), closely tied to God's self-revelation at the burning bush in Exodus 3 ("I AM WHO I AM").

  • Distinction between Lexicon and Theology: While the canonical revelation of Scripture reveals God's self-existence, eternal nature, covenant faithfulness, and supreme sovereignty, the lemma itself functions lexically as a proper name designating Israel's covenant Lord. In English translation tradition, YHWH is represented by "LORD" using capital letters to distinguish it from Adonai ("master" or "lord").

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Throughout the Hebrew Scriptures, YHWH distinguishes the living God from false idols and abstract concepts of divinity. The name appears across historical narratives, wisdom literature, prophetic proclamations, and psalmody.

In biblical usage, YHWH frequently appears in construction with descriptive titles or relative clauses that articulate His attributes or redemptive deeds. For example, in Genesis 2:4, YHWH is paired with Elohim in the narrative of creation; in Exodus 13:5, YHWH is named as the One fulfilling His oath to give Israel the promised land; in Psalm 24:10, YHWH is identified as the "King of glory"; and in Isaiah 43:16, YHWH is described as the One who makes a way through the sea. In Psalm 134:3, the name is joined directly to the participial description "who made heaven and earth."

4. Theological Implications

  • Covenant and Creation (Layer 3): Psalm 134:3 unites two vital theological truths in the person of YHWH: He is the covenant God of Israel localized by His presence in Zion, and He is the universal Creator of heaven and earth. The God who blesses His covenant people is not a limited local deity, but the omnipotent Maker of the cosmos.

  • Personal Invocation: The use of YHWH ensures that divine blessing is rooted in personal relation rather than impersonal ritual. Blessing flows from a personal, relational God who communicates His favor to His people.

  • Canonical Horizon: In the broader biblical context, the supreme revelatory authority and covenant devotion attached to YHWH in the Old Testament finds its ultimate canonical fulfillment in the New Testament proclamation of Jesus Christ as Lord (Kyrios).

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

The structure of Psalm 134 presents a meaningful reciprocal dynamic:

  • Verses 1–2 issue an imperative to the temple ministers: "bless the LORD" (YHWH). Here, human worshipers offer praise to God.

  • Verse 3 shifts the direction of blessing: "The LORD who made heaven and earth bless you from Zion!" Here, YHWH confers His divine favor upon the worshiper.

The context clarifies the scope of this blessing through two key phrases:

  • "from Zion": Zion was the earthly sanctuary, the appointed place of covenant fellowship where God promised His presence among His people. The blessing originates from the locus of covenant communion.

  • "who made heaven and earth": This appositional description guarantees that the blessing originating from Zion is backed by absolute cosmic power. The authority of YHWH is unlimited; therefore, the blessing pronounced by His ministers carries ultimate efficacy.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 2:4 — "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens."

  • Joshua 14:12 — "Now therefore, give me this mountain of which the LORD spoke in that day..."

  • Psalms 24:10 — "Who is this King of glory? The LORD of hosts, He is the King of glory."

  • Isaiah 43:16 — "Thus says the LORD, who makes a way in the sea and a path through the mighty waters..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

  • Transition in Direct Address: While verses 1–2 address the priests in the plural ("all you servants of the LORD"), verse 3 shifts to a singular object ("bless you"). This syntactic transition indicates that while corporate worship is conducted by the assembly, the priestly blessing from YHWH is applied to each individual pilgrim.

  • Thematic Completion of the Ascents: As the final verse of the Songs of Ascents, Psalm 134:3 sends the pilgrim away from the Jerusalem sanctuary with the assurance that YHWH's covenant favor attends them beyond the temple gates, backed by His power as Creator of all things.
