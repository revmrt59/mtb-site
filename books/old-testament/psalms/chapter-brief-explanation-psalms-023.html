Psalm 23:1–3 — The LORD Is My Shepherd

Psalm 23:1
Brief Explanation: In this psalm of David, the shepherd who became Israel’s king confesses that the LORD is his own Shepherd, a title kings bore for their people and Scripture gives to God Himself, so that under His care David lacks nothing he truly needs. Every believer may make the same personal confession, because the LORD who shepherded David shepherds His people through Jesus, the Good Shepherd (John 10:11).

Psalm 23:2
Brief Explanation: The Shepherd makes David lie down in green pastures and leads him beside still waters, literally waters of rest, so that rest and refreshment come from the Shepherd’s leading rather than the sheep’s own searching. Read through the rest of Scripture, this is the rest Christ gives to those who come to Him (Matthew 11:29).

Psalm 23:3
Brief Explanation: The LORD revives David’s whole weary life and leads him on right paths, tracks that go where the Shepherd intends and keep him in what is right before God, and He does so “for His name’s sake,” for the honor of His own character. Spiritually, the Shepherd restores believers who have fallen or wandered, as He restored David after his sin, and His guidance rests on His faithfulness rather than on the sheep’s performance.

Psalm 23:4 — Through the Valley, You Are with Me

Psalm 23:4
Brief Explanation: Even in the valley of the shadow of death, a place of real and deadly danger, David will fear no evil, and here he turns from speaking about the LORD to speaking to Him: “For You are with me.” The rod that defended the flock and the staff that supported and guided it comfort him, and read through the rest of Scripture they speak of the Shepherd’s loving discipline and guidance of His people, as Hebrews 12:6 shows.

Psalm 23:5–6 — The LORD’s Table and the LORD’s House

Psalm 23:5
Brief Explanation: The picture changes from shepherd to host: the LORD prepares a table for David in full view of his enemies, honors him as a welcomed guest by anointing his head with oil, and fills his cup to overflowing. Spiritually, the table points to communion with God amid the enemies that surround the soul, seen most fully at the Lord’s table (1 Corinthians 10:16), and the oil points to the Holy Spirit poured out on the believer.

Psalm 23:6
Brief Explanation: Where enemies once pursued David, he is sure that the LORD’s goodness and His mercy, His steadfast love, will pursue him all the days of his life, and that he will dwell in the house of the LORD, living in God’s presence for the length of his days. Scripture carries this hope on to unending dwelling with God (Revelation 21:3), the home to which the Shepherd leads His flock.
