Psalm 117
Hearing God Through His Word

Psalm 117:1
“Praise the LORD, all you Gentiles! Laud Him, all you peoples!” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says
The shortest psalm in the Bible opens with a call for every nation and people on earth to praise the LORD. Paul later quotes this verse to show that God always planned for the Gentiles to join in worshiping Him through Christ.

Observe: Something to notice
The people called here are the “Gentiles,” the nations beyond Israel. These nations bowed to idols and often fought against God’s people. Yet Israel’s own song invites every one of them, saying “all” twice, to praise “the LORD,” God’s personal name, the name He revealed to Moses. The second line repeats the call: “Laud Him,” meaning commend Him and declare His greatness. This was God’s heart from the beginning, when He told Abraham that all the families of the earth would be blessed through him (Genesis 12:3). Paul quotes this verse to a church of Jewish and Gentile believers, urging them to “receive one another, just as Christ also received us” (Romans 15:7–11). Israel had been singing about that welcome all along.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me
Most of us reading this are among the “Gentiles” this psalm was calling. We were once far from God, and now we are invited to praise Him by name. The invitation was being sung over us long before we knew Him.

Because Christ has received people from every nation into one family of praise, I can gladly receive believers whose background, language, and culture differ from mine. Our voices belong together in the same song.

Observe: Something to notice
Psalm 117 belongs to the Hallel (Psalms 113–118), the psalms Israel sang at Passover. Early Jewish sources place it among those sung after the meal. Matthew records that after the Passover meal, Jesus and His disciples “had sung a hymn” and then went out to the Mount of Olives (Matthew 26:30). The Gospels do not name the hymn, but it was most likely this closing part of the Hallel. On the night before His death, Jesus probably sang this call for all nations to praise the LORD. Then He went out toward the cross that would make the call possible to answer.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me
Jesus likely sang this invitation on His way to the cross. Only days earlier He had said that when He was lifted up, He would “draw all peoples” to Himself (John 12:32), and He endured the cross “for the joy that was set before Him” (Hebrews 12:2). I am one of the people He went to the cross to gather. When I praise the LORD, I am answering a call that cost Him His life. My praise can begin with thanks for that.

Psalm 117:2
“For His merciful kindness is great toward us, And the truth of the LORD endures forever. Praise the LORD!” (NKJV)

Understand: What the verse says
The reason all nations should praise God is that His faithful love toward His people is great and His truth, meaning His reliability in keeping His promises, lasts forever. The psalm closes as it began, with “Praise the LORD,” showing that God’s steady love and faithfulness are the heart of all true worship.

Observe: Something to notice
The psalm gives a reason for its praise, and the reason is who God is. “Merciful kindness” translates the Hebrew word hesed, God’s loyal, steadfast love. “Is great” means strong or prevailing, and the same Hebrew word describes the floodwaters that “prevailed” over the earth (Genesis 7:18). “Truth” means His reliability in keeping His word, and it endures forever. These are the same two words God used when He revealed Himself to Moses: “abounding in goodness and truth” (Exodus 34:6). His love moves Him to make His promises, and His faithfulness keeps them.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me
My praise rests on who God is, so I can praise Him on hard days as well as good ones. His love toward me is great, and His faithfulness will last through every generation after mine. The word that described the floodwaters prevailing over the earth now describes His merciful kindness prevailing over my life. When I come to the last word of this psalm, Hallelujah, I can answer it gladly.

Observe: Something to notice
“Us” most naturally refers to Israel, the people who had experienced God’s kindness firsthand. Yet the psalm calls the nations to praise God for that kindness. Paul explains why just before he quotes Psalm 117. Christ came “to confirm the promises made to the fathers, and that the Gentiles might glorify God for His mercy” (Romans 15:8–9). In the same verse Paul says Christ came “for the truth of God,” so he uses the psalm’s two words, truth and mercy. God made promises to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. He kept them by sending Christ through Israel, and through Christ His mercy reached the nations.

Hear: What God may be speaking to me
The mercy I have received came to me because God kept promises He made long before I was born. He was faithful to Israel, and that faithfulness reached me. I can thank Him for keeping His word to Israel, and I can trust Him to keep His word to me.
