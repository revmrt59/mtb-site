Ruth Book Outline

Revision 2 • October 29, 2026

Major Movements

I. Loss in Moab and the Return to Bethlehem (Ruth 1:1–22)

Famine drives Elimelech’s family from Bethlehem to Moab, where Naomi buries her husband and both sons; then she hears that “the LORD had visited His people by giving them bread” and turns for home. Orpah goes back, but Ruth clings to Naomi and binds herself to her people and her God: “Your people shall be my people, And your God, my God.” Naomi tells the women of Bethlehem that the LORD has brought her “home again empty,” yet she arrives with Ruth beside her “at the beginning of barley harvest.”

II. Favor in the Field of Boaz (Ruth 2:1–23)

Ruth goes out to glean and “happened to come to the part of the field belonging to Boaz,” a man of Elimelech’s family. Boaz protects her, welcomes her at his meal, has grain left for her on purpose, and blesses her as one who has come for refuge under the wings of “the LORD God of Israel.” When Naomi hears his name, hope begins to stir: “This man is a relation of ours, one of our close relatives.”

III. The Appeal at the Threshing Floor (Ruth 3:1–18)

Naomi seeks security for Ruth and sends her to Boaz at the threshing floor by night. There Ruth asks, “Take your maidservant under your wing, for you are a close relative,” and Boaz blesses her kindness and promises to act, while honoring the claim of a relative closer than he. She returns with six ephahs of barley, not empty-handed, and Naomi tells her that “the man will not rest until he has concluded the matter this day.”

IV. Redemption at the Gate (Ruth 4:1–12)

At the city gate, before ten elders, Boaz sets Elimelech’s land before the nearer relative, together with Ruth “to perpetuate the name of the dead through his inheritance.” When that relative declines, lest he ruin his own inheritance, Boaz declares before the elders and all the people that he has bought all that was Elimelech’s and has acquired Ruth the Moabitess as his wife. The people and elders bless the woman coming into his house with the names of Rachel, Leah, and Perez.

V. A Son for Naomi and the Line of David (Ruth 4:13–22)

Boaz takes Ruth as his wife, and the LORD gives her conception. The women of Bethlehem, whose blessing answers the scene of Naomi’s bitter homecoming, now bless the LORD, “who has not left you this day without a close relative.” The child, “a restorer of life and a nourisher of your old age,” is named Obed, and the closing genealogy carries the family from Perez through Boaz and Obed to David.
