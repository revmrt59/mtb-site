Mastering the Bible

Ruth

Book Introduction

Background

Author

Unknown. Jewish tradition associates the book with Samuel, though Scripture does not identify the author.

Audience

Originally written for the people of Israel to preserve the account of God's redemptive work and David's ancestry; profitable for all believers.

Date

Events occurred during the time of the Judges (c. 1200–1100 BC). The book was likely written during the early monarchy or later, after David's genealogy had become significant.

Historical Setting

The events take place "in the days when the judges ruled" (Ruth 1:1), a period marked by spiritual decline, moral instability, and repeated apostasy. Against this dark backdrop, God quietly accomplishes His purposes through ordinary, faithful people.

Message

Occasion

To demonstrate God's providence, faithful covenant love (hesed), redemption through the kinsman-redeemer, the inclusion of a Gentile into God's covenant people, and the preservation of the Messianic line leading to David and ultimately Jesus Christ.

Purpose

To reveal how God sovereignly works through ordinary circumstances, faithful obedience, and covenant love to redeem broken lives, preserve His covenant promises, and advance His plan of salvation through the promised Messiah.

Theme(s)

  • Redemption through the Kinsman-Redeemer
  • God's Hidden Providence
  • Hesed (Faithful Covenant Love)
  • Grace Toward Outsiders
  • Restoration After Loss
  • Refuge Under God's Wings
  • Faithfulness in Ordinary Life

What is a Kinsman-Redeemer?

Big Idea

Though God often works quietly behind the scenes, He faithfully directs ordinary events to redeem His people, restore what has been lost, and accomplish His redemptive purposes through the promised Redeemer.

Structure & Redemption

Outline

Chapter 1 — Loss and LoyaltyNaomi's family leaves Bethlehem, tragedy strikes in Moab, and Ruth commits herself to Naomi and the God of Israel.
Chapter 2 — Providence and ProvisionGod providentially leads Ruth to Boaz, who extends protection, favor, and abundant provision.
Chapter 3 — Redemption SoughtRuth appeals to Boaz as kinsman-redeemer, and Boaz promises to act honorably according to God's law.
Chapter 4 — Redemption AccomplishedBoaz redeems Ruth and Naomi's inheritance, Obed is born, and the royal lineage leading to David is established.

Key Verse(s)

Ruth 1:16–17 – Ruth's covenant commitment to Naomi and the God of Israel.
Ruth 2:12 – Ruth finds refuge under the wings of the LORD.
Ruth 3:9 – Ruth appeals to Boaz as her kinsman-redeemer.
Ruth 4:14–17 – God provides a redeemer, and the genealogy leads to David.

Christ in This Book

Boaz serves as a type of Jesus Christ, the greater Kinsman-Redeemer. As Boaz willingly redeemed Ruth and restored her inheritance, Christ willingly paid the price to redeem sinners, bring outsiders into God's family, provide refuge under His covenant care, and secure an eternal inheritance for all who believe.
