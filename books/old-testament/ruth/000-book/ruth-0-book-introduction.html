Ruth

Ruth - Book Introduction

Background

Author

Unknown. Jewish tradition associates the book with Samuel, though Scripture does not identify the author.

Audience

Originally written for the people of Israel to preserve how God worked to secure David's ancestry and to teach faithful love and communal responsibility; profitable for all believers.

Date

Events are set in the days of the Judges (c. 1200-1100 BC). The book was likely composed later-once David's genealogy mattered-commonly dated by conservatives broadly to the monarchy period (roughly 10th-6th century BC).

Occasion

God gave this story when famine, death, and widowhood left Naomi's household vulnerable and threatened the continuity of a family line; the narrative preserves how God used faithful love, Israel's social duties, and a willing kinsman-redeemer to rescue the vulnerable and secure an heir for David's ancestry.

Historical Setting

The story opens "in the days when the judges ruled," a time of social instability and famine that drives Naomi's family from Bethlehem to neighboring Moab. There Naomi's husband and sons die, leaving widows vulnerable and landless; when Naomi returns to Israel the community practices of gleaning, city‑gate legal transactions, and the kinsman‑redeemer become the ordinary means God uses to provide and restore. Pastoral insight: the setting shows God often works through everyday institutions and faithful people rather than dramatic signs.

Message

Purpose

To show how God, in quiet providence, uses ordinary faithfulness and loyal love to redeem broken lives, preserve the promise of David's line, and advance the coming of the promised King.

Book Summary

God shows how his quiet, sovereign care turns loss into blessing through ordinary, faithful people. Out of famine and widowhood, Ruth's costly loyalty and Boaz's willing redemption restore Naomi's family, secure an heir, and prepare the Davidic line-teaching that faithful love, community responsibility, and hidden providence accomplish God's promises.

Major Themes

  • Redemption through the Kinsman-Redeemer
  • God's hidden providence
  • Hesed - loyal, faithful love
  • Grace toward outsiders
  • Restoration after loss
  • Faithfulness in ordinary life
  • Community care and social provision

Central Message

Though God often works quietly, He faithfully uses ordinary people and their costly loyal love to redeem loss, restore families, and advance His promised plan of redemption through David's line.

Study Guide

Outline

  • Ruth 1:1-22 - Part 1: Tragedy in Moab and Return to Bethlehem
  • Ruth 2:1-23 - Part 2: Ruth Meets Boaz in the Harvest Field
  • Ruth 3:1-18 - Part 3: The Appeal to Boaz at the Threshing Floor
  • Ruth 4:1-22 - Part 4: Redemption Executed and the Lineage of David

Key Verse(s)

  • Ruth 1:16-17 - Ruth's covenant commitment to Naomi and to the God of Israel.
  • Ruth 2:12 - A blessing that places Ruth under the LORD's protection, finding refuge in his care.
  • Ruth 3:9 - Ruth's appeal to Boaz to act as her kinsman-redeemer.
  • Ruth 4:14-17 - God provides a redeemer; Ruth and Boaz's son secures Naomi's restoration and continues the line that leads to David.

Christ in This Book

Boaz points forward to Jesus as the greater Kinsman‑Redeemer: he redeems land, marries Ruth, and secures an heir-actions that foreshadow Christ's work to redeem sinners, welcome outsiders into God's family, protect the vulnerable, and guarantee an inheritance. Ruth's being brought into Israel and the genealogy that leads to David highlight God's plan to include the nations and prepare the royal line ultimately fulfilled in Jesus.

Introductory Summary

Ruth is a short, winsome story about how God quietly redeems ordinary loss. It opens with famine, widowhood, and Naomi's bitter grief, yet it centers on Ruth's costly loyalty and Boaz's willing act of redemption. The book's central tension-human bitterness and vulnerability versus God's faithful love and hidden providence-moves the narrative from private sorrow to public restoration. By the end God provides an heir and secures the line that leads to David. Come ready to see how small, faithful choices and community responsibility become the means of God's surprising salvation.
