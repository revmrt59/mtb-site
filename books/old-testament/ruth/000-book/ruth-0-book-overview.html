Ruth

Ruth - Book Overview

Background

Author

Traditionally attributed to Samuel.

Audience

The people of Israel, demonstrating God's faithful care for His people and preserving the family line through which King David-and ultimately the Messiah-would come.

Date

Likely written during the early monarchy, after David became king.

Historical Setting

The events of Ruth take place during the time of the Judges, a period marked by spiritual decline and national instability. While the nation often turned away from God, this account shows His quiet faithfulness at work in the lives of ordinary people who trusted Him.

Occasion

Ruth was written to demonstrate God's providential care, illustrate the beauty of faithful love and redemption, affirm David's family line, and reveal how God accomplishes His purposes through ordinary people who trust Him.

Understanding the Book

What This Book Is About

Ruth tells the story of God's quiet work in the lives of Naomi, Ruth, and Boaz. What begins with tragedy, loss, and emptiness gradually becomes a story of hope, redemption, restoration, and blessing through God's unseen providence.

The book shows that even when God seems silent, He is faithfully directing events to accomplish His purposes for His people and ultimately for the world.

Why This Book Matters

Ruth reminds us that God is often working most powerfully in the ordinary events of everyday life.

The book teaches that:

  • God remains faithful during seasons of suffering.

  • God's providence is often seen only in hindsight.

  • Redemption is at the heart of God's work.

  • Faithfulness in ordinary responsibilities matters to God.

  • God delights in bringing hope out of hopeless situations.

Ruth encourages us to trust God's unseen hand, even when we cannot understand His plan.

Key Themes to Watch For

  • God's providence

  • Redemption

  • Faithfulness

  • Kindness

  • Hope and restoration

  • God's sovereign guidance

  • Family and inheritance

  • God's unfolding plan of redemption

Key Concepts to Understand Before Reading

Providence - God's unseen guidance of people and events to accomplish His good purposes.

Redeemer (Kinsman-Redeemer) - A close relative who had the responsibility to redeem family property or preserve the family line.

Kindness (Hebrew: hesed) - Faithful, steadfast love demonstrated through loyal actions.

Inheritance - The family land and heritage God gave to Israel.

Redemption - Restoring what has been lost by paying the necessary cost.

Faithfulness - Remaining loyal to God and to others regardless of circumstances.

Harvest - More than a setting for the story, it symbolizes God's provision and blessing.

Book Flow

Ruth 1 - From Fullness to Emptiness

Naomi returns to Bethlehem grieving the loss of her husband and sons, while Ruth demonstrates extraordinary faith by committing herself to Naomi and to the LORD.

Ruth 2 - God's Providence Begins to Appear

God quietly directs Ruth to Boaz's field, where unexpected kindness reveals that He has not forgotten Naomi or Ruth.

Ruth 3 - Redemption Is Sought

Following Naomi's guidance, Ruth humbly appeals to Boaz to act as her family redeemer, and Boaz willingly accepts the responsibility while honoring God's law.

Ruth 4 - Redemption Brings Restoration

Boaz redeems Ruth and Naomi's family, their joy is restored, and the book concludes by revealing God's larger purpose through the birth of Obed, grandfather of King David.

Christ and Life

Ruth beautifully points to Jesus Christ through Boaz, the kinsman-redeemer. Just as Boaz willingly redeemed Ruth and restored what had been lost, Jesus willingly gave Himself to redeem us from sin and bring us into God's family. Ruth, a Gentile, also reminds us that God's plan of redemption has always extended beyond Israel to include people from every nation who trust Him.

For believers today, Ruth reminds us that God is at work even when we cannot see Him. He often accomplishes His greatest purposes through ordinary acts of faithfulness, kindness, and obedience. As His redeemed people, we can trust His providence, knowing that He is working all things together for His glory and our good.

Nothing in the book happens by accident. Even the "ordinary" details-a famine, a gleaning field, a chance meeting, a legal transaction-become part of God's larger purpose. God's providence is often quiet, but it is never absent.

Reading Guide

One-Sentence Summary

Ruth reveals God's faithful providence as He brings redemption, restoration, and hope through ordinary people who trust Him.

Read This Book Remembering...

God is working even when He seems silent. As you read, watch how His unseen hand turns loss into restoration, weaves ordinary acts of faithfulness into His extraordinary plan of redemption, and forms a people who are wholly His.

Helpful Notes

The Kinsman-Redeemer

A close relative who could redeem family property, protect the family inheritance, or preserve the family line. Boaz's role prepares the reader to understand one of the book's central pictures of redemption.
