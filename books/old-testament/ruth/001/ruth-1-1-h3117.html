DAYS - ( yom , Strong's H3117 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word yom (plural yamim) in Ruth 1:1 refers to a specific historical epoch or period of time ("the days when the judges ruled"). It sets the historical and moral backdrop for the narrative of Ruth, highlighting an era characterized by spiritual decay, covenant unfaithfulness, and social chaos, yet demonstrating that God's silent providence operates faithfully even during dark historical seasons.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew noun yom fundamentally refers to a day, beginning with the basic cycle of light and darkness. However, in its plural form yamim, it frequently functions as a metaphor for a larger canvas of time—an era, a lifetime, or a historical epoch. In the ancient Hebrew mindset, time was not merely a series of chronological ticks on a clock; it was qualified by its content, characteristics, and the spiritual climate of those who lived within it. To speak of "the days" of a certain group or ruler is to invoke the entire spiritual, social, and political atmosphere of that era. In Ruth 1:1, the setting of the narrative in "the days when the judges ruled" instantly prepares the reader for a time of profound instability, moral confusion, and national struggle.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The singular noun yom means a day, defined either as the daylight hours (in contrast to night) or a literal 24-hour period. In the plural (yamim), and specifically in the construct state (bimei, meaning "in the days of"), the word shifts semantically to designate a generalized, extended period of time, a ruling dynasty, or a historical epoch. In Ruth 1:1, this construct form refers to the historical era of the Judges, a period spanning approximately three centuries from the death of Joshua to the rise of Saul. The lexical range of the word allows it to scale from a single sunrise-to-sunset cycle to a sweeping historical epoch.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The word yom is used extensively throughout the Old Testament to convey different aspects of time:

• The Literal Day: The standard unit of creation and daily life (Genesis 1:5).

• A Historical Era: A distinct epoch marked by specific events or leaders, often introduced by the formula "in those days" (Judges 17:6).

• A Lifetime or Age: Representing the span of human existence, often described as "all the days of one's life" (Genesis 5:5).

• The Prophetic Day: A designated time of divine intervention, judgment, or deliverance, most notably "the Day of the LORD" (Joel 2:31).

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, the use of yamim in Ruth 1:1 links the intimate, personal story of a single family to the broader covenant history of Israel. The "days when the judges ruled" were days of cyclic rebellion, oppression, cry for help, and temporary deliverance. It was an era of spiritual darkness where "everyone did what was right in his own eyes." By framing the book of Ruth within these specific "days," the text highlights a profound theological truth: God's redemptive plan is not suspended during periods of cultural decay and national unfaithfulness. Even in the darkest historical epochs, God quietly preserves a remnant, acts with covenant faithfulness (chesed), and advances His sovereign line toward the ultimate Ruler, Jesus Christ.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:1, the phrase "in the days when the judges ruled" serves as a literary contrast to the dark and chaotic book of Judges that precedes it. While Judges records national failure, military conflict, and spiritual apostasy, the "days" of Ruth reveal quiet faithfulness, kindness, and devotion in the ordinary lives of everyday people. Additionally, the text notes that in these "days," there was a "famine in the land." Under the Deuteronomic covenant, famine was a direct sign of divine discipline for covenant unfaithfulness. The "days" of the judges were therefore days of physical hunger reflecting Israel’s spiritual starvation, making the subsequent unfolding of God's grace in the harvest fields of Bethlehem even more dramatic.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 1:5 - "God called the light Day, and the darkness He called Night. So the evening and the morning were the first day."

• Genesis 6:4 - "There were giants on the earth in those days, and also afterward, when the sons of God came in to the daughters of men and they bore children to them."

• Judges 17:6 - "In those days there was no king in Israel; everyone did what was right in his own eyes."

• Joel 2:31 - "The sun shall be turned into darkness, and the moon into blood, Before the coming of the great and awesome day of the LORD."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Hebrew wisdom literature, human "days" are frequently described as fleeting, fragile, and brief, like a shadow or a breath (Psalm 144:4). Consequently, believers are commanded to "number our days, that we may gain a heart of wisdom" (Psalm 90:12). While human days are limited and subject to the changing tides of history, God is the "Ancient of Days" (Daniel 7:9), holding all historical epochs, including the difficult days of the judges and our own present days, securely in His hands.
