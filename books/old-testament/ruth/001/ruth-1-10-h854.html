WITH - ( 'eth , Strong's H854 )

Summary:

The Hebrew preposition 'eth expresses the fundamental concept of proximity, association, and fellowship. Far more than a mere grammatical connector, it denotes a close, relational presence—being side-by-side with someone in shared experience, destiny, or covenant. In Ruth 1:10, when Orpah and Ruth declare their intention to return "with" Naomi, the word signifies a deep, personal commitment to share in her journey, her hardships, and her community, moving beyond casual acquaintance into shared life.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The concept of 'eth centers on physical and relational accompaniment. It paints a picture of two parties walking together, working together, or standing in close alignment. It visually represents two distinct entities occupying the same space or undertaking the same path, moving in absolute solidarity. Historically and culturally, to go "with" someone into their homeland meant abandoning one's own safety net to share in the protection, social standing, and physical reality of the other person.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The preposition 'eth means "with," "near," "together with," "in the company of," or "alongside." It indicates association, proximity, and cooperation. It describes a relational bond where one party accompanies another. This preposition establishes a connection of presence and mutual involvement, expressing a shared state of being or joint action.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, 'eth depicts several distinct aspects of relationship:

• Companionship and Walk: It is the word used to describe Enoch and Noah walking in close, intimate fellowship "with" God.

• Divine Assistance and Backing: It frequently describes God's active, protective presence "with" His chosen instruments, such as Joseph in Egypt, ensuring success and survival despite hostile surroundings.

• Covenant Alignment: It defines being aligned with a household, nation, or leader, indicating that the speaker’s destiny is bound up with the destiny of the other.

### 4. Theological Implications

The concept of "withness" lies at the very heart of biblical theology. God’s ultimate desire is to dwell with His people, a theme that culminates in the name Immanuel ("God with us"). In human relationships, covenant love (chesed) is demonstrated through physical and spiritual accompaniment. To be "with" someone in the biblical sense means sharing their trials, their God, and their destiny. It represents a transition from self-preservation to sacrificial solidarity.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:10, Naomi has urged her widowed daughters-in-law to return to their mothers' houses in Moab. Their immediate, collective response is: "Surely we will return with you to your people."

• The use of 'eth here emphasizes their refusal to let Naomi walk alone in her grief, poverty, and vulnerability.

• It represents an initial commitment to cross geographic, cultural, and spiritual boundaries to remain in her company.

• While Orpah eventually relents under further pressure, this initial declaration highlights the power of shared grief to forge deep relational bonds. Ruth's subsequent, permanent commitment unpacks the full, sacrificial depth of this "withness."

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 5:24: "And Enoch walked with God; and he was not, for God took him."

• Genesis 39:2: "The LORD was with Joseph, and he was a successful man; and he was in the house of his master the Egyptian."

• Genesis 39:21: "But the LORD was with Joseph and showed him mercy, and He gave him favor in the sight of the keeper of the prison."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

While other Hebrew prepositions (such as 'im) also translate as "with" and are used interchangeably in poetic parallel, 'eth carries a strong sense of immediate, side-by-side presence. It emphasizes proximity and direct association. In the book of Ruth, this human "withness" becomes the vehicle for divine redemption. By choosing to go with Naomi, Ruth is brought under the wings of the Lord God of Israel, illustrating how human companionship can serve as the catalyst for saving faith.
