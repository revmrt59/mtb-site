SONS - ( ben, Strong's H1121 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word ben (plural banim) primarily denotes a male offspring, descendant, or member of a family. In the patriarchal culture of the ancient Near East, sons represented the continuation of the family name, the preservation of ancestral land, and economic security. In the poignant context of Ruth 1:11, Naomi uses the word to emphasize her biological inability to produce future husbands for her widowed daughters-in-law. This highlights the bleakness of their immediate circumstances, the legal realities of levirate marriage, and the necessity of their return to Moab.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The noun ben is derived from the root verb banah, which means "to build." This etymological connection provides a powerful word picture: a son is one who builds up the household and continues the family line. In the ancient world, a family was established, strengthened, and "built up" through descendants, particularly male heirs who kept the family inheritance intact. Without sons, a family name faced extinction, and ancestral land could be permanently lost to other clans.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun ben refers to a son, male child, grandson, or descendant. In a broader sense, it denotes members of a specific group (such as the "sons of Israel" or "sons of the prophets") or individuals characterized by a particular quality (such as "sons of valor"). The primary lexical definition centers on immediate male offspring. In its plural form (banim), it can refer generally to children of both genders, but in specific legal, genealogical, and kinship contexts, it refers strictly to male descendants who inherit the family estate.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

As one of the most frequent nouns in the Old Testament, occurring nearly 5,000 times, ben paints several vivid pictures in scripture:

• Arrows in a Quiver: Children, specifically sons, are described as arrows in the hand of a warrior, representing protection, strength, and future stability for an aging father.

• The Household Builder: Because of its connection to the verb "to build," a son is the physical block upon which the legacy and household of the father are erected.

• Heir of the Promise: The term represents the chain of covenantal transmission, passing the promise from father to son across generations.

### 4. Theological Implications

Sons are central to the biblical concepts of covenant, inheritance, and redemption. God’s covenantal promise to Abraham relied entirely on the provision of a ben (Genesis 15:3-4). Under the Mosaic Law, the concept of levirate marriage (Deuteronomy 25:5-6) was designed specifically to raise up a ben for a deceased brother to keep his name from being blotted out of Israel. Ultimately, the theology of ben points forward to the ultimate Son—the Messianic heir of David—who perfectly builds the house of God and secures the eternal inheritance of His people.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:11, Naomi asks, "Are there still sons in my womb, that they may be your husbands?" This rhetorical question relies directly on the practice of levirate marriage. Naomi points out the biological impossibility of her situation to convince Orpah and Ruth to return to their homeland. She is a widow, past the age of childbearing, and even if she were to marry and bear sons immediately, her daughters-in-law could not realistically wait for them to grow to maturity.

By using banim, Naomi emphasizes that there are no physical heirs left to secure the family's future, redeem their land, or provide social and economic security for the young widows. The word highlights the theme of absolute emptiness that dominates the beginning of the book of Ruth, setting up the dramatic contrast for God's eventual provision.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 22:2: "Then He said, 'Take now your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love, and go to the land of Moriah, and offer him there as a burnt offering on one of the mountains of which I shall tell you.'"

• Deuteronomy 25:5: "If brothers dwell together, and one of them dies and has no son, the widow of the dead man shall not be married to a stranger outside the family; her husband’s brother shall go in to her, take her as his wife, and perform the duty of a husband’s brother to her."

• Proverbs 1:8: "My son, hear the instruction of your father, And do not forsake the law of your mother;"

• Ruth 4:17: "Also the neighbor women gave him a name, saying, 'There is a son born to Naomi.' And they called his name Obed. He is the father of Jesse, the father of David."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

The tragedy of Naomi's initial lack of a ben is beautifully resolved at the climax of the book. When Boaz acts as the kinsman-redeemer and marries Ruth, they conceive a son. The women of Bethlehem celebrate this birth by declaring, "There is a son born to Naomi" (Ruth 4:17). This declaration directly answers Naomi's despairing cry in Ruth 1:11. Through the birth of Obed, the house of Elimelech is rebuilt, and the lineage is preserved to lead directly to King David and, ultimately, to Jesus Christ.
