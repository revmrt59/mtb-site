WITH - ( 'eth , Strong's H854 )

Summary:

The Hebrew preposition 'eth denotes close proximity, association, accompaniment, and relational presence. Translated as "with," it goes beyond mere physical co-location to signify shared experience, covenant commitment, and solidarity. In Naomi's poignant plea to her daughters-in-law in Ruth 1:11, the concept of going "with" her highlights the profound cost of companionship, contrasting a comfortable return to Moab with a difficult, sacrificial journey of shared destiny.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The preposition 'eth expresses the fundamental idea of proximity, association, and being "at one" with another. While grammatically simple, it carries deep relational weight in Hebrew thought. To be "with" someone is to share in their circumstances, stand in solidarity with them, and enter into their sphere of existence. It is the linguistic foundation for expressing companionship, partnership, and covenant. Unlike spatial prepositions that merely describe location, 'eth describes relationship and shared destiny.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The preposition 'eth means "with," "near," "together with," or "in the company of." It indicates a close connection or association between subjects. It is used to express:

• Accompaniment: physically traveling or staying in the company of someone.

• Alliance or association: standing alongside someone in conflict, work, or life.

• Mutual relationship: experiencing a state of peace, favor, or covenant together.

• It is semantically closely related to, and often interchangeable with, the preposition 'im, both of which emphasize relational proximity and shared experience.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

In the Old Testament, being "with" someone is often a picture of life-altering alliance:

• Covenantal Presence: The phrase "the Lord is with you" depicts divine backing, protection, and fellowship. It is not a passive existence but an active, protective alliance.

• Shared Destiny: Walking "with" someone means aligning one's path, pace, and destination with theirs. It requires mutual agreement and submission to a common goal.

• Social Solidarity: In legal and social contexts, doing something "with" another indicates shared responsibility, joint ownership, or mutual accountability.

### 4. Theological Implications

The theology of "with" is central to the biblical narrative of redemption. God’s ultimate desire is to dwell with His people:

• Incarnational Shadow: The human commitment to go "with" someone in their suffering prefigures the ultimate "with-ness" of God. When Ruth refuses to leave Naomi and insists on going with her, she models the covenant-keeping love (chesed) of Yahweh, who walks with His people through the valley of the shadow of death.

• Costly Discipleship: Accompaniment is not cheap. To go "with" an impoverished, bitter widow meant forfeiting security, social status, and future marriage prospects. The theological weight of "with" in this context is a picture of self-denying, sacrificial love.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:11, Naomi asks, "why will you go with me?" She is trying to persuade Orpah and Ruth to return to Moab.

• Naomi looks at her situation through the lens of human impossibility. She has no more sons to offer as husbands, and she is returning to Judah in poverty.

• By questioning why they would go with her, Naomi defines "with" in terms of material benefit and social utility. She assumes that since she can offer them nothing, there is no logical reason for them to accompany her.

• The nuance of "with" here exposes two different responses to suffering: Orpah decides that going "with" Naomi is too costly and returns to her gods, while Ruth redefines "with" not as a transaction, but as a covenant. Ruth's subsequent vow ("where you go, I will go") is the ultimate fulfillment of what it means to be with Naomi, regardless of the cost.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 5:24 - "And Enoch walked with God; and he was not, for God took him."

• Genesis 6:9 - "This is the genealogy of Noah. Noah was a just man, perfect in his generations. Noah walked with God."

• Genesis 39:2 - "The LORD was with Joseph, and he was a successful man; and he was in the house of his master the Egyptian."

• Genesis 39:21 - "But the LORD was with Joseph and showed him mercy, and He gave him favor in the sight of the keeper of the prison."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• While 'eth is a simple preposition, it forms the conceptual root of many key concepts in Hebrew covenant theology. The idea of "with" is the heart of Emmanuel, "God with us."

• The contrast between Naomi’s pragmatism ("why will you go with me?") and Ruth’s loyalty illustrates that true, biblical companioning is not based on what we can get from someone, but on our willingness to be with them in their darkest hours.
