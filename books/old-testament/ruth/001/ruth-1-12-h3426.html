HAVE - ( yesh , Strong's H3426 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word yesh fundamentally denotes existence, presence, or substantial reality, often translated as "there is," "there are," or "have" when paired with a preposition. In Ruth 1:12, Naomi uses this term to despairingly evaluate her life, questioning whether there is any substantial ground for hope or future restoration. By asserting that even if she "had" hope, the biological and temporal realities make the restoration of her family line humanly impossible, Naomi highlights the depth of her grief and sets the stage for God's unexpected, sovereign provision.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The particle yesh is not a standard verb but a particle of existence. It represents concrete reality, presence, and substance, standing in direct contrast to ayin (nothingness or nonexistence). It affirms that something is tangibly present, active, or available. In the ancient Near Eastern context, where survival and legacy depended heavily on tangible family lines, land, and inheritance, yesh represents the concrete presence of these life-sustaining realities. When Naomi uses it, she is weighing her current existence against the utter lack of tangible prospects for her and her daughters-in-law.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• yesh functions to assert existence or possession. Its primary meanings include "there is," "there exists," "being," or "substance." When followed by the preposition le (to/for) and a pronominal suffix, it expresses possession (e.g., yesh li, "there is to me" or "I have"). It signals substantial, undeniable reality rather than a mere possibility or abstract concept. It denotes something that can be counted on, possessed, or experienced as a present fact.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• yesh is frequently used to describe tangible wealth, substance, or resource, representing that which is firmly established and currently in hand.

• It expresses absolute, undeniable presence, such as when Jacob realized that God was actively present in a specific location.

• In Ruth 1:12, it is paired with tiqvah (hope, chord, expectation), creating a vivid image of a tangible, graspable cord of hope. Naomi claims that this cord of hope is entirely out of her reach, lacking any real substance in her life.

4. Theological Implications

The theology of yesh touches on the shift from human emptiness to divine fullness. Throughout the Old Testament, human beings often reach a point of ayin (nothingness)—where they "have" nothing left—so that God can manifest His yesh (His real, substantial presence and provision). Naomi’s focus is entirely horizontal; she sees no yesh (substance or hope) in her circumstances. The theological arc of Ruth demonstrates that even when human resources are utterly exhausted, God's sovereign grace remains an active, existing reality capable of bringing substance out of nothingness.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:12, Naomi is urging her daughters-in-law to return to Moab because, from a human perspective, her situation is utterly hopeless. She says, "If I should say I have [yesh] hope..." Here, yesh functions as a hypothetical assertion of reality. Naomi is setting up a logical impossibility: even if she possessed the actual, substantial presence of hope right now, and even if she were to have a husband and bear sons immediately, it would still be futile for Orpah and Ruth to wait for them to grow up. The use of yesh emphasizes that Naomi cannot find even a shred of tangible reality upon which to base a positive future. Her evaluation is starkly realistic on a human level, which magnifies the miraculous nature of the redemption that unfolds later through Boaz.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 28:16 – "Surely the LORD is [yesh] in this place, and I did not know it."

• Genesis 33:9 – "But Esau said, 'I have [yesh] enough, my brother; keep what you have for yourself.'"

• Genesis 43:7 – "Is your father still alive? Have [yesh] you another brother?"

• Proverbs 8:21 – "That I may cause those who love me to inherit wealth [yesh], that I may fill their treasuries."

• Jeremiah 31:17 – "There is [yesh] hope in your future, says the LORD, that your children shall come back to their own border."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between yesh (there is) and ayin (there is not) is a recurring motif in Hebrew wisdom literature. While human wisdom seeks to secure yesh through physical descendants, wealth, and strategic planning, the book of Ruth reveals that true yesh (lasting substance and hope) is a gift of God’s covenant faithfulness (hesed). Naomi’s emptiness (reqam in Ruth 1:21) is directly tied to her perception of having no yesh. Ultimately, God fills her emptiness, proving that His presence and promises are the ultimate reality.
