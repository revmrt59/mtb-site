LODGE - ( lun , Strong's H3885 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb lun primarily means to spend the night, stay over, or lodge temporarily, but it frequently carries the deeper theological and relational weight of permanent association, dwelling, or making one's home in a specific place. In Ruth's famous declaration to Naomi in Ruth 1:16, the word transcends a mere overnight stay; it signifies a lifelong covenant commitment to share in Naomi's daily life, struggles, shelter, and ultimate destiny, refusing to seek an easier path in her native Moab.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete background of lun is rooted in the nomadic and agrarian lifestyle of the ancient Near East. Travelers in a land without modern commercial lodging relied heavily on hospitality to survive the night. To lun was to find a safe haven, a shelter, or a pitching of one's tent for the evening to escape the dangers of the darkness, wild beasts, and bandits. Over time, the term expanded from a literal "sleeping overnight" to describe abiding, remaining, or settling down in a place of security and community.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The primary lexical definition of lun (also spelled lin) is to spend the night, stay, or lodge.

• In its literal sense, it refers to overnight lodging, often in contrast to traveling during the day.

• In a figurative or permanent sense, it denotes remaining, dwelling, or abiding continuously in a state, condition, or location.

• The word is distinct from yashab (to sit, dwell, or inhabit permanently) as lun emphasizes the intimate, daily reality of finding shelter and a resting place, which can then mature into permanent residence.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Traveler's Refuge: Used frequently of travelers seeking shelter before sunset, as when Jacob "stayed there all night" because the sun had set (Genesis 28:11).

• The Abiding Presence: Used metaphorically to describe wisdom, righteousness, or even God's presence abiding in a place or person. For example, justice once "lodged" in Jerusalem (Isaiah 1:21).

• Animal Habitation: Used to describe wild animals making their home or nesting in ruins, showing the transition of the word from human lodging to permanent, settled habitation (Zephaniah 2:14).

4. Theological Implications

In the context of covenant loyalty (hesed), lun represents a willingness to share in another person's vulnerability. To lodge where another lodges is to accept their standard of living, their poverty or wealth, and their exposure to danger. Ruth’s use of lun reflects a deep spiritual devotion. She is not merely offering temporary companionship on the road to Bethlehem; she is binding her physical existence and daily rest to Naomi’s. This mirrors the believer's call to abide in God—not as a temporary visitor, but as a permanent resident sharing in His life, protection, and suffering.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:16, Ruth uses the imperfect form of lun ("where you lodge, I will lodge"). The immediate context is Naomi’s urging for her daughters-in-law to return to their Moabite families and gods. While Orpah departs, Ruth clings to Naomi. The progression in Ruth's vow is highly structured, moving from outer actions to inner convictions: "wherever you go... wherever you lodge... your people... your God." By stating "where you lodge, I will lodge," Ruth is embracing Naomi's humble condition. Naomi is a destitute widow returning to a town she left years ago. She has no home of her own; she will likely have to find temporary, meager lodging. Ruth willingly embraces this lowliness. It is an expression of self-emptying love that refuses to seek comfort apart from the beloved.

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a homonym root lun which means "to murmur, grumble, or complain," used frequently of the Israelites in the wilderness. While lexicons treat these as two homonymous roots, the contrast is homiletically rich: instead of murmuring (lun) in her affliction as Naomi did (changing her name to Mara, "bitter"), Ruth chooses to lodge (lun) in faithfulness and quiet trust. Additionally, the Greek Septuagint (LXX) translates lun in Ruth 1:16 using the verb aulizomai, which means to lodge, spend the night, or find shelter in a courtyard, highlighting the physical reality of seeking protection together.
