STOPPED - ( chadal , Strong's H2308 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb chadal fundamentally means to cease, desist, or leave off doing something. In the context of Ruth 1:18, Naomi "stopped speaking" to Ruth once she realized that Ruth was utterly determined to accompany her back to Bethlehem. This cessation represents not a cold shoulder or angry silence, but a profound yielding to Ruth’s resolute, covenant-bound commitment. Naomi recognizes that further attempts to persuade Ruth to return to Moab are futile, and she accepts Ruth's shared destiny.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The root chadal carries the primary concrete idea of coming to an end, pausing an activity, or leaving something idle or unfinished. In ancient Near Eastern culture, the word often described agricultural or daily activities that ground to a halt, such as a land lying fallow, work ceasing, or an active endeavor being abandoned. It conveys a conscious decision to discontinue a specific path of action, whether due to inability, change of heart, or recognizing that further effort is useless.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic meaning is to cease, leave off, desist, or refrain. Depending on the grammatical construction, the verb indicates:

• To stop doing an action (often followed by an infinitive, as in Ruth 1:18).

• To refrain or decline from doing something (such as holding back from fighting or sinning).

• To be idle, quiet, or at rest.

• To fail or come to an end (as in the fading of strength or life).

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Chadal is used when someone actively halts an ongoing effort. For example, builders stop building a city (as in the Tower of Babel) or warriors cease fighting.

• It portrays the transition from active resistance or persuasion to absolute silence and acceptance. It is a verbal "dropping of the hands."

• It is also used to describe things that fade away or cease to exist, like the wealth of the rich, the physical strength of the elderly, or the flow of water.

### 4. Theological Implications

• Ceasing as an Act of Submission: When humans "cease" (chadal) their striving, it often represents a recognition of a higher reality or divine will. Naomi’s silence reflects a surrender to the sovereign hand of God working through Ruth’s loyalty (chesed).

• The Silence of Acceptance: Silence in Scripture can signify awe, acceptance of God's providence, or the end of human striving. When Naomi stops her protests, it marks the moment human argument yields to divine covenantal loyalty.

• Covenantal Resolve: Ruth's determination was so absolute that it silenced Naomi's pragmatic counsel. True covenant commitment (chesed) has a silencing, stabilizing effect on the anxiety and grief of others.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 1, Naomi had been actively and repeatedly urging her daughters-in-law to return to Moab. She used every logical, cultural, and spiritual argument to push them away for their own good.

• Orpah yielded and turned back, but Ruth clung to Naomi with her famous vow (Ruth 1:16-17).

• When Naomi saw that Ruth "was determined" (literally, "strengthened herself"), she chadal (stopped/ceased) speaking to her.

• This "stopping" indicates Naomi’s deep realization that Ruth’s mind was immovably set. Naomi's words of discouragement had run their course and met an unstoppable force of love and faith. The cessation of speech opens the way for their silent, shared journey back to the Promised Land.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 11:8 - "So the Lord scattered them abroad from there over the face of all the earth, and they stopped building the city." (Ceasing of human rebellion/striving)

• Exodus 9:34 - "And when Pharaoh saw that the rain, the hail, and the thunder had ceased, he sinned yet more; and he hardened his heart, he and his servants." (Ceasing of divine judgment)

• Deuteronomy 23:22 - "But if you abstain from vowing, it shall not be sin to you." (Refraining from an action)

• 1 Samuel 2:5 - "Those who were full have hired themselves out for bread, and the hungry have ceased to hunger..." (The cessation of a state of deprivation)

• Proverbs 19:27 - "Cease listening to instruction, my son, and you will stray from the words of knowledge." (A warning against halting a good spiritual habit)

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• Chadal contrasts sharply with continuous actions. Naomi's previous actions were loud, grief-stricken, and persistent. Her transition to chadal signals a dramatic shift in the narrative's volume—from weeping and arguing to a quiet, resolute walk toward Bethlehem.

• The Septuagint (LXX) translates chadal in Ruth 1:18 with the Greek verb ekopasen (from kopazo), which means to abate, grow weary, or cease. This is the same Greek word used when Jesus calmed the storm on the Sea of Galilee and the wind "ceased" (Mark 4:39, 6:51). The storm of Naomi's grief and argument was calmed by the steady anchor of Ruth’s commitment.
