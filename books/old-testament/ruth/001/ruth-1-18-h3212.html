GO - ( yalak , Strong's H3212 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word yalak primarily means to walk, go, or travel, representing physical movement from one place to another. In the narrative of Ruth, however, this "going" transcends mere geography. When Ruth is described as "determined to go" with Naomi, yalak signifies a profound, life-altering commitment. It is a walk of faith, a departure from her pagan homeland of Moab, and an intentional entry into the covenant community of Israel and the worship of Yahweh.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• Yalak (which is closely related to and shares forms with halak) conjures the image of taking active steps forward. In the Ancient Near East, walking or traveling was a rigorous, deliberate process requiring preparation, effort, and vulnerability. To "go" with someone meant sharing their path, their dust, their dangers, and their destination. It implies companionship, shared destiny, and a willingness to face the unknown.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Yalak means to walk, carry, conduct, or guide. It spans several nuances:

• Physical movement: to journey, travel, or depart.

• Behavioral/Ethical lifestyle: to "walk" in a certain manner or way of life (e.g., walking in uprightness).

• Covenantal loyalty: to follow after a leader, king, or deity.

In the grammatical form found in Ruth 1:18 (the infinitive construct laleketh), it expresses the direct, continuous action of journeying toward a specific, permanent destination.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Water flowing: Rivers "go" or run to the sea (Ecclesiastes 1:7).

• The wind blowing: The wind "goes" toward the south (Ecclesiastes 1:6).

• Spiritual devotion: Walking (yalak) after other gods (Deuteronomy 6:14) or walking in the law of Yahweh (Exodus 16:4).

• In Ruth 1:18, the word picture is of two widows traveling a hazardous highway from Moab to Bethlehem, symbolizing a transition from death and emptiness to life and fullness.

4. Theological Implications

• Yalak is deeply tied to the biblical concept of discipleship and covenant loyalty. To "go with" someone in a covenantal context is to bind one's destiny to theirs. Ruth's resolve to yalak with Naomi is the physical manifestation of her confession: "Your people shall be my people, and your God, my God." Just as Abraham was commanded to "go" (lek-leka) from his country to a land God would show him (Genesis 12:1), Ruth undergoes her own "Abrahamic" departure, walking by faith into an unknown future because she has trusted under the wings of the Lord God of Israel.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:18, Naomi sees that Ruth is "determined to go with her." The Hebrew word for "determined" (amats) means to be strong, courageous, or stouthearted. Combined with yalak, it depicts an unshakeable, stubborn resolve. Orpah kissed Naomi and turned back; Ruth clung to her and set her face to go. The verb highlights the contrast between Orpah’s retreat and Ruth’s advance. It shows that Ruth’s decision was not emotional sentimentality but a concrete, active resolve that silenced Naomi's attempts to persuade her otherwise.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 12:4 - "So Abram departed as the LORD had spoken to him, and Lot went with him."

• Exodus 13:21 - "And the LORD went before them by day in a pillar of cloud to lead the way..."

• Joshua 24:17 - "...and preserved us in all the way that we went, and among all the people through whom we passed."

• Psalm 23:4 - "Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Hebrew thought, truth is not merely intellectual; it is walked out. Yalak emphasizes that faith is kinetic. Ruth’s conversion was not finalized in a temple or through a ritual, but on a dusty road as she walked toward Bethlehem. Her theology was put into her feet.
