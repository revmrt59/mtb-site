STEADFASTLY MINDED - ( amats , Strong's H553 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb amats carries the core meaning of being physically strong, mentally alert, or resolutely courageous. In the context of Ruth 1:18, it appears in the intensive reflexive stem to describe a self-strengthening and unwavering resolve. Rather than a fleeting emotional reaction, Ruth’s decision to follow Naomi and embrace the God of Israel represents a hardened determination, a deliberate steeling of the will, and an unshakeable commitment that closed the door to any turning back.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root amats conveys the idea of physical toughness, internal strength, and dynamic energy. It depicts someone tightening their grip, bracing their feet, or hardening their resolve to withstand external pressure. In agricultural and military settings, it describes a soldier strengthening his arms for battle or a builder securing a foundation so it cannot be moved. Unlike passive endurance, this word portrays an active, self-fortifying strength. When applied to the mind and heart, it indicates a conscious decision to lock in one's focus, banish doubt, and press forward despite intimidating obstacles.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb amats refers to being strong, bold, courageous, or established. In its basic active forms, it means to make firm, to strengthen, or to secure. In the intensive reflexive stem (Hitpael), as seen in Ruth 1:18, the word means to strengthen oneself, to persist obstinately, or to show determination. This reflexive aspect indicates that the subject is actively summoning their own inner resolve, girding their mind, and making themselves firm. It denotes a deliberate hardening of purpose that renders a person impervious to persuasion, emotional pleas, or shifting circumstances.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, amats is frequently paired with chazaq (to be strong) in the command to "be strong and of good courage." While chazaq often emphasizes outward grip or physical holding fast, amats highlights the internal, mental, and physical stamina needed to conquer fear and remain steadfast. It is used to describe the strengthening of knees that are weak and the hardening of one's heart. In every context, it pictures an internal bracing that prepares a person to withstand testing or carry out a difficult course of action.

4. Theological Implications

Ruth’s "steadfast mind" illustrates the nature of genuine biblical covenant loyalty (chesed). Her resolve is not based on temporary sentimentality or youthful optimism; it is a self-consecrating determination that aligns her will with the God of Israel. Theologically, this highlights how saving faith and covenant commitment involve the total person—including a fortified will. When a believer resolves to follow God, they must strengthen themselves against the pull of their old life, choosing a path of no return. Ruth’s determination showcases a faith that is settled, resolute, and immune to discouragement.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:18, Naomi has repeatedly urged her daughters-in-law to return to their pagan homelands, presenting them with the grim reality of poverty and childlessness in Israel. Orpah yields to this pressure and returns, but Ruth clings to Naomi. When Naomi sees that Ruth is "steadfastly minded" (mit'ammetsah), she ceases her efforts to persuade her. The reflexive form shows that Ruth has actively steeled her own mind. She has weighed the costs, rejected the easier path, and locked in her decision. Her determination is so absolute and visibly immovable that Naomi realizes further argument is useless.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Deuteronomy 31:7 - "Then Moses called Joshua and said to him in the sight of all Israel, 'Be strong and of good courage, for you must go with this people to the land which the LORD has sworn to their fathers to give them, and you shall cause them to inherit it.'"

• Joshua 1:6 - "Be strong and of good courage, for to this people you shall divide as an inheritance the land which I swore to their fathers to give them."

• Psalm 27:14 - "Wait on the LORD; Be of good courage, And He shall strengthen your heart; Wait, I say, on the LORD!"

• Proverbs 31:17 - "She girds herself with strength, And strengthens her arms."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A striking contrast exists in the book of Ruth between the physical emptiness of Moab and the spiritual resolution of Ruth. While Moab represents compromise and death, Ruth’s steadfast mind becomes the bridge to redemption. In Jewish commentary, Ruth's determination is seen as the ultimate model for conversion—a candidate who cannot be dissuaded by any hardship. Pastoral application of this word highlights that spiritual growth and covenant faithfulness are not passive experiences. While grace enables our faith, we are called to actively strengthen ourselves in the Lord, making a definitive, unshakeable break from the past.
