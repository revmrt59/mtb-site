EXCITED - ( hum , Strong's H1949 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb hum (H1949) conveys the idea of making a great noise, ringing out, or being in a state of loud, vibrant commotion. In the context of Ruth 1:19, it describes the collective, high-energy stir and emotional commotion of the entire city of Bethlehem upon the sudden and unexpected return of Naomi and Ruth. Rather than a quiet arrival, their presence triggers an immediate, resonant uproar of astonishment, whispering, and excitement throughout the community.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root of hum is deeply connected to auditory impact. It represents a physical vibration or a resonant wave of sound that fills an entire space. The word picture is that of a vessel or an enclosed valley echoing with a loud, vibrating noise, such as a great shout that causes the earth itself to reverberate, or a city square filled with the chaotic clamor of an unexpected event. It is not a silent, internal emotion but an external, audible, and visible wave of communal energy that sweeps through a population.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb hum means to show stir, to make a great noise, to be in an uproar, or to resound. In the Hebrew semantic range, it denotes an intense state of agitation, excitement, or disturbance that manifests as sound. Lexically, it describes the collective buzzing, shouting, or emotional turbulence of a crowd. It highlights a transition from quiet normalcy to sudden, noisy activity.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

In biblical usage, hum is employed in highly charged communal settings:

• The roaring of a crowd or a city thrown into sudden excitement or panic.

• The reverberating echo of a collective shout that makes the ground feel as though it is shaking.

• The chaotic, buzzing noise of a sheepfold filled with a vast multitude of animals, symbolizing the bustling return of a scattered people.

Each picture reinforces the concept of a sound-driven, contagious movement that cannot be ignored or quieted.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, hum demonstrates the social nature of God’s redemptive and disciplinary actions. Naomi’s departure from Bethlehem was quiet and marked by famine, but her return—orchestrated by the sovereign hand of God bringing her back empty yet preserved—creates a public stir. The uproar of the city serves as a public witness to Naomi's transition from blessing to bitterness, setting the stage for God's providential restoration. It shows that God's dealings with His people are not done in secret; they impact the wider community of faith, prompting reflection, conversation, and ultimately, praise.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:19, the use of hum captures the sudden shock waves that went through Bethlehem. Ten years had passed since Naomi left. She departed with a husband and two sons; she returned destitute, accompanied only by a Moabitess daughter-in-law. The sight of Naomi, aged by grief and hardship, causes the entire town to buzz with whispers and exclamations. The community does not merely notice her; they are thrown into a noisy stir. The women's question, "Is this Naomi?" is a collective gasp of disbelief and pity, echoing the acoustic power of the word.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• 1 Samuel 4:5: "And when the ark of the covenant of the Lord came into the camp, all Israel shouted with a great shout, so that the earth rang again."

• 1 Kings 1:45: "and Zadok the priest and Nathan the prophet have anointed him king at Gihon; and they have gone up from there rejoicing, so that the city is in an uproar. This is the noise that you have heard."

• Micah 2:12: "I will surely assemble all of you, O Jacob, I will surely gather the remnant of Israel; I will put them together like sheep of Bozrah, Like a flock in the midst of their pasture; They shall make a loud noise because of the multitude of men."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Greek Septuagint translates the Hebrew hum in Ruth 1:19 with the verb echeo, which means "to make a sound, to ring, or to echo." This further solidifies the acoustic nature of the word. The entire city did not merely experience an internal feeling of surprise; they literally echoed with the news. The report of Naomi's return bounced from house to house like a loud echo in an enclosed valley.
