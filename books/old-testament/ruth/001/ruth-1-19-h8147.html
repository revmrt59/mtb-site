TWO - ( shenayim , Strong's H8147 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word shenayim denotes the cardinal number "two," but in the narrative of Ruth 1:19, it functions as a poignant marker of companionship, shared grief, and exclusive solidarity. In this passage, "the two of them" (shtēhem) refers to Naomi and Ruth journeying together from Moab to Bethlehem. This term highlights their singular bond after the devastating loss of their husbands, emphasizing that out of a fractured family of several members, only these two vulnerable widows remain to face an uncertain future together.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient Hebrew thought, the number two (shenayim) represents the basic unit of companionship, testimony, and partnership. Traveling in the ancient Near East was fraught with danger, particularly for women without male protection. By describing Naomi and Ruth as "the two of them," the narrative paints a picture of mutual reliance and vulnerability. The phrase evokes the image of a pair walking in step, carrying a shared burden of grief, yet bound by a covenant commitment that transcends biological ties. It is a word picture of solidarity against a hostile or indifferent world.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word shenayim is the cardinal number two. It is used in dual or plural constructions to indicate a pair, a duplicate, or a couple. In its feminine form with a pronominal suffix, as seen in Ruth 1:19 (shtēhem), it means "the two of them" or "both of them." The root signifies duplication or repetition, representing a complete, self-contained unit of two individuals acting in unison. It denotes not merely a mathematical count, but a functional unity where two distinct parties operate as one cohesive entity.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, the use of shenayim to describe two people walking together carries deep narrative weight.

• Cooperation and Strength: It illustrates the principle of shared labor and mutual defense, demonstrating that a pair can withstand hardships that would overwhelm an individual.

• Covenant and Agreement: It is used to describe two parties entering into a solemn agreement or walking in unified purpose.

• Shared Destiny: When applied to travelers, it depicts a shared journey where the destination, risks, and outcomes belong equally to both individuals.

### 4. Theological Implications

Theologically, "the two of them" walking toward Bethlehem illustrates the operational reality of God’s covenant love (hesed) in times of tragedy.

• God's Provision of Companionship: Naomi felt entirely empty and abandoned by God, yet God did not leave her completely alone; He provided Ruth. The "two" represents the minimal remnant of a family that God will miraculously use to rebuild a lineage.

• The Seed of Redemption: This pair contains the seed of Israel's future. From this fragile duo of grieving widows, God will bring forth Boaz’s household, King David, and ultimately the Messiah. It shows that God often begins His greatest works of redemption with a small, vulnerable, and overlooked pair.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:19, the phrase "the two of them went" marks a transition from the destructive environment of Moab to the potential restoration of Bethlehem.

• The Silence of the Journey: The text does not record a single word spoken between Naomi and Ruth during their long, arduous trek. The phrase "the two of them went" conveys a quiet, determined resolve. They walk in a silence born of grief, but also of profound mutual understanding.

• Contrast with the Beginning: The book of Ruth begins with a family of four (Elimelech, Naomi, and their two sons). It now narrows down to just "two." This reduction emphasizes Naomi’s sense of loss, but also highlights the miraculous nature of the grace that will multiply this "two" into a legacy of hope.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:25 - "And they were both naked, the man and his wife, and were not ashamed." (Here, shenayim emphasizes the original, uncorrupted unity of the first couple).

• Genesis 22:6 - "So he took the wood of the burnt offering and laid it on Isaac his son; and he took the fire in his hand, and a knife, and the two of them went together." (This echoes the silent, solemn walk of faith between father and son toward a sacrifice).

• 2 Kings 2:6 - "Then Elijah said to him, 'Stay here, please, for the Lord has sent me to the Jordan.' But he said, 'As the Lord lives, and as your soul lives, I will not leave you!' So the two of them went on." (This parallel highlights a disciple's refusal to leave his mentor, mirroring Ruth's loyalty to Naomi).

• Ecclesiastes 4:9 - "Two are better than one, Because they have a good reward for their labor." (A direct statement on the practical and spiritual value of companionship).

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

The repetition of "the two of them" in scripture often signals a moment of deep testing or transition. Just as Abraham and Isaac walked together toward Moriah, and Elijah and Elisha walked together toward the Jordan, Naomi and Ruth walk together toward Bethlehem. In each case, "the two of them" represents a bond tested by fire, characterized by absolute commitment, and destined to witness a supernatural intervention of God.
