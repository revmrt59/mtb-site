MOAB - ( Mo'ab , Strong's H4124 )

Summary:

Moab refers to the geographical region east of the Dead Sea and the Jordan River, as well as the people group descended from Lot's son Moab. In the context of Ruth 1:2, it represents a place of physical refuge during a famine, but one carrying deep theological tension, as Moab was a historical adversary of Israel and associated with idolatry.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• Moab originates from the incestuous union between Lot and his eldest daughter after the destruction of Sodom (Genesis 19:37).

• The name literally means "from father" or "of my father" (mê-'āb).

• Geographically, Moab is a high, fertile plateau east of the Dead Sea, characterized by deep ravines, suitable for agriculture and sheep-rearing.

• Historically, Israel and Moab had a volatile relationship. Balak, king of Moab, hired Balaam to curse Israel (Numbers 22-24). During the period of the Judges, Eglon, king of Moab, oppressed Israel for eighteen years until assassinated by Ehud (Judges 3).

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The proper noun Mo'ab designates the progenitor of the nation, the nation itself (the Moabites), and the territory they occupied.

• The territory was bounded by the Arnon gorge to the north (though boundaries shifted) and the Zered brook to the south, with the Arabian desert to the east and the Dead Sea to the west.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Moab is used as a geographic designation (the "country" or "fields" of Moab—sedeh mo'ab).

• Moab is used as a collective noun for the descendants of Lot who worshipped Chemosh, their national deity (Numbers 21:29; 1 Kings 11:7).

• Moab is sometimes pictured in prophetic literature as a symbol of pride and security due to its natural, high-elevation defenses, which eventually face divine judgment (Isaiah 16; Jeremiah 48).

4. Theological Implications

• Moab represents a complex theological paradox in Israel’s history. Deuteronomy 23:3–4 strictly barred Moabites from entering the assembly of the Lord to the tenth generation because of their hostility during the Exodus.

• However, Moab also serves as a sovereign instrument of God's providence and mercy. God specifically commanded Israel not to harass Moab or take their land as an inheritance because He had given Ar to the descendants of Lot (Deuteronomy 2:9).

• Ultimately, the inclusion of Ruth the Moabitess in the lineage of King David and Jesus Christ demonstrates that God’s redemptive grace transcends national and covenantal barriers, redeeming those from cursed origins through faith and covenant loyalty.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 1:2, Elimelech leads his family from Bethlehem ("House of Bread") in Judah to the "country of Moab" due to a famine.

• This move represents an ironic and tragic spiritual compromise. Seeking physical sustenance, the family leaves the Promised Land of inheritance to dwell in a pagan territory that was historically excluded from Israel’s assembly.

• The word "country" (sadeh) literally means "fields" or "tableland" of Moab, highlighting its agricultural fertility at a time when Judah was barren. The narrative uses "Moab" to set up a stark contrast between Israel's spiritual/physical famine and the worldly abundance of an enemy nation, setting the stage for loss and eventual redemption.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 19:37 - "And the firstborn bore a son and called his name Moab; he is the father of the Moabites to this day."

• Numbers 22:3 - "And Moab was exceedingly afraid of the people because they were many, and Moab was sick with dread because of the children of Israel."

• Deuteronomy 34:5 - "So Moses the servant of the Lord died there in the land of Moab, according to the word of the Lord."

• Ruth 1:22 - "So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabitess her daughter-in-law with her, who returned from the country of Moab. Now they came to Bethlehem at the beginning of barley harvest."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The "Moabite Stone" (Mesha Stele), discovered in 1868, dates to the 9th century BC and provides a non-biblical perspective on the conflicts between Moab (under King Mesha) and Israel (under the dynasty of Omri), confirming the historical details of 2 Kings 3 and the worship of Chemosh.

• The journey from Bethlehem to Moab is physically demanding, requiring a descent of about 2,000 feet to the Jordan Rift Valley and a climb of over 3,000 feet up to the Moabite plateau, symbolizing a deep downward and arduous journey away from the place of God's presence.
