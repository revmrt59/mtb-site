WENT - ( bo , Strong's H935 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb bo' is a fundamental and highly versatile word of motion, primarily meaning "to go in," "to enter," or "to come." In Ruth 1:2, it describes Elimelech and his family entering the foreign territory of Moab. Far more than a mere geographical transition, this word marks a pivotal boundary-crossing from the promised land of covenant provision into a pagan land of compromise, setting the stage for the unfolding tragedy and ultimate redemption in the narrative.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The root concept of bo' depicts movement from one space into another, emphasizing the crossing of a threshold or boundary to arrive at a destination. In the ancient Near East, entering a foreign territory was not merely a matter of travel but of changing jurisdictions. It meant leaving behind the safety of one's community, inheritance, and the immediate sphere of covenant life to enter a realm governed by different gods and customs. The word picture is one of transition, arrival, and settling into a new domain.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The basic semantic range of bo' includes "to come in," "to go in," "to enter," "to bring," or "to arrive."

• It stands in contrast to halak (to walk or go), which emphasizes the process of journeying. Bo' focuses on the arrival or entry into the destination itself.

• When used of people entering a country, it signifies arrival with the intent of staying, residing, or taking up residence.

• It functions as the standard Hebrew verb for entering a house, a city, a tent, or a territory.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

The word is used in a variety of literal and figurative contexts across the Old Testament:

• Astronomical: It describes the setting of the sun, picturing the sun "going in" or entering its chamber at the end of the day.

• Relational: It is used euphemistically for sexual intimacy, described as "going in" to a partner.

• Covenantal: It is used of entering into an oath or covenant relationship with God.

• Agricultural: It describes the harvest being brought in or gathered.

### 4. Theological Implications

Leaving Bethlehem ("House of Bread") in the Promised Land to enter (bo') Moab represents a failure of faith in the covenant promises of Yahweh. The Promised Land was the sphere of God's blessing and discipline; even in famine, God's people were called to trust Him there. To leave it was to step outside the geographic sphere of covenant blessing. Moab was born of incest and historically associated with idolatry and opposition to Israel. Entering Moab represents seeking survival through worldly compromise rather than trusting in Yahweh's provision.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:2, "they went (bo') to the country of Moab and remained there." The narrative progression is tragic: they went (entered) and they remained (literally, "were there" or settled). What was intended as a temporary escape from famine became a permanent dwelling, leading to the death of the father and his two sons. The verb bo' highlights that the family did not just travel toward Moab; they successfully crossed the threshold, entered the land, and fully committed to their new environment.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 7:7: "So Noah, with his sons, his wife, and his sons’ wives, went into the ark because of the waters of the flood."

• Genesis 15:15: "Now as for you, you shall go to your fathers in peace; you shall be buried at a good old age."

• Psalm 100:2: "Serve the Lord with gladness; Come before His presence with singing."

• Psalm 118:20: "This is the gate of the Lord, Through which the righteous shall enter."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

The structural movement of the Book of Ruth relies heavily on the tension between bo' (to enter/go) and shub (to return). While Elimelech's family "went" (bo') into Moab, the turning point of the book occurs when Naomi decides to "return" (shub) to Bethlehem. Salvation and restoration do not come by going deeper into foreign compromises, but by returning to the place of God's covenant presence.
