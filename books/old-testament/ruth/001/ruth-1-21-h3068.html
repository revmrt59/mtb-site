LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh (LORD) is the personal, covenant name of the God of Israel, representing His eternal self-existence, absolute faithfulness, and relational closeness to His people. In Ruth 1:21, Naomi uses this sacred covenant name alongside Shaddai (the Almighty) to express her deep grief, acknowledging that the very God who bound Himself to Israel in love is the sovereign ruler who has brought empty-handed calamity upon her life.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew name Yahweh is the personal and covenant-keeping name of God. It is derived from the Hebrew verb hayah, which means "to be" or "to exist." This name represents God's active, self-determining, and eternal existence. Historically, this name was uniquely revealed to Moses at the burning bush as "I AM WHO I AM," establishing that God is self-sufficient, unchanging, and intimately involved in the history of His people. While Elohim is a generic title for God as the powerful Creator, Yahweh is His personal name, signifying His relational closeness, holiness, and binding commitment to His covenant partners.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The proper noun Yahweh refers to the one true God of Israel. Lexically, the name means "He who is" or "the Self-Existent One." It denotes the absolute, uncaused, and eternal nature of God. The semantic range is tied strictly to the unique identity of the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. It designates Him as the sovereign Creator, Savior, and Lawgiver who is eternally faithful to His word. In English translations, it is traditionally represented by the capitalized "LORD" to maintain the distinction from the Hebrew word Adonai (lord or master).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Yahweh is used more than 6,800 times in the Old Testament, representing the primary designation for God in scripture. In biblical usage, this name is associated with several vivid word pictures:

• The Shepherd who guides, protects, and feeds His flock, demonstrating intimate care.

• The Husband who is passionately committed to His covenant partner, Israel, exhibiting deep faithfulness and jealousy for her affection.

• The Rock and Strong Tower, symbolizing immutability, stability, and absolute safety for those who seek refuge in Him.

• The Deliverer and Warrior who rescues His people from bondage with a mighty hand, demonstrating that He is actively present in human affairs.

4. Theological Implications

• Eternal Self-Existence: Yahweh has no beginning and no end. He is entirely self-sufficient, needing nothing outside of Himself, while all creation depends on Him for continuous existence.

• Covenant Faithfulness: The name is structurally linked to God's covenants. When God reveals Himself as Yahweh, He binds Himself to His promises. He cannot lie, change, or fail to fulfill His word.

• Sovereign Grace: The name is revealed in the context of redemption. It displays a God who hears the cries of the oppressed, descends to deliver them, and establishes a relationship with them based on His sovereign grace rather than their merit.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:21, Naomi returns to Bethlehem from Moab, grieving the loss of her husband and her two sons. She declares, "I went out full, and the LORD has brought me home again empty. Why do you call me Naomi, since the LORD has testified against me, and the Almighty has afflicted me?"

• Relational Pain: Naomi does not blame blind fate, chance, or Moabite gods for her misery. She attributes her loss directly to Yahweh. Because Yahweh is her covenant God, her pain is deeply relational; she feels that the God who promised to bless His people has personally brought empty-handed ruin upon her.

• Sovereign Adversary: By stating that the LORD has "testified against me," Naomi uses legal language, casting Yahweh as a prosecuting witness or legal adversary who has brought a judgment of affliction upon her.

• Sovereign Contrast: Naomi pairs Yahweh with Shaddai (the Almighty). This juxtaposition balances God's covenant-relational name with His all-powerful sovereignty, underscoring Naomi's conviction that nothing happens outside of God's direct, sovereign administration, even when His actions feel intensely hostile.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:4 - "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens,"

• Exodus 3:15 - "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

• Psalm 23:1 - "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

• Proverbs 18:10 - "The name of the LORD is a strong tower; The righteous run into it and are safe."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Ineffable Name: In Jewish tradition, the name Yahweh was treated with such profound reverence that it became ineffable (unpronounceable). To avoid violating the command against taking the LORD's name in vain, the post-exilic community substituted Adonai (Lord) or HaShem (The Name) when reading the scriptures aloud.

• The Irony of Naomi's Grief: Though Naomi returns "empty" and feels that Yahweh has afflicted her, the broader narrative reveals that Yahweh is in the process of restoring her. The very emptiness she laments is the canvas upon which Yahweh will paint His redemption through Ruth, Boaz, and ultimately, the birth of Obed, keeping His covenant promises alive for future generations.
