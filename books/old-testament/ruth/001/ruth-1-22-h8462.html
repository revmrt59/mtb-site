BEGINNING - ( techillah , Strong's H8462 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word techillah refers to the initiation, commencement, or first phase of an event, period of time, or sequence of actions. In Ruth 1:22, it marks a critical pivot point in the narrative, designating the precise onset of the barley harvest. This timing is not merely chronological but deeply theological, signaling the transition from Naomi's bitter emptiness to God's gracious provision and redemption.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The word techillah is derived from the root chalal, which in its causative form means to begin, open, or initiate.

• As a noun, techillah specifically denotes the very first step, opening act, or starting point of a process. It represents the threshold of an event—the moment when something that was dormant or non-existent begins to unfold.

• In the agrarian rhythm of ancient Israel, the beginning of a harvest was a time of immense anticipation. It marked the transition from winter scarcity to springtime abundance, symbolizing hope and the physical manifestation of God's faithfulness to His covenant people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Techillah means the beginning, start, or first part of an event, time period, or series of actions.

• It is used to designate:

• The commencement of a historical period or event (such as the beginning of a reign or a prophet's ministry).

• The initial phase of an agricultural cycle (such as the barley harvest).

• The first in a sequence of events or decisions.

• While another Hebrew word for beginning (reshith) often emphasizes the chief part, firstfruits, or ultimate origin (as in Genesis 1:1), techillah emphasizes the temporal start or the opening sequence of a concrete, historical process.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The term is used throughout the Old Testament to frame major historical and spiritual transitions. It describes the beginning of Hosea's prophecy (Hosea 1:2) and the beginning of the settlement of foreign nations in Samaria (2 Kings 17:25).

• The word picture is that of crossing a threshold. It is the opening of a gate or the first stroke of a sickle in the field. It carries the weight of expectation—what begins here will have a progression and a completion.

• In agrarian terms, the beginning of the harvest represents the physical manifestation of God's renewed favor to His land, ending the famine that drove Elimelech's family away in the first place.

4. Theological Implications

• The timing of Naomi and Ruth's arrival at the "beginning" of the barley harvest demonstrates the quiet, sovereign providence of God. Throughout the first chapter of Ruth, Naomi laments her emptiness and bereavement. However, God's timing is impeccable.

• It illustrates how God coordinates natural cycles (the agricultural seasons) with redemptive history. The harvest is a physical provision that becomes the canvas for spiritual and relational restoration.

• God does not just provide food; He provides a redeemer (Boaz) whose introduction is made possible because Naomi and Ruth arrived at this precise starting point. The techillah of the harvest is the techillah of their redemption.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 1:22, techillah acts as a literary hinge. The chapter begins with a famine (scarcity) and ends with the "beginning of barley harvest" (abundance).

• Naomi has just declared that she went out full and the Lord brought her home empty (Ruth 1:21). The narrator immediately counters Naomi's bitter assessment of her life by placing her in Bethlehem at the techillah of the barley harvest.

• The word contrasts her internal sense of finality and death with God's external reality of new beginnings and life. Her empty hands are about to be filled because she has arrived at the starting line of God’s provision.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 13:3: "And he went on his journey from the South as far as Bethel, to the place where his tent had been at the beginning, between Bethel and Ai,"

• 2 Samuel 21:9: "and they fell all seven together, and were put to death in the days of harvest, in the first days, in the beginning of barley harvest."

• Daniel 9:23: "At the beginning of your supplications the command went out, and I have come to tell you, for you are greatly beloved;"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The barley harvest is the earliest of the harvests in Israel, occurring in late March or early April (the month of Abib/Nisan). It preceded the wheat harvest by several weeks.

• Entering Bethlehem at the techillah of this harvest meant that Naomi and Ruth arrived exactly when the gleaning laws (Leviticus 19:9-10) were being put into active practice. This was not a day too early or a day too late; it was the exact moment required for Ruth to go into the fields and meet Boaz.

• The literary structure of Ruth uses this word to frame the transition from tragedy to hope, laying the foundation for the unfolding redemption in chapters 2, 3, and 4.
