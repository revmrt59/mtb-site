CAME - ( bo , Strong's H935 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb bo means to come in, enter, or arrive. In Ruth 1:22, it marks the physical arrival of Naomi and Ruth in Bethlehem after a long, grueling journey of grief and exile in Moab. This movement is far more than a geographical transition; it signifies a spiritual and emotional pivot from emptiness to fulfillment, and from foreign isolation back into the sphere of God's covenant blessings, perfectly timed with the beginning of the barley harvest.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root bo is one of the most frequently used verbs in the Hebrew Bible, occurring over 2,500 times. Its primary physical action involves entering a specific destination, crossing a threshold, or arriving at a designated space. In the ancient Near East, travel was fraught with danger, especially for two unaccompanied widows. To safely "come" or "arrive" at a destination was a profound sign of preservation and provision. For Naomi and Ruth, leaving the pagan fields of Moab and crossing the Jordan to enter the promised land of Israel was a journey from death back to life.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb bo fundamentally means to enter, come, or go in. Depending on the context, it denotes the movement of a person or object toward a specific goal. In the Qal stem, which is the form used in Ruth 1:22, it expresses the simple active reality of reaching a destination or entering a city. It carries the semantic weight of completed arrival and presence—they are no longer merely on the way; they have crossed the threshold and are now physically present in the destination.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Entrance and Covenant: Bo is frequently used to describe entering into a covenant, entering the tabernacle, or bringing an offering into the presence of the Lord.

• The Merism of Life: It is often paired with its antonym yatsa ("to go out") to form a Hebrew merism ("to go out and come in"), which represents the entirety of a person's life, daily activities, and leadership responsibility.

• The Widow's Threshold: In Ruth 1:22, the word picture is that of two vulnerable women crossing the town borders of Bethlehem. It portrays a homecoming, transitioning from the status of wandering outsiders to established insiders who have come home to the community of faith.

4. Theological Implications

The arrival in Bethlehem ("House of Bread") is a vivid narrative illustration of repentance, restoration, and providence.

• The Return to Grace: Naomi went out full and returned empty, yet her "coming" back to the land of promise is the indispensable first step toward her restoration.

• Sovereign Timing: The timing of their arrival—coinciding precisely with the beginning of the barley harvest—highlights God’s unseen hand. Their act of coming to Bethlehem positions them to receive the abundant grace and redemption that God has already prepared for them.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:22, bo serves as the literary hinge of the entire chapter and the prelude to the rest of the book. The narrative shifts from tragedy, famine, and death in Moab to hope, life, and harvest in Bethlehem. The specific destination, Bethlehem, is crucial because it is the place of covenant promise and ancestral heritage. The simple statement "they came" resolves the tension of their long, dangerous trek and sets the stage for the meeting with Boaz in the fields of harvest.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 7:1: "Then the LORD said to Noah, 'Come into the ark, you and all your household, because I have seen that you are righteous before Me in this generation.'"

• Joshua 2:1: "Now Joshua the son of Nun sent out two men from Acacia Grove to spy secretly, saying, 'Go, view the land, especially Jericho.' So they went, and came to the house of a harlot named Rahab, and lodged there."

• Psalm 121:8: "The LORD shall preserve your going out and your coming in from this time forth, and even forevermore."

7. Other Relevant Insights

A structural contrast exists between Naomi's lament in Ruth 1:21 ("I went out full") and the narrator's statement in Ruth 1:22 ("And they came to Bethlehem"). The movement of "going out" brought empty grief, but the movement of "coming in" brings them to the harvest. This reminds the reader that even when we feel empty, our steps of returning to God's place of blessing are met with His prepared abundance.
