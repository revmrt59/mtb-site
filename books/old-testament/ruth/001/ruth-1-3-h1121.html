SONS - ( ben , Strong's H1121 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word ben refers primarily to a male offspring, son, or descendant, symbolizing the continuation of a family line, inheritance, and covenantal legacy. In Ruth 1:3, "her two sons" (Mahlon and Chilion) represent Naomi's remaining hope for survival, legacy, and provision in a foreign land after the death of her husband, Elimelech. Their presence underscores both temporary security and the impending crisis of total loss when they too pass away.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word ben is derived from the root banah, which means "to build." In ancient Hebrew thought, a son is literally "a builder" of the family house or lineage. Through sons, a father's name, property, and legacy are built up and preserved for future generations. In ancient Israelite culture, sons were not merely loved family members; they were the economic safety net, the legal heirs, and the essential link in preserving the family’s allotment of the Promised Land.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The primary meaning of ben is a male child, son, or direct male offspring.

• The term extends semantically to denote a grandson, descendant, or member of a specific group, nation, or guild (such as "sons of Israel" or "sons of the prophets").

• It is also used figuratively to describe someone characterized by a certain quality or age (such as "son of worthlessness" or "a son of fifty years").

• In the plural form (banim), as found in Ruth 1:3, it denotes the immediate male offspring of Naomi and Elimelech.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Builder of the House: Because ben shares a root with banah (to build), Hebrew thought envisions sons as the living stones that construct and support the household.

• The Arrow in the Hand: Old Testament imagery likens sons born in one's youth to arrows in the hand of a warrior, representing protection, strength, and future impact.

• Covenantal Continuity: Sons carry the covenant sign and the family name forward, ensuring that a family's portion in Israel is not blotted out.

4. Theological Implications

• The concept of the "son" is central to the Abrahamic Covenant, which relies on the provision of a promised son (Isaac) to carry the seed.

• In the broader context of redemptive history, the genealogy of the "son" leads ultimately to David, and eventually to the ultimate Son, Jesus Christ.

• The loss of sons in Israel was equivalent to the death of the family line and the loss of God's inheritance, highlighting the depths of Naomi's grief and the magnitude of God's subsequent redemption.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 1:3, the mention of Naomi's "two sons" immediately follows the death of her husband, Elimelech. In the patriarchal ancient Near East, a widow without a husband was highly vulnerable, but the survival of her two sons meant she still had legal protection, economic viability, and a future for her family name.

• The narrative introduces them as her remaining support system, only to heighten the tragedy in verse 5 when both sons also die, leaving Naomi completely destitute, empty, and without a "builder" for her household.

• The contrast between having "two sons" in verse 3 and being "bereft of her two sons" in verse 5 emphasizes the total collapse of Naomi's earthly security.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 22:2 - "Then He said, 'Take now your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love, and go to the land of Moriah...'"

• Isaiah 9:6 - "For unto us a Child is born, Unto us a son is given; And the government will be upon His shoulder..."

• Ruth 4:17 - "Also the neighbor women gave him a name, saying, 'There is a son born to Naomi.' And they called his name Obed."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The names of the two sons, Mahlon ("sickly") and Chilion ("pining/frail"), ironically foreshadow their short lives and their inability to fulfill the traditional, robust role of the ben (the builder of the house).

• The book of Ruth begins with the loss of sons (banim) and reaches its theological and emotional climax in Ruth 4:17 with the birth of a new "son" (ben) to Naomi through Ruth and Boaz, demonstrating God’s power to rebuild what was completely destroyed.
