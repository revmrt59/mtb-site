TWO - ( shenayim , Strong's H8147 )

Summary:

The Hebrew cardinal number shenayim denotes the numeral "two," representing a pair, a dual relationship, or a minimum plurality. In the narrative of Ruth 1:3, it refers specifically to Naomi's two sons, Mahlon and Chilion, who represent her remaining familial hope and future in Moab after the sudden death of her husband, Elimelech.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• shenayim is the dual form derived from a root that carries the basic idea of repetition, doing something a second time, or doubling. In ancient Near Eastern thought and Hebrew literature, numbers often carry both literal mathematical value and symbolic weight. The number two represents the basic unit of companionship, division, or legal testimony. In genealogical and narrative contexts, "two sons" often sets up a structural contrast or a dual path of destiny, as seen historically with Cain and Abel, Ishmael and Isaac, or Jacob and Esau. In Ruth, the two sons represent Naomi's remaining strength and the fragile continuity of Elimelech's lineage in a foreign land.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word functions primarily as a cardinal numeral meaning "two" or a dual adjective meaning "both" or "a pair." It can express doubling, division into two parts, or a collaborative pair. In grammatical construct states (such as shene in Ruth 1:3), it directly quantifies the masculine plural noun that follows, translating directly to "two sons of" Naomi.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, shenayim is used to establish pairs that function in unison or contrast, such as the two tablets of the covenant, two witnesses required for legal testimony, or the animals entering the ark in pairs. It serves as a literary device to create symmetry. When applied to siblings, it often prepares the reader for a division of outcomes or a shared tragic fate. The pairing of two individuals emphasizes complete representation or the minimum requirement for legal validation and strength, reflecting the biblical principle that "two are better than one."

4. Theological Implications

The number two represents testimony and legal confirmation under the Mosaic Law. In the Book of Ruth, the two sons—and later the two daughters-in-law, Orpah and Ruth—serve as dual witnesses to Naomi's grief and the unfolding providence of God. The reduction of Naomi's family from three men (Elimelech and his two sons) to none highlights the themes of emptiness and vulnerability. The death of the "two" sons represents the complete severance of the family line, setting the stage for God's redemptive provision through the kinsman-redeemer. The two sons also illustrate the vulnerability of leaving the land of promise for the land of compromise, where both sons ultimately perish without heirs.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:3, the word shene ("two") quantifies her sons (baneha). After the death of Elimelech, these two sons are Naomi's only remaining connection to her deceased husband's name and inheritance. The presence of "two sons" highlights Naomi's temporary security. She is not yet entirely destitute, as she still has two male heirs who can marry and preserve the family line. However, the narrative immediately transitions to their marriages to Moabite women and their subsequent deaths, turning the "two sons" into a double portion of grief. The loss of both sons leaves Naomi doubly bereaved, stripped of both her husband and her entire posterity.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The names of the two sons, Mahlon ("sickly") and Chilion ("pining" or "vanishing"), structurally mirror their physical destiny. The numeral "two" groups them together in their brief lives, foreign marriages, and untimely deaths, emphasizing that apart from God's covenant land, even a double portion of sons cannot guarantee the survival of a lineage. This pairs structurally with the "two" daughters-in-law in the subsequent verses. One daughter-in-law will return to her gods, while the other will cleave to Naomi and the God of Israel, showing how a pair can split into two divergent spiritual destinies.
