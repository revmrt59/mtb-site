SURVIVED - ( sha'ar , Strong's H7604 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb sha'ar refers to being left behind, remaining as a remnant, or surviving after a devastating loss. In Ruth 1:5, it captures Naomi’s profound grief and vulnerability. Having lost her husband and both sons in Moab, she is the sole "surviving" remnant of her family. Though she feels utterly empty and abandoned, her survival is the quiet starting point for God’s unexpected, redemptive story.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient agrarian and military contexts, sha'ar describes what is left over after a sweeping harvest, a consuming plague, or a devastating battle. It is the residue left behind when everything else has been stripped away. The word evoke images of a single tree standing in a field after a forest fire, or a couple of forgotten grapes hanging on a vine after the harvest has concluded. In the ancient Near East, survival was not merely a matter of biological existence; it was tied to family line, protection, and inheritance. For a woman in Naomi’s day, to "survive" her husband and her sons meant she was left without a legal identity, social standing, or economic security.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The root sha'ar means to remain, be left behind, survive, or exist as a remnant. In the Niphal stem, which is used in Ruth 1:5 (vattishsha'er), the verb means to be left over, to remain behind after others have died or departed, or to be preserved. It denotes the survival of a small portion after the destruction or depletion of the larger group. The lexical focus is not on the strength of the survivor, but on the fact of their solitary existence in the wake of tragedy.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, sha'ar is frequently used in contexts of judgment and survival:

• It describes Noah and his family remaining alive while the rest of the earth was swept away by the flood.

• It describes the small, vulnerable remnant of Israel that survives military defeat and exile.

• The word picture is one of extreme contrast: the vast majority is gone, and only a tiny fraction remains.

When applied to Naomi, the word picture highlights her utter isolation. The household of Elimelech, which had journeyed to Moab to find life, has been almost entirely wiped out. Naomi is the lone survivor of this family shipwreck.

### 4. Theological Implications

Theologically, sha'ar is deeply connected to the doctrine of the Remnant. While Naomi's survival feels like a curse of loneliness to her, it is actually the vehicle of God's preserving grace.

• The Seed of Redemption: God does not allow the line of Elimelech to be utterly extinguished. The "surviving" Naomi is the bridge to the future birth of Obed, Jesse, David, and ultimately, Jesus Christ.

• Grace in the Emptiness: The term teaches us that God’s redemptive work often begins with what is "left over." When human strength, security, and hope are completely depleted, the small remnant that survives becomes the canvas for God’s sovereign grace and restoration.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:5, the writer uses sha'ar to deliver a heavy literary and emotional blow. The text states: "Then both Mahlon and Chilion also died; so the woman survived her two sons and her husband."

• This phrasing is intentionally unnatural. In the ancient world, parents expected to be buried by their children. Here, Naomi is left behind by those who should have outlived her.

• By calling her "the woman" rather than "Naomi" in this specific clause, the narrator emphasizes her loss of status. She is no longer defined as a wife or a mother; she is simply a woman who has survived, stripped of her relational anchors.

• The word highlights the contrast between the death of her family and her own continued existence in a foreign land.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 7:23 - "Only Noah and those who were with him in the ark remained alive."

• Exodus 10:19 - "And the LORD turned a very strong west wind, which took the locusts away and blew them into the Red Sea. There remained not one locust in all the territory of Egypt."

• Deuteronomy 4:27 - "And the LORD will scatter you among the peoples, and you will be left few in number among the nations where the LORD will drive you."

• Joshua 13:1 - "Now Joshua was old, advanced in years. And the LORD said to him: 'You are old, advanced in years, and there remains very much land yet to be possessed.'"

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

The noun form of this root, she'ar, is the standard Old Testament word for "remnant." Isaiah uses this root extensively to describe the survivor-remnant of Israel (such as in his son's prophetic name, Shear-Jashub, meaning "a remnant shall return"). This connects Naomi’s personal story to the grander national story of Israel. Just as Naomi survived her losses to see a redeemer raised up in Bethlehem, so too would Israel survive its exile to see the ultimate Redeemer emerge from that same small town.
