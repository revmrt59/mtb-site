LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh (translated as "LORD" in all capital letters in the NKJV) is the personal, covenant-keeping name of the God of Israel. Revealed most significantly to Moses at the burning bush, this name denotes God's self-existence, eternal presence, immutability, and absolute faithfulness to His promises. In the narrative of Ruth, particularly in Ruth 1:6, the name "LORD" serves as a powerful reminder that even in times of national apostasy, famine, and intense personal suffering, the covenant God has not abandoned His people. His sovereign "visitation" to provide bread is not a random meteorological or agricultural event, but a personal, active expression of His covenant mercy and care for His heritage.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The name Yahweh is represented in the Hebrew text by the four consonants YHWH, known as the Tetragrammaton. This name is deeply connected to the Hebrew verb of being, hayah, which means "to be" or "to exist." When God revealed this name to Moses in Exodus 3:14, He declared, "I AM WHO I AM."

The word picture behind this name is one of absolute self-sufficiency and unchanging presence. Unlike the pagan deities of the ancient Near East, who were localized, dependent on human provision, and subject to change, Yahweh is the uncaused Cause, the One who possesses life inherent within Himself. He is the ever-present, active God who is intimately involved in the history of His creation and the redemption of His people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, Yahweh is the proper name of the one true God of Israel. It denotes the eternal, self-existent One who is faithful to His covenant. While the general name for God (Elohim) emphasizes His power as Creator and Ruler over all nations, Yahweh is His intimate, personal name used exclusively in the context of His relationship with His covenant people.

The name expresses God’s absolute immutability; He is the same yesterday, today, and forever. It signifies that His character, purposes, and promises are completely dependable. He is the God who initiates covenants, binds Himself to His people by oath, and guarantees the fulfillment of His word.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, the name Yahweh is associated with various titles that form vivid word pictures of His relationship with His people:

• Yahweh Yireh: The LORD Will Provide, painting a picture of God's timely intervention and provision.

• Yahweh Ro'i: The LORD Is My Shepherd, presenting a picture of intimate care, guidance, and protection.

• Yahweh Nissi: The LORD Is My Banner, representing God as the rallying point and victorious leader of His people.

• In the book of Ruth, Yahweh is portrayed as the ultimate fabric weaver of human destiny. While the characters make decisions and face tragedies, Yahweh works quietly behind the scenes of everyday life—directing the harvest, granting conception, and turning empty lives into full ones.

4. Theological Implications

The theological significance of the name Yahweh in the Old Testament cannot be overstated.

• Covenant Faithfulness (Chesed): Yahweh is the source of chesed—covenant love, loyalty, and kindness. His actions are always consistent with His holy character and His promises to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

• Sovereignty Over Nature and History: The name implies that Yahweh has absolute authority over the elements of nature (such as rain and famine) and the movements of nations.

• Grace to the Margin: Yahweh is the defender of the widow, the orphan, and the stranger. His covenant law contains specific provisions to protect and sustain the vulnerable, a theme that lies at the very heart of the Book of Ruth.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:6, Naomi is in the pagan land of Moab. She is spiritually and physically empty, having lost her husband, her two sons, and her livelihood. Moab represents a place of death and exile.

The turning point of the narrative hinges on this phrase: "she had heard in the country of Moab that the LORD had visited His people in giving them bread."

• The Sovereign Turn: The famine that drove Elimelech's family away was a consequence of covenant disobedience during the era of the Judges. The return of rain and crops is not attributed to chance or the Moabite god Chemosh, but to Yahweh.

• The Visitation of Grace: The Hebrew word for "visited" (paqad) means to intervene graciously or to take notice of. When Yahweh "visits" His people, He acts to rescue, deliver, or provide.

• Bethlehem Redefined: The name Bethlehem means "House of Bread." Because of the famine, it had become a place of emptiness. By the action of Yahweh, Bethlehem once again becomes true to its name. The mention of Yahweh reminds Naomi—and the reader—that despite her grief and her perception that the Almighty had dealt bitterly with her, the covenant God of Israel was still actively working to restore His people.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 3:15: "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

• Psalm 23:1: "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

• Proverbs 18:10: "The name of the LORD is a strong tower; The righteous run to it and are safe."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In ancient Jewish tradition, the name Yahweh was held in such immense reverence that scribes and readers avoided pronouncing it aloud to prevent taking the name of the Lord in vain (Exodus 20:7). Instead, they substituted the Hebrew word Adonai (meaning "Lord" or "Master") when reading the Scriptures. To indicate this to readers, the vowel points of Adonai were applied to the consonants of YHWH. This practice carried over into English translations, where YHWH is translated as "LORD" in capital letters, while Adonai is translated as "Lord" in standard capitalization.

In the New Testament, this covenant name finds its ultimate expression in the person of Jesus Christ. The Greek name Iesous (Jesus) is derived from the Hebrew Yehoshua, which means "Yahweh is Salvation." When Jesus claimed the title "I AM" in John 8:58, He was identifying Himself directly as the Yahweh of the Old Testament.
