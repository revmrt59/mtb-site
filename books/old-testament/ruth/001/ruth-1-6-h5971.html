PEOPLE - ( 'am , Strong's H5971 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun 'am refers to a group of people bound together by common blood, history, covenant, or purpose. Unlike other terms that emphasize political or geographical boundaries, 'am highlights relational proximity, kinship, and family ties. It is most famously used to designate Israel as Yahweh’s special covenant family. In Ruth 1:6, the term underscores God's faithful, familial care for His covenant partners, the Israelites, when He visited them to end their famine by "giving them bread."

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun 'am derives from a root associated with joining together, associating, or being close. It carries a strong connotation of kinship, family ties, and communal solidarity. While another common Hebrew word, goy, refers to a nation as a political or territorial entity (often used for pagan nations), 'am focuses on the relational bond that unites a family or community. It is a term of warmth and mutual belonging, portraying the nation not as an abstract state, but as a gathered family sharing a common father, history, and destiny.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word 'am means a people, nation, members of a family, or kin. When applied to Israel, it defines them as a corporate unit defined entirely by their relationship with God. The term encompasses the entire community of Israel, emphasizing their collective identity under the covenant. It can also refer to a specific subgroup, such as the common people, soldiers, or citizens, but its primary theological weight lies in its expression of divine ownership and familial relationship ("My people").

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Covenant Family: 'am is frequently paired with possessive pronouns to form "My people" ('ammi) or "Your people" ('ammeka). This usage is highly relational, mirroring ancient marriage and adoption language to show Israel’s secure position before God.

• Collective Solidarity: It is used to describe the gathering of Israel for worship, war, or decision-making, emphasizing that they act as one cohesive, relational body rather than isolated individuals.

• Kinship in Death: The idiomatic expression "gathered to his people" in the patriarchal narratives illustrates that the bond of 'am extends beyond physical life into the realm of ancestral heritage.

4. Theological Implications

The word 'am is central to the Old Testament covenant formula: "I will be their God, and they shall be My people." This reveals that God does not merely rule over subjects; He covenants with a family. The use of 'am shows that Israel's identity is completely derived from their relationship with Yahweh. God’s holiness, mercy, and discipline are all directed toward shaping this "people" to reflect His character to the surrounding nations. It reminds believers that God's redemptive work is corporate, calling individuals into a shared, covenantal community.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:6, Naomi hears that "the LORD had visited His people in giving them bread." The context is one of returning after a period of tragic wandering in pagan Moab. During the period of the Judges, Israel's disobedience led to famine, a covenant curse. Yet, the phrase "His people" ('ammo) indicates that despite Israel's unfaithfulness, Yahweh had not abandoned His covenant relationship with them. He "visited" them—an intense term of active, redemptive intervention—to restore life and sustenance. The use of 'am here underscores God's enduring loyalty (hesed) to His family, prompting Naomi's physical and spiritual journey back to the community of faith.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 6:7 - "I will take you as My people, and I will be your God. Then you shall know that I am the LORD your God who brings you out from under the burdens of the Egyptians."

• Deuteronomy 7:6 - "For you are a holy people to the LORD your God; the LORD your God has chosen you to be a people for His treasured possession, above all the peoples who are on the face of the earth."

• Ruth 1:16 - "But Ruth said: 'Entreat me not to leave you, Or to turn back from following after you; For wherever you go, I will go; And wherever you lodge, I will lodge; Your people shall be my people, And your God, my God.'"

• 2 Samuel 7:23 - "And who is like Your people, like Israel, the one nation on the earth whom God went to redeem for Himself as a people, to make for Himself a name..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Ruth 1:16, Ruth uses this same word when she pledges, "Your people shall be my people." This is a radical declaration of naturalization. As a Moabitess, Ruth willingly renounces her national and religious identity to join the covenant family of Yahweh. The theological transition from being outside the 'am to being integrated into it highlights the missional heartbeat of the Old Testament: God's covenant family is ultimately open to those who trust in Yahweh, anticipating the New Testament inclusion of the Gentiles into the household of faith.
