VISITED - ( paqad , Strong's H6485 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word paqad, translated as "visited" in Ruth 1:6, is a profound covenant term that describes God’s active, purposeful intervention in human affairs. Rather than a casual social call, a divine visitation in the Old Testament is a sovereign act where God enters the circumstances of His people to demonstrate His care, fulfill His promises, or bring about redemption. In the opening chapter of Ruth, this word serves as the dramatic turning point. It marks the end of a devastating decade of famine in Israel as Yahweh steps in to provide "bread" for His people, a display of grace that prompts the widowed and destitute Naomi to begin her long journey back to the Promised Land.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The word paqad carries the physical imagery of a superior personally attending to, overseeing, or inspecting a situation. It describes a ruler or a shepherd who does not merely look from a distance, but physically steps into the field to assess, manage, and care for those under his charge.

• In pastoral contexts, it evokes the picture of a shepherd counting his sheep (mustering them) to ensure that none are missing, injured, or neglected.

• When applied to God, this word highlights His active, personal involvement in the physical reality of His creation. It stands in stark contrast to any deistic view of a distant God; when Yahweh paqads, His presence is immediately felt through concrete, history-altering actions.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The verb paqad possesses a exceptionally broad semantic range, meaning to attend to, look after, take care of, visit, muster, appoint, or call to account.

• In a positive or benevolent sense, the word denotes God intervening to deliver, rescue, remember, or bless His people. This visitation is an expression of His grace, mercy, and covenant faithfulness.

• In a negative or judicial sense, the word refers to God "visiting" iniquity, which means He intervenes to execute justice, bring discipline, or punish sin.

• In the specific context of Ruth 1:6, the word designates a merciful intervention where God acts favorably to relieve suffering and restore the agricultural productivity of the land of Judah.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Sovereign Overseer: Just as a deputy is appointed to oversee a task, God acts as the ultimate Overseer of the earth. His visitation is an exercise of His divine management, ensuring that the natural order and His covenant plans remain aligned.

• The Covenant Keeper: Often, God’s visitation is the direct response to His memory of a promise. When God "visits" someone, it is the moment His long-standing promise translates into visible reality.

• The Provider of Life: paqad is frequently associated with the miraculous overcoming of barrenness, famine, and death. It is the life-giving touch of God that reverses seemingly hopeless situations.

4. Theological Implications

• Grace Overcoming Judgment: The famine in Israel during the days of the judges was a direct consequence of covenant disobedience (Deuteronomy 28:15-18). God's visitation to provide bread demonstrates that His mercy and grace ultimately triumph over judgment. He initiates restoration even when His people have been unfaithful.

• Sovereign Initiative: The text does not say the people repented first, but rather that God "visited His people." God is the prime mover in redemption. Salvation and provision are initiated by His sovereign grace.

• The True Provider: Bethlehem means "House of Bread," yet without God's blessing, it became a house of emptiness. The return of food to the land reminds the reader that humanity does not live by its own labor alone, but by every word and provision that proceeds from the mouth of God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 1, Naomi is stranded in Moab—a place associated with idolatry, compromise, death, and exile. She has lost her husband, her sons, her wealth, and her security. She is empty, bitter, and hopeless.

• The transition of the entire narrative hinges on Naomi hearing a report: "that the LORD had visited His people in giving them bread."

• This divine visitation of Bethlehem acts as a spiritual and physical magnet. The news of God’s grace is what triggers Naomi’s decision to arise and return (shub). Without the sovereign visitation of God in Judah, Naomi and Ruth would have remained in the spiritual graveyard of Moab. God's provision of physical bread is the catalyst for the spiritual restoration of the family line that would lead to David and ultimately to Jesus Christ, the Bread of Life.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 21:1: "And the LORD visited Sarah as He had said, and the LORD did for Sarah as He had spoken."

• Exodus 3:16: "Go and gather the elders of Israel together, and say to them, 'The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, of Isaac, and of Jacob, appeared to me, saying, "I have surely visited you and seen what is done to you in Egypt;'"

• Exodus 4:31: "So the people believed; and when they heard that the LORD had visited the children of Israel and that He had looked on their affliction, then they bowed their heads and worshiped."

• 1 Samuel 2:21: "And the LORD visited Hannah, so that she conceived and bore three sons and two daughters. Meanwhile the child Samuel grew before the LORD."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• New Testament Connection: In the Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament), paqad is regularly translated by the Greek verb episkeptomai. This is the exact word used in the New Testament when the people of God realize that the Messiah has arrived.

• Zacharias’s Song: In Luke 1:68, Zacharias prophesies, "Blessed is the Lord God of Israel, for He has visited and redeemed His people."

• The Raising of the Widow's Son: In Luke 7:16, after Jesus raises a widow's dead son, the crowd is struck with fear and glorifies God, saying, "A great prophet has risen up among us," and, "God has visited His people." The ultimate visitation of God to give "bread" and life to His people is realized in the incarnation of Jesus Christ.
