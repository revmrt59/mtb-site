AROSE - ( qum , Strong's H6965 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb qum literally means to rise, stand up, or get up, but in narrative contexts, it often serves as an "initiatory" verb signaling a decisive transition from inactivity to action, or from despair to purposeful movement. In Ruth 1:6, Naomi's act of "arising" marks the pivotal turning point of the chapter. She decides to leave the land of tragedy (Moab) and return to the land of promise (Bethlehem) upon hearing that the Lord has visited His people with bread. It represents a physical movement that mirrors a profound internal shift from grief to hope.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root qum is a highly frequent verb in the Old Testament, representing a physical movement from a lower to a higher position, such as lying down to standing, or sitting to rising.

• Historically and culturally, rising up is associated with preparation for a journey, preparation for battle, or standing in respect before an authority.

• In the ancient Near East, standing up also carried legal and covenantal weight. It was the stance of an individual preparing to testify in court, execute a decree, or establish an oath.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The root qum primarily means to arise, stand up, stand, or rise.

• In its basic active form (Qal), it denotes the physical act of getting up from a bed, seat, or the ground. However, it frequently carries the idiomatic sense of preparing oneself for immediate, purposeful action, setting out on a journey, or coming onto the scene of history to fulfill a specific task.

• In its causative forms (Hiphil), the verb means to raise up, establish, erect, or perform a vow.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Physical Elevation: Rising from sleep, sickness, or prostration (e.g., Genesis 19:35, Joshua 7:10).

• Initiation of a Journey: "Arise, go" is a standard biblical formula for initiating a divine mission, a significant relocation, or a flight from danger (e.g., Genesis 31:13, Jonah 1:2).

• Legal/Covenantal Confirmation: Rising to establish a testimony or confirm an oath.

• Resurrection/Restoration: Rising up from ruin, defeat, or death (e.g., Isaiah 26:19).

4. Theological Implications

• Naomi's physical rising mirrors a spiritual awakening and a return to covenant alignment. She had gone down to Moab—a place of judgment, idolatry, and death—and had been brought low by grief and loss. Her decision to qum represents the physical outworking of repentance (turning back to God).

• This action demonstrates that God’s providential provision ("the LORD had visited His people") provokes active, physical faith in His people. Faith is not static; it arises and moves toward God's promise.

• The verb underscores the reality that while God's grace initiates salvation and provision, believers are called to actively arise and leave behind the lands of their exile and grief.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 1:6, Naomi has spent ten years in Moab, experiencing the deaths of her husband and her two sons. She is empty, bitter, and culturally dead.

• The Hebrew verb wattāqom ("and she arose") is a sudden, sharp shift in the narrative. It marks the precise moment despair is replaced by action.

• She does not merely stand up; she prepares to depart. This "arising" is the catalyst for her daughters-in-law to arise with her, initiating the journey of redemption that ultimately leads to Bethlehem, the lineage of King David, and the birth of Jesus Christ.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 13:17 - "Arise, walk in the land through its length and its width, for I give it to you."

• Genesis 31:13 - "I am the God of Bethel, where you anointed the pillar and where you made a vow to Me. Now arise, get out of this land, and return to the land of your family."

• Joshua 1:2 - "Moses My servant is dead. Now therefore, arise, go over this Jordan, you and all this people, to the land which I am giving to them—the children of Israel."

• Jonah 1:2 - "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and cry out against it; for their wickedness has come up before Me."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Septuagint (LXX) translates qum in Ruth 1:6 with the Greek verb anistemi, which is the same word used in the New Testament for resurrection. This linguistic link highlights the transition from the "death" of Moab to the "new life" of Bethlehem. Naomi's arising is a shadow of resurrection life emerging from the grave of her losses.
