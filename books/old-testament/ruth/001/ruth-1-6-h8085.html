HEARD - ( shama` , Strong's 8085 )

Summary:

In Ruth 1:6, the word "heard" translates the Hebrew verb shama`, a rich term that signifies far more than the passive physiological perception of sound. It denotes receiving a significant report, processing it with deep attention, and being moved to decisive action. For Naomi, dwelling in the dark, grief-stricken exile of Moab, this "hearing" of the Lord’s gracious visitation to Bethlehem becomes the divine turning point of her life. The report of God's provision pierces her despair and acts as the immediate catalyst for her physical and spiritual journey back home.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew concept of hearing is deeply active and relational. Unlike the Western division between hearing (an auditory sensation) and obeying (a subsequent action), the Hebrew mind views them as a single, continuous event. To hear God, or to hear of God's work, is to respond to what has been heard. The ear is understood as the gateway to the heart, the seat of the will and intellect. When someone truly hears (shama`), it reshapes their perspective, redirects their affections, and moves their feet.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb shama` occurs over 1,100 times in the Old Testament, expressing a wide semantic range:

• To perceive sound or listen with attention.

• To receive information, news, or a report of distant events.

• To understand or comprehend the meaning of what is spoken.

• To hear with compliance, which is frequently translated in English as "obey."

• In the specific context of Ruth 1:6, the verb carries the sense of receiving a credible, life-altering report of historical events—specifically, the news that the famine in Judah had ended because of Yahweh's intervention.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament utilizes shama` in several distinct theological and practical ways:

• The Hearing of faith: Hearing acts of deliverance inspires faith and covenant commitment in the hearer (such as Rahab hearing of the Exodus).

• The Shema: In Deuteronomy 6:4, "Hear, O Israel" demands a listening that results in exclusive, loving allegiance to Yahweh.

• God's Hearing: When God shama` His people, it means He hears their cries with compassion and moves to save them (Exodus 2:24).

• Hearing and Returning: In prophetic literature, Israel is repeatedly called to "hear" the voice of the Lord as the essential first step of turning away from idols and returning to the covenant land.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of shama` in the Old Testament rests on the reality that Yahweh is a God who acts in history, and whose acts are reportable and hearable. Faith does not arise from speculative philosophy, but from hearing the record of God's historical faithfulness. Furthermore, Naomi's hearing highlights the reach of God's grace. Even in the spiritually desolate plains of Moab, the news of Yahweh’s covenant faithfulness is heard. This demonstrates that God's grace is never silent; it reverberates across borders to reach and rescue the brokenhearted.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:1–5, the narrative is characterized by famine, flight, compromise, and death. Naomi is left entirely empty—without a husband, without sons, and without a homeland. Her circumstances in Moab are physically and spiritually terminal.

• In verse 6, the word shama` breaks this cycle of tragedy. The report that "the Lord had visited His people by giving them bread" changes everything.

• The placement of "heard" here is highly intentional. It is the hinge upon which the entire narrative turns.

• The news Naomi hears is a report of grace. The name "Bethlehem" means "House of Bread," which had been a mockery during the famine. But now, Naomi hears that the House of Bread is full again because Yahweh has visited (paqad) His people.

• This hearing immediately triggers a physical response: "Then she arose... that she might return." The report of God's goodness breaks her paralysis, giving her the hope needed to abandon Moab and return to the place of blessing.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 2:24: "So God heard their groaning, and God remembered His covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob."

• Deuteronomy 6:4: "Hear, O Israel: The Lord our God, the Lord is one!"

• Joshua 2:10: "For we have heard how the Lord dried up the water of the Red Sea for you when you came out of Egypt, and what you did to the two kings of the Amorites who were on the other side of the Jordan, Sihon and Og, whom you utterly destroyed."

• 1 Kings 10:1: "Now when the queen of Sheba heard of the fame of Solomon concerning the name of the Lord, she came to test him with hard questions."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Pastors and teachers will find a powerful homiletical connection between shama` (hearing) and shub (returning/repentance) in the book of Ruth. Naomi's physical return to Judah is a picture of spiritual repentance. Genuine repentance always begins with hearing the gospel—the good news of God's gracious visitation and provision. Just as Naomi could not find bread in Moab but had to return to Bethlehem to receive it, the sinner cannot find life in the far country of independence but must hear the report of God's mercy and arise to go to Him.
