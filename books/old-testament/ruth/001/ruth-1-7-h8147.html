TWO - ( shenayim , Strong's H8147 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word shenayim is the cardinal number "two," representing a pair, a duplicate, or a division. In the context of Ruth 1:7, it refers to Orpah and Ruth, Naomi's "two daughters-in-law." This number highlights the companionship Naomi had in her grief, while also setting up the poignant contrast of the diverging paths these two women will soon take—one returning to Moab, and the other clinging to Naomi and the God of Israel.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The word shenayim is the dual form of sheni (second), derived from a root meaning to repeat, double, or do again. In ancient Hebrew thought, numbers carry both literal and relational weight. The number two represents the minimum requirement for companionship, testimony, and agreement, but also carries the potential for division. In the patriarchal family structure, a pair of daughters-in-law represented the potential continuation of the family line through Naomi's two deceased sons. Their presence with Naomi as she departs Moab symbolizes both her lingering connection to her deceased family and the shared weight of their collective grief.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Shenayim is a cardinal numeral meaning "two" or "a pair." It functions as a dual noun, reflecting things that naturally occur in pairs (such as eyes, hands, or feet) or grouping two distinct entities together as a single unit for a shared purpose. When applied to people, it describes two individuals bound by a common circumstance, relationship, or mission. It denotes a pairing that is either united in action or destined to be contrasted.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament narrative and law, shenayim establishes legal witness, partnership, and distinction. Two witnesses are required to establish a legal matter, representing the minimum standard for truth and validation. In the sacrificial system, pairs of animals are offered together to accomplish a single spiritual reality. In Hebrew narrative, pairs of characters often serve as foils for one another, illustrating diverging paths of faith or obedience. In the book of Ruth, the "two" daughters-in-law begin on the same road but ultimately illustrate two entirely different directions of faith and covenant loyalty.

### 4. Theological Implications

Theologically, the number two in Ruth 1:7 underscores the reality of human agency and divine selection within the covenant community. God frequently uses pairs to reveal the condition of the human heart. Both Orpah and Ruth suffered the same loss, lived in the same household, and loved the same mother-in-law. Yet, when faced with the crisis of decision, the "two" split. One returns to her pagan gods, while the other steps into the line of the Messiah. This highlights that external circumstances and shared suffering do not dictate spiritual outcomes; rather, individual faith and devotion to the living God make the ultimate difference.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:7, the mention of "her two daughters-in-law with her" emphasizes the depth of Naomi's relational wealth despite her material poverty. She is not entirely alone when she begins her journey back to Judah. The presence of shenayim serves to heighten the drama of the subsequent verses. Naomi's plea for them to return to Moab is addressed to "her two daughters-in-law." The unity of the "two" weeping and walking together makes the eventual parting in verse 14 even more painful, where one kisses her mother-in-law goodbye, but the other clings to her. The word emphasizes that what started as a unified pair on a singular journey of return ends with a profound theological separation.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Ruth 1:19 - "Now the two of them went until they came to Bethlehem."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

In Hebrew narrative style, the reduction from "three" to "two" to "one" is a common literary device to focus the reader's attention. The story begins with three men (Elimelech, Mahlon, Chilion) who die, leaving three women (Naomi, Orpah, Ruth). As they set out, we have the "two" daughters-in-law with Naomi. By the end of the chapter, the "two" of them (Naomi and Ruth) arrive in Bethlehem, and eventually, the focus narrows to the "one" redeemer and the "one" loyal daughter-in-law who will raise up the name of the dead.
