MOTHER'S - ( em , Strong's H517 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word em primarily denotes a biological mother, but its usage extends to ancestry, maternal care, and metaphorical origins. In Ruth 1:8, Naomi's instruction to Orpah and Ruth to return to "her mother's house" (rather than the typical patriarchal "father's house") serves as a poignant, culturally rare expression of ultimate safety, emotional comfort, and the preparation for remarriage within the domestic sphere of their maternal origins.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew word em is a primary, monosyllabic noun found throughout the Semitic languages. It is an onomatopoeic word, mimicking the earliest, most basic labial sounds a nursing infant makes ("am" or "ma"). In the ancient Near East, the mother was the cornerstone of the domestic sphere, representing nurture, primary instruction, and emotional comfort, while the father represented authority, legal standing, and lineage.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• em refers to a female parent (biological mother). It also carries broader semantic meanings, including grandmother, matriarch of a clan, or female ancestor. Metaphorically, it can describe a point of origin, a mother city (metropolis), or a person who acts in a nurturing, protective role within a community.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The term appears over 220 times in the Old Testament. It is frequently paired with ab (father) to represent parental authority and the wholeness of the family unit.

• It is used as a picture of profound grief, security, and comfort, as in the depiction of a quieted soul resting like a weaned child with its mother.

• It can denote the source or origin of something, such as the earth being the mother of all living, or Eve as the "mother of all living" (em kol-chay).

• It is used of a nation or a city, depicting the relationship between a homeland and its citizens.

4. Theological Implications

The concept of em points to God's design for nurture and relational warmth within the covenant community. While God is primarily revealed in masculine terms as Father, He occasionally compares His comforting nature to that of a mother. The commandment to honor one's mother elevates her status to equal honor with the father, a revolutionary concept in the patriarchal ancient Near East where women were often marginalized.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:8, Naomi bids Orpah and Ruth to return to "her mother's house" (bet immah). This is highly unusual; ancient Near Eastern customs almost exclusively refer to the "father's house" (bet ab) as the place of legal identity, inheritance, and return for a widowed woman. By directing them to their "mother's house," Naomi emphasizes the intimate, tender, and domestic sphere of maternal care. It is a place of emotional refuge where the young widows can find comfort in their shared grief. Furthermore, the "mother's house" was the location where marriage chambers were prepared and where daughters were counseled regarding courtship and marriage. Naomi is releasing them back to the realm of maternal affection and the prospect of finding new security through marriage.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 3:20 - "And Adam called his wife’s name Eve, because she was the mother of all living."

• Genesis 24:28 - "So the young woman ran and told her mother’s household these things."

• Judges 5:7 - "Village life ceased, it ceased in Israel, Until I, Deborah, arose, Arose a mother in Israel."

• Psalm 131:2 - "Surely I have calmed and quieted my soul, Like a weaned child with his mother; Like a weaned child is my soul within me."

• Proverbs 1:8 - "My son, hear the instruction of your father, And do not forsake the law of your mother."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The connection between em (mother) and ammah (cubit/measure) suggests a root concept of "matrix" or "measurement/standard." The mother is the standard-setter of life and household order. The "mother's house" in poetic and narrative biblical literature stands as a sanctuary of love, free from the formal legalities of the patriarchal gate, making Naomi's blessing a profound gesture of maternal love, selflessly releasing her daughters-in-law to the gentlest possible environment.
