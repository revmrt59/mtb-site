DEALT - ( asah , Strong's H6213 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'asah is a foundational Old Testament word meaning to do, make, fashion, or execute. In Ruth 1:8, it is translated "dealt" in the context of Naomi's prayer that Yahweh would deal kindly (chesed) with Ruth and Orpah, just as they had "dealt" ('asah) kindly with their deceased husbands and with Naomi. In this covenantal and familial context, the word moves beyond mere physical action to represent the active, concrete demonstration of loyalty, love, and familial duty. It highlights how human actions of kindness reflect and invite the reciprocal, active kindness of God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The verb 'asah is one of the most frequently used verbs in the Hebrew Bible, occurring over 2,600 times.

• Its basic concept is the expenditure of energy to produce a tangible, observable result—whether creating something new, performing a duty, or executing a plan.

• Unlike bara' (to create out of nothing, used exclusively of God), 'asah usually involves working with existing materials or carrying out actions within established relationships.

• In the ancient Near East, relationships were defined by deeds rather than mere sentiments. To "deal" or "do" toward someone meant fulfilling the practical obligations of a covenant or family bond. In Ruth 1:8, the background is the family covenant, where the daughters-in-law went far beyond the legal minimum in their care for their husbands and mother-in-law.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The primary meaning of 'asah is to do, work, make, produce, or fashion.

• When paired with terms of relationship or attitude (such as chesed, meaning lovingkindness or loyal love), it means to practice, extend, show, or deal out that quality to someone.

• The grammatical form in Ruth 1:8 is the Qal perfect (second person plural), indicating a completed, historical action. Ruth and Orpah had fully and consistently "dealt" in this manner; their kindness was a proven, historical reality.

• The semantic range includes:

• To fashion or manufacture physical objects (Genesis 6:14).

• To perform an act or carry out an obligation (Deuteronomy 6:18).

• To bring about a state of affairs or execute a judgment (1 Kings 11:25).

• To show or practice a moral quality toward another person (Genesis 24:12).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The word is used to describe God’s creative work in forming the heavens and the earth (Genesis 1:16).

• It is used of making a covenant ('asah berit), which emphasizes that covenants are not merely spoken but enacted and lived out through concrete actions.

• In the context of interpersonal relationships, "dealing" ('asah) kindly is often associated with hospitality, protection, or life-saving deeds. For example, Abraham "dealt" (prepared) a calf for his heavenly guests (Genesis 18:7-8).

• The word picture is one of active, hands-on involvement. It is not passive wishing; it is the physical manifestation of internal commitment through tangible deeds.

4. Theological Implications

• There is a powerful theological connection between human action and divine action in Ruth 1:8. Naomi prays that the Lord will 'asah (deal) kindly with the women just as they have 'asah (dealt) kindly with the dead and with her. Human kindness serves as the pattern and the catalyst for asking for divine kindness.

• God is the ultimate Doer ('asah). His character is revealed through His works and His active dealing with humanity.

• True covenant loyalty requires action. Biblical faith and love are never purely interior states of mind; they are always expressed in what one does ('asah).

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 1:8, Naomi uses the word twice: "The LORD deal kindly with you, as you have dealt with the dead and with me."

• The "dead" refers to Mahlon and Chilion, the deceased husbands. Ruth and Orpah stayed loyal to their husbands even unto death, and continued that loyalty to their surviving mother-in-law.

• The combination of 'asah (to do/deal) and chesed (loyal love/mercy/kindness) is a standard Hebrew idiom for showing covenant faithfulness.

• By using the Qal perfect tense for the daughters' actions, Naomi acknowledges that their past actions of devotion are complete, verified, and beyond question. By using the Qal imperfect (jussive) for Yahweh's dealing, she petitions God to continually work out His grace in their future lives.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 24:12 - "Then he said, 'O Lord God of my master Abraham, please give me success this day, and show kindness to my master Abraham.'" (The verb "show" is 'asah).

• Genesis 32:10 - "I am not worthy of the least of all the mercies and of all the truth which You have shown to Your servant..." (The verb "shown" is 'asah).

• Joshua 2:12 - "Now therefore, please swear to me by the Lord, since I have shown you kindness, that you also will show kindness to my father’s house, and give me a true token," (Both "shown" and "show" are 'asah).

• 2 Samuel 2:5 - "So David sent messengers to the men of Jabesh Gilead, and said to them, 'May you be blessed of the Lord, because you have shown this kindness to your lord, to Saul, and have buried him.'" (The verb "shown" is 'asah).

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The word 'asah highlights the dignity of human agency in the book of Ruth. In a time when the judges ruled and society was disintegrating into self-interest, these foreign women actively chose to "deal" righteously and kindly.

• The word serves as a literary link to the rest of the book, where Ruth's active labor (gleaning in the fields) and Boaz's active protection are all manifestations of 'asah—doing what is required to redeem and preserve life.
