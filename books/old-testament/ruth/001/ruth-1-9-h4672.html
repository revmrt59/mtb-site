FIND - ( matsa , Strong's H4672 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb matsa primarily means to find, secure, or acquire something, often after a search, but it frequently carries the sense of receiving something as a gift or encountering something through divine providence. In the context of Naomi’s prayer in Ruth 1:9, it denotes the securing of a place of safety, security, and quiet rest which comes only through the gracious provision of Yahweh.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root meaning of matsa involves coming upon something, reaching a destination, or attaining a state of being. It stands in contrast to losing, seeking, or wandering. In the ancient Near East, finding something was not merely an intellectual realization but a physical obtaining and securing of a tangible reality. In nomadic and agrarian contexts, finding water, finding a pasture, or finding a home meant the difference between life and death. The word paints a picture of a weary traveler arriving at a place of security and provision after a long, perilous journey.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb matsa occurs over 450 times in the Hebrew Bible. In the basic Qal stem, it means to find something that was lost or sought, to meet or encounter someone, to reach or attain a goal, or to obtain a condition. It also carries the legal and intellectual sense of detecting, discovering, or recognizing a condition (such as finding guilt or finding favor). When paired with abstract nouns like "rest" or "favor," it means to experience, receive, or be granted a specific state of well-being. It is the experiential attainment of a condition.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Finding Favor: One of the most common idiomatic uses of matsa is "finding favor" (chen) in the eyes of another. This describes receiving unmerited grace, acceptance, and protection from a superior.

• Finding Life or Peace: The word is used of finding life through wisdom or finding rest for the soul.

• Sufficient Provision: In some contexts, it refers to finding or acquiring enough to satisfy a pressing need, representing a transition from lack to abundance, and from searching to resting.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, matsa highlights the cooperative but ultimately dependent relationship between human seeking and divine giving. While humans search, it is God who allows them to find. In the economy of God's covenant grace, finding is often an act of divine revelation or provision rather than human ingenuity. When God causes someone to "find," He is intervening in their circumstances to bring them into alignment with His purposes. The theological weight of "finding rest" (menuhah) points back to the Sabbath rest of God and forward to the ultimate security found in a covenant relationship under His protection.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 1:9, Naomi is speaking to her daughters-in-law, Orpah and Ruth, as they stand on the border of Moab and Judah. Naomi urges them to return to their mother's houses, praying: "The LORD grant that you may find rest, each in the house of her husband." Here, matsa is coupled with menuhah (a resting place of security and peace). Naomi recognizes that for a young widow in the ancient world, "rest"—which meant protection, provision, and social standing—was found primarily in a stable marriage and home. Naomi acknowledges that this finding is not a matter of luck; it is a gift that must be granted by Yahweh. The literary arc of the book reveals a beautiful irony: Naomi sends them away to "find" rest in Moab, but Ruth will ultimately "find" her rest and "find" favor in Israel under the wings of Yahweh, through the kinsman-redeemer Boaz.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 6:8 - "But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD."

• Ruth 2:10 - "So she fell on her face, bowed down to the ground, and said to him, 'Why have I found favor in your eyes, that you should take notice of me, since I am a foreigner?'"

• Proverbs 8:35 - "For whoever finds me finds life, And obtains favor from the LORD;"

• Jeremiah 6:16 - "Thus says the LORD: 'Stand in the ways and see, And ask for the old paths, where the good way is, And walk in it; Then you will find rest for your souls.' But they said, 'We will not walk in it.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Greek Septuagint (LXX), matsa in Ruth 1:9 is translated by the Greek verb heurisko (to find, obtain). This is the same verb used in the New Testament when Jesus invites the weary to come to Him to "find rest" for their souls (Matthew 11:29). Furthermore, the structural development of the Book of Ruth revolves around this word: Naomi prays they will "find" rest (1:9); Ruth asks to "find" favor (2:2, 2:10, 2:13); Naomi seeks "rest" for Ruth (3:1); and ultimately, Ruth finds it in Boaz's home, showing how God answered Naomi’s prayer in an unexpected way.
