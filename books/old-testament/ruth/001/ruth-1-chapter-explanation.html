Chapter Study

Ruth 1

Verse-by-Verse
Ruth 1:1

NKJV

NKJV

Ruth 1:1 Now it came to pass, in the days when the judges ruled, that there was a famine in the land. And a certain man of Bethlehem, Judah, went to dwell in the country of Moab, he and his wife and his two sons.(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Ruth 1:1 In the days when the judges ruled in Israel, a severe famine came upon the land. So a man from Bethlehem in Judah left his home and went to live in the country of Moab, taking his wife and two sons with him. (NLT)

Main Idea

A severe famine drives Elimelech and his family from Bethlehem to Moab, setting the stage for God's work of redemption through ordinary people in extraordinary circumstances.

Verse Explanation

"Now it came to pass in the days when the judges ruled..." - The events of Ruth take place during the period of the Judges-a time marked by spiritual decline, moral confusion, and repeated cycles of sin, oppression, repentance, and deliverance. The closing verse of Judges summarizes the era: "Everyone did what was right in his own eyes" (Judges 21:25). Against this dark backdrop, the Book of Ruth shines as a testimony to God's quiet faithfulness through the lives of ordinary people.

"...that there was a famine in the land." - Famine in the Old Testament was often connected with God's discipline or testing, though Scripture does not specifically state why this famine occurred. Regardless of its cause, it became the means by which God began unfolding His larger plan of redemption.

"And a certain man of Bethlehem, Judah..." - Bethlehem means "House of Bread." Ironically, the "House of Bread" had no bread. This irony highlights the severity of the famine and reminds us that outward circumstances do not always reflect God's ultimate purposes.

"...went to dwell in the country of Moab..." - Moab lay east of the Dead Sea and had a long history of hostility toward Israel. The Moabites descended from Lot through an incestuous relationship with his older daughter (Genesis 19:30-38). Later, they opposed Israel during the wilderness journey and became associated with idolatry and spiritual compromise (Numbers 22-25). From a human perspective, Moab seemed like an unlikely place to find hope, yet God would use even this foreign land to bring Ruth into His redemptive plan.

"...he and his wife and his two sons." - The family leaves Bethlehem together, hoping to escape famine. They cannot yet see that this difficult journey will become the means by which God accomplishes far greater purposes than any of them could imagine.

Remark(s)

Elimelech's name means "My God is King." The famine tested that confession. When life became difficult, he left the land God had given His people and sought provision in Moab. Every believer faces moments when faith is tested by circumstances. The question is not simply what we profess about God when life is easy, but whether we continue to trust that He is King when His ways are difficult to understand.

Ruth 1:2

NKJV

NKJV

Ruth 1:2 The name of the man was Elimelech, the name of his wife was Naomi, and the names of his two sons were Mahlon and Chilion"” Ephrathites of Bethlehem, Judah. And they went to the country of Moab and remained there.(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Ruth 1:2 The man’s name was Elimelech, and his wife was Naomi. Their two sons were Mahlon and Kilion. They were Ephrathites from Bethlehem in the land of Judah. And when they reached Moab, they settled there. (NLT)

Main Idea

The author introduces Naomi, Elimelech, and their two sons, showing that an ordinary family from Bethlehem has unknowingly become part of God's extraordinary plan.

Verse Explanation

"The name of the man was Elimelech..." - Elimelech means "My God is King." His name reflects Israel's confession that the LORD alone is King over His people.

"...the name of his wife was Naomi..." - Naomi means "pleasant" or "delightful." By the end of the chapter she will ask to be called Mara ("bitter"), believing that God has dealt harshly with her. The contrast between her two names foreshadows the journey from joy to sorrow-and ultimately back to restoration.

"...and the names of his two sons were Mahlon and Chilion..." - The meaning of their names is uncertain, though many understand Mahlon to be associated with sickness or weakness and Chilion with frailty or wasting away. Scripture does not explain whether these names reflected their condition or simply became significant in light of what followed. The emphasis is on introducing the family whose future will soon appear to come to an end.

"...Ephrathites of Bethlehem, Judah." - This identifies them as members of a respected family or clan from the Bethlehem area. They are ordinary Israelites living in a familiar town, not kings, priests, or prophets. Yet God often accomplishes His greatest purposes through ordinary people who faithfully live before Him.

"And they went to the country of Moab and remained there." - What began as a temporary move because of famine gradually became a settled residence. The family likely expected to return once conditions improved, but their plans would be dramatically altered by events they could not foresee.

Remark(s)

Elimelech sought temporary relief from the pressures of famine, but what was intended as a temporary solution became a permanent residence. Decisions made simply to escape today's pressures can shape the course of our lives far longer than we expect. The little phrase, "they remained there," quietly reminds us that expedient decisions can have lasting consequences.

Ruth 1:3

NKJV

NKJV

Ruth 1:3 Then Elimelech, Naomi's husband, died; and she was left, and her two sons.(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Ruth 1:3 Then Elimelech died, and Naomi was left with her two sons. (NLT)

Main Idea

Elimelech dies, leaving Naomi a widow in a foreign land and marking the beginning of a series of devastating losses that seem to extinguish all hope.

Verse Explanation

"Then Elimelech, Naomi's husband, died..." - The famine that prompted the family to leave Bethlehem is now overshadowed by an even greater tragedy. Elimelech's death leaves Naomi without the protection, provision, and security that a husband provided in that culture. The man who sought to preserve his family's future is no longer able to care for them.

"...and she was left..." - This simple phrase captures the deep loneliness of Naomi's situation. She is now a widow living in a foreign land, far from her extended family and the familiar surroundings of Bethlehem. Throughout the opening chapter, the expression "she was left" becomes increasingly significant as Naomi experiences one loss after another.

"...and her two sons." - Although Naomi's sons remain with her, the family's future has become uncertain. In the ancient world, widows often depended on their sons for care and protection. For the moment, there is still hope that the family line will continue, but the reader soon discovers that even this hope will be tested.

Remark(s)

The book begins to unfold through a series of losses that seem to move farther and farther from God's promises. Yet what appears to be the unraveling of Naomi's life is actually the beginning of God's redemptive work. God's providence is often at work long before we can recognize it.

Ruth 1:4

NKJV

NKJV

Ruth 1:4 Now they took wives of the women of Moab: the name of the one was Orpah, and the name of the other Ruth. And they dwelt there about ten years.(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Ruth 1:4 The two sons married Moabite women. One married a woman named Orpah, and the other a woman named Ruth. But about ten years later, (NLT)

Main Idea

Mahlon and Chilion marry Moabite women, Ruth and Orpah, and the family remains in Moab for about ten years.

Verse Explanation

"Now they took wives of the women of Moab..." - After Elimelech's death, Mahlon and Chilion marry Moabite women. While the Law specifically prohibited marriage with certain Canaanite nations, it did not expressly forbid marriage to Moabites. However, Moab's history of idolatry and opposition to Israel made such marriages spiritually significant. The Book of Ruth ultimately demonstrates that what mattered most was not Ruth's nationality but her faith in the God of Israel.

"The name of the one was Orpah, and the name of the other Ruth." - Scripture introduces the two women who will respond very differently when tragedy strikes. Both begin the story in the same place, but only Ruth will choose to leave Moab, embrace the God of Israel, and become part of God's redemptive plan.

"And they dwelt there about ten years." - What began as a temporary move had become a ten-year stay in Moab. Ten years also highlights another painful reality: despite a decade of marriage, neither Ruth nor Orpah had children. As the story unfolds, this childlessness deepens the family's crisis because there is still no heir to continue Elimelech's family line.

Main Idea

Naomi's two sons also die, leaving her completely alone with no husband, no children, and no apparent future.

Verse Explanation

"Then both Mahlon and Chilion also died..." - The tragedy deepens as Naomi loses not only her husband but also both of her sons. Any remaining hope that the family line might continue through them is now gone.

"So the woman survived her two sons and her husband." - The expression "the woman survived" emphasizes Naomi's complete loss. She is left a widow in a foreign land with no children to care for her and no heir to preserve her husband's family line. Humanly speaking, her future appears hopeless.

For an Israelite widow, this was more than personal grief. It meant the apparent end of her family's inheritance, security, and legacy.

Remark(s)

Before God restores Naomi's life, He allows us to see the full depth of her loss. Only when we understand how empty she had become can we fully appreciate the greatness of what the redemption Christ brings.

Ruth 1:6

NKJV

NKJV

Ruth 1:6 Then she arose with her daughters-in-law that she might return from the country of Moab, for she had heard in the country of Moab that the LORD had visited His people by giving them bread.(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Ruth 1:6 Then Naomi heard in Moab that the LORD had blessed his people in Judah by giving them good crops again. So Naomi and her daughters-in-law got ready to leave Moab to return to her homeland. (NLT)

Main Idea

Naomi hears that the LORD has ended the famine in Judah and decides to return home, taking the first step in God's work of restoring her life.

Verse Explanation

"Then she arose with her daughters-in-law..." - After years in Moab, Naomi decides it is time to return to Bethlehem. Ruth and Orpah initially set out with her, demonstrating their love and commitment to Naomi even before their paths eventually diverge.

"...that she might return from the country of Moab." - Naomi's decision marks a turning point in the story. The journey that began because of famine now begins to reverse as she leaves Moab and returns to the land of God's people.

"For she had heard in the country of Moab..." - Even while living in a foreign land, news from Judah reaches Naomi. God's work of restoration often begins with a simple report, a providential circumstance, or an opportunity that calls us to take the next step of faith.

"...that the LORD had visited His people..." - The phrase "visited His people" means that God had graciously intervened on their behalf. It emphasizes His care and His active involvement in meeting the needs of His people.

"...by giving them bread." - The famine has ended, and Bethlehem-the "House of Bread"-once again has bread. The God who had allowed the famine has now provided for His people, opening the way for Naomi's return.

Remark(s)

One of the ways the LORD "visits" His people is by giving them bread. Throughout Scripture, bread becomes a picture of God feeding His people with something of Himself. He fed Israel with manna in the wilderness, placed the Bread of the Presence in the tabernacle, and ultimately gave us Jesus Christ, the Bread of Life. Today, God continues to nourish His people through His Word and through His Presence.

Ruth 1:8

NKJV

NKJV

Ruth 1:8 And Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law, "Go, return each to her mother's house. The LORD deal kindly with you, as you have dealt with the dead and with me.(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Ruth 1:8 But on the way, Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law, “Go back to your mothers’ homes. And may the LORD reward you for your kindness to your husbands and to me. (NLT)

Main Idea

Naomi urges Ruth and Orpah to return to their own families, praying that the LORD will bless them for the kindness they have shown to her family.

Verse Explanation

"And Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law..." - As they journey toward Judah, Naomi stops and urges Ruth and Orpah to return to Moab. Rather than asking them to continue with her into an uncertain future, she seeks what she believes will be best for them.

"'Go, return each to her mother's house.'" - Naomi encourages each woman to return to her own family. The reference to their mother's house likely emphasizes returning to the security and care of their families, where they might eventually find new husbands.

"'The LORD deal kindly with you...'" - The word "kindly" translates the Hebrew word hesed, one of the richest words in the Old Testament. It describes faithful, steadfast, covenant love expressed through loyal actions. Ruth and Orpah had demonstrated this kind of love by remaining with Naomi and caring for her through the deaths of their husbands.

"...as you have dealt with the dead and with me." - Naomi recognizes the genuine love and devotion both women have shown. Before their paths diverge, Scripture honors both Ruth and Orpah for their kindness, faithfulness, and care toward their husbands and Naomi.

Remark(s)

Naomi's first concern is not for herself but for Ruth and Orpah. Even in the midst of her own grief, she desires God's blessing for others. Deep sorrow does not have to extinguish a heart that seeks the good of others.

Ruth 1:9

NKJV

NKJV

Ruth 1:9 The LORD grant that you may find rest, each in the house of her husband." So she kissed them, and they lifted up their voices and wept.(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Ruth 1:9 May the LORD bless you with the security of another marriage.” Then she kissed them good-bye, and they all broke down and wept. (NLT)

Main Idea

Naomi prays that the LORD will give Ruth and Orpah new husbands and secure homes, but their emotional response reveals the depth of their love for her.

Verse Explanation

"'The LORD grant that you may find rest...'" - Naomi prays that the LORD will graciously provide for both women. The word "rest" refers to the security, stability, and provision found in a loving marriage and home. It is more than physical rest; it is the settled peace that comes from belonging and being cared for.

"...each in the house of her husband." - Naomi believes their best hope is to begin new lives in Moab. As young widows, they would have little opportunity for security by following Naomi to Bethlehem. Her counsel is motivated by love, not rejection.

"Then she kissed them..." - Naomi's kiss is a farewell. She believes this is the end of their journey together and releases them with her blessing.

"...and they lifted up their voices and wept." - Their grief is immediate and sincere. Ruth and Orpah are not merely losing a mother-in-law; they are facing the painful possibility of losing the woman who has become family to them. Their tears reveal the depth of the relationship that has been forged through years of shared sorrow.

Remark(s)

Naomi's prayer for "rest" becomes one of the key themes of the book. Although she expected Ruth to find that rest in Moab, God had prepared something far better. True rest would be found through the redeemer He had already ordained.

NKJV

NLT

NLT

Ruth 1:10 “No,” they said. “We want to go with you to your people.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Ruth and Orpah refuse Naomi's request and express their desire to remain with her and return to Judah.

Verse Explanation

"And they said to her..." - Both women respond together. At this point in the story, there is no difference between Ruth and Orpah. Both genuinely love Naomi and sincerely intend to remain with her.

"'Surely we will return with you...'" - Their response is immediate and heartfelt. Neither woman is ready to leave Naomi, despite the uncertainty that awaits them in Judah.

"...to your people." - Their commitment is expressed in terms of Naomi and her people. At this stage, the focus is still on their relationship with Naomi rather than a personal commitment to the LORD. The distinction between human affection and genuine faith will become clear in the verses that follow.

Remark(s)

At this moment, Ruth and Orpah appear identical. Both love Naomi, both weep, and both express the same desire to stay with her. Their true differences will only become evident when each must choose between the familiar life she is leaving behind and the uncertain future that lies ahead.

Ruth 1:11

NKJV

NKJV

Ruth 1:11 But Naomi said, "Turn back, my daughters; why will you go with me? Are there still sons in my womb, that they may be your husbands?(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Ruth 1:11 But Naomi replied, “Why should you go on with me? Can I still give birth to other sons who could grow up to be your husbands? (NLT)

Main Idea

Naomi urges Ruth and Orpah to return to Moab because she believes she has nothing left to offer them.

Verse Explanation

"'But Naomi said, "Turn back, my daughters...'" - Naomi lovingly urges them a second time to return. She is not pushing them away but trying to spare them from a future she believes will bring only hardship.

"'Why will you go with me?'" - Naomi sees no practical advantage in their continuing the journey. From her perspective, there is nothing waiting for them in Bethlehem except poverty and uncertainty.

"'Are there still sons in my womb...'" - Naomi refers to the custom that a close family member could marry the widow of a deceased brother to preserve the family line (later reflected in the law of levirate marriage; Deuteronomy 25:5-10). She is saying that she has no possibility of bearing more sons who could one day become their husbands.

"...that they may be your husbands?" - Naomi's argument is entirely practical. She believes every door of hope has closed. Humanly speaking, she sees no future for Ruth or Orpah if they remain with her.

Remark(s)

Naomi could only see what she did not have-no husband, no sons, no provision, and no future. She could not yet see what God had already prepared for her in Bethlehem. Human limitations never limit God's ability to provide.

NKJV

NKJV

Ruth 1:12 Turn back, my daughters, go"”for I am too old to have a husband. If I should say I have hope, if I should have a husband tonight and should also bear sons,(NKJV)

NLT

Main Idea

Naomi explains that, humanly speaking, there is no possible way she can provide Ruth and Orpah with the security or future they need.

Verse Explanation

"'Turn back, my daughters, go-for I am too old to have a husband.'" - Naomi again urges Ruth and Orpah to return to Moab. She believes she is beyond the age of marriage and childbearing and has nothing left to offer them.

"'If I should say I have hope...'" - Naomi refers to the family responsibility of preserving a deceased man's family line. Under Israel's law, if a married man died without children, a close male relative could marry the widow to raise up offspring in the name of the deceased and preserve his inheritance (see Deuteronomy 25:5-10). Naomi's point is that even if she somehow remarried and had more sons, they would still have to grow to adulthood before they could marry Ruth or Orpah.

"'If I should have a husband tonight and should also bear sons...'" - Naomi deliberately describes an impossible scenario. She stretches the argument to its limit to show that, from a human perspective, every possibility of providing a future for Ruth and Orpah has disappeared.

NKJV

NKJV

Ruth 1:13 would you wait for them till they were grown? Would you restrain yourselves from having husbands? No, my daughters; for it grieves me very much for your sakes that the hand of the LORD has gone out against me!"(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Ruth 1:13 Would you wait for them to grow up and refuse to marry someone else? No, of course not, my daughters! Things are far more bitter for me than for you, because the LORD himself has raised his fist against me.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Naomi urges Ruth and Orpah not to sacrifice their futures for her, believing that the LORD has dealt harshly with her.

Verse Explanation

"'Would you wait for them till they were grown?'" - Naomi continues her impossible illustration. Even if she could somehow bear sons, it would be unreasonable for Ruth and Orpah to wait many years for them to reach adulthood.

"'Would you restrain yourselves from having husbands?'" - Naomi asks whether they would be willing to give up the opportunity to marry and build families of their own. She knows such a sacrifice would place an unfair burden on them.

"'No, my daughters...'" - Naomi firmly rejects the idea. Her concern is genuine and selfless. She does not want Ruth and Orpah to give up their futures because of her circumstances.

"'For it grieves me very much for your sakes...'" - Naomi's sorrow is intensified by the thought that Ruth and Orpah might also suffer because of their relationship with her. Even in her grief, she is thinking more about their welfare than her own.

"'That the hand of the LORD has gone out against me!'" - Naomi believes the tragedies she has experienced have come from the LORD's hand. Although she correctly recognizes God's sovereignty, she wrongly concludes that God is against her. She cannot yet see that the God she believes has abandoned her is already preparing the very redemption that will restore her life.

Remark(s)

Naomi interpreted God's hand through her circumstances rather than interpreting her circumstances through God's character. Like Naomi, we often understand God's purposes only after He has finished writing the chapter we are living.

NLT

NLT

Ruth 1:14 And again they wept together, and Orpah kissed her mother-in-law good-bye. But Ruth clung tightly to Naomi. (NLT)

Main Idea

Orpah returns to her family, but Ruth remains with Naomi, revealing the different directions their hearts will ultimately take.

Verse Explanation

"Then they lifted up their voices and wept again." - Both women are deeply moved by Naomi's words. Their tears are genuine, showing that both loved Naomi and felt the pain of the coming separation.

"And Orpah kissed her mother-in-law..." - Orpah says goodbye and returns to Moab. Her decision should not be viewed as heartless or uncaring. She had shown genuine love for Naomi and had accompanied her part of the journey. She simply chose the familiar life that Naomi herself had encouraged her to pursue.

"...but Ruth clung to her." - The word "clung" describes holding on firmly and refusing to let go. Ruth's commitment goes beyond affection for Naomi. The verses that follow reveal that her heart has become attached not only to Naomi but also to Naomi's people and Naomi's God.

Remark(s)

Both women began the journey together. Both loved Naomi. Both wept. Both desired to stay. The difference between them was not revealed until the cost of following became clear. Genuine faith is often revealed when it requires us to choose between the familiar and obedience to God.

Ruth 1:15

NKJV

NKJV

Ruth 1:15 And she said, "Look, your sister-in-law has gone back to her people and to her gods; return after your sister-in-law."(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Ruth 1:15 “Look,” Naomi said to her, “your sister-in-law has gone back to her people and to her gods. You should do the same.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Naomi points to Orpah's decision as a reasonable example and urges Ruth to return to her people and her gods.

Verse Explanation

"'And she said, "Look...'" - Naomi points to Orpah's departure as evidence that returning to Moab is the sensible choice. From a human perspective, Orpah has chosen the safer and more secure path.

"'Your sister-in-law has gone back...'" - Naomi is not criticizing Orpah. She simply presents her decision as an example for Ruth to follow.

"'To her people and to her gods...'" - This statement reveals that the decision is about far more than geography. Returning to Moab also meant returning to its culture, its identity, and its worship. Ruth's choice is ultimately a spiritual one. She must decide not only where she will live, but whom she will worship.

"'Return after your sister-in-law.'" - Naomi genuinely believes this is the better path for Ruth. She still cannot see the future God has prepared, and she has no idea that Ruth's decision will become one of the greatest demonstrations of faith in the Old Testament.

Remark(s)

Every person must eventually choose between the familiar life they are leaving behind and the life of faith to which God is calling them. Ruth's decision was not simply about leaving Moab. It was about leaving her old life, idols, and all that was familiar to her in order to follow the One true God.

Ruth 1:16

NKJV

NKJV

Ruth 1:16 But Ruth said: "Entreat me not to leave you, Or to turn back from following after you; For wherever you go, I will go; And wherever you lodge, I will lodge; Your people shall be my people, And your God, my God.(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Ruth 1:16 But Ruth replied, “Don’t ask me to leave you and turn back. Wherever you go, I will go; wherever you live, I will live. Your people will be my people, and your God will be my God. (NLT)

Main Idea

Ruth makes one of the greatest declarations of faith in Scripture, committing herself to Naomi, to the people of Israel, and to the LORD.

Verse Explanation

"But Ruth said..." - Ruth now speaks for the first time in the book. Her words reveal that her decision is no longer based merely on love for Naomi but on a settled commitment to Naomi's God.

"'Entreat me not to leave you, or to turn back from following after you...'" - Ruth respectfully but firmly asks Naomi to stop urging her to return. Her decision has been made. She will not abandon Naomi.

"'For wherever you go, I will go; and wherever you lodge, I will lodge.'" - Ruth commits herself to share Naomi's future completely. Whether that future brings hardship or blessing, she will remain by Naomi's side.

"'Your people shall be my people...'" - Ruth willingly leaves behind her former identity to become part of God's people. She is no longer thinking of Moab as her home.

"'And your God, my God.'" - This is the heart of Ruth's confession. She is not merely changing countries or families; she is placing her faith in the God of Israel. Her commitment is spiritual before it is geographical.

Remark(s)

Ruth's commitment did not begin with a relationship with God; it began with a relationship with one of God's people. Naomi's life became the bridge that led Ruth to the LORD. God often draws people to Himself through the faithful witness of ordinary believers.

Ruth 1:17

NKJV

NKJV

Ruth 1:17 Where you die, I will die, And there will I be buried. The LORD do so to me, and more also, If anything but death parts you and me."(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Ruth 1:17 Wherever you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. May the LORD punish me severely if I allow anything but death to separate us!” (NLT)

Main Idea

Ruth seals her commitment with a solemn oath, declaring that nothing-not even death-will separate her from Naomi or from the God she has chosen to follow.

Verse Explanation

"'Where you die, I will die, and there will I be buried.'" - Ruth's commitment extends beyond Naomi's lifetime. She is not promising to accompany Naomi only until they reach Bethlehem or until circumstances improve. She is pledging to remain with her for the rest of her life.

To be buried in Israel rather than Moab also demonstrates that Ruth has completely identified herself with Naomi, Naomi's people, and Naomi's God.

"'The LORD do so to me, and more also...'" - Ruth calls upon the LORD-the covenant name of Israel's God-as the witness and judge of her promise. This is further evidence that she has embraced the God of Israel as her own.

"'If anything but death parts you and me.'" - Ruth binds herself with the strongest possible oath. Only death will end her commitment. Her words reveal unwavering love, steadfast loyalty, and genuine faith.

Remark(s)

Ruth left herself no path back to Moab. She committed her future, her home, her people, her God, and even the place of her burial to the LORD. Genuine faith is marked by wholehearted commitment, no divided loyalties and no going back.

Ruth 1:19

NLT

Ruth 1:19 So the two of them continued on their journey. When they came to Bethlehem, the entire town was excited by their arrival. “Is it really Naomi?” the women asked. (NLT)

Main Idea

Naomi and Ruth arrive in Bethlehem, and Naomi's return stirs the entire town because of the dramatic change in her life.

Verse Explanation

"Now the two of them went until they came to Bethlehem." - Naomi and Ruth complete the journey together. Ruth has left her homeland, her family, and her former way of life behind. The journey that began with uncertainty now brings them to Bethlehem, where God has already prepared the next chapter of their story.

"And it happened, when they had come to Bethlehem..." - Naomi returns to the place she left about ten years earlier. Many in the town apparently remembered her, making her return a significant event for the community.

"...that all the city was excited because of them." - The arrival of the two women quickly becomes the talk of Bethlehem. Naomi had left with a husband and two sons. She now returns as a poor widow accompanied by a Moabite daughter-in-law. The contrast is striking.

"And the women said, 'Is this Naomi?'" - The question reflects both recognition and astonishment. Naomi's years of sorrow and hardship have changed her so much that those who once knew her can scarcely believe she is the same woman.

Remark(s)

The people of Bethlehem immediately noticed that Naomi had changed. Our sin, poor decisions, and suffering, whether self-inflicted or simply the result of living in a fallen world, often leave visible marks on our lives. Yet God was not finished with Naomi's story. The woman who returned broken would one day become a testimony to His redeeming grace.

NLT

NLT

Ruth 1:20 “Don’t call me Naomi,” she responded. “Instead, call me Mara, for the Almighty has made life very bitter for me. (NLT)

Main Idea

Naomi expresses the depth of her grief by asking the people of Bethlehem to call her "Mara," believing that God has dealt bitterly with her.

Verse Explanation

"'But she said to them...'" - Naomi immediately responds to the women's recognition. Instead of celebrating her return, she speaks from the deep pain that has shaped the last ten years of her life.

"'Do not call me Naomi...'" - The name Naomi means "pleasant." Naomi believes that name no longer reflects her life or circumstances.

"'Call me Mara...'" - Mara means "bitter." Naomi is not changing her identity before God but expressing how she feels after losing her husband, her sons, and the future she once expected.

"'For the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me.'" - Naomi attributes her suffering to God's sovereign hand. She does not blame chance or fate. While she rightly recognizes God's sovereignty, she cannot yet see His loving purpose behind her suffering. Her theology is stronger than her understanding. She knows God is in control, but she does not yet know what He is accomplishing.

Remark(s)

Naomi allowed her circumstances to redefine her identity. God never again calls her "Mara." Throughout the rest of the book, He continues to call her "Naomi," reminding us that our suffering should not define who we are-God does.

Ruth 1:21 I went out full, and the LORD has brought me home again empty. Why do you call me Naomi, since the LORD has testified against me, and the Almighty has afflicted me?"(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Ruth 1:21 I went away full, but the LORD has brought me home empty. Why call me Naomi when the LORD has caused me to suffer and the Almighty has sent such tragedy upon me?” (NLT)

Main Idea

Naomi pours out her grief, believing that she left Bethlehem full but has returned empty because the LORD has afflicted her.

Verse Explanation

"'I went out full...'" - When Naomi left Bethlehem, she had a husband, two sons, and the hope of a future. Although the land was suffering from famine, she still considered herself rich in the blessings of family.

"'...and the LORD has brought me home again empty.'" - Naomi now returns without her husband or sons. In her mind, she has lost everything that gave her life security, joy, and hope. She cannot yet see that she has returned with Ruth, the very person through whom God will restore her future.

"'Why do you call me Naomi...'" - Naomi believes the name "Pleasant" no longer fits her life. Her circumstances seem to contradict everything her name once represented.

"'Since the LORD has testified against me, and the Almighty has afflicted me?'" - Naomi honestly expresses how she feels before God. She recognizes His sovereignty, but she misunderstands His purposes. She believes God has turned against her, when in reality He is already working to redeem and restore her life.

Remark(s)

Naomi believed she had returned empty, yet Ruth was standing beside her. Sometimes God's greatest blessings are already present in our lives long before we recognize them. Grief has a way of focusing our attention on our losses while hiding God's present mercies.

Ruth 1:22 So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabitess her daughter-in-law with her, who returned from the country of Moab. Now they came to Bethlehem at the beginning of barley harvest.(NKJV)

NLT

NLT

Ruth 1:22 So Naomi returned from Moab, accompanied by her daughter-in-law Ruth, the young Moabite woman. They arrived in Bethlehem in late spring, at the beginning of the barley harvest. (NLT)

Main Idea

Naomi and Ruth arrive in Bethlehem at the beginning of the barley harvest, quietly signaling that God's work of restoration is about to begin.

Verse Explanation

"So Naomi returned..." - The chapter closes where Naomi's journey home began. She returns to Bethlehem believing she has come back empty, unable to see what God is about to do.

"...and Ruth the Moabitess her daughter-in-law with her..." - Once again, Ruth is identified as "the Moabitess." The reminder of her background highlights the remarkable grace of God. An outsider from a nation often hostile to Israel has become part of God's unfolding plan of redemption.

"...who returned from the country of Moab." - Ruth's return is more than a change of location. She has left behind her homeland, her people, and her former gods to identify herself with the LORD and His people.

"Now they came to Bethlehem at the beginning of barley harvest." - This seemingly simple detail marks a major turning point. The famine is over, food is once again available, and the harvest is about to begin. More importantly, the timing prepares the reader for Ruth's meeting with Boaz in the fields. What appears to be an ordinary note of timing is actually the first visible sign that God's restoration has already begun.

Remark(s)

Naomi returned believing her life had ended in emptiness, but God brought her home at the very beginning of the harvest. When we think our story is ending, God is often just beginning a new chapter.
