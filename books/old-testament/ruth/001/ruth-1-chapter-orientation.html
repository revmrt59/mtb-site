Ruth 1 Orientation

Unifying Theme

In Ruth 1, Naomi experiences famine, loss, death, bitterness, and emptiness. Yet beneath her painful circumstances, God is already working through hidden providence and Ruth’s loyal love to begin the romance of redemption, drawing a Moabite outsider toward refuge, belonging, and the coming redeemer.

I. Overview of the Chapter

Ruth 1 begins during the time of the Judges, a period marked by spiritual unfaithfulness when everyone did what was right in his own eyes. During a famine in Bethlehem, Elimelech moves his family roughly 50–70 miles eastward across the Jordan into Moab, a neighboring nation with a troubled spiritual history in relation to Israel.

What begins as a search for provision becomes a story marked by death, grief, and tragedy. Elimelech dies. Naomi’s sons marry Moabite women, Ruth and Orpah, and then her sons, Mahlon and Chilion, also die. Naomi is left without husband, sons, security, or visible hope.

The rest of the chapter centers on Naomi’s return from Moab to Bethlehem and Ruth’s remarkable decision to remain with Naomi and identify herself with Naomi’s people and Naomi’s God. Though Naomi believes she is returning empty, the chapter quietly shows that God has already begun preparing the path of restoration.

II. Historical and Cultural Context

A. The Time of the Judges

The events occur “in the days when the judges ruled” (Ruth 1:1), a spiritually dark period in Israel’s history marked by spiritual unfaithfulness, idolatry, moral compromise, and instability.

Ruth stands in striking contrast to that period. While many in Israel were doing what was right in their own eyes, Ruth highlights personal faithfulness, loyalty, and quiet obedience before God.

B. Famine in Bethlehem

Bethlehem means “house of bread,” yet the chapter opens with famine in the land. This creates an intentional irony. The “house of bread” has no bread.

The famine creates the crisis that moves Naomi’s family to Moab, but it also sets the stage for God’s providence to unfold.

C. Moab and the Moabites

Moab had a long and troubled history with Israel. Moab opposed Israel, was associated with idolatry and immorality, and Moabites were restricted from the assembly of the LORD (Deuteronomy 23:3-6).

This makes Ruth’s later faith and inclusion especially striking. Ruth is not merely a foreign widow. She is a Moabite woman who leaves her former people and gods to identify herself with Israel and the God of Israel.

III. Structure of the Chapter

A. Naomi’s Tragedies: Crisis After Crisis (1:1-5)

  • The events occur during the spiritually dark day seen in the book of Judges.

  • A famine strikes Bethlehem.

  • Elimelech moves his family to Moab.

  • Elimelech dies.

  • Naomi’s sons, Mahlon and Chilion, marry Moabite women.

  • Both sons eventually die, leaving Naomi widowed, childless, empty, bitter and seemingly hopeless.

B. Naomi Begins the Journey Home (1:6-14)

  • Naomi hears that the LORD has visited His people with bread.

  • She begins returning to Bethlehem.

  • Naomi urges her daughters-in-law, Ruth and Orpah, to remain in Moab.

  • Orpah eventually returns, but Ruth remains with Naomi.

C. Ruth’s Commitment (1:15-18)

  • Ruth refuses to turn back.

  • She identifies herself with Naomi, Israel, and the God of Israel.

  • Ruth commits herself fully to Naomi and her people.

D. Naomi Returns in Bitterness and Emptiness (1:19-22)

  • Naomi returns to Bethlehem in grief and bitterness.

  • She asks to be called “Mara.”

  • She says she went out full but has returned empty.

  • The chapter closes at the beginning of barley harvest.

IV. Major Themes in Ruth 1

A. Loss and Emptiness

Ruth 1 confronts the painful realities of famine, death, widowhood, disappointment, uncertainty, and bitterness. The Bible does not minimize Naomi’s suffering. Her grief is presented honestly and deeply.

B. Return

Return is one of the major movements of the chapter. Naomi returns to Bethlehem. Orpah returns to Moab. Ruth refuses to turn back.

The chapter uses physical return to reveal deeper spiritual realities: Naomi is returning to the place of promise, Orpah is returning to the old life, and Ruth is being drawn toward the God of Israel and the redemption He has prepared.

C. Loyal Love

Ruth’s devotion to Naomi reflects hesed, steadfast loyal love expressed through faithful loyalty and sacrificial commitment.

Ruth remains faithful when there is no visible reward, when the future is uncertain, and when following Naomi may cost her everything.

Ruth’s loyal love is also part of the romance of redemption, because her costly devotion places her on the path where she will come under the care of the redeemer

D. God’s Hidden Providence

God does not speak directly in the chapter, yet His providence quietly governs the story. The famine ends. Naomi hears there is bread in Bethlehem. Ruth remains with Naomi. They return at the beginning of barley harvest.

God is already preparing redemption before Naomi can see it

E. Faith in Difficult Seasons

Ruth demonstrates faith expressed through action and commitment. Her decision to follow Naomi and the God of Israel requires surrender, trust, sacrifice, and perseverance.

Her faith is not merely emotional attachment to Naomi. It is wholehearted allegiance to Naomi’s people and Naomi’s God.

F. The Beginning of the Romance of Redemption

Ruth 1 begins the movement from being aliened from God to belonging to His people. Ruth starts the chapter as a Moabite widow outside Israel, outside Bethlehem, and outside visible hope. Yet by the end of the chapter, she is walking toward Bethlehem with Naomi, identifying with Naomi’s people, and confessing Naomi’s God as her own.

This begins the larger redemption story. Before Ruth ever meets Boaz, God is already drawing her toward refuge, provision, and the redeemer who will bring her into rest and belonging.

V. Key Words and Concepts

  • Famine - a season of lack, hardship, and need.

  • Return - the act of turning back or coming home, often carrying spiritual significance.

  • Moab - a place associated with Israel’s troubled history, opposition, and spiritual compromise.

  • Widowhood - a condition of deep vulnerability, loss, and need in the ancient world.

  • Mahlon and Chilion — Naomi’s sons whose names may carry ideas of weakness, sickness, frailty, or wasting. Some interpreters believe their names function symbolically within the tragic movement of the chapter.

  • Hesed - steadfast loyal love expressed through faithful loyalty, kindness, mercy, and commitment.

  • Mara - “bitter,” the name Naomi chooses to describe her grief and sense of loss.

  • Empty - Naomi’s description of her condition after losing her husband and sons.

  • Providence - God’s sovereign guidance and active involvement in human events to accomplish His purposes.

  • Harvest - a sign of provision, new beginning, and the quiet emergence of restoration. The harvest signals the beginning of God’s restoring work.

VI. Key Verses

Ruth 1:16-17 But Ruth said: Entreat me not to leave you, Or to turn back from following after you; For wherever you go, I will go; And wherever you lodge, I will lodge; Your people shall be my people, And your God, my God. Where you die, I will die, And there will I be buried. The LORD do so to me, and more also, If anything but death parts you and me. (NKJV)

Ruth’s faithful loyalty and declaration marks her wholehearted identification with Naomi, Israel, and the God of Israel.

Ruth 1:20-21 But she said to them, Do not call me Naomi; call me Mara, for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me. I went out full, and the LORD has brought me home again empty. Why do you call me Naomi, since the LORD has testified against me, and the Almighty has afflicted me? (NKJV)

Naomi interprets her life through grief, bitterness, and emptiness, yet we can already see the beginning of God’s redemptive work.

Ruth 1:22 So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabitess her daughter-in-law with her, who returned from the country of Moab. Now they came to Bethlehem at the beginning of barley harvest. (NKJV)

The chapter closes with Naomi’s return, Ruth’s presence, and the beginning of harvest, quietly preparing the reader for God’s provision and redemption.

VII. Why Ruth 1 Matters

Ruth 1 matters because it speaks honestly to seasons of grief, death, famine, disappointment, bitterness, uncertainty, waiting, loss, and tragedy in the lives of God’s people.

The chapter reminds believers that God is still working in painful seasons, even when His work is hidden. Bitterness does not have to be the end of the story. God often begins restoration before we recognize it. Faithfulness to God and to others matters, and God’s providence is active even when He seems silent.

Ruth 1 teaches that even in extreme tragedy and sorrow, God is at work quietly laying the foundation for future redemption and restoration.
