Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 1
Main Idea:

Famine and three deaths empty Naomi’s household in Moab, but on the road home Ruth the Moabitess binds herself to Naomi, her people, and her God, and the two widows reach Bethlehem as the barley harvest begins.

Setting:

The story is set “in the days when the judges ruled” (1:1). It moves from Bethlehem in Judah to the country of Moab and then back to Bethlehem.

Chapter Outline:

  • Ruth 1:1–5 — Famine, Moab, and Naomi Bereaved

  • Ruth 1:6–7 — The LORD Gives Bread, and Naomi Sets Out for Home

  • Ruth 1:8–14 — Naomi Releases Her Daughters-in-Law

  • Ruth 1:15–18 — Ruth’s Vow of Allegiance to Naomi and Her God

  • Ruth 1:19–22 — Naomi’s Lament and Return at Barley Harvest

Key Verse:

“But Ruth said: ‘Entreat me not to leave you, Or to turn back from following after you; For wherever you go, I will go; And wherever you lodge, I will lodge; Your people shall be my people, And your God, my God.’” (Ruth 1:16)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Ruth 1 sets out the need the rest of the book will answer. A man whose name means My God is King leads his family out of Bethlehem, the house of bread, to escape famine. Scripture does not report his thoughts, but his name stands beside his choice: when famine tested trust in God’s provision, he sought relief in Moab, and temporary relief became years of settled compromise. Elimelech dies, and then both of his sons; Scripture gives no reason for these deaths and passes no verdict on them. Naomi is left without husband, sons, or heir.

Yet the LORD is already at work. News that He has “visited His people by giving them bread” (1:6) turns Naomi toward home while she is still grieving. Orpah goes back, but Ruth clings to Naomi and vows, “Your people shall be my people, And your God, my God” (1:16). Her vow joins loyal compassion for Naomi with a response to the real faith she has seen in her, for even in loss Naomi blesses her daughters-in-law in the LORD’s name and keeps speaking of Him (1:8–9, 13). A woman from a people long hostile to Israel turns to Israel’s God, and the story of redeeming grace begins.

In Bethlehem, Naomi’s honest lament still speaks of the LORD: “I went out full, and the LORD has brought me home again empty” (1:21). Yet Ruth stands beside her and the barley harvest is beginning. Her emptiness is real, but it is not the end of God’s purpose.

Chapter Sections

Ruth 1:1–5 — Famine, Moab, and Naomi Bereaved

In the days of the judges, famine drives Elimelech’s family from Bethlehem to Moab, where a refuge from hunger becomes a settled life. Elimelech dies, his sons marry Orpah and Ruth, and after about ten years both sons die, leaving Naomi without husband or sons.

Ruth 1:6–7 — The LORD Gives Bread, and Naomi Sets Out for Home

When Naomi hears that the LORD has given His people bread, she sets out for Judah with both daughters-in-law. This first report of the LORD’s action moves a grieving widow toward home.

Ruth 1:8–14 — Naomi Releases Her Daughters-in-Law

Naomi urges both women to go back, blessing them in the LORD’s name for the kindness they have shown and praying that they will find rest in a husband’s house. She has no sons left to offer them; Orpah kisses her goodbye, but Ruth clings to her.

Ruth 1:15–18 — Ruth’s Vow of Allegiance to Naomi and Her God

When Naomi urges Ruth to follow Orpah back to her people and her gods, Ruth vows to share Naomi’s people, God, death, and burial, sealing her words with an oath in the LORD’s name. Naomi sees that Ruth is determined and stops urging her.

Ruth 1:19–22 — Naomi’s Lament and Return at Barley Harvest

Bethlehem is stirred by their arrival, and Naomi asks to be called Mara because the Almighty has dealt bitterly with her. Yet she has come home with Ruth the Moabitess, and they arrive just as the barley harvest begins.

Why This Chapter Matters

Ruth 1 shows that God’s people can feel the full weight of loss without abandoning faith. Naomi’s bitterness is lament, not unbelief: she brings her grief into the open and still speaks of the LORD as the One who rules her life. Scripture makes room for that kind of honest sorrow.

The chapter also shows God’s grace reaching beyond Israel’s borders. Ruth comes from Moab, a nation barred from Israel’s assembly, yet she chooses Israel’s God at real cost. The book will trace that choice into the family line of David, and in the larger story of Scripture into the family of Jesus Christ (Matthew 1:5).

Key Themes to Watch For

Emptiness and need. Naomi loses her husband, her sons, and her future in Moab, and she names her condition as empty (1:3–5, 21). The rest of the book answers that emptiness.

Returning home. The language of return runs through the chapter as Naomi turns back toward Bethlehem after hearing of the LORD’s provision (1:6–7, 22).

The LORD’s hidden provision. Bread returns to Bethlehem (1:6), and the women arrive as the barley harvest begins (1:22), though Naomi cannot yet see what God is doing.

Loyal kindness. Naomi prays that the LORD will deal kindly with her daughters-in-law as they have dealt with her (1:8), and Ruth’s clinging and vow show that same loyal love (1:14–17).

Costly allegiance. Ruth turns away from her people and their gods to join Naomi’s people and Naomi’s God for life (1:15–17).

Key Words & Concepts

Bethlehem, Judah. The town’s name means house of bread, so famine there (1:1) and bread restored (1:6) frame the chapter. Bethlehem later becomes the hometown of David.

Moab. A people living east of the Dead Sea who had opposed Israel on the way to Canaan and were barred from the assembly of the LORD (Deuteronomy 23:3–6). Ruth’s Moabite background makes God’s welcome of her especially striking.

Elimelech. His name means My God is King. Set beside his move to Moab during a famine (1:1–2), the name raises the question of where a person turns for provision when trust becomes costly.

Kindness (1:8). The Hebrew word hesed means loyal, steadfast love that goes beyond what duty requires. It appears here for the first time in Ruth and returns at key moments in the story (2:20; 3:10).

Rest (1:9). A settled, secure home with a husband’s protection. Naomi’s prayer for rest prepares for her plan to seek security for Ruth in chapter 3 (3:1).

Mara (1:20). Naomi asks to be called Mara, meaning bitter, because of her losses. The narrator keeps calling her Naomi.
