WEALTH - ( chayil , Strong's H2428 )

Summary:

In Ruth 2:1, the Hebrew word chayil, translated as "wealth," refers to far more than material riches. It encompasses physical strength, financial abundance, social standing, moral integrity, and influential capability. Applied to Boaz as "a man of great wealth" (gibbôr chayil), it presents him not merely as a wealthy landowner but as a man of noble character, resourcefulness, and social responsibility. He is introduced as a fit redeemer who possesses both the material means and the moral fortitude to rescue Naomi's family line.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The verbal root of chayil carries the primary idea of strength, power, and efficiency.

• Historically, the term is associated with military force, a host, or physical capability. Over time, this concept of "strength" broadened to include the "power" that comes from material substance (wealth) and the "strength of character" that constitutes moral virtue or valor.

• In the agrarian society of ancient Israel, true "wealth" was not a passive accumulation of currency but an active, dynamic force. It represented the capability to mobilize resources, lead laborers, protect the vulnerable, and maintain order in the community.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The semantic range of chayil includes:

• Strength, power, or physical might.

• Wealth, substance, riches, or property.

• Ability, efficiency, capability, or valor.

• Virtue, noble character, or integrity.

• An army, host, or band of soldiers.

• In Ruth 2:1, the phrase gibbôr chayil is used. While gibbôr means "mighty man" or "champion," combined with chayil in this context, it designates a person of high social standing, substantial financial means, and outstanding moral integrity.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• chayil as Military Power: It frequently refers to an army or host, illustrating a force that is organized, powerful, and ready for action.

• chayil as Material Wealth: It represents resources acquired through effort or divine blessing, functioning as the tool by which a household is secured and maintained.

• chayil as Character/Virtue: It is the defining quality of a person who acts with capability and integrity. It bridges physical/financial competence with moral excellence, showing that true strength is never divorced from character.

4. Theological Implications

• Divine stewardship: chayil is a gift from God. Deuteronomy 8:18 establishes that Yahweh gives the power to get wealth. Therefore, wealth is not for self-indulgence but for the execution of righteousness and the preservation of the covenant community.

• The character of the Redeemer: Boaz’s chayil mirrors God's own strength and resourcefulness. Wealth in the biblical economy is designed for the redemption of the helpless. Boaz's capability enables him to act as the kinsman-redeemer (goel), demonstrating how material wealth should serve spiritual and covenantal purposes.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 2:1, Boaz is introduced immediately after Naomi and Ruth arrive in Bethlehem in deep poverty. By describing Boaz as a "man of great wealth" (gibbôr chayil), the author sets up a dramatic contrast and a ray of hope for the destitute widows.

• While "wealth" captures his financial ability to buy back Elimelech’s land and support Naomi and Ruth, the deeper nuance of chayil assures the reader that Boaz is also a man of honor who will not take advantage of the vulnerable Moabite widow.

• The same term is later applied to Ruth herself in Ruth 3:11 ("a virtuous woman," eshet chayil), showing that despite her material poverty, she possesses the same noble strength of character as Boaz. They are spiritual and moral equals.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Deuteronomy 8:18 - "And you shall remember the LORD your God, for it is He who gives you power to get wealth, that He may establish His covenant which He swore to your fathers, as it is this day."

• Ruth 3:11 - "And now, my daughter, do not fear. I will do for you all that you request, for all the people of my town know that you are a virtuous woman."

• Proverbs 31:10 - "Who can find a virtuous wife? For her worth is far above rubies."

• Psalm 60:12 - "Through God we will do valiantly, For it is He who shall tread down our enemies."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The linguistic connection between Ruth and Boaz is striking. Boaz is a gibbôr chayil (man of wealth/valor) in Ruth 2:1, and Ruth is called an eshet chayil (woman of virtue/valor) in Ruth 3:11. Their union represents the merging of two types of strength: his material and social capacity, and her resilient, faithful character.

• This term counters the fallen human tendency to tie wealth solely to power and exploitation. In Boaz, chayil is subverted to mean protective strength, kindness, and covenant faithfulness (chesed).
