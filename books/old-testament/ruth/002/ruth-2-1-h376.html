MAN - ( 'îš , Strong's H376 )

Summary:

In Ruth 2:1, the Hebrew word 'îš (translated "man") designates an individual of high standing, capability, and moral character. Rather than merely indicating gender, its pairing with the phrase gibbôr hayil ("of great wealth" or "of valor") introduces Boaz not just as a male relative, but as a person of substance, influence, and noble character who is positioned by God's providence to act as a redeemer. This word establishes the dignity and capacity of the one who will rescue Ruth and Naomi from their vulnerability.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The Hebrew word 'îš refers primarily to an individual man, a husband, or a person of high standing. It is distinguished from adam (which often refers to humanity collectively or humankind from the dust) and enosh (which frequently emphasizes mortal frailty).

• Historically and culturally, 'îš carries connotations of individuality, strength, and responsibility within the covenant community. In the patriarchal society of ancient Israel, an 'îš was expected to represent, protect, and provide for his household.

• In the context of the Ancient Near East, when 'îš is joined with descriptive modifiers, it describes a person's social standing, moral reputation, or functional capacity within the city gate and the community.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The primary meaning of 'îš is an adult male human being, a husband, or a person of note. It designates a specific individual in contrast to a group, emphasizing personal identity and agency.

• The semantic range of the word includes:

• An adult male or husband in relation to a wife.

• A champion, soldier, or man of war.

• A person of high social status, noble character, or outstanding qualities.

• An individual person, used distributively to mean "each" or "every one."

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Throughout the Old Testament, 'îš is used to paint a picture of personal responsibility and agency. When God looks for an individual of courage and action to stand in the gap, this is the term employed.

• In legal and covenantal contexts, 'îš designates a responsible citizen who is accountable before the law and God's covenant.

• The word is frequently contrasted with ishah (woman), reflecting the complementary relationship established in Genesis 2:23, where the man says, "She shall be called Woman (ishah), because she was taken out of Man ('îš)." This establishes a deep connection of identity, responsibility, and mutual destiny.

4. Theological Implications

• The use of 'îš points to the theological reality of individual accountability before God. God deals not only with nations collectively, but with individual men who must choose to walk in covenant faithfulness.

• In the context of redemption, the "man" represents the active agent of deliverance. Just as the first 'îš (Adam) failed his bride in the Garden, God raises up faithful men throughout salvation history to foreshadow the ultimate 'îš—Jesus Christ—the true husband and redeemer of His people.

• The designation of a man of moral and financial substance highlights how God uses human agency, resources, and social standing to execute His providential care for the vulnerable, the widow, and the stranger.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 2:1, 'îš introduces Boaz as a "man of great wealth" ('îš gibbôr hayil). The placement of 'îš at the beginning of Boaz's introduction is highly deliberate. It contrasts sharply with the deaths of the three men (Elimelech, Mahlon, and Chilion) in chapter 1, which left Naomi and Ruth in a state of utter vulnerability.

• The text signals to the reader that where there was a vacuum of male protection and leadership, God has provided an 'îš—a strong, capable, and wealthy man who has the legal and economic power to redeem.

• The phrase gibbôr hayil is elsewhere translated as "mighty man of valor" (used of warriors like Gideon or David). Applied to Boaz in an agricultural and social context, it denotes a man of noble character, social prominence, and financial capability. He is not merely wealthy; he is a man of honor, strength, and integrity.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:23 - "And Adam said: 'This is now bone of my bones And flesh of my flesh; She shall be called Woman, Because she was taken out of Man.'"

• 1 Kings 2:2 - "I go the way of all the earth; be strong therefore, and prove yourself a man."

• Psalm 1:1 - "Blessed is the man Who walks not in the counsel of the ungodly, Nor stands in the path of sinners, Nor sits in the seat of the scornful;"

• Jeremiah 5:1 - "Run to and fro through the streets of Jerusalem; See now and know; And seek in her open places If you can find a man, If there is anyone who executes judgment, Who seeks the truth, And I will pardon her."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The word 'îš is often used in contrast to adam. While adam emphasizes the earthly origin of humanity (from the ground, adamah), 'îš highlights the dignity, capability, and social relationship of the individual.

• In the book of Ruth, Boaz represents the ideal covenant man. Where the book of Judges displays the breakdown of manhood, leadership, and morality in Israel, the character of Boaz stands out as a beacon of what a covenant-keeping 'îš is designed to be: generous, protective, respectful, and obedient to the Torah.
