FATHER - ( 'ab , Strong's H1 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'ab primarily denotes a male parent, but its semantic range extends far beyond biological paternity to encompass ancestors, originators of crafts, counselors, and protectors. In the patriarchal culture of the ancient Near East, the father was the source of identity, security, inheritance, and covenant continuity. Ruth's decision to leave her father highlights her profound sacrifice, choosing to abandon her primary source of natural protection and identity to embrace the God and people of Israel.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word 'ab is one of the most fundamental words in the Semitic language family. In early pictographic Hebrew, the letters aleph (representing strength or an ox) and bet (representing a house or tent) combine to form a word picture of "the strength of the house" or "the leader of the household." The father was the foundational pillar of the family unit, responsible for its governance, protection, spiritual guidance, and economic survival. In the ancient Near East, leaving one's father's house meant relinquishing legal rights, economic security, social standing, and familial gods.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun 'ab carries several distinct meanings based on context:

• The immediate male parent (biological father).

• An ancestor, forefather, grandfather, or founder of a tribe or nation.

• The originator, founder, or pioneer of a class, profession, or way of life (such as the father of those who dwell in tents).

• An honorific title of respect and authority for a ruler, counselor, prophet, or priest.

• God as the Creator, Covenant King, Father of Israel, and protector of the vulnerable.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The concept of the father is deeply woven into the fabric of covenant theology. The father represents the source of blessing and inheritance, as seen in Isaac blessing Jacob. The father's house (bet 'ab) was the basic socio-economic unit in Israel, representing safety, belonging, and identity. Metaphorically, the term describes a source of wisdom. A teacher acts as a father to his disciples, passing down life-giving instruction. It is also used to depict God's tender, disciplinary love: "As a father pities his children, so the Lord pities those who fear Him."

4. Theological Implications

The fatherhood of God establishes the pattern for human fatherhood, characterized by authority, provision, protection, instruction, and compassion. Leaving one's father and mother is a biblical motif of radical departure and realignment. It occurs first in the creation mandate for marriage (Genesis 2:24) and later in the call of Abraham (Genesis 12:1). By leaving her father, Ruth replicates the faith of Abraham. She departs from her biological father and her ancestral gods to find refuge under the wings of the Lord, God of Israel, illustrating that spiritual alignment and covenant loyalty supersede biological and cultural origins.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:11, Boaz acknowledges Ruth's remarkable virtue, noting that she left her "father and your mother and the land of your birth." In Moabite society, as in Israel, a widow without a husband or son would naturally return to her father’s house for protection and sustenance, which is exactly what Naomi urged her daughters-in-law to do. By leaving her biological father, Ruth surrendered her ultimate safety net. Her action was not mere relocation; it was a profound act of faith and self-emptying love toward Naomi and Naomi's God. Boaz recognizes this voluntary vulnerability as highly commendable and worthy of divine reward.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:24 - "Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and be joined to his wife, and they shall become one flesh."

• Genesis 12:1 - "Now the Lord had said to Abram: 'Get out of your country, From your family And from your father's house, To a land that I will show you.'"

• Psalm 68:5 - "A father of the fatherless, a defender of widows, Is God in His holy habitation."

• Proverbs 3:12 - "For whom the Lord loves He corrects, Just as a father the son in whom he delights."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between Ruth's biological father and her new spiritual reality is striking. In Moabitish paganism, the ultimate "father" of the nation was the deity Chemosh. By leaving her earthly father, Ruth also turned her back on her national and spiritual father, choosing instead Yahweh. This text serves as a powerful Old Testament precursor to Christ's teachings in the New Testament regarding discipleship, where followers must be willing to prioritize the kingdom of God over father, mother, and earthly security.
