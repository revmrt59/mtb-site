SINCE - ( 'achar , Strong's H310 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'achar fundamentally denotes behind, after, or following, whether in physical space or linear time. In Ruth 2:11, translated as "since" in the NKJV, it marks a critical temporal transition—the dividing line between Ruth's past life of security in Moab and her new life of sacrificial devotion in Israel. It highlights that her remarkable kindness (chesed) was not a fleeting sentiment but a sustained, active commitment that began immediately after her husband's death and continued through her self-exile to Bethlehem.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word 'achar comes from a root meaning to remain behind, delay, or tarry. In physical geography, it refers to what is "behind" someone's back or "after" them in a physical line of progression. When applied to time, it designates what comes "afterwards" or "since" a landmark event. It evokes the image of looking back at a point of departure. In the ancient Near Eastern mind, time was often viewed with the past in front of you (visible to the mind's eye) and the future behind you (unknown and unseen). Thus, 'achar marks the boundary stone of a life-altering event from which all subsequent actions flow.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

As a preposition, 'achar means "behind" (spatial) or "after" (temporal). As an adverb, it translates to "afterwards," "then," or "behind." As a conjunction, it denotes "after that" or "since." The core semantic range spans from physical location (following behind someone) to temporal sequence (after these things). In Ruth 2:11, it functions as a temporal preposition governing the noun phrase "the death of your husband," establishing a clear point of origin for Ruth's exceptional behavior.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Physical Following: Used frequently to describe walking after or behind someone, denoting discipleship, loyalty, or pursuit. It is the posture of a follower behind a leader.

• Temporal Sequence: Marking historical epochs or successive generations (e.g., "after these things" or "after the death of Moses").

• The Boundary of Bereavement: In Ruth 2:11, 'achar functions as a historical pivot. It separates the period of Ruth's marriage in Moab from her widowhood and voluntary relocation. It pictures her turning her back on her past and stepping into an unseen future.

4. Theological Implications

• Covenant Loyalty: In the Old Testament, true faith is often expressed as walking "after" ('achar) Yahweh. Ruth's choice to follow Naomi and Yahweh after her husband's death mirrors this covenantal posture.

• Character Proven in Crisis: Ruth's faith and character (chesed) were not merely theoretical; they were proven since the tragedy. Suffering often causes individuals to turn inward in self-preservation, but Ruth turned outward in self-giving love.

• Providential Turning Points: The word underscores that God uses catastrophic endings (death) to initiate redemptive beginnings. What occurs after the tragedy is where God's quiet providence weaves a new story of redemption.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:11, Boaz acknowledges that Ruth's reputation has preceded her. He highlights two major realities divided by this word 'achar ("since"): first, her consistent kindness to her mother-in-law since the death of her husband, and second, her radical departure from her homeland and parents. The use of "since" ('achar) proves that Ruth's loyalty was not a momentary impulse during the funeral or a short-lived reaction to grief. It was a sustained, enduring lifestyle choice. Her covenant love filled the painful void left by the death of her husband, showing that her devotion was directed toward the living (Naomi) and the true God of Israel.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 15:1 - "After ('achar) these things the word of the LORD came to Abram in a vision, saying, 'Do not be afraid, Abram. I am your shield, your exceedingly great reward.'"

• Genesis 22:13 - "Then Abraham lifted his eyes and looked, and there behind ('achar) him was a ram caught in a thicket by its horns."

• Joshua 1:1 - "After ('achar) the death of Moses the servant of the LORD, it came to pass that the LORD spoke to Joshua the son of Nun, Moses' assistant, saying..."

• 1 Kings 19:12 - "and after ('achar) the earthquake a fire, but the LORD was not in the fire; and after ('achar) the fire a still small voice."

• Proverbs 20:7 - "The righteous man walks in his integrity; His children are blessed after ('achar) him."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Hebrew construct 'acharei moth ("after the death of") is a common biblical formula used to introduce significant transition periods in Israel's history, such as after the death of Moses (Joshua 1:1), after the death of Joshua (Judges 1:1), and after the death of Saul (2 Samuel 1:1). By using this exact formula in Ruth 2:11, the narrative elevates Ruth’s personal story of faithfulness to the level of these great national transitions, signaling that her quiet devotion in a time of grief is of monumental significance in the redemptive history of Israel, ultimately leading to King David and the Messiah.
