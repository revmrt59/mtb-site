PEOPLE - ( 'am , Strong's H5971 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'am refers to a nation, people group, or family unit bound together by common blood, covenant, or community. In Ruth 2:11, it designates the community of Israel—a people defined not just by geography but by their covenant relationship with Yahweh. Ruth's willingness to leave her homeland and join this "people whom [she] did not know before" highlights her profound faith and radical commitment, choosing to graft herself into God’s covenant family.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The word 'am carries a deep sense of relational and familial connection. Unlike other Hebrew words for nation (such as goy, which often emphasizes a political or geopolitical entity), 'am stresses consanguinity, relationship, and kinship.

• It is derived from a root suggesting association, companionship, and being together.

• In the ancient Near East, to belong to a 'am was to have an identity, protection, and a spiritual inheritance. To leave one's 'am was to strip oneself of all social, economic, and spiritual security.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The noun 'am means a people, nation, folk, or member of a kin group. It represents a collective group of human beings sharing a common lineage, language, or covenant.

• In the singular, it most frequently refers to the nation of Israel as the special, chosen people of God.

• It can also refer to a specific localized community, a gathering of citizens, or an army of men.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• In Scripture, 'am is frequently contrasted with goy. While goy can refer to any national body (and is often used for pagan, Gentile nations), 'am is the preferred term for God's relational family, Israel.

• It paints a picture of a flock under a shepherd, a family under a father, or a congregation gathered for worship.

• The word is foundational to the covenant formula repeated throughout Scripture: "I will be their God, and they shall be My people."

4. Theological Implications

• The concept of 'am is central to biblical covenant theology. God did not just save individuals; He formed a people for His own possession.

• Ruth’s transition from the 'am of Moab (Chemosh's people) to the 'am of Israel (Yahweh's people) represents true conversion. It is a physical journey that mirrors a spiritual reality—moving from alien status to fellow citizens with the saints.

• This showcases the missionary heart of God in the Old Testament. Even under the Old Covenant, the boundaries of God's 'am were porous enough to welcome a repentant, faithful foreigner who sought refuge under His wings.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 2:11, Boaz recognizes the sheer weight of Ruth's sacrifice. She left her father, mother, and land of birth to come to a "people" she did not previously know.

• This closely echoes Abraham's call in Genesis 12:1. Like Abraham, Ruth steps out in faith into the unknown, trusting in the God of Israel.

• By joining this 'am, Ruth was not just changing her physical address; she was adopting their God, their laws, their customs, and their future. This fulfills her famous vow in Ruth 1:16: "Your people shall be my people, and your God, my God."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Ruth 1:16 – "But Ruth said: 'Entreat me not to leave you, or to turn back from following after you; for wherever you go, I will go; and wherever you lodge, I will lodge; your people shall be my people, and your God, my God.'"

• Exodus 19:5 – "Now therefore, if you will indeed obey My voice and keep My covenant, then you shall be a special treasure to Me above all people; for all the earth is Mine."

• Deuteronomy 7:6 – "For you are a holy people to the LORD your God; the LORD your God has chosen you to be a people for Himself, a special treasure above all peoples on the face of the earth."

• Psalm 100:3 – "Know that the LORD, He is God; It is He who has made us, and not we ourselves; We are His people and the sheep of His pasture."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The transition of Ruth from an outsider to a core member of God's 'am is consummated in the genealogy at the end of the book. She becomes the great-grandmother of King David and an ancestor of Jesus Christ.

• This demonstrates that inclusion in God's true 'am is ultimately based on faith, grace, and covenant loyalty (hesed), rather than physical pedigree alone.
