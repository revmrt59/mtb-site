ANSWERED - ( 'anah , Strong's H6030 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'anah primarily means to answer, respond, or testify. In the Old Testament, it goes far beyond a casual conversational reply, frequently denoting a formal, significant, or life-altering response to a situation, cry, or inquiry. In Ruth 2:11, when Boaz "answered" Ruth, his response was not a mere social exchange but a profound legal, social, and spiritual affirmation of her character and faith. By answering her plea for favor, Boaz speaks as a witness to her righteousness, paving the way for her redemption and showing how God provides a physical answer to the cries of the vulnerable.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete background of 'anah is rooted in relational reciprocity—the idea of one deep reality responding to another. In the ancient agrarian and covenantal world, to "answer" was to respond to a physical stimulus, a vocal call, or a legal charge. It is the language of covenantal relationship. When a petitioner cried out in distress, the superior party's act of "answering" was an exercise of grace, power, and protection. In legal settings, 'anah was the term used for giving formal testimony in court. The word picture is not of a passing remark, but of a deliberate, weighty declaration that establishes truth and alters the hearer's standing.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb 'anah refers to the act of responding, replying, or testifying. It occurs over 300 times in the Hebrew Bible. Its semantic range includes:

• Responding to an inquiry or a direct statement.

• Declaring a formal or legal testimony (either for or against someone).

• Responding to a cry of distress with active, saving help.

• Singing responsively or shouting in triumph.

In Ruth 2:11, the word functions in its conversational and testimonial sense. Boaz is not merely replying to Ruth’s question; he is making a formal declaration that validates her presence, her history, and her character.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout Scripture, 'anah is used in several vivid contexts:

• The Divine Response: It describes God hearing and responding to the prayers of His people. When God "answers," He does not just speak; He acts to deliver.

• The Legal Witness: It is used in the context of bearing witness. To "answer" in court is to provide testimony that decides a case.

• The Liturgical Shout: It describes responsive singing or declaring praises in a worship setting, where one group calls out and another answers.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, 'anah reveals that God is a relational being who hears and responds to human dependency. He is not silent. Furthermore, in the narrative of redemption, human agents often act as the means through which God "answers" the afflicted.

There is a powerful literary and theological connection in the Book of Ruth involving this very word. In Ruth 1:21, Naomi returned to Bethlehem in bitterness, crying out, "the LORD has testified ['anah] against me." She felt God's formal testimony was one of condemnation and affliction. However, in Ruth 2:11, Boaz "answers" ('anah) Ruth with words of blessing, protection, and grace. Through Boaz’s gracious answer, God reverses Naomi’s bitter perspective, showing that His ultimate "testimony" toward them is one of redemption, not destruction.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:10, Ruth falls on her face, bows to the ground, and asks Boaz, "Why have I found favor in your eyes, that you should take notice of me, since I am a foreigner?" She is acutely aware of her vulnerability and her status as an outsider.

In Ruth 2:11, Boaz "answered and said to her, 'It has been fully reported to me, all that you have done...'"

Boaz’s 'anah is a protective, counter-cultural intervention. Instead of ignoring her or treating her as a cheap laborer, Boaz uses his authority to give a public testimony of her worth. He "answers" her self-deprecation by listing her virtues—her loyalty to Naomi, her self-sacrifice, and her decision to trust under the wings of the Lord God of Israel. His answer is a legal and social shield, establishing her reputation in the eyes of everyone in the field.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Isaiah 58:9 - "Then you shall call, and the LORD will answer; You shall cry, and He will say, 'Here I am.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

For the preacher and teacher, Boaz’s "answer" in Ruth 2:11 serves as a beautiful type of Jesus Christ’s response to the sinner. Like Ruth, we come as foreigners, empty-handed, asking why we have found favor in the eyes of the Lord. Christ does not turn us away; He "answers" our brokenness with a declaration of His grace. He testifies on our behalf, covering our past and inviting us to find refuge under His wings. Boaz shows us that godly leadership uses its voice to answer the cries of the marginalized with life-giving, protective truth.
