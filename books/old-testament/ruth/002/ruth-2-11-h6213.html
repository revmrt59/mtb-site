DONE - ( asah , Strong's H6213 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb asah means to do, make, fashion, or accomplish. In the context of Ruth 2:11, it highlights the active, concrete deeds of loyalty and kindness that Ruth performed for her mother-in-law, Naomi. Boaz recognizes that Ruth's faith and devotion were not merely spoken sentiments but were demonstrated through tangible, self-sacrificial actions. This word underscores the biblical truth that genuine covenant love (chesed) is always accompanied by practical action.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The verb asah is one of the most foundational and frequently used action words in the Hebrew Bible, appearing over 2,600 times. It spans the entire spectrum of physical and ethical activity—from God's cosmic work of creation to the mundane daily labor of human hands. Unlike the Hebrew verb bara (which is used exclusively of God creating something entirely new), asah typically involves working with existing materials, shaping, fashioning, preparing, or executing a plan. It paints a picture of hands-on, industrious effort. It is the word of construction, duty, productivity, and ethical behavior. In the ancient Near East, a person's character was not evaluated by their abstract philosophies, but by what they did (asah) with their hands and life.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meaning of asah is to do, make, manufacture, or produce. Depending on the context, its semantic range includes:

• To produce or bear fruit (as a tree).

• To fashion or construct an object.

• To perform a deed, duty, or ritual.

• To show, deal, or practice a moral quality (such as kindness or justice).

• To bring about, accomplish, or execute a plan.

In ethical and relational contexts, asah describes the outward expression of an inward commitment. It turns an abstract attitude (like love or loyalty) into a visible reality.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament employs asah to contrast passive intentions with active execution:

• The Fruitful Tree: A tree that asah (yields) fruit demonstrates its inner life and health through outward productivity.

• The Obedient Servant: A servant who asah the command of his master demonstrates loyalty through compliance, not merely verbal agreement.

• The Creator's Work: God did not merely contemplate the universe; He asah (made) the heavens, the earth, and the sea, giving physical shape to His divine wisdom.

### 4. Theological Implications

Theological usage of asah emphasizes that biblical faith is thoroughly active. In Hebrew thought, hearing and doing are inseparable. To truly know God is to asah His commandments and walk in His ways. Furthermore, God's own covenant faithfulness is marked by what He has asah (done) for His people. When humans act in self-sacrificial love, they mirror the active character of Yahweh. Ruth's actions toward Naomi represent human chesed (loyal love) in action, reflecting the very heart of God's protective care for the vulnerable.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:11, Boaz says, "It has been fully reported to me, all that you have done (asah) for your mother-in-law since the death of your husband." In this specific context, asah summarizes a series of difficult, deliberate, and costly choices. Ruth did not merely feel sorry for Naomi; she acted. She left her homeland, her parents, and her security to care for an impoverished widow. She humbled herself to glean in the fields under the hot sun. Boaz recognizes that Ruth's character is defined by this doing. Her faith in the God of Israel was validated by her daily, practical labor of love.

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a beautiful parallel between Ruth's active labor (asah) and the work of the virtuous woman (eshet chayil) in Proverbs 31, who "willingly works (asah) with her hands" (Proverbs 31:13). In Ruth 3:11, Boaz explicitly calls Ruth a "virtuous woman" using this exact Hebrew phrase. Ruth’s doing in the fields of Bethlehem was the primary evidence of her noble character, proving that righteousness is best seen in quiet, faithful service.
