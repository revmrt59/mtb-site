LAND - ( 'eres , Strong's H0776 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'eres is a foundational Old Testament term referring to the earth, a specific country, territory, or ground. In the context of Ruth 2:11, it designates Ruth's native homeland of Moab, highlighting her profound sacrifice in leaving her geographic, cultural, and spiritual origins to align herself with the God and covenant people of Israel.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near East, a person's identity, security, and spiritual standing were deeply tethered to their 'eres (native land). Land was not merely real estate; it was an inheritance, a place of ancestral burial, and the domain of a nation's deity. To leave one’s native land was to strip oneself of legal protection, economic stability, and familial support, essentially choosing the vulnerable status of a resident alien. In this historical landscape, Ruth’s departure from her 'eres is a radical act of self-renunciation and faith.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun 'eres occurs over 2,500 times in the Old Testament, making it one of the most common words in the Hebrew Bible. It possesses three primary levels of meaning:

• The entire earth: The physical globe or world in distinction from the heavens (Genesis 1:1).

• A specific country or territory: A defined geographical region inhabited by a particular people group (Genesis 12:1).

• The physical ground: The soil, dust, or surface of the earth upon which humans walk or fall prostrate (Genesis 18:2).

In Ruth 2:11, the word carries the second meaning. Modified by the Hebrew term moledet ("nativity" or "birth"), 'eres denotes Ruth's homeland, the territory of Moab.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout Scripture, 'eres is often contrasted with shamayim (the heavens) to show the vast, sovereign rule of Yahweh over both realms. When used of specific territories, the word often symbolizes spiritual belonging:

• The Land of Egypt: Symbolizes captivity, bondage, and the old life of slavery.

• The Promised Land (Canaan): Symbolizes covenant rest, inheritance, and the place of God's immediate presence.

• The Foreign Land (Moab/Babylon): Symbolizes exile, spiritual famine, and separation from the sanctuary of God.

In the book of Ruth, the word functions as a geographical barometer of spiritual condition. The narrative begins with a famine in the 'eres of Israel, prompting Elimelech to seek refuge in the 'eres of Moab—a tragic move that resulted in death and loss. The restoration begins when Naomi hears that Yahweh has visited His people by giving them bread in their 'eres.

### 4. Theological Implications

Ruth's departure from her 'eres is a powerful theological picture of true conversion. Her actions directly parallel the call of Abraham in Genesis 12:1. Like the patriarch of faith, Ruth receives no guarantee of material success, yet she abandons her land, her family, and her gods to seek refuge under the wings of the God of Israel. Her abandonment of her 'eres demonstrates that covenant loyalty (hesed) is not bound by blood or geography; it is a matter of faith and spiritual devotion.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:11, Boaz acknowledges Ruth’s virtue, stating, "It has been fully reported to me, all that you have done for your mother-in-law since the death of your husband, and how you have left your father and your mother and the land of your nativity, and have come to a people whom you did not know before."

The triad of what Ruth left behind—father, mother, and land—represents the complete loss of her past. In ancient patriarchal societies, these three elements provided a woman's sole means of survival. By leaving her 'eres, Ruth walked away from her legal rights, her safety net, and her ancestral inheritance. The text emphasizes that she did this voluntarily out of love for Naomi and faith in Yahweh.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 12:1 - "Now the LORD had said to Abram: 'Get out of your country ('eres), From your family And from your father’s house, To a land ('eres) that I will show you.'"

• Genesis 24:4 - "...but you shall go to my country ('eres) and to my family, and take a wife for my son Isaac."

• Ruth 1:1 - "Now it came to pass, in the days when the judges ruled, that there was a famine in the land ('eres). And a certain man of Bethlehem, Judah, went to dwell in the country ('eres) of Moab, he and his wife and his two sons."

• Psalm 37:3 - "Trust in the LORD, and do good; Dwell in the land ('eres), and feed on His faithfulness."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

The ultimate resolution of the book of Ruth involves the redemption of land (sadeh and 'eres). Under Mosaic law, the land was a permanent inheritance that could not be permanently sold outside the family. When Boaz redeems the estate of Elimelech, he secures both the physical land of the family and a future for Ruth. Thus, the foreigner who abandoned her native 'eres is rewarded with a permanent inheritance and a place of honor in the genealogy of Jesus Christ, the ultimate Redeemer.
