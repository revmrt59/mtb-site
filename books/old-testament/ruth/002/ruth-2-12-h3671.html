WINGS - ( kanaph, Strong's H3671 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word kanaph refers literally to the wing of a bird, but it carries a rich double meaning in the Old Testament, denoting both the protective wings of God and the protective corner of a man's garment. In Ruth 2:12, it represents the ultimate safety, shelter, and covenantal protection found in Yahweh. This word bridges the physical reality of seeking refuge in Israel with the spiritual reality of trusting in God’s sovereign care, a theme beautifully realized when Boaz later spreads his own "wings" (garment corner) over Ruth as an instrument of God's providence.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The physical word picture of kanaph is drawn from the natural world. It depicts a mother bird sheltering her vulnerable young beneath her wings, keeping them safe from predators, scorching heat, and driving rain. In ancient Near Eastern culture, the word also extended to the "wings" or corners of a person's outer garment. The hem or corner of a garment was highly significant; it represented a person’s identity, status, and authority. To cover someone with the corner of one’s garment was a cultural gesture of protection, ownership, and marriage.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term kanaph primarily means a wing of a bird or a winged creature. By extension, it means an extremity, edge, border, or corner, especially of a garment, bedcover, or even the earth (such as the "four corners of the earth"). In figurative theological usage, it denotes shelter, protection, care, and spiritual refuge under the divine presence.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Mother Bird: God is described as an eagle or mother bird who carries, protects, and nurtures His young under His wings (Deuteronomy 32:11).

• The Tabernacle Cherubim: The wings of the cherubim on the mercy seat of the Ark of the Covenant overshadowed the place of atonement, representing God's holy, protective presence (Exodus 25:20).

• The Garment Corners: Israelites were commanded to put tassels on the corners (kanaph) of their garments to remind them of God's commandments (Numbers 15:38).

• Divine Healing: The prophetic promise of the Messiah speaks of the "Sun of Righteousness" arising with "healing in His wings" (Malachi 4:2).

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of kanaph is rooted in the covenantal faithfulness of God. To seek refuge under Yahweh's wings is to actively trust in His character and covenant promises. It is not a passive hiding, but a decisive choice to reject false securities and find ultimate safety in the shadow of the Almighty. For a Gentile foreigner like Ruth, fleeing her homeland to seek refuge under the wings of the God of Israel demonstrates saving faith and covenant conversion.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:12, Boaz pronounces a blessing over Ruth: "The LORD repay your work, and a full reward be given you by the LORD God of Israel, under whose wings you have come for refuge." Here, Boaz recognizes Ruth’s immense faith. She left her family and gods to shelter under Yahweh.

The beauty of this word choice unfolds in Ruth 3:9. When Ruth approaches Boaz on the threshing floor, she uses the exact same word, asking him to "spread your wing [garment corner] over your maidservant." Ruth boldly asks Boaz to be the physical answer to his own prayer. By spreading his kanaph (garment corner) over her in marriage, Boaz becomes the human instrument through whom God provides His divine kanaph (protection and redemption) to Ruth.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 19:4 - "You have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I bore you on eagles’ wings and brought you to Myself."

• Ruth 3:9 - "And he said, 'Who are you?' So she answered, 'I am Ruth, your maidservant. Take your maidservant under your wing, for you are a close relative.'"

• Psalm 17:8 - "Keep me as the apple of Your eye; Hide me under the shadow of Your wings,"

• Psalm 36:7 - "How precious is Your lovingkindness, O God! Therefore the children of men put their trust under the shadow of Your wings."

• Psalm 91:4 - "He shall cover you with His feathers, And under His wings you shall take refuge; His truth shall be your shield and buckler."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the New Testament, Jesus uses this exact Old Testament imagery when He weeps over the rebellious city of Jerusalem in Matthew 23:37: "How often I wanted to gather your children together, as a hen gathers her chicks under her wings, but you were not willing!" The offer of divine protection and covenantal shelter under God's kanaph remains open, but it must be received with the same faith demonstrated by Ruth.
