WORK - ( po'al , Strong's H6467 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun po'al refers to an action, deed, or the product of one's labor, often carrying the theological weight of a moral act that invites divine recompense. In Ruth 2:12, it captures Ruth's sacrificial loyalty and diligent labor in the fields of Bethlehem, showcasing how human faithfulness aligns with God's providential care.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun po'al is derived from the root verb pa'al, which means to do, make, or practice. In the ancient Near Eastern agrarian culture, work was not merely an economic transaction or a daily routine; it was the outward, tangible expression of a person's inner character and covenantal loyalty. To look at a person’s po'al was to look at the fruit of their life's choices. The word carries a dual sense of both the physical effort expended in labor and the moral quality of the deeds performed.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Po'al denotes an act, deed, work, or achievement. It is used in several specific ways across the Old Testament:

• The daily labor, occupation, or business of human beings.

• The specific moral acts or conduct of individuals, whether righteous or wicked.

• The wages, reward, or acquisition resulting from work (functioning as a metonymy where the work stands for the payment of the work).

• The majestic, creative, and redemptive deeds of God in history.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The word occurs 37 times in the Old Testament, clustering heavily in the poetic and wisdom literature of Job, Psalms, Proverbs, and Isaiah. It paints a vivid picture of life as a field of moral sowing and reaping. Every human action is a po'al that leaves an indelible mark. When applied to God, po'al describes His sovereign, flawless acts of creation and judgment, which stand in sharp contrast to the fragile and often corrupt works of humanity. When applied to humans, it emphasizes accountability; a person's work is a physical testimony that speaks before God.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, po'al connects human agency to divine justice. God is not a passive observer of human history; He evaluates the po'al of every individual. This term reinforces the biblical principle that faith is never abstract—it is always embodied in concrete deeds. Furthermore, God is depicted as the ultimate employer who notices the labor of His servants and promises to render a fair and abundant return.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:12, Boaz pronounces a blessing over Ruth: "The LORD repay your work..." Here, po'al refers to Ruth’s extraordinary devotion to Naomi, her willingness to leave her homeland and pagan gods, and her grueling labor in the barley fields. Boaz recognizes that her daily physical labor of gleaning is fueled by a deeper, covenantal faithfulness (chesed). By pairing po'al with the concept of a "full reward" (maskoret), the passage uses a beautiful commercial/labor metaphor. Boaz prays that God will act as the righteous master who pays Ruth the full spiritual and physical wages her sacrificial love deserves. Remarkably, Boaz himself becomes the primary instrument God uses to answer this prayer.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Job 34:11: "For He repays a man according to his work, And makes him to find it according to his way."

• Psalm 104:23: "Man goes out to his work And to his labor until the evening."

• Isaiah 5:12: "The harp and the strings, The tambourine and flute, And wine are in their feasts; But they do not regard the work of the LORD, Nor consider the operation of His hands."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint or LXX), po'al in Ruth 2:12 is rendered using ergon (work or deed). This creates a direct thematic link to the New Testament understanding of good works. Just as Ruth's "work" was an expression of her faith in the God of Israel, New Testament believers are called to walk in "good works, which God prepared beforehand" (Ephesians 2:10), knowing that their labor in the Lord is never in vain.
