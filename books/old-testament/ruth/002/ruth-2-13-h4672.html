FIND - ( matsa , Strong's H4672 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb matsa primarily means to find, reach, secure, or acquire. In the context of Ruth 2:13, it is used in the deeply significant covenantal idiom "to find favor" (matsa chen), which means to receive unmerited grace, acceptance, and protective goodwill from a superior. Rather than a passive stumbling upon something lost, Ruth's use of matsa expresses a humble plea to obtain ongoing, active kindness and security under the wings of Boaz, foreshadowing the believer's pursuit of divine grace.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient Semitic culture, the concrete concept of matsa involves physical movement and discovery. It depicts a traveler coming upon an oasis, a shepherd locating a lost sheep, or a warrior securing necessary supplies. The word denotes the transition from a state of lack, searching, or vulnerability to a state of security, possession, and relief. When applied to human relationships, particularly between a superior and a subordinate, "finding" something shifts from locating an object to securing a favorable disposition or receiving protective grace. It implies that the one seeking has met with the generous heart of the one who has the power to bless.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb matsa occurs over 450 times in the Old Testament. In the Qal stem, its basic definitions include:

• To find what was lost or sought.

• To meet, encounter, or reach a person or place.

• To obtain, acquire, or secure a possession or state of being.

• To find or experience a spiritual or emotional reality (such as peace or rest).

When paired with the Hebrew noun chen (favor, grace), matsa functions as a legal and relational idiom. To "find favor" in the eyes of someone means to secure their acceptance, to be looked upon with kindness rather than hostility, and to be granted unmerited protection and provision.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament employs matsa in several distinct word pictures:

• The Joy of Discovery: Finding something of immense value that was lost or hidden, highlighting a shift from anxiety to relief.

• The Sufficiency of Provision: Finding enough resources to meet a pressing, life-threatening need, such as water in the wilderness.

• The Asylum of Grace: Coming under the custody and care of a powerful patron. When a vulnerable person "finds" favor, they transition from an exposed, defenseless status to a position of safety and belonging.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, matsa underscores the nature of grace. In the Old Testament, finding favor (matsa chen) is never presented as earned wages; it is always a gift granted by a sovereign superior.

• It highlights human helplessness: The one seeking to "find" favor has no bargaining chips. They are entirely dependent on the character of the one who looks upon them.

• It illustrates covenant security: When God allows His people to "find" grace, it establishes a bond of protection and fellowship.

• It foreshadows Christ: Ruth’s discovery of favor in the eyes of Boaz, her kinsman-redeemer, serves as a beautiful type of the sinner finding redeeming grace in Jesus Christ, who meets us in our spiritual poverty and brings us into His household.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:13, Ruth has already been granted permission to glean in the fields and has received protective instructions from Boaz. Yet, she responds with, "Let me find favor in your sight." Her use of matsa in the imperfect tense expresses a desire for an ongoing, continuous state of acceptance and grace.

Ruth recognizes her status as a destitute Moabite widow—a foreigner with no legal claim to Boaz’s charity. By asking to "find favor," she is not demanding rights; she is humbly appealing to his mercy. The nuance here is one of deep gratitude and voluntary submission. She finds comfort because Boaz’s words have spoken to her heart, and she desires to remain in this state of secure benevolence, even though she is "not like one of" his handmaidens.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 6:8 - "But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD."

• Genesis 18:3 - "and said, 'My Lord, if I have now found favor in Your sight, do not pass on by Your servant.'"

• Ruth 2:10 - "So she fell on her face, bowed down to the ground, and said to him, 'Why have I found favor in your eyes, that you should take notice of me, since I am a foreigner?'"

• Proverbs 18:22 - "He who finds a wife finds a good thing, and obtains favor from the LORD." (Note: Both "finds" and "obtains" translate matsa).

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint or LXX), matsa is translated using the verb heurisko, which means to find, observe, or obtain. This same Greek verb is used in the New Testament to describe finding mercy and grace in times of need (Hebrews 4:16). Thus, the Old Testament picture of Ruth "finding" favor in the fields of Boaz directly connects to the New Testament reality of believers boldly approaching the throne of grace to "find" mercy in our hours of deepest need.
