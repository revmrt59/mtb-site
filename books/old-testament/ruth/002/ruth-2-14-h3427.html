SAT - ( yashab , Strong's H3427 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yashab fundamentally means to sit, dwell, remain, or abide. In the context of Ruth 2:14, where Ruth "sat beside the reapers," the word goes beyond the mere physical posture of sitting. It signifies a profound shift in her social and relational status. As a foreign widow and destitute gleaner, Ruth is invited by Boaz into the intimate circle of his workers. Sitting with them indicates welcome, safety, provision, and emerging community, foreshadowing her eventual full integration into the covenant family of Israel.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root yashab carries the primary concrete idea of sitting down, which naturally extends to settling down, dwelling, inhabiting, or remaining in a place.

• In ancient Near Eastern culture, sitting together at a meal was an act of covenant, peace, and mutual acceptance. To sit with someone's household or workers was to be granted access to their protection and hospitality.

• For a Moabitess like Ruth, who initially identified herself as a foreigner, being invited to sit beside the reapers represents an extraordinary transition from the margins of society to the table of fellowship.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The verb yashab is highly frequent in the Hebrew Scriptures, occurring over 1,000 times.

• The core semantic range includes:

• To sit down, seat oneself (on a seat, throne, or the ground).

• To dwell, live, or inhabit a place (describing a permanent or semi-permanent residence).

• To remain, stay, or tarry in a location or state.

• To sit enthroned (often used of kings or of God sitting enthroned in heaven).

• In Ruth 2:14, the verb appears in the Qal stem, expressing the simple, active movement of Ruth seating herself.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Dwelling in Safety: The word is frequently used to describe Israel dwelling safely in the Promised Land. This conveys rest, stability, and divine protection.

• Sitting in Judgment or Authority: Kings, judges, and elders sit in the gate or on thrones to execute justice and govern.

• Abiding in the Divine Presence: Dwelling in the house of the Lord emphasizes continuous communion and fellowship with Yahweh.

4. Theological Implications

• Grace and Inclusion: Ruth's action of sitting beside the reapers reflects the operational power of lovingkindness (hesed). Boaz, acting as a type of Christ (the Kinsman-Redeemer), brings the outsider near. Ruth does not sit in a corner as a beggar; she sits directly beside those who belong to the household.

• The Messianic Table: This physical act of sitting and eating points forward to the ultimate inclusion of the Gentiles at the Messianic banquet. It illustrates how divine grace overcomes national, social, and gender barriers to bring the marginalized into covenant fellowship.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 2:14, the transition is sharp. Ruth was gleaning in the fields under the hot sun, keeping her distance as a vulnerable foreigner.

• Boaz's invitation ("Come here... eat... dip your piece of bread") culminates in Ruth sitting beside the reapers. This positional change denotes equality in terms of fellowship at that moment. She is treated not as a slave or an outcast, but as an honored guest.

• Furthermore, sitting beside the reapers places her under the direct oversight and protection of Boaz's young men, whom Boaz had strictly charged not to touch her. Her posture of sitting signifies safety and rest in the midst of her labor.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 1:1: "Blessed is the man / Who walks not in the counsel of the ungodly, / Nor stands in the path of sinners, / Nor sits in the seat of the scornful;"

• Psalm 23:6: "Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me / All the days of my life; / And I will dwell in the house of the LORD / Forever."

• Psalm 91:1: "He who dwells in the secret place of the Most High / Shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty."

• 1 Kings 2:12: "Then Solomon sat on the throne of his father David; and his kingdom was firmly established."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The structural flow of Ruth 2:14 moves from invitation ("Come here"), to sitting ("she sat"), to provision ("he passed parched grain to her"), to satisfaction ("she ate and was satisfied"), and finally to stewardship ("and kept some back"). The act of sitting is the critical transition point that enables her satisfaction and abundance.

• The Greek Septuagint (LXX) translates yashab in Ruth 2:14 with the verb kathizo ("to sit down" or "seat"), which is the same verb used in the New Testament when Jesus invites the multitudes to sit on the grass before multiplying the bread, representing miraculous provision and divine hospitality.
