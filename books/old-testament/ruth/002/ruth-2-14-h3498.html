KEPT BACK - ( yathar , Strong's H3498 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yathar fundamentally means to let remain, leave behind, have a surplus, or preserve a remainder. In Ruth 2:14, translated in the NKJV as "kept some back," the word describes Ruth saving the leftovers of her abundant meal provided by Boaz. Far from a minor detail, this surplus points to the overwhelming grace of God through Boaz, who provided far more than Ruth could consume. This saved portion becomes a tangible link of provision and hope for Naomi, demonstrating Ruth's immediate, selfless care for her mother-in-law even in the midst of her own unexpected blessing.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root yathar evokes the image of a vessel overflowing or a scale tipping under the weight of abundance. It is not merely about having enough, but about having a remainder—something left over after everyone has been fully satisfied. In the ancient Near East, meals were subsistence-level events for the poor, and leftovers were a rare luxury. To have yathar (a surplus) was a concrete sign of immense favor, prosperity, and divine blessing. In a pastoral context, it represents the transition from scraping by to experiencing abundance.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb yathar occurs primarily in the Niphal (to be left over, remain) and Hiphil (to leave over, reserve, have a surplus) stems. In Ruth 2:14, it appears in the Hiphil form (vattotar), meaning to cause to remain, leave over, or reserve. The semantic range of yathar spans:

• To have more than enough, leaving a surplus.

• To spare or preserve life (leaving a remnant).

• To excel or show preeminence (having an excess of quality or stature).

In the context of food, it means to leave uneaten portions after one's appetite is fully satisfied.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Scripture frequently employs yathar to highlight the physical reality of divine abundance. It is used of the manna in the wilderness, where Moses commanded that none of it should be left (yathar) until morning. It also appears in the context of the miraculous multiplication of food, such as Elisha feeding the hundred men, where they ate and had some left over (yathar) according to the word of the Lord. The word picture is consistently one of grace that exceeds human capacity to consume.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, yathar in the Ruth narrative is an objective sign of grace (chen). Boaz does not merely satisfy Ruth's hunger; he overwhelms it. This mirrors the character of God, who is able to do exceedingly abundantly above all that we ask or think. The presence of leftovers (yathar) is proof that God's provision is never stingy or barely sufficient. Furthermore, the covenant theme of the remnant is embedded in this root; God always preserves a remainder (yathar) for the continuation of His promises. Ruth’s leftovers physically sustain the very line through which the Messiah would come.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:14, Ruth sits with the reapers at Boaz’s invitation. He serves her parched grain. The text notes she ate, was satisfied, and "kept some back" (yathar). The sequence is vital: satisfaction precedes the surplus. Ruth did not starve herself to save food for Naomi; she ate until she was fully content, and still there was an abundance left over. This shows the lavishness of Boaz's heart. Ruth's immediate instinct to keep the surplus back for Naomi reveals her loyal-love (chesed). She does not forget her needy mother-in-law in her moment of personal feasting.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 12:10: "You shall let none of it remain (yathar) until morning, and what remains (yathar) of it until morning you shall burn with fire."

• Exodus 16:19: "And Moses said, 'Let no one leave (yathar) any of it till morning.'"

• 2 Kings 4:44: "So he set it before them; and they ate and left (yathar) some of it, according to the word of the Lord."

• 2 Chronicles 31:10: "Since the people began to bring the offerings into the house of the Lord, we have had enough to eat and have plenty left (yathar), for the Lord has blessed His people."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The action of keeping back the food foreshadows the ultimate redemption of Naomi's empty life. Naomi had declared she went out full and returned empty (Ruth 1:21). The leftover parched grain that Ruth brings home at the end of the day (Ruth 2:18) is the first physical token of "fullness" returning to Naomi’s household. It is a prophetic sign that God is beginning to reverse their famine and emptiness through the grace of a redeemer.
