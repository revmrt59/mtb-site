PASSED - ( tsabat , Strong's H6642 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb tsabat is a rare action word occurring only once in the Old Testament, denoting the act of reaching out, holding out, or passing food to someone. In the context of the harvest field, it portrays Boaz personally handing a portion of parched grain to Ruth. This gesture goes far beyond the standard customs of legal charity, serving as a powerful demonstration of covenant kindness, personal dignity, and unexpected grace extended to a foreign widow.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• Culturally, sharing a meal in the ancient Near East was a significant act of acceptance, protection, and covenant fellowship. Ruth, as a destitute Moabitess and a foreigner, expected to glean only on the dangerous fringes of the field.

• Boaz defies social conventions by inviting Ruth to sit among his own reapers at mealtime. The act of reaching out to hand her food represents a high level of personal hospitality and care, signaling that she is no longer viewed as an outcast but as an honored guest.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The verb tsabat means to reach out, extend, hold out, or pass something to another person.

• The root carries the physical idea of grasping, pinching, or holding a portion of food. In related Semitic dialects, it describes pinching off a piece or holding an item securely to hand it over.

• As a hapax legomenon, its definition is clarified by both its immediate context and its cognate usage, all pointing to the deliberate act of reaching out one's hand to offer a specific portion of food.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The word paints a vivid picture of personal connection. Boaz does not command a servant to feed Ruth, nor does he simply point her toward the food supplies. Instead, he personally reaches out his hand to give her the parched grain.

• This action conveys warmth and deliberate attention. The physical gesture of reaching out or passing the food bridges the social chasm between a wealthy Judean landowner and a marginalized Moabite widow.

• The portion passed to Ruth was generous. The text records that she ate, was satisfied, and had plenty left over, showing that Boaz's outstretched hand held an abundant, overflowing supply.

4. Theological Implications

• Sovereign Grace: The word illustrates the nature of divine grace. Ruth did not ask for the parched grain, nor could she claim any right to it. It was reached out to her solely because of the favor of the redeemer-figure, Boaz.

• Picture of the Redeemer: Boaz serves as a type of Christ, the ultimate Kinsman-Redeemer. Just as Boaz reached out to feed a needy stranger, Christ reaches out to extend spiritual provision and fellowship to those who are spiritually destitute.

• Covenant Faithfulness: This action embodies the covenant love of God. God uses the physical hands of His people to reach out and deliver His providential care to the hurting and vulnerable.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 2:14, the use of tsabat marks a significant shift in Ruth’s status. She went from seeking a safe place to glean to sitting in the place of privilege next to the harvesters.

• The direct action of Boaz passing the grain indicates his personal oversight of her well-being. It establishes an atmosphere of safety and honor that would characterize her entire experience in his fields.

• The fact that she kept some back from this portion shows that the reaching hand of Boaz provided enough to sustain not only Ruth but also Naomi, demonstrating how God's provision overflows to bless others.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Because tsabat is a hapax legomenon, there are no other Old Testament passages that contain this specific Hebrew word. Its sole occurrence is:

• Ruth 2:14 – "Now Boaz said to her at mealtime, 'Come here, and eat of the bread, and dip your piece of bread in the vinegar.' So she sat beside the reapers, and he passed her parched grain; and she ate and was satisfied, and kept some back."

• In the Septuagint, the translators used the Greek verb bounizo, meaning to heap up, emphasizing the generous and abundant portion that Boaz piled up and handed to Ruth.

7. Other Relevant Insights

• Parched grain (qali) was a staple food of the ancient Near East, prepared by roasting fresh ears of green wheat or barley on an iron plate, making it flavorful, portable, and highly nutritious for field workers.

• The gesture of handing this specific food to Ruth was an act of immediate refreshment, showing that Boaz recognized her physical exhaustion and sought to restore her strength directly.

• The Hebrew term highlights the quiet, unpretentious way that genuine biblical love manifests—not through grand declarations, but through the simple, physical act of reaching out a hand to help.
