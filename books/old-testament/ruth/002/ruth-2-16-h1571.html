ALSO - ( gam , Strong's H1571 )

Summary:

The Hebrew particle gam is an adverbial conjunction of addition, emphasis, and escalation, meaning "also," "indeed," "even," or "moreover." In Ruth 2:16, it serves as the grammatical turning point where Boaz’s compassion shifts from passive permission to active, lavish provision. By instructing his workers to "also" drop handfuls of grain purposely for Ruth, Boaz demonstrates the heart of covenant lovingkindness (chesed), showing that true godly charity does not merely meet the bare minimum of legal requirements but rejoices in overflowing, intentional generosity.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the agricultural economy of ancient Israel, the Torah protected the poor, the stranger, the widow, and the fatherless through the law of gleaning. Landowners were commanded not to reap the very corners of their fields or gather the gleanings of their harvest, leaving them for the marginalized. Under this law, Ruth had the legal right to walk behind the reapers and gather what fell naturally. However, Boaz goes far beyond the letter of the law. The background of the particle gam is rooted in the concept of addition and piling on. It acts as a linguistic building block, stacking one layer of kindness upon another. In the cultural setting of Ruth 2, gam functions to transition Ruth from a mere recipient of legal charity to a guest of honor who receives direct, intentional abundance.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Gam is a monosyllabic particle functioning primarily as an adverb or conjunction. Its semantic range covers addition ("also," "too," "as well"), intensification ("even," "indeed," "yea"), and concession ("although," "though"). When prefixed with the conjunction ve ("and"), the resulting form ve-gam translates as "and also" or "and even." The word does not merely link two equal items; it frequently introduces an element that is surprising, unexpected, or of greater significance than the preceding one. It carries an inherent force of inclusion, signifying that the boundary of what was previously discussed has been expanded to encompass something more.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The usage of gam throughout the Hebrew Scriptures reflects several distinct grammatical and rhetorical patterns:

• Cumulative Addition: It links multiple subjects or objects together in a shared experience, indicating that what applies to one applies equally to another.

• Climactic Escalation: It introduces a shocking or highly significant addition, often translated as "even." This usage draws the reader's attention to an extreme or unexpected case that exceeds the standard expectation.

• The "Heap" Word Picture: Conceptually, gam evokes the image of a heap or an overflowing measure. If a standard measure is represented by a container filled to the brim, gam represents the extra portion that is piled on top until it runs over the sides. It is the language of abundance and extension.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, gam illustrates the expansive and overflowing nature of divine grace (chesed). In the Old Testament, God's relationship with His people is marked not by sterile transactions or minimum requirements, but by lavish additions. When God covenants with His people, He does not merely deliver them from bondage; He also blesses them with inheritance, status, and direct access to His presence. Boaz, acting as a human agent of God’s protective wings, models this divine attribute. By employing gam, he demonstrates that godliness is not content with fulfilling bare legal duties. True righteousness mimics God’s heart by adding grace upon grace, transforming a survival-level existence into a life of dignity and abundance.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In the immediate context of Ruth 2:15–16, Ruth has just received permission from Boaz to glean "even among the sheaves." This in itself was a massive concession, as gleaners were typically restricted to the edges of the field and were kept away from the bound sheaves to prevent theft. Having granted this extraordinary permission, Boaz turns to his young men in verse 16 and commands them, "Also (gam) let some grain from the bundles fall purposely for her." The nuance of gam here is highly tactical and deeply compassionate. It shifts the entire dynamic of the field. Ruth is no longer merely tolerated as she searches for scraps; she is actively provided for through deliberate acts of self-limitation by the reapers. Boaz’s use of gam turns a passive allowance into an active, hidden ministry of grace, ensuring that her labor is fruitful without bruising her dignity.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 3:6: "So when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree desirable to make one wise, she took of its fruit and ate. She also gave to her husband with her, and he ate." Here, gam functions to show the tragic inclusion of Adam in the first transgression, emphasizing that the sin was not isolated to Eve but was shared immediately by her husband.

• Ruth 2:21: "Ruth the Moabitess said, 'He also said to me, "You shall keep close by my young men until they have finished all my harvest."'" In this verse, Ruth recounts Boaz’s words to Naomi, using gam to highlight the compounding nature of his hospitality, which went beyond physical food to long-term protection and security.

• 1 Samuel 19:24: "And he also stripped off his clothes and prophesied before Samuel in like manner, and lay down naked all that day and all that night. Therefore they say, 'Is Saul also among the prophets?'" This double use of gam conveys extreme surprise and climactic irony, showing that even the rebellious King Saul was overtaken by the Spirit of God.

7. Other Relevant Insights

Syntactically, the connection of gam to the verb shal-talal (to pull out or draw out) in Ruth 2:16 highlights the secret, quiet nature of true biblical charity. Boaz instructs his reapers to let the grain fall "purposely" while also (gam) ensuring she does not feel ashamed. The placement of gam at the beginning of this instruction serves as an authoritative mandate. It binds the reapers to a higher law than the standard code of harvest. By inserting gam, Boaz establishes a precedent: the redemptive community must always look for ways to add to the welfare of the vulnerable, going the second mile to ensure that grace is both practical and preserving of human dignity.
