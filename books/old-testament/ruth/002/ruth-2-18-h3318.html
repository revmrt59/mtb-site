OUT - ( yatsa , Strong's H3318 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yatsa fundamentally refers to the act of going out, emerging, or coming forth. In its causative (Hiphil) form used in Ruth 2:18, it means "to bring out," "to produce," or "to present." In this narrative context, the word describes Ruth bringing out and giving to Naomi the leftover food she had carefully set aside from her meal with Boaz's reapers. This physical action serves as a beautiful, practical demonstration of Ruth's covenant love (chesed) and selflessness, proving that she was actively mindful of her mother-in-law's hunger even while she herself was being favored in the field.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root yatsa is one of the most common verbs in the Hebrew Bible, appearing over one thousand times. Its primary spatial dynamic is movement from an interior domain to an exterior domain—crossing a threshold or boundary. It is used to describe the sun rising (going forth) in the morning, water flowing out of a spring, a plant sprouting from the ground, or a person leaving a house or city. In its causative Hiphil form (vattotse in Ruth 2:18), the word picture shifts from simple exit to active extraction or presentation. It is the act of bringing something hidden, stored, or kept safe out into the open to make it available to another.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

In the basic Qal stem, yatsa means to go out, go forth, depart, or proceed. In the Hiphil stem, which is causative, the semantic range includes:

• To cause to go out, to lead out, or to conduct out.

• To bring forth, produce, or extract (such as bringing water from a rock or silver from a furnace).

• To present, deliver, or hand over an object from one's possession to another.

In Ruth 2:18, the Hiphil imperfect form expresses a completed, deliberate action: Ruth brought out from her personal keeping the parched grain she had saved, transferring it directly into Naomi’s hands.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The verb yatsa carries rich associations throughout the Old Testament:

• The Exodus: The Hiphil form of yatsa is the definitive theological term for God "bringing Israel out" of Egypt. It portrays a powerful rescue from confinement into a spacious place of freedom.

• Creation and Provision: God causes the earth to "bring forth" vegetation and food. This paints a picture of God opening His hand to provide sustenance out of the hidden riches of the soil.

• Revelatory Actions: Bringing something "out" from a private bag, tent, or vessel is a gesture of disclosure and sharing. It moves an item from the realm of private ownership to the realm of shared relationship.

4. Theological Implications

The use of yatsa in Ruth 2:18 links human action with divine character. Throughout the Old Testament, God is the ultimate agent who "brings out" food from the earth to satisfy the hungry and "brings out" the oppressed from their misery. When Ruth "brings out" the preserved food for Naomi, she acts as the human instrument of God's providential care. Her action demonstrates that true covenant faithfulness (chesed) is not passive; it is an active, tangible expression of love that "brings forth" resources to meet the needs of the vulnerable. Ruth becomes the answer to her own prayers and Naomi's silent grief by bringing out physical sustenance.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

The context of Ruth 2:18 is filled with contrasts and emotional weight. Ruth returns to Naomi after a long day of labor in Boaz's field. She returns with a massive physical burden—an ephah of gleaned barley. But before Naomi can even process the sheer volume of grain, Ruth performs a secondary, intimate act of care: she "brought out and gave to her" the roasted grain she had kept back from her lunch.

Earlier in the day, Boaz had treated Ruth with unexpected grace, inviting her to eat until she was "satisfied" (Ruth 2:14). Instead of consuming everything or forgetting her destitute mother-in-law in her moment of abundance, Ruth consciously saved a portion. The verb yatsa highlights this deliberate transition. The food was hidden away in her pocket or wrap, and by bringing it out, she surprises Naomi with an immediate, prepared meal, sparing her the wait of grinding and baking the raw barley. It shows that Naomi was on Ruth's mind even when Ruth was in the presence of the wealthy Boaz.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 14:18: "Then Melchizedek king of Salem brought out bread and wine; he was the priest of God Most High."

• Exodus 13:3: "And Moses said to the people: 'Remember this day in which you went out from Egypt, out of the house of bondage; for by strength of hand the LORD brought you out from this place.'"

• Psalm 104:14: "He causes the grass to grow for the cattle, And vegetation for the service of man, That he may bring forth food from the earth,"

• Jeremiah 15:19: "Therefore thus says the LORD: 'If you return, Then I will bring you back; You shall stand before Me; If you take out the precious from the vile, You shall be as My mouth.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a beautiful literary symmetry in Ruth 2:18. The verse lists three distinct actions performed by Ruth: she took up the gleaned barley, she went into the city, and she "brought out" the saved food. The first two actions are heavy, laborious, and expected of a gleaner. The final action—bringing out the leftovers—is light, spontaneous, and motivated purely by love. This detail transforms Ruth from a mere survivor trying to keep herself and her mother-in-law alive into a generous partner in grace, reflecting the same abundant generosity she had just received from Boaz.
