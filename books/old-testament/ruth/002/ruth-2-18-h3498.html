KEPT BACK - ( yathar , Strong's H3498 )

Summary:

In Ruth 2:18, the Hebrew verb yathar (translated "kept back") refers to leaving behind, reserving, or having a surplus of provision after being completely satisfied. In this context, it highlights Ruth’s selflessness, foresight, and deep care for her mother-in-law, Naomi. Rather than consuming the abundant provision of Boaz solely for herself, Ruth intentionally set aside the leftover parched grain to nourish Naomi, transforming a physical surplus into a tangible expression of covenant loyalty (chesed).

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew root yathar denotes a surplus, an abundance that overflows beyond immediate needs. It presents the picture of a vessel filled to the brim and running over, leaving an excess. In the ancient Near East, where daily survival was a constant struggle and food security was rare, having something "left over" or "kept back" was a sign of extraordinary abundance and divine favor. The word evokes the transition from scarcity to surplus, illustrating a physical manifestation of grace that extends beyond the primary recipient to bless others.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb yathar primarily means to remain, be left over, or exceed. In its Hiphil (causative) stem used in Ruth 2:18, it means to cause to abound, leave behind, preserve, or reserve a portion. The semantic range spans from leaving a remnant in a historical or military sense to having a surplus of food after a feast. It speaks not of hoarding or holding back out of greed, but of reserving a portion of an abundant supply for later use or for the benefit of another.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Overflow of Grace: When Boaz invites Ruth to eat with his reapers, he provides so generously that she is not only fully satisfied but has a surplus.

• The Preservation of Life: In several Old Testament narratives, yathar represents the preservation of a remnant or the reserving of portions for those who are vulnerable.

• The Miracle of Divine Provision: The word is frequently associated with divine multiplication, where God's provision exceeds human capacity, leaving an abundance even after everyone has eaten.

### 4. Theological Implications

• God’s Superabundant Grace: Yathar reminds us that God does not merely provide the bare minimum. His grace is overflowing, yielding a surplus.

• The Steward of Abundance: Ruth acts as a conduit of grace. The surplus she received from Boaz was not wasted or kept for selfish indulgence; it was channeled to care for another. This illustrates the theological principle that God blesses His people so that they may be a blessing to others.

• Covenant Faithfulness (Chesed): By keeping back food for Naomi, Ruth demonstrates active chesed. True love is practical, thinking of the needs of others even when enjoying one's own refreshment.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:18, Ruth returns to the city after her first day of gleaning in Boaz's field. She presents Naomi with an immense amount of barley (about an ephah) and then brings out the food she had "kept back" (hotirah) from her midday meal. Boaz had given her parched grain, and she ate until she was satisfied (Ruth 2:14). The phrase "kept back" indicates intentional preservation. Ruth did not overeat or waste the food; she immediately thought of Naomi's hunger. This small detail underscores Ruth's character—she is a woman of noble character who honors her family obligations with joy and foresight.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Ruth 2:14: "So she ate and was satisfied, and kept some back."

• Exodus 16:18: "So when they measured it by omers, he who gathered much had nothing left over, and he who gathered little had no lack."

• 2 Kings 4:44: "So he set it before them; and they ate and had some left over, according to the word of the Lord."

• 2 Chronicles 31:10: "Since the people began to bring the offerings into the house of the Lord, we have had enough to eat and have plenty left, for the Lord has blessed His people; and what is left is this great store."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Connection to the New Testament: The concept of yathar foreshadows the feeding of the five thousand and four thousand, where the fragments "left over" (using the Greek equivalent perisseuo) were gathered up, showing the Messiah's limitless provision.

• The Shift in Naomi's Perspective: When Naomi sees the raw barley and then tastes the prepared food Ruth "kept back," her despair begins to turn to hope. The leftover food is tangible proof that there is a redeemer in Israel who cares.
