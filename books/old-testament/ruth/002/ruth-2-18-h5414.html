GAVE - ( natan , Strong's H5414 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb natan is a central covenantal word meaning to give, hand over, or permit. In the narrative of Ruth 2:18, it denotes Ruth’s sacrificial act of presenting Naomi with the food she had set aside from her own meal in the fields of Boaz. This simple physical transfer of nourishment serves as a profound expression of covenant faithfulness (chesed), showing that God's provision flows through human hands to sustain the empty and vulnerable.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete reality of the Hebrew language frequently roots abstract spiritual concepts in physical actions. The verb natan represents the basic human physical gesture of opening the hand to deliver, entrust, or yield an object to another. Historically and culturally in ancient Israel, giving was not merely transactional; it was deeply relational. An open hand symbolized safety, fellowship, and covenant responsibility, while a closed hand indicated greed, rejection, and covenant unfaithfulness. Within the agricultural life of the ancient Near East, survival depended heavily on the willingness of family members and neighbors to extend their hands to give to one another during times of famine, harvest, and hardship.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word natan occurs over 2,000 times in the Hebrew Bible, making it one of its most common verbs. Its primary semantic range encompasses:

• To give, present, or bestow a gift or portion.

• To put, set, or place an object in a specific location.

• To deliver up or hand over a possession, land, or person.

• To grant, permit, or allow a request or condition.

In the specific context of Ruth 2:18, natan carries the sense of bestowing nourishment—physically handing over prepared food that was saved from a meal.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Scripture employs natan in several significant theological word pictures:

• The Hand of God: God is the ultimate Giver who gives the land of promise, gives rain in its season, gives breath to humanity, and gives wisdom to the humble.

• Covenant Commitment: To "give" one's hand or heart implies a deep, binding oath of loyalty and protection.

• Generosity to the Poor: The righteous are described as those who open their hands and give to the needy without a grudging heart, mirroring the character of God.

In Ruth 2:18, the word picture shifts from God's cosmic giving to a domestic, intimate setting. Ruth's open hand becomes the physical manifestation of God's answer to Naomi's emptiness.

4. Theological Implications

Several profound theological truths emerge from this simple act of giving:

• Mediated Grace: God rarely drops provision directly from the sky; He typically mediates His grace through human instruments. Ruth becomes the pipeline of divine care to Naomi.

• The Principle of Leftovers: Ruth gave Naomi what she had "kept back after she was satisfied." This illustrates the biblical concept of stewardship and stewardship of abundance. God satisfies our needs so that we can give our surplus to others.

• Covenant Love (Chesed): True covenant love is not passive; it is active and physical. Ruth's love for Naomi is demonstrated not in words of sympathy, but in the tangible act of giving food.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

Ruth 2:18 marks a critical turning point in the book of Ruth. Naomi had returned to Bethlehem claiming that the Lord had dealt bitterly with her and brought her back empty (Ruth 1:21). However, when Ruth returns from her first day of gleaning in Boaz's field, she brings home about an ephah of barley and then physically gives (natan) Naomi the leftover parched grain she had saved. The act of giving here is a direct, tangible rebuttal to Naomi’s perceived emptiness. It shows Naomi that God has not abandoned them. The physical food given by Ruth is a signpost of the spiritual restoration that is beginning to unfold.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 1:29: "And God said, 'See, I have given you every herb that yields seed which is on the face of all the earth...'"

• Leviticus 26:4: "then I will give you rain in its season, the land shall yield its produce, and the trees of the field shall yield their fruit."

• Ruth 4:13: "So Boaz took Ruth and she became his wife; and when he went in to her, the LORD gave her conception, and she bore a son."

• Proverbs 3:34: "Surely He scorns the scornful, But He gives grace to the humble."

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a beautiful parallel between Boaz and Ruth in this chapter. Boaz gave (natan) Ruth permission to glean and provided her with a meal. Ruth, in turn, takes the blessing she received and gives (natan) it to Naomi. This illustrates the contagious nature of biblical generosity. When we are recipients of grace, we are called to be conduits of that same grace. Furthermore, the verb natan reappears at the climax of the book in Ruth 4:13, where the Lord gives Ruth conception, showing that the ultimate Giver in this narrative is God Himself, who restores life and lineage to a dying family.
