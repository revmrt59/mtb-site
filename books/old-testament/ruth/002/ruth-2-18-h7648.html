SATISFIED - ( soba , Strong's 7648 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word soba (along with its verbal root saba) denotes the state of being filled to satisfaction, having enough, or being completely sated. It speaks of a fullness that leaves no remaining deficit, whether referring to physical hunger, material prosperity, or spiritual contentment. In the narrative of Ruth, her being "satisfied" after eating the grain provided by Boaz is not merely a statement of physical satiety, but a powerful sign of God's abundant, overflowing grace providing for a destitute widow through a kinsman-redeemer.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The cultural background of soba is rooted in the agricultural reality of the ancient Near East, where famine was a constant threat and food security was rare. To be "satisfied" was not a mundane daily occurrence but a profound blessing from God.

• Visually, the word portrays a vessel filled to the brim, a person who has eaten until they can eat no more, or land that has received rain to its maximum capacity. It stands in direct contrast to being empty, dry, or wanting.

• In the pastoral context of Israel, the word evokes the image of sheep grazing in lush pastures until they lie down fully contented, having no further need.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Soba functions as a noun meaning satiety, fullness, or abundance, derived from the verb saba, which means to be satisfied, sated, or filled to excess.

• It defines a state where desire is completely met and no lack remains.

• The word is used both literally (to describe the physical consumption of food and drink) and metaphorically (to describe the satisfaction of the soul, a long life, or emotional fulfillment).

• It denotes not just a bare minimum of survival, but an abundance that leads to rest and security.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Eating to Fullness: The most common literal usage refers to eating until hunger is completely gone. This represents God's covenant promise to His people that if they obey Him, they will eat and be satisfied.

• The Sated Soul: Metaphorically, the word describes the satisfying of spiritual hunger and thirst. God is the only source who can truly satisfy the weary and empty soul.

• Satiety of Days: The word is frequently used to describe a long, blessed life, where a person dies contented with the life they lived under God's favor.

4. Theological Implications

• Divine Provision: True satisfaction is always presented as a gift from God. Humanity can labor, but only God can satisfy the desires of every living thing.

• Covenant Faithfulness: God's provision of satisfaction is linked to His covenant. When Israel is faithful, He satisfies them with the finest of wheat; when they rebel, they eat but are not satisfied.

• Redemption and Grace: The transition from emptiness to satisfaction is a core redemptive theme. God looks upon the needy, hears their cry, and satisfies them in times of drought.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 2:18, the use of this concept occurs as Ruth returns to her mother-in-law, Naomi. Ruth had gone out to glean as a destitute foreigner, but under Boaz’s care, she ate, was satisfied, and had leftovers.

• This physical satisfaction is a turning point in the narrative. It signals the end of the famine that drove Elimelech's family to Moab and marks the beginning of Naomi's restoration.

• The leftovers Ruth brought home to Naomi demonstrate that Boaz's grace was not minimal but abundant. It foreshadows the spiritual and physical redemption that Boaz, as the kinsman-redeemer, would ultimately bring to their entire family line.

• The contrast between Ruth’s previous empty-handed state and this overflowing satisfaction emphasizes that God’s grace always exceeds basic necessities.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 16:11: "You will show me the path of life; In Your presence is fullness of joy; At Your right hand are pleasures forevermore."

• Proverbs 13:25: "The righteous eats to the satisfying of his soul, But the stomach of the wicked shall want."

• Psalm 103:5: "Who satisfies your mouth with good things, So that your youth is renewed like the eagle's."

• Isaiah 58:11: "The Lord will guide you continually, And satisfy your soul in drought, And strengthen your bones; You shall be like a watered garden, And like a spring of water, whose waters do not fail."

• Exodus 16:12: "At twilight you shall eat meat, and in the morning you shall be filled with bread. And you shall know that I am the Lord your God."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The word is antonymous to words representing hunger, scarcity, and greed. While the wicked are never satisfied and always crave more, the righteous find satisfaction even in modest portions because of God's blessing.

• In the Septuagint, the Greek equivalent often used is chortazo (to feed, fatten, or satisfy), which is the same word Jesus used in the Beatitudes ("for they shall be filled") and in the miraculous feeding of the five thousand, where the crowds "ate and were satisfied." This links Ruth's experience directly to the abundant, satisfying grace of Christ.
