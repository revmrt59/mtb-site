WENT - ( bow' , Strong's H935 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb bow' is a primary word of movement meaning "to enter," "to come in," or "to go in." In Ruth 2:18, it depicts Ruth's physical transition from the vulnerable, open harvest fields back into the protective boundaries of the city of Bethlehem. Structurally and spiritually, this movement marks a vital turning point: Naomi's household transitions from the empty, bitter exile described in chapter one to a state of providential abundance as Ruth enters laden with the fruits of Boaz's favor.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The verb bow' is one of the most frequent action words in the Hebrew Bible, occurring more than 2,500 times.

• At its core, it represents spatial movement across a threshold—moving from an outer, exposed space to an inner, secure environment, or arriving at a designated destination.

• In the ancient Near Eastern context, entering a walled city gate (bow') meant leaving behind the wild, unregulated countryside to step into a place of covenant community, legal protection, and social order. For Ruth, a vulnerable foreign widow, "going" back into Bethlehem was an act of quiet courage and familial devotion.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The fundamental meaning of bow' is "to enter," "to come in," or "to go in."

• In the Qal stem (the simple active voice used in Ruth 2:18), the verb refers to the physical arrival of a person at a specific place, such as a tent, house, city, or land.

• It stands in semantic contrast to yatsa' ("to go out"), functioning as the natural conclusion to a journey or a period of labor.

• Depending on the narrative perspective, the verb can be translated as "to come" (moving toward the narrator/speaker) or "to go/enter" (moving toward a destination).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Covenantal and Domestic Spaces: The verb frequently describes entering a home, a sacred space, or entering into a marriage relationship (often idiomatically rendered "to go in to" a spouse).

• The Harvest and Provision: It is used to describe "bringing in" the harvest or entering the storehouse with the grain. Ruth's physical entry into the city carrying her gleaned grain serves as a living illustration of bringing home the harvest sheaves.

• Cosmic Boundaries: It is the standard Hebrew term for the setting of the sun (the sun "goes in"), marking the transition from the labor of the day to the safety and rest of the evening.

4. Theological Implications

• The Reversal of Emptiness: In Ruth 1:21, Naomi lamented, "I went out (yalak) full, and the LORD has brought me home empty." In Ruth 2:18, when Ruth "went" (bow') into the city, she did so heavily laden with an ephah of barley. This physical arrival represents the literal reversal of Naomi's emptiness, signaling that God has begun to "visit" His people with bread.

• Providential Arrival: Throughout the Old Testament, safe arrival (bow') is associated with divine preservation. Ruth's safe return to the city highlights God's protective wings hovering over the foreign widow who trusted in Him.

• The Gathering of the Gentile: Ruth’s entry into the covenant community of Bethlehem prefigures the prophetic promise of the nations "coming" to the light of Israel to find redemption and rest.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 2:18, the verb occurs in the Qal imperfect form (wattabo'), describing her immediate action after beating out the grain she had gleaned in the field of Boaz.

• The physical weight of what she carried (nearly thirty pounds of barley) made her entry conspicuous. She did not slip back into the city unnoticed; her arrival was a visible testament to unexpected grace.

• Her entrance into the city initiates the pivotal dialogue with Naomi. The physical transition from the field to the home shifts the narrative from Ruth's solitary labor to the mutual restoration of the broken family.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 7:1 - "Then the LORD said to Noah, 'Come into the ark, you and all your household, because I have seen that you are righteous before Me in this generation.'"

• Ruth 1:22 - "So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabitess her daughter-in-law with her, who returned from the country of Moab. Now they came to Bethlehem at the beginning of barley harvest."

• Psalm 100:2 - "Serve the LORD with gladness; Come before His presence with singing."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• There is a beautiful narrative symmetry in the book of Ruth built around this verb. In chapter one, Naomi and Ruth "came" (bow') to Bethlehem in grief and obscurity. In chapter two, Ruth "comes" (bow') carrying the tangible proof of Boaz's redeeming grace. This progression mirrors the spiritual reality of the believer's walk: we may begin our journey coming to God in brokenness, but His grace ensures we return rejoicing, carrying the fruit of His provision.
