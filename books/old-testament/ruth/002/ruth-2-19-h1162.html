BOAZ - ( Bo'az , Strong's H1162 )

Summary:

Boaz is a Hebrew proper name meaning "in him is strength" or "quickness." Primarily referring to the wealthy and righteous Bethlehemite who marries Ruth, Boaz serves as the Old Testament archetype of the kinsman-redeemer (goel). Through his covenant faithfulness (chesed), social integrity, and voluntary redemption of Naomi and Ruth, he restores a destitute family and preserves the lineage of King David and Jesus Christ. The name also designates one of the two massive bronze pillars at the entrance of Solomon’s temple, symbolizing God's sustaining might.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The name Boaz is composed of two Hebrew elements: the preposition be (meaning "in") and the noun oz (meaning "strength," "might," or "power"). Together, the name translates to "in him [or it] is strength." In the cultural landscape of ancient Israel, names carried prophetic weight and reflected a person's character or destiny. Boaz lived during the dark and chaotic era of the Judges, a period marked by spiritual apostasy, moral decay, and social instability. Against this bleak backdrop, Boaz emerges as a living embodiment of his name—a pillar of spiritual, moral, and financial strength in the community of Bethlehem.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Bo'az is a masculine proper noun occurring 24 times in the Hebrew Bible. Lexical analysis confirms its derivation from the root associated with strength and protection. In the context of the book of Ruth, the name signifies a person of standing, capability, and resources. When applied to the temple pillar in 1 Kings, the name functions as a noun declaration of Yahweh’s sovereign strength. The semantic range of the name's root elements emphasizes structural stability, protective power, and the capacity to prevail over adversity.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Pillar of the Community: In the narrative of Ruth, Boaz is described as a gibbor chayil, a phrase often translated as "a man of great wealth" or "a man of standing." This denotes not just material riches, but a man of valor, noble character, and influence. He is a pillar of strength for the vulnerable, using his power to protect and provide rather than exploit.

• The Temple Pillar: Solomon named the left (northern) bronze pillar at the entrance of the temple vestibule "Boaz" (1 Kings 7:21). Positioned alongside "Jachin" ("He shall establish"), the two pillars formed a word picture for every worshiper entering the temple: "He will establish, and in Him is strength." It was a visual reminder that Israel's security rested entirely on the strength of Yahweh.

• The Shadow of the Wings: Boaz instructs Ruth to seek refuge under the wings of Yahweh (Ruth 2:12). Later, when Ruth asks Boaz to spread his "wings" (the hem of his garment) over her (Ruth 3:9), Boaz becomes the physical instrument of God's protective strength.

4. Theological Implications

• Covenant Faithfulness (Chesed): Boaz demonstrates that strength is truly realized when it is submitted to God's covenant law. While the book of Judges shows the destructive nature of unchecked self-interest, Boaz demonstrates how biblical strength serves the weak, the widow, and the foreigner.

• The Kinsman-Redeemer (Goel): Boaz provides the clearest Old Testament type of Jesus Christ as the Redeemer. To qualify as a goel, a person had to be a near kinsman, possess the financial means to redeem the lost inheritance, and be willing to do so. Boaz met all three criteria, willingly absorbing the financial risk and social stigma of marrying a Moabitess to redeem Elimelech's family.

• Grace to the Gentiles: Boaz's redemption of Ruth, a Moabitess, foreshadows the inclusive scope of the Gospel. His strength breaks down ethnic barriers, bringing a Gentile outsider into the covenant community and the direct lineage of the Messiah.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:19, Naomi asks Ruth where she worked, amazed by the massive quantity of barley (about an ephah) Ruth brought home. When Ruth utters the name "Boaz," it functions as a dramatic pivot point in the narrative. To Naomi, the name is not just an identity; it is an immediate answer to prayer and a sign of God's providence. The mention of "Boaz" instantly signals to Naomi that God has not abandoned them. The "strength" of this man's position and wealth means that salvation, food, and family restoration are legally and practically possible.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Ruth 2:1 - "There was a relative of Naomi’s husband, a man of great wealth, of the family of Elimelech. His name was Boaz."

• Ruth 4:9 - "And Boaz said to the elders and all the people, 'You are witnesses this day that I have bought all that was Elimelech’s, and all that was Chilion’s and Mahlon’s, from the hand of Naomi.'"

• Ruth 4:21 - "Salmon begot Boaz, and Boaz begot Obed;"

• 1 Kings 7:21 - "Then he set up the pillars by the vestibule of the temple; he set up the pillar on the right and called its name Jachin, and he set up the pillar on the left and called its name Boaz."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• Contrast with the "Closer Relative": In Ruth 4, a closer redeemer is introduced who has the financial capability but is unwilling to redeem because it might jeopardize his own inheritance. This anonymous relative represents a strength that is self-protective and ultimately forgotten. In contrast, Boaz’s self-sacrificing strength gains him eternal remembrance in the genealogies of David and Jesus Christ (Matthew 1:5).

• Healthy Workplace Culture: Ruth 2 shows Boaz greeted his harvesters with, "The LORD be with you!" and they responded, "The LORD bless you!" This demonstrates that Boaz's strength was exercised through godly leadership, creating a culture of mutual respect and spiritual vitality.
