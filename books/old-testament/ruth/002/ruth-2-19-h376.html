MAN - ( 'ish , Strong's H0376 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'ish is the primary term for an individual male, husband, or person of distinct character and standing. In Ruth 2:19, Naomi pronounces a blessing on the "man" who took notice of Ruth, and Ruth identifies this "man" as Boaz. Rather than representing a generic human being, 'ish in this context highlights an individual of capability, moral responsibility, and noble status, pointing directly to Boaz as the providential provider and future kinsman-redeemer.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun 'ish is used throughout the Old Testament to designate an individual male, frequently in contrast to a woman ('ishshah). It highlights the distinct personality, strength, and responsibility of the individual. While other Hebrew words for man emphasize different aspects of humanity—such as 'adam (generic humanity, dust-born) or 'enosh (mankind in its frailty and mortality)—'ish underscores capability, standing, and personal identity. In ancient Near Eastern culture, an 'ish was expected to be a protector, provider, and active participant in the covenant community. The word carries a sense of presence and agency, describing someone who has the capacity to act, make covenants, and impact the lives of others.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meanings of 'ish include:

• An individual male person, as distinguished from a woman or child.

• A husband, representing the male partner in a covenant marriage.

• A champion, leader, or person of high social standing and authority.

• "Each" or "every one," when used distributively in a sentence to indicate individual responsibility.

• In specific pairings, such as 'ish gibbor chayil (a man of great wealth or valor), it denotes a noble citizen of significant influence and moral integrity.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Husband and Covenant Partner: In the creation account, 'ish is used in relation to 'ishshah (woman), establishing the foundational, complementary relationship of marriage. The man is called to leave his father and mother and cleave to his wife, depicting 'ish as the initiator of covenantal care and union.

• The Man of Standing: When combined with words for strength or valor, 'ish paints a word picture of a protector or leader. He is not merely a male biological specimen, but a person who commands respect, exercises wisdom, and acts righteously in the public square.

• The Representative Agent: Frequently, 'ish represents a person acting on behalf of others, such as a champion in battle or a representative before God. It indicates one who stands in the gap to accomplish a specific task or mission.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, 'ish connects deeply to the themes of covenant, responsibility, and redemption. In the Book of Ruth, the designation of Boaz as an 'ish of standing and valor underscores God's provision of a qualified redeemer. The law of the kinsman-redeemer (goel) required a specific, capable, and willing relative to redeem land and carry on a family name. Boaz represents the ideal biblical 'ish—one who possesses the physical means, the legal right, and the spiritual willingness to show loving-kindness (chesed) to the vulnerable. This points forward to the ultimate 'ish, Jesus Christ, who took on human flesh to become our true Kinsman-Redeemer, stepping into our poverty to rescue and restore us.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:19, the word 'ish appears twice, framing a moment of transition from desperate survival to profound hope.

• First, Naomi asks, "Where did you work? Blessed be the man who took notice of you." At this point, Naomi is unaware of the identity of the benefactor. She only knows that some 'ish—some individual of means and compassion—has broken through their bleak circumstances by showing unexpected grace to Ruth.

• Second, Ruth responds, "The man's name with whom I worked today is Boaz." Here, the anonymous 'ish is given a name. This specific naming connects the generous provider to their own family line.

The contextual nuance reveals God's silent providence. The "man" Naomi blessed from afar is not a random stranger, but a close relative with the legal right to redeem them. The term transitions from a generic expression of gratitude for an unknown benefactor to a specific, joyful recognition of the answer to their prayers.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:24: "Therefore a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and they shall become one flesh."

• Ruth 2:1: "There was a relative of Naomi’s husband, a man of great wealth, of the family of Elimelech. His name was Boaz."

• Psalm 1:1: "Blessed is the man Who walks not in the counsel of the ungodly, Nor stands in the path of sinners, Nor sits in the seat of the scornful."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Hebrew poetic parallelism, 'ish is occasionally contrasted with 'adam to denote social distinctions. For example, "sons of 'adam" can refer to common people of low degree, while "sons of 'ish" refers to men of high rank or noble birth. This distinction reinforces the character of Boaz in the narrative of Ruth. He is not just any passenger through life; he is a man of substance, authority, and noble character who uses his elevated position to lift up the lowly.
