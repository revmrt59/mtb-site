FAVOR - ( chen , Strong's H2580 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word chen refers to grace, favor, or a disposition of goodwill that inclines one person to look upon another with kindness, often resulting in unearned material or relational benefit. In Ruth 2:2, it describes the unexpected acceptance and goodwill that a vulnerable outsider hopes to find in the eyes of a stranger, pointing forward to the greater, sovereign grace of God that orchestrates redemption in the darkest of circumstances.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

- The root verb from which chen is derived means to bend or stoop down in kindness to an inferior, or to show favor and grace.

- In the Ancient Near East, a foreign gleaner like Ruth had no legal standing, economic security, or social protection; her survival depended entirely on finding a benefactor willing to show unmerited kindness.

- The word evokes the posture of a superior looking down with compassion and practical mercy upon someone in desperate need.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

- Chen primarily denotes favor, grace, charm, or acceptance.

- It highlights an attitude of kindness and goodwill that leads to gracious action.

- Crucially, it emphasizes that the recipient has no legal claim or inherent merit to demand the benefit received; it is entirely at the discretion of the giver.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

- Used frequently in the Old Testament to describe human interactions where one person finds favor in the sight of another (e.g., Genesis 33:8, where Jacob hopes to find favor in Esau's eyes).

- Often coupled with finding "grace" or mercy in the eyes of kings, rulers, or potential employers.

- Frequently used vertically regarding humanity finding favor in the sight of God (e.g., Genesis 6:8, "Noah found grace in the eyes of the Lord").

4. Theological Implications

- While Ruth is asking for human favor from Boaz, the concept of chen gently bridges the gap between human kindness and divine providence.

- The unexpected favor Ruth receives in a foreign land serves as the earthly instrument through which God's hidden, sovereign redemption operates.

- It foreshadows the New Testament reality of divine grace (charis), where God stoops down in unmerited favor to redeem the spiritually destitute.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

- In Ruth 2:2, Ruth speaks to Naomi: "Please let me go to the field, and glean heads of grain after him in whose sight I may find favor."

- Here, chen represents Ruth's practical hope for a benevolent landowner who will not chase her away as a Moabite trespasser, but will allow her to gather the leftover scraps ordained by the Law of Moses for the poor.

- It marks the turning point from passive despair in Bethlehem to active, humble reliance on the kindness of others under God's providence.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

- Genesis 33:8 - And he said, "What do you mean by all this company which I met?" And he said, "These are to find favor in the sight of my lord."

- Genesis 39:4 - So Joseph found favor in his sight, and served him. Then he made him overseer of his house, and all that he had he put under his authority.

- Exodus 33:17 - So the Lord said to Moses, "I will also do this thing that you have spoken; for you have found grace [chen] in My sight, and I know you by name."

- Proverbs 3:4 - And so find favor and high esteem In the sight of God and man.

7. Other Relevant Insights

- The quest for chen in the Book of Ruth underscores how God often works through ordinary human kindness rather than loud, miraculous interventions.

- Boaz's later response to Ruth in chapter 2 directly answers this request, demonstrating that human compassion is often the tangible expression of answered prayer.
