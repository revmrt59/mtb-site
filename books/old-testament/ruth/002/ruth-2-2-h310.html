AFTER - ( 'achar , Strong's H0310 )

Summary:

The Hebrew preposition 'achar primarily denotes location (behind, after) or time (afterwards, after). In the context of Ruth 2:2, it conveys both a physical position—following behind the active reapers in the field—and a posture of profound humility, dependence, and faith. It highlights Ruth's proactive step of obedience and her willingness to occupy the lowest place of a gleaner, trusting in the providence of God to lead her behind the right person who would extend grace.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew preposition 'achar is derived from a root meaning to delay, linger, or remain behind. Physically, it paints a picture of being positioned at the rear or following in the footsteps of another. In the ancient agrarian culture of Israel, the harvesting process was highly structured. The landowners and their hired reapers walked through the fields first, cutting and binding the standing grain. Behind them came the poor, the strangers, the widows, and the orphans, walking "after" them to gather the dropped stalks. This physical arrangement visually represented social status, vulnerability, and dependence. To be "after" was to occupy the lowest place of survival, relying entirely on the crumbs left behind by those of higher status.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word 'achar functions primarily as a preposition of place and time. Spatially, it means "behind," "after," or "following at the rear." Temporally, it means "afterwards," "after," or "succeeding." In relational and idiomatic contexts, it describes the act of pursuing, following after, or showing allegiance to a leader, shepherd, or deity. In Ruth 2:2, the term is used spatially to define Ruth’s intended physical position in the harvest fields—moving behind the harvesters to gather what they dropped or overlooked.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Posture of a Disciple: In Hebrew thought, to walk 'achar (after) someone is to align oneself with their direction, authority, and destiny. It is the posture of a loyal follower or servant.

• The Path of the Poor: In the context of gleaning, 'achar represents the boundary of grace. The gleaner has no right to harvest side-by-side with the owners; they must remain "behind" to receive charity.

• The Shepherd and the Flock: Sheep naturally walk 'achar (behind) their shepherd, illustrating trustful dependence and security in the leader's provision.

4. Theological Implications

• The Humility of Faith: Ruth’s willingness to walk 'achar (after) the reapers demonstrates the profound humility required to receive grace. She did not demand rights as Naomi’s daughter-in-law or as a resident of Bethlehem; she took the lowest position of a foreign gleaner.

• Providential Leading: Though Ruth walked "behind" the reapers, she was actually being led "forward" by the unseen hand of God. The posture of following after others in humility often positions us directly in the path of divine redemption.

• The Way of Grace: Grace is not demanded; it is received from behind. We do not walk ahead of God prescribing His actions; we follow 'achar Him, gathering the expressions of His goodness that He drops along our path.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:2, Ruth's use of 'achar is paired with her hope to "find favor." She does not merely seek a field; she seeks a person "in whose sight I may find favor." Her physical position of walking "after" the reapers is a deliberate act of submission to the sovereignty and kindness of the field owner. She recognizes her status as a Moabitess and a widow, and she enters the field not with entitlement, but with a hope that whoever is ahead of her will look back with compassion on the one who is behind ('achar).

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Deuteronomy 13:4 - "You shall walk after the LORD your God and fear Him, and keep His commandments and obey His voice; you shall serve Him and hold fast to Him."

• Ruth 2:9 - "Let your eyes be on the field which they reap, and go after them. Have I not commanded the young men not to touch you? And when you are thirsty, go to the vessels and drink from what the young men have drawn."

• Jeremiah 2:2 - "Go and cry in the hearing of Jerusalem, saying, 'Thus says the LORD: "I remember you, the kindness of your youth, the love of your betrothal, when you went after Me in the wilderness, in a land not sown."'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a beautiful literary and spiritual progression in the Book of Ruth involving this word. Ruth begins her journey by choosing to go "after" Naomi (Ruth 1:16), leaving her homeland. She then humbles herself to glean "after" the reapers in the field (Ruth 2:2, 9). This path of intentional, humble following ultimately leads her to the feet of Boaz, where she is elevated from a foreign gleaner working "after" the servants to a beloved wife walking beside her redeemer. Her willingness to occupy the lowest place of 'achar becomes the very catalyst for her exaltation.
