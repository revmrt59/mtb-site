GLEAN - ( laqat , Strong's H3950 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb laqat refers to the act of picking up, collecting, or gathering items from the ground, especially leftover grain during a harvest. In the Old Testament, gleaning was a divinely instituted welfare system designed to provide food for the poor, the widow, the orphan, and the stranger. When Ruth requests to glean in Ruth 2:2, she is stepping out in faith to utilize this provision, demonstrating her humility, diligent work ethic, and reliance on God’s providential care.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient Israel, harvesting was done by reapers who quickly cut stalks of wheat or barley with sickles, followed by others who bound them into sheaves. In their haste, reapers inevitably dropped stalks of grain, or left patches untouched at the edges of the fields. Under God's covenant law, landowners were forbidden from going back to harvest these leftovers. This intentional omission created a safety net for the marginalized. Gleaners followed behind the harvesters, bending down to pick up the remaining stalks one by one. This practice prevented starvation while preserving the dignity of the poor, who had to work hard for their daily bread.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb laqat primarily means to pick up, gather, or collect. It describes the physical action of bending down to gather objects off the ground piece by piece. In agricultural contexts, it specifically means to glean—to collect the ears of grain left behind by the reapers. Outside of agriculture, the term denotes gathering other physical items, such as manna in the wilderness, stones for a heap, wood for a fire, or arrows shot from a bow. The focus of the word is on the systematic, diligent gathering of individual items to form a larger collective.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Daily Provision of Manna: The word is used extensively in Exodus 16 to describe the daily gathering of manna in the wilderness. The Israelites had to go out each morning and gather (laqat) their portion. This established a picture of daily, active dependence on God's provision.

• Gathering Leftovers as Judgement: In a metaphorical sense, the word is used to describe a thorough sweeping or gathering up of leftovers, sometimes representing a complete judgment where nothing is left behind, or conversely, the preservation of a tiny, picked-over remnant.

• Gathering Arrows: In Jonathan's covenant warning to David, his young servant is told to run and find (laqat) the arrows he has shot, using the word in a simple, non-agricultural sense of picking up specific objects.

4. Theological Implications

The law of gleaning (laqat) reveals key aspects of God's character and His design for community:

• God’s Ownership of the Land: Landowners were reminded that they did not ultimately own the harvest; the land belonged to God, and they were merely stewards. Leaving gleanings was an act of obedience and worship.

• Compassion and Justice: God has an active concern for the vulnerable. The gleaning laws protected widows like Naomi and foreigners like Ruth from exploitation, ensuring they had access to life-sustaining food.

• The Dignity of Work: The poor were not given hand-outs; they had to enter the field, endure the heat of the day, and labor alongside the harvesters. It was a partnership of generosity and industry.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:2, Ruth takes the initiative, asking Naomi for permission to go and glean (laqat). This request highlights her proactive character and her willingness to perform menial, exhausting, and potentially dangerous labor for their survival. Ruth does not demand her legal rights as a poor widow; instead, she seeks a field where she might "find favor." Because she was a Moabitess, she was vulnerable to mistreatment by local field workers. Her step of faith to glean is the catalyst that brings her "by chance" to the field of Boaz, setting the stage for redemption.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Leviticus 19:9 - "When you reap the harvest of your land, you shall not wholly reap the corners of your field, nor shall you gather the gleanings of your harvest."

• Exodus 16:21 - "So they gathered it every morning, every man according to his need. And when the sun became hot, it melted."

• Ruth 2:7 - "And she said, 'Please let me glean and gather after the reapers among the sheaves.' So she came and has continued from morning until now, except for a little while she stayed in the house."

• Ruth 2:15 - "And when she rose up to glean, Boaz commanded his young men, saying, 'Let her glean even among the sheaves, and do not reproach her.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

Boaz exceeds the minimum requirements of the Mosaic law. While the law required landowners to tolerate gleaners at the edges of the field, Boaz welcomes Ruth into the center of the harvest. He instructs his young men to purposely let grain fall from the bundles for her and allows her to glean directly among the standing sheaves. Through Boaz's extraordinary generosity, Ruth's grueling labor of gleaning (laqat) becomes an encounter with abundant, redeeming grace.
