FIND - ( matsa' , Strong's H4672 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb matsa' fundamentally means to encounter, secure, reach, or acquire something, often after a deliberate search, but frequently as an act of unexpected discovery or reception under God's providence. In the context of "finding favor" (or grace), it denotes receiving unmerited acceptance, goodwill, and protective care from another. In Ruth 2:2, Ruth's desire to "find favor" reflects her vulnerability as a foreign widow and her humble reliance on God's sovereign guidance to lead her to a compassionate protector.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The verb matsa' occurs over 450 times in the Old Testament. Its concrete background involves coming across something, meeting a person, or securing a necessary resource, such as finding water in a desert well. It carries the sense of transition from a state of lacking or seeking to a state of obtaining or experiencing. In the Ancient Near East, a stranger, widow, or disenfranchised person entering someone's land had no inherent rights or legal protection. To "find" favor was a matter of physical and economic survival, shifting the status of the outsider from a vulnerable target to a protected guest under the landowner's patronage.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb matsa' means to find, attain to, reach, acquire, or encounter. Lexically, it spans several distinct nuances:

• To locate something sought, such as lost animals, objects, or paths.

• To meet or encounter someone, either by chance or design.

• To obtain or secure a condition or state, such as finding rest or favor.

• To detect or discover a matter, such as finding guilt or finding a matter to be true.

• In the common idiom "to find favor in the eyes of" (matsa' chen be'eyney), it means to secure the goodwill, acceptance, or grace of a superior.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The word is used in various vivid ways throughout the Old Testament to describe physical and spiritual discoveries:

• Securing Safety and Rest: Finding a place of security or home (as when the dove found no resting place for the sole of her foot in Genesis 8:9).

• An Answer to Seeking: Finding God when He is sought with the whole heart, demonstrating that spiritual finding is the reward of diligent, sincere seeking.

• The Eye-to-Eye Encounter of Grace: "Finding favor" depicts a lower party looking up to a higher party, hoping to receive gracious recognition rather than hostility. It suggests a visual connection where the look of the superior changes from judgment or apathy to warm acceptance.

4. Theological Implications

• Providence versus Chance: While matsa' can denote a seemingly accidental occurrence to human eyes, the biblical narrative consistently frames "finding" as the hand of God's providence directing human steps. What appears to be "hap" or "coincidence" is God's active provision.

• The Nature of Grace: True favor (chen) cannot be demanded or earned; it must be found—that is, received as a gift. Ruth’s search for favor mirrors the sinner's search for grace before a holy God. Human beings cannot command God's grace, but they find it because He has already positioned Himself to be gracious to those who seek Him.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:2, Ruth says, "Please let me go to the field, and glean heads of grain after him in whose sight I may find favor." As a Moabitess and a destitute widow, Ruth has the legal right to glean according to Israel's poor laws (Leviticus 19:9-10). However, foreign women faced severe dangers of exploitation, assault, or expulsion in the harvest fields. Ruth does not demand her rights; instead, she seeks to "find favor." She recognizes her dependence on the kindness of a landowner who will actively protect her. This "finding" is immediately answered in verse 3, where her "hap" (chance) is to light upon the portion of the field belonging to Boaz. God directs her steps so that she finds the very favor she sought.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 6:8 - "But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD."

• Genesis 32:5 - "I have sent to tell my lord, that I may find favor in your sight."

• Proverbs 8:35 - "For whoever finds me finds life, And obtains favor from the LORD;"

• Jeremiah 29:13 - "And you will seek Me and find Me, when you search for Me with all your heart."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Hebrew thought, matsa' (finding) is the natural resolution of baqash (seeking). However, in Ruth's case, she does not know whom she is seeking. She goes out looking for a category of person ("him in whose sight..."), and God connects her seeking with the specific finding. This initial request to "find favor" in the eyes of an unknown benefactor (Ruth 2:2) finds its ultimate fulfillment when Boaz grants her protection and eventually provides her with ultimate "rest" (manoach) through marriage.
