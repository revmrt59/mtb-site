PLEASE - ( na' , Strong's H4994 )

Summary:

The Hebrew particle na' is a term of entreaty, exhortation, or polite request, often translated in the New King James Version as "please," "now," or "I pray you." Rather than acting as a substantive noun or verb, this logical and emotional modifier softens an imperative or expression of intent, transforming what could be a bold assertion or demand into a respectful, deferential request. In the context of Ruth 2:2, this small but significant word highlights Ruth’s deep humility, her filial respect for her mother-in-law Naomi, and her submissive character as she seeks permission to glean in the fields, beautifully illustrating how covenant love expresses itself in courtesy.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient Near Eastern culture, social hierarchies, familial structures, and interpersonal respect were deeply embedded in daily speech. Addressing an elder, a parent, or a social superior required careful linguistic markers to preserve honor. The particle na' functions as an oral gesture of bowing. While it does not possess a physical or concrete word picture, its presence in Hebrew dialogue acts as a relational buffer. By appending na' to her proposal to go and glean, Ruth linguistically positions herself as a humble daughter seeking maternal blessing rather than an independent operator announcing her personal agenda.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The Hebrew word na' is an enclitic particle of entreaty or exhortation. It is used to express a polite request, a gentle urge, or an earnest petition. Lexical analysis indicates its primary function is to soften commands, express wishes with deference, or add emotional urgency to an appeal. Grammatically, it is frequently attached to cohortatives (verbs expressing self-encouragement or requests for permission, such as "let me go") and imperatives (commands, softening them to "please do this"). It serves to bridge the gap between two parties in a manner that honors the recipient of the speech.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The particle occurs over 400 times in the Hebrew Bible, demonstrating its widespread necessity in godly discourse.

• It is used in prayers to Yahweh, expressing deep dependency, urgency, and the recognition of God's absolute sovereignty. In these contexts, it underscores the petitioner’s awareness of their own unworthiness.

• It is used in polite human-to-human discourse, especially when a speaker recognizes a difference in status, power, or age. Abraham uses it when addressing his heavenly visitors, and Jacob uses it when seeking reconciliation with Esau.

• In narrative dialogue, it functions to indicate courtesy, respect, and emotional warmth. It signals peaceful intentions, acting as an olive branch in conversation and ensuring that communication remains constructive and honoring.

4. Theological Implications

Though small, na' carries profound theological weight regarding the nature of grace, humility, and human relationships under God's covenant. True godliness is marked by courtesy and respect. Ruth’s use of this particle reflects the quiet, meek, and gentle spirit that God highly values. Furthermore, the frequent biblical use of na' in prayer reveals that God permits His people to entreat Him with earnest, polite petition. It underscores that covenant relationship is not based on cold, demanding transactions but on respectful, intimate communion where even the sovereign God hears the "please" of His dependent children.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:2, Ruth and Naomi are destitute widows who have recently arrived in Bethlehem. Although Ruth is a devoted daughter-in-law, she is also a foreigner (a Moabitess) who has legal rights to glean under the Mosaic Law. Despite having the law on her side, Ruth does not demand her rights, nor does she act independently of Naomi. By saying, "Please let me go," Ruth submits her actions to Naomi's oversight and blessing. This deferential posture shows that Ruth's devotion to Naomi is functional and respectful, honoring Naomi’s role as the matriarch. This humility prepares the reader for Ruth’s ultimate exaltation; her willingness to take the lowest place sets the stage for God's providential lifting of her status through Boaz.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 13:8: "So Abram said to Lot, 'Please let there be no strife between you and me, and between my herdsmen and your herdsmen; for we are brethren.'"

• Genesis 18:3: "and said, 'My Lord, if I have now found favor in Your sight, do not pass by Your servant.'"

• Genesis 22:2: "Then He said, 'Take now your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love, and go to the land of Moriah, and offer him there as a burnt offering on one of the mountains of which I shall tell you.'"

• Psalm 118:25: "Save now, I pray, O LORD; O LORD, I pray, send now prosperity."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The translation of na' in the Septuagint (LXX) often utilizes particles like de (indeed, now) or specific verbal moods to capture the polite nuance of the Hebrew. In modern English translations, small words like "now" or "please" are easily overlooked, but in Hebrew narrative, they provide crucial characterization. The presence of na' distinguishes the speech of the humble and righteous from the coarse, demanding speech of the arrogant. It reminds the modern reader that biblical holiness extends to our manner of speech and interpersonal politeness.
