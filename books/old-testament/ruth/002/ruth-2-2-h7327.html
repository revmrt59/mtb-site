RUTH - ( Rut , Strong's H7327 )

Summary:

The name Ruth (transliterated Rut) most likely derives from a root meaning "friendship" or "companion." As a Moabite widow who binds her destiny to her Judean mother-in-law, Naomi, and the God of Israel, Ruth's name beautifully foreshadows her character. She embodies loyal, self-sacrificing companionship (chesed) and becomes a key link in the redemptive lineage leading to King David and ultimately Jesus Christ. In Ruth 2:2, she steps forward as a humble provider, taking the initiative to glean in the fields to sustain her family.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The name Rut is closely associated with the Hebrew word re'ut, meaning "friendship" or "female companion," which stems from re'eh, meaning "friend" or "companion."

• The background of the name shines against the dark historical period of the Judges, a time of national disintegration, spiritual apostasy, and broken relationships. In contrast to the self-interest of the era, Ruth represents covenant-like loyalty and companionship.

• Her narrative begins in Moab, a nation historically hostile to Israel, but through marriage and subsequent devotion to Naomi, she crosses ethnic and geographical boundaries to become a model of faithful companionship.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The proper noun Rut means "friendship" or "companion."

• The root verb ra'ah means to associate with, tend, or pasture, suggesting a nurturing, close relationship.

• As a proper name, it designates the Moabitess who became the wife of Mahlon first, and later Boaz, and who is the prominent subject of the Book of Ruth.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The name occurs 12 times in the Old Testament, all within the Book of Ruth.

• A "companion" who clings: Ruth’s name is a living word picture of her actions in Chapter 1, where she "clung" (dabaq) to Naomi. Her life defines companionship not as proximity, but as covenant commitment.

• The "stranger" who becomes a "friend": Ruth enters Bethlehem as a destitute foreigner (nokriyah) but her actions transform her name into a symbol of the ultimate sisterly and daughterly friend.

4. Theological Implications

• Redemptive History and Gentile Inclusion: Ruth, a Moabitess, is brought into the covenant community of Israel, proving that God's grace extends beyond ethnic boundaries to all who seek refuge under His wings.

• Covenant Loyalty (chesed): Ruth's name and character serve as a human reflection of God’s covenant loyalty. Her commitment to Naomi represents the sacrificial love that God expects His people to show to one another.

• Lineage of the Messiah: Ruth is the great-grandmother of David, securing her place in the genealogy of Jesus Christ. Her name represents God's providence working through ordinary, seemingly insignificant acts of daily faithfulness.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 2:2, the designation "Ruth the Moabitess" is deliberately used. This emphasizes her foreign origin, highlighting the vulnerability she faced as she went out to glean.

• Her name ("friendship/companion") is vindicated by her actions: she does not wait to be served but takes the initiative to feed her mother-in-law. Her speech to Naomi is tender, respectful ("Please let me go"), and active, demonstrating that her "friendship" is not merely passive sentiment but active, risk-taking love.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Ruth 1:14: "Then they lifted up their voices and wept again; and Orpah kissed her mother-in-law, but Ruth clung to her."

• Ruth 2:8: "Then Boaz said to Ruth, 'Listen, my daughter, will you not go to glean in another field, nor go from here, but stay close by my young women?'"

• Ruth 3:9: "And he said, 'Who are you?' So she answered, 'I am Ruth, your maidservant. Take your maidservant under your wing, for you are a close relative.'"

• Ruth 4:13: "So Boaz took Ruth and she became his wife; and when he went in to her, the LORD gave her conception, and she bore a son."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The literary structure of the Book of Ruth revolves around her name and identity. She is introduced as "Ruth the Moabitess" but ends as the mother of Obed, the ancestor of David.

• In Jewish tradition, Ruth is held up as the supreme model of a righteous convert. Her willingness to abandon her homeland and gods to embrace Naomi's God ("Your people shall be my people, and your God, my God") is the ultimate expression of true spiritual companionship with Yahweh.
