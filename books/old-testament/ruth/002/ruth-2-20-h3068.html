LORD - ( YHWH , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The word YHWH (the Tetragrammaton) is the personal, covenant name of the one true God of Israel. Revealed most famously to Moses at the burning bush, it denotes God’s self-existence, eternal nature, and unchangeable faithfulness to His covenant promises. In the Book of Ruth, and specifically in Ruth 2:20, this name emphasizes God's active, faithful, and personal involvement in the lives of ordinary people, orchestrating what seems like chance to show His loyal love (chesed) to those who trust in Him.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The covenant name YHWH is intimately connected to the Hebrew verb hayah, meaning "to be" or "to exist." It reflects God's eternal, self-existent nature—He is the One who is, who was, and who is to come. Historically, because of the extreme holiness associated with this name, later Jewish tradition avoided pronouncing it aloud to prevent taking it in vain, substituting the title Adonai ("Lord") during public reading. This practice is preserved in modern translations, including the NKJV, by rendering YHWH as "LORD" in all capital letters. Unlike generic terms for deity, YHWH is a proper noun, marking God's desire to be known personally and intimately by His people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• YHWH signifies the self-existent, eternal, personal God of Israel. It denotes the absolute independence and sovereignty of God, who relies on nothing outside of Himself for His existence, power, or actions. Lexically, the name represents the active, present reality of God as the Savior, Creator, and Sustainer. It is the name that guarantees God's promises; because He eternally exists and does not change, His word is entirely reliable.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• YHWH is used over 6,800 times in the Old Testament, making it the most frequent designation for God.

• It is often coupled with rich metaphors: YHWH is described as a rock, a shield, a shepherd, and a husband, illustrating His protective, intimate, and guiding relationship with His people.

• In Hebrew narrative, the name stands in stark contrast to Elohim (the general word for God as Creator and Judge). While Elohim emphasizes God's distant, transcendent power over the cosmos, YHWH emphasizes His nearness, accessibility, and covenant-keeping loyalty to individuals.

4. Theological Implications

• Covenant Faithfulness: YHWH is the God who makes and keeps covenants. His actions are bound to His promises. When God acts under this name, He is demonstrating that His character is bound to the welfare of His people.

• Immutability and Sovereignty: Because He is YHWH, He does not change. His purposes stand secure despite human failure, national tragedy, or personal grief.

• Grace and Redemption: The name is structurally linked to Israel's redemption from Egypt. It reveals a God who hears the cries of the afflicted, steps down into history, and delivers His people from bondage.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:20, Naomi exclaims, "Blessed be he of the LORD, who has not forsaken His kindness to the living and the dead!" This marks a massive theological pivot in the book. Previously, in chapter 1, Naomi felt that the Almighty (Shaddai) had dealt bitterly with her and gone out against her. Here, upon hearing that Ruth "happened" to glean in the field of Boaz, Naomi recognizes the active, behind-the-scenes hand of YHWH.

The use of YHWH highlights that the covenant God of Israel has not abandoned Naomi and Ruth, even in their deepest grief and poverty. The "kindness" (chesed) mentioned is the loyal, covenant-keeping love of YHWH executed through human instruments like Boaz. God is revealed not as a distant observer, but as the active, loving Provider who orchestrates ordinary daily events to bring about redemption.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:4 - "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens,"

• Exodus 3:15 - "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

• Psalm 23:1 - "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

• Proverbs 3:5 - "Trust in the LORD with all your heart, And lean not on your own understanding;"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The revelation of YHWH to Naomi in the midst of her mundane circumstances—a day's work of gleaning barley—teaches that God's covenant faithfulness is not reserved only for grand national events like the Exodus, but is intimately woven into the daily, ordinary lives of His people. Furthermore, the name YHWH links the narrative of Ruth directly to the grand narrative of Israel's redemption. Boaz, acting as a kinsman-redeemer (goel), reflects the ultimate redeeming character of YHWH Himself, who would centuries later bring forth the ultimate Redeemer, Jesus Christ, from this very lineage.
