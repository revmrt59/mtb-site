MAN - ( 'ish , Strong's H376 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'ish refers to an individual man, a husband, or a person of distinction. In Ruth 2:20, it designates Boaz not merely as a male human being, but as a specific, significant man of status who qualifies as a kinsman-redeemer (goel). The term emphasizes his identity, responsibility, and capacity to act as a protector and provider for the vulnerable household of Naomi and Ruth.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew word 'ish stands in contrast to other Old Testament words for "man," such as adam (which refers to humanity collectively or man in relation to the ground) and enosh (which emphasizes human frailty and mortality). By contrast, 'ish denotes an individual of energy, presence, and responsibility. It suggests someone who is capable of taking action, making decisions, and entering into covenant relationships. In the cultural setting of the ancient Near East, an 'ish was the head of a household, a defender of the family, and the one legally and socially responsible for upholding the family line and protecting vulnerable relatives.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meaning of 'ish is an individual male person. It carries several layered nuances depending on the context:

• A Husband: The counterpart to 'ishah (woman/wife), denoting a man in a covenant marriage relationship.

• A Person of Distinction: A man of high standing, wealth, or valor (often paired with other terms to denote leadership or courage).

• An Individual: Used distributively to mean "each one" or "every man."

• In Ruth 2:20, the word functions to point out a specific individual of status and capacity who is uniquely positioned to alter the destiny of Naomi and Ruth.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, 'ish is used to draw attention to personal character and responsibility:

• The Man of Valor: Used to describe brave warriors or noble leaders who step forward to deliver Israel.

• The Husband: In the creation account, the term establishes the relational bone-and-flesh union between husband and wife, illustrating protective care.

• In the book of Ruth, Boaz is introduced in Ruth 2:1 as a "man of great wealth" ('ish gibbor chayil). This structural link connects his financial and social capability directly to his personal character. When Naomi calls him "this man" in Ruth 2:20, she is identifying him as the specific provider and protector they desperately need.

### 4. Theological Implications

Theologically, 'ish highlights the dignity of individual agency and covenant responsibility under God. God works through specific individuals to accomplish His redemptive purposes. Boaz, as the 'ish who steps forward to redeem Naomi's land and marry Ruth, serves as a powerful type of Jesus Christ. Just as Boaz was a man of standing who willingly took on the burden of redemption, Christ became the ultimate 'ish—the Man of Sorrows who possesses both the right and the power to redeem helpless humanity.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:20, Naomi’s exclamation marks the dramatic turning point of the narrative. Having previously declared herself empty and bitter, the realization of Boaz’s kindness causes her to bless the Lord. When she says, "This man is a relation of ours," she is recognizing God's providential hand. Boaz is not merely a random landowner; he is the specific 'ish who holds the legal right of redemption (geullah). The word underscores that God does not help in abstract ways; He provides concrete, physical deliverance through a specific, capable person.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:24: "Therefore a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and they shall become one flesh."

• Ruth 2:1: "There was a relative of Naomi’s husband, a man of great wealth, of the family of Elimelech. His name was Boaz."

• 1 Samuel 13:14: "But now your kingdom shall not continue. The Lord has sought for Himself a man after His own heart, and the Lord has commanded him to be commander over His people, because you have not kept what the Lord commanded you."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

A helpful contrast exists between 'ish and the collective term adam. While adam reminds us of our dust-born nature and physical limits, 'ish points to the moral, social, and spiritual duties we carry in community. When the narrative focuses on Boaz as an 'ish, it highlights his willingness to rise above self-interest to fulfill a duty of covenant love (chesed) toward those who cannot repay him.
