RELATION - ( qarob , Strong's H7138 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word qarob fundamentally means "near" or "close," referring to proximity in space, time, or relationship. In familial contexts, it designates a close relative who shares an intimate bond of kinship and legal responsibility. In Ruth 2:20, Naomi uses this word to identify Boaz as a near relation, shifting her perspective from bitter isolation to covenantal hope. This nearness is not merely genealogical; it activates the legal rights and protective duties of the kinsman, positioning Boaz as a potential redeemer of their family line and inheritance.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The root verb qarab means "to draw near" or "to approach." Historically, in the communal and agrarian lifestyle of ancient Israel, nearness dictated survival, safety, and identity. Those who lived nearby—neighbors and immediate family—formed a protective boundary against external threats, poverty, and loss of land. Qarob represents the inner circle of those who are closest to an individual, carrying both the emotional warmth of intimacy and the legal weight of covenantal duty. In a society without modern state safety nets, having a "near" relative was the difference between restoration and ruin.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Qarob functions as an adjective indicating proximity. It contains a range of specific applications:

• Spatial Nearness: Describing physical proximity, such as a city that is geographically close by.

• Temporal Nearness: Describing an event, day, or judgment that is fast approaching in time.

• Relational Nearness: Describing intimate companions, close friends, or neighbors.

• Kinship Nearness: Substantively identifying a "near relative" or "close relation" who belongs to the immediate clan and therefore bears legal kinship obligations.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament frequently uses qarob to contrast closeness with distance. Relational distance (rahoq) signifies alienation, abandonment, or exile, while nearness (qarob) signifies presence, safety, and alliance.

• The Nearness of God: The word paints a vivid picture of God's accessibility. He is not a distant deity but is "near" to those who call upon Him in truth and those who are brokenhearted.

• The Guardian Circle: In the legal codes of Israel, qarob outlines a physical and social boundary. If an Israelite falls into poverty, the relative who is "near" to him must step in. The word picture is one of a protective arm reaching out to pull a drowning family member back into the safety of the community.

### 4. Theological Implications

• God’s Covenantal Presence: The theological heartbeat of the Old Testament is that Yahweh is a God who draws near to His people. His nearness brings salvation, justice, and comfort.

• The Principle of Redemption: The concept of the "near relation" is the legal foundation for redemption. A redeemer cannot be a stranger; he must be a qarob—someone closely related to the one in need. This legal requirement directly points to the incarnation of Jesus Christ, who became "near" to humanity by taking on flesh, qualifying Him to act as our Kinsman-Redeemer.

• Covenantal Responsibility: Relational nearness in Scripture always carries ethical demands. To be qarob to someone means one cannot ignore their suffering; closeness demands action, protection, and provision.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:20, Naomi’s exclamation marks the turning point of the entire narrative. Having returned from Moab destitute, bitter, and feeling abandoned by God, she suddenly learns that Ruth has providentially gleaned in the field of Boaz.

Naomi declares, "The man is a relation of ours, one of our close relatives." By identifying Boaz as qarob, Naomi recognizes that he is not a distant stranger, but someone within their intimate family circle. This realization immediately connects Boaz to the role of the goel (redeemer). The physical nearness of Boaz's field and his relational nearness to Elimelech's family combine to spark hope. His status as a qarob means he has the legal right to buy back their lost land and raise up an heir for the deceased, transforming Naomi's bitterness into joyful expectation.

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

In the legal resolution of the book of Ruth (chapters 3 and 4), the degree of "nearness" becomes the central plot point. Boaz reveals that while he is indeed a qarob (near relation), there is another relative who is even "nearer" (qarob) than he. This highlights the precise hierarchy of legal responsibility in Israelite law. The redemption of Ruth and Naomi could not bypass God's established order of relational proximity; the nearer relative had to be given the first opportunity to redeem before Boaz could legally step forward.
