KEEP CLOSE - (dabaq, Strong's H1692)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb dabaq means to cling, cleave, adhere, or stick closely to someone or something. It expresses a deep, permanent physical or relational bond, such as a husband cleaving to his wife or Israel clinging faithfully to Yahweh. In Ruth 2:21, it describes Ruth's instructions to remain joined closely with Boaz's workers for safety, sustenance, and ultimate redemption, reflecting her fierce loyalty and God's protective providence.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root meaning of dabaq is physical adhesion—like skin clinging to bones in times of famine (Job 19:20) or a tongue sticking to the roof of the mouth from extreme thirst (Psalm 137:6). It represents two distinct entities becoming permanently joined together. It is the same word used in Genesis 2:24 to describe how a husband "cleaves" to his wife, depicting an inseparable connection and an intentional holding fast that resists external pressures to separate.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term denotes sticking to, adhering to, remaining close, or pursuing closely. Its semantic range spans from physical gluing or catching up with someone in a chase to intense loyalty, affection, and covenant faithfulness. It is a verb of deep relational commitment and physical proximity.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Covenant Loyalty: Israel is repeatedly commanded to "cling" (dabaq) to the Lord (Deuteronomy 10:20, Joshua 23:8). This is not passive proximity but active, tenacious devotion.

• Marital Bond: The foundational blueprint for marriage in Genesis 2:24 uses dabaq to show a permanent union of heart, mind, and flesh.

• Pursuit in Battle: It can mean to press hard after or overtake an enemy (Judges 20:45).

4. Theological Implications

• Dabaq is central to the Old Testament concept of covenant intimacy. It depicts a faith that does not merely believe in God but clings to Him desperately and lovingly, recognizing Him as the sole source of life and security. In the Book of Ruth, this word bridges the physical reality of gleaning in a safe field with the spiritual reality of finding refuge under the wings of the God of Israel.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:21, Ruth tells Naomi that Boaz instructed her to "keep close" (dabaq) to his young men (specifically his workers, which includes the young women in verse 22) until the harvest is completed. In this context, dabaq signifies physical safety, economic provision, and social alignment. By staying close to Boaz's household, Ruth is protected from the dangers facing a foreign widow in the fields of others during the chaotic period of the Judges. It also foreshadows her ultimate, permanent "cleaving" to Boaz in marriage. Naomi reinforces this in verse 22, noting that it is good to go out with his young women so that she is not ruined in another field.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:24 - "Therefore a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and they shall become one flesh."

• Ruth 1:14 - "Then they lifted up their voices and wept again; and Orpah kissed her mother-in-law, but Ruth clung to her."

• Joshua 23:8 - "but you shall hold fast to the LORD your God, as you have done to this day."

• Psalm 63:8 - "My soul follows close behind You; Your right hand upholds me."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Septuagint (LXX) often translates dabaq with kollao, which means to glue or cement together. This Greek word is used in the New Testament to describe joining oneself to a spouse (Matthew 19:5), clinging to what is good (Romans 12:9), and being joined to the Lord in one spirit (1 Corinthians 6:17).
