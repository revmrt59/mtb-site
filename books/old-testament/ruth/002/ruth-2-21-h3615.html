FINISHED - ( kalah , Strong's H3615 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb kalah means to complete, bring to an end, finish, or consume. In Ruth 2:21, it highlights the thoroughness, completion, and duration of Boaz's generous provision and protection. By instructing Ruth to stay with his workers until they "finished" the entire harvest, Boaz guaranteed her safety and sustenance through both the barley and wheat seasons, representing a full cycle of God's providential care.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete, agrarian imagery of ancient Hebrew associates kalah with the boundary or completion of a process. It is the action of bringing something to its ultimate limit or exhausting a supply. In an agricultural society, a harvest was not merely a task, but a life-sustaining season. To "finish" a harvest meant to gather every last stalk, secure the grain, and close the season of labor. The word picture of kalah evokes the transition from active labor to rest and security once a vital task has been fully executed.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb kalah is a primitive root that expresses the concept of bringing a process to its absolute completion, ending a task, or exhausting a resource.

• It denotes the cessation of an activity because the goal has been reached.

• It carries the sense of completing a physical structure or fulfilling a period of time.

• In some contexts, it can mean to consume, destroy, or spend entirely, as in exhausting one’s strength or depleting food reserves.

• In Ruth 2:21, the verb appears in the infinitive construct form (kallotam), functioning as a temporal clause meaning "until they have completed" or "until their finishing of" the entire harvest.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The verb kalah is used extensively throughout the Old Testament to depict various forms of completion:

• The Completion of Creation: It describes God finishing His work of creation on the seventh day, signaling that the cosmos was fully formed and ready for rest.

• The Completion of Sacred Construction: It is used when Moses finished setting up the tabernacle and when Solomon finished building the temple. In these cases, the completion represents the realization of God’s dwelling place among His people.

• Consuming or Spent Resources: It describes physical depletion, such as water running out of a skin or eyes failing from weeping and longing.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, kalah points to the faithfulness and order of God. God is not a God of half-measures; what He initiates, He brings to absolute completion. This is reflected in the agricultural cycle He established, where the harvest runs its full course under His sovereign care. Furthermore, kalah carries a redemptive arc. Just as the Tabernacle and Temple had to be "finished" for God’s presence to dwell visibly, Boaz’s harvest must be "finished" to fully transition Ruth and Naomi from emptiness to abundance. This points forward to the ultimate completion of redemption found in Christ, who declared on the cross that the work of salvation was finished.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:21, Ruth tells Naomi that Boaz instructed her to stay close to his workers "until they have finished all my harvest." The contextual placement of kalah here is vital.

• First, it highlights the extensive duration of Boaz’s kindness. The harvest season lasted about seven weeks, encompassing both the barley and wheat harvests. Boaz was not offering a single meal, but a guaranteed, long-term sanctuary.

• Second, it underscores security. Ruth was safe from harassment as long as the harvest was active.

• Third, it represents a transition of status. Staying until the harvest was "finished" ensured that Ruth remained in close proximity to Boaz, setting the stage for the deeper redemption that would take place at the threshing floor once the crop was gathered.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:2: "And on the seventh day God ended His work which He had done, and He rested on the seventh day from all His work which He had done."

• Genesis 6:16: "You shall make a window for the ark, and you shall finish it to a cubit from above; and set the door of the ark in its side. You shall make it with lower, second, and third decks."

• Exodus 40:33: "And he raised up the court all around the tabernacle and the altar, and hung up the screen of the court gate. So Moses finished the work."

• 1 Kings 6:38: "And in the eleventh year, in the month of Bul, which is the eighth month, the house was finished in all its details and according to all its plans. So he was seven years building it."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the broader narrative of Ruth, kalah contrasts sharply with the "emptiness" (reqam) Naomi lamented in chapter 1. The finishing of the harvest is the physical manifestation of God filling their emptiness. It also demonstrates Boaz's character as a worthy man (gibbor chayil), whose word can be trusted to stand firm from the beginning of the harvest season until its absolute end.
