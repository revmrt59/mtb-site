GOOD - ( tob , Strong's H2896 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word tob is a rich, multifaceted term expressing that which is pleasant, beneficial, right, beautiful, and in perfect alignment with God's design. In Ruth 2:22, Naomi uses tob to affirm that Ruth's gleaning in Boaz's field is not only safe and practical but also a providential good, signaling the beginning of God's restoration and protection over the vulnerable widows.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The ancient Near Eastern concept of tob is deeply practical and concrete, rather than abstract or purely philosophical. To the ancient Hebrew mind, something was "good" when it functioned exactly as its creator intended it to, bringing harmony, order, and life. In an agrarian covenant community, "good" described rain in its proper season, a rich harvest, a safe pasture, and a secure home. It is the sensory and moral experience of God's order overcoming chaos, famine, and isolation.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The semantic range of tob is remarkably broad, functioning as an adjective, noun, or verb depending on the context. The primary categories of its meaning include:

• Practical Goodness: Beneficial, advantageous, useful, fit for purpose, or safe.

• Esthetic Goodness: Beautiful, fair, pleasant, or appealing to the senses.

• Moral Goodness: Right, virtuous, upright, ethical, and in line with God’s law.

• Experiential Goodness: Joyful, happy, prosperous, and abundant.

At its core, tob indicates that which promotes, sustains, and enriches life in accordance with God's design.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Creation Standard: The word first appears in Genesis 1, where God surveys His creative work and repeatedly declares it "good" (tob), culminating in the declaration that everything was "very good" (tob me'od). This establishes tob as the ultimate state of divine order and completeness.

• The Land of Promise: Canaan is frequently described as a "good land"—a place of abundance, safety, and rest, contrasting with the wilderness and Egypt.

• Relational Harmony: It describes the pleasantness of unity, sweet aroma, and the peace of a well-ordered family or community.

### 4. Theological Implications

Theological goodness in the Old Testament is fundamentally anchored in the character of God. God is not merely the arbiter of what is good; He is the source and definition of it. When humans attempt to define tob apart from God (as did Eve in the Garden), it leads to spiritual and physical death.

In the narrative of Ruth, tob operates as a quiet marker of divine providence. While God rarely speaks directly or performs overt miracles in this book, His goodness is woven into the ordinary, daily decisions of faithful people. The transition from the "bad" of famine and death to the "good" of harvest and marriage illustrates how God's providential goodness works through human kindness (chesed) and obedience.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:22, Naomi tells Ruth, "It is good, my daughter, that you go out with his young women, and that people do not meet you in any other field."

• Safety and Provision: Here, tob carries a heavy nuance of practical benefit, wisdom, and safety. A foreign, young widow gleaning alone faced extreme vulnerability to physical and sexual assault. Staying in Boaz’s field among his female workers was "good" because it provided a protective sanctuary.

• Providential Confirmation: Naomi recognizes that Ruth's "chance" arrival at the field of Boaz, their close relative, is a divine signal. Her declaration "it is good" is an acknowledgment of God's unfolding favor. The bitterness Naomi expressed in chapter 1 is beginning to give way to the realization that God has not abandoned them; He is bringing about a practical, redeeming "good."

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:18 - "And the LORD God said, 'It is not good that man should be alone; I will make him a helper comparable to him.'"

• Psalm 34:8 - "Oh, taste and see that the LORD is good; Blessed is the man who trusts in Him!"

• Psalm 119:71 - "It is good for me that I have been afflicted, That I may learn Your statutes."

• Psalm 133:1 - "Behold, how good and how pleasant it is For brethren to dwell together in unity!"

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

The book of Ruth represents a structural movement from what is "not good" to what is "good."

• It begins with famine, displacement, and the death of husbands (the ultimate disruption of tob).

• It moves through the quiet faithfulness of Ruth and Boaz, who act as agents of God's goodness to one another.

• It culminates in Ruth 4, where the townspeople declare that Ruth is "better (tob) to you than seven sons," and Naomi is restored. Naomi’s advice in Ruth 2:22 is the pivotal turning point where she shifts from a passive, grief-stricken victim to an active participant in God's good provision.
