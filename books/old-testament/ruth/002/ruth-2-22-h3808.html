NOT - ( lo , Strong's H3808 )

Summary:

The Hebrew negative particle lo functions as the absolute, objective negation in the Old Testament, representing a complete and permanent boundary or prohibition. In the context of Ruth 2:22, it serves as a protective linguistic shield, indicating that by remaining in Boaz's field, Ruth will absolutely not encounter the danger, harassment, or assault that would otherwise threaten her in another field during the lawless era of the Judges.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew particle lo is the primary adverb of negation in the Old Testament, expressing absolute, objective negation. Unlike the subjective negation of al (which expresses a wish, deprecation, or temporary prohibition), lo establishes a permanent, objective, or unconditional "no." In the context of ancient Near Eastern agricultural life and social customs, protection and boundaries were paramount. The use of this absolute negation in Naomi's advice highlights the definitive barrier between safety in Boaz's field and the very real dangers present in other fields.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The adverb lo serves as the absolute negative particle, translated as "not," "no," "neither," or "none." It negates verbs, nouns, adjectives, and entire clauses. When modifying a verb in the imperfect tense, it signifies a permanent prohibition or an objective statement of fact about what will not occur. Unlike the prohibitive al, which is advisory or immediate, lo represents an absolute negation of the action. In Ruth 2:22, it negates the verb paga ("to meet, encounter, fall upon, or assault"), establishing an absolute safeguard against harmful encounters.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The particle lo functions as a linguistic wall, drawing a sharp boundary between what is permitted, safe, or true, and what is forbidden, dangerous, or false.

• Absolute Prohibition: In the Decalogue, lo is used with imperfect verbs to establish permanent moral boundaries ("You shall not murder," "You shall not commit adultery").

• Complete Prevention: It signifies the total absence of an occurrence, acting as a shield against potential outcomes.

• Objective Reality: It contrasts a factual negative state with a positive one, defining reality by what is excluded.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, the absolute negation represented by lo reflects God's protective boundaries and the providential care He extends to the vulnerable. In the story of Ruth, God's providence operates through the wisdom of Naomi and the generosity of Boaz. The absolute negation of danger ("that they do not meet you") illustrates how God's grace secures His children from harm. In the economy of redemption, God's "no" to danger is simultaneously His "yes" to safety, provision, and covenant faithfulness.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:22, Naomi counsels Ruth to remain with Boaz's young women, "and that they do not meet you in any other field." The Hebrew verb translated "meet" (paga) can carry a neutral meaning of "encounter," but often carries a negative, violent connotation of "assault," "fall upon," or "harass." By using the absolute negative lo, Naomi emphasizes that staying in Boaz's field guarantees Ruth will not be subjected to the physical or sexual harassment common to vulnerable foreign gleaners during the chaotic period of the Judges. It marks Boaz's field as a sanctuary of absolute safety, contrasted with the vulnerability of "any other field."

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:17 - "but of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil you shall not eat, for in the day that you eat of it you shall surely die."

• Genesis 3:4 - "Then the serpent said to the woman, 'You will not surely die.'"

• Exodus 20:13 - "You shall not murder."

• Psalm 23:1 - "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Hebrew text of Ruth 2:22, the verb negated is yifge'u (from paga). Without the negative lo, Ruth's vulnerability as a foreign widow would leave her exposed to the harsh realities of a lawless era. The presence of this single, small negative particle represents the difference between ruin and redemption. It serves as a reminder that the guidance of godly counsel and the boundaries of God's provision are designed to keep believers from paths of unnecessary peril.
