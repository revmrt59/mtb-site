SAID - ( 'amar , Strong's H0559 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'amar is one of the most common words in the Old Testament, fundamentally meaning "to say," "speak," or "declare." In the context of Ruth 2:22, Naomi's speaking to Ruth represents more than casual conversation; it is an utterance of wise, pastoral, and maternal counsel. Naomi directs Ruth to remain within the safe, providential boundaries of Boaz's fields, reinforcing God's protective care and positioning Ruth for future redemption.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root 'amar fundamentally represents the externalization of internal thought. It is the verbalization of a person's heart, intent, or will. In the ancient Near East, spoken words carried substantial weight, often functioning as binding counsel, commitments, or authoritative declarations. In the narrative of Ruth, speech acts as the vehicle through which character is revealed, relationships are forged, and covenant loyalty (chesed) is expressed. Naomi’s words in Ruth 2:22 carry the weight of elder wisdom and familial stewardship, guiding Ruth in her path of safety and integration into Israelite life.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb 'amar primarily means to say, speak, utter, tell, or command. In its basic Qal stem, it denotes the act of verbal communication between parties. Depending on the context, it can carry the nuance of:

• Declaring or pronouncing a judgment or observation.

• Commanding or instructing a subordinate.

• Intending or thinking within oneself (literally "saying in one's heart").

• Offering wise counsel, advice, or instruction.

In Ruth 2:22, the verb is in the Qal imperfect consecutive form (wa-to'mer), signaling the next sequential action in the dialogue: Naomi's active, directive response to Ruth's report.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The word 'amar is used over 5,000 times in the Hebrew Bible. While it often functions as a simple narrative transition ("and he said"), its theological depth is revealed in how speech acts create reality:

• Divine Speech: In Genesis 1, God "said" ('amar) "Let there be light," and creation responded. Here, 'amar is the sovereign instrument of creation and order.

• Self-Deliberation: When a person "says in his heart" (as in Genesis 17:17), 'amar represents internal reasoning or silent belief.

• Instructional Counsel: In wisdom literature and family narratives, when a parent or elder "says" something to a child, it represents the transmission of Torah-aligned wisdom and boundary-setting.

4. Theological Implications

Speech in the book of Ruth reflects the unseen hand of God. God rarely speaks directly in this narrative; instead, His will and character are mediated through the godly speech of His people. When Naomi speaks to Ruth in Ruth 2:22, she speaks with the wisdom of a restored faith. Earlier, Naomi spoke words of bitterness (Ruth 1:20-21). Now, acknowledging God's kindness through Boaz, her speech transforms into protective, redemptive counsel. Human words spoken in alignment with God’s law become the instruments of His providential care.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:22, Naomi's "saying" is a crucial turning point of affirmation and boundary-setting. Ruth has just recounted how Boaz told her to stay close to his young men (Ruth 2:21). Naomi refines and corrects this slightly, advising Ruth to stay with his "young women," recognizing the vulnerability of a young, foreign widow in other fields. Naomi's 'amar here functions as authoritative, loving maternal protection. She confirms that Boaz's offer is "good," validating the providential open door and commanding Ruth to maintain this safe boundary so that others do not assault her. Her speech serves to safeguard the line of redemption that is beginning to unfold.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 1:3 - "Then God said, 'Let there be light'; and there was light."

• Ruth 1:16 - "But Ruth said: 'Entreat me not to leave you, or to turn back from following after you...'"

• Ruth 2:5 - "Then Boaz said to his servant who was in charge of the reapers, 'Whose young woman is this?'"

• Ruth 2:21 - "Ruth the Moabitess said, 'He also said to me, "You shall stay close by my young men until they have finished all my harvest."'"

• Psalm 14:1 - "The fool has said in his heart, 'There is no God.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between Naomi’s speech in Chapter 1 and Chapter 2 is striking. In Chapter 1, Naomi uses 'amar to turn her daughters-in-law away, telling them to return to their mothers' houses because she has no hope to offer. In Chapter 2, her 'amar is welcoming, strategic, and protective. This shift highlights her journey from despair to hope, showing how a heart aligned with God's grace begins to speak words of life, wisdom, and safety to those under its care.
