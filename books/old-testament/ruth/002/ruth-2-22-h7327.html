RUTH - ( Rut , Strong's H7327 )

Summary:

The name Ruth represents friendship, companionship, or association. As the protagonist of her narrative, Ruth exemplifies her name through unwavering loyalty, sacrificial love, and covenant faithfulness. In Ruth 2:22, her identity as a dedicated companion is brought to the forefront as she acts to preserve her family unit with Naomi. Rather than remaining an outsider, her character transforms her name into a portrait of redemptive grace, demonstrating how faith and devotion bridge the gap between foreign alienation and covenant inclusion.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The name Ruth (Rut) is widely understood as a contraction of re'ut, which means "companion," "friend," or "partner."

• This noun shares its root with ra'ah, meaning to associate with, tend, pasture, or be a friend.

• Historically and culturally, Ruth was a Moabitess, a member of a nation born out of incestuous origins and traditionally hostile to Israel.

• In the ancient Near East, names carried deep prophetic and character-defining weight. For a Moabitess to bear a name meaning "friend" or "companion" in Hebrew scripture sets up a profound narrative irony that is resolved through her absolute fidelity to her Judean mother-in-law.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The proper noun refers specifically to the Moabitess daughter-in-law of Naomi and wife of Boaz.

• The etymological definition means "friendship" or "female companion."

• The word denotes a relational partner who is bound by affection, duty, or covenant. It emphasizes proximity, mutual support, and voluntary association.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The name occurs 12 times in the Old Testament, exclusively within the Book of Ruth.

• The ultimate word picture of "Ruth" is her actual life and choices. She models the Hebrew concept of chesed—loyal, covenant-keeping love that goes far beyond the letter of the law.

• While her sister-in-law Orpah (meaning "back of the neck" or "stiff-necked") turns her back to return to Moab, Ruth clings to Naomi. She visually and physically embodies the role of a true companion by walking the dusty road to Bethlehem, abandoning her homeland, her gods, and her security.

4. Theological Implications

• Sovereign Grace and Inclusion: Ruth's presence in the narrative demonstrates that faith, not pedigree, determines one’s place in God’s kingdom. She is a foreign woman who is grafted into the lineage of David and ultimately Jesus Christ.

• The Character of God’s Kingdom: Through her name and actions, Ruth shows that godliness is characterized by faithful companionship. God uses human relationships, characterized by loyal love, to channel His providential care to the destitute and broken.

• Typology of the Church: As a Gentile bride who finds refuge under the wings of the God of Israel and is redeemed by Boaz (the kinsman-redeemer), Ruth serves as a type of the Church—redeemed from foreign exile into intimate covenant partnership with God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 2:22, Naomi addresses Ruth after her first day of gleaning in the fields of Boaz.

• Ruth has returned with an abundance of food and news of Boaz's protection. Naomi’s response, "It is good, my daughter, that you go out with his young women," highlights the transition from vulnerability to safety.

• Within this dialogue, the name Ruth underscores her active role as Naomi’s companion and provider. She is no longer just a foreign burden; she is the active agent of blessing. The context highlights that her safety is essential because she is the singular companion through whom Naomi's hope and lineage will be restored.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Ruth 1:14: "Then they lifted up their voices and wept again; and Orpah kissed her mother-in-law, but Ruth clung to her."

• Ruth 1:16: "But Ruth said: 'Entreat me not to leave you, Or to turn back from following after you; For wherever you go, I will go; And wherever you lodge, I will lodge; Your people shall be my people, And your God, my God.'"

• Ruth 2:2: "So Ruth the Moabitess said to Naomi, 'Please let me go to the field, and glean heads of grain after him in whose sight I may find favor.' And she said to her, 'Go, my daughter.'"

• Ruth 4:13: "So Boaz took Ruth and she became his wife; and when he went in to her, the LORD gave her conception, and she bore a son."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The canonical placement of the Book of Ruth directly after Judges in the English Bible provides a stark contrast. During a period of moral anarchy where "everyone did what was right in his own eyes," Ruth provides a brilliant, quiet testimony of quiet faithfulness and genuine companionship.

• In the Hebrew Bible (the Tanakh), Ruth is placed in the Ketuvim (Writings) immediately after the Book of Proverbs. This creates a powerful connection, as the Virtuous Woman (Eshet Chayil) described in Proverbs 31 is immediately exemplified in the historical person of Ruth, who is explicitly called a "virtuous woman" in Ruth 3:11.
