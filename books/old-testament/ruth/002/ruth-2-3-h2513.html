PART - ( chelqah , Strong's H2513 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word chelqah refers to a portion, plot, or designated tract of land. Derived from a root meaning "to divide" or "to apportion," it indicates an allotted piece of territory belonging to a specific owner. In the narrative of Ruth, her arrival at the specific "part" of the field belonging to Boaz highlights the exquisite intersection of human daily labor and sovereign divine providence, turning what seemed like a random occurrence into a redemption story.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word chelqah is closely tied to the agrarian culture of ancient Israel. It is derived from the root chalaq, which means to divide, share, or distribute, and also carries a secondary nuance of "making smooth." In the ancient Near East, lands were not usually divided by tall fences or stone walls. Instead, a large agricultural field was shared among various families, with individual plots or "parts" (chelqah) demarcated by small boundary stones or landmarks. To step onto a specific chelqah was to enter the legal jurisdiction, economic space, and protection of a particular household. This term highlights the physical reality of the Promised Land as a divided inheritance, where every family had a designated portion assigned to them by covenant.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meaning of chelqah is a portion, tract, plot, or parcel of land. Depending on the context, the word can refer to:

• A specific plot of ground cultivated for agriculture.

• A portion of land designated for a inheritance.

• A parcel of ground purchased for a specific use, such as a burial site.

• In rare poetic settings, derived from its secondary root meaning, it can refer to "smoothness" or "flattery" (e.g., smooth words), though its narrative use is overwhelmingly geographical.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Boundary Stone and Covenant Inheritance: In Israel, land was a gift from God, partitioned among the tribes. The chelqah was a tangible representation of a family’s standing in the covenant community. Stealing or shifting the boundary stone of a neighbor’s chelqah was strictly forbidden and carried a divine curse, illustrating how sacred these boundaries were.

• The Plot of Cultivation: A chelqah was not a wild wilderness; it was an active plot of ground meant for plowing, sowing, and reaping. It represents a place of labor, stewardship, and fruitfulness.

• The Field of Redemption: In the book of Ruth, the chelqah represents the stage where God’s covenant laws—specifically gleaning and land redemption—meet human obedience.

4. Theological Implications

• Sovereign Alignment: The term chelqah demonstrates that God’s sovereign care operates within highly specific, physical boundaries. Ruth did not just happen upon a random field; she came to the exact part of the field owned by Boaz. God directs the steps of His people to precise locations where His provision is waiting.

• Divine Ownership: Ultimately, all land belonged to Yahweh. The Israelites were tenants on God’s land. Therefore, the division of the land into chelqah portions was a reminder that God had personally distributed His resources to sustain His people.

• The Stage for Redemption: The preservation of a family's chelqah was the driving force behind the laws of the kinsman-redeemer (goel). When a family fell into poverty and had to sell their chelqah, it was the duty of a close relative to buy it back, keeping the inheritance alive.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:3, the text states that Ruth "happened to come to the part of the field belonging to Boaz." The narrator uses a Hebrew idiom that literally translates to "her hap happened." From Ruth's perspective, she was simply looking for a safe place to glean and keep herself and Naomi alive. She did not plan this destination. However, the designation of this specific chelqah (part of the field) reveals the hand of God working behind the scenes. Her "chance" arrival on the plot of Boaz—the very man who had the legal right and moral willingness to act as her kinsman-redeemer—proves that what humans view as coincidence is actually God’s providence. The boundaries of Boaz's chelqah became the boundaries of God's sanctuary and provision for Ruth.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 33:19: "And he bought the parcel of a field where he had pitched his tent, from the children of Hamor, Shechem’s father, for one hundred pieces of money."

• Joshua 24:32: "The bones of Joseph, which the children of Israel had brought up out of Egypt, they buried at Shechem, in the plot of ground which Jacob had bought from the sons of Hamor the father of Shechem for one hundred pieces of silver, and which had become an inheritance of the children of Joseph."

• 2 Samuel 23:12: "But he stationed himself in the middle of the plot, defended it, and killed the Philistines. So the LORD brought about a great victory."

• 2 Kings 9:21: "Then Joram said, 'Make ready.' And his chariot was made ready. Then Joram king of Judah and Ahaziah king of Judah went out, each in his chariot; and they went out to meet Jehu, and met him on the plot of Naboth the Jezreelite."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The contrast between Naboth’s faithful stewardship of his chelqah in 1 Kings 21 and Ahab's greedy desire to seize it shows how highly a plot of land was valued. Naboth refused to sell his chelqah because it was his ancestral inheritance from Yahweh.

• The story of Eleazar, one of David’s mighty men, defending a chelqah (plot) of barley in 1 Chronicles 11:13-14 highlights how even a small piece of land was worth fighting for, as it represented God's provision for the community.
