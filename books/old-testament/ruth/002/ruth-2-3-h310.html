AFTER - ( achar , Strong's H310 )

Summary:

The Hebrew preposition achar (often occurring in its plural form acharey) primarily denotes physical position ("behind" or "following") or temporal sequence ("after" or "afterward"). In the context of Ruth 2:3, it establishes Ruth's physical location as she gleans "behind" the harvesters, portraying her deep humility, vulnerability, and dependence. While it highlights her low social standing as a destitute foreigner relying on the gleaning laws of Israel, it also serves as a beautiful picture of divine providence. As Ruth contentedly follows "after" the reapers, God's sovereign hand goes before her, leading her to the field of Boaz, her kinsman-redeemer.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root verb of achar carries the idea of remaining behind, delaying, tarrying, or procrastinating. As a preposition or adverb, it transitions from this passive sense of lagging behind to an active position of following in sequence or location. In the agrarian culture of the ancient Near East, the position "after" the reapers was reserved for the most marginalized members of society—widows, orphans, and resident aliens. Reapers quickly cut down the standing grain, and those following "after" them gathered the stray stalks left behind. It was a position requiring physical stamina, patience, and a willingness to accept the scraps of others.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• achar has three primary areas of meaning:

• Spatial Locative: Expressing physical placement "behind" or "at the back of" someone or something (e.g., standing behind a wall, walking behind a leader).

• Temporal Sequence: Expressing time, meaning "afterwards," "after these things," or "subsequent to" an event.

• Relational/Metaphorical: Expressing allegiance, loyalty, or pursuit, such as "following after" a leader, "going after" other gods, or "walking after" the Lord.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament frequently uses achar to construct vivid relational pictures:

• Following in Covenant Loyalty: To "walk after" Yahweh is a standard covenantal idiom for obedience, love, and total allegiance. It depicts a sheep following its shepherd or a disciple walking in the dust of their rabbi.

• Spiritual Straying: Conversely, "going after" other gods depicts spiritual adultery, where Israel leaves the protective care of Yahweh to pursue empty idols.

• Protection and Deliverance: In military contexts, those who march "after" a champion are protected by his strength. The position of being "behind" implies a reliance on the one who goes before to clear the path.

4. Theological Implications

The theological significance of achar in the book of Ruth lies in the intersection of human humility and divine providence. Ruth's physical posture of being "after" the reapers reflects her spiritual posture of surrender and trust. She does not demand rights; she seeks favor.

Furthermore, this word illustrates the concept of God's quiet sovereignty. Ruth cannot see what is ahead; she only sees the backs of the reapers and the dirt at her feet. Yet, as she walks "after" them, she is walking directly into the unfolding plan of redemption. What seems like a position of disadvantage is actually the pathway to the lineage of King David and, ultimately, Jesus Christ.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:3, Ruth "gleaned in the field after the reapers." This specific use of achar emphasizes her vulnerability. Gleaning directly behind young male reapers was dangerous for a foreign woman, which is why Boaz later instructs his workers not to touch or insult her. By placing herself "after" them, Ruth displays remarkable courage and sacrificial love for Naomi. She occupies the lowest station in the field, yet this lowliness is the very space where grace meets her. Boaz notices her precisely because of her humble posture "behind" his workers.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 22:13: "Then Abraham lifted his eyes and looked, and there behind him was a ram caught in a thicket by its horns; so Abraham went and took the ram, and offered it up for a burnt offering instead of his son."

• Deuteronomy 13:4: "You shall walk after the LORD your God and fear Him, and keep His commandments and obey His voice; you shall serve Him and hold fast to Him."

• Ruth 2:9: "Let your eyes be on the field which they reap, and go after them. Have I not commanded the young men not to touch you? And when you are thirsty, go to the vessels and drink from what the young men have drawn."

• Genesis 37:17: "So Joseph went after his brothers and found them in Dothan."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The position of being "after" the reapers was protected by the Mosaic Law. Leviticus 19:9-10 and 23:22 forbade landowners from reaping the very corners of their fields or gathering the gleanings of the harvest; these were to be left "after" them for the poor and the stranger. Therefore, Ruth’s position "after" the reapers is a direct testimony to the grace of God's law, which provided a legal right for the marginalized to find sustenance in Israel.
