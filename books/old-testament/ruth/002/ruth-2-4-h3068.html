LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh (rendered as "LORD" in all capital letters in most English translations) is the personal, covenant-keeping name of the God of Israel. Revealed most famously to Moses at the burning bush, this name denotes God's self-existence, eternal presence, and unwavering faithfulness to His promises. In the book of Ruth, and specifically in the greeting of Boaz in Ruth 2:4, the use of Yahweh transforms a routine agricultural interaction into a sacred, covenantal exchange, reminding us that God is actively present in the ordinary rhythms of daily life.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The name Yahweh is the personal name of God, often referred to as the Tetragrammaton because it consists of four Hebrew consonants: Y-H-W-H. Historically, this name is connected to the Hebrew verb hayah, which means "to be" or "to exist." It is the name God used to identify Himself to Moses in Exodus 3:14: "I AM WHO I AM." Unlike generic titles for deity such as Elohim (which emphasizes God's power as Creator), Yahweh is God's intimate, proper name. It represents His self-revelation to His chosen people and establishes a personal, relational bond.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name signifies the eternal, self-existent, and unchangeable One who is the source of all life and being. It defines God not merely as an abstract philosophical concept of existence, but as the actively present God who is dynamically involved in the history of His people. The primary lexical definition centers on God's absolute independence and His self-determined, faithful commitment to His creation and His covenant partners.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Occurring over 6,800 times in the Hebrew Bible, Yahweh is by far the most frequent name for God in the Old Testament.

• It is used in contexts highlighting redemption, covenant stipulations, and divine promises.

• It is associated with rich word pictures: Yahweh is described as a shepherd who guides and feeds His flock, a shield of protection, a strong tower of safety, and a faithful husband to Israel.

• When contrasted with Elohim, which highlights transcendence, Yahweh emphasizes immanence—God drawing near to humanity, making promises, and listening to prayers.

4. Theological Implications

• Covenant Faithfulness: Yahweh is the God who keeps His word even when His people fail. His grace and mercy are bound to this name.

• Sovereignty and Self-Sufficiency: Because He is "I AM," He relies on nothing outside of Himself. He is the ultimate reality and the sustainer of all things.

• Holy Presence: The name implies that God is present to save, judge, and dwell among His people. He is not a distant clockmaker but an active participant in human affairs.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:4, Boaz arrives from Bethlehem and greets his reapers: "The LORD [Yahweh] be with you!" and they respond, "The LORD [Yahweh] bless you!"

• In the dark, chaotic era of the Judges—a period characterized by spiritual decline, moral anarchy, and social breakdown—this exchange shines as a beacon of light.

• It reveals that the covenant faith of Israel was not confined to tabernacle rituals but was actively lived out in daily labor and labor relations.

• By invoking the name Yahweh, Boaz recognizes that even the mundane work of harvesting barley is under the sovereign care and blessing of the covenant God.

• It sets the theological tone for the rest of the narrative: the unfolding story of Ruth's survival and redemption is not a matter of luck or chance, but the quiet, providential hand of Yahweh working through the lives of ordinary, faithful people.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 3:15 - "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

• Psalm 23:1 - "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

• Proverbs 18:10 - "The name of the LORD is a strong tower; The righteous run to it and are safe."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• Due to extreme reverence for the sacred name (to avoid taking it in vain, in accordance with the Third Commandment), ancient Jewish readers substituted the spoken word Adonai (Lord) when reading the Hebrew text. This practice is why modern English translations continue to render Yahweh as "LORD" in capital letters.

• In the book of Ruth, God rarely speaks directly or performs overt, spectacular miracles. Instead, His presence (Yahweh) is felt and made visible through the faith-filled words, kindness, and obedience of characters like Boaz, Naomi, and Ruth.
