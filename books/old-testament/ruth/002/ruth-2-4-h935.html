CAME - ( bo' , Strong's H0935 )

Summary:

In Ruth 2:4, the Hebrew verb bo' (translated as "came") marks the sudden, providential arrival of Boaz at his harvest field at the precise moment Ruth is gleaning. While the verb itself is a common term of motion meaning to come, go, or arrive, its placement in the narrative undergirds the theme of divine sovereignty. God orchestrates the paths of the vulnerable widow and the wealthy redeemer to intersect perfectly, turning a routine journey into a life-altering meeting of grace.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root bo' represents basic, purposeful movement toward a destination. In the agricultural life of ancient Israel, the arrival of the landowner at the harvest field was a moment of supreme authority, responsibility, and leadership. The owner did not merely visit; he arrived to supervise the work, protect his estate, and ensure that the poor-laws of Israel—such as allowing the stranger and widow to glean—were being uprightly observed. The word conveys the physical entry of a person of influence into a space of vulnerability.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The Hebrew verb bo' is a primary root meaning to come, enter, or arrive. Occurring over 2,500 times in the Old Testament, it has a broad semantic range that includes physical entry into a place, the setting of the sun, and the occurrence of predicted events. In the active Qal stem used in Ruth 2:4, it indicates straightforward, physical arrival at a destination. It represents a completed action that alters the current state of a scene, bringing a new subject into direct interaction with the existing environment.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Physical Entry: bo' frequently denotes entering a tent, home, or city, often symbolizing safety, intimacy, or association.

• Temporal Transit: The verb describes the setting of the sun (ba' hashamesh), representing the transition from day to night and the completion of a daily cycle.

• Covenantal Entering: The term is used metaphorically for entering into an oath or a covenant relationship with God.

• Fulfillment of Prophecy: It is used to describe events or blessings "coming to pass" or arriving at their appointed times.

4. Theological Implications

The arrival of Boaz represents the intersection of human action and divine timing. Ruth "happened" to glean in his field (Ruth 2:3), and immediately Boaz "came" (Ruth 2:4). This highlights the doctrine of providence. God does not need spectacular miracles to deliver His people; He aligns the ordinary movements of ordinary men to accomplish His extraordinary redemptive purposes. The coming of Boaz is the answer to Naomi’s empty hands, proving that Yahweh has not abandoned His covenant loyalty (chesed).

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:4, the narrative pairs bo' with the exclamation ve-hinneh ("and behold"). This literary construction grabs the reader's attention, shifting the scene immediately from Ruth’s labor to Boaz's sudden arrival. His entry is not a mere footnote; it is the turning point of the entire story. It demonstrates that the redeemer is actively present, and his first words ("The LORD be with you!") establish that his coming brings the blessings of Yahweh's covenant into the lives of those under his care.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The root bo' is also the basis for the common Hebrew greeting barukh haba ("blessed is he who comes"). Boaz’s coming to redeem his land and care for the vulnerable stranger prefigures the arrival of the ultimate Redeemer, Jesus Christ, who "came" into the world not to be served, but to serve, and to give His life as a ransom for many.
