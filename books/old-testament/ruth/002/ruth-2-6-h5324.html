CHARGE - ( natsab , Strong's H5324 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word natsab in Ruth 2:6 describes an appointed position of established authority, stewardship, and responsibility. Stationed as a foreman over the reapers, this servant represents structured order, reliable oversight, and the faithful execution of his master’s will. In the narrative of Ruth, his position acts as a crucial link of protective stewardship, ensuring that the vulnerable find favor and provision in the fields of Boaz.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The verb natsab fundamentally conveys the physical act of standing upright, taking a firm stand, or being set firmly in place. It evokes the image of a pillar, a monument, or a soldier stationed at a post. Unlike verbs that mean a casual standing, natsab denotes a deliberate, established, and unyielding posture. In the ancient agricultural world, the foreman of the harvest was not merely a laborer but a designated sentinel. He was "stationed" at his post to safeguard the crop, maintain order among the workers, and act as the eyes and ears of the landowner.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

In its basic form, natsab means to take a stand, stand erect, or establish. In the Niphal stem, it functions as a participle meaning "stationed over," "appointed," or "officer." It describes someone who has been formally positioned in an office of supervision. The term implies stability, duty, and official delegation. The one "in charge" (nitstab) does not rule in his own right but stands as a deputy, embodying the presence and authority of the master who placed him there.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The word depicts monuments or altars set up to endure, signaling permanence and covenant (Genesis 35:14).

• It describes the parting of the Red Sea, where the waters stood upright like a wall, showing supernatural firmness and structure (Exodus 15:8).

• It is used for military or guard duty, where a sentinel stands watch, vigilant and unmoving (1 Samuel 14:9).

• In administrative records, it designates royal deputies or governors appointed by the king to manage provinces and secure resources (1 Kings 22:47).

4. Theological Implications

• Stewardship under Authority: The foreman's role illustrates the biblical pattern of delegated authority. Human leaders, ministers, and pastors are "stationed" by God to care for His flock and His harvest. They are accountable to the Master of the Harvest.

• Providential Ordering: God uses established structures and reliable servants to accomplish His redemptive purposes. The presence of an orderly structure in Boaz's field ensured that the poor could glean in safety, reflecting God’s law of compassion.

• Faithfulness in the Ordinary: The foreman's quiet competence demonstrates that secular labor, when done under the fear of God, becomes a conduit of divine grace.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:6, the foreman’s designation as the one "in charge" highlights his gatekeeping role. Ruth could not simply wander into the field without oversight; she had to ask this positioned servant for permission. His character is revealed in how he executed his charge. He was observant, noting Ruth's background as "the young Moabite woman" and her industrious character ("she has continued from morning until now"). By presenting an honest, favorable report to Boaz, the foreman acted as a bridge of grace. His firm, reliable standing in his office protected Ruth from harassment and paved the way for her redemption.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 37:7 – "For behold, we were binding sheaves in the field. Then behold, my sheaf arose and also stood upright; and indeed your sheaves stood around and bowed down to my sheaf."

• Exodus 15:8 – "And with the blast of Your nostrils / The waters were gathered together; / The floods stood upright like a heap; / The depths congealed in the heart of the sea."

• 1 Kings 22:47 – "There was then no king in Edom, only a deputy was king."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The root of natsab is closely linked to matstsebah, a sacred pillar or memorial stone. This connection suggests that those whom God places in charge are meant to be living monuments of His order, faithfulness, and truth. In the New Testament, the pastoral office reflects a similar call to be "stationed" as stewards of the household of God, maintaining order and extending Christ's care to the vulnerable.
