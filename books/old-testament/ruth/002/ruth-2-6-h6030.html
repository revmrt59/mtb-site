ANSWERED - ( 'anah , Strong's H6030 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'anah primarily means to answer, respond, or testify. In the context of Ruth 2:6, it denotes a direct, informative, and respectful reply to an inquiry from a superior. This word highlights the themes of divine providence and human cooperation, as the servant's accurate answer identifies Ruth to Boaz, setting in motion the chain of redemptive events that leads to her provision, her marriage, and ultimately the lineage of King David and Jesus Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root 'anah carries the conceptual background of relational reciprocity and responsiveness. It is not merely the production of speech, but a targeted reaction to a vocal stimulus, a situation, or a person. In ancient Near Eastern culture, speech was relational and carried covenantal weight. To answer someone was to enter into a mutual space of responsibility, acknowledging the speaker and validating their query. The word implies attentive listening followed by a purposeful declaration, creating a verbal bridge that connects the initiator with the respondent.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb 'anah means to answer, respond, declare, or testify. Lexically, it refers to speaking in response to a statement, a question, or a crying need. The word has a broad semantic range that includes simple conversational replies, legal testimonies in a judicial setting (where a witness "answers" charges or testifies to the truth), and liturgical responses in worship. It is also the primary verb used to describe God responding to the prayers and cries of His people, illustrating His active engagement in human history.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Conversational Responsiveness: The most common usage of the word is to maintain dialogue by offering a reply to a question, keeping communication flowing between parties.

• Judicial Testimony: In legal contexts, 'anah represents giving formal testimony. To "answer" in court was to establish legal facts, rendering a verdict of truth or falsehood.

• Divine Intervention: A beautiful word picture emerges when God answers humanity. It signifies that the Creator of the universe is not silent or indifferent; His "answering" is an act of salvation, deliverance, and covenant faithfulness.

4. Theological Implications

Theological usage of 'anah reveals a God who desires dialogue and relationship with His creation. Unlike the silent idols of the nations, Yahweh hears and responds to His people. Consequently, human responsiveness to God's voice is a core covenant duty; when God calls, humanity must "answer" in faith and obedience. In the book of Ruth, human speech reflects this divine attribute of faithful responsiveness. The characters speak to one another with grace, truth, and respect, showing how God's quiet providence operates through the responsible, everyday conversations of righteous people.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:6, Boaz notices a new woman gleaning in his field and asks his foreman, "Whose young woman is this?" The foreman answered ('anah) with key identifying details. The foreman does not dismiss Ruth as an insignificant foreigner or a trespasser. Instead, his response is informative, objective, and respectful, identifying her as the young Moabite woman who returned with Naomi. This honest and prompt answer acts as a crucial link in God's providence. By answering Boaz accurately, the servant facilitates the connection between Ruth's vulnerability and Boaz's redeeming grace, demonstrating how even minor characters carrying out routine duties with integrity serve God's redemptive purposes.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 41:16: "So Joseph answered Pharaoh, saying, 'It is not in me; God will give Pharaoh an answer of peace.'" (Joseph's response deflects praise from himself and points directly to God as the source of true answers).

• Joshua 24:16: "So the people answered and said: 'Far be it from us that we should forsake the LORD to serve other gods;'" (The collective answer of Israel serves as a formal covenant vow of loyalty to Yahweh).

• Job 40:3: "Then Job answered the LORD and said:" (After experiencing God's majestic speech, Job's verbal response is one of self-humbling and awe).

7. Other Relevant Insights

A striking thematic connection exists in Hebrew homonyms. While 'anah (H6030, to answer) is linguistically distinct from 'anah (H6031, to be afflicted, humbled, or bowed down), their sounds are identical. Throughout the Old Testament, when God's people are afflicted ('anah H6031) and cry out, God answers ('anah H6030) them. In the narrative of Ruth, Naomi and Ruth are deeply afflicted, yet through the faithful, compassionate answers and actions of those around them, God answers their affliction with redemption and joy.
