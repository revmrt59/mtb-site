MORNING - ( boqer , Strong's H1242 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun boqer refers to the dawn, the break of day, or the early morning hours, signifying the transition from darkness to light and the beginning of daily activity. In Ruth 2:7, it highlights Ruth’s immediate, diligent, and uninterrupted labor from the very start of the workday, underscoring her remarkable work ethic and devotion to providing for Naomi.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word boqer stems from a root verb meaning "to plow," "to break through," or "to inspect/seek." Culturally and visually, the morning represents the breakthrough of light parting the dark canvas of night. In the agrarian world of ancient Israel, the morning was not merely a marker of time but the daily signal to begin life-sustaining labor. The arrival of boqer brought the end of vulnerability associated with the night and heralded the start of purposeful activity, worship, and community interaction.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun boqer defines the point of sunrise, the dawn, or the early morning season of the day. It represents:

• The precise boundary line when darkness gives way to light.

• The early hours of the day up until noon, when the sun reaches its zenith.

• A marker of the next day, often translated in temporal phrases as "tomorrow morning" or "by morning."

• In contrast to ereb (evening), it serves as the opening bracket of the daytime.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament employs boqer in several vivid conceptual categories:

• The Rhythm of Creation: In Genesis 1, the completion of each creative epoch is marked by the phrase "evening and morning," demonstrating that God establishes order, light, and progression out of darkness.

• The Altar of Devotion: The daily tabernacle and temple services required a morning sacrifice. The morning symbolized the dedication of the day's firstfruits to God, establishing a pattern of seeking Him before engaging in worldly tasks.

• The Arrival of Deliverance: Morning is frequently depicted as the time when God’s judgment ends and His mercy arrives. Joy, rescue, and divine vindication consistently "come in the morning" after a dark season of trial.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, boqer is linked directly to the character of God. Lamentations declares that God’s mercies are "new every morning," illustrating that His covenant faithfulness (chesed) is as reliable and constant as the daily sunrise. The morning stands as a physical sermon of hope; no matter how deep the darkness of night, the light of God's presence will break through. It points forward to the ultimate dawn of redemption, where the light of the Messiah dispels the darkness of sin and death.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:7, the foreman of the reapers reports to Boaz concerning Ruth: "So she came and has continued from morning until now, except that she rested a little in the house."

Here, boqer emphasizes Ruth’s extraordinary diligence and initiative. Gleaning was grueling, humiliating work reserved for the destitute. Rather than arriving late in the day or working half-heartedly, Ruth was in the fields at the first break of dawn (boqer). This detail highlights her proactive love for Naomi; she did not wait to be forced but immediately went to work. The contrast between her early start at boqer and her brief rest in the shelter portrays her not as a passive victim of tragedy, but as a woman of profound character, stamina, and resolve.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 1:5: "God called the light Day, and the darkness He called Night. So the evening and the morning were the first day."

• Exodus 12:10: "You shall let none of it remain until morning, and what remains of it until morning you shall burn with fire."

• Psalm 30:5: "For His anger is but for a moment, His favor is for life; Weeping may endure for a night, But joy comes in the morning."

• Lamentations 3:23: "They are new every morning; Great is Your faithfulness."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In ancient Middle Eastern agriculture, harvesting began as early as possible to take advantage of the cool morning air before the oppressive midday heat set in. Ruth’s presence at the field from boqer onwards shows she integrated seamlessly into the demanding schedule of the professional reapers. Her early arrival was her testimony; before she ever spoke a word to Boaz, her morning-to-midday work ethic declared her nobility of character.
