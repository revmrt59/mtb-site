FROM - ( 'az , Strong's H0227 )

Summary:

The Hebrew adverb 'az primarily means "then" or "at that time," pointing to a specific, identifiable moment. When combined with the preposition min ("from"), it forms the compound mē-’āz, which translates as "since" or "from that time." In Ruth 2:7, this word emphasizes the uninterrupted duration of Ruth's labor, marking the precise starting point of her work ("from morning") and highlighting her extraordinary diligence, endurance, and character before Boaz and his reapers.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word 'az is a temporal demonstrative adverb used in Biblical Hebrew to draw attention to a specific point in time, distinguishing it from the present. When prefixed with the preposition min ("from"), the resulting term mē-’āz shifts the focus from a single point in time to a continuous duration flowing out of that point. Historically and culturally, agricultural work in Israel was demanding, beginning at the earliest light of dawn. By specifying that Ruth worked mē-’āz (since/from) the morning, the narrator establishes a physical boundary of time that showcases Ruth's relentless commitment to providing for Naomi.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The adverb 'az denotes a specific point in time, past or future, meaning "then," "at that time," or "thereupon." When joined with the preposition min, the compound mē-’āz functions as a preposition or conjunction meaning "since," "from the time of," or "from of old." Lexically, it signifies the initiation of an ongoing state or action. In the context of Ruth 2:7, mē-’āz habbōqer translates literally to "from then of the morning" or "since the morning," pointing to the dawn as the absolute commencement of her labor.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

In the Old Testament, 'az and its compound form mē-’āz are used to construct temporal frameworks for historical events and personal testimonies.

• It defines historical starting points, such as "from that time" or "since of old," often used to describe God's ancient decrees or the long-standing existence of nations.

• It illustrates sudden shifts in narratives, signaling a decisive moment when an action occurred ("then he spoke" or "then they feared").

• The word picture in Ruth 2:7 is one of unbroken continuity. It establishes a starting line at daybreak, creating a vivid contrast with her continued presence "until now." The term acts as a temporal anchor, proving that her effort was not a brief burst of energy but a sustained, heroic endeavor.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, 'az and mē-’āz underscore the reality of God's sovereignty working through concrete historical moments and human faithfulness. God's plans are described as being ordained "from of old" (mē-’āz), showing His eternal nature and unchanging purposes. On a human level, Ruth's diligent work "from morning" reflects the biblical concept of active faith. Her commitment to Naomi was not merely spoken; it was lived out in grueling, physical labor. This small temporal marker highlights how divine providence interacts with human responsibility—Ruth's faithful labor from the earliest hour placed her directly in the path of Boaz, which was central to God's redemptive plan for the lineage of David and ultimately the Messiah.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:7, the overseer of the reapers is speaking to Boaz, explaining who Ruth is and accounting for her actions. The phrase mē-’āz habbōqer ("from morning") carries significant contextual weight. The overseer highlights her diligence to Boaz: she arrived early, asked politely for permission to glean, and has been working continuously from the morning until that very moment, with only a brief rest in the shelter. The word 'az fixes the beginning of her labor at the earliest possible hour, emphasizing her industriousness and lack of entitlement. This testimony of her unwavering work ethic immediately commands Boaz’s respect and prompts his extraordinary favor toward her.

• Isaiah 16:13: "This is the word which the LORD has spoken concerning Moab since that time."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The compound mē-’āz is structurally a combination of the preposition min (from) and the adverb 'az (then). In the flow of Hebrew syntax, it turns a simple temporal point into a vector of time. In Ruth 2:7, the Masoretic text points mē-’āz with a maqqep (hyphen) to habbōqer (the morning), binding the two concepts tightly together as a single adverbial unit of time. This grammatical construction leaves no room for gaps; her labor was continuous and unbroken from the very start of the workday.
