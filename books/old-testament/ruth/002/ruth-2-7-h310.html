AFTER - ( 'achar , Strong's H0310 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'achar fundamentally denotes a spatial or temporal position of being "behind," "after," or "following." In Ruth 2:7, Ruth humbles herself by asking to glean "after" (behind) the reapers, positioning herself at the very rear of the harvest line. This word underscores her vulnerability, industriousness, and submission, while also highlighting the marvelous ways of God’s providence. By willingly taking the lowest place of a follower, Ruth is positioned to receive the abundant favor of her redeemer, Boaz.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete background of 'achar lies in the physical orientation of the human body. It designates the back part of an object or person, or the physical space directly behind them. In the ancient world, whether traveling through the wilderness or working in an agricultural field, someone had to lead, and others followed in their steps, occupying the space behind. To be "after" someone is to follow their path, depend on their movement, and receive whatever they leave behind. In ancient Near Eastern culture, walking "after" a leader, shepherd, or master was a vivid picture of loyalty, discipleship, and submission.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word functions primarily as a preposition, adverb, or conjunction. Its semantic range includes:

• Spatial: "behind," "at the back of," or "following after" a person or object.

• Temporal: "afterward," "after these things," or "subsequent to" a specific point in time.

• Relational/Metaphorical: "following after" in terms of allegiance, devotion, obedience, or imitation.

In its plural construct form ('acharei), it retains this sense of following closely behind.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Shepherd and the Flock: Sheep naturally walk 'achar (behind) their shepherd, relying entirely on his guidance, pathfinding, and protection.

• The Military Rear Guard: In military movements, the rear guard is described as coming 'achar (behind) the main body of troops, providing protection and securing the rear.

• Covenant Loyalty: To "walk after" Yahweh is the standard Old Testament idiom for covenant faithfulness, portraying Israel as a loyal follower walking in the footsteps of her divine King. Conversely, "going after" other gods represents spiritual adultery and wandering off the true path.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, 'achar represents the posture of discipleship, humility, and faith. God's people do not run ahead of Him; they walk after Him. True spiritual safety is found in the trailing position—letting the Lord lead, clear the path, and fight the battles, while the believer follows behind in obedience. Additionally, the word reveals God's tender care for those who are "behind" in society. The law of gleaning was specifically designed for those relegated to the rear of the fields—the poor, the widow, and the stranger—ensuring that those who followed 'achar the harvesters would still find life-sustaining nourishment.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:7, Ruth asks permission to glean and gather "after" the reapers. This is a highly nuanced request. As a foreign widow, Ruth has no legal leverage; she depends entirely on the grace of the landowner to let her glean. By asking to glean behind the reapers, she chooses a position of extreme physical labor, vulnerability, and social inferiority. She is exposed to the heat, the dust, and potential harassment from the young male workers. Yet, her willing adoption of this "after" position demonstrates her profound work ethic and her trust in the God of Israel. She does not demand to be at the front; she is content to take the leftovers behind the workers. It is precisely in this low, vulnerable position of following "after" that Boaz notices her, protects her, and ultimately elevates her.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 15:1: "After these things the word of the Lord came to Abram in a vision, saying, 'Do not be afraid, Abram. I am your shield, your exceedingly great reward.'"

• Genesis 22:13: "Then Abraham lifted his eyes and looked, and there behind him was a ram caught in a thicket by its horns. So Abraham went and took the ram, and offered it up for a burnt offering instead of his son."

• Deuteronomy 13:4: "You shall walk after the Lord your God and fear Him, and keep His commandments and obey His voice; you shall serve Him and hold fast to Him."

• Ruth 1:15: "And she said, 'Look, your sister-in-law has gone back to her people and to her gods; return after your sister-in-law.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

In ancient Near Eastern harvest customs, reapers were followed by binders who tied the grain into sheaves, who were then followed by gleaners. For Ruth to ask to gather among the sheaves directly "after" the reapers was an unusual request that went beyond standard gleaning rights, which usually occurred only after the entire harvest field was cleared. Her request to work intimately "after" the reapers shows both her desperate need and her bold faith. Boaz's subsequent command to his young men to let her glean even among the sheaves without reproach shows how her humble "after" position was met with extraordinary, boundary-breaking grace.
