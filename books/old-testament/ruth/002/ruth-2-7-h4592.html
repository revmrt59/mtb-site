LITTLE - ( me'at , Strong's H4592 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word me'at denotes a small quantity, a brief duration of time, or a few in number. In the context of Ruth 2:7, it highlights the briefness of Ruth's rest, emphasizing her extraordinary diligence and work ethic as she gleaned in the fields of Boaz. Out of a long, grueling day of physical labor under the Judean sun, she sat in the shelter for only a "little" while. This detail caught the attention of the foreman and ultimately Boaz himself, serving as an outward proof of her inner character and devotion to Naomi.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root meaning of me'at relates to smallness, diminution, or scarcity. It stands in direct contrast to Hebrew terms of abundance, greatness, or multitude.

• In the ancient Near Eastern agrarian culture, resources and time were highly valued and carefully measured. To describe an action, resource, or timeframe as me'at is to draw attention to its minimal footprint—it is the bare minimum, a tiny fraction of the whole.

• It often carries a comparative weight, highlighting how small something is in relation to a larger expectation or a surrounding environment.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Me'at functions as a noun, adjective, or adverb depending on the grammatical construction of the sentence.

• It signifies a small quantity when referring to physical measurements (e.g., a little water or a little food).

• It denotes a short period of time, translated frequently as "a little while" or "momentarily."

• It expresses fewness in number when quantifying people, years, or objects.

• In Ruth 2:7, the word acts adverbially in conjunction with her sitting or resting, pointing specifically to the very brief duration of her break: "except for a little that she sat in the house."

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Old Testament uses me'at to describe physical limitations and scarcity, such as a meager portion of food that must be stretched to survive, or the small amount of oil left in a jar.

• Temporally, it is used to depict a fleeting moment. Just as a shadow sweeps across a field and disappears, me'at highlights how quickly an event, a breath, or a lifespan passes.

• It is also used contrastively in Scripture to emphasize the magnifying power of God's blessing, where a "little" is miraculously multiplied into abundance, or where a "few" faithful individuals overcome a massive, overwhelming obstacle.

4. Theological Implications

• The use of me'at in the narrative of Ruth underscores the themes of divine providence, human character, and grace. Ruth did not leverage her status as a vulnerable, grieving widow to demand handouts or special treatment. Her brief rest—only a "little" while—reflects her deep commitment to provide for her mother-in-law, Naomi.

• In the broader biblical theology of work, diligence in the "little" things prepares an individual for greater spiritual and physical responsibilities. Ruth's faithfulness during a small, seemingly insignificant moment of rest serves as an enduring witness to her character.

• This term serves as a gentle reminder that God observes the smallest details of our daily labor, our moments of weariness, and our integrity when we think no one of importance is watching.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 2:7, the servant in charge of the reapers is speaking directly to Boaz, explaining who Ruth is and accounting for her conduct throughout the morning.

• The phrase "except for a little while that she sat in the house" refers to the temporary shade shelter or hut erected in the field for the workers to escape the heat.

• This description serves as a glowing endorsement of Ruth's character. The foreman is highly impressed; Ruth has been working relentlessly from morning until afternoon, taking only a minute, "little" break.

• This contextual detail validates Naomi’s praise of Ruth and justifies Boaz's immediate favor toward her. It proves she is a woman of valor who works with her hands with minimal pause, making her transition from a foreign outsider to a respected member of the community seamless and culturally honorable.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 18:4 - "Please let a little water be brought, and wash your feet; rest yourselves under the tree."

• Genesis 47:9 - "The days of the years of my pilgrimage are one hundred and thirty years; few and evil have been the days of the years of my life..."

• Psalm 8:5 - "For You have made him a little lower than the angels, and You have crowned him with glory and honor."

• Proverbs 6:10 - "A little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to sleep—"

• Proverbs 15:16 - "Better is a little with the fear of the Lord, than great treasure with trouble."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• There is a beautiful literary contrast in the Book of Ruth between the "little" (me'at) Ruth starts with—a small amount of hope, a small amount of food, a small break—and the overwhelming abundance (rab) she eventually receives under the protective wings of the Lord God of Israel.

• The "house" mentioned in Ruth 2:7 is not her home in Bethlehem, but a crude, temporary shelter in the fields. Sitting there only a "little" while under the blistering sun of the harvest season demonstrates extraordinary physical resilience and mental resolve.
