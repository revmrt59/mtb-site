SAID - ( 'amar , Strong's H559 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'amar is the primary Old Testament word meaning to say, speak, command, or think. In Ruth 2:7, this word introduces Ruth’s humble and polite petition to glean in the fields, reflecting her respectful character, vulnerable status, and reliance on the favor of others. Her spoken request serves as the catalyst for the unfolding of God’s providential care through Boaz.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root of 'amar relates to the externalizing of an internal thought, intention, or command. It is one of the most frequently used verbs in the Hebrew Bible, appearing over 5,000 times. In ancient Semitic culture, speech was not merely the transmission of cognitive data, but an active force that created relationships, bound covenants, and established realities. To speak was to initiate action. In the narrative of Ruth, speech acts as a primary vehicle for revealing the heart's motivations and moving the plot toward redemption.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meaning of 'amar is to say, speak, or utter. The semantic range includes several distinct nuances determined by the context:

• To speak or declare a message to another.

• To command or decree (most often used when God or a ruler is the speaker).

• To think or say within oneself (when followed by the phrase "in the heart").

• To promise, intend, or resolve to perform an action.

In Ruth 2:7, the verb appears in the Qal imperfect/wayyiqtol form ("and she said"), marking a specific, historical declaration of a polite and deferential request.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Power of Spoken Words: Throughout the Old Testament, 'amar carries the power to define situations. When a character speaks, it lays bare their integrity or folly.

• Creative Utterance: The ultimate word picture of 'amar is found in the creation narrative, where God speaks ('amar) light and life into existence, demonstrating that His speech is inherently creative and sovereign.

• Internal Dialogue: The word is often used to describe a person's inner thoughts, depicting the human mind as an internal conversation chamber where decisions are made before they are externalized.

4. Theological Implications

• The Posture of Grace: Ruth's spoken request ('amar) represents the posture of faith and humility. Although the Mosaic Law provided for the poor to glean, Ruth does not demand her rights; she asks permission. This illustrates how the needy approach the sovereign provider—with respect, hope, and humility.

• Providence in the Ordinary: God uses the ordinary spoken interactions of human beings to accomplish His grand redemptive purposes. Ruth's polite speech to the overseer of the reapers is later reported to Boaz, which sparks his protective interest.

• Covenant Faithfulness (Chesed): The book of Ruth shows that covenant love is manifested through spoken words of kindness, reassurance, and blessing. 'amar is the vehicle through which characters bless one another and promise protection.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 2:7, the foreman reports Ruth's request to Boaz, saying: "And she said, 'Please let me glean and gather after the reapers among the sheaves.'"

• The use of 'amar here introduces a request marked by the Hebrew particle of entreaty (na, translated as "Please"). This combination highlights her extreme courtesy. As a foreign woman and a destitute widow, she possesses no social standing, yet her spoken words command respect because of their dignity and modesty.

• Her speech stands in stark contrast to a demanding or entitled attitude. This verbal humility wins the favor of the foreman and ultimately the favor of Boaz.

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The repetition of speech throughout the second chapter of Ruth underscores the development of the relationship between Ruth and Boaz. Almost every significant shift in the plot is preceded by "and he/she said."

• Ruth’s speech reveals her quick assimilation into the godly culture of Israel, utilizing language that respects the boundaries of land ownership while appealing to the mercy of the landowner.
