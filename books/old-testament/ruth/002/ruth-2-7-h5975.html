CONTINUED - ( 'amad , Strong's H5975 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'amad primarily means "to stand," "to remain," "to endure," or "to stay." In the context of Ruth 2:7, translated as "continued" in the NKJV, it describes Ruth's persistent, unwavering labor in the harvest fields of Boaz. Rather than representing idle standing, her "standing" represents a steady, enduring presence and diligent work ethic. It paints a picture of a devoted woman who, driven by covenant love and survival, remains steadfastly at her task from early morning onward, earning the respect of the overseer and setting the stage for Boaz's favor.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete, physical picture behind 'amad is that of a person standing upright, firmly planted on their feet. It evokes the image of a pillar holding up a roof, a soldier standing at his post, or a servant waiting attentively on a master. To "stand" is the opposite of falling down, running away, or reclining in leisure. In an ancient near eastern agrarian society, standing in the open fields from morning until night was a grueling physical reality. It required immense strength, determination, and resilience. Ruth’s "standing" in the field was a visible expression of her inner resolve to support Naomi and secure their survival.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The root 'amad means to take one's stand, to stand still, to remain, or to persist. The semantic range of the word includes:

• To stand physically on one's feet (as opposed to sitting or lying down).

• To stand before a superior in a position of service, waiting for instructions.

• To stand firm or endure against an adversary or a trial.

• To remain, stay, or continue in a particular state, place, or activity over a duration of time.

In the Qal stem, which is used in Ruth 2:7, the verb emphasizes the active persistence of the subject. Ruth did not merely arrive at the field; she established a persistent, ongoing presence there, remaining steadfast in her gleaning.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Standing in Service: The word frequently depicts a priest standing before the Lord to minister, or a courtier standing before a monarch. This denotes readiness, submission, and faithful duty.

• Standing Firm in Battle: It describes a soldier refusing to retreat, maintaining his ground against the enemy.

• Stopping or Halting: It can describe motion coming to a standstill, such as the sun standing still in the sky.

• In the Book of Ruth: The term emphasizes the transition from Ruth's journey to her settled diligence. She did not just pass through the field; she "stood" to her task, working continuously with minimal rest.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, 'amad underlines the biblical virtue of steadfastness and covenant loyalty (chesed). Ruth’s persistence in the field is a practical demonstration of her pledge to Naomi in Ruth 1:16 ("Wherever you go, I will go"). Her physical endurance in gleaning reflects her spiritual trust in the God of Israel, under whose wings she had come to seek refuge. The narrative links human diligence with divine providence; as Ruth stands faithfully in the fields of Boaz, God stands ready to redeem her. It shows that faith is often expressed through quiet, ordinary, and enduring hard work.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:7, the servant in charge of the reapers is reporting Ruth’s conduct to Boaz. He notes her humility in asking for permission to glean and then highlights her extraordinary industry: "she came and has continued from morning until now." The contrast is sharp; she has "stood" (worked actively) with only a very "short stay in the house" (likely a temporary shelter or hut in the field for rest). The use of 'amad here functions temporally to mean "has remained active" or "has persisted." It demonstrates that her character matched her reputation; she was not looking for a handout but was fully committed to the hard work of survival.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 41:46 - "Joseph was thirty years old when he stood before Pharaoh king of Egypt."

• Exodus 14:13 - "Do not be afraid. Stand still, and see the salvation of the Lord, which He will accomplish for you today."

• 1 Kings 17:1 - "As the Lord God of Israel lives, before whom I stand, there shall not be dew nor rain these years, except at my word."

• Psalm 1:5 - "Therefore the ungodly shall not stand in the judgment, Nor sinners in the congregation of the righteous."

• Psalm 24:3 - "Who may ascend into the hill of the Lord? Or who may stand in His holy place?"

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is an instructive contrast between "standing" and "sitting" in Hebrew thought. While sitting often represents taking authority, resting, or lingering in idleness, standing represents active service, preparedness, and labor. Ruth chose to "stand" in the field of blessing rather than sit in the passivity of grief and despair. Her standing caught the eye of the landowner, proving that diligence in the small, difficult tasks of life is often the catalyst for God's greater redemptive purposes.
