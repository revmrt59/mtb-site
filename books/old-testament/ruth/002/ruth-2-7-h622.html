GATHER - ( asaph , Strong's H0622 )

Summary:

In Ruth 2:7, the Hebrew word asaph translated as "gather" describes Ruth’s request to collect cut grain in the harvest field. It conveys the physical act of assembling, harvesting, and taking in, which serves as a vivid demonstration of both Ruth's diligent labor and her dependence on the landowner's favor. Throughout Scripture, this word functions as a key thematic term for harvesting crops, receiving outcasts into safety, and God's eventual gathering of His people to Himself.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The primary physical concept behind asaph is the act of bringing diverse items together into a single, unified location. In an ancient agrarian society, this was most commonly experienced during the harvest, when crops were gathered from the fields into barns, storehouses, or homes. It carries a strong connotation of safety, completion, and protection. To gather crops was to secure sustenance for the coming winter; to gather a person into a home was to offer shelter and hospitality to the vulnerable. For Ruth, a destitute Moabite widow, the act of gathering was a matter of physical survival, representing her active trust in the agricultural laws of Israel designed to protect the poor.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb asaph means to gather, assemble, collect, receive, or bring in. The term has a broad semantic range across several categories:

• Agricultural Gathering: Collecting crops, grain, wine, or oil from the fields (Exodus 23:10).

• Social Assembly: Gathering people together for a specific purpose, such as worship, war, or instruction (Numbers 21:16).

• Domestic Hospitality: Taking a stranger, wanderer, or marginalized individual into one's home for care and safety (Joshua 20:4).

• Eschatological and Divine Action: God gathering His scattered, exiled, or broken people back to Himself (Isaiah 11:12).

• Euphemism for Death: Being "gathered to one's people," signifying a peaceful joining of ancestors in the afterlife (Genesis 25:8).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Harvest Collective: Gathering is the joyful culmination of labor. When the grain is gathered, the community celebrates the faithfulness of God’s provision. Conversely, the inability to gather represents judgment and famine.

• The Shield of Hospitality: When someone is abandoned or in danger, to asaph them is to pull them inside the safety of the household. It functions as an antonym to abandonment.

• The Shepherd's Gathering: Just as a shepherd gathers lambs into his arms to protect them from predators, God gathers those who are vulnerable, weak, or scattered.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, asaph highlights the partnership between divine provision and human responsibility. God provides the growth, but humans must labor to gather the harvest. Furthermore, the word illustrates the redemptive heart of God. The Torah established laws requiring landowners to leave the corners of their fields unharvested so that the poor and the stranger could gather food. This systemic mercy reveals a God who gathers the marginalized into His economy of grace. Ultimately, the term points toward God's grand design to gather the nations and the outcasts of Israel into His covenant family.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:7, Ruth asks Boaz’s foreman, "Please let me glean and gather after the reapers among the sheaves." The use of asaph alongside laqat ("glean") is highly significant. While gleaning (laqat) refers to picking up individual stalks that fell to the ground behind the harvesters, gathering (asaph) refers to collecting these stalks into larger bundles. More importantly, Ruth asks to do this "among the sheaves." Normally, poor gleaners were restricted to the edges of the field and were not allowed near the bound sheaves of grain where theft could easily occur. Ruth's request is humble yet remarkably bold; she asks for access to the areas of greatest abundance, where she can gather efficiently. Her diligent labor throughout the day validates her request and secures the favor of Boaz.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 25:8: "Then Abraham breathed his last and died in a good old age, an old man and full of years, and was gathered to his people." (Here, the passive use of asaph describes the transition of death as a reunion with ancestors.)

• Exodus 23:10: "Six years you shall sow your land and gather in its produce," (The standard agricultural application of harvesting the land's yield.)

• Psalm 27:10: "When my father and my mother forsake me, Then the Lord will take me up." (The term is translated "take me up," depicting God receiving and adopting the abandoned child into His care.)

• Isaiah 11:12: "He will set up a banner for the nations, And will assemble the outcasts of Israel, And gather together the dispersed of Judah From the four corners of the earth." (A prophetic look at God's sovereign retrieval of His scattered covenant people.)

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a beautiful parallel between Ruth's physical act of gathering grain in Boaz's field and how Boaz—acting as a kinsman-redeemer—subsequently gathers Ruth and Naomi into his own household. Ruth enters the field as an impoverished foreigner seeking leftovers, but through her faithful labor and Boaz's righteousness, she is gathered into the lineage of King David and Jesus Christ. What begins as a desperate search for physical bread becomes a spiritual gathering into the unfolding story of redemption.
