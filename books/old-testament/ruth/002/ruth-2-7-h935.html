CAME - ( bo' , Strong's H935 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb bo' is a foundational and highly versatile word meaning "to come," "to enter," or "to go in." In the context of Ruth 2:7, it describes Ruth's physical arrival and entry into the field of Boaz. This simple action represents a pivotal step of faith, active obedience, and divine providence. Ruth did not merely wait for provision; she actively "came" to the field, stepping directly into the path of God's sovereign grace and the redemption that lay ahead.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root bo' is one of the most frequent verbs in the Hebrew Bible, occurring over 2,500 times. It carries the primary physical concept of movement toward a destination, specifically entering a space or arriving at a designated point. Unlike verbs of generic movement, bo' emphasizes arrival or the crossing of a threshold—moving from "outside" to "inside," or from "departure" to "presence." In the agricultural setting of ancient Israel, entering a field to glean was a vulnerable act for a foreign widow. This makes her arrival a moment of both physical risk and spiritual hope, as she steps onto the stage where her redemption will unfold.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb bo' primarily means to go in, enter, come, or bring. In its basic active form (Qal), it denotes the physical act of coming into a specific location, entering a tent or house, or arriving at a geographic destination. It can also signify the onset of time (such as the coming of the sun or a season) or the fulfillment of a prophecy or event. In causative forms (Hiphil), it means to bring in, lead in, or introduce. In Ruth 2:7, the verb appears in the active form (Qal imperfect), expressing the historical reality of Ruth's arrival at the field: "she came."

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Because of its high frequency, bo' develops several vivid metaphorical and idiomatic usages in Scripture:

• Entering into Covenant: The verb is frequently used to describe parties entering into a covenant or treaty, signifying a formal transition into a binding relationship.

• The Setting Sun: It idiomatically describes the sun "going down" or entering its chamber at evening, marking the transition of the day.

• Worship and Presence: It is used liturgically of worshippers coming into the tabernacle or sanctuary, entering the presence of the Lord.

• Inheritance: It frequently describes Israel "coming into" the Promised Land to possess it.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, bo' highlights the beautiful intersection of human agency and divine providence. Ruth's physical act of coming to the field is the human instrument through which God's sovereign plan is executed. While Ruth "happened to come" to the portion of the field belonging to Boaz (Ruth 2:3), her arrival was divinely orchestrated. In the broader scope of biblical theology, this word highlights how God works through the ordinary, physical steps of His people. Ruth’s coming to the field is a beautiful picture of a needy stranger coming to find refuge under the wings of the Lord God of Israel.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:7, the foreman of the reapers reports to Boaz: "So she came and has continued from morning until now..." The context emphasizes Ruth's initiative, diligence, and humility. By coming into the field, she was actively pursuing the survival of herself and Naomi. The foreman's testimony highlights that her coming was not a passive or half-hearted effort; she arrived early in the morning and worked continuously. The use of bo' here highlights the transition from her desperate situation of emptiness in Moab to her entry into the place of divine abundance and redemption in Bethlehem.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between Ruth's departure from Moab and her arrival (bo') in Bethlehem, and subsequently in the field of Boaz, is structurally central to the narrative. In chapter 1, Naomi's family departed from the Promised Land, resulting in loss and emptiness. In chapter 2, Ruth’s "coming" (bo') to the field of Boaz reverses this movement, leading to life, provision, and ultimately the lineage of David and Jesus Christ. The foreman's description of her arrival and steadfastness serves as a commendation of her character, proving her worthy of the favor she is about to receive.
