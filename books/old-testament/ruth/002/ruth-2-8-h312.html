ANOTHER - ( acher , Strong's H312 )

Summary:

The Hebrew adjective acher denotes something other, different, next, or foreign. In the context of the agricultural laws of ancient Israel, "another field" represents a place outside the safety, provision, and covenantal protection of one's immediate family redeemer. In Ruth 2:8, Boaz uses this word to urge Ruth to remain exclusively in his field, highlighting the spiritual and physical security found under the wings of Israel's God through His appointed redeemer, rather than seeking sustenance in a strange, unsafe, or unauthorized place.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the agrarian society of ancient Israel, land was not merely property but a divine inheritance. Fields were separated by landmarks rather than fences, making it easy to cross from one owner's territory into "another" (acher). For a foreign widow like Ruth, entering acher (another) field was a risk of extreme physical vulnerability. She had no legal standing, no protection from predatory laborers, and no guarantee of receiving food. A different field belonged to a different family line, with no covenantal obligation to show her favor. By telling her not to go to "another field," Boaz is establishing a boundary of safety and grace, marking his own estate as her sanctuary.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The adjective acher is derived from the root verb achar, which means to remain behind, delay, or stay. In its descriptive form, acher signifies that which is subsequent, next, different, or foreign. It denotes a person, place, or thing that is distinct from the primary subject. It can refer simply to the next item in a sequence, or it can carry a qualitative distinction, indicating something of a completely different nature, such as a foreign land or a strange god.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The word acher occurs over 160 times in the Old Testament. It is used in several distinct theological and practical ways:

• Covenant Loyalty: The most frequent and critical use of acher is in the plural form (acherim) referring to "other gods" (Exodus 20:3; Deuteronomy 6:14). Here, the word pictures a tragic departure from the true Husband of Israel to seek intimacy with foreign, unauthorized deities.

• Lineage and Inheritance: It is used to describe "another seed" or generation that replaces what was lost, such as Seth replacing Abel (Genesis 4:25).

• Personal Transformation: Samuel tells Saul that the Spirit of the Lord will come upon him, and he will be "turned into another man" (1 Samuel 10:6), picturing a radical, divinely wrought change of character.

4. Theological Implications

The warning against going to "another" (acher) field has deep covenantal and typological significance. Just as Israel was commanded not to go after acherim (other gods), Ruth is instructed not to seek grace in acher (another) field. In the redemptive storyline of Scripture, Boaz serves as a type of Jesus Christ, our Kinsman-Redeemer. His field represents the sphere of God's sovereign grace, covenant community, and divine provision. To leave this field for "another" is to look for satisfaction, safety, and salvation in the world, which ultimately leads to spiritual destitution and danger. The call to Ruth is a call to exclusive dependency on the redeemer.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:8, Boaz speaks to Ruth with paternal warmth and protective authority: "Listen, my daughter, do not go to glean in another field, nor go from here, but stay close by my young women." The word acher here carries a tone of warning. Boaz understands the harsh realities facing an unprotected Moabite woman. In "another" field, she might be mistreated, driven away, or abused (as implied in Ruth 2:22). By commanding her not to cross the boundary into acher territory, Boaz binds her well-being to his own household. The field of Boaz is a place of abundant grace; "another field" is a place of hazard and isolation.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 20:3 - "You shall have no other gods before Me."

• Isaiah 42:8 - "I am the LORD, that is My name; And My glory I will not give to another, Nor My praise to carved images."

• Jeremiah 22:9 - "Then they will answer, 'Because they have forsaken the covenant of the LORD their God, and worshiped other gods and served them.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a beautiful parallel between Ruth's physical location and her spiritual posture. In Ruth 1, she chose to leave her homeland of Moab—which had become "another" place of spiritual darkness—to seek refuge under the wings of the Lord God of Israel. In Ruth 2, this spiritual decision is practicalized. Her commitment to Yahweh is mirrored in her commitment to stay in the field of Boaz, refusing to wander into "another" field. For the believer today, this highlights the necessity of abiding in Christ (John 15:4). The world offers many "other" fields of philosophy, security, and pleasure, but true safety and eternal inheritance are found only in the field of our Redeemer.
