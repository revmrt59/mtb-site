HERE - ( koh , Strong's H3541 )

Summary:

The Hebrew adverb koh serves as a demonstrative particle pointing to a specific location ("here") or a specific manner ("thus," "so"). In Ruth 2:8, within Boaz's compassionate speech to Ruth, it functions to locate her place of safety, instruction, and provision. Though translated in the NKJV as part of the phrase "stay close," koh literally directs Ruth to "here" or "in this manner," anchoring her to the immediate presence of Boaz’s young women. It represents a boundary of protection and grace, marking the transition from Ruth's vulnerable wandering to her secure placement within the covenant-keeping community of Bethlehem.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The adverb koh is derived from ancient Semitic demonstrative roots pointing directly to what is immediate, observable, and present. Culturally, in the ancient Near East, boundaries, fields, and physical locations carried deep legal and social significance, especially for vulnerable individuals like foreign widows. To be told to remain koh ("here" or "in this manner") was not merely a geographical instruction but a social and protective assignment. It established a designated zone of security where the rules of hospitality and protection under the redeemer (goel) were actively enforced, shielding the outsider from harassment and exploitation.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Lexically, koh is defined as a demonstrative adverb meaning "here," "hither," "thus," or "so." It possesses both spatial and modal dimensions:

• Spatial: It designates a specific physical spot or direction, meaning "in this place" or "to this point."

• Modal: It denotes the manner in which an action is performed, meaning "in this way" or "so."

In the narrative of Ruth 2:8, the spatial nuance dominates, directing Ruth to a specific place of safety ("here"), while the modal element reinforces the manner of her gleaning—staying joined to Boaz's household.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Hebrew Scriptures, koh serves as a focal point for divine declarations and physical boundaries:

• Divine Announcements: It is the standard introductory particle in the prophetic formula koh amar YHWH ("Thus says the LORD"), pointing to the precise words of Yahweh.

• Physical Limits: It establishes geographic boundaries, as when Abraham tells his servants to stay with the donkey while he and Isaac go ad-koh ("yonder" or "unto here") to worship (Genesis 22:5).

• Positional Security: In military or pastoral settings, it commands individuals to stand "here" in a posture of readiness and alignment (2 Samuel 18:30).

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, koh in the book of Ruth represents the boundary of divine providence and covenant protection. By instructing Ruth to remain "here," Boaz acts as the earthly instrument of Yahweh's wings, under which Ruth has come to trust (Ruth 2:12). This word underscores that God's mercy is localized and concrete; He provides a specific place, a specific community, and a specific redeemer to meet the needs of the destitute. Remaining koh is an act of faith and obedience for Ruth, representing her willingness to abandon foreign fields and cling to the household of faith.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 2:8, Boaz commands Ruth: "Do not go to glean in another field, nor go from here, but stay close by my young women." The phrase translated "stay close" is literally ve-choh tidbaqin ("and here you shall cling"). Boaz pairs the adverb koh with the verb dabaq (to cling, keep close, or hold fast—the same word used of Ruth clinging to Naomi in Ruth 1:14). By utilizing koh, Boaz defines the exact perimeter of Ruth’s safety. He forbids her from wandering into unknown fields where she might face physical assault or economic ruin, anchoring her instead to the immediate vicinity of his female workers. It is a transition from isolation to belonging.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 15:5: "Then He brought him outside and said, 'Look now toward heaven, and count the stars if you are able to number them.' And He said to him, 'So shall your descendants be.'"

• Genesis 22:5: "And Abraham said to his young men, 'Stay here with the donkey; the lad and I will go yonder and worship, and we will come back to you.'"

• Exodus 3:14: "And God said to Moses, 'I AM WHO I AM.' And He said, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel, "I AM has sent me to you."'"

• 2 Samuel 18:30: "So the king said, 'Turn aside and stand here.' And he turned aside and stood still."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Septuagint (LXX), koh is frequently translated using Greek demonstrative adverbs such as hode ("here/this way") or houtos ("thus/so"). In Ruth 2:8, the Septuagint translates ve-choh using hode ("here"), reinforcing the localized nature of Boaz’s instruction. This spatial anchoring contrasts sharply with the wandering life Ruth left behind in Moab. It reminds the reader that restoration begins when one stops wandering and settles in the place where God’s provision is active.
