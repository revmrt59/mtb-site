Chapter Study

Ruth 2

Verse-by-Verse
Ruth 2:1

NKJV

Ruth 2:1 There was a relative of Naomi's husband, a man of great wealth, of the family of Elimelech. His name was Boaz.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 2:1 Now there was a wealthy and influential man in Bethlehem named Boaz, who was a relative of Naomi’s husband, Elimelech. (NLT)

Main Idea

The author introduces Boaz, a wealthy and prominent relative of Elimelech, preparing the reader for God's provision through a redeemer.

Verse Explanation

"There was a relative of Naomi's husband..."

The story now introduces a new character who is related to Elimelech. At this point, neither Naomi nor Ruth appears to know the role Boaz will play. The reader, however, begins to see that God has already prepared the answer to Naomi's need.

"...a man of great wealth..."

The phrase can also describe a man of standing, influence, and noble character. Boaz was certainly prosperous, but the chapter soon reveals that his greatest riches were his integrity, generosity, and devotion to the LORD.

"...of the family of Elimelech."

This detail is crucial. Because Boaz is a relative of Elimelech, he is potentially able to serve as a family redeemer (go'el). Under Israel's laws, a close relative could redeem family property, help relatives in distress, and preserve a deceased family member's inheritance. The significance of this relationship will become clear as the story unfolds.

"His name was Boaz."

The name Boaz may mean "in him is strength." True to his name, Boaz becomes a source of strength, protection, provision, and redemption for Ruth and Naomi. More importantly, he points beyond himself to Jesus Christ, our ultimate Redeemer.

Remark(s)

Before Ruth or Naomi knew there was a redeemer, God had already prepared one. Isn't this also our story? Long before we knew we needed a Savior, God had already provided one in Jesus Christ.

Ruth 2:2

NKJV

Ruth 2:2 So Ruth the Moabitess said to Naomi, "Please let me go to the field, and glean heads of grain after him in whose sight I may find favor." And she said to her, "Go, my daughter."(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 2:2 One day Ruth the Moabite said to Naomi, “Let me go out into the harvest fields to pick up the stalks of grain left behind by anyone who is kind enough to let me do it.” Naomi replied, “All right, my daughter, go ahead.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Ruth takes the initiative to gather leftover grain in the fields, trusting God to provide for her and Naomi through honest work.

Verse Explanation

"So Ruth the Moabitess said to Naomi..."

Although Ruth is still identified as a Moabite, her actions demonstrate the character of someone who has embraced the God of Israel. Rather than waiting for others to solve their problems, she takes the initiative to care for both herself and Naomi.

"'Please let me go to the field...'"

Ruth respectfully asks Naomi's permission before leaving. Her humility and respect for Naomi continue to characterize her actions.

"...and glean heads of grain..."

Under God's Law, landowners were instructed not to harvest every stalk or gather every leftover sheaf. Instead, they were to leave some grain for the poor, widows, foreigners, and others in need (Leviticus 19:9-10; 23:22; Deuteronomy 24:19-22). Gleaning was God's gracious provision for those in need. It preserved their dignity by allowing them to work rather than simply receive charity.

"...after him in whose sight I may find favor."

Ruth knows that not every landowner will necessarily welcome a foreign widow into his fields. She hopes to find someone who will treat her with kindness and allow her to glean safely.

"And she said to her, 'Go, my daughter.'"

Naomi approves Ruth's plan. Although she still sees little reason for hope, she encourages Ruth to do what is necessary to provide for them.

Remark(s)

God's provision did not eliminate Ruth's responsibility to work. Throughout Scripture, God's provision and human responsibility go hand in hand. We trust God, but we also must faithfully work and steward that which He has placed before us.

Ruth 2:3

NKJV

Ruth 2:3 Then she left, and went and gleaned in the field after the reapers. And she happened to come to the part of the field belonging to Boaz, who was of the family of Elimelech.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 2:3 So Ruth went out to gather grain behind the harvesters. And as it happened, she found herself working in a field that belonged to Boaz, the relative of her father-in-law, Elimelech. (NLT)

Main Idea

Ruth begins gleaning in a field that "just happens" to belong to Boaz, revealing God's quiet providence at work behind ordinary events.

Verse Explanation

"Then she left..."

Ruth immediately acts on the opportunity before her. She does not wait passively for God to provide but faithfully does the work that is available.

"...and went and gleaned in the field after the reapers."

Ruth follows behind the harvesters, gathering the stalks of grain they leave behind in accordance with God's law. She comes as a poor foreign widow, depending entirely upon the kindness of the landowner and the provision God had built into His Law.

"And she happened to come to the part of the field belonging to Boaz..."

From a human perspective, it appears to be a coincidence. Ruth has no idea whose field she has entered. Yet the reader knows something Ruth does not: Boaz is a close relative of Elimelech and a potential family redeemer. What appears accidental is actually God's providence quietly directing her steps.

"...who was of the family of Elimelech."

The author reminds us that Boaz is not just another landowner. God has already brought Ruth to the very person through whom He intends to provide redemption and restoration.

Remark(s)

To Ruth, it was simply another field. To God, it was exactly the right field. God's providence often works so quietly that what appears to us as coincidence is actually His sovereign hand guiding our steps.

Ruth 2:4

NKJV

Ruth 2:4 Now behold, Boaz came from Bethlehem, and said to the reapers, "The LORD be with you!" And they answered him, "The LORD bless you!"(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 2:4 While she was there, Boaz arrived from Bethlehem and greeted the harvesters. “The LORD be with you!” he said. “The LORD bless you!” the harvesters replied. (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz arrives at the field and immediately reveals himself to be a man whose life and leadership are centered on the LORD.

Verse Explanation

"Now behold, Boaz came from Bethlehem..."

Boaz arrives at the field just as Ruth is gleaning. What appears to be perfect timing is another example of God's quiet providence preparing the way for their meeting.

"...and said to the reapers, 'The LORD be with you!'"

Boaz's first recorded words are a blessing. His relationship with God is not confined to worship or private devotion; it is evident in his everyday work and in the way he treats those under his leadership.

"And they answered him, 'The LORD bless you!'"

The workers gladly return Boaz's blessing. Their response suggests a relationship of mutual respect rather than fear or resentment. Boaz's character has shaped the culture of his workplace.

Remark(s)

The first thing we learn about Boaz is not that he is wealthy but that he is a man who walks with God. His relationship with the LORD is reflected in the way he leads, speaks, and treats others. True godliness is seen not only in what we believe but in how we live every day.

Ruth 2:5

NKJV

Ruth 2:5 Then Boaz said to his servant who was in charge of the reapers, "Whose young woman is this?"(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 2:5 Then Boaz asked his foreman, “Who is that young woman over there? Who does she belong to?” (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz notices Ruth among the gleaners and immediately asks about her, beginning the relationship that God has been quietly preparing.

Verse Explanation

"Then Boaz said to his servant who was in charge of the reapers..."

Boaz speaks to the foreman responsible for overseeing the harvest. His question shows that he knows his workers and pays attention to what is happening in his fields.

"'Whose young woman is this?'"

Boaz is not asking who owns Ruth, but to whom she belongs or with whose household she is associated. Since he does not recognize her as someone from Bethlehem, he naturally inquires about her identity.

The question also shows that Ruth's character or appearance has distinguished her from the other gleaners. Something about her has caught Boaz's attention, prompting him to ask who she is.

Remark(s)

Ruth came to the field simply looking for a place to glean. She had no idea that the man who noticed her was the very relative God would use to change the course of her life. What was simply an ordinary day of work to Ruth became part of God's eternal plan

NKJV

Ruth 2:6 So the servant who was in charge of the reapers answered and said, "It is the young Moabite woman who came back with Naomi from the country of Moab.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 2:6 And the foreman replied, “She is the young woman from Moab who came back with Naomi. (NLT)

Main Idea

No content added yet.

Verse Explanation

“So the servant who was in charge of the reapers answered and said...”
Boaz's foreman knows who Ruth is and is able to answer immediately. Ruth may be new to Bethlehem, but her arrival with Naomi has apparently become known among the people of the town.

“It is the young Moabite woman...”
Ruth is once again identified by her Moabite background. She is living among God's people and has committed herself to the LORD, yet she is still recognized as an outsider from Moab. This makes the kindness and acceptance she will receive from Boaz all the more significant.

“...who came back with Naomi...”
Ruth is known not merely as a Moabite but as the woman who returned with Naomi. Her decision in chapter 1 has now become part of her reputation. She had left her homeland, her family, and her former life to remain with Naomi and follow the God of Israel.

“...from the country of Moab.”
The foreman's description reminds us how far Ruth has come. Only a short time earlier, Moab had been her home. Now she is in Bethlehem, gleaning among the people of the God she has chosen to follow, without knowing what God has already prepared for her there.

Remark
Ruth's costly decision to follow Naomi and the LORD had already become known before she ever met Boaz. She wasn't trying to build a reputation; she was simply living out the commitment she had made. A godly reputation is often built the same way—through faithful choices made when we have no idea who is watching.

Yet Ruth was still known as “the Moabitess.” Others may keep reminding us where we came from or what we once were, but God reminds us of who we are in Christ. His grace is greater than our past.

Ruth 2:7

NKJV

Ruth 2:7 And she said, "˜Please let me glean and gather after the reapers among the sheaves.' So she came and has continued from morning until now, though she rested a little in the house."(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 2:7 She asked me this morning if she could gather grain behind the harvesters. She has been hard at work ever since, except for a few minutes’ rest in the shelter.” (NLT)

Main Idea

The foreman describes Ruth's humble request to glean and commends her diligent work throughout the day.

Verse Explanation

"'And she said, "Please let me glean and gather after the reapers among the sheaves."'"

Ruth does not presume upon anyone's generosity. Although God's Law provided for gleaning, she respectfully asks permission before entering the field. Her humility is evident in the way she approaches the foreman.

"So she came and has continued from morning until now..."

The foreman reports that Ruth has worked steadily since arriving. Rather than taking advantage of the opportunity, she has labored faithfully and consistently throughout the day.

"...though she rested a little in the house."

Ruth has taken only a brief rest in the workers' shelter before returning to her work. Her diligence has made a strong impression on those who have been watching her.

Remark(s)

Ruth's reputation was established before her relationship with Boaz ever began. Her humility, diligence, and faithful work spoke for her before she ever spoke for herself.

Ruth 2:8

NKJV

Ruth 2:8 Then Boaz said to Ruth, "You will listen, my daughter, will you not? Do not go to glean in another field, nor go from here, but stay close by my young women.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 2:8 Boaz went over and said to Ruth, “Listen, my daughter. Stay right here with us when you gather grain; don’t go to any other fields. Stay right behind the young women working in my field. (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz personally speaks to Ruth, inviting her to remain in his fields where she will be protected and provided for.

Verse Explanation

"Then Boaz said to Ruth..."

This is the first recorded conversation between Boaz and Ruth. Boaz takes the initiative, approaching Ruth rather than waiting for her to come to him.

"'You will listen, my daughter, will you not?'"

"My daughter" is an affectionate expression of kindness and care. It likely reflects both Boaz's older age and his compassionate concern for Ruth. From the beginning, he treats her with dignity and respect.

"'Do not go to glean in another field, nor go from here...'"

Boaz invites Ruth to remain in his fields instead of searching elsewhere for grain. His invitation provides both stability and safety in a world where a poor foreign widow would have been especially vulnerable.

"'...but stay close by my young women.'"

Boaz instructs Ruth to work alongside the women who gather in his fields. This not only provides companionship but also offers protection from the dangers she might face in other fields.

Remark(s)

Boaz sought Ruth before she sought him and invited her to remain close where she would be safe and cared for. He also told her, "Do not go to glean in another field." In the same way, Jesus calls us to abide in Him, for He alone is our true source of life and provision. Every other field promises satisfaction but ultimately leaves us empty. Our Redeemer's field is where true provision is found.

Ruth 2:9

NKJV

Ruth 2:9 Let your eyes be on the field which they reap, and go after them. Have I not commanded the young men not to touch you? And when you are thirsty, go to the vessels and drink from what the young men have drawn."(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 2:9 See which part of the field they are harvesting, and then follow them. I have warned the young men not to treat you roughly. And when you are thirsty, help yourself to the water they have drawn from the well.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz assures Ruth of his protection and provides for her physical needs, demonstrating remarkable kindness toward a poor foreign widow.

Verse Explanation

"'Let your eyes be on the field which they reap, and go after them.'"

Boaz gives Ruth permission to remain with his harvesters and continue gleaning in his fields. She no longer needs to wonder whether she is welcome. She has a place where she can work with confidence.

"'Have I not commanded the young men not to touch you?'"

Boaz has already instructed his workers not to harass, mistreat, or take advantage of Ruth. As a foreign widow, Ruth was especially vulnerable, but Boaz uses his authority to ensure her safety.

"'And when you are thirsty, go to the vessels...'"

Boaz does more than allow Ruth to glean. He invites her to drink from the water his servants have drawn. This was an unexpected act of generosity. A poor foreign gleaner would not normally receive such treatment.

"...and drink from what the young men have drawn."

Ruth is welcomed to enjoy the same provision as Boaz's own workers. She is no longer treated as an outsider merely surviving on leftovers but as someone receiving the generous care of the landowner.

Remark(s)

Boaz did far more than meet Ruth's minimum needs. He welcomed her, protected her, and generously shared his own provision with her. Our Redeemer does the same. Jesus does not merely save us from judgment; He brings us near, provides abundantly for us, and welcomes us into His family.

Ruth 2:10

NKJV

Ruth 2:10 So she fell on her face, bowed down to the ground, and said to him, "Why have I found favor in your eyes, that you should take notice of me, since I am a foreigner?"(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 2:10 Ruth fell at his feet and thanked him warmly. “What have I done to deserve such kindness?” she asked. “I am only a foreigner.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Overwhelmed by Boaz's kindness, Ruth humbly asks why she has found such favor despite being a foreigner.

Verse Explanation

"So she fell on her face, bowed down to the ground..."

Ruth's response reveals genuine humility and gratitude. She does not assume she deserves Boaz's kindness. Instead, she is overwhelmed by the generosity she has received.

"'Why have I found favor in your eyes...'"

Ruth recognizes that Boaz's treatment of her goes far beyond what the law required. She understands that she is receiving grace, not merely justice.

"...that you should take notice of me...'"

Ruth is amazed that Boaz has even noticed her. As a poor widow and a foreigner, she expected to remain unnoticed among the gleaners.

"...since I am a foreigner?"

Ruth knows that her nationality places her outside Israel. She has not forgotten where she came from and believes she has no claim upon Boaz's kindness. Her question highlights that everything she is receiving is an undeserved gift.

Remark(s)

Grace always leaves us asking, "Why me?" Ruth knew she had no claim upon Boaz's kindness, just as we have no claim upon God's grace. The wonder of salvation is not that God saves those who deserve it, but that He graciously welcomes those who do not.

Ruth 2:11

NKJV

Ruth 2:11 And Boaz answered and said to her, "It has been fully reported to me, all that you have done for your mother-in-law since the death of your husband, and how you have left your father and your mother and the land of your birth, and have come to a people whom you did not know before.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 2:11 “Yes, I know,” Boaz replied. “But I also know about everything you have done for your mother-in-law since the death of your husband. I have heard how you left your father and mother and your own land to live here among complete strangers. (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz explains that he has heard of Ruth's extraordinary love and sacrifice for Naomi and commends her for leaving everything to join God's people.

Verse Explanation

"And Boaz answered and said to her..."

Boaz's kindness is not based on impulse or appearance. He has already learned about Ruth's character and the remarkable sacrifices she has made.

"'It has been fully reported to me...'"

Ruth's reputation has spread throughout Bethlehem. The reports are not about her beauty or background but about her love, faithfulness, and selfless care for Naomi.

"'...all that you have done for your mother-in-law since the death of your husband.'"

Boaz specifically commends Ruth's steadfast devotion to Naomi. She remained with Naomi when she was under no obligation to do so, placing Naomi's needs above her own future.

"'And how you have left your father and your mother and the land of your birth...'"

Ruth willingly left her family, homeland, and everything familiar. Her decision involved real sacrifice and an uncertain future.

"'...and have come to a people whom you did not know before.'"

Ruth entered a land where she knew no one except Naomi. She trusted the God of Israel enough to leave the security of the familiar for the uncertainty of faith.

Remark(s)

Ruth left everything that was familiar because she believed the God of Israel was worth following. Genuine faith always costs us something, but whatever we leave behind can never compare with what we gain in knowing our Redeemer.

Ruth 2:12

NKJV

Ruth 2:12 The LORD repay your work, and a full reward be given you by the LORD God of Israel, under whose wings you have come for refuge."(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 2:12 May the LORD, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to take refuge, reward you fully for what you have done.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz prays that the LORD will richly reward Ruth because she has placed her trust in the God of Israel.

Verse Explanation

"'The LORD repay your work...'"

Boaz prays that the LORD Himself will reward Ruth for her faith, love, and sacrifice. He recognizes that while he can show her kindness, only God can fully repay what she has done.

"'...and a full reward be given you by the LORD God of Israel...'"

Boaz desires that Ruth receive a complete reward from the LORD. Ironically, God will answer Boaz's prayer in part through Boaz himself. The one asking God to bless Ruth will become one of the primary instruments through whom God does so.

"'...under whose wings you have come for refuge.'"

This is one of the book's most beautiful pictures of faith. Ruth has left Moab and placed herself under the protection, care, and authority of the God of Israel. Like a young bird finding safety beneath the wings of its mother, Ruth has found refuge in the LORD.

The imagery of God's wings appears throughout Scripture as a picture of His protection and care (Psalm 17:8; 36:7; 57:1; 91:4).

Remark(s)

Boaz prayed that Ruth would find refuge under the wings of the LORD. Later, Ruth would ask Boaz to spread his own wing over her in marriage (3:9). God answered Boaz's prayer through Boaz himself, making him a beautiful picture of our Redeemer, Jesus Christ.

Ruth 2:13

NKJV

Ruth 2:13 Then she said, "Let me find favor in your sight, my lord; for you have comforted me, and have spoken kindly to your maidservant, though I am not like one of your maidservants."(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 2:13 “I hope I continue to please you, sir,” she replied. “You have comforted me by speaking so kindly to me, even though I am not one of your workers.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Ruth gratefully receives Boaz's kindness, acknowledging that his words have encouraged her even though she considers herself unworthy of such favor.

Verse Explanation

"'Then she said, "Let me find favor in your sight, my lord..."'"

Ruth gratefully accepts Boaz's kindness and expresses her desire to continue receiving his favor. She responds with humility rather than entitlement.

"'...for you have comforted me...'"

Boaz's words have brought hope and encouragement to a woman who has experienced great loss and uncertainty. His kindness extends beyond meeting her physical needs; it also strengthens her heart.

"'...and have spoken kindly to your maidservant...'"

Literally, Boaz had "spoken to the heart" of Ruth. His gracious and reassuring words ministered to her deepest fears and uncertainties.

"'...though I am not like one of your maidservants.'"

Ruth remains conscious of her position as a foreigner. She does not presume equality with Boaz's servants but is deeply grateful for the undeserved kindness he has shown her.

Remark(s)

Boaz's kindness was expressed not only through what he gave Ruth but also through the words he spoke to her. Our words have the power to strengthen discouraged hearts, bring comfort to the hurting, and become channels of God's grace to others.

Ruth 2:14

NKJV

Ruth 2:14 Now Boaz said to her at mealtime, "Come here, and eat of the bread, and dip your piece of bread in the vinegar." So she sat beside the reapers, and he passed parched grain to her; and she ate and was satisfied, and kept some back.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 2:14 At mealtime Boaz called to her, “Come over here, and help yourself to some food. You can dip your bread in the sour wine.” So she sat with his harvesters, and Boaz gave her some roasted grain to eat. She ate all she wanted and still had some left over. (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz invites Ruth to eat with his workers and personally serves her, treating a poor foreign widow with unexpected honor and generosity.

Verse Explanation

"Now Boaz said to her at mealtime..."

Boaz does more than allow Ruth to glean in his fields. He invites her to share a meal with his workers, extending fellowship as well as provision.

"'Come here, and eat of the bread...'"

Rather than remaining on the outside, Ruth is welcomed to the table. This invitation goes far beyond what the Law required and demonstrates Boaz's gracious acceptance of her.

"'...and dip your piece of bread in the vinegar.'"

The vinegar was a refreshing drink or sauce commonly used by harvest workers. Ruth is invited to enjoy the same food and drink as everyone else, not something separate or inferior.

"So she sat beside the reapers..."

Ruth, the foreign widow, now sits among Boaz's own workers. The outsider is welcomed into fellowship with those who belong.

"...and he passed parched grain to her..."

Boaz personally serves Ruth. This simple act reveals his humility, generosity, and genuine care for her.

"...and she ate and was satisfied, and kept some back."

Not only does Ruth have enough to eat, but she has food left over to take home to Naomi. God's provision through Boaz is abundant rather than merely sufficient.

Remark(s)

Boaz welcomed Ruth to his table, personally served her, gave her bread and drink, satisfied her hunger, and left her with more than she needed. This beautiful picture points us to Jesus Christ. Our Redeemer welcomes us into His presence, feeds us with Himself as the Bread of Life, gives us living water, and invites us to His table. In Him we are fully satisfied, and there is always more than enough.

Ruth 2:16

NKJV

Ruth 2:16 Also let grain from the bundles fall purposely for her; leave it that she may glean, and do not rebuke her."(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 2:16 And pull out some heads of barley from the bundles and drop them on purpose for her. Let her pick them up, and don’t give her a hard time!” (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz instructs his workers to intentionally leave extra grain for Ruth to gather, providing for her with extraordinary generosity.

Verse Explanation

"'Also let grain from the bundles fall purposely for her...'"

Boaz goes beyond allowing Ruth to glean. He instructs his workers to deliberately pull grain from the harvested bundles and leave it where Ruth can easily gather it. What appears to be ordinary gleaning is actually the result of Boaz's intentional generosity.

"'Leave it that she may glean...'"

Boaz wants Ruth to receive an abundant harvest without knowing that he is the one providing it. His kindness is thoughtful, generous, and discreet.

"'...and do not rebuke her.'"

Ruth 2:17

NKJV

Ruth 2:17 So she gleaned in the field until evening, and beat out what she had gleaned, and it was about an ephah of barley.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 2:17 So Ruth gathered barley there all day, and when she beat out the grain that evening, it filled an entire basket. (NLT)

Main Idea

Ruth works faithfully until evening and gathers an abundant amount of barley through Boaz's generous provision.

Verse Explanation

"So she gleaned in the field until evening..."

Ruth continues working throughout the day. She does not stop after receiving Boaz's kindness but remains diligent until the day's work is finished.

"...and beat out what she had gleaned..."

After gathering the stalks, Ruth separates the edible grain from the husks. This required additional labor after a full day's work, demonstrating her continued diligence.

"...and it was about an ephah of barley."

An ephah was a substantial amount of grain—approximately 30 to 50 pounds (13–22 kg), depending on the measure used. This was an extraordinary amount for a single day's gleaning and far more than would normally be expected. It quietly reveals the abundance of Boaz's generosity.

Remark(s)

Ruth simply set out to gather enough grain for another day, but she returned home with far more than she expected. Our Redeemer often does the same. He delights in doing far more than we could ask or think, providing not merely enough, but an abundance..

Ruth 2:18

NKJV

Ruth 2:18 Then she took it up and went into the city, and her mother-in-law saw what she had gleaned. So she brought out and gave to her what she had kept back after she had been satisfied.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 2:18 She carried it back into town and showed it to her mother-in-law. Ruth also gave her the roasted grain that was left over from her meal. (NLT)

Main Idea

Ruth returns to Naomi with an abundant harvest and shares both the grain and the leftover food Boaz had given her.

Verse Explanation

"Then she took it up and went into the city..."

After a full day of hard work, Ruth carries the grain back to Bethlehem. The unusually large amount immediately catches Naomi's attention.

"...and her mother-in-law saw what she had gleaned."

Naomi quickly realizes that something extraordinary has happened. No ordinary day of gleaning would normally produce such a large harvest.

"So she brought out and gave to her what she had kept back after she had been satisfied."

Ruth shares the leftover food she had saved from Boaz's table (2:14). Rather than keeping it for herself, she thinks first of Naomi. Even after experiencing unexpected generosity, Ruth continues to demonstrate the same selfless love that has characterized her from the beginning of the story.

Remark(s)

Ruth's first thought was not, "How much can I keep?" but, "What can I take home to Naomi?" One of the clearest evidences of a heart transformed by God's grace is the desire to share His blessings with others.

Naomi is astonished by Ruth's abundant harvest and learns that the man who showed her such kindness was Boaz.

Naomi asks the same question in a different way, emphasizing her amazement. She knows that such generosity could only have come from an unusually kind landowner.

"'Blessed be the one who took notice of you.'"

Before Naomi even knows the man's identity, she pronounces a blessing on him. She recognizes that whoever treated Ruth with such kindness deserves God's blessing.

"So she told her mother-in-law with whom she had worked..."

Ruth simply reports the facts of her day. She does not exaggerate Boaz's kindness or attempt to interpret its significance.

"'The man's name with whom I worked today is Boaz.'"

To Ruth, Boaz is simply the kind landowner she met that day. To Naomi, that name changes everything. In an instant, she realizes that God has already begun providing the answer to a problem she believed had no solution.

Remark(s)

To Ruth, "Boaz" was simply a name. To Naomi, it was the first clear evidence that God had not abandoned her after all. Sometimes a single piece of news can change the way we see everything God has been doing behind the scenes.
