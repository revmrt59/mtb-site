SEEK - ( baqash , Strong's H1245 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb baqash describes an earnest, intentional quest to find, secure, or obtain someone or something. It goes far beyond a casual look, representing a focused, active pursuit driven by desire, duty, or necessity. In Ruth 3:1, Naomi uses this term to express her deliberate, loving determination to secure a permanent home and husband for Ruth. It marks a transition from passive grief to active faith, demonstrating how human initiative and covenant loyalty work in harmony with God's providential care.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The root of baqash is deeply tied to concrete physical actions in ancient Near Eastern life. It brings to mind a shepherd searching diligently for a lost sheep, a servant hunting for missing property, or a subject seeking an audience with a king. It is a word of movement and focused attention. In the Hebrew Scriptures, baqash is never passive. It involves the alignment of the heart, mind, and strength toward a specific objective. Whether seeking God's face, pursuing justice, or seeking to protect a loved one, the word depicts an individual actively striving to bridge the gap between their current lack and a desired reality.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb baqash occurs predominantly in the intensive Piel stem in the Old Testament. In this form, it means to seek, search out, strive after, require, or demand. The intensive stem amplifies the action, signaling a heightened level of effort and resolve. The lexical range includes:

• To search for something lost or hidden.

• To strive to secure a particular outcome, state, or relationship.

• To beg, plead, or request something earnestly from another.

• To demand accountability or require a legal reckoning.

• To seek the presence, favor, or counsel of a superior, especially God.

Unlike verbs that describe a state of desiring, baqash emphasizes the physical or strategic steps taken to actualize that desire.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, baqash is used in several distinct theological and practical contexts:

• The Pursuit of Safety or Harm: It can describe an enemy seeking someone's life (as Saul sought David), or conversely, a protector seeking the welfare of the vulnerable.

• The Search for Wisdom and Truth: It describes the noble quest for understanding, where a person searches for wisdom as one searches for hidden silver.

• Relational Seeking: It is the primary verb used for seeking the face of Yahweh, which means entering His sanctuary, desiring His presence, and conforming one's life to His will.

• Providential Initiative: In relational contexts, it depicts a superior seeking an inferior for a special purpose, such as God seeking a man after His own heart.

### 4. Theological Implications

Theologically, baqash highlights the relationship between divine sovereignty and human agency. While God is the ultimate provider of rest, security, and redemption, He often works through the active, faithful steps of His covenant people. When Naomi seeks security for Ruth, her action reflects the covenant love (chesed) of God Himself. Naomi does not sit idly by, waiting for a miracle to fall from heaven; instead, she actively seeks a righteous outcome, demonstrating that living faith involves strategic, obedient action. Furthermore, this seeking points to the greater redemptive pattern of scripture, where God seeks out the lost, and His people, in turn, are called to seek His kingdom and righteousness.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 3:1, Naomi asks, "My daughter, shall I not seek security for you, that it may be well with you?" Here, baqash is used by a mother-in-law whose heart has been transformed from bitter emptiness (Ruth 1:20-21) to hopeful faith. The "security" (manoach) Naomi seeks for Ruth is a place of rest, specifically the safety, provision, and stability of a covenant marriage. Naomi's use of baqash indicates that she is taking personal responsibility for Ruth’s future. She is formulating a plan to approach Boaz, the kinsman-redeemer. In this context, baqash is the driving force of familial love, transitioning the narrative from survival in the barley fields to redemption in the home.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 4:19 - "Now the LORD said to Moses in Midian, 'Go, return to Egypt; for all the men who sought your life are dead.'"

• 1 Samuel 13:14 - "But now your kingdom shall not continue. The LORD has sought for Himself a man after His own heart, and the LORD has commanded him to be commander over His people, because you have not kept what the LORD commanded you."

• Psalm 27:8 - "When You said, 'Seek My face,' My heart said to You, 'Your face, LORD, I will seek.'"

• Proverbs 2:4 - "If you seek her as silver, And search for her as for hidden treasures;"

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

It is valuable to contrast baqash with another common Hebrew verb for "seek," which is darash. While darash often carries the sense of inquiring, investigating, or studying (such as inquiring of a prophet or searching the Scriptures), baqash is more relational, experiential, and possession-oriented. One darashes (inquires about) a matter of truth, but one baqashes (actively strives to find and secure) a person, a relationship, or a physical state of safety. Naomi does not merely want to investigate the possibility of rest for Ruth; she is actively working to secure it.
