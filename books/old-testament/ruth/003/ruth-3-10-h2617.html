KINDNESS - ( chesed , Strong's H2617 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word chesed represents a deep, covenant-based loyalty, active goodness, and lovingkindness that goes far beyond mere emotion or legal obligation. In Ruth 3:10, Boaz uses this word to commend Ruth's remarkable devotion, noting that her willingness to marry a redeemer to preserve her late husband's lineage is an even greater act of sacrificial love than her initial decision to leave Moab to care for Naomi. It represents love in action, rooted in commitment and expressed through faithful, self-sacrificing deeds.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The cultural background of chesed is deeply rooted in the covenantal life of ancient Israel. It is not a passive feeling of affection, but an active, loyal commitment that sustains a relationship. In the ancient Near East, survival depended heavily on family, tribal, and covenantal bonds. To show chesed meant to step forward and meet a desperate need, often when there was no legal compulsion to do so. It is the generous act of a stronger party or a committed partner coming to the aid of another out of sheer loyalty and devotion. In the book of Ruth, this human loyalty mirrors the divine loyalty of Yahweh toward His people, showing how God works through the hands of ordinary people who practice covenant love.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term chesed denotes covenant loyalty, steadfast love, mercy, active kindness, and devotion. It defies easy translation because it blends the concepts of love, loyalty, and action into a single virtue. The definition includes:

• Covenant Loyalty: A commitment to preserve and honor a relationship, even when the other party is vulnerable or unable to reciprocate.

• Active Goodness: Practical, tangible help rendered to those in need. It is never merely internal; it is always demonstrated through concrete deeds.

• Superabundant Kindness: Generosity that goes beyond what is legally required or standardly expected.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, chesed is used to describe both God's relational character and the ethical standards expected among human beings.

• The Divine Picture: It is the primary word used to describe God’s faithful covenant love for Israel. Even when Israel is faithless, God's chesed remains unbroken, serving as the foundation of His mercy and forgiveness.

• The Human Picture: In human relationships, it is depicted as a life-preserving bond. It is the loyalty between David and Jonathan, the kindness Rahab requested from the Israelite spies, and the devotion Ruth showed to Naomi. It is often paired with truth, faithfulness, and covenant, illustrating that true kindness is both reliable and enduring.

### 4. Theological Implications

Theologically, chesed is the very heartbeat of God's relationship with humanity. It reveals that God relates to His people not on the basis of cold legalism, but through a committed, enduring relationship of love.

• Reflecting the Divine Character: When human beings display chesed, they reflect the image of God. Ruth’s kindness is a tangible expression of Yahweh’s wings of protection, under which she has come to take refuge.

• Grace and Redemption: The concept is closely tied to redemption. It is the motivation behind God’s saving acts. In the book of Ruth, human chesed becomes the vehicle through which God’s providential care and redemption are realized for a grieving family, ultimately leading to the birth of King David and the lineage of Jesus Christ.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 3:10, Boaz says, "Blessed are you of the Lord, my daughter! For you have shown more kindness (chesed) at the end than at the beginning, in that you did not go after young men, whether poor or rich."

• The "Beginning" Kindness: This refers to Ruth’s initial decision to cling to Naomi, leave her homeland, and work in the fields to provide for her mother-in-law (Ruth 1:16–17). It was an act of extraordinary devotion to a destitute widow.

• The "Latter" Kindness: This refers to Ruth's proposal to Boaz at the threshing floor. By seeking Boaz as a redeemer, Ruth was not looking for romantic fulfillment or personal wealth with a younger man. Instead, she was seeking to secure the inheritance and name of her deceased husband (Mahlon) and to provide long-term security for Naomi.

• The Nuance: Ruth’s chesed is sacrificial. She prioritizes family duty, loyalty, and redemption over her own personal desires, choosing an older relative to fulfill the role of the kinsman-redeemer. Boaz recognizes this selflessness as an even greater act of covenant loyalty than her initial move to Bethlehem.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 24:12 - "Then he said, 'O Lord God of my master Abraham, please give me success this day, and show kindness to my master Abraham.'"

• Ruth 2:20 - "Then Naomi said to her daughter-in-law, 'Blessed be he of the Lord, who has not forsaken His kindness to the living and the dead!'"

• 2 Samuel 9:3 - "Then the king said, 'Is there not still someone of the house of Saul, to whom I may show the kindness of God?'"

• Micah 6:8 - "He has shown you, O man, what is good; and what does the Lord require of you but to do justly, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God?"

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• A Foreigner as the Exemplar: Remarkably, it is Ruth—a Moabite woman from an enemy nation—who becomes the supreme human example of chesed in the Old Testament. This highlights that true covenant loyalty is not a matter of ethnic heritage, but of heart-level devotion and obedience to God.

• The Chain Reaction of Kindness: Ruth's chesed toward Naomi inspires Boaz to show chesed to Ruth. In turn, Boaz's response enables Ruth to show an even greater chesed to her family. This chain of kindness demonstrates how one act of faithful obedience can ripple outward, ultimately affecting generations and paving the way for the Messiah.
