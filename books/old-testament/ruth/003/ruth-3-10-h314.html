END - ( 'acharon , Strong's H314 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'acharon carries the foundational meaning of "hind," "latter," "last," or "following." In Ruth 3:10, translated as "end" in the NKJV, it functions in contrast to the "beginning," comparing Ruth's initial acts of loyal love toward Naomi with her latter act of seeking marriage with Boaz to preserve her family's name and inheritance. Rather than referring to a chronological cessation, "end" here denotes the culmination or latest expression of Ruth's ongoing covenant faithfulness, which Boaz recognizes as even greater than her first.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root 'achar relates to backing up, staying behind, or remaining. From this root, 'acharon spatializes as "behind" or "western" (since the ancient Hebrews oriented themselves facing east, making "behind" the west). Chronologically, it refers to what comes after, the latter, or the final part of a sequence.

• In the cultural context of ancient Israel, time was often viewed in terms of posture: the past is in front of us (already seen), while the future is behind us ('acharon), coming up from behind. Thus, 'acharon points to the unfolding future, the latter deeds, or the final legacy of a person's life choices.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The adjective 'acharon means behind, latter, last, or succeeding.

• Depending on the context, it expresses:

• Spatial relation: western, hindmost, or back (e.g., the "western sea").

• Temporal relation: latter, later, following, or future generations.

• Sequential relation: last in a series or final.

• In Ruth 3:10, it acts as a substantive adjective, "the latter [kindness]" or "at the end," directly contrasted with "the beginning."

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Western Sea: In several geographical contexts, 'acharon describes the Mediterranean Sea as the "hindmost sea" or "western sea," reflecting the geographical orientation of standing in Israel and looking toward the sunrise.

• Future Generations: It frequently refers to those who come after—the "generation to come" or the "succeeding generation."

• The First and the Last: In the prophetic books, the word is used of Yahweh, who is the "first" and the "last" ('acharon), meaning He encompasses all history and remains when all else has passed.

4. Theological Implications

• The transition from "beginning" to "latter" ('acharon) in the life of faith highlights the beauty of progressive obedience. In biblical theology, a good start is commendable, but a faithful "latter end" is where the true weight of covenant loyalty (chesed) is proved.

• Ruth's covenant loyalty was not a fleeting emotion but a sustained, escalating commitment. Her "latter" kindness surpassed her "beginning" because it moved from mere survival (gleaning to feed Naomi) to redemption and legacy (seeking a family redeemer to preserve Elimelech's line). True biblical love grows deeper and more sacrificial over time.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 3:10, Boaz observes Ruth's midnight request at the threshing floor. By asking him to spread his wings over her as a kinsman-redeemer, Ruth is bypassing younger, perhaps more physically attractive or wealthy men, to honor her family obligation.

• Boaz recognizes this as her "latter" ('acharon) kindness. Her "beginning" kindness was leaving her homeland to care for her widowed mother-in-law. Her "latter" kindness is her willingness to marry an older man to raise up an heir for her deceased husband. The "end" of her actions demonstrates that her primary motivation was never self-interest, but the preservation of Naomi's family line.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Deuteronomy 11:24: "Every place on which the sole of your foot treads shall be yours: from the wilderness and Lebanon, from the river, the River Euphrates, even to the Western Sea, shall be your territory."

• Job 19:25: "For I know that my Redeemer lives, and He shall stand at last on the earth;"

• Proverbs 31:25: "Strength and honor are her clothing; She shall rejoice in time to come."

• Isaiah 44:6: "Thus says the LORD, the King of Israel, And his Redeemer, the LORD of hosts: 'I am the First and I am the Last; Besides Me there is no God.'"

• Haggai 2:9: "'The glory of this latter temple shall be greater than the former,' says the LORD of hosts."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The contrast between the "former" and the "latter" ('acharon) is a recurring motif in the Old Testament, representing God's ability to bring greater glory out of humble beginnings. This is seen in Haggai's temple prophecy and is beautifully dramatized in Ruth's life. What began in bitter loss and bereavement ends in redemption, joy, and the lineage of Jesus Christ.

• By choosing a path of self-sacrificing covenant love in her "latter" actions, Ruth mirrors the heart of God, who also reserves His greatest display of redemption for the "latter" days through the ultimate Kinsman-Redeemer, Jesus Christ.
