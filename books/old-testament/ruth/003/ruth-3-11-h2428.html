VIRTUOUS - ( chayil , Strong's H2428 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word chayil carries a primary meaning of strength, power, capability, and moral integrity. When applied to a woman, as in the phrase eshet chayil, it describes a person of noble character, strength of will, and unwavering moral fiber. In Ruth 3:11, Boaz uses this term to affirm Ruth's reputation throughout Bethlehem, demonstrating that true beauty and worth are found in active, faithful obedience to God and loyalty to family, rather than external circumstances or social status.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun chayil is rooted in the concept of strength, efficiency, and resourcefulness. In the ancient Near East, it was most frequently used in military contexts to describe an army, valor in battle, or the physical power required for warfare. It also referred to material wealth or substance, representing the accumulated strength of an individual's resources. When applied to moral character, chayil represents an inner, robust strength—a fortified integrity that withstands pressure. Instead of passive goodness, it depicts an active, vigorous, and efficient virtue that accomplishes good deeds and remains steadfast under trial.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The word indicates power, strength, wealth, army, capability, or valor.

• When qualifying a person, it denotes moral strength, noble character, competence, and worthiness.

• In the specific idiom eshet chayil ("woman of valor" or "virtuous woman"), it defines a woman of outstanding character, industrious capability, and moral excellence. It serves as the female equivalent of gibbor chayil ("mighty man of valor").

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Military Strength: chayil frequently describes an organized, disciplined force of soldiers. This evokes a picture of strength that is structured, focused, and prepared for battle.

• Wealth and Substance: The word often denotes the physical resources, land, or riches that give a person standing and capability within their community.

• Moral Excellence: When applied to individuals, it denotes capability and integrity. The connection between strength and character is vivid; moral integrity is viewed as a form of spiritual strength and capability. True virtue is not weak or fragile; it is robust and resilient.

4. Theological Implications

• chayil demonstrates that biblical virtue is dynamic rather than passive. The virtuous person is not merely one who avoids sin, but one who actively pursues righteousness, works diligently, and protects the vulnerable.

• The theological portrait of a virtuous person is someone whose inner spiritual strength translates into practical, hard-working, and sacrificial love for others.

• This term reflects the character of God, who is the ultimate source of strength and moral perfection. When God equips His people with chayil, He enables them to live lives of high moral standing and practical capability in a fallen world.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 3:11, Ruth is a destitute Moabite widow, yet she is recognized by the entire town as a virtuous woman (eshet chayil). This transcends her ethnic background and economic status; her character is her true wealth.

• There is a deliberate literary parallel in the Book of Ruth. Boaz is introduced in Ruth 2:1 as a "man of great wealth" (literally gibbor chayil, a mighty man of standing/valor). By calling Ruth an eshet chayil, Boaz recognizes her as his moral equal.

• This statement occurs on the threshing floor after Ruth asks Boaz to spread his cloak over her, which was a proposal for marriage and redemption. Boaz reassures her that her reputation is spotless. Her bold act of faith was not seen as scandalous, but as another manifestation of her loyal, virtuous character.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Proverbs 31:10 - "Who can find a virtuous [chayil] wife? For her worth is far above rubies."

• Proverbs 12:4 - "A virtuous [chayil] woman is the crown of her husband, But she who causes shame is like rottenness in his bones."

• Ruth 2:1 - "There was a relative of Naomi’s husband, a man of great wealth [chayil], of the family of Elimelech. His name was Boaz."

• Exodus 18:21 - "Moreover you shall select from all the people able [chayil] men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness; and place such over them to be rulers..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• In the Hebrew Bible, the Book of Ruth is placed in the Ketuvim (Writings) immediately following the Book of Proverbs. This creates a profound canonical connection: the Proverbs 31 poem ends with the description of the eshet chayil ("virtuous woman"), and the very next page introduces Ruth, showing the reader a historical, flesh-and-blood example of this virtuous woman in action.

• The word also suggests resilience. Ruth had suffered the loss of her husband, homeland, and financial security, yet her chayil remained intact. Her strength was not dependent on her circumstances, but on her trust under the wings of the Lord God of Israel.
