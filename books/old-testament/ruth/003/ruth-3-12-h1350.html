CLOSE RELATIVE - ( ga'al , Strong's H1350 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb ga'al refers to the act of redeeming, rescuing, or playing the role of a kinsman-redeemer. In the ancient Israelite social structure, the go'el (the active participle of ga'al) was a close male relative responsible for protecting the family’s land, liberty, and lineage. In Ruth 3:12, the term is used twice to describe Boaz and an unnamed relative who had the legal right and responsibility to buy back Naomi's family land and marry Ruth. This concept points directly to the ultimate redemption found in Jesus Christ, who became our near kinsman to redeem us from the bondage of sin and death.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient Israel, family solidarity was the bedrock of society. The go'el served as a divinely ordained safety net. If an Israelite fell into poverty and had to sell his family land or even sell himself into slavery, the nearest male relative was responsible for stepping in to buy back (redeem) the land or the person. Furthermore, if a man died childless, the kinsman was expected to marry the widow to raise up an heir for the deceased (the levirate custom). The go'el was also responsible for avenging the blood of a murdered relative. Thus, the word picture is one of restoration, protection, and preservation of family life and inheritance.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The root ga'al means to redeem, buy back, deliver, or act as a kinsman. Lexically, it refers to the legal release of property or persons from a state of forfeiture or bondage back to their original, rightful owner or state. It is distinct from other words for redemption (such as padah, which emphasizes paying a ransom price without requiring a family relationship) because ga'al inherently carries the requirement of kinship. The redeemer must be a close blood relative.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Redeeming Property: Leviticus outlines how a kinsman must buy back family land so that the ancestral inheritance remains within the tribe.

• Redeeming Persons: If an Israelite sells himself to a foreigner due to debt, the go'el must pay the redemption price to set him free.

• The Avenger of Blood: The go'el haddam (avenger of blood) seeks justice for a slain relative, restoring moral order and peace to the community.

• Levirate Duty: Fulfilling the duty of marrying a deceased relative's widow to carry on his name, ensuring his line does not die out and the widow is provided for.

4. Theological Implications

• God as Israel's Redeemer: Throughout the Old Testament, God is revealed as the ultimate Go'el. He redeemed Israel from Egypt not because of a legal debt, but out of His covenant relationship as their "Father" and nearest relative.

• Messianic Fulfillment: The kinsman-redeemer is a profound type of Jesus Christ. To redeem humanity, the Redeemer had to meet three qualifications: He must be a near kinsman (Jesus became human in the Incarnation), He must have the resources to pay the redemption price (Jesus offered His sinless blood), and He must be willing to redeem (Jesus laid down His life voluntarily).

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 3:12, Boaz acknowledges his role as a go'el ("close relative") but reveals that another man is a closer relative than he is. The passage highlights the precise legal and moral structure of redemption in Israel. Boaz cannot simply act on his desires or emotions; he must defer to the rightful order of the law. This emphasizes Boaz’s stellar integrity and underscores that redemption is not a lawless rescue but a righteous, legal transaction. The presence of a "closer relative" raises the dramatic tension of the narrative, testing whether the nearer kinsman will perform his duty or if Boaz will be cleared to do so.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Leviticus 25:25: "If one of your brethren becomes poor, and has sold some of his possession, and if his redeeming relative comes to redeem it, then he may redeem what his brother sold."

• Ruth 4:14: "Then the women said to Naomi, 'Blessed be the LORD, who has not left you this day without a close relative; and may his name be famous in Israel!'"

• Job 19:25: "For I know that my Redeemer lives, And He shall stand at last on the earth;"

• Isaiah 43:1: "But now, thus says the LORD, who created you, O Jacob, And He who formed you, O Israel: 'Fear not, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by your name; You are Mine.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Cost of Redemption: Redemption is never cheap. For Boaz, redeeming Naomi's land and marrying Ruth meant risking his own inheritance (as the nearer kinsman explicitly feared doing in Ruth 4:6). True redemption always costs the redeemer.

• Pastoral Application: Boaz did not force his way into Ruth's life. He respected the legal process. Similarly, Christ's redemption is perfectly aligned with the holiness and justice of God's law. He did not bypass the law to save us; He fulfilled its righteous demands.
