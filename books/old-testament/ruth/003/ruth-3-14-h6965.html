AROSE - ( qum , Strong's H6965 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb qum primarily means to rise, stand up, or arise. In the context of Ruth 3:14, it denotes Ruth rising early in the morning from the threshing floor after placing herself under Boaz's protection as her kinsman-redeemer. This physical act of rising marks a pivotal transition in the narrative, moving from a posture of humble appeal to the active unfolding of redemption. It highlights both the need for discretion to protect Ruth's reputation and the decisive readiness of both characters to pursue the legal steps of redemption.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew verb qum is a common root found over 600 times in the Old Testament. Its concrete, physical background is the act of moving from a prostrate, seated, or kneeling position to an upright, standing posture. In ancient Near Eastern culture, physical posture carried deep relational and legal meaning. To lie down or bow was an act of submission, vulnerability, or petition. To arise (qum) was to prepare for action, assert readiness, or receive validation. In Ruth 3:14, Ruth rises from the feet of Boaz. Having lain down in submission and petition, her rising signals the conclusion of this highly sensitive nocturnal encounter and the initiation of Boaz's promised redemption.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb qum means to rise, stand up, or get up from a recumbent position. In the Qal stem, it indicates the physical action of rising from sleep, bed, or the ground. It also denotes rising up to perform a duty, embarking on a journey, or initiating a significant action. In the Hiphil (causative) stem, it means to raise up, establish, or confirm—such as establishing a covenant or raising up descendants. In the context of Ruth 3:14, the verb is in the Qal imperfect with a waw-consecutive, expressing a sequential historical action: "and she arose."

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• qum is used across the Old Testament to depict several distinct pictures:

• Physical Rising: Getting up from sleep in the morning or standing up out of respect for an elder.

• Legal and Covenantal Standing: "Establishing" or making a promise stand firm.

• Call to Action: A command to wake up, prepare, and move forward into God's purposes.

• In the book of Ruth, the word appears in several key moments: Naomi arose with her daughters-in-law to return from Moab, and Boaz promises to raise up the name of the dead. Thus, Ruth's physical rising in 3:14 foreshadows the ultimate raising up of her deceased husband's lineage.

4. Theological Implications

The transition from lying down to rising reflects the spiritual trajectory of redemption. Ruth begins the night in a position of ultimate vulnerability at Boaz's feet, symbolizing her complete reliance on his grace. Her rising early in the morning represents hope and the dawning of a new day under the protection of the redeemer. Additionally, the connection between Ruth's personal action of rising (qum) and Boaz's legal obligation to "raise up" (qum) the name of the deceased illustrates the cooperative nature of faith and redemption. God works through the obedience, courage, and discreet actions of His people to fulfill His covenant promises.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 3:14, Ruth's arising is marked by extreme discretion: "she arose before one could recognize another." The timing is critical. She had to lie at his feet until morning to demonstrate her petition, but she arose while it was still dark to preserve her moral reputation and Boaz's integrity from malicious gossip. The verb here highlights the delicate balance of faith and wisdom. Ruth does not linger longer than necessary, nor does she leave prematurely. Her arising is prompt, orderly, and guided by Boaz's protective instruction.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 22:3: "So Abraham rose early in the morning and saddled his donkey, and took two of his young men with him, and Isaac his son; and he split the wood for the burnt offering, and arose and went to the place of which God had told him."

• Ruth 1:6: "Then she arose with her daughters-in-law that she might return from the country of Moab, for she had heard in the country of Moab that the Lord had visited His people by giving them bread."

• Joshua 1:2: "Moses My servant is dead. Now therefore, arise, go over this Jordan, you and all this people, to the land which I am giving to them—the children of Israel."

• Ruth 4:10: "Moreover, Ruth the Moabitess, the widow of Mahlon, I have acquired as my wife, to raise up the name of the dead on his inheritance, that the name of the dead may not be cut off from among his brethren and from his position at the gate. You are witnesses this day."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The theme of "rising" (qum) is central to the restoration of the marginalized in Hebrew Scripture. Whenever God's people are brought low, the call or act of arising signals divine restoration. Boaz's concern for Ruth's reputation is shown immediately after she arose. He fills her shawl with six measures of barley, ensuring she does not return to her mother-in-law empty-handed. Her arising is not just an exit, but a departure marked by abundance, safety, and the assurance of a future.
