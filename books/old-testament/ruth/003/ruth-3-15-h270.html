HOLD - ( 'achaz , Strong's H270 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb 'achaz primarily means to grasp, take hold of, seize, or acquire. In the narrative context of Ruth 3:15, it depicts Ruth actively gripping and sustaining her hold on her cloak to receive the massive, generous measure of barley from Boaz. This physical act of holding fast reflects both her dependence on his provision and the security of the redeemer's promise, serving as a beautiful picture of faith grasping grace.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The verb 'achaz carries a concrete, physical image of grasping something with the hand to secure it, prevent it from falling, or take possession of it. It is used in ancient Semitic contexts for catching prey in a trap, grasping a handle, or acquiring land. In the ancient Near East, to hold or seize something often implied a transition of ownership or a firm grip of security. In the context of Ruth 3:15, Ruth is commanded to hold out her shawl. She must actively grip it with both hands to bear the heavy weight of six measures of barley, symbolizing a joint effort of Boaz's abundant giving and Ruth's active, trusting receiving.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb 'achaz occurs over 60 times in the Hebrew Bible. The primary lexical definitions and semantic ranges include:

• To grasp, take hold of, or take possession (physically holding an object).

• To catch or take hold of (as in a snare or trap).

• To fasten or bind (structural terms, joining things together).

• To acquire or take possession of land (often used in the passive or reflexive sense, to be settled).

• To be seized by emotion (such as fear, trembling, or pain).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Physical grasping: Moses catching the serpent by the tail (Exodus 4:4), or gripping the horns of the altar for safety (1 Kings 1:51).

• Emotional seizure: Terror or trembling "taking hold" of a person or nation (Exodus 15:15; Psalm 48:6).

• Structural connection: Parts of a building or the temple being fastened or held together (1 Kings 6:6).

• Inheritance/Possession: The patriarchs obtaining a possession of land in Egypt (Genesis 47:27).

4. Theological Implications

The word connects the physical action of holding to the spiritual reality of faith and security. Just as Ruth had to hold the garment to receive the seed, the believer must grasp God's promises. Furthermore, the word is used elsewhere of God holding the believer's hand (Psalm 73:23). The theology of 'achaz involves both human responsibility (holding fast to what is good and God's provision) and divine security (God holding fast to His covenant people).

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 3:15, Boaz tells Ruth, "Bring the shawl that is on you and hold it." The word functions as an imperative, followed by the narrative confirmation, "And when she held it..." The immediate context is one of redemption, security, and transition. Ruth has risked everything at the threshing floor, asking Boaz to spread his wings over her. Boaz responds with immense generosity. The six measures of barley are so heavy she cannot carry them without using her garment as a sling. By holding the shawl, she positions herself as a recipient of his grace. Her hands are full of his provision, representing a guarantee that she will not return to her mother-in-law empty-handed. It marks the shift from Naomi's empty bitter state (Ruth 1:21) to fullness.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Psalm 73:23 - "Nevertheless I am continually with You; You hold me by my right hand."

• Song of Solomon 3:4 - "Scarcely had I passed by them, When I found the one I love. I held him and would not let him go, Until I had brought him to the house of my mother, And into the chamber of her who conceived me."

• Job 23:11 - "My foot has held fast to His steps; I have kept His way and not turned aside."

• 2 Samuel 6:6 - "And when they came to Nachon's threshing floor, Uzzah put out his hand to the ark of God and took hold of it, for the oxen stumbled."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The physical strength required of Ruth to hold this load highlights her character as a "virtuous woman" (Ruth 3:11, eshet chayil - a woman of strength/valor). The six measures of barley she "held" represent a massive weight, likely between 60 to 80 pounds. It is not a passive reception but an active, strong grip on the blessing of the redeemer. This mirrors the biblical exhortation to "hold fast" to what is good.
