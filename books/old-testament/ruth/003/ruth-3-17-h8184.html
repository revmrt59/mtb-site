BARLEY - ( se'orah , Strong's H8184 )

Summary:

Barley (se'orah) was a staple grain in ancient Israel, typically associated with the common people, the poor, and the spring harvest. In the book of Ruth, it serves as a powerful physical symbol of transition from bitter emptiness to abundant provision and covenant redemption. In Ruth 3:17, Boaz's gift of six measures of barley to Ruth is not merely a generous hand-out but a formal, symbolic guarantee of security and rest, demonstrating his commitment to redeem Naomi's family line and reverse their loss.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• Barley (se'orah) derives from the root sa'ar, which means "to bristle" or "to stand on end." This refers to the long, hair-like awns (or "beards") that project from the ears of the barley plant.

• It was the first crop harvested in Israel during the early spring, coinciding with the month of Abib/Nisan and the feast of Passover.

• Hardier than wheat, barley could grow in poorer soils and harsher climates. Because of this, it was cheaper to purchase and served as the primary food source for the lower socioeconomic classes, livestock, and people enduring times of recovery from famine. It represents basic, life-sustaining nourishment provided by God to the needy.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Se'orah refers specifically to the barley plant (Hordeum vulgare) or its harvested grain.

• The term denotes a basic agricultural commodity used for bread-making, brewing, and animal fodder.

• In commercial and legal contexts, barley served as a standard unit of trade and valuation, often valued at half the price of wheat, reflecting its status as a commoner's crop rather than a luxury item.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Sustenance for the Lowly: Barley bread was the food of the marginalized. Eating barley bread signified humility, low estate, and direct dependence on God’s basic provision.

• Symbol of Firstfruits and Deliverance: The barley harvest marked the beginning of agricultural renewal and the end of winter scarcity. The waving of the firstfruits of the barley harvest during Passover linked this grain to God's redeeming power and Israel's liberation from Egypt.

• An Instrument of Blessing or Judgment: An abundance of barley indicated divine favor and covenant blessing, while the destruction of barley crops by blight or foreign armies signified spiritual and physical ruin.

4. Theological Implications

• Providential Timing: The narrative of Ruth begins "at the beginning of barley harvest" (Ruth 1:22). This timing is a physical manifestation of God’s immediate provision for the widows who returned empty from Moab.

• Covenant Redemption: Barley is the physical medium through which Boaz expresses his grace. Boaz's abundant gifts of barley demonstrate that God does not merely provide enough for survival, but overflows with life-sustaining mercy.

• Grace to the Outsider: Ruth, a Moabitess, is allowed to glean barley, demonstrating that God's covenant grace extends to the foreigner who seeks refuge under His wings.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 3:17, Ruth returns to Naomi after her night at the threshing floor. Boaz gives her "six measures of barley."

• The Hebrew text does not state the exact unit of measurement, but the emphasis is on the quantity ("six") and the purpose ("Do not go empty-handed"). Naomi had returned from Moab "empty" (Ruth 1:21). The overflowing load of barley represents the literal and symbolic reversal of Naomi's emptiness into covenant fullness.

• The number six is highly symbolic. It represents the six days of labor prior to the Sabbath rest. By giving Ruth six measures of barley, Boaz provides a pledge of the ultimate "seventh-day" rest he intends to secure for her through marriage and redemption.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 9:31 - "Now the flax and the barley were struck, for the barley was in the head and the flax was in bud."

• Judges 7:13 - "And Gideon came, and there was a man telling a dream to his companion. He said, 'I have just had a dream: To my surprise, a loaf of barley bread tumbled into the camp of Midian; it came to a tent and struck it so that it fell, and overturned, and the tent collapsed.'"

• Ruth 1:22 - "So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabitess her daughter-in-law with her, who returned from the country of Moab. Now they came to Bethlehem at the beginning of barley harvest."

• Ruth 2:17 - "So she gleaned in the field until evening, and beat out what she had gleaned, and it was about an ephah of barley."

• 2 Kings 4:42 - "Then a man came from Baal-Shalisha, and brought the man of God bread of the firstfruits, twenty loaves of barley bread, and newly ripened grain in his knapsack. And he said, 'Give it to the people, that they may eat.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Weight of the Gift: Six seahs (the likely unit for the "six measures") would equal about two ephahs, or roughly 40 to 60 pounds of grain. Carrying this weight demonstrates both Ruth's physical strength as a "virtuous woman" (Ruth 3:11) and Boaz’s extraordinary, life-altering generosity.

• New Testament Connection: The connection between barley and divine multiplication is completed in John 6:9, where a young boy's "five barley loaves" are used by Jesus to feed the five thousand, showing how God uses the humble crop of the poor to accomplish His greatest miracles of provision.
