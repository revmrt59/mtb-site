CONCLUDED - ( kalah , Strong's H3615 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb kalah means to bring a process to complete realization, to finish, to accomplish, or to settle a matter fully. In the context of Ruth 3:18, it denotes Boaz’s relentless determination to fully resolve and execute the redemption of Naomi and Ruth without delay. It conveys a sense of finality and decisive action, assuring the reader that the kinsman-redeemer will not rest until his covenant obligation is completely fulfilled.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete visual background of kalah is the drawing of a process to its absolute boundary or limit, leaving nothing left undone. It is the picture of a vessel being completely emptied, a journey fully traveled, or a building project completed to the very last stone. The word points to the transition from preparation to absolute realization. Once kalah occurs, the old state of waiting is over, and a new reality is established. In ancient Near Eastern legal and covenantal transactions, to "conclude" meant to finalize a binding agreement so thoroughly that no legal loopholes remained and no further debate could occur.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary lexical definition of kalah is to end, to finish, to complete, to accomplish, or to bring to pass. The verb exists in several stems, most notably the Qal and Piel:

• In the Qal stem, it often means to be complete, to be finished, to be spent, or to come to an end.

• In the Piel stem (as used in Ruth 3:18), it functions transitively, meaning to complete, to finish, to perform, or to bring to a successful conclusion.

• The semantic range spans constructive completion (such as finishing the construction of the tabernacle or temple) to exhaustive consumption (such as eyes failing from weeping or a supply of water being totally spent). In covenant and legal matters, it means to settle or execute a decision decisively.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, kalah is used in several rich contexts:

• Creative and Constructive Finish: It describes God finishing the heavens and the earth (Genesis 2:1–2) and Moses finishing the Tabernacle (Exodus 40:33). This usage represents the orderly execution of a divine plan.

• Satiation and Depletion: It describes a container being completely emptied of its contents, such as Hagar’s water skin being entirely spent (Genesis 21:15).

• The Completion of Speech: It frequently introduces the moment a speaker finishes delivering their message, emphasizing that nothing more needs to be said (Genesis 18:33).

• Relentless Execution of Purpose: When applied to human actions of high resolve, it describes a person who has set their mind on a course of action and will not stop until the purpose is fully accomplished.

### 4. Theological Implications

Theologically, kalah reveals that God is a finisher. He is not a God of half-measures or incomplete projects. Just as He "finished" (kalah) creation in Genesis 2, and Moses "finished" (kalah) the Tabernacle to allow God’s glory to dwell among men, God’s redemptive plans always move toward a definitive, completed end.

When applied to Boaz, who acts as the kinsman-redeemer (goel), kalah serves as a powerful type of Jesus Christ. Boaz’s refusal to rest until he has "concluded" the matter reflects the relentless grace of Christ. Our Redeemer did not leave the work of salvation unfinished; rather, He took it to its absolute completion on the cross, crying out, "It is finished" (John 19:30).

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 3:18, Naomi tells Ruth, "Sit still, my daughter, until you know how the matter will fall; for the man will not rest until he has concluded the matter this day."

The context is crucial: Ruth has just returned from the threshing floor after risking her reputation to ask Boaz to spread his wings over her as redeemer. Boaz has accepted the responsibility but noted there is a closer relative who has first rights.

Naomi, possessing deep wisdom and understanding of Boaz's character, recognizes that Boaz is a man of covenant integrity. By stating he will not rest until he has "concluded" (kalah) the matter today, Naomi assures Ruth of three things:

• Urgency: Boaz will act "this day." There will be no bureaucratic delay.

• Thoroughness: He will not leave the legal details partially resolved. He will confront the closer relative at the gate and secure a final verdict.

• Certainty: The Hebrew construction emphasizes that the outcome is guaranteed. Ruth's period of striving and striving to survive is over; she must now rest in the active, concluding work of her redeemer.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:2 - "And on the seventh day God ended His work which He had done, and He rested on the seventh day from all His work which He had done."

• Genesis 18:33 - "So the Lord went His way as soon as He had left speaking with Abraham; and Abraham returned to his place."

• Exodus 40:33 - "And he raised up the court all around the tabernacle and the altar, and hung up the screen of the court gate. So Moses finished the work."

• 1 Kings 6:38 - "And in the eleventh year, in the month of Bul, which is the eighth month, the house was finished in all its details and according to all its plans."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a beautiful homonymic relationship in the Hebrew text. The root verb kalah (to finish/conclude) is phonetically and structurally close to the Hebrew noun kallah, which means "bride" or "daughter-in-law" (used frequently of Ruth herself in Ruth 1:6, 1:7, 1:8, 1:22). While they are distinct words, the auditory connection would not be lost on an ancient Hebrew listener: the redeemer will not "conclude" (kalah) his labor until he has fully secured his "bride" (kallah). The work of redemption is intrinsically tied to the acquisition of the bride.
