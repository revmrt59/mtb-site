TONIGHT - ( laylah , Strong's H3915 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word laylah refers to the period of darkness between sunset and sunrise. Prefixed with the definite article in Ruth 3:2, it translates as "tonight." This specific night marks a critical turning point in the book of Ruth, representing the transition from vulnerability and widowhood to security and redemption. Winnowing grain at night took advantage of the cooler evening breezes, setting the stage for Ruth’s bold, faith-filled encounter with Boaz at the threshing floor.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The word laylah represents the night, the natural cycle of darkness established at Creation to contrast with the light of day. In the ancient Near East, night was a time of vulnerability, rest, and occasionally, strategic or clandestine activities. For agricultural workers, particularly during the harvest season, the late afternoon and early night hours were highly productive. Threshing floors were situated on elevated areas or ridges to catch the prevailing evening winds. Winnowing involved throwing the crushed grain into the air; the heavy grain fell to the floor, while the wind blew the lighter chaff away. Because these breezes typically picked up at dusk and continued into the night, the work of winnowing frequently extended into the evening hours.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun laylah refers to the night, the dark hours of the day, or midnight. When prefixed with the Hebrew article ha- (hallylah), it functions temporally to mean "this night" or "tonight." The term denotes the literal period of darkness, but it also carries metaphorical weight in Hebrew thought, often representing times of distress, waiting, secrecy, or divine visitation.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Contrast with Day: Throughout the Old Testament, laylah is paired with yom (day) to express the totality of time, such as meditating on the law day and night.

• Time of Divine Action: God frequently moves during the laylah. The Passover took place at night, the Exodus began at night, and God regularly spoke to His prophets and patriarchs in night visions.

• Vulnerability and Protection: The night is a time of vulnerability where protective measures must be taken. In the narrative of Ruth, Naomi exploits this vulnerable yet opportunistic window to secure Ruth's future.

4. Theological Implications

The transition from night to day mirrors the overarching theological movement of the Book of Ruth: from the night of grief, famine, and widowhood to the morning of joy, redemption, and new life. Naomi's instruction to Ruth to go "tonight" highlights human initiative cooperating with divine providence. The dark hours at the threshing floor do not hide immoral acts, but rather provide a private, solemn space for a covenantal appeal. Redemption often occurs in the night seasons of life. God uses the cover of night not for the secrecy of sin, but to work out His sovereign, redemptive purposes quietly and powerfully.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 3:2, "tonight" is highly strategic. Naomi knows Boaz will be winnowing barley at the threshing floor because of the evening breeze. He will also sleep there to guard his valuable harvest from thieves. This setting provides the perfect, uninterrupted opportunity for Ruth to approach Boaz privately. The mention of "tonight" builds dramatic tension. Instead of a tale of compromise, "tonight" becomes the stage for an extraordinary demonstration of covenant loyalty (chesed) by both Ruth and Boaz.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The threshing floor at night was historically associated with pagan fertility rites and sexual compromise in the ancient Near East. Naomi's plan was incredibly risky. By contrast, Ruth’s behavior "tonight" reclaims the night and the threshing floor for righteousness. Instead of seduction, she requests legal redemption under the wings of the Lord. Furthermore, the cover of darkness protected Ruth's reputation. Boaz was highly sensitive to this, ensuring she left before anyone could recognize her in the morning, guarding her honor.
