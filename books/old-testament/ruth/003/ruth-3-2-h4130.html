RELATIVE - ( moda'ath , Strong's H4131 )

Summary:

In Ruth 3:2, the English word "relative" translates the Hebrew noun moda'ath (which is linguistically intertwined with moledeth, Strong's H4130, and moda', Strong's H4129). Derived from the root verb yada ("to know"), this term denotes a known person, an intimate acquaintance, or a kinsman with whom one has a recognized, binding covenantal relationship. In the story of Naomi and Ruth, Boaz is not merely a distant blood relative, but a recognized member of the family clan who possesses the legal standing and potential moral responsibility to act as their redeemer (goel). The term highlights a relationship built on mutual recognition, legal obligation, and covenantal knowledge.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The Hebrew noun moda'ath and its close relatives moda' (kinsman) and moledeth (kindred/birth) derive from the primary verb yada, which means "to know" intimately, deeply, and experientially.

• In ancient Israelite culture, a relative was not merely someone who shared genetic markers, but someone "known" within the covenant community as carrying specific mutual obligations.

• The word picture is one of relational recognition. A moda'ath is not a stranger (nokri), but someone within the recognized boundaries of the family clan (mishpachah) who has an active, acknowledged place in the household's social and legal sphere.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Moda'ath refers to a kinsman, relative, or intimate acquaintance.

• The closely related term moledeth (Strong's H4130) refers to kindred, birth, offspring, or homeland.

• The lexical range of this word group encompasses:

• An intimate associate or recognized friend.

• A blood relative or kinsman within the clan.

• A person with recognized legal and familial status capable of redeeming land, maintaining lineage, or inheriting family obligations.

• The term emphasizes recognized kinship and covenant responsibility rather than mere biological connection.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• In the Old Testament, the family unit was the primary source of economic, social, and spiritual security. To have a moda'ath (relative) was to have a safety net in times of destitution, debt, or death.

• The word picture of "knowing" (yada) implies that the relative is fully aware of the family's plight and cannot easily plead ignorance. To be a relative means to acknowledge the bond of kinship and to be legally and morally visible to the family in need.

4. Theological Implications

• The concept of the close relative is foundational to the biblical theology of redemption (geulah). God instituted the laws of the kinsman-redeemer (goel) to prevent the permanent loss of family land and the extinction of a family line.

• For redemption to occur, the redeemer had to meet three criteria: he had to be a close relative, he had to have the financial means to redeem, and he had to be willing to do so.

• This legal-relational framework points directly to Jesus Christ, the ultimate Kinsman-Redeemer. He took on human flesh—becoming our relative—so that He might have the legal right to redeem us from the bondage of sin and death.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 3:2, Naomi asks Ruth, "Now Boaz, whose young women you were with, is he not our relative (moda'ath)?"

• Naomi uses this specific term to transition Ruth from a state of passive survival (gleaning in the fields) to active pursuit of redemption.

• By identifying Boaz as their moda'ath, Naomi establishes his legal standing to act on their behalf. He is not an outsider; he is a recognized kinsman who has already shown extraordinary kindness (chesed) to Ruth. This realization sets the stage for Ruth's midnight appeal to Boaz at the threshing floor.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Ruth 2:1 – "There was a relative (moda', Strong's H4129) of Naomi's husband, a man of great wealth, of the family of Elimelech; his name was Boaz."

• Ruth 2:11 – "...and how you have left your father and your mother and the land of your birth (moledeth, Strong's H4130)..."

• Genesis 12:1 – "Get out of your country, from your family (moledeth, Strong's H4130) and from your father's house, to a land that I will show you."

• Genesis 24:4 – "but you shall go to my country and to my family (moledeth, Strong's H4130), and take a wife for my son Isaac."

• Proverbs 7:4 – "Say to wisdom, 'You are my sister,' and call understanding your nearest kin (moda', Strong's H4129)."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• There is a beautiful literary symmetry in the Book of Ruth. In Ruth 2:11, Boaz commends Ruth for leaving the land of her birth (moledeth / H4130) to come to a people she did not know. In Ruth 3:2, Naomi reveals that Boaz is their close relative (moda'ath / H4131). Ruth surrendered her earthly, pagan family, and in return, Yahweh provided her with a redeeming covenant family.
