DOWN - ( yarad , Strong's H3381 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yarad literally means to descend, move from a higher elevation to a lower one, or go down. In the Old Testament, it serves as a foundational verb of physical movement, but it frequently carries profound spiritual, social, and relational significance. In the context of Ruth 3:3, Naomi instructs Ruth to "go down" to the threshing floor of Boaz. This physical descent from the elevated town of Bethlehem to the lower-lying agricultural plains symbolizes a step of deep humility, vulnerability, and radical faith, setting the stage for one of Scripture's most beautiful pictures of redemption.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The verb yarad is a common Semitic root occurring over 1,000 times in the Hebrew Bible. Its primary, concrete meaning is to descend or move downward. Ancient geography heavily influenced the use of this word. Cities were typically built on hills for defense (such as Bethlehem or Jerusalem), while agricultural fields, water sources, and threshing floors were situated in the lower-lying valleys or plains. Therefore, leaving a city to go to a field or threshing floor always required a literal, physical descent. Beyond the physical landscape, "going down" often carries a cultural and psychological weight of lowering oneself, stepping into vulnerability, or initiating a significant transition.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb yarad refers to the action of descending, coming down, or going down. It describes:

• Physical movement from a higher to a lower geographical point (such as going down a mountain, going down to Egypt, or going down to a valley).

• Descending from a vehicle or animal.

• The falling of rain, dew, or tears.

• Metaphorically, descending into the grave (Sheol), declining in social or economic status, or experiencing a state of humiliation and sorrow.

• The divine action of God "coming down" to intervene in human affairs, either in judgment or in mercy.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The verb yarad is rich with word pictures across the Old Testament:

• The Divine Condescension: When God observes human rebellion (as at the Tower of Babel) or when He prepares to deliver His people (as at the burning bush), He "comes down." This depicts a transcendent, infinite God actively entering time and space to engage with His creation.

• Spiritual and Moral Decline: "Going down" is often used of journeying to Egypt or other foreign lands, symbolizing a spiritual descent from reliance on Yahweh to reliance on worldly power and pagan alliances.

• Humiliation and Death: Descending into the pit or Sheol represents the ultimate physical and spiritual lowering of a person, signaling weakness, defeat, mortality, or absolute grief.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, yarad reminds believers that redemption requires condescension and humility. Just as God must "come down" to save humanity because we cannot climb up to Him, those who seek redemption must often humble themselves and "go down" to a place of vulnerability. Ruth's willingness to go down to the threshing floor mirrors the posture of faith that abandons self-preservation and relies entirely on the mercy of a redeemer. This prefigures the ultimate condescension of Jesus Christ, who emptied Himself and came down from heaven to serve, suffer, and redeem His people.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 3:3, Naomi tells Ruth: "Therefore wash yourself and anoint yourself, put on your best garment and go down to the threshing floor..." The threshing floors of Bethlehem were located on the flatter, lower plains below the elevated town. To obey Naomi, Ruth must literally descend from the relative safety of the city walls under the cover of night.

This physical descent represents a massive spiritual, social, and moral risk. A young, foreign widow entering a male-dominated threshing floor at night risked her reputation, her safety, and her entire future. By "going down," Ruth actively humbles herself, laying aside her security to appeal to Boaz as her kinsman-redeemer. Her physical descent reflects her inward posture of total surrender and trust in God's providence.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 11:5 - "But the LORD came down to see the city and the tower which the sons of men had built."

• Exodus 3:8 - "So I have come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians, and to bring them up from that land to a good and large land..."

• Judges 14:1 - "Now Samson went down to Timnah, and saw a woman in Timnah of the daughters of the Philistines."

• Psalm 133:3 - "It is like the dew of Hermon, Descending upon the mountains of Zion; For there the LORD commanded the blessing— Life forevermore."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between "going down" (yarad) and "going up" (alah) is a recurring motif in Hebrew narrative. While "going up" is often associated with worship, ascending to the temple, or spiritual exaltation, "going down" is the necessary precursor to being lifted up by God. Ruth's descent to the threshing floor ultimately leads to her being raised up to a position of honor, security, marriage, and inclusion in the direct lineage of King David and Jesus Christ. This illustrates the biblical principle that humility precedes honor.
