FINISHED - ( kalah , Strong's H3615 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb kalah carries the primary meaning of bringing something to completion, ending, or fully consuming a task or process. In the pastoral context of Ruth 3:3, Naomi instructs Ruth to wait until Boaz has "finished" eating and drinking before making her presence known. This word signals the transition from labor and physical nourishment to a time of rest, celebration, and ultimate decision. It underscores a divine timing where every preparatory step is completed before the critical encounter takes place, illustrating how God's providential plans unfold in orderly, completed stages.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root kalah evokes the completion of a cycle. It is used to describe finishing a physical project (like building the tabernacle or temple), completing a speech, or bringing food and drink to an end. The background points to a boundary line where what was in progress is now entirely done, leaving nothing incomplete or lacking. In agrarian life, finishing a meal after a hard day of harvesting and threshing marked the shift from exhausting labor to secure rest. It represents the satisfaction of a job well done and the peaceful transition into a time of repose.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb kalah means to complete, bring to an end, finish, or be spent. In its various verbal stems, it expresses the execution of a task to its absolute conclusion, or the state of being fully ended or consumed. It does not merely signify stopping an action, but successfully achieving the intended goal of that action. It denotes completion in terms of quantity, time, or purpose, ensuring that the process is rounded out perfectly.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament uses kalah in several graphic ways:

• Creative and Constructive Completion: Used for finishing the creation of the heavens and the earth, as well as finishing the construction of the tabernacle and the temple.

• Communication: Used when a speaker has finished delivering all their words, indicating that nothing more needs to be said and the message is fully communicated.

• Consumption and Exhaustion: Used when food, water, or physical strength is completely spent, indicating that the container or resource is entirely empty.

• Resolution of Time: The completion of an appointed period of time or a specific event, allowing a transition into a new season.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, kalah highlights the orderly and sovereign timing of God. God is not a God of half-finished works; when He acts, He brings His purposes to absolute completion. Just as He finished the work of creation, He finishes His plans of redemption and provision. In the book of Ruth, this word reminds us that human efforts and divine providence work in harmony. Boaz's finishing of his meal represents the transition to the next stage of Naomi’s plan, showing that God's timing operates through the natural completions of daily life. It reassures believers that God will complete the good work He has started in them.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 3:3, Naomi tells Ruth to wait until Boaz "has finished eating and drinking." The context of the threshing floor is filled with joy and relief after a successful harvest. Naomi's strategy requires precise timing. If Ruth approaches too early, she disrupts his rest; if she waits too long, the opportunity is lost. By waiting until Boaz has "finished," Ruth approaches him when he is content, relaxed, and secure. This nuance emphasizes patience and respect for order, ensuring that the redeemer is in the best frame of mind to receive a proposal of marriage and redemption.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:1 - "Thus the heavens and the earth, and all the host of them, were finished."

• Genesis 24:15 - "And it happened, before he had finished speaking, that behold, Rebekah... came out with her pitcher on her shoulder."

• Genesis 49:33 - "And when Jacob had finished commanding his sons, he drew his feet up into the bed and breathed his last, and was gathered to his people."

• 1 Kings 6:38 - "And in the eleventh year, in the month of Bul, which is the eighth month, the house was finished in all its details and according to all its plans."

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a beautiful parallel between Boaz finishing his meal before redeeming Ruth, and the ultimate Redeemer, Jesus Christ. On the cross, Jesus cried out, "It is finished!" (using the Greek equivalent tetelestai, which shares the semantic force of kalah in contexts of completion). Just as Boaz had to finish his daily work and sustenance before he could address the redemption of Ruth, Christ completed the entire work of redemption on our behalf, allowing us to enter into His eternal rest.
