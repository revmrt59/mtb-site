NOTICE - ( yada , Strong's H3045 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yada carries a deep, experiential meaning that goes far beyond mere intellectual apprehension. While often translated as "to know," in the context of Naomi's instructions to Ruth, it means to observe closely, mark, or notice with careful attention. It describes an active, intentional perception rather than a passive glance. Throughout the Old Testament, yada encompasses relational intimacy, covenantal commitment, and experiential realization. In Ruth 3:4, it serves as a critical tactical and relational instruction, directing Ruth to carefully observe and mark the exact location where Boaz lies down, setting the stage for her bold appeal to him as her kinsman-redeemer.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The verb yada is one of the most vital and frequently used terms in the Hebrew Bible. Its foundational concept is not academic or theoretical, but experiential, relational, and sensory. To "know" or "notice" in the Hebrew mind is to encounter reality directly through seeing, hearing, touching, and personal experience.

Unlike Greek philosophical concepts of knowledge, which emphasize abstract contemplation and mental categorization, Hebrew thought links knowledge to action, relationship, and responsibility. In a physical or spatial context, yada means to familiarize oneself so thoroughly with a person, object, or location that one can recognize it instantly and act appropriately. It is the visual and mental process of marking something for a specific, purposeful end.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb yada has a broad semantic range that adapts to its grammatical stem and literary context. In the Qal stem, which is used in Ruth 3:4, the primary meanings include:

• To perceive, observe, or notice through the physical senses.

• To acquire knowledge or information about a fact, person, or situation.

• To recognize or discern a particular reality.

• To experience or undergo a condition (such as knowing grief, peace, or sickness).

• To enter into relational intimacy, including the covenantal bond of marriage.

• To acknowledge, care for, or take notice of someone with protective interest.

In Ruth 3:4, the specific nuance is active visual observation and mental marking. Ruth is commanded to "notice" or "mark" the precise place where Boaz lies down so that she can locate him without error in the dark.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Observation and Discernment: yada frequently pairs with physical sight. It is the transition from merely looking at something to understanding its significance. It is the difference between a passing glance and a focused, purposeful gaze that registers details.

• Relational Intimacy: It is the word used for the most intimate human union, as well as the deep, protective relationship God has with His covenant people.

• Covenant Recognition: In legal and social transactions, yada implies acknowledging a claim or duty. When a person "knows" a legal right or a relative's need, they are expected to act upon that knowledge.

### 4. Theological Implications

The theological depth of yada lies in its insistence that true knowledge must lead to relationship and action.

• Divine Initiative and Human Response: Throughout scripture, God’s "knowing" of His people is an act of sovereign choice and affectionate care. In response, human obedience is characterized by "knowing" God—acknowledging His character, obeying His commands, and recognizing His hand in history.

• The Providential Details: Naomi’s instruction to "notice" the spot demonstrates that faith operates through practical wisdom. God's providential plan to redeem the line of Elimelech is worked out through careful, deliberate, and respectful human steps. Ruth’s act of noticing is not trivial; it is the physical mechanism through which she positions herself to receive Boaz's redemptive commitment.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 3:4, Naomi instructs Ruth: "Then it shall be, when he lies down, that you shall notice the place where he lies..."

This instructions occurs during the harvest celebration at the threshing floor. The environment was dark, crowded, and chaotic, with multiple workers sleeping near their respective grain heaps to guard their hard-earned harvest from thieves. For a young foreign woman like Ruth, entering this space was highly sensitive and potentially dangerous.

If Ruth approached the wrong man in the dark, she would risk her reputation, safety, and the entire hope of redemption. Therefore, Naomi charges her to yada—to watch Boaz from a safe distance, tracking his movements as he finishes eating and settles down to sleep. She must lock her eyes on his specific resting place so that she can approach him with absolute certainty under the cover of night. The word highlights the caution, wisdom, and focused attention required to execute this bold appeal for redemption.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 4:1 - "Now Adam knew Eve his wife, and she conceived and bore Cain..." (Relational and physical intimacy).

• Exodus 3:7 - "And the LORD said: 'I have surely seen the oppression of My people who are in Egypt, and have heard their cry because of their taskmasters, for I know their sorrows;'" (Experiential, compassionate awareness).

• Psalm 1:6 - "For the LORD knows the way of the righteous, But the way of the ungodly shall perish." (Protective, covenantal care).

• Psalm 46:10 - "Be still, and know that I am God; I will be exalted among the nations, I will be exalted in the earth!" (Experiential certainty and recognition of sovereignty).

• Proverbs 3:6 - "In all your ways acknowledge Him, And He shall direct your paths." (Intellectual and practical submission to God’s lordship).

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

A subtle wordplay exists between yada ("notice/know") and nakar ("recognize/take notice of"), which appears earlier in the narrative. In Ruth 2:10, Ruth marvels that Boaz should "take notice" (nakar) of her, a foreign stranger. Now, in chapter 3, the tables are gently turned. Under Naomi's guidance, Ruth must "notice" (yada) the place where Boaz lies, initiating the process where Boaz will legally and intimately "know" (yada) and claim her as his bride, removing her status as a stranger and establishing her place in Israel.
