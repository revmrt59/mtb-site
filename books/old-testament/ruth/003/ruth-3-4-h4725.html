PLACE - ( maqowm , Strong's H4725 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word maqowm refers to a physical space, station, or locality where someone stands, dwells, or acts. In the narrative of Ruth 3:4, it denotes the specific, observable spot on the threshing floor where Boaz lies down to sleep. This "place" serves as the focal point for Ruth's act of faith, obedience, and symbolic appeal for redemption, transforming a common agricultural site into a sacred space of covenantal loyalty and divine providence.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• maqowm is derived from the root verb qum, which means "to arise," "to stand up," or "to establish."

• Structurally, the prefix ma- indicates a physical location or instrument. Therefore, a maqowm is literally "the place where one stands" or "a station."

• In the Ancient Near East, a threshing floor was a flat, elevated, open-air area where grain was separated from chaff. At night, it became a vulnerable spot where owners slept to guard their valuable harvest. Naomi's instruction to Ruth to mark this specific spot (maqowm) is highly strategic, requiring careful observation in the dark.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Denotes a physical place, spot, or locality.

• Refers to a room, space, or region.

• Indicates a home, dwelling place, or site of residence.

• Signifies a place of worship, sanctuary, or sacred site.

• In the context of Ruth 3:4, it defines a precise, localized position where Boaz's head and feet would lie.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• A Place of Rest: Used to describe physical locations of rest or sleep. Much like Jacob’s maqowm in Genesis 28, a common, ordinary spot can become a portal of divine encounter and promise.

• A Place of Security: In the law of Moses, a maqowm was designated as a city of refuge—a safe space for those fleeing avengers.

• A Place of Presence: Often used of the Tabernacle or Temple as the maqowm where God chooses to make His name dwell.

4. Theological Implications

• Providential Positioning: God uses specific, seemingly ordinary physical spaces to accomplish extraordinary redemptive purposes. Just as Jacob happened upon "a certain place," Ruth is directed to a specific "place" that will seal her future.

• The Intersection of Duty and Redemption: The threshing floor—a place of hard labor and commercial transaction—becomes the setting of covenantal commitment (hesed).

• The Redeemer's Station: Boaz's place of sleep represents his vulnerability and accessibility as a kinsman-redeemer, prefiguring the ultimate Redeemer who pitched His tent among us.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 3:4, Naomi instructs Ruth to "notice the place (maqowm) where he lies." In the darkness of the threshing floor, filled with sleeping workers, identifying the exact spot was critical. It required patience, stealth, and acute attentiveness.

• This was not a casual looking, but a deliberate "marking" or "knowing" of the location.

• The maqowm becomes a boundary of transition: Ruth steps out of her widowhood and into the place of proposed marriage and redemption. By lying at Boaz's feet in this specific place, she claims her legal and spiritual place under the wings of the Lord.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 28:11 - "So he came to a certain place and stayed there all night, because the sun had set. And he took one of the stones of that place and put it at his head, and he lay down in that place to sleep."

• Exodus 3:5 - "Then He said, 'Do not draw near this place. Take your sandals off your feet, for the place where you stand is holy ground.'"

• 1 Samuel 20:19 - "And when you have stayed three days, go down quickly and come to the place where you hid on the day of the deed; and remain by the stone Ezel."

• Proverbs 15:3 - "The eyes of the Lord are in every place, keeping watch on the evil and the good."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• In later Jewish thought, Hamakoam ("The Place") became a metonym and rabbinic name for God Himself, emphasizing that God is not contained by the universe, but the universe is contained within Him.

• The journey of Ruth is a transition of places: from Moab (a place of curse and death), to Bethlehem (the place of bread), to the threshing floor (the place of decision), to Boaz's home (the place of rest and legacy).
