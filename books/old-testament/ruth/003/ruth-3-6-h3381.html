WENT DOWN - ( yarad , Strong's H3381 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yarad means to descend, go down, or come down from a higher elevation to a lower one. In Ruth 3:6, it describes Ruth's physical descent from the elevated town of Bethlehem to the threshing floor located in the lower valley. Beyond the literal geographical movement, this word carries profound theological and relational weight, symbolizing Ruth's humble submission, active obedience, and willingness to lower herself to secure redemption for her family.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• Bethlehem is situated in the hill country of Judah, sitting on a ridge. Threshing floors, by contrast, were typically located on lower, flat areas, such as low ridges or valley floors, to catch the evening breezes necessary for winnowing grain.

• To go from Bethlehem to the threshing floor required a physical descent down the hillside.

• In ancient Near Eastern culture, going "down" often signified leaving a place of safety or established community to enter a space of vulnerability, testing, or potential danger. Ruth left the security of her home under the cover of darkness to place herself in a highly vulnerable position at the feet of Boaz.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The primary meaning of yarad is to go down, descend, decline, march down, or sink.

• It is a high-frequency verb occurring over 370 times in the Old Testament, representing a highly versatile root that expresses literal vertical movement, social humbling, or a descent into death (going down to Sheol).

• In the Qal stem (the simple active form used in Ruth 3:6), it denotes the physical act of descending a slope, stairs, or pathway.

• In causative forms (the Hiphil stem), it means to bring down, lay down, or cast down. In the context of Ruth 3:6, the active Qal form highlights Ruth's personal agency and deliberate choice to obey Naomi’s instructions.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Old Testament uses yarad to describe physical journeys from the highlands of Canaan to the lowlands of Egypt, often indicating a shift from a place of promise to a place of trial (Genesis 12:10).

• It is used of divine condescension, where God "comes down" to inspect human affairs, deliver His people, or reveal His glory (Exodus 19:11).

• The word also paints a picture of humbling or lowering of status. To "go down" is to occupy a lower position of service, submission, or vulnerability.

• In the narrative of Ruth, yarad marks the pivot point where Naomi's strategic plan is set into motion. Naomi tells Ruth to "go down" (Ruth 3:3), and Ruth immediately "went down" (Ruth 3:6). This physical descent is a picture of step-by-step obedience.

4. Theological Implications

• Humility and Submission: Ruth's physical descent mirrors her spiritual posture. She is a Moabite widow lowering herself before an Israelite redeemer. Her obedience to Naomi, despite the risk to her reputation and personal safety, showcases the biblical pattern of lowering oneself before being lifted up by God's grace.

• Active Faith: True faith is not static; it descends into the realities of life. Ruth did not merely agree with Naomi's advice; she acted upon it by physically walking down to the threshing floor.

• Redemptive Condescension: The passage foreshadows the ultimate kinsman-redeemer, Jesus Christ, who descended from the heights of heaven to the lowliness of earth to redeem His bride. Ruth's descent to seek redemption from Boaz echoes the pattern of the incarnation, where the redeemer and the one seeking redemption meet in a place of lowliness.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 3:6, the use of yarad signals the transition from private counsel to public (though secretive) action.

• The threshing floor was a place of harvest, joy, and abundance, but also a place of potential moral hazard, particularly at night. By going down, Ruth was leaving the protective custody of Bethlehem's city gates to venture into a risky environment, trusting entirely in the character of Boaz and the protection of Yahweh.

• The physical descent highlights the contrast between the safe, elevated town and the exposed, dark valley, magnifying the courage and determination of Ruth's faith.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 12:10: "Now there was a famine in the land, and Abram went down to Egypt to dwell there, for the famine was severe in the land."

• Exodus 19:11: "And let them be ready for the third day. For on the third day the Lord will come down upon Mount Sinai in the sight of all the people."

• Jonah 1:3: "But Jonah arose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the Lord. He went down to Joppa, and found a ship going to Tarshish; so he paid the fare, and went down into it, to go with them to Tarshish from the presence of the Lord."

• Psalm 133:3: "It is like the dew of Hermon, descending upon the mountains of Zion; For there the Lord commanded the blessing— Life forevermore."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The structural symmetry of the book of Ruth employs geographical movements to trace spiritual conditions. Elimelech's family went down to Moab and suffered loss; Naomi and Ruth returned up to Bethlehem and found hope. Now, Ruth's temporary descent to the threshing floor secures the permanent elevation of her family line.

• The threshing floor was a place of separation—where the wheat was separated from the chaff. Ruth's descent to this specific location represents a major turning point where her past identity as a destitute Moabitess is about to be separated from her new identity as a redeemed wife and mother in Israel.
