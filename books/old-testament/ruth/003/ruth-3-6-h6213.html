DID - ( asah , Strong's H6213 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb asah is one of the most fundamental and versatile action words in the Old Testament, meaning to do, make, fashion, or accomplish. In Ruth 3:6, it highlights Ruth’s prompt, complete, and faithful obedience to Naomi's highly unusual and culturally delicate instructions. Rather than acting out of self-interest, hesitation, or fear of reputational damage, Ruth translates her covenant loyalty (chesed) into concrete, observable action, illustrating that true biblical faith is characterized by active obedience and trust in God's providential care.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The primary root verb asah occurs over 2,600 times in the Hebrew Bible. While the verb bara is used exclusively for divine creation out of nothing, asah emphasizes the active process of fashioning, executing, or carrying out a plan from existing materials or commands. It is the language of labor, construction, performance, and ethical action, picturing the transition from a mental concept or verbal instruction into physical reality. In the ancient Near East, actions spoke far louder than abstract declarations; to "do" was the ultimate measure of a person's character and covenant alignment.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The semantic range of asah spans "to do," "to make," "to produce," "to perform," "to execute," and "to practice." Depending on its grammatical context, it refers to physical manufacture (such as building the tabernacle), performing a duty (keeping the law), or accomplishing a specific course of action. In the context of a command or instruction, it denotes full execution and obedience, translating an authoritative word into completed deed.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Creation and Craftsmanship: God "made" (asah) the firmament, the sun, and the moon (Genesis 1:7, 16). Here, the word pictures divine craftsmanship shaping raw material into order and beauty.

• Covenant Obedience: God commands Israel to "do" all His commandments (Deuteronomy 6:25). Obedience is not merely mental assent but physical action.

• Preparing Food or Offerings: Often used for "preparing" a meal or a sacrifice (Genesis 18:7, Leviticus 9:7), suggesting careful preparation and presentation.

• Completing a Charge: The word pictures a servant carrying out a master’s exact orders to completion, leaving nothing unfinished.

4. Theological Implications

• The link between faith and works: Just as New Testament writers argue that faith without works is dead, the Old Testament conceptualizes righteousness through asah—the active doing of the good.

• Covenant loyalty (chesed) is not a passive emotion; it is always demonstrated through concrete action. Ruth's loyalty to Naomi and Yahweh is validated by what she does.

• The reflection of divine action: Humans are called to "do" because they are made in the image of a God who acts. Our faithful doing mirrors God's faithful doing in redemption and providence.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 3:6, asah occurs at a critical narrative pivot. Naomi has laid out a highly risky, counter-cultural plan for Ruth to approach Boaz at the threshing floor at night. The plan carries immense moral, physical, and social risk for a foreign widow.

The text emphasizes the exact correspondence between Naomi's command and Ruth's execution: "she did according to all that her mother-in-law instructed her." The use of asah here highlights Ruth's absolute trust. She does not modify, negotiate, or delay; she executes the plan precisely. Her obedience is the human catalyst for Boaz's redemption of the family line, showing how human action aligns with divine providence.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 1:31: "Then God saw everything that He had made, and indeed it was very good."

• Exodus 19:8: "Then all the people answered together and said, 'All that the Lord has spoken we will do.'"

• Joshua 24:31: "...who had known all the works of the Lord which He had done for Israel."

• Ruth 1:8: "And Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law, 'Go, return each to her mother's house. The Lord deal kindly with you, as you have dealt with the dead and with me.'"

• Ruth 3:11: "And now, my daughter, do not fear. I will do for you all that you request, for all the people of my town know that you are a virtuous woman."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• asah contrasted with bara: While bara is used exclusively of God's effortless, miraculous creation (Genesis 1:1), asah is used of both God and humans, emphasizing the process of labor, molding, and finishing. This reminds believers that our daily, mundane labors, when done in faith, participate in the unfolding of God's redemptive plan.

• The structure of Ruth 3 shows a perfect parallel relationship between Naomi's detailed instructions (Ruth 3:1-5) and Ruth's exact execution (Ruth 3:6-9). The repetition of asah binds these sections together, proving that Ruth’s integrity matches her instruction.
