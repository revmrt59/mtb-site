INSTRUCTED - ( tsavah , Strong's H6680 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb tsavah conveys the act of giving a binding charge, command, or instruction. It is not merely a polite suggestion, but an authoritative direction carrying the weight of the speaker's position and the expectation of complete obedience. In Ruth 3:6, it highlights Ruth’s deep trust and submission to Naomi, as she carries out her mother-in-law's specific, culturally sensitive directives at the threshing floor of Boaz.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• tsavah is a foundational covenantal word in the Old Testament, most frequently used for divine commands. The underlying word picture is that of setting up a boundary, assigning a duty, or establishing a pathway. When someone commands or instructs (tsavah), they are laying down a structured plan of action for another to walk in. It establishes order, defines relationships, and creates a moral pathway for the recipient to follow.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb tsavah is a primary root meaning to charge, command, order, commission, or appoint. In the Piel stem (the form used in Ruth 3:6), the verb denotes an active, intense commission of a duty to another. It signifies an authoritative instruction that binds the recipient to a specific course of action, leaving no room for negotiation or alteration.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• tsavah occurs over 490 times in the Hebrew Bible.

• It is overwhelmingly used of God commanding human beings, such as the giving of the Ten Commandments or the detailed instructions for constructing the Tabernacle.

• It is also used in a domestic or patriarchal context, such as a father giving a final, solemn charge to his household (e.g., Jacob in Genesis 49:33) or a leader commissioning a successor.

• The noun form of this root is mitzvah, which is the standard Hebrew word for a commandment.

4. Theological Implications

Instruction (tsavah) requires trust. For the recipient, obeying a command reflects faith in the character and wisdom of the one giving the instruction. Throughout Scripture, tsavah is intrinsically linked to covenantal life and blessing; obedience to what is commanded leads to life and order, while disregard leads to ruin. Ruth’s willing obedience to Naomi’s instruction mirrors the obedience of faith that God desires from His people, illustrating how human submission aligns with divine providence.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 3:6, Naomi instructs Ruth on a highly delicate, culturally specific, and risky plan to approach Boaz at the threshing floor. This plan required Ruth to put her safety, reputation, and future on the line. By stating that Ruth did "according to all that her mother-in-law instructed (tsavah) her," the text emphasizes Ruth's absolute, unquestioning loyalty. She does not alter the plan or hesitate. Her compliance with Naomi's authoritative instruction serves as the catalyst for Boaz to act as the kinsman-redeemer.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:16: "And the LORD God commanded the man, saying, 'Of every tree of the garden you may freely eat...'"

• Exodus 12:28: "Then the children of Israel went away and did so; just as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron, so they did."

• Joshua 1:9: "Have I not commanded you? Be strong and of good courage; do not be afraid, nor be dismayed, for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go."

• Psalm 133:3: "It is like the dew of Hermon, descending upon the mountains of Zion; for there the LORD commanded the blessing—life forevermore."

7. Other Relevant Insights

Ruth's obedience to Naomi's instruction is a pivotal turning point in the lineage of David and Jesus Christ. The use of tsavah here elevates Naomi's advice from casual elder counsel to a sacred charge, which Ruth honors fully. This obedience ultimately leads to the redemption of the family line, demonstrating that quiet obedience to proper authority is often the vehicle God uses to bring about extraordinary redemption.
