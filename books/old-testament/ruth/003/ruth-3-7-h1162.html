BOAZ - ( Bo'az , Strong's H1162 )

Summary:

Boaz is a proper noun meaning "in him is strength" or "fleetness." In the Old Testament, Boaz is a wealthy Bethlehemite landowner of the tribe of Judah who acts as the kinsman-redeemer (goel) for Ruth, a Moabite widow, and Naomi. His character represents covenant faithfulness, moral integrity, and protective strength. In the context of Ruth 3:7, the name Boaz represents a man of resources, security, and honor who uses his strength not to exploit the vulnerable, but to provide redemption and shelter under the wings of the Lord.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The name Boaz is set against the dark and chaotic spiritual backdrop of the period of the Judges, a time when "everyone did what was right in his own eyes" (Judges 21:25). In contrast to this societal decay, Boaz emerges as a beacon of godliness, order, and covenant loyalty (chesed).

The etymology of Boaz points to a compound of be (in) and oz (strength), yielding the meaning "in him is strength." This name fits his status as a "man of great wealth" or "mighty man of valor" (Ruth 2:1) and foreshadows his role as a strong, capable redeemer. The historical and cultural background of the name is also preserved in the construction of Solomon’s temple centuries later, where the southern bronze pillar was named Boaz (1 Kings 7:21), symbolizing the strength and stability of God's dwelling place.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

Boaz is a masculine proper noun. The primary lexical definition is "in him is strength" or, alternatively, "fleetness" or "alacrity."

• Root and Origin: Derived from an unused root meaning to be fleet or strong, or a compound of the prepositional prefix b- (in, with, by) and the noun oz (strength, might, power).

• Semantic Range: In the biblical text, the name refers exclusively to two entities: the historical figure Boaz of Bethlehem in the book of Ruth (and his entries in biblical genealogies) and the southern pillar of the porch of Solomon's temple.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

The name Boaz is used as a powerful word picture of divine strength channeled through human obedience:

• The Protective Shelter: In the narrative of Ruth, Boaz is depicted as a human extension of God’s protective wings (Ruth 2:12, 3:9). His name, "in him is strength," paints a picture of a sanctuary for the weary and marginalized.

• The Pillar of the Temple: The naming of the temple pillar Boaz serves as an architectural word picture. Positioned alongside the pillar Jachin ("He will establish"), Boaz ("In Him is strength") declared to every worshiper entering the temple that God establishes His people and provides them with the strength to endure.

### 4. Theological Implications

The theological significance of Boaz lies primarily in his role as the kinsman-redeemer (goel), serving as a profound Old Testament type of Jesus Christ:

• The Qualified Redeemer: Under Mosaic law, a redeemer had to meet three criteria: he must be a near kinsman (Leviticus 25:25), he must possess the financial capability to purchase the lost inheritance, and he must be willing to redeem. Boaz met all three criteria. This typifies Christ, who became our near kinsman through the Incarnation, possessed the infinite spiritual resources to pay our debt, and willingly laid down His life to redeem us.

• The Power of Grace: Boaz's strength was not used for self-aggrandizement but to bring a foreign outsider (Ruth the Moabite) into the covenant community. This illustrates God's sovereign grace in grafting Gentiles into the family of faith.

• The Lineage of the Messiah: Boaz’s redemption of Ruth preserved the line of Judah, leading directly to King David and ultimately to Jesus Christ (Matthew 1:5).

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 3:7, the text states, "And when Boaz had eaten and drunk, and his heart was cheerful, he went to lie down at the end of the heap of grain; and she came softly, uncovered his feet, and lay down."

The context of this passage is highly sensitive and culturally loaded. The "cheerful" heart of Boaz reflects his contentment and gratitude at the end of a successful harvest season, signifying peace and divine blessing rather than excessive indulgence. Sleeping at the edge of the grain heap demonstrates his diligent oversight of his assets, protecting his harvest from thieves.

When Ruth approaches him in the dark to ask him to spread his cloak over her—a formal request for marriage and redemption—the name Boaz ("in him is strength") takes on intense moral significance. In a situation fraught with temptation and potential scandal, Boaz demonstrates supreme moral strength, self-control, and integrity. Instead of taking advantage of Ruth’s vulnerability, he protects her honor, blesses her, and immediately commits to taking the legal steps necessary to redeem her. His strength is proven to be moral and spiritual, not merely physical or financial.

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

A striking contrast is drawn in Ruth chapter 4 between Boaz and the unnamed closer relative (Ploni Almoni). The closer relative represents self-preservation; he refuses to redeem Ruth because it might "ruin" his own inheritance. Boaz, however, willingly risks his own estate and name to preserve the name of the dead. Consequently, the name of the closer relative is forgotten and omitted from the biblical record, while the name of Boaz ("in him is strength") is immortalized in the genealogy of the King of Kings.
