UNCOVERED - ( galah , Strong's H1540 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb galah primarily means to strip, uncover, reveal, or lay bare. In the agricultural and highly symbolic setting of Ruth 3:7, the word describes Ruth's action of peeling back the cover from Boaz's feet while he slept on the threshing floor. Far from an act of impropriety, this was a culturally understood gesture of profound submission and a formal appeal for protection and marriage. By uncovering his feet, Ruth invited Boaz to fulfill his role as her kinsman-redeemer, setting the stage for one of the most beautiful pictures of redemption in Scripture.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The root word galah carries the concrete, physical imagery of stripping away a barrier, veil, or covering to make something visible. In the ancient Near East, clothing represented a person's status, identity, and authority. To cover someone with a garment or a corner of a cloak symbolized taking them under one's protective custody and covenant care. Conversely, to uncover someone or something was to expose it, make it vulnerable, or bring what was hidden into the open. In nomadic and agrarian cultures, uncovering the feet of a sleeping protector was a silent, respectful, yet unmistakable petition for that person to extend their cloak—and thus, their protection—over the petitioner.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb galah occurs over 180 times in the Hebrew Bible and possesses a rich semantic range depending on its grammatical stem and context:

• In the Qal stem, it means to uncover oneself, to depart, or to go into exile (as a land is "stripped" of its inhabitants).

• In the Piel stem (used in Ruth 3:7), it means to actively lay bare, disclose, strip, or reveal.

• In the Niphal and Hiphil stems, it often refers to being revealed, showing oneself, or leading into captivity.

At its core, the word speaks of removing an obstacle to sight, knowledge, or physical access. Whether it is uncovering a secret, exposing nakedness, or revealing God's own glory, galah transitions something from a state of concealment to a state of complete exposure.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament employs galah in several vivid conceptual categories:

• Physical Exposure: It is used of exposing physical nakedness, often in legal and moral contexts such as Leviticus 18, where "uncovering nakedness" refers to illicit sexual relations.

• Divine Revelation: It describes God "uncovering" the ears of prophets to speak to them or "revealing" Himself to His people. This is the root concept of biblical revelation—taking away the veil so that truth can be clearly seen.

• Exile and Captivity: Because exile involves stripping a nation of its land, homes, and dignity, galah is frequently translated as "departed" or "carried away into captivity." The land is left bare, and its people are exposed to foreign nations.

### 4. Theological Implications

In the context of biblical theology, galah reminds us that redemption requires vulnerability and revelation. Ruth’s act of uncovering Boaz’s feet was a voluntary step of self-exposure. She placed herself in a highly vulnerable position—at night, on a threshing floor, at the feet of a powerful man—relying entirely on his character and integrity.

This paints a beautiful picture of faith. True faith approaches the Redeemer not with demands, but with a humble posture of surrender, exposing one's need and helplessness. Just as Ruth uncovered Boaz's feet to ask for his covering, the sinner must lay bare their need before Christ, who covers our spiritual nakedness with His righteousness.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 3:7, Naomi instructs Ruth to wait until Boaz is asleep, go to the threshing floor, "uncover his feet," and lie down. The term "feet" in Hebrew literature can sometimes carry euphemistic overtones, leading some interpreters to suggest an inappropriate sexual element. However, the literary and moral context of the Book of Ruth strongly rejects this interpretation.

Boaz is consistently presented as a man of outstanding character, and Ruth as a woman of noble character. The act of uncovering his feet was a symbolic, culturally specific legal claim. It woke Boaz up due to the cold, prompting him to notice her. Ruth then asks him to "spread your wing over your maidservant, for you are a close relative" (v. 9). The uncovering (galah) of his feet was the necessary, respectful catalyst that prompted the covering of Ruth under his covenant protection.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Isaiah 40:5 - "The glory of the Lord shall be revealed, And all flesh shall see it together; For the mouth of the Lord has spoken."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

The word galah highlights the thematic transition in the Book of Ruth from emptiness to fullness, and from hiddenness to disclosure. Throughout the first two chapters, God’s hand is largely hidden behind ordinary events and providence. In chapter 3, through this physical act of uncovering, the hidden intentions of the characters and the covenant faithfulness of God are brought into the open light. What was covered in the darkness of the threshing floor is fully revealed as a glorious plan of redemption that would ultimately lead to the birth of David and, eventually, the Messiah.
