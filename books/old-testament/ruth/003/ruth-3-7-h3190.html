CHEERFUL - ( yatab , Strong's H3190 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb yatab fundamentally means to be good, well, pleasing, or glad. In Ruth 3:7, translated as "cheerful," it describes a state of deep contentment, joy, and peace of heart following a successful harvest. It goes beyond mere lightheartedness to signify a deep sense of satisfaction, security, and well-being, acknowledging the providential provision of God.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The word yatab carries the primary concrete idea of something being in its proper, optimal, or beautiful state. When applied to the heart (leb), it paints a picture of a heart that has transitioned from anxiety or labor into a state of rest, satisfaction, and gladness.

• In the agricultural cycle of ancient Israel, the harvest was a high-stakes endeavor. A successful harvest meant survival for another year. Thus, "eating and drinking" at the threshing floor followed by a "cheerful" heart represents the culmination of hard work, relief from the threat of famine, and gratitude for God's bounty.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Yatab is a common Hebrew verb with a wide semantic range. It primarily means to be good, to do good, to deal well with, or to make pleasant.

• When paired with "heart" (leb), it takes on the meaning of making glad, joyful, or cheerful.

• In the Qal stem used in Ruth 3:7, it expresses a state of being glad, joyful, or well-pleasing. In the Hiphil (causative) stem, it means to do good, to make things go well, or to adorn.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• As a state of emotional well-being: The phrase "his heart was cheerful" (wayyitah leb) indicates that Boaz experienced a deep sense of peace and joy. It is not a state of drunken revelry, but a wholesome contentment.

• As an aesthetic or ethical good: In other contexts, yatab refers to doing what is right or pleasing in the eyes of God or men.

• The "Good Heart": In Hebrew psychology, the heart is the center of the intellect, will, and emotions. For the heart to be yatab means that the entire inner being is aligned with goodness, peace, and satisfaction.

4. Theological Implications

• God as the Source of Joy: The cheerfulness of Boaz's heart is an indirect praise of God’s covenant faithfulness (hesed). God had visited His people by giving them bread (Ruth 1:6), and now Boaz experiences the physical and spiritual reality of that blessing.

• Holy Joy vs. Carnal Indulgence: The text balances Boaz’s cheerfulness with his high moral character. His joy is clean and thankful, showing that godliness and a cheerful heart are entirely compatible.

• Sabbath Rest: The satisfaction of a cheerful heart after labor mirrors the divine pattern of looking at creation, seeing that it is "good" (tob, a cognate concept), and resting.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 3:7, Boaz has finished winnowing barley. He eats and drinks, and his heart is yatab (cheerful). This specific state of relaxed, secure contentment sets the stage for Ruth’s vulnerable and bold petition.

• Because his heart was cheerful and at peace, Boaz was not on high alert or anxious, but rather in a state of open, generous, and receptive goodwill. His cheerful heart predisposed him to act with grace, mercy, and protection toward Ruth when she requested him to perform the duty of the kinsman-redeemer.

• It contrasts the dark, anxious days of the Judges with a peaceful, orderly, and joyful scene of righteous living and divine provision.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Judges 19:6 - "So they sat down, and the two of them ate and drank together. Then the young woman’s father said to the man, 'Please agree to stay tonight, and let your heart be merry.'"

• 1 Kings 21:7 - "Then Jezebel his wife said to him, 'You now exercise authority over Israel! Arise, eat food, and let your heart be cheerful; I will give you the vineyard of Naboth the Jezreelite.'"

• Proverbs 15:13 - "A merry heart makes a cheerful countenance, but by sorrow of the heart the spirit is broken."

• Ecclesiastes 11:9 - "Rejoice, O young man, in your youth, and let your heart cheer you in the days of your youth; walk in the ways of your heart, and in the sight of your eyes; but know that for all these things God will bring you into judgment."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The relationship between yatab (to be good/cheerful) and tob (good) is highly significant. They are closely related etymologically and semantically. When Boaz's heart was yatab, it was filled with tob—the goodness of God's creation and provision.

• In the Septuagint (LXX), yatab in similar contexts is often translated by the Greek verb agathyno (to make good, gladden) or euphraino (to rejoice, make merry). This highlights that biblical cheerfulness is an active, positive state of joy, rather than just the absence of sadness.
