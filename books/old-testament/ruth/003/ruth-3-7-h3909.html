SOFTLY - ( lat , Strong's H3909 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word lat refers to doing something in secret, quietly, softly, or stealthily. In Ruth 3:7, it describes Ruth's cautious, respectful, and silent approach to Boaz's sleeping place on the threshing floor, ensuring she does not wake him prematurely or cause a public scene, highlighting her modesty and the delicate nature of her redemption request.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew word lat is deeply connected to the concept of secrecy, quietness, and wrapped mystery. Ancient Semitic usage links this word group to the action of wrapping or veiling an object to keep it hidden from sight. In a broader cultural sense, to do something ba-lat (softly or secretly) is to shroud the action in silence, moving in a way that avoids drawing any public attention.

In the agrarian context of ancient Israel, the threshing floor during the harvest season was a place of celebration, sleep, and vulnerability. At night, owners slept near their grain heaps to guard their hard-earned crop from thieves. Approaching someone under the cover of darkness required absolute caution. To walk softly was not merely a matter of physical coordination, but a vital measure of safety, social respect, and moral protection.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The term lat is a noun meaning "secrecy," "quietness," or "stealth." When prefixed with the Hebrew preposition be, it forms the adverbial phrase ba-lat, which translates as "softly," "quietly," "secretly," or "stealthily."

The semantic range includes:

• Moving with complete silence to avoid waking someone.

• Executing a plan in private, away from the public gaze.

• Covering or wrapping an action so that its execution remains hidden until the appropriate moment.

The word describes a highly focused, deliberate quietness that is born out of serious intent, rather than casual or accidental silence.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The use of lat paints a vivid word picture of muffled, deliberate footsteps. It evokes the image of someone holding their breath, carefully placing each step to ensure not a single dry leaf or twig crackles underfoot.

This word appears in moments of intense gravity and high stakes. It is the silence of Jael entering Sisera’s tent, and the stealth of David slipping behind King Saul in the cave. In these accounts, the word conveys a tension where the slightest sound could mean discovery, failure, or death. The word picture is one of sacred or critical concealment, where an entire destiny hangs on the preservation of absolute quiet.

4. Theological Implications

Ruth's quiet approach to Boaz reflects a profound theological reality: God's redemptive work often unfolds in quietness and humility rather than with outward show or noise. In the economy of God, redemption is not demanded through loud self-assertion but is petitioned through humble, silent submission.

Furthermore, this term underscores the biblical priority of discretion and the protection of reputation. Ruth and Boaz were individuals of high moral character. By approaching softly and secretly, Ruth ensures that her request for redemption is kept private until it can be legally and honorably established. Discretion protects holy things from unholy gossip.

Finally, Ruth’s silent approach models the posture of a believer coming to the Redeemer. She does not come with loud demands or self-righteous claims; she approaches softly, laying herself down at his feet, relying entirely on his mercy and grace to cover her.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 3:7, Ruth's action is the culmination of Naomi's strategic advice. Boaz had eaten, drunk, and laid down to sleep at the end of the grain heap. As a vulnerable sleeper, any sudden noise or intrusion would have startled him, potentially causing a defensive reaction or a public scene.

By coming "softly," Ruth protects Boaz from embarrassment and preserves the sanctity of her petition. She slips in silently, uncovers his feet, and lies down. Her soft approach transforms what could have been misconstrued as an aggressive or inappropriate advance into a highly respectful, symbolic act of submission. It demonstrates her complete trust in Boaz's character. She does not force a conversation; she waits quietly in the dark for him to wake, placing the initiative entirely in his hands.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Judges 4:21: "Then Jael, Heber’s wife, took a tent peg and took a hammer in her hand, and went softly to him and drove the peg into his temple, and it went down into the ground; for he was fast asleep and weary. So he died."

• 1 Samuel 24:4: "Then the men of David said to him, 'This is the day of which the Lord said to you, "Behold, I will deliver your enemy into your hand, that you may do to him as it seems good to you."' And David arose and secretly cut off a corner of Saul’s robe."

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a beautiful literary symmetry in the Book of Ruth regarding what is hidden and what is revealed. What Ruth petitions "softly" in the dark of the threshing floor is later proclaimed boldly and legally by Boaz in the bright light of the city gate before the elders of the town. This progression shows that those who humble themselves in secret will be vindicated and honored openly by their redeemer.

Additionally, the phonetic and conceptual link to "covering" or "wrapping" mirrors the spiritual petition of the scene. Ruth went softly (wrapped in secret) to ask Boaz to spread his garment (cover) over her. Her quiet manner perfectly matched the protective covering she was seeking from her kinsman-redeemer.
