LAY DOWN (shakab, Strong's H7901)

Summary:

The Hebrew verb shakab refers to the physical act of reclining, resting, sleeping, or lying down. In the context of Ruth 3:7, it describes both Boaz and Ruth lying down at the threshing floor. Far from carrying any illicit or immoral connotation in this passage, the word highlights Ruth’s humble submission and bold appeal to Boaz as her kinsman-redeemer. It serves as a beautiful picture of seeking refuge and protection under the wings of the Lord through covenant obedience and moral purity.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root shakab fundamentally depicts a transition from an upright, active posture to a horizontal, resting posture. In the ancient Near East, lying down was associated with vulnerability, rest, sleep, death, or sexual intimacy. On the agricultural calendar, the threshing floor during the barley harvest was a place of joy, hard work, and security. Men slept on the threshing floor to guard the freshly winnowed grain from thieves. The act of lying down in this setting was a practical necessity of farming life, but in Ruth’s case, it became the stage for a profound covenantal petition.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb shakab is a primary root occurring over 200 times in the Old Testament. Its semantic range includes:

• To lie down for rest or sleep.

• To recline or rest, representing safety, security, and peace.

• To lie down in death (often phrased as "lying down with one's fathers").

• To lie down with someone in sexual intimacy (used both of lawful marriage and unlawful relationships).

In Ruth 3:7, the verb is used in the Qal stem. It first describes Boaz going to lie down (shakab) at the end of the heap of grain, and then describes Ruth coming softly, uncovering his feet, and lying down (shakab).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Posture of Peace and Trust: Lying down to sleep is a recurring picture of trust in God's protection. When a person is secure in the covenant, they can lie down without fear of enemies or disaster.

• The Posture of Submission: To lie down at someone's feet is the classic posture of a servant or supplicant. By lying down at Boaz's feet, Ruth physically acts out her position as a humble servant seeking the favor of her master.

• The Posture of Intimacy/Marriage: While the term can refer to sexual relations, the narrative in Ruth uses it to build tension. The reader is kept in suspense, wondering if the encounter will turn into compromise. However, the holiness of both characters shines through, turning a vulnerable situation into a solemn, pure pledge of future covenant marriage.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, shakab in Ruth 3 underscores the beauty of righteous vulnerability and redemption. Ruth’s act of lying down at Boaz’s feet represents the ultimate surrender of her future. She places herself entirely at his mercy, asking him to fulfill the role of the goel (kinsman-redeemer). This mirrors the spiritual posture of a believer who comes to Christ, casting themselves at His feet, seeking His covering, protection, and redemption. It demonstrates that true faith is not passive; it takes active steps of obedience and places itself in a posture of complete dependence on the Redeemer.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 3:7, the double use of shakab is masterfully contrasted. Boaz lies down in content exhaustion after a hard day's work and a cheerful meal. His lying down is simple, restful, and unsuspecting. Ruth’s lying down, however, is calculated, courageous, and highly symbolic. By uncovering his feet and lying down, she is not seducing him; she is fulfilling Naomi's strategic advice to invoke the ancient custom of the "wing" or cover. When Boaz wakes up startled at midnight, he finds her lying there. Instead of exploiting her vulnerability, he recognizes her virtue, and the night of lying down at the threshing floor ends not in shame, but in a promise of redemption.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 28:11: "So he came to a certain place and stayed there all night, because the sun had set. And he took one of the stones of that place and put it at his head, and he lay down in that place to sleep."

• Deuteronomy 6:7: "You shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, when you walk by the way, when you lie down, and when you rise up."

• Psalm 4:8: "I will both lie down in peace, and sleep; For You alone, O LORD, make me dwell in safety."

• Proverbs 3:24: "When you lie down, you will not be afraid; Yes, you will lie down and your sleep will be sweet."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between Ruth's conduct and the historical origins of the Moabite people is striking. The Moabite nation began in a cave through an incestuous act where Lot's daughters lay down (shakab) with their father (Genesis 19:32-35). Ruth, a Moabitess, redeems this dark family history. By lying down (shakab) at the feet of Boaz in absolute purity, virtue, and honor, she participates in a righteous union that ultimately leads to the birth of King David and, ultimately, Jesus Christ.
