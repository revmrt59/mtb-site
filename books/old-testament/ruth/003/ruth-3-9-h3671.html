WING - ( kanaph , Strong's H3671 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word kanaph refers literally to the wing of a bird or the extremity and corner of a garment. In the Old Testament, it serves as a powerful double entendre representing both physical protection (the corner of a cloak) and divine shelter (the wings of the Almighty). In Ruth 3:9, Ruth appeals to Boaz to spread his "wing" (garment corner) over her, a symbolic request for marriage, protection, and the fulfillment of his role as her kinsman-redeemer. This act beautifully mirrors the divine refuge she had already sought under the "wings" of Yahweh, illustrating how God uses human obedience to answer prayers for protection.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concrete imagery of kanaph is deeply rooted in the agrarian and nomadic life of ancient Israel. It describes the wing of a bird, which provides warmth, shelter, and defense for its young. It also describes the outermost edge, corner, or hem of a standard four-cornered Hebrew garment (the beged or simlah), where the tassels (tzitzit) were commanded to be attached to remind Israel of the commandments. By pulling the corner of a garment over someone, a man symbolically brought that person under his personal care, authority, and covenant protection.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meanings of kanaph include:

• The physical wing of a bird or winged celestial creature.

• The corner, hem, or skirt of a garment.

• The extremities, quarters, or "corners" of the earth.

• Metaphorically, a place of safety, shelter, defense, or refuge.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Wing of Protection: Just as a mother bird gathers her chicks under her wings to protect them from predators and the elements, kanaph is used anthropomorphically for God's protective care. Under His "wings" the vulnerable find safety.

• The Garment Corner: Cutting off the kanaph of Saul's robe by David in the cave was a highly symbolic act, representing the cutting off of Saul's royal authority and protection.

• The Covenant of Marriage: Spreading a skirt (kanaph) over a woman was a formal ancient Near Eastern gesture of marriage proposal and the legal assumption of responsibility for her well-being.

4. Theological Implications

The word kanaph connects human actions of redemption and mercy directly to the character of God. When Boaz spreads his kanaph over Ruth, he acts as the physical hands and feet of Yahweh. This demonstrates the concept of hesed (covenant faithfulness and lovingkindness) translated into social action. Protection and redemption are not merely passive concepts; they are active, costly, and legal. Ultimately, the term points forward to the Messiah, who brings healing under His "wings" and longs to gather His people under His protective care.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 3:9, Ruth’s request to Boaz ("Spread your wing over your maidservant") uses the singular kanaph. This is a direct, deliberate echo of Boaz’s blessing to her in Ruth 2:12, where he prayed that Ruth would receive a full reward from Yahweh, "under whose wings [plural kenapayim] you have come for refuge." Ruth essentially tells Boaz: "You prayed that God would protect me under His wings. Now, be the answer to your own prayer. Spread your wing over me." By asking him to spread his kanaph (skirt/wing), she is proposing marriage to her kinsman-redeemer (goel), asking him to cover her socially, financially, and relationally.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Ruth 2:12: "The LORD repay your work, and a full reward be given you by the LORD God of Israel, under whose wings you have come for refuge."

• Ezekiel 16:8: "When I passed by you again and looked upon you, indeed your time was the time of love; so I spread My wing over you and covered your nakedness. Yes, I swore an oath to you and entered into a covenant with you, and you became Mine,” says the Lord GOD."

• Psalm 91:4: "He shall cover you with His feathers, And under His wings you shall take refuge; His truth shall be your shield and buckler."

• 1 Samuel 24:4: "Then the men of David said to him, 'This is the day of which the LORD said to you, "Behold, I will deliver your enemy into your hand, that you may do to him as seems good to you."' And David arose and gently cut off a corner of Saul's robe."

7. Other Relevant Insights

In Malachi 4:2, the prophet speaks of the "Sun of Righteousness" who will arise "with healing in His wings" (kanaph). This messianic prophecy was on the minds of first-century Jews. When the woman with the issue of blood sought healing from Jesus in Matthew 9:20, she reached out to touch the hem (the Greek kraspedon, which translates the Hebrew kanaph or the tassels attached to it) of His garment. She was literally reaching for the "wings" of the Messiah for healing.
