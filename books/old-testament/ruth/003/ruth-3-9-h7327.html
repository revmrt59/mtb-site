RUTH - ( Rut , Strong's H7327 )

Summary:

The name Ruth, transliterated as Rut, means "companion," "friend," or "friendship." It represents a character defined by covenant loyalty, loving-kindness, and deep devotion. In the narrative of the Book of Ruth, and specifically in Ruth 3:9, her name reflects her role not just as a historical figure, but as an exemplary companion who binds herself to Naomi, to the people of Israel, and to Yahweh. Her declaration of identity to Boaz at the threshing floor sets the stage for redemption and illustrates the beauty of a faithful companion seeking shelter under the wings of the Lord.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The name Rut derives from a root meaning friendship or companion, likely related to the Hebrew noun re'ut. Historically, Ruth was a Moabitess, an outsider from a nation often hostile to Israel. Her name becomes a profound word picture of reconciliation, inclusion, and faithful companionship. A daughter of Moab becomes the ultimate companion to a grieving Judean widow, Naomi. In the ancient Near Eastern context, a companion is not merely an acquaintance but one bound by mutual commitment and loyalty. Ruth embodies this through her self-sacrificial oath to Naomi, demonstrating that true companionship is defined by action and covenant loyalty rather than bloodlines.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The proper noun Rut refers to the Moabitess who became the wife of Mahlon and later of Boaz, and an ancestress of King David and Jesus Christ. Lexically, the name is connected to the Hebrew noun re'ut (female companion, mate, neighbor) or the broader root ra'ah (to associate with, be a friend). The name conveys the primary definitions of "friendship," "companion," or "associated one." It highlights a relationship characterized by loyalty, intimacy, and shared destiny.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The name occurs 12 times in the Old Testament, exclusively in the Book of Ruth. The word picture of Ruth is inextricably linked to chesed (covenant faithfulness or loving-kindness). When her name is spoken, it carries the weight of her actions: leaving her homeland, comforting her mother-in-law, gleaning in the fields, and seeking redemption. In the narrative, Ruth serves as a living illustration of the "virtuous woman" (Ruth 3:11, eshet chayil), a companion whose worth is far above rubies. Her name becomes synonymous with faithful companionship that transcends ethnic, national, and cultural boundaries.

4. Theological Implications

• Redemptive History and Inclusion: Ruth’s presence in the genealogy of David (and ultimately the Messiah) proves that God’s redemptive plan transcends ethnic barriers. A Moabitess companion is grafted into the covenant line, demonstrating God's sovereign grace.

• Covenant Companionship: Ruth’s loyalty mirrors God's loyalty to Israel. Her name, meaning companion, reflects the relational nature of God’s covenant. True faith is expressed through loving companionship with God and His people.

• Typology of the Bride: Ruth seeking Boaz at the threshing floor represents the helpless seeker coming to the redeemer. As a companion, she seeks the covering of his "wings" (skirt/cloak), showcasing the believer's relationship with Christ, our Kinsman-Redeemer.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 3:9, Ruth responds to Boaz's middle-of-the-night inquiry, "Who are you?" with: "I am Ruth, your maidservant." By stating her name, "Ruth," she identifies herself not as a stranger or a foreign intruder, but as the faithful companion known to him for her virtue. She immediately links her name to her petition: "Spread your skirt over your maidservant, for you are a close relative." She asks Boaz to act as her goel (Kinsman-Redeemer). The nuance here is powerful: Ruth, the loyal companion to Naomi, now seeks a covenant companion in Boaz to redeem the deceased family's name and land. Her name reinforces her petition for a covenantal, redemptive relationship.

• Ruth 4:10 - "Moreover, Ruth the Moabitess, the widow of Mahlon, I have acquired as my wife, to perpetuate the name of the dead through his inheritance, that the name of the dead may not be cut off from among his brethren and from his position at the gate. You are witnesses this day."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The contrast between Orpah (whose name can be linked to "back of the neck," representing turning away) and Ruth ("companion," representing clinging) emphasizes the theme of choices in companionship. In Matthew 1:5, she is one of the few women listed in the genealogy of Jesus Christ, cementing her status as an eternal companion in the royal, messianic line. Her transition from "Moabitess" (foreigner) to "maidservant" (shiphhah, a low servant) to "wife" and "mother" in Israel charts the journey of redemption from alienation to intimate companionship.
