Chapter Study

Ruth 3

Verse-by-Verse
Ruth 3:1

NKJV

Ruth 3:1 Then Naomi her mother-in-law said to her, "My daughter, shall I not seek security for you, that it may be well with you?(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 3:1 One day Naomi said to Ruth, “My daughter, it’s time that I found a permanent home for you, so that you will be provided for. (NLT)

Main Idea

Naomi begins to seek a permanent future for Ruth by pursuing God's provision through Boaz, a potential kinsman-redeemer.

Verse Explanation

"Then Naomi her mother-in-law said to her…"

After the barley and wheat harvests had ended (Ruth 2:23), Naomi realized that Ruth's opportunity to work in Boaz's fields would soon come to an end. While God had faithfully provided food, Ruth's long-term future as a young widow was still uncertain. Naomi now takes the initiative to seek a lasting solution.

"My daughter…"

Naomi's words reveal genuine love and concern. She no longer thinks only of her own loss but desires what is best for Ruth. Throughout the book, their relationship reflects sacrificial love and mutual devotion.

"Shall I not seek security for you…"

The word translated "security" (or "rest" in some translations) refers to a settled home, protection, and lasting provision. Naomi had prayed in Ruth 1:9 that Ruth would find this kind of rest, and now she begins to act toward that end. Her hope is not merely that Ruth will have enough food but that she will have a secure future.

"That it may be well with you?"

Naomi's motivation is entirely for Ruth's good. She recognizes that God has opened a door through Boaz and wisely seeks to follow His providential leading. This verse marks the transition from God's daily provision to His unfolding plan of redemption for Ruth and Naomi.

Remark(s)

Naomi sought "rest" for Ruth through a redeemer. That longing ultimately points to the greater rest and security found in Jesus Christ, our Redeemer, who brings us into God's family and provides an eternal inheritance.

Ruth 3:2

NKJV

Ruth 3:2 Now Boaz, whose young women you were with, is he not our relative? In fact, he is winnowing barley tonight at the threshing floor.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 3:2 Boaz is a close relative of ours, and he’s been very kind by letting you gather grain with his young women. Tonight he will be winnowing barley at the threshing floor. (NLT)

Main Idea

Naomi recognizes that God has already provided an opportunity through Boaz and encourages Ruth to act wisely at the right time.

Verse Explanation

"Now Boaz, whose young women you were with…"

Naomi reminds Ruth that Boaz is no stranger. Ruth has spent weeks working safely in his fields under his protection. During that time, Boaz has consistently shown kindness, generosity, and respect toward her. Naomi sees God's hand in these events and recognizes that Boaz is a man of godly character.

"Is he not our relative?"

Boaz was a relative of Elimelech's family, making him a potential kinsman-redeemer. According to Israel's laws, a close relative could redeem family property and help preserve a family's inheritance. Naomi understands that Boaz may be able to provide not only for Ruth but also for the future of their family.

"In fact, he is winnowing barley tonight at the threshing floor."

The barley harvest has concluded, and Boaz is spending the night at the threshing floor to oversee the winnowing process and guard the harvested grain. Winnowing separated the edible grain from the chaff by tossing it into the evening breeze. Naomi recognizes that this is an appropriate and timely opportunity for Ruth to approach Boaz privately.

Ruth 3:3

NKJV

Ruth 3:3 Therefore wash yourself and anoint yourself, put on your best garment and go down to the threshing floor; but do not make yourself known to the man until he has finished eating and drinking.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 3:3 Now do as I tell you — take a bath and put on perfume and dress in your nicest clothes. Then go to the threshing floor, but don’t let Boaz see you until he has finished eating and drinking. (NLT)

Main Idea

Naomi instructs Ruth to prepare herself respectfully before approaching Boaz, demonstrating wisdom, dignity, and proper conduct.

Verse Explanation

"Therefore wash yourself and anoint yourself…"

Washing and applying fragrant oil were ordinary acts of personal preparation and grooming. Naomi is encouraging Ruth to present herself with dignity and to set aside the outward signs of prolonged mourning. This marks a transition from grieving the past to embracing the future God may be providing.

"Put on your best garment…"

Ruth is to dress appropriately for the occasion. The emphasis is not on attracting Boaz through outward beauty but on approaching him with respect and honor. Naomi's instructions reflect careful planning rather than manipulation.

"And go down to the threshing floor…"

The threshing floor was an open area where grain was threshed and winnowed after harvest. Because valuable grain was stored there overnight, Boaz would remain at the site until morning to protect it.

"But do not make yourself known to the man until he has finished eating and drinking."

Naomi instructs Ruth to wait patiently until Boaz has completed the day's work and the harvest celebration. She is not to interrupt or draw unnecessary attention to herself. Her approach is to be discreet, respectful, and at the proper time.

Ruth 3:4

NKJV

Ruth 3:4 Then it shall be, when he lies down, that you shall notice the place where he lies; and you shall go in, uncover his feet, and lie down; and he will tell you what you should do."(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 3:4 Be sure to notice where he lies down; then go and uncover his feet and lie down there. He will tell you what to do.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Naomi instructs Ruth to humbly appeal to Boaz as her potential redeemer, trusting him to act with wisdom and integrity.

Verse Explanation

"Then it shall be, when he lies down, that you shall notice the place where he lies."

Naomi tells Ruth to observe carefully where Boaz lies down for the night. Since several workers could be sleeping at the threshing floor, Ruth needed to avoid any confusion. The instruction is practical and emphasizes discretion.

"And you shall go in, uncover his feet, and lie down."

This action was a respectful and symbolic appeal, not a sexual advance. It is generally understood that by uncovering Boaz's feet and lying near them, Ruth placed herself in the position of a humble petitioner, asking him to consider fulfilling the responsibilities of a kinsman-redeemer. Nothing in the text suggests immoral behavior; rather, the integrity of both Ruth and Boaz is highlighted throughout the chapter.

"And he will tell you what you should do."

Naomi trusts Boaz's character. She does not instruct Ruth to demand anything or manipulate the situation. Instead, Ruth is to place the matter before Boaz and allow him to respond according to God's law and his own integrity.

Remark(s)

Faith does not seek to control the outcome. Ruth humbly presents her need and entrusts the response to a man whose character has already proven trustworthy. In the same way, believers are called to bring their needs before God with humble confidence, trusting Him to act in His wisdom and timing.

Ruth 3:5

NKJV

Ruth 3:5 And she said to her, "All that you say to me I will do."(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 3:5 “I will do everything you say,” Ruth replied. (NLT)

Main Idea

Ruth responds to Naomi with humble trust and a willing heart, demonstrating faith through obedience.

Verse Explanation

"And she said to her…"

Ruth offers no objections, questions, or hesitation. She has learned to trust Naomi's wisdom and recognizes her sincere desire to seek what is best for her.

"All that you say to me I will do."

Ruth's response reflects a pattern that has characterized her life since leaving Moab. She willingly follows wise counsel and acts in faith, even when she cannot see how events will unfold. Her obedience reflects the trust that has developed between Ruth and Naomi and Ruth's willingness to act even when she cannot see how events will unfold.

This simple statement also prepares the reader for the events that follow. Ruth will faithfully carry out Naomi's instructions, while the outcome remains entirely in God's hands.

Remark(s)

Ruth's obedience is marked by humility and trust. Faith is often demonstrated not by dramatic actions but by a willingness to obey God one step at a time, leaving the results in His hands.

Ruth 3:6

NKJV

Ruth 3:6 So she went down to the threshing floor and did according to all that her mother-in-law instructed her.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 3:6 So she went down to the threshing floor that night and followed the instructions of her mother-in-law. (NLT)

Main Idea

Ruth faithfully follows Naomi's instructions, demonstrating humble obedience and trust in God's unfolding plan.

Verse Explanation

"So she went down to the threshing floor…"

Ruth goes exactly where Naomi instructed her. There is no hesitation or attempt to alter the plan. Her actions reflect confidence in Naomi's wisdom and in God's providential leading.

"And did according to all that her mother-in-law instructed her."

The emphasis is on Ruth's complete obedience. Throughout the book, Ruth is consistently portrayed as a woman of humility, faithfulness, and integrity. She does not attempt to manipulate events or take matters into her own hands. Instead, she faithfully fulfills her responsibility and entrusts the outcome to God.

This verse also builds anticipation. The narrator records Ruth's obedience before revealing the results, reminding the reader that faithful obedience often precedes seeing God's purposes unfold.

Remark(s)

Throughout the book, Ruth's faithful obedience consistently precedes God's unfolding provision. While obedience never earns God's favor, He often accomplishes His purposes through those who humbly walk in His ways.

Ruth 3:7

NKJV

Ruth 3:7 And after Boaz had eaten and drunk, and his heart was cheerful, he went to lie down at the end of the heap of grain; and she came softly, uncovered his feet, and lay down.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 3:7 After Boaz had finished eating and drinking and was in good spirits, he lay down at the far end of the pile of grain and went to sleep. Then Ruth came quietly, uncovered his feet, and lay down. (NLT)

Main Idea

After the day's work and harvest celebration, Ruth quietly approaches Boaz exactly as Naomi instructed, trusting his integrity and God's timing.

Verse Explanation

"After Boaz had eaten and drunk…"

This describes the normal conclusion of a successful harvest day. The phrase does not imply drunkenness but satisfaction after the labor and celebration of the harvest. The context continues to present Boaz as a man of integrity and self-control.

"And his heart was cheerful…"

Boaz is content and thankful after God's blessing on the harvest. The expression refers to a joyful and peaceful frame of mind, not impaired judgment.

"He went to lie down at the end of the heap of grain."

Boaz sleeps beside the harvested grain to guard it through the night, a common practice during harvest season.

"And she came softly, uncovered his feet, and lay down."

Ruth carries out Naomi's instructions quietly and respectfully. The narrator records her actions without any suggestion of immorality. The focus is on Ruth's humble appeal and the integrity that both she and Boaz will demonstrate throughout the remainder of the chapter.

Remark(s)

Ruth and Boaz are portrayed as people of unquestioned integrity. In a setting that could easily be misunderstood, their conduct reinforces one of the book's central themes: genuine godliness is displayed in both character and actions.

Ruth 3:8

NKJV

Ruth 3:8 Now it happened at midnight that the man was startled, and turned himself; and there, a woman was lying at his feet.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 3:8 Around midnight Boaz suddenly woke up and turned over. He was surprised to find a woman lying at his feet! (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz awakens unexpectedly and discovers Ruth at his feet, setting the stage for her appeal to him as her redeemer.

Verse Explanation

"Now it happened at midnight…"

The turning point comes quietly in the middle of the night. The timing appears ordinary, yet God's providence continues to guide the events without dramatic intervention.

"That the man was startled…"

Boaz suddenly awakens during the night and becomes aware that something is unusual. Some think Boaz may have awakened because his feet had become cold after Ruth uncovered them.

"And turned himself…"

As Boaz adjusts himself, he realizes someone is lying at his feet. The narrator builds suspense, allowing the reader to experience the moment from Boaz's perspective.

"And there, a woman was lying at his feet."

Boaz discovers Ruth exactly where Naomi had instructed her to be. The scene is now set for the conversation that will reveal Ruth's request and Boaz's response.

Ruth 3:9

NKJV

Ruth 3:9 And he said, "Who are you?" So she answered, "I am Ruth, your maidservant. Take your maidservant under your wing, for you are a close relative."(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 3:9 “Who are you?” he asked. “I am your servant Ruth,” she replied. “Spread the corner of your covering over me, for you are my family redeemer.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Ruth identifies herself and humbly asks Boaz to fulfill the role of her kinsman-redeemer by taking her under his protection.

Verse Explanation

"Then he said, 'Who are you?'"

Awakened in the darkness, Boaz does not immediately recognize who is at his feet. His question gives Ruth the opportunity to explain why she has come.

"So she answered, 'I am Ruth, your maidservant.'"

Ruth identifies herself with humility. Although Boaz has already shown her great kindness, she approaches him respectfully, making no demands or assumptions about how he should respond.

"Take your maidservant under your wing..."

Literally, Ruth asks Boaz to spread the corner of his garment over her. In the culture of ancient Israel, this was a symbolic request for marriage and protection. She is asking Boaz to receive her under his care as her husband and redeemer.

The word translated "wing" (or "corner of your garment") is the same Hebrew word used in Ruth 2:12, where Boaz praised Ruth for coming to take refuge under the wings of the LORD. Ruth is now asking Boaz to become the human instrument through whom God answers that prayer.

"For you are a close relative."

Ruth bases her appeal on God's provision in the Law, not on emotion or personal desire alone. She recognizes that Boaz is qualified to act as a kinsman-redeemer, though she leaves the decision entirely in his hands.

Remark(s)

The same "wing" under which Ruth sought refuge in Ruth 2:12 now becomes the "wing" she asks Boaz to spread over her. God often answers our prayers by working through the faithful obedience of His people.

Ruth 3:10

NKJV

Ruth 3:10 Then he said, "Blessed are you of the LORD, my daughter! For you have shown more kindness at the end than at the beginning, in that you did not go after young men, whether poor or rich.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 3:10 “The LORD bless you, my daughter!” Boaz exclaimed. “You are showing even more family loyalty now than you did before, for you have not gone after a younger man, whether rich or poor. (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz commends Ruth for her faithful character, recognizing that her request reflects kindness, integrity, and godly motives rather than personal ambition.

Verse Explanation

"Then he said, 'Blessed are you of the LORD, my daughter!'"

Boaz immediately responds with a blessing rather than surprise or criticism. His respectful address, "my daughter," reflects both affection and the likely age difference between them. He recognizes God's hand in the moment and invokes the LORD's blessing upon Ruth.

"For you have shown more kindness at the end than at the beginning..."

The "kindness" refers to Ruth's steadfast love and faithfulness. Earlier, she had demonstrated remarkable kindness by leaving Moab to care for Naomi (Ruth 1:16-17). Now Boaz says her present act displays even greater kindness because she is seeking the preservation of Naomi's family and inheritance through a kinsman-redeemer.

"In that you did not go after young men, whether poor or rich."

Boaz understands Ruth's motives. As a younger widow, she could have pursued marriage to someone closer to her own age, whether for love or financial security. Instead, she has chosen the path that honors God and seeks the good of Naomi's family. Boaz admires her character far more than her circumstances.

Remark(s)

Boaz sees that Ruth is seeking to preserve Naomi's family rather than simply secure a husband for herself. Her selfless concern for others reveals the godly character that has marked her throughout the book.

Ruth 3:11

NKJV

Ruth 3:11 And now, my daughter, do not fear. I will do for you all that you request, for all the people of my town know that you are a virtuous woman.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 3:11 Now don’t worry about a thing, my daughter. I will do what is necessary, for everyone in town knows you are a virtuous woman. (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz reassures Ruth that he will honor her request because her godly character is well known throughout the community.

Verse Explanation

"And now, my daughter, do not fear."

Boaz begins by calming Ruth's fears. She has made a bold and vulnerable request, uncertain how he will respond. His first words remove her anxiety and assure her that she has nothing to fear.

"I will do for you all that you request..."

Boaz willingly accepts Ruth's request in principle. He intends to act as her redeemer if he is legally able to do so. His response demonstrates both compassion and a commitment to obey God's law.

"For all the people of my town know..."

Boaz explains why he is willing to respond so favorably. Ruth has earned a reputation that extends beyond the harvest fields. Her life has consistently displayed humility, faithfulness, and integrity.

"That you are a virtuous woman."

The phrase "virtuous woman" describes a woman of noble character, moral excellence, and spiritual maturity. It is the same Hebrew expression used of the excellent wife in Proverbs 31. Ruth's reputation was not built on a single act but on a pattern of godly living that others had come to recognize.

Remark(s)

Remarks: Ruth came to Bethlehem as a foreign widow with nothing, yet she became known throughout the community for her character. Godly character is often built quietly over time and becomes a testimony that speaks louder than words.

Ruth 3:12

NKJV

Ruth 3:12 Now it is true that I am a close relative; however, there is a relative closer than I.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 3:12 But while it’s true that I am one of your family redeemers, there is another man who is more closely related to you than I am. (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz explains that although he is willing to redeem Ruth, another relative has the first legal right to do so.

Verse Explanation

"And now it is true that I am a close relative..."

Boaz confirms that Ruth has correctly identified him as a potential kinsman-redeemer. He does not reject her request but acknowledges that he is qualified to fulfill that role if the legal requirements can be met.

"However, there is a relative closer than I."

Boaz immediately reveals an important complication. According to Israel's customs, another man was more closely related to Elimelech's family and therefore had the first opportunity to act as the redeemer.

Rather than ignoring this fact or acting for his own benefit, Boaz chooses to follow God's law with complete integrity. His desire to marry Ruth never outweighs his commitment to do what is right.

This verse introduces the final obstacle that must be resolved before redemption can take place, building anticipation for the events of chapter four.

Remark(s)

Remarks: Boaz's integrity is revealed by his willingness to do what is right even when it could cost him what he desires most. Godly character is demonstrated not only by pursuing good things but also by refusing to obtain them in the wrong way.

Ruth 3:13

NKJV

Ruth 3:13 Stay this night, and in the morning it shall be that if he will perform the duty of a close relative for you"”good; let him do it. But if he does not want to perform the duty for you, then I will perform the duty for you, as the LORD lives! Lie down until morning."(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 3:13 Stay here tonight, and in the morning I will talk to him. If he is willing to redeem you, very well. Let him marry you. But if he is not willing, then as surely as the LORD lives, I will redeem you myself! Now lie down here until morning.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz promises to resolve the matter honorably, trusting God's law while assuring Ruth that she will not be abandoned.

Verse Explanation

"Stay this night..."

Boaz asks Ruth to remain until morning. This protects her from traveling alone in the dark and allows the matter to be handled properly the next day.

"And in the morning it shall be that if he will perform the duty of a close relative for you—good; let him do it."

Boaz is completely committed to doing what is right. If the nearer relative chooses to redeem Ruth, Boaz accepts that decision without resentment. His desire for Ruth never takes precedence over obedience to God's order.

"But if he does not want to perform the duty for you, then I will perform the duty for you..."

Boaz gives Ruth his personal assurance. If the nearer relative declines, Boaz will gladly become her redeemer. Ruth no longer has to wonder whether she will be rejected.

"As the LORD lives!"

This was a solemn oath invoking God's own name. Boaz is not making a casual promise but binding himself before the LORD to do exactly what he has said.

"Lie down until morning."

Boaz again acts with wisdom and integrity. He provides for Ruth's safety while ensuring that everything is handled honorably and in accordance with God's law.

Remark(s)

Remarks: Boaz's promise is strengthened by his oath, "As the LORD lives." His word was trustworthy because it was anchored in his reverence for God. People whose lives are governed by the fear of the Lord become people whose promises can be trusted.

Ruth 3:14

NKJV

Ruth 3:14 So she lay at his feet until morning, and she arose before one could recognize another. Then he said, "Do not let it be known that the woman came to the threshing floor."(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 3:14 So Ruth lay at Boaz’s feet until the morning, but she got up before it was light enough for people to recognize each other. For Boaz had said, “No one must know that a woman was here at the threshing floor.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Ruth leaves before dawn at Boaz's instruction, and Boaz carefully protects both her reputation and his own by acting with wisdom and integrity.

Verse Explanation

"So she lay at his feet until morning..."

Ruth remains exactly where Boaz instructed her. The night passes without any inappropriate conduct. The account continues to emphasize the purity and honorable character of both Ruth and Boaz.

"And she arose before one could recognize another."

Ruth leaves while it is still dark enough to avoid being recognized. This was not because they had done anything wrong, but to prevent misunderstanding and protect their reputations.

"Then he said, 'Do not let it be known that the woman came to the threshing floor.'"

Boaz is concerned that neither Ruth's character nor his own be damaged by false assumptions. In a culture where appearances could quickly lead to gossip, he acts wisely to guard both of their reputations. His concern demonstrates respect for Ruth and a commitment to live above reproach.

Remark(s)

Boaz and Ruth had nothing to hide, yet they wisely avoided creating unnecessary suspicion. Scripture calls believers not only to live with integrity but also to consider what is honorable “not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men” (2 Corinthians 8:21).

Ruth 3:15

NKJV

Ruth 3:15 Also he said, "Bring the shawl that is on you and hold it." And when she held it, he measured six ephahs of barley, and laid it on her. Then she went into the city.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 3:15 Then Boaz said to her, “Bring your cloak and spread it out.” He measured six scoops of barley into the cloak and placed it on her back. Then he returned to the town. (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz generously provides for Ruth once again, sending her home with an abundant gift as a tangible expression of his commitment to care for her and Naomi.

Verse Explanation

"Also he said, 'Bring the shawl that is on you and hold it.'"

Boaz asks Ruth to spread out the outer garment or shawl she is wearing so it can be used to carry grain. What follows is another deliberate act of generosity.

"And when she held it, he measured six measures of barley..."

Boaz fills Ruth's shawl with six measures of barley. The exact amount is uncertain, but it was a substantial gift—enough to demonstrate his generosity and provide for Ruth and Naomi. His actions match his words; he not only promises to act but immediately begins caring for their needs.

"And laid it on her..."

Boaz helps place the heavy load on Ruth, preparing her for the journey back to Naomi. His kindness is practical as well as generous.

"Then she went into the city."

Ruth returns to Bethlehem carrying both the grain and the assurance that Boaz intends to resolve the matter. She no longer returns merely as a gleaner but as one who has received the promise of a redeemer's care.

Remark(s)

Boaz had not yet completed Ruth's redemption, but he sent her home with a tangible expression of his commitment. His provision gave Ruth and Naomi something they could see and taste while they waited for him to fulfill what he had promised. Likewise, our redemption in Christ has not yet reached its full completion, yet He has given us the Holy Spirit as a present foretaste and guarantee of our inheritance, something of the life to come that we can experience now while we wait for the full completion of our redemption.

Ruth 3:16

NKJV

Ruth 3:16 When she came to her mother-in-law, she said, "Is that you, my daughter?" Then she told her all that the man had done for her.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 3:16 When Ruth went back to her mother-in-law, Naomi asked, “What happened, my daughter?” Ruth told Naomi everything Boaz had done for her, (NLT)

Main Idea

Ruth returns to Naomi and faithfully reports everything that Boaz has said and done.

Verse Explanation

"When she came to her mother-in-law..."

Ruth returns directly to Naomi, who has been eagerly waiting for news. The plan Naomi set in motion has reached a critical point, and both women are anxious to know how Boaz responded.

"She said, 'Is that you, my daughter?'"

Naomi's question is more than a simple greeting. She is asking, "How did it go?" or "What happened?" She is eager to know whether Ruth's appeal has been accepted and what the future now holds.

"Then she told her all that the man had done for her."

Ruth faithfully recounts the entire encounter. She neither embellishes nor minimizes what happened but reports Boaz's words and actions honestly. Her confidence in Boaz's integrity is now matched by Naomi's growing confidence that God is continuing to work.

Remark(s)

Ruth returns with more than grain; she returns with hope. The conversation that began in uncertainty (3:1) now moves toward confident expectation because God's providence has become increasingly evident through Boaz's faithful response.

Ruth 3:17

NKJV

Ruth 3:17 And she said, "These six ephahs of barley he gave me; for he said to me, "˜Do not go empty-handed to your mother-in-law.' "(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 3:17 and she added, “He gave me these six scoops of barley and said, ‘Don’t go back to your mother-in-law empty-handed.’” (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz sends a generous gift to Naomi through Ruth as a tangible assurance of his commitment to care for them.

Verse Explanation

"And she said, 'These six measures of barley he gave me...'"

Ruth reports Boaz's generous gift to Naomi. The grain was not merely practical provision for their immediate needs but a visible demonstration that Boaz intended to follow through on his promise.

"For he said to me..."

Ruth repeats Boaz's own words, emphasizing that the gift carried a message as well as provision. Boaz wanted Naomi to understand his intentions.

"'Do not go empty-handed to your mother-in-law.'"

The expression "empty-handed" recalls Naomi's words when she returned from Moab:

"The LORD has brought me home again empty." (Ruth 1:21)

Boaz's gift is a reminder that Naomi is no longer empty. God has been quietly reversing her circumstances from famine to provision, from bitterness to hope, and soon from loss to redemption.

Remark(s)

Boaz's words echo Naomi's earlier complaint that she had returned "empty" (Ruth 1:21). God is already answering Naomi's bitterness with His provision, showing that what He begins quietly, He often completes faithfully.

Ruth 3:18

NKJV

Ruth 3:18 Then she said, "Sit still, my daughter, until you know how the matter will turn out; for the man will not rest until he has concluded the matter this day."(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 3:18 Then Naomi said to her, “Just be patient, my daughter, until we hear what happens. The man won’t rest until he has settled things today.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Naomi confidently encourages Ruth to wait, trusting Boaz to act promptly and faithfully.

Verse Explanation

"Then she said, 'Sit still, my daughter...'"

Naomi tells Ruth to wait patiently. Up to this point, both women have acted wisely and faithfully. Now there is nothing more for Ruth to do except trust God to work through Boaz.

"Until you know how the matter will turn out..."

Naomi is confident that the uncertainty will soon be resolved. The future is no longer in doubt because of Boaz's proven character.

"For the man will not rest..."

There is a beautiful contrast in Naomi's words. Earlier she desired that Ruth would find rest (Ruth 1:9; 3:1). Now Boaz himself will not rest until he has secured that rest for Ruth. His determination reflects the seriousness with which he accepts his responsibility as a potential redeemer.

"Until he has concluded the matter this day."

Naomi knows Boaz well enough to be certain that he will not delay. A man of integrity keeps his word, and Boaz's actions throughout the story have consistently demonstrated that quality.

Remark(s)

Naomi tells Ruth to rest because Boaz will not. Ruth can wait with confidence because her redeemer is already at work on her behalf. This beautifully foreshadows the greater Redeemer, Jesus Christ, who accomplished everything necessary for our redemption while we could do nothing to save ourselves.
