Chapter Study

Ruth Chapter 3

Verse-by-Verse
Section 1

Ruth 3:1–5 - Naomi's Plan

Overview

≡Section Summary

Naomi tells Ruth that she desires to find "rest" for her—a secure home, a husband, and a hopeful future. Knowing that Boaz will be winnowing barley that evening, she instructs Ruth to prepare herself, go to the threshing floor after the evening meal, and quietly wait until Boaz has lain down. Ruth is then to uncover Boaz's feet and lie down, symbolically placing herself under his protection and appealing for him to fulfill the role of kinsman-redeemer. Ruth humbly accepts Naomi's counsel and commits herself to obey.

◎Key Observations

  • Naomi has moved from despair to hopeful faith. In Chapter 1 she believed God had left her empty; now she actively seeks God's provision.• The goal is "rest," a settled home, security, and covenant marriage—not merely financial provision.• Naomi's plan is built upon the events of Chapter 2. She acts because she has recognized God's providence, not because she is attempting to manipulate circumstances.• The threshing floor was a public agricultural site where grain was processed and often guarded overnight during harvest.• Ruth's washing, anointing, and putting on her best garment symbolize preparation for a new season rather than seduction.• "Uncover his feet" was a respectful, symbolic act requesting Boaz's protection and redemption, not an act of sexual immorality.• Naomi instructs Ruth to wait for Boaz to tell her what to do, demonstrating humility and respect for his leadership.• Ruth's immediate response, "All that you say to me I will do," reflects the same obedient faith that has characterized her throughout the book.

★Main Idea

Faith not only recognizes God's providence—it responds to it with humble obedience. Redemption unfolds as God's people wisely act upon the opportunities He provides while trusting Him to direct the outcome.

Section 2

Ruth 3:6–9 - Ruth at Boaz's Feet

Overview

≡Section Summary

Ruth goes to the threshing floor and carefully follows Naomi's instructions. After Boaz finishes eating and drinking and lies down to guard the grain, Ruth quietly uncovers his feet and lies down. At midnight Boaz awakens and discovers a woman at his feet. When he asks her identity, Ruth identifies herself and respectfully asks him to "spread your wing over your maidservant," appealing to him as a kinsman-redeemer. Her request is not a proposal based on romance but a humble appeal for covenant redemption according to God's provision in Israel.

◎Key Observations

  • Ruth's obedience is complete and immediate, demonstrating her trust in Naomi's wisdom.• The threshing floor is a place of work and legal significance, not secrecy or immorality.• Boaz's conduct throughout the evening reflects integrity and purity.• Ruth waits for Boaz to awaken rather than initiating the conversation herself, showing humility and respect.• "Who are you?" invites Ruth to declare not merely her name but her request.• "Spread your wing over your maidservant" intentionally echoes Boaz's earlier prayer that Ruth had come to find refuge under the wings of the LORD (2:12).• Ruth appeals to Boaz not on the basis of emotion but because he is a kinsman-redeemer.• The request shifts the story from daily provision to the possibility of permanent redemption.

★Main Idea

Faith ultimately seeks the Redeemer Himself, not merely His gifts. Ruth's humble appeal demonstrates that redemption is received through trusting the one whom God has appointed to redeem, while leaving the outcome in His hands.

Section 3

Ruth 3:10–13 - Boaz's Blessing & the Nearer Redeemer

Overview

≡Section Summary

Boaz blesses Ruth for her faithful love, recognizing that her appeal is motivated by godly character rather than personal ambition. He assures her that he is willing to do everything she has requested because her reputation for virtue is well known. However, Boaz explains that another man has a prior legal right to redeem Naomi's family. He promises that the matter will be settled the very next day. If the nearer redeemer fulfills his responsibility, so be it; if not, Boaz solemnly swears before the LORD that he himself will redeem Ruth. He then instructs her to remain until morning under his protection.

◎Key Observations

  • Boaz interprets Ruth's request as an act of hesed (faithful love), not personal romance.• Ruth sought Boaz because he was the redeemer God had provided, not because he was wealthy or influential.• Boaz publicly affirms Ruth's godly reputation, calling her a woman of noble character.• Boaz's willingness to redeem is matched by his commitment to obey God's law.• The existence of a nearer redeemer introduces a legal obstacle that must be resolved before redemption can occur.• Boaz refuses to bypass God's established order even though doing so would have benefited him personally.• His oath, "As the LORD lives," demonstrates the certainty of his commitment.• Boaz protects both Ruth's safety and her reputation by having her remain until morning rather than sending her into the night.

★Main Idea

True redemption is characterized by both love and righteousness. God never accomplishes His saving purposes by ignoring His own standards. Boaz demonstrates that genuine love patiently submits to God's order while remaining fully committed to accomplishing redemption.

Section 4

Ruth 3:14–18 - Ruth Returns with Hope

Overview

≡Section Summary

Ruth remains at the threshing floor until early morning, leaving before daylight to protect both her reputation and Boaz's. Before she departs, Boaz gives her six measures of barley, sending her back to Naomi with a tangible assurance that he has not forgotten their need. When Ruth recounts everything that has happened, Naomi confidently declares that Boaz will not rest until he has settled the matter that very day. The chapter closes with anticipation, shifting the focus from preparation to the final act of redemption.

◎Key Observations

  • Boaz carefully protects both Ruth's purity and her reputation, demonstrating that integrity values appearances as well as actions.• The six measures of barley are more than a gift—they are a visible pledge that Boaz intends to fulfill his promise.• Ruth faithfully reports every detail to Naomi, continuing the pattern of openness and trust between them.• Naomi's confidence stands in sharp contrast to her despair in Chapter 1, revealing how hope has been restored through God's providence.• Naomi understands Boaz's character well enough to know that he will not delay in doing what is right.• The repeated theme of rest reaches another milestone. Naomi sought rest for Ruth (3:1), but true rest cannot come until redemption is complete.• The chapter ends with waiting, reminding the reader that God's promises are sometimes fulfilled in stages rather than all at once.• The narrative intentionally builds anticipation for the legal proceedings at the city gate in Chapter 4.

★Main Idea

Faith not only seeks the Redeemer—it confidently waits for Him to complete His work. God's promises may unfold progressively, but His faithful character gives believers confidence that He will accomplish everything He has promised in His perfect time.
