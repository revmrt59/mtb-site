Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 3
Main Idea:

At Naomi’s direction, Ruth makes an honorable appeal to Boaz at the threshing floor for marriage and the protection of a close relative, and he blesses her, promises to act, and honors the claim of a nearer relative.

Setting:

Ruth has gleaned in Boaz’s fields through the harvest (2:23), and Boaz is now winnowing barley at the threshing floor (3:2). Naomi knows that he is one of the family’s close relatives (2:20).

Chapter Outline:

  • Ruth 3:1–5 — Naomi Seeks Security for Ruth

  • Ruth 3:6–9 — Ruth’s Appeal at the Threshing Floor

  • Ruth 3:10–13 — Boaz Blesses Ruth and Pledges to Act

  • Ruth 3:14–18 — Ruth Returns with Barley to Wait

Key Verse:

“And he said, ‘Who are you?’ So she answered, ‘I am Ruth, your maidservant. Take your maidservant under your wing, for you are a close relative.’” (Ruth 3:9)

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Ruth 3 moves the story from favor toward redemption. Naomi, who once prayed that her daughters-in-law would find rest in a husband’s house (1:9), now seeks that security for Ruth (3:1). Her plan sends Ruth to the threshing floor at night. Naomi is not manipulating Ruth; she is seeking her good.

At midnight Boaz wakes to find a woman at his feet. Ruth asks, “Take your maidservant under your wing, for you are a close relative” (3:9). Her words take up the very image Boaz used when he blessed her as one who had come for refuge under the LORD’s wings (2:12). Ruth is the Moabite woman, from a people long hostile to Israel, whom God’s grace has already welcomed under those wings; now that grace is carrying her toward settled belonging through marriage and redemption. Boaz had prayed for God to shelter her; now Ruth asks him to give her that shelter himself by taking her as his wife. Her request is an honorable proposal of marriage tied to the redeemer’s duty to the family.

Boaz answers with blessing, not suspicion. He praises her loyal kindness, calls her “a virtuous woman” (3:11), and promises by an oath in the LORD’s name to act, while honoring a relative who has the first right. He guards her reputation and sends her home with barley, so that she will not return to Naomi empty-handed. Ruth has acted faithfully, and now she waits while Boaz settles the nearer relative’s claim, the step that must come before the redemption carried out in chapter 4.

Chapter Sections

Ruth 3:1–5 — Naomi Seeks Security for Ruth

Naomi resolves to seek security for Ruth through marriage to Boaz, their relative, who is winnowing barley that night. She tells Ruth how to prepare and approach him, and Ruth agrees to do everything she says.

Ruth 3:6–9 — Ruth’s Appeal at the Threshing Floor

Ruth goes to the threshing floor and does as Naomi instructed, and at midnight Boaz is startled to find her at his feet. She names herself and asks him to spread his wing over her, because he is a close relative.

Ruth 3:10–13 — Boaz Blesses Ruth and Pledges to Act

Boaz blesses Ruth for this greater kindness of seeking a husband within the family rather than going after young men. He promises to do all she asks, but explains that a nearer relative has the first right and swears by the LORD to act if that man will not.

Ruth 3:14–18 — Ruth Returns with Barley to Wait

Ruth stays until morning and leaves before anyone can recognize her, and Boaz protects her reputation and sends her home with a generous gift of barley. Naomi tells her to wait, confident that Boaz will settle the matter that very day.

Why This Chapter Matters

Ruth 3 shows that faithful initiative and honest integrity belong together. Naomi and Ruth act boldly, yet every step is honorable, and Boaz responds with the same integrity: he will not bypass the rightful order even when his own heart is willing. The chapter reveals that God’s purposes are served by people who seek one another’s good.

It also deepens the picture of redemption. Ruth, a Moabite who once called herself a foreigner (2:10), asks not only for help but for covering and belonging in a redeemer’s household. In this way the chapter points beyond Boaz to Jesus Christ, the greater Redeemer, who brings those who come to Him into relationship with Himself.

Key Themes to Watch For

Security and rest. Naomi seeks “security” for Ruth (3:1), the settled home she prayed for in 1:9.

Coming under the redeemer’s wing. Ruth’s request (3:9) answers Boaz’s blessing about refuge under the LORD’s wings (2:12).

Loyal kindness. Boaz calls Ruth’s request a greater kindness than her loyalty to Naomi (3:10).

Integrity in the right order. Boaz honors the nearer relative’s claim and binds himself by oath to act (3:12–13).

Not empty-handed. Boaz sends Ruth home with barley so she will not go back to Naomi “empty-handed” (3:17), answering Naomi’s word “empty” (1:21).

Key Words & Concepts

Threshing floor (3:2). A hard, open surface where harvested grain was beaten and then tossed in the air so the breeze would carry away the chaff. Boaz probably spent the night there to guard his grain.

Uncover his feet (3:4). Part of Naomi’s plan for Ruth’s appeal. The text does not explain the custom fully, but Boaz’s response shows that he understood it as an honorable request for marriage.

Under your wing (3:9). The Hebrew word kanaph can mean a bird’s wing or the corner of a garment. To spread it over a woman was a way of speaking about marriage and protection (compare Ezekiel 16:8).

A virtuous woman (3:11). A woman of noble character and worth. The Hebrew word chayil in this phrase also appears in 2:1, where the NKJV calls Boaz “a man of great wealth.” The English wording differs, but the shared Hebrew word marks both as people of strength and worth: Ruth for her character, Boaz for his character and standing. The two are matched before they are joined in marriage.

A relative closer than I (3:12). Another man is more closely related to Elimelech’s family and has the first right to act as redeemer. His claim must be settled before Boaz can act.
