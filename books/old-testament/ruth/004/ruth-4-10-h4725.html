POSITION - ( maqom , Strong's H4725 )

Summary:

The Hebrew noun maqom denotes much more than a geographical spot; it refers to an established station, office, standing, or rightful presence within a community. Derived from the root verb qum, which means "to arise" or "to stand," the word carries the rich theological nuance of a secure, recognized status. In Ruth 4:10, Boaz’s act of redemption guarantees that Mahlon’s legal identity, covenantal inheritance, and social standing—his "position" at the city gate—will not be erased from Israel's history. It beautifully illustrates how redemption restores not just survival, but rightful place, honor, and legacy within God's family.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The noun maqom literally means "a place of standing." It is built from the common Semitic root qum, which means to arise, stand up, or be established. Historically, a person's maqom was their home, their physical property, or the specific town where their lineage was rooted. In the Ancient Near East, where identity was deeply communal rather than individualistic, having no maqom meant being a rootless wanderer, cut off from protection, resources, and legal rights. To have a "place" was to have a life of security, belonging, and purpose.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary definition of maqom is a physical place, space, or locality. It frequently designates a room, a home, a town, or a worship site (a sanctuary). In a legal or official capacity, the word denotes a station, post, office, or "position" of authority. It signifies the established seat or rightful territory belonging to a specific individual or family. In covenantal contexts, it represents the specific inheritance portion allotted by God to each family within Israel.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Because maqom is rooted in the action of standing, it often carries a dynamic sense of stability and preservation.

• A Place of Worship: The word frequently identifies a sacred site where God meets humanity, such as Bethel ("the place" of Jacob’s ladder) or the temple in Jerusalem. It represents the space where God's presence "stands" among His people.

• A Secure Station: It describes a secure, unshakeable position. When someone is given a maqom, they are granted a stable platform from which to live and operate, contrasted with the slippery, unstable ground of the wicked.

• The Legal Seat: In civic life, maqom refers to the seat of authority at the city gate where the elders sat to execute justice and conduct business.

### 4. Theological Implications

Theologically, maqom is tied to the concept of covenantal security. God did not call Israel merely to survive, but to dwell in a prepared "place" of rest and inheritance. To lose one’s maqom was a tragedy of covenantal proportions—it meant the extinction of a family line and the loss of one's portion in the Promised Land.

The preservation of a man's maqom through the law of the kinsman-redeemer (goel) reflects God's heart for restoration. God is deeply concerned with preserving the identity, name, and inheritance of His people. In ultimate redemption, the Messiah acts as the ultimate goel who recovers our lost standing, restoring us to our rightful "position" as heirs of God's eternal kingdom.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 4:10, Elimelech's family had abandoned their physical maqom in Bethlehem due to famine, migrating to Moab where the sons died. This left their name on the brink of being permanently "cut off" (karat)—the opposite of being established (qum).

When Boaz steps forward as the redeemer, he declares his intention to marry Ruth "to perpetuate the name of the dead through his inheritance, that the name of the dead may not be cut off from among his brethren and from his position at the gate." Here, "position at the gate" refers to the civic and legal status that Mahlon's descendants would hold. The gate of the city was the place of legal transaction and social recognition. By redeeming the land and marrying Ruth, Boaz ensures that Mahlon's lineage retains its voice, its voting rights, its property, and its honored standing in the community assembly.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 28:11: "So he came to a certain place and stayed there all night, because the sun had set. And he took one of the stones of that place and put it at his head, and he lay down in that place to sleep."

• Exodus 3:5: "Then He said, 'Do not draw near this place. Take your sandals off your feet, for the place where you stand is holy ground.'"

• Joshua 1:3: "Every place that the sole of your foot will tread upon I have given you, as I said to Moses."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint), maqom is frequently translated as topos. This is the exact word Jesus uses in John 14:2: "I go to prepare a place (topos) for you." Just as Boaz acted as a redeemer to secure a legal, lasting "position" (maqom) for the family of Elimelech in Israel, Jesus Christ acts as our redeemer to secure an eternal, secure "place" (topos) for us in the presence of the Father.
