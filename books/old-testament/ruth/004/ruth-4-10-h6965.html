TO PERPETUATE - ( qum , Strong's H6965 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb qum in its basic form means "to arise" or "to stand up." In the Hiphil (causative) stem used in Ruth 4:10, it means "to cause to stand," "to raise up," or "to establish." In the context of the levirate marriage custom, Boaz pledges to "raise up" or "perpetuate" the name of the deceased Mahlon by marrying Ruth. This ensures that Mahlon's lineage, memory, and inheritance do not perish from Israel, demonstrating covenant faithfulness, sacrificial redemption, and the preservation of a family's legacy within God's covenant community.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The root qum is one of the most common verbs in the Old Testament, occurring over 600 times. Its basic physical picture is moving from a prostrate, seated, or inactive position to an upright, standing posture. It represents life, activity, readiness, and established presence.

• In the ancient Near Eastern context, to "fall" or to "lie down" often symbolized death, defeat, or ruin, while "arising" or "standing" represented life, restoration, and vindication.

• In the causative form, the word shifts from "arising" to "causing to arise" or "erecting." It is used for setting up pillars, establishing covenants, and, in genealogical contexts, raising up offspring to continue a family line that was threatened with extinction.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• In the Qal stem, the verb means to arise, stand up, get up, or establish oneself.

• In the Hiphil stem (used in Ruth 4:10 as lehaqim), the verb means to cause to arise, raise up, erect, establish, or maintain.

• When applied to a deceased person's name or inheritance, the causative stem carries the legal and relational nuance of preserving, perpetuating, or keeping alive. It refers to restoring a name or estate that was on the verge of collapsing or being forgotten, ensuring its ongoing existence and validity in the community.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Erecting Monuments: Used for setting up physical stones or pillars to serve as lasting memorials (such as Joshua raising up stones after crossing the Jordan).

• Establishing Covenants: God "establishes" or "confirms" His covenant with His people, making it firm, binding, active, and enduring.

• Raising Up Leaders: God raises up judges, prophets, and kings to deliver and guide His people when they are in distress.

• Sustaining Lineage: The specific legal use in levirate marriage where a relative "raises up" a name for his deceased brother, ensuring that the deceased man's line does not die out.

4. Theological Implications

• Covenant Faithfulness (Chesed): Perpetuating a name is an act of deep covenant love. Boaz acts not out of self-interest, but to preserve the name of his deceased relative, reflecting God's commitment to preserve His people.

• Redemption and Resurrection: In a theological sense, raising up the name of the dead from obscurity prefigures the ultimate redemption. Out of the "death" of Elimelech's line, God raises up new life, ultimately leading to David and the Messiah.

• God as the One who Raises: While human agents act, it is ultimately Yahweh who raises up the fallen, establishes the weak, and ensures that His promises do not fail.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 4:10, Elimelech, Mahlon, and Chilion have died, leaving the family line apparently extinct. By Hebrew custom, a name "cut off" from the gate of his town was a tragedy—a loss of inheritance, memory, and presence in Israel.

• Boaz steps in as the kinsman-redeemer. By marrying Ruth, the firstborn son they produce will legally carry the inheritance of the deceased Mahlon.

• The word perpetuate (to raise up) here demonstrates that Boaz's act of redemption is completely unselfish. He buys the land and takes the wife, not to expand his own estate, but to "raise up" and secure the estate of another.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 6:18 - "But I will establish My covenant with you; and you shall go into the ark..."

• Deuteronomy 25:7 - "...'My husband’s brother refuses to raise up a name for his brother in Israel; he will not perform the duty of my husband’s brother.'"

• Deuteronomy 28:9 - "The LORD will establish you as a holy people to Himself..."

• 1 Samuel 2:8 - "He raises the poor from the dust and lifts the beggar from the ash heap..."

• 2 Samuel 7:12 - "...I will set up your seed after you, who will come from your body, and I will establish his kingdom."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The contrast in Ruth 4 is between the unnamed closer relative and Boaz. The closer relative refuses to redeem because it might "ruin" his own inheritance. He is unwilling to raise up (qum) the name of Mahlon at his own expense. Boaz, however, gladly assumes the cost, illustrating the sacrificial nature of true biblical redemption.

• The ultimate fulfillment of this "raising up" goes far beyond Mahlon. Through this act of perpetuating a name, the line of Judah is preserved, leading directly to King David (Ruth 4:22) and eventually to Jesus Christ, who raises believers from death to eternal life.
