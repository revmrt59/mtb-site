ACQUIRED - ( qanah , Strong's H7069 )

Summary:

In Ruth 4:10, the word "acquired" describes Boaz’s legal and covenantal act of taking Ruth, the Moabitess, to be his wife as part of his duty as the kinsman-redeemer. Far from being a cold, commercial transaction or treating a human being as mere property, this word highlights a protective acquisition that restores honor, provides legal security, preserves a deceased family's inheritance, and serves as a profound Old Testament type of Jesus Christ, who purchases and redeems His bride, the Church.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew root qanah operates within the sphere of ancient Near Eastern legal, familial, and commercial transactions. In a nomadic and agrarian society, the acquisition of land, livestock, or servants was central to survival and community preservation. However, the term takes on a unique theological and relational weight within Israel's covenant law.

In the book of Ruth, qanah is tied directly to the laws of levirate marriage (Deuteronomy 25:5–10) and the redemption of land (Leviticus 25:23–28). The legal setting of Ruth 4 is the city gate, the ancient equivalent of a supreme court. Boaz is not merely "buying" a wife in a modern mercenary sense; he is legally establishing his right and responsibility to redeem the land of the deceased Elimelech and, by extension, taking Elimelech's widowed daughter-in-law, Ruth, into his care to raise up an heir. The word picture is one of redemption through legal purchase, transferring a vulnerable individual from a state of destitution and foreign alienation into a state of secure, covenantal possession.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meaning of qanah is to get, acquire, buy, purchase, or possess. In some contexts, it can also carry the nuance of "creating," "originating," or "forming," as God is described as the "Possessor" or "Creator" of heaven and earth.

In commercial passages, qanah refers to purchasing fields, cattle, or goods with money. In the theological and relational realm, the word describes God acquiring Israel as His special possession through the redemption of the Exodus. When applied to marriage in the context of levirate redemption, it signifies the legal act of acquiring a spouse through the payment of a bride-price or the assumption of deceased family debts, establishing her as a rightful, protected member of the household.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Commercial Purchase: The word frequently denotes the standard purchase of property. For example, Abraham purchased the cave of Machpelah, and Jacob purchased a plot of land near Shechem.

• Parental Origin: It is used by Eve at the birth of Cain when she declares she has "acquired" or "gotten" a man from the Lord, expressing parental possession and the arrival of new life.

• Divine Ownership: God is depicted as acquiring His people out of Egyptian bondage. Here, the word pictures Israel as God's hard-won, cherished possession, rescued from a foreign power.

• Acquiring Wisdom: In wisdom literature, the term is repeatedly used to urge believers to "get" or "acquire" wisdom and understanding, framing wisdom as an invaluable possession worth more than gold.

### 4. Theological Implications

The use of qanah in Ruth 4:10 points directly to the doctrine of redemption.

• The Kinsman-Redeemer (Goel): Boaz acts as the redeemer. To complete the redemption, a price must be paid and a legal transaction finalized. The acquisition of Ruth demonstrates that redemption is never free; it requires a cost.

• Christ and His Bride: This passage serves as a vivid foreshadowing of Christ's redemption of the Church. Believers were once destitute, spiritually bankrupt, and alienated foreigners (like Ruth the Moabitess). Christ, our Ultimate Kinsman-Redeemer, entered the legal assembly of God's justice, paid the price with His own blood, and "acquired" us to be His bride.

• Security and Grace: To be "acquired" by the redeemer means transferring from a state of vulnerability to absolute covenant security. Ruth’s status is elevated from a foreign gleaner in the fields to an honorable wife in Israel, eventually becoming an ancestor of King David and Jesus Christ.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 4:10, Boaz stands before the elders at the city gate and says, "Moreover, Ruth the Moabitess, the widow of Mahlon, I have acquired as my wife." The immediate context is a contrast with the closer kinsman who refused to redeem the land because it would "ruin" his own inheritance. That kinsman viewed the acquisition of a poor Moabite widow as a financial liability.

Boaz, however, uses qanah to willingly bind himself to this liability. He publicly assumes the financial burden of the land and the social responsibility of the widow. By using qanah, Boaz legally seals the transaction, leaving no room for future disputes. The term elevates the transaction above mere sentimentality; it is a legally binding covenant of grace.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 4:1 - "Now Adam knew Eve his wife, and she conceived and bore Cain, and said, 'I have acquired a man from the LORD.'"

• Genesis 25:10 - "The field which Abraham purchased from the sons of Heth. There Abraham was buried, and Sarah his wife."

• Exodus 15:16 - "Fear and dread will fall on them; By the greatness of Your arm They will be as still as a stone, Till Your people pass over, O LORD, Till the people pass over whom You have purchased."

• Proverbs 4:7 - "Wisdom is the principal thing; Therefore get wisdom; And with all your getting, get understanding."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

A key distinction exists between qanah (to acquire/purchase) and gaal (to redeem/act as a kinsman). While gaal emphasizes the relationship and the right of redemption based on kinship, qanah emphasizes the actual execution and completion of the transaction. Boaz cannot be the goel (redeemer) in name only; he must perform the qanah (the actual purchase/acquisition) to make the redemption reality. In the Christian life, Christ did not merely sympathize with our fallen state (acting as our kinsman); He went to the cross to pay the price, successfully acquiring us for Himself.
