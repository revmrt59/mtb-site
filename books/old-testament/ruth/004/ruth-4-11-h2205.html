ELDERS - ( zaqen , Strong's H2205 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word zaqen refers to an older person, a bearded man, or a recognized community leader who exercises legal, political, and spiritual authority. In the ancient Near Eastern context, and specifically in Ruth 4:11, these elders represent the official governing body of the city of Bethlehem. They gather at the city gate to witness, validate, and bless Boaz's redemption of Naomi's land and his marriage to Ruth, securing the covenant continuity of the family line.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The term zaqen is etymologically connected to the Hebrew word for "chin" or "beard" (zaqan). This concrete physical feature represents aging, maturity, and the wisdom accumulated through a long life. In ancient Israel, gray hair and a beard were symbols of honor, dignity, and experiential wisdom. Consequently, the elders were not merely old men, but respected leaders who sat at the city gates—the civic, economic, and judicial hubs of ancient towns—to arbitrate disputes, witness contracts, and preserve the community's moral and legal order.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word zaqen functions both as an adjective meaning "old" or "aged" and as a masculine noun meaning "elder," "senator," or "leader." Lexically, it denotes a class of local authorities who composed a governing council. In singular or plural form, it describes those possessing the maturity to govern. The semantic range spans from literal biological age (an old man) to an official title of political and judicial office within a clan, tribe, or city.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Literal Age: Used to describe someone advanced in years, representing the natural progression of life and the honor due to longevity.

• Official Dignity: Used of the "elders of Egypt" or "elders of Israel," representing formal, structured leadership.

• Judicial Assembly: Standing or sitting at the gate, acting as a living archive of community law, custom, and genealogy. The elder represents an anchor of stability, bridging past traditions with present legal decisions.

4. Theological Implications

The role of the zaqen highlights God's design for community governance and covenant accountability. Elders are guardians of the covenant. They ensure that the vulnerable (widows, orphans, strangers) are protected according to the Mosaic Law. Their presence and endorsement of Boaz's actions demonstrate that redemptive acts in Scripture are not merely private affairs but public, covenantal transactions recognized by the community and validated under God's law.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 4:11, the ten elders of Bethlehem (convened by Boaz in Ruth 4:2) serve as formal legal witnesses to the transaction between Boaz and the closer redeemer. When they declare, "We are witnesses," they legally ratify the redemption of Elimelech's land and the marriage to Ruth the Moabitess. Their subsequent blessing over Ruth (linking her to Rachel, Leah, and Tamar) transforms a legal procedure into a highly theological and prophetic moment, integrating a foreign widow into the messianic line of Israel.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The council of elders in Bethlehem consists of ten men (Ruth 4:2). In later Jewish tradition, ten men became the minimum quorum (a minyan) required to establish a synagogue or conduct public legal and religious proceedings. This underscores the orderly, official, and indisputable nature of Boaz's redemption, leaving no room for future legal challenges to Obed’s legitimacy or inheritance.
