LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh represents the personal, covenant-keeping, and eternal God of Israel. In Ruth 4:11, the elders and community at the gate invoke this sacred name to bless the union of Boaz and Ruth. By calling upon the LORD, they appeal to the supreme Ruler who sovereignly orchestrates history, reverses emptiness into fullness, and actively builds His covenant family. This name assures believers of God's active, personal involvement in the ordinary details of life to bring about His grand redemptive purposes.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The name Yahweh is the tetragrammaton (YHWH), the most sacred, unique, and distinctive name of God in the Old Testament. It was revealed most fully to Moses at the burning bush in Exodus 3:14 as "I AM WHO I AM" (derived from the Hebrew verb hayah, meaning "to be" or "to become"). While other titles, such as Elohim, emphasize God's role as the supreme Creator and ruler over all nations, Yahweh is His deeply personal covenant name. It depicts the God who enters into relationships with human beings, binds Himself to promises, and acts dynamically in human history to redeem His people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name signifies "the Self-Existent One," "the Eternal One," or "the One who causes to be." It denotes a God who is uncaused, unchanging, self-sufficient, and entirely independent of creation, yet chooses to make Himself intimately available to His people. Within the semantic range of Hebrew thought, Yahweh is the active Lord of history, the Savior of Israel, the righteous Judge, and the merciful Provider whose actions are always consistent with His holy character.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Hebrew Bible, this name is written using the four consonants Y-H-W-H. Out of profound reverence, Jewish tradition avoided pronouncing the name aloud, substituting the title Adonai (Lord) during public reading. This tradition is preserved in modern English translations by rendering the name in capital letters as "LORD."

• The primary word picture associated with Yahweh is that of an unshakeable, always-present, covenant-bound Guardian.

• It is the name invoked when establishing altars, swearing oaths, offering praise for national deliverance, and seeking blessing for family lineages. It stands as a verbal monument to His absolute faithfulness.

4. Theological Implications

Yahweh is both the Author of life and the Sovereign of history. The use of this name guarantees that God’s promises are completely reliable because His very identity is tied to His word. In the theological fabric of the Old Testament, Yahweh balances transcendent majesty with immanent kindness (hesed). He is the Creator of the universe who nevertheless hears the cry of a destitute widow. In redemptive history, Yahweh is the God who reverses the consequences of the fall. Throughout the narrative of Ruth, His covenant name is the ground of hope, demonstrating that even when God seems silent, He is actively weaving together lives, lineages, and legacies to fulfill His messianic plans.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 4:11, the townspeople and elders gather at the city gate to witness Boaz's redemption of Ruth. They transition from legal witnesses to intercessors, pronouncing a prophetic blessing upon the couple: "The LORD make the woman who is coming to your house like Rachel and Leah..."

• The invocation of the name Yahweh recognizes that only the covenant God of Israel has the power to open the womb and build a household.

• This blessing deliberately echoes the history of Rachel and Leah, whose wombs Yahweh opened to build the twelve tribes of Israel.

• By invoking the LORD, the community declares that this marriage is not merely a social or economic transaction; it is a sacred event. Yahweh is the true Builder of this home. This prayer is answered directly in Ruth 4:13 when "the LORD gave her conception," inserting Ruth—a Moabite—into the line of David and ultimately Jesus Christ.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 15:6: "And he believed in the LORD, and He accounted it to him for righteousness."

• Exodus 3:15: "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

• Psalm 23:1: "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

• Proverbs 18:10: "The name of the LORD is a strong tower; The righteous run into it and are safe."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The book of Ruth begins with a famine in Bethlehem (meaning "House of Bread"), signaling a spiritual and physical distance from the blessings of Yahweh. Yet, the story concludes with the abundance of harvest, a restored family, and the birth of Obed. The repetitive use of the name Yahweh in prayers, blessings, and oaths throughout the book underscores a vital truth: even during the chaotic period of the Judges, when "everyone did what was right in his own eyes," the LORD remained faithfully and quietly at work in the lives of ordinary people who feared His name.
