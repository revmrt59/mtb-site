ISRAEL - ( Yisra'el , Strong's H3478 )

Summary:

The name Israel, meaning "God strives" or "one who wrestles with God," originates from Jacob's life-changing encounter with the Angel of the Lord at Peniel. In Ruth 4:11, the term "Israel" refers to the entire covenant community, specifically the twelve tribes descended from Jacob's wives, Rachel and Leah. By invoking the "house of Israel" at Boaz and Ruth's wedding, the elders at the gate of Bethlehem bless this union, praying that Ruth, a Moabitess, will be integrated into the lineage of God's covenant people. Through this marriage, she would play a pivotal role in building and preserving the messianic line.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The name Yisra'el is a compound word derived from two Hebrew roots: sarah (meaning to contend, strive, or exert power) and 'el (the shortened name for God, emphasizing His power and sovereignty).

• The name was first given to the patriarch Jacob in Genesis 32:28 after his all-night wrestling match with God. It marked a physical and spiritual transition from Jacob (the "heel-catcher" or "deceiver") to Israel (the one who prevails through divine grace).

• Historically, the term expanded from the personal name of a single patriarch to a collective designator. It came to represent his twelve sons, their descendants (the tribes of Israel), the unified monarchy, the northern kingdom after the civil split, and the theological concept of God's chosen covenant nation.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The name means "God strives," "He will rule as God," or "one who struggles with God."

• Lexically, the noun serves as:

• The proper name of the patriarch Jacob.

• The national title for the descendant tribes of Jacob (bene Yisra'el or "sons of Israel").

• The localized designation of the Northern Kingdom as opposed to the Southern Kingdom of Judah.

• A metonymy for the "house of Israel" (beit Yisra'el), referring to the theological, domestic, and social household of God's people.

• In Ruth 4:11, the term refers to the entirety of the covenant nation, conceptualized as a single household built up from historical foundations.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The phrase "built the house of Israel" in Ruth 4:11 employs a powerful domestic metaphor. The Hebrew verb for "built" (banah) shares a close linguistic connection with the word for "sons" or "children" (banim).

• To "build a house" means to establish a lineage, raise up descendants, and ensure the perpetuation of a family name. Rachel and Leah are pictured as the builders who laid the physical and familial foundation of the nation by bearing the patriarchs of the twelve tribes.

• The "house of Israel" is pictured not as a physical structure of wood and stone, but as a living, breathing temple of people, unified by covenant and bloodline.

4. Theological Implications

• The inclusion of "Israel" in this blessing highlights the theme of redemption and incorporation. Ruth was a Moabitess, a member of a nation excluded from the assembly of Israel under normal circumstances. By marrying Boaz, she is brought into the "house of Israel."

• It demonstrates that the boundaries of God's covenant family, while historically distinct, were always open to those who demonstrated faith and loyalty to the God of Israel.

• This passage links the local, domestic events of Bethlehem to the grand narrative of redemptive history. The building of this specific household contributes directly to the preservation of the tribe of Judah, from which would come King David and ultimately the Messiah, Jesus Christ.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• Ruth 4:11 takes place at the city gate of Bethlehem, a place of legal transaction and community witness.

• The elders pray that Ruth will be like Rachel and Leah. This is a remarkable blessing, as Rachel and Leah were the primary matriarchs of the nation. For Hebrew elders to compare a Moabite widow to the founding mothers of Israel is a testament to Ruth's outstanding character ("a woman of virtue") and the redemptive grace of God.

• The mention of "the house of Israel" in Bethlehem of Ephrathah grounds this specific, local family line within the grander covenant purposes of the nation. Boaz's obedience as a kinsman-redeemer serves the larger interest of Israel's survival and spiritual destiny.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 32:28 - "And He said, 'Your name shall no longer be called Jacob, but Israel; for you have struggled with God and with men, and have prevailed.'"

• Exodus 4:22 - "Then you shall say to Pharaoh, 'Thus says the Lord: "Israel is My son, My firstborn."'"

• Joshua 24:1 - "Then Joshua gathered all the tribes of Israel to Shechem and called for the elders of Israel, for their heads, for their judges, and for their officers; and they presented themselves before God."

• Psalm 121:4 - "Behold, He who keeps Israel shall neither slumber nor sleep."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• Rachel and Leah were often rivals in life, yet in Hebrew memory, they are remembered in unison as the co-builders of the nation. Their historical rivalry is set aside in the theological reflection of the elders, who see only the unified work of God in constructing His people.

• The blessing is prophetic. Ruth would indeed build the house of Israel by giving birth to Obed, who fathered Jesse, who fathered David.
