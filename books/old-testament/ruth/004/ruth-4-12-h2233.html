OFFSPRING - ( zera` , Strong's H2233 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word zera` refers fundamentally to seed, whether agricultural or biological. In the context of human lineage, it represents descendants, posterity, or offspring. In Ruth 4:12, the word carries both immediate and ultimate significance: it is a prayer for physical children to preserve a family line threatened by tragedy, while simultaneously serving as a crucial link in the grand redemptive narrative that leads to King David and, ultimately, Jesus Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• In the ancient Near East, survival, identity, and legacy were inextricably tied to agriculture and family lineage. The word zera` is rooted in the physical reality of sowing seed in a field.

• Sowing was an act of faith and hope. A farmer buried a small, seemingly dead seed in the soil with the expectation that it would produce life, growth, and a future harvest.

• When biblical writers applied this concrete agricultural image to family lines, zera` became a vivid metaphor for human reproduction and the continuation of a household. Children were seen as the harvest of a family line, carrying the identity, name, and inheritance of their ancestors into the future.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The noun zera` is derived from the verb zara`, which means to sow or scatter seed.

• The primary lexical definitions include:

• The seed of plants and agricultural crops.

• Biological semen (the seed of copulation).

• Offspring, children, descendants, or posterity.

• A family line, race, or class of people sharing a common ancestry or moral character.

• In Ruth 4:12, the term is used in its personal and genealogical sense, referring to the physical children that would be born from the marriage of Boaz and Ruth.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Old Testament employs zera` to depict the forward movement of human history under God’s sovereignty.

• The Sown Seed: Represents physical life and agricultural abundance, illustrating God's physical provision for His people.

• The Biological Seed: Represents the physical continuity of a household, particularly when a line is threatened with extinction.

• The Multiplied Seed: Often compared to the dust of the earth, the sand of the seashore, or the stars of heaven, emphasizing the immense, uncountable scale of divine blessing and covenant expansion.

4. Theological Implications

• The Messianic Promise: The concept of zera` is central to biblical theology, beginning with the "Protoevangelium" in Genesis 3:15, where God promises that the "seed" of the woman will eventually crush the head of the serpent.

• The Abrahamic Covenant: God established His covenant with Abraham, promising to bless his zera` and make them a vehicle of blessing for all nations.

• The Davidic Covenant: God promised David that His covenant would continue through David's zera`, whose throne would be established forever.

• Redemptive Continuity: The Book of Ruth sits at the intersection of these covenants. The preservation of the "seed" in the line of Judah (through Perez, Boaz, and Obed) ensures that the line of the Messiah remains unbroken.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 4:12, the elders and people of Bethlehem offer a blessing upon the union of Boaz and Ruth, praying that their house would be like the house of Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah.

• This comparison to Perez is highly deliberate. Perez was born out of a levirate-like union to raise up "seed" for a deceased brother, mirroring the situation of Boaz marrying Ruth to redeem the name of Mahlon and Elimelech.

• The blessing highlights that "the offspring" is a direct gift from God: "the offspring which the LORD will give you." This is particularly poignant because Ruth had previously been barren during her ten-year marriage to Mahlon.

• The ultimate fulfillment of this blessing transcended the preservation of Elimelech's estate; it resulted in the birth of Obed, the grandfather of King David.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 3:15: "And I will put enmity Between you and the woman, And between your seed and her Seed; He shall bruise your head, And you shall bruise His heel."

• Genesis 15:5: "Then He brought him outside and said, 'Look now toward heaven, and count the stars if you are able to number them.' And He said to him, 'So shall your descendants be.'"

• Genesis 22:17: "blessing I will bless you, and multiplying I will multiply your descendants as the stars of the heaven and as the sand which is on the seashore; and your descendants shall possess the gate of their enemies."

• 2 Samuel 7:12: "When your days are fulfilled and you rest with your fathers, I will set up your seed after you, who will come from your body, and I will establish his kingdom."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• In the Septuagint (the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament), zera` is translated as sperma, from which the English word "sperm" is derived.

• This Greek term is utilized by the Apostle Paul in Galatians 3:16 to make a profound theological point: the ultimate recipient of the Abrahamic promise was not "seeds" (plural), but to Abraham's "Seed" (singular), who is Jesus Christ.

• The transition of Ruth—a Moabitess outside the covenant community—into the lineage of the holy zera` demonstrates that God's redemptive plan has always been inclusive, grafting in those of faith to inherit the covenant promises.
