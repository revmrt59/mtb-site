LORD - ( Yahweh , Strong's H3068 )

Summary:

The name Yahweh (translated as LORD in all capital letters) is the personal, covenantal name of the God of Israel, signifying His self-existence, eternal presence, unshakeable faithfulness, and active relationship with His people. In Ruth 4:12, the elders and people at the gate invoke this covenant name to bless Boaz and Ruth, recognizing the LORD as the ultimate author of life, family, and legacy. It is a powerful reminder that while humans make legal and personal commitments, it is the LORD who sovereignly orchestrates redemption, breathes life into barrenness, and establishes a heritage that spans generations.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The name Yahweh is the sacred Tetragrammaton (the four-letter Hebrew name YHWH), historically derived from the Hebrew verb hayah, which means "to be" or "to exist." This name was dynamically revealed to Moses at the burning bush in Exodus 3, where God declared His identity as "I AM WHO I AM."

Unlike the generic title Elohim (God), which emphasizes God's supreme power and role as Creator of the universe, Yahweh is His personal, intimate name. It depicts a God who is not distant or detached, but one who actively enters into human history, binds Himself to His people through solemn covenants, and remains unchangingly faithful to His promises.

In the ancient Near East, where pagan deities were viewed as capricious and localized, Yahweh stood apart as the self-existent, sovereign Lord over all creation, yet intimately involved in the daily, ordinary lives of His worshippers. In the book of Ruth, which takes place during the chaotic and unfaithful period of the Judges, this name stands as a beacon of hope, showing that God's covenant loyalty remains alive in a faithful remnant.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• Yahweh refers to the one true, self-existent, and eternal God of Israel. The core of its lexical meaning centers on active presence and absolute reliability. It denotes:

• The Self-Existent One: He who has life in Himself and depends on nothing else for His existence.

• The Eternal and Unchanging One: He who is the same yesterday, today, and forever, ensuring that His character and promises never fail.

• The Covenant Keeper: The specific name associated with God’s redemptive initiatives, grace, mercy, and relational commitments to His people.

Because of the extreme holiness associated with this name, later Jewish tradition avoided pronouncing it aloud, substituting the word Adonai (Lord) during public readings. This practice is preserved in modern English translations by rendering the Tetragrammaton in capital letters as "LORD."

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

The name occurs over 6,800 times in the Old Testament, making it the most frequent designation for God. It is used in Scripture to paint vivid word pictures of God's relational attributes:

• A Shepherd: Leading, feeding, and protecting His flock (Psalm 23:1).

• A Rock and Fortress: Providing absolute security, stability, and shelter in times of trouble (Psalm 18:2).

• A Husband: Depicting His deep, passionate, and enduring covenant love for His people, despite their spiritual unfaithfulness (Isaiah 54:5).

In the narrative of Ruth, Yahweh is invoked in prayers, blessings, and oaths. Rather than performing dramatic miracles like parting seas or sending fire from heaven, Yahweh is pictured as the quiet Director of providence—the One who guides Ruth to Boaz’s field, who grants favor in the eyes of others, and who ultimately heals the deep emptiness of Naomi’s heart.

### 4. Theological Implications

The presence of the name Yahweh in Ruth 4:12 carries profound theological weight:

• Sovereignty Over Life: The text reminds us that human effort alone cannot produce a legacy. The elders pray for the offspring "which the LORD will give you." Children are a heritage from Yahweh, and He is the sole author of physical and spiritual life.

• The Power of Grace: The blessing references Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah. This history was filled with human failure, yet Yahweh redeemed that broken family line. By invoking Yahweh, the elders recognize that God specializes in bringing beauty out of brokenness and life out of barrenness.

• Covenant Loyalty (Chesed): Yahweh is the source of all true lovingkindness. The human kindness demonstrated by Ruth and Boaz throughout the story is a reflection of Yahweh’s own character. His covenant faithfulness guarantees that those who take refuge under His wings will never be abandoned.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 4:12, Boaz has just completed the legal transaction at the city gate to redeem Naomi’s land and acquire Ruth the Moabitess as his wife. The elders and the townsfolk gather as witnesses to this redemption.

Their blessing is deeply theological: "May your house be like the house of Perez... because of the offspring which the LORD will give you from this young woman."

The context reveals several crucial nuances:

• A Shift from Foreign to Covenant Identity: Ruth, a Moabite outsider, is now fully brought into the covenant blessing of Yahweh. The community prays that Yahweh Himself will establish her home.

• Redemption Achieved Through Divine Action: While Boaz paid the redemption price, the community acknowledges that only Yahweh can make the marriage fruitful.

• The Seed of Promise: The prayer is answered in the very next verse (Ruth 4:13), where "the LORD gave her conception, and she bore a son." This son, Obed, becomes the grandfather of King David, demonstrating that Yahweh was using this humble, local marriage to advance His grand plan of salvation.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 2:4: "This is the history of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens,"

• Exodus 3:15: "Moreover God said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations."'"

• Psalm 23:1: "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."

• Proverbs 3:5: "Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and lean not on your own understanding;"

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

In the Hebrew text of the Book of Ruth, there is a beautiful literary symmetry regarding the name Yahweh. The story begins with Naomi declaring that "the hand of the LORD has gone out against me" (Ruth 1:13) and that "the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me" (Ruth 1:20).

However, as the narrative progresses, the community consistently speaks of Yahweh as the source of blessing and restoration. The climax in chapter 4 reverses Naomi’s bitterness. The invocation of Yahweh in verse 12 shows that the very God Naomi thought had abandoned her was actually working behind the scenes to crown her life—and the history of Israel—with redemption.
