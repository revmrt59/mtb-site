CLOSE RELATIVE - ( ga'al , Strong's H1350 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word ga'al refers to the act of redeeming, buying back, or acting as a kinsman-redeemer. In Israelite law, a close relative (goel) stepped in to deliver a family member from poverty, slavery, or the loss of ancestral land, and to perpetuate the family line. In Ruth 4:14, the "close relative" is celebrated as a provision of God's grace, serving as a powerful picture of redemptive restoration and pointing forward to Jesus Christ, our ultimate Kinsman-Redeemer.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The concept of ga'al is deeply rooted in Israel's social and economic laws. The goel (the active participle of ga'al, meaning "redeemer" or "close relative") was the nearest male relative responsible for protecting the family's lives, property, and lineage. If a man fell into poverty and had to sell his land or himself into slavery, the close relative was obligated to step in and buy back (redeem) the land or the person. Additionally, if a man died without an heir, the close relative would marry the widow to raise up an heir for the deceased (levirate marriage). The word conjures up a picture of restoration, rescue, and familial duty, where a willing and able relative steps into another's hopeless situation to restore what was lost.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb ga'al fundamentally means to redeem, deliver, rescue, or act as a kinsman. Lexical resources highlight several primary applications:

• To buy back land or property that has been sold due to poverty.

• To redeem individuals from indentured servitude or slavery.

• To avenge the blood of a slain relative, acting as the goel haddam (avenger of blood).

• To marry a childless widow of a deceased relative to preserve the family name and inheritance.

• God acting as the Redeemer of Israel, delivering them from Egypt and Babylon.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Avenger of Blood: While often associated with mercy, the goel also executed justice. If a family member was murdered, the close relative was the one responsible for seeking justice and maintaining societal order.

• The Purchaser of Land: The close relative kept the ancestral inheritance within the family tribe, symbolizing the preservation of the covenant promise given by Yahweh.

• The Restorer of Life: In the Book of Ruth, the goel marries Ruth, preserving the line of Elimelech and transforming a story of bitter emptiness into one of abundant life.

4. Theological Implications

The goel serves as one of the most profound Old Testament types of Jesus Christ. For a person to act as a kinsman-redeemer, three criteria had to be met:

• He must be a close relative (Jesus became human, our brother, to redeem us).

• He must have the resources to pay the redemption price (Jesus possessed the sinless life required to pay our debt).

• He must be willing to redeem (Jesus freely laid down His life).

Yahweh Himself is frequently called Israel’s Redeemer (Goel), emphasizing His covenant faithfulness and personal rescue of His people from exile.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 4:14, the women of Bethlehem praise God because He has not left Naomi without a "close relative" (goel). Interestingly, while Boaz performed the legal work of the kinsman-redeemer by purchasing the land and marrying Ruth, the women point to the newborn baby, Obed, as the ultimate goel for Naomi in her old age. The child represents the restoration of Naomi's family line, the security of her future, and the practical provision of her physical needs. The verse transitions Naomi from a state of emptiness and bitter grief to a state of fullness and joy through the birth of a redeemer.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Leviticus 25:25 - "If one of your brethren becomes poor, and has sold some of his possession, and if his redeeming relative comes to redeem it, then he may redeem what his brother sold."

• Ruth 3:12 - "Now it is true that I am a close relative; however, there is a relative closer than I."

• Job 19:25 - "For I know that my Redeemer lives, and He shall stand at last on the earth;"

• Psalm 19:14 - "Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be acceptable in Your sight, O LORD, my strength and my Redeemer."

• Isaiah 43:1 - "But now, thus says the LORD, who created you, O Jacob, and He who formed you, O Israel: 'Fear not, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by your name; you are Mine.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The ultimate lineage of the goel in Ruth leads directly to King David, and eventually to Jesus Christ. This demonstrates that the small, seemingly mundane acts of covenant faithfulness (hesed) by Boaz and Ruth in Bethlehem were part of God’s grand, cosmic design to provide the Savior of the world.
