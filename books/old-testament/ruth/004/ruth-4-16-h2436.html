BOSOM - ( cheyq , Strong's H2436 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word cheyq refers to the chest, lap, or fold of a garment near the chest, symbolizing the most intimate place of care, protection, nourishment, and relational closeness. In Ruth 4:16, when Naomi places Obed on her "bosom," it signifies far more than a physical posture; it represents legal adoption, the restoration of her empty life, and the fulfillment of God’s covenant faithfulness. This term beautifully illustrates how God folds the vulnerable and broken into His intimate care and integrates them into His redemptive purposes.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• Cheyq (also spelled chêq) refers to the front of the body, specifically the chest or the lap where one holds something closely. In ancient Near Eastern culture, the fold of the outer garment (the cloak or tunic above the belt) served as a pocket where valuables, food, or young animals were carried. To hold a child or a loved one in one's bosom was a physical expression of ultimate safety, tender affection, and ownership. The word pictures the safe harbor of a parent's arms, the place of nurture for an infant, or the intimate embrace of spouses.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The noun cheyq denotes the bosom, lap, chest, or fold of a garment. It represents the center of physical closeness and emotional intimacy. Lexically, it refers to:

• The physical chest or lap of a person (Genesis 16:5; 1 Kings 3:20).

• The fold of a garment above the girdle where items are kept or hidden (Exodus 4:6; Proverbs 17:23).

• An intimate, relational space of safety and care (Numbers 11:12; 2 Samuel 12:3).

• In a figurative sense, the inner depth of a person, such as the heart or mind (Ecclesiastes 7:9).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Lap of Provision and Care: Moses uses cheyq to describe a nursing father carrying an infant (Numbers 11:12), illustrating tender, sustained care.

• The Fold of Secrecy: In Proverbs, a "gift in secret" or a bribe is described as being drawn from the cheyq (garment fold), representing hidden transactions (Proverbs 21:14).

• The Place of Spousal Intimacy: The phrase "wife of your bosom" (eshet cheyq) describes the unique, covenantal intimacy of marriage (Deuteronomy 13:6).

• The Harbor of Rest: Abraham's bosom (referenced in the New Testament but reflecting this Old Testament Hebrew concept) represents the place of ultimate security, rest, and fellowship in the afterlife.

4. Theological Implications

Theologically, cheyq reveals the deeply personal and relational nature of God’s covenant care. God does not merely govern His people from a distance; He gathers them to His chest. Isaiah 40:11 depicts the Messiah carrying the lambs "in His bosom," highlighting Christ's gentle pastoral care. In the context of Ruth, the word demonstrates how God restores the outcast and empty. Naomi, who declared herself "Mara" (bitter) because the Lord had dealt bitterly with her, ends her narrative with a child in her cheyq. This shows that God’s redemptive plan moves us from the wilderness of isolation into the warmth of His familial embrace.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 4:16, Naomi takes Obed and lays him on her bosom. This act is highly symbolic and legally significant in ancient Israelite custom. By placing Obed on her bosom, Naomi is not merely baby-sitting; she is performing a formal act of adoption and claiming him as her own heir. The community recognizes this in verse 17, declaring, "There is a son born to Naomi." This child, born to Ruth and Boaz, legally secures the line of Elimelech, preserving his name in Israel. Naomi's empty lap, once barren and bereaved of her two sons, is now filled with the seed of redemption. The grandson she holds is the ancestor of King David and ultimately Jesus Christ, proving that God weaves our personal griefs into His grand tapestry of redemption.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 16:5 – "Then Sarai said to Abram, 'My wrong be upon you! I gave my maid into your bosom; and when she saw that she had conceived, I became despised in her eyes. The LORD judge between you and me.'"

• Numbers 11:12 – "Did I conceive all these people? Did I beget them, that You should say to me, 'Carry them in your bosom, as a guardian carries a nursing child,' to the land which You swore to their fathers?"

• 2 Samuel 12:3 – "But the poor man had nothing, except one little ewe lamb which he had bought and nourished; and it grew up together with him and with his children. It ate of his own food and drank from his own cup and lay in his bosom; and it was like a daughter to him."

• 1 Kings 3:20 – "So she arose in the middle of the night and took my son from my side, while your maidservant slept, and laid him in her bosom, and laid her dead child in my bosom."

• Isaiah 40:11 – "He will feed His flock like a shepherd; He will gather the lambs with His arm, and carry them in His bosom, and gently lead those who are with young."

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Septuagint (LXX) translates cheyq in Ruth 4:16 using the Greek noun kolpos. This is the exact same word used in the New Testament to describe Jesus’ relationship with the Father in John 1:18 ("who is in the bosom of the Father") and Lazarus in "Abraham’s bosom" (Luke 16:22). This linguistic link emphasizes that the Old Testament concept of the cheyq as a place of safety, union, and supreme favor is fully realized in the New Covenant through our union with Christ, who rests eternally in the closest fellowship with the Father.
