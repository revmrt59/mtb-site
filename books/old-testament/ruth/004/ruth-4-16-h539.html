NURSE - ( 'aman , Strong's H0539 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word 'aman fundamentally means to support, confirm, uphold, or establish. In its participial form (as seen in Ruth 4:16), it refers to a guardian, foster parent, caretaker, or nurse. Rather than signifying a biological wet nurse, this term denotes a person who provides dependable, stabilizing, and nurturing care to sustain a child's life and growth. In the context of Ruth 4, Naomi becoming a "nurse" ('omenet) to Obed represents the ultimate restoration of her life. The empty, bitter widow who once lamented her lack of offspring is now the faithful guardian of the child who will preserve her family line and ultimately lead to the Messiah.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The verbal root 'aman carries the primary idea of firmness, stability, and reliability. It is the linguistic foundation for the well-known word "Amen," which affirms that something is true, trustworthy, and certain. To "nurse" or "foster" a child under this root is to provide a solid foundation of safety, nourishment, and preservation. The word picture is not of a biological mother nursing an infant at the breast, but of a devoted caretaker holding, carrying, and securing a child in their arms, ensuring they are firmly supported and protected from harm.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

In the Qal active participle form, 'aman functions as a noun indicating one who fosters, nurtures, or acts as a guardian:

• The masculine participle ('omen) refers to a "foster father" or "guardian."

• The feminine participle ('omenet) refers to a "foster mother," "governess," or "nurse."

The lexical range emphasizes the action of holding, sustaining, carrying, and faithfully rearing a child. It is distinct from yanaq (the Hebrew word for a biological wet nurse who lactates). An 'omenet is a custodian of life, responsible for the daily physical, emotional, and social development of the child.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

Scripture uses this root to paint vivid pictures of deep responsibility, tender care, and absolute dependability:

• The Nursing Father: Moses uses the masculine form when complaining to God about the burden of leading Israel, asking if he must carry the rebellious nation in his bosom "as a nursing father carries a sucking child" (Numbers 11:12). This highlights the physical exhaustion and absolute dependency inherent in the role of an 'omen.

• The Guardian of Royalty: The term describes the caretakers of royal children who are responsible for their physical safety and education (2 Kings 10:1).

• The Tender Protector: Mordecai acts as the 'omen ("bringing up" or fostering) to the orphaned Esther, demonstrating a protective, self-sacrificing guardianship (Esther 2:7).

### 4. Theological Implications

The theological depth of 'aman as a nurse or guardian lies in its connection to covenant faithfulness. God portrays Himself throughout Scripture with these same nurturing qualities—carrying His people in His arms, stabilizing them, and ensuring their growth. When a human serves as an 'omenet, they reflect God's own sustaining grace.

Furthermore, this word showcases God's power of redemption. The transformation of Naomi from a hopeless widow with "empty" hands to a "nurse" holding a baby on her bosom demonstrates that God secures, stabilizes, and establishes those who trust in Him, turning their mourning into active, life-giving service.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 4:16, Naomi takes Ruth and Boaz's newborn son, Obed, places him on her bosom, and becomes his 'nurse ('omenet).

• The Reversal of Emptiness: In Ruth 1:21, Naomi lamented, "I went out full, and the Lord has brought me home again empty." In Ruth 4:16, her hands and lap are literally filled with the next generation.

• Legal and Spiritual Guardianship: Because Naomi is past childbearing age, she cannot be a wet nurse. By becoming his 'omenet, she assumes the role of a foster mother and guardian, integrating the child into the inheritance of her deceased husband, Elimelech.

• A Community Affirmation: The neighborhood women recognize this redemptive role, declaring in the following verse, "There is a son born to Naomi" (Ruth 4:17). Naomi's faithful care as a nurse helps secure the lineage of David and, ultimately, Jesus Christ.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Numbers 11:12 - "Have I conceived all this people? Have I begotten them, that You should say to me, ‘Carry them in your bosom, as a nursing father carries a sucking child,’ to the land which You swore to their fathers?"

• 2 Samuel 4:4 - "Jonathan, Saul’s son, had a son who was lame in his feet. He was five years old when the news about Saul and Jonathan came from Jezreel; and his nurse took him up and fled."

• Esther 2:7 - "And Mordecai had brought up Hadassah, that is, Esther, his uncle’s daughter, for she had neither father nor mother."

• Isaiah 49:23 - "Kings shall be your foster fathers, and their queens your nursing mothers; they shall bow down to you with their faces to the earth, and lick the dust of your feet."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• The transition of the root 'aman from meaning "to be firm/faithful" to "to nurse/foster" shows that in the ancient Hebrew mind, rearing a child was fundamentally about establishing them in a secure, stable environment.

• The Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint) often translates this Hebrew participle using the Greek word tithene, which means a nurse, tutor, or one who cherishes and rears. This same nurturing imagery is picked up by the Apostle Paul in 1 Thessalonians 2:7, where he describes his ministry: "but we were gentle among you, just as a nursing mother cherishes her own children."
