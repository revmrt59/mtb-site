LAID - ( shith , Strong's H7896 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb shith signifies the deliberate act of placing, setting, or positioning something with intent and purpose. In the context of Ruth 4:16, where Naomi takes her newborn grandson Obed and lays him on her bosom, this word captures far more than a simple physical action. It represents a profound moment of emotional restoration and covenantal redemption. By placing the child close to her heart, Naomi transitions from her self-proclaimed state of bitter emptiness to one of divine fullness. This deliberate act symbolizes her embrace of God's renewed favor and her formal role in nurturing the lineage that would ultimately lead to King David and the Messiah.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The root meaning of shith is to put, place, or set in a physical location. Unlike verbs that suggest a hurried or casual dropping of an object, shith carries a sense of intentionality, care, and specific placement. In agricultural, domestic, and ancient Near Eastern cultural contexts, the word is used for setting boundaries, placing hands on someone for blessing, or positioning an object exactly where it belongs to fulfill its designated purpose. In Ruth 4:16, the word picture is tender and highly personal: a grandmother carefully and lovingly positioning a newborn child against her chest, a posture of security, warmth, and ownership.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb shith has a broad semantic range that includes:

• To put, place, or lay (physically positioning an object or person).

• To set or station (such as placing guards, setting boundaries, or establishing limits).

• To appoint, make, or designate (establishing a status, destiny, or role).

• To direct or set one's mind or heart toward something.

In the active Qal stem, as seen in Ruth 4:16, it denotes the direct physical action of placing or laying the child.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, shith is employed to describe both physical and metaphorical actions of setting or placing:

• The Blessing of the Hand: It is used when a patriarch deliberately places his hand on a descendant to confer a blessing, indicating a transfer of authority, favor, and inheritance.

• Divine Boundaries: The word describes God placing boundaries on the sea or establishing the physical order of creation, showing sovereign design and control.

• Mental Focus: It is used idiomatically for setting one's heart or mind on a specific path, truth, or goal.

The common thread is that whatever is "laid" or "set" by shith is positioned with a definitive future outcome in mind.

4. Theological Implications

The theological significance of shith in the narrative of Ruth is found in the theme of reversal and redemption. When Naomi returned to Bethlehem, she declared, "I went out full, and the Lord has brought me home again empty" (Ruth 1:21). When she takes Ruth's son and lays (shith) him on her bosom, the physical gesture represents the reversal of that emptiness. God has provided a "redeemer" for Naomi's family line. By laying the child on her bosom, Naomi participates in a symbolic act of adoption and integration. The child is not merely Ruth’s; he is placed into Naomi’s lap and life as the heir of Elimelech, securing the family name and land. This highlights God’s hesed (covenant faithfulness) to restore what was lost.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 4:16, the choice of shith highlights Naomi’s active embrace of her new reality. The action of laying the child on her bosom (cheq) serves as a formal, public sign of her role as the child's guardian and nurse. This physical placement represents the binding of the child to her family unit. The women of the neighborhood recognize the weight of this act, declaring, "There is a son born to Naomi" (Ruth 4:17). The bitter widow who once demanded to be called "Mara" is now pictured with a child laid upon her chest—a vivid, physical demonstration of comfort, restoration, and hope.

7. Other Relevant Insights

There is a beautiful linguistic connection between shith and the name of Adam's third son, Seth (Sheth). In Genesis 4:25, Eve uses the verbal root of shith when she names her son Seth, stating that God has "appointed" (shith) another seed to replace Abel. In the same way, the child Obed is "laid" (shith) on Naomi's bosom as an appointed seed to replace the sons she lost in Moab. What was cut off in death is replanted and appointed for life through Naomi's act of laying the child on her chest.
