DAVID - ( Dawid , Strong's H1732 )

Summary:

The name David means "beloved" and serves as the ultimate historical and theological climax of the book of Ruth. By tracing the lineage of Ruth and Boaz to King David, the narrative transitions from a local story of family redemption to a national and cosmic story of divine providence, proving that even during the chaotic period of the Judges, God was quietly preparing the dynastic line of Israel's greatest earthly king and, ultimately, the Messiah.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The name Dawid is closely related to the Hebrew root dod, which translates as "beloved," "uncle," or "love." In the ancient Near East, names carried profound prophetic and character-defining weight. This name reflects one who is specially loved by God and chosen to shepherd His people. In the context of Israel's history, David was the youngest son of Jesse of Bethlehem who rose from tending sheep to become the anointed king of Israel, a man after God's own heart. His name represents the historic shift from the tribal confederation of the Judges to the unified, covenantal monarchy of Israel.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The proper masculine noun Dawid means "beloved" or "dear one." The lexical root points to the concept of deep affection, kinship, and intimate relationship. The term designates the historical figure of King David, the successor of Saul, who ruled over Israel for forty years and established Jerusalem as the political and spiritual capital of the nation. It is used exclusively in the Old Testament to refer to this specific king, his descendants (the house of David), or prophetically to the future Messiah who would rule on his eternal throne.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

The name David appears over 1,000 times in the Old Testament, making it one of the most prominent proper names in Scripture.

• Historical Narrative: It is used to recount his life, military exploits, and political reign in 1 and 2 Samuel, 1 Kings, and 1 Chronicles.

• Poetic Literature: It is used in the Psalms, many of which are dedicated to or written by David, capturing his deep emotional and spiritual relationship with Yahweh.

• Prophetic Literature: It is used in the major and minor prophets to foreshadow the coming Davidic King, the Messiah, who will reign in righteousness forever.

• Word Picture: The core word picture associated with David is that of the shepherd-king—one who protects, guides, and feeds the flock of God with integrity of heart and skillful hands.

### 4. Theological Implications

• Covenantal Faithfulness: The name David is inextricably linked to the Davidic Covenant, where God promises an eternal dynasty, throne, and kingdom to David's line. The mention of David in Ruth 4:17 shows that God's covenantal promises are worked out through the ordinary, faithful lives of ordinary people like Ruth and Boaz.

• Divine Providence: The book of Ruth takes place "in the days when the judges ruled," a time of spiritual decay and moral chaos. The sudden introduction of David at the end of the genealogy reveals that God was not silent or absent; He was sovereignly working behind the scenes to bring forth Israel's redeemer-king.

• Grace to the Gentiles: David's great-grandmother was Ruth, a Moabite woman. This highlights the expansive grace of God, incorporating a foreigner into the direct lineage of Israel's royalty and the ultimate Savior of the world, Jesus Christ.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 4:17, the name David serves as the ultimate climax of the entire book. The placement of his name at the very end of the narrative acts as a literary and historical anchor, explaining the divine purpose behind the story of Ruth. The hardships, the famine, the migration to Moab, the deaths of Elimelech and his sons, the loyalty of Ruth, and the redemption by Boaz were all part of the divine tapestry to bring David into the world. Without this verse, the book of Ruth is a beautiful story of family loyalty and local redemption. With this verse, it becomes a crucial link in redemptive history, connecting the patriarchs to the monarchy, and eventually to the New Testament.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• 1 Samuel 16:13 - "Then Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him in the midst of his brothers; and the Spirit of the LORD came upon David from that day forward. So Samuel arose and went to Ramah."

• Psalm 89:3 - "I have made a covenant with My chosen, I have sworn to My servant David:"

• Isaiah 9:7 - "Of the increase of His government and peace There will be no end, Upon the throne of David and over His kingdom, To order it and establish it with judgment and justice From that time forward, even forever. The zeal of the LORD of hosts will perform this."

• Jeremiah 23:5 - "Behold, the days are coming," says the LORD, "That I will raise to David a Branch of righteousness; A King shall reign and prosper, And execute judgment and righteousness in the earth."

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

• The genealogy in Ruth 4:18-22 lists ten generations from Perez to David. This ten-generation genealogy mirrors the genealogies in Genesis, signaling a new epoch in redemptive history. David represents the culmination of this line, showing that God's hand was guiding history through periods of darkness to establish his chosen ruler.

• By ending with David, the book of Ruth provides a powerful apologetic for the legitimacy of David's kingship. Even though he had Moabite ancestry through Ruth, this lineage was orchestrated by God Himself through the honorable obedience of Ruth and the righteous redemption of Boaz, silencing critics who might question David's pedigree.
