NEIGHBOR - ( shaken , Strong's H7934 )

Summary:

The word shaken refers to a neighbor, inhabitant, or someone who dwells nearby. Derived from a root meaning to settle down or abide, it highlights geographical and relational proximity within a community. In Ruth 4:17, it refers to the local women of Bethlehem who surrounded Naomi, celebrated the birth of her grandson, and served as communal witnesses to God's covenant faithfulness in restoring a deceased man's line.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The noun shaken comes from the verbal root shakan, which means to settle down, abide, or dwell. A shaken is literally a "dweller-near." In the ancient Near East, life was highly communal. Families lived in close proximity, often sharing courtyards, agricultural tools, and daily chores. A neighbor was not merely someone who lived in an adjacent dwelling, but a vital partner in daily survival, mutual defense, and covenant accountability. Neighbors witnessed each other's joys, struggles, and legal transactions.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The primary meaning of shaken is an inhabitant or a neighbor. It refers to individuals or nations residing nearby. In its feminine plural form (shekenot), as used in Ruth 4:17, it specifically denotes the female neighbors of a household. These women shared a close-knit bond, assisting in times of childbirth, mourning, and domestic labor. The term emphasizes physical proximity that naturally translates into social and emotional closeness.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Geographical Proximity: The term is frequently used to describe people living in adjacent houses or lands, emphasizing the physical reality of shared space.

• Covenant Community: Because God commanded His people to love their neighbors, the shaken is often the object of ethical duty and covenant responsibility.

• National Neighbors: On a macro level, the word describes neighboring nations bordering Israel, who could either be sources of trouble or partners in peace.

4. Theological Implications

The root shakan is also the source of the word Tabernacle (mishkan) and the concept of God's dwelling (Shekinah) among His people. Because Yahweh condescended to dwell (shakan) in the midst of Israel, the relationships between human "dwellers-near" (shaken) took on sacred significance. Neighborly relations were governed by covenant law. The presence of neighbors celebrating a birth, as seen in the story of Ruth, reflects the communal outworking of God's blessing. It shows that redemption is never a private affair; it restores and enlivens the entire community.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 4:17, the "neighbor women" (shekenot) play a highly unusual and significant role: they name the child born to Ruth and Boaz. In ancient Israel, parents—specifically the mother or father—almost always named the child. Here, the neighboring women step in to name the boy Obed, meaning "servant" or "one who worships."

This action highlights several key realities:

• The Reversal of Naomi's Grief: In Ruth 1:20-21, Naomi returned to Bethlehem empty and bitter, calling out to the women of the city. In Ruth 4, those same neighbors gather around her to declare that "there is a son born to Naomi." The empty woman is now full, and her community officially recognizes this restoration.

• Communal Validation: The neighbors serve as a living chorus of praise, confirming that the child is the redeemer of Naomi's life and the nourisher of her old age.

• God's Providence: The neighbors' active participation shows that the birth of Obed is a community-wide victory, keeping Elimelech's name alive in Bethlehem.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The naming of Obed by the neighbor women connects the immediate story of Ruth to the grander narrative of Israel's history. By naming the child, the neighbors prophetically frame his destiny. Obed would become the father of Jesse, the father of David. The simple, local actions of ordinary Bethlehem neighbors were woven directly into the royal lineage of King David and, ultimately, Jesus Christ.
