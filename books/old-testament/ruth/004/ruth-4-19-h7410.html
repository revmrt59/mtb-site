RAM - ( Ram , Strong's H7410 )

Summary:

The name Ram means "high" or "exalted." In the book of Ruth, Ram serves as a critical link in the messianic genealogy, connecting Judah's grandson Hezron to King David, and ultimately to Jesus Christ. His placement in Ruth 4:19 highlights God's sovereign oversight in preserving the royal line of promise through seasons of national and personal crisis.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The name Ram is a proper noun derived from the Hebrew root verb rum, which means to be high, lifted up, or exalted. In the ancient Near East, names were often prophetic, prayerful, or descriptive of a family's hope or circumstances. While primarily used as a personal name in the genealogies of the tribe of Judah, the underlying word picture evokes the idea of elevation, stature, or dignity. As an ancestor of David, Ram occupies a prominent place in the lineage of Israel's greatest earthly king, foreshadowing the ultimate "exalted" King, the Messiah.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The name Ram translates directly as "high," "exalted," or "highly praised." It is a shortened form of compound names like Amram ("an exalted people") or Jehoram ("Yahweh is exalted"). Lexically, it functions strictly as a proper masculine noun in the Old Testament, representing a specific individual in the family tree of Judah who lived during the period of Israel's sojourn in Egypt and the subsequent Exodus.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

In the Old Testament, the name Ram is used almost exclusively in genealogical lists. The word picture associated with its root, rum, is frequently used in scripture to describe God's supreme position, the lifting up of a banner, or the physical elevation of a city. When applied to a person as a name, it conveys a prayer or declaration of dignity, honor, or divine elevation. In the context of the Davidic line, it paints a picture of a family being raised from obscurity in Bethlehem to the highest position of royalty in Israel.

4. Theological Implications

The inclusion of Ram in the genealogy of Ruth 4:19 has profound messianic and theological significance. First, it demonstrates God's faithfulness to His covenant promises. God promised Judah that the scepter would not depart from his line. Ram represents a crucial, unbroken link during the dark centuries in Egypt and the wilderness, proving that God was quietly working out His plan of redemption behind the scenes. Second, this line leads directly to Jesus Christ, who is the ultimate fulfillment of the name "Exalted." He is the one whom God has highly exalted and bestowed with the name above every name.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 4:19, the name Ram occurs within the closing genealogy of the book (Ruth 4:18-22). This genealogy transitions the narrative from a local story of a grieving widow to a cosmic story of national redemption. The name sits between Hezron, associated with the initial descent into Egypt, and Amminadab, associated with the Exodus generation. The nuance here is one of continuity and stability. Amidst the chaos of the period of the Judges, this genealogy acts as a theological anchor, proving that God's sovereign hand was never lifted from the line of promise.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Ruth 4:19: "Hezron begot Ram, and Ram begot Amminadab;"

• 1 Chronicles 2:9: "The sons of Hezron who were born to him were Jerahmeel, Ram, and Chelubai."

• 1 Chronicles 2:10: "Ram begot Amminadab, and Amminadab begot Nahshon, leader of the children of Judah;"

7. Other Relevant Insights

In the New Testament genealogies, Ram is referred to as Aram or Arni in Greek. This minor spelling variation is due to the Greek transliteration of the Hebrew name. Despite the silent centuries between the Old and New Testaments, the preservation of Ram's name in both testaments underscores the meticulous care God took to document the legal and biological lineage of the Messiah, ensuring that Jesus' credentials as the Son of David were beyond dispute.
