ELDERS - ( zaqen , Strong's H2205 )

Summary:

In the Old Testament, the elders represent the older, wiser men of a community who served as local leaders, judges, and guardians of justice and covenant faithfulness. In Ruth 4:2, Boaz gathers ten elders of Bethlehem to the city gate to witness and legally ratify his redemption of Naomi's land and his marriage to Ruth. These elders do not merely represent political authority, but communal wisdom, legal integrity, and the preservation of family lines within Israel.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• The Hebrew noun zaqen is closely related to the noun zaqan, which means "beard" or "chin."

• In the ancient Near East, growing a beard was a symbol of maturity, dignity, and adulthood. An elder was literally a "bearded one," indicating someone who had reached an age of wisdom, life experience, and social responsibility.

• Age was highly venerated in Israelite society. The physical sign of aging was associated with the accumulation of wisdom, making the elders the natural leaders, counselors, and judges of the community.

• The gate of the city was the ancient civic center, the place of legal transactions, business, and local governance. It was here that the elders sat to hear cases, resolve disputes, and witness agreements.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The word zaqen refers to an old man, an elder, or a representative leader of a tribe or city.

• When used of a person's age, it denotes being old or advanced in years.

• When used as a title or collective noun, it denotes a class of leaders who possessed authority within a clan, tribe, or city.

• The semantic range includes:

• An elderly person (chronological age).

• A local municipal authority or representative of a town.

• National leaders who represented the entire congregation of Israel.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The term describes the primary governing body of Israel before and during the monarchy. Moses gathered the elders of Israel to announce God's deliverance from Egypt.

• Elders served as representatives of the people in covenant ceremonies, during times of war, and in local judicial decisions.

• The word picture is one of stability and preservation. The elders were the living memory of the community, carrying forward the laws of God, family genealogies, and historical covenants.

• In judicial contexts, they functioned as an impartial council, ensuring that transactions were done openly, justly, and in accordance with the law.

4. Theological Implications

• The institution of elders reflects God's design for communal accountability and leadership. Governance was not meant to be autocratic but distributed among wise, experienced men who feared God.

• The elders protected the weak, the widow, and the fatherless by ensuring that the covenant laws—such as the levirate marriage and land redemption—were strictly and fairly enforced.

• They represented the covenant continuity of Israel. By witnessing Boaz's redemption of Ruth, the elders certified that Boaz's actions were honorable, lawful, and pleasing to Yahweh, thereby securing the line of David and ultimately the Messiah.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 4:2, Boaz gathers "ten men of the elders of the city."

• The number ten is significant. In Jewish legal practice, ten men formed a quorum necessary to establish a public assembly for official legal transactions, marriages, and blessings.

• By convening this formal council at the town gate, Boaz ensures that his redemption of Naomi’s estate and marriage to Ruth the Moabitess is completely transparent, legally binding, and socially validated.

• This public endorsement protects Ruth from any future challenges to her status and guarantees the legitimacy of the heir born to her. The elders' subsequent blessings in Ruth 4:11-12 show their joy and theological understanding of God's grace at work.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Exodus 3:16 - "Go and gather the elders of Israel together, and say to them, 'The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, of Isaac, and of Jacob, appeared to me, saying, "I have surely visited you and seen what is done to you in Egypt..."'"

• Deuteronomy 19:12 - "then the elders of his city shall send and bring him from there, and deliver him into the hand of the avenger of blood, that he may die."

• Joshua 24:31 - "Israel served the LORD all the days of Joshua, and all the days of the elders who outlived Joshua, who had known all the works of the LORD which He had done for Israel."

• Proverbs 31:23 - "Her husband is known in the gates, When he sits among the elders of the land."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The concept of the zaqen laid the foundation for the New Testament office of the elder (presbyteros).

• Just as the Old Testament elders shepherd-guided the community, protected the covenant, and made legal and spiritual decisions at the city gates, New Testament elders are called to shepherd the flock, guard sound doctrine, and lead the local church with wisdom and maturity.

• The transition from local municipal leaders to spiritual overseers demonstrates a consistent biblical pattern of plural, qualified leadership.
