NAHSHON - ( Nachshon , Strong's H5177 )

Summary:

Nahshon, meaning "enchanter" or "serpent-like," was a prominent prince of the tribe of Judah during the wilderness wanderings. He is a pivotal link in the Davidic and Messianic genealogy, representing the faithfulness of God's covenant line through transition and trial. As the leader who made the first offering for the dedication of the altar and led the vanguard of Israel's march, Nahshon embodies courageous leadership, dedication to God's house, and the preservation of the royal seed of Judah that eventually leads to King David and Jesus Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• Nahshon was the son of Amminadab and a prominent leader of the tribe of Judah during the Exodus from Egypt.

• His sister, Elisheba, married Aaron the high priest, which closely linked the royal tribe of Judah with the priestly tribe of Levi early in Israel's history.

• The name is derived from the Hebrew root nachash, which is associated with whispering, hissing, practicing divination, or a serpent. The suffix -on functions as a diminutive or a noun-forming ending, making the literal rendering "little serpent" or "enchanter."

• During the wilderness journey, Nahshon was appointed as the prince or chief leader of the descendants of Judah. He commanded the tribal host, which was the largest of all the tribes of Israel.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The proper noun Nachshon designates a specific historical figure: the prince of Judah during the wilderness generation.

• Lexically, the name derives from nachash, meaning to hiss, whisper, practice divination, or observe signs.

• In the ancient Near East, the serpent was often associated with wisdom, life, or protective power, which may explain the name's origin during the period of Israel's sojourn in Egypt, prior to any negative association with divination.

• In biblical genealogies, the name functions exclusively as a proper noun tracing the line of Judah through the wilderness era.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Nahshon is consistently presented as a pioneer of faith and order. When the Tabernacle was completed, he was the first of the twelve tribal leaders to bring his dedication offering to the altar.

• When the camp of Israel set out on its march through the wilderness, the standard of the camp of the sons of Judah went first, led by Nahshon.

• In Jewish historical tradition, Nahshon is celebrated as the man of immense faith who first stepped into the Red Sea before it parted, showing immediate obedience to God's command while others hesitated.

4. Theological Implications

• Covenant Lineage: Nahshon serves as a vital bridge in the preservation of the Messianic seed. He connects the patriarchal era (Jacob, Judah, Perez) with the monarchy (Salmon, Boaz, Obed, Jesse, David).

• The Scepter of Judah: As the leader of Judah, Nahshon's preeminence in the wilderness marches and offerings underscores the fulfillment of Jacob's prophecy that the scepter would belong to Judah.

• Priestly and Royal Union: The marriage between Nahshon’s sister and Aaron the high priest represents a theological intersection of the royal and priestly lines. This union anticipates the ultimate priest-king, Jesus Christ, who is the Lion of the tribe of Judah and our Great High Priest.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 4:20, Nahshon is positioned within the closing genealogy that legitimizes the line of King David.

• The book of Ruth occurs during the chaotic and dark period of the Judges, characterized by spiritual and moral decay. The insertion of this genealogy at the end of the narrative demonstrates that God's hand was silently, sovereignly directing history behind the scenes.

• By naming Nahshon, the author reminds the audience that Boaz did not come from an obscure lineage, but from the premier noble family of Judah. This elevates the union of Boaz and Ruth from a local family redemption story to a covenantal milestone of national and global significance.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Ruth 4:20: "Amminadab begot Nahshon, and Nahshon begot Salmon;"

• Exodus 6:23: "Aaron took to himself Elisheba, daughter of Amminadab, sister of Nahshon, as wife; and she bore him Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar."

• Numbers 1:7: "from Judah, Nahshon the son of Amminadab;"

• Numbers 7:12: "And the one who offered his offering on the first day was Nahshon the son of Amminadab, from the tribe of Judah."

• 1 Chronicles 2:10: "Ram begot Amminadab, and Amminadab begot Nahshon, leader of the children of Judah;"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• In the New Testament, Nahshon appears in the genealogies of Jesus in both Matthew 1:4 and Luke 3:32 (transliterated in Greek as Naassōn).

• Nahshon’s immediate descendant, Salmon, married Rahab of Jericho. This shows that the elite leadership line of Judah was the very line that welcomed a Gentile convert into the lineage of the Messiah, mirroring the theme of the book of Ruth.
