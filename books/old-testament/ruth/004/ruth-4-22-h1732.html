DAVID - ( Dawid , Strong's H1732 )

Summary:

The name David means "beloved," derived from the Hebrew root word for love or a beloved one. In the context of Ruth 4:22, this name serves as the triumphant climax of the entire book, transitioning Israel's history from the chaotic, dark era of the Judges to the covenantal hope of the Davidic monarchy. By ending the narrative with David, the text demonstrates God's sovereign, behind-the-scenes providence in weaving ordinary acts of faithfulness into the grand tapestry of redemptive history, ultimately pointing forward to the ultimate "Son of David," Jesus Christ.

Full Study:

### 1. Word Picture / Background

The Hebrew name Dawid is closely connected to the vocabulary of deep affection. It is etymologically linked to the noun dod, which refers to a "beloved" or "one who is loved," a term used frequently in the Song of Solomon to describe a romantic partner, and elsewhere to refer to a close family relation like an uncle.

In the ancient Near East, names were not merely labels but were prophetic, descriptive, and deeply theological. For the youngest son of Jesse to be named "David" was to mark him out as "beloved"—not only by his family, but uniquely by God. The name is virtually unique to this historical figure in the biblical record, highlighting his distinct, chosen status in the history of Israel.

### 2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The proper noun dawid refers specifically to David, the son of Jesse, who became the second king of Israel.

• Lexically, the name designates "beloved one."

• It represents the covenantal head of the Judean dynasty.

• In later prophetic and poetic literature, the name extends beyond the historical king to represent the royal lineage ("house of David") and the ideal, future Messianic Ruler who would reign in David’s character and authority.

### 3. Usage and Word Pictures

The name David appears over one thousand times in the Old Testament, cementing his role as the human standard for Israel's kingship.

• The Shepherd-King: The primary biblical word picture associated with David is the transition from a literal shepherd of sheep in Bethlehem to a spiritual shepherd of Yahweh’s flock, Israel.

• The Covenantal Anchor: The name represents the recipient of the unconditional covenant in 2 Samuel 7, where God promises that David’s throne will be established forever.

• The Man After God's Heart: David’s life serves as a picture of sincere, repentant faith. Unlike Saul, who sought his own honor, David's name is synonymous with a passion for God's presence, worship, and law.

### 4. Theological Implications

The presence of the name David in the final verse of Ruth is highly theological.

• Providence in the Ordinary: The Book of Ruth proves that the birth of Israel's greatest king was not an accident of history but the result of God’s meticulous, quiet providence. God redirected the steps of a grieving widow (Naomi) and a foreign convert (Ruth) to preserve the line of the Messiah.

• Inclusion of the Gentiles: David's great-grandmother was Ruth, a Moabitess. This ancestral link demonstrates that the savior-king of Israel carried the blood of the nations, foreshadowing the global scope of the Messianic kingdom.

• The Covenantal Thread: This name bridges the gap between the Abrahamic promise of kings coming from Abraham’s line (Genesis 17:6) and the realization of that promise in the monarchy, which ultimately culminates in the New Testament with the arrival of Jesus Christ.

### 5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 4:22, "David" is the final word of the book. This placement is intentional and dramatic. The Book of Ruth begins in the bleak, spiritually bankrupt period of the Judges (Ruth 1:1), where "everyone did what was right in his own eyes" (Judges 21:25). By ending with the name "David," the author signals to the reader that God has provided the solution to Israel’s leadership crisis.

The genealogy in Ruth 4:18–22 connects David directly to Perez (the son of Judah), validating his royal lineage according to the prophecy of Genesis 49:10. The name David at the end of this list turns a localized story of family loyalty in Bethlehem into a monumental chapter of cosmic redemption.

### 6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

### 7. Other Relevant Insights

In biblical Hebrew, letters also carried numerical values (a practice known as gematria). The Hebrew spelling of David (dalet-vav-dalet) corresponds to the numbers 4, 6, and 4, which total 14. This numerical significance is directly utilized by Matthew in his Gospel (Matthew 1:17) to structure the genealogy of Jesus Christ into three sets of fourteen generations, highlighting Jesus as the ultimate, threefold fulfillment of the Davidic promise.
