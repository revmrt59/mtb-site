PIECE - ( cheleq , Strong's H2513 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word cheleq represents a portion, share, or tract of land assigned to an individual or family. In the Old Testament, land was not merely a financial asset but a sacred, ancestral inheritance granted by God. In Ruth 4:3, the "piece" of land belonging to Elimelech represents the family's legacy, identity, and survival, which now stands in jeopardy of being permanently lost unless a kinsman-redeemer steps forward to purchase it and preserve the family line.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• In the ancient Near East, particularly within the covenant community of Israel, land was the foundation of family survival and identity.

• Under the leadership of Joshua, the Promised Land was divided among the tribes and families of Israel by lot. Each family received a specific cheleq—a measured-out portion of the land.

• This division was considered a divine appointment. The boundaries of these portions were sacred and could not be permanently moved or altered without violating covenant law.

• The physical boundary of a cheleq was often determined by a measuring line. Thus, the word carries the visual of a clearly demarcated territory, legally secured and assigned to a specific household.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

• The noun cheleq signifies a portion, share, tract of land, territory, or allotment.

• It is derived from the root verb chalaq, which means to divide, distribute, apportion, or share.

• Lexically, it refers to a distinct part of a larger whole that has been officially and legally distributed to an heir.

• In legal and inheritance contexts, it refers specifically to the tract of land that falls to someone as their rightful share of an estate.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• Literal Usage: It frequently refers to a physical plot of ground, a portion of food, or a share of the spoils of war.

• Figurative Usage: It is used to describe one's destiny, fate, or spiritual inheritance.

• Spiritual Allotment: In a beautiful metaphor, God Himself is described as the cheleq of His people. Because the tribe of Levi was not given physical land, their cheleq was the Lord Himself.

• Covenant Security: To have a cheleq in Israel meant having a name, a place, and a future within the covenant community. To lose one's cheleq was to be cut off from the inheritance of God's people.

4. Theological Implications

• God is the Ultimate Owner: Levitical law establishes that the land belongs to Yahweh; the Israelites are merely tenants and stewards. Therefore, a family's cheleq could not be sold permanently.

• The Kinsman-Redeemer (Goel): If poverty forced a family to sell their cheleq, the nearest relative had the right and duty to redeem it, keeping the property within the family line and preserving the name of the deceased.

• Christological Connection: The redemption of Elimelech's cheleq points directly to Jesus Christ, our ultimate Kinsman-Redeemer, who enters into our poverty, redeems our lost inheritance, and secures our eternal portion.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

• In Ruth 4:3, Naomi has returned from Moab destitute. Having lost her husband Elimelech and her two sons, she is forced to sell the cheleq of land that belonged to her husband.

• The use of cheleq here highlights the gravity of the situation. It is not just "soil" being sold; it is Elimelech's covenant portion. If the land is sold to a stranger, Elimelech’s name and legacy in Israel will be erased.

• Boaz uses the legal necessity of redeeming this cheleq to challenge the closer relative. The redeemer must not only buy the piece of land but also take Ruth the Moabitess as his wife to raise up an heir for the deceased, keeping the cheleq in the family name.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 31:14: "Then Rachel and Leah answered and said to him, 'Is there still any portion or inheritance for us in our father’s house?'"

• Deuteronomy 10:9: "Therefore Levi has no portion nor inheritance with his brethren; the LORD is his inheritance, as the LORD your God promised him."

• Joshua 19:9: "The inheritance of the children of Simeon was taken from the share of the children of Judah, for the share of the children of Judah was too much for them."

• Psalm 16:5: "O LORD, You are the portion of my inheritance and my cup; You maintain my lot."

• Lamentations 3:24: "'The LORD is my portion,' says my soul, 'Therefore I hope in Him!'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The preservation of the cheleq was directly tied to the preservation of the Messianic line. By redeeming the piece of land and marrying Ruth, Boaz preserved the family line that would ultimately produce King David and, eventually, Jesus the Messiah.

• In modern pastoral application, this word reminds believers that while worldly wealth and physical properties fade, those who belong to Christ have an imperishable, undefiled inheritance—a permanent cheleq secured in heaven.
