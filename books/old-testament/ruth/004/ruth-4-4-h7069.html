BUY - ( qanah , Strong's 7069 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb qanah represents the act of acquiring, purchasing, or gaining legal possession of something, often through a formal transaction. In the book of Ruth, it serves as a crucial legal term that links the redemption of land to the redemption of people. By purchasing Naomi's land, the redeemer also takes on the responsibility to acquire Ruth as a wife, preserving the family line and inheritance. This demonstrates that true redemption in Scripture is never merely financial or material; it is deeply relational, sacrificial, and covenantal, pointing forward to the ultimate purchase of believers by Jesus Christ.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In ancient Near Eastern culture, land ownership was not merely a matter of personal wealth but a sacred trust. Land belonged ultimately to Yahweh, and families held it in perpetuity as an inheritance. If a family fell into poverty and had to sell their ancestral land, it was the duty of a close relative (the goel, or kinsman-redeemer) to "buy" or redeem it to keep it within the family clan. The word qanah goes beyond a cold commercial exchange. While it is used for buying physical property, it carries a deep undertone of establishing a legal relationship of ownership and care. In the ancient world, to "buy" or "acquire" a person meant entering into a protective covenantal relationship where the purchaser assumed responsibility for the well-being of the acquired.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb qanah possesses a primary semantic range of acquiring, purchasing, buying, or obtaining. In certain poetic and theological contexts, particularly when referring to God as the "possessor" of heaven and earth, the word carries the nuance of creating, originating, or bringing into existence. In legal and commercial contexts, it denotes the transfer of ownership from one party to another in exchange for a price. In the legal setting of Ruth 4, it is used synonymously with securing a title deed and assuming all accompanying ancestral obligations.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Purchased Nation: qanah is used to describe God's redemption of His people from Egypt, picturing Israel as a people He "purchased" or "acquired" for Himself through His mighty acts of deliverance.

• The Acquisition of Wisdom: It is frequently used in Wisdom Literature, especially in Proverbs, where the reader is urged to "get" (acquire) wisdom and understanding as one's most valuable spiritual possession.

• The Jubilee Principle: In the agrarian laws of Leviticus, qanah describes the temporary purchase of land or houses, which must always respect the ultimate ownership of God and the law of the Jubilee, ensuring that nothing is permanently lost to its original owner.

4. Theological Implications

The concept of qanah underlies the biblical theology of redemption. Redemption is not an abstract legal declaration; it requires a costly transaction. A price must be paid to transfer the helpless debtor out of their state of ruin. The link between "buying" the land and "acquiring" the bride in Ruth 4 beautifully prefigures the work of Christ. Christ did not merely pay a price to rescue the creation; He gave Himself to "purchase" His bride, the Church, out of her spiritual poverty. Ownership implies responsibility. When God "buys" a people, He becomes their Lord, Protector, and Provider, demanding their exclusive loyalty while guaranteeing His covenant faithfulness.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 4:4, Boaz uses the word qanah in a strategic legal maneuver. He presents the transaction of Elimelech’s land to the closer relative, who is initially eager to "buy" (qanah) the property because it would increase his own wealth and estate. However, in verse 5, Boaz reveals the hidden condition: the purchase (qanah) of the land from Naomi also requires the acquisition (qanah) of Ruth the Moabitess to raise up the name of the dead. The closer relative backs out because this acquisition would jeopardize his own inheritance. Boaz, representing the selfless kinsman-redeemer, gladly steps in to pay the full price, demonstrating that his motivation is pure covenant love (hesed), not personal gain.

7. Other Relevant Insights

The Hebrew root q-n-h shares phonetic and conceptual similarities with another root meaning "to nest" or "to build a home." While linguistically distinct, ancient literary expressions often play on this connection, as acquiring a wife and property is the fundamental step to "building a household." Furthermore, there is a powerful contrast in Ruth 4 between goel (redeemer/redeem) and qanah (buy/acquire). Redemption focuses on the restoration of family rights and freedom, while purchasing focuses on the payment of the price and the transfer of ownership. Together, they paint a complete picture of salvation: a price paid to restore a lost relationship.
