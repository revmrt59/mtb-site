BUY - ( qanah , Strong's H7069 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb qanah primarily means to acquire, buy, or possess. In the ancient Near East, it signified the legal transfer of property or rights from one party to another, often sealed with a price or a formal transaction. In the book of Ruth, particularly in the climactic legal assembly at the city gate of Bethlehem, qanah is the pivotal legal term used to describe the redemption of Elimelech's land and the subsequent acquisition of Ruth the Moabitess as a wife to perpetuate the deceased husband's lineage. This word beautifully connects physical land redemption with personal, covenantal redemption, ultimately pointing to Jesus Christ, our Kinsman-Redeemer, who bought His bride, the Church, at the ultimate price.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

• qanah has roots in the ancient Near Eastern agrarian and legal systems. It originally carried the physical sense of getting, securing, or establishing ownership over something. It was often associated with purchasing land, livestock, or servants. In a broader ancient context, it could also denote a parent "getting" or "forming" a child, or God "creating" or "possessing" His people. The background is deeply rooted in covenantal law, particularly the laws of land tenure in Israel where land was a divine leasehold that could not be permanently sold away from the family tribe. Thus, qanah in a redemption context involves buying back what was lost to keep it within the family.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb qanah refers to the act of acquiring possession of something, typically through a commercial or legal transaction. Its primary meanings include:

• To buy or purchase (such as land, grain, or slaves).

• To acquire or obtain (such as wisdom, understanding, or a reputation).

• To possess or own as a rightful master or creator.

In legal and redemptive contexts, it denotes the formal acquisition of a title deed or guardianship. In Ruth 4:5, it carries both the sense of purchasing a field (a real estate transaction) and acquiring a wife (a covenantal and legal obligation under the levirate-like marriage custom).

3. Usage and Word Pictures

The Old Testament uses qanah to depict several vivid pictures:

• The Commercial Picture: Buying food or land. It highlights the cost paid to secure ownership.

• The Parental/Creative Picture: God as the Father who bought or acquired Israel out of Egyptian bondage. Here, the word pictures a deep, personal relationship of belonging; Israel belongs to God because He paid the price to acquire them.

• The Wisdom Picture: Getting wisdom as a priceless possession. It portrays the active pursuit and high valuation of spiritual truth.

4. Theological Implications

Theological implications of qanah are profound, especially when connected to redemption:

• The Principle of Ownership: Ownership implies responsibility and care. When God buys His people, they become His treasured possession.

• The Legal and Covenantal Cost: Redemption is never free; it requires a transaction. The redeemer must have the right, the resources, and the willingness to pay the purchase price.

• The Kinsman-Redeemer Type: Boaz acts as a type of Christ. By buying the land and Ruth, Boaz restores what was lost, brings the outsider into the covenant community, and preserves the family name. This directly prefigures Christ, who bought us with His own blood to bring us into the family of God.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 4:5, the word qanah occurs in a tense legal negotiation. Boaz presents the closer relative with the option to redeem Naomi's land. The relative is initially eager to buy the land. However, Boaz introduces a crucial stipulation: "On the day you buy the field... you must also buy it from Ruth the Moabitess... to perpetuate the name of the dead." Here, qanah shifts from a simple, profitable real estate transaction to a costly personal obligation. To acquire the land, the redeemer must also acquire the widowed foreign daughter-in-law to raise up an heir for the deceased. This would split the redeemer's own inheritance, making the purchase too costly for the anonymous relative. For Boaz, however, the buying is an act of sacrificial love, demonstrating that true redemption values people over property.

• Genesis 14:19 - "And he blessed him and said: 'Blessed be Abram of God Most High, Possessor of heaven and earth;'"

• Proverbs 4:7 - "Wisdom is the principal thing; Therefore get wisdom. And in all your getting, get understanding."

• Ruth 4:10 - "Moreover, Ruth the Moabitess, the widow of Mahlon, I have acquired as my wife, to perpetuate the name of the dead through his inheritance, that the name of the dead may not be cut off from among his brethren and from his position at the gate. You are witnesses this day."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• There is a significant play on words in the Book of Ruth between "redeem" (gaal) and "buy" (qanah). While gaal emphasizes the relational duty of a kinsman to deliver his relative from bondage or poverty, qanah emphasizes the actual legal transaction that makes this deliverance a reality.

• The transaction at the gate involved the removal of a sandal as a sign of attestation. This custom confirmed the transfer of the right of qanah from the closer relative to Boaz.

• The Greek translation of the Old Testament (the Septuagint) translates qanah in Ruth 4:5 using the verb ktaomai, which means to acquire, procure, or get for oneself. This same Greek word is used in the New Testament to describe securing or possessing one's own vessel/body (1 Thessalonians 4:4) or obtaining citizenship (Acts 22:28), highlighting a transition from slavery to freedom and ownership.
