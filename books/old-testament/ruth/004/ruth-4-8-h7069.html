BUY - ( qanah , Strong's H7069 )

Summary:

The Hebrew verb qanah means to acquire, purchase, or possess. In the narrative of Ruth 4:8, it is the pivotal legal command that transfers the right of redemption from the unnamed closer relative to Boaz. This word goes beyond a simple commercial transaction; it represents a life-altering covenantal acquisition that rescues a destitute family line, secures an inheritance, and ultimately paves the way for the lineage of King David and the Messiah.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

In the ancient Near Eastern world, land was the lifeblood of a family's legacy, identity, and survival. Under the Mosaic Law, land belonged ultimately to Yahweh, and families held it as a perpetual inheritance. If poverty forced a family to sell their land, it was the duty of the closest relative (the goel or kinsman-redeemer) to buy (qanah) it back to keep it within the clan. The word picture behind qanah is the securing of ownership, the act of laying hold of something and bringing it under one's protective custody and legal possession. It implies a transition of guardianship.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The verb qanah fundamentally signifies acquiring possession of something. This acquisition can occur in several ways:

• Buying or purchasing property, goods, or slaves through a commercial transaction.

• Obtaining or getting non-material realities, such as wisdom, understanding, or knowledge.

• Creating, forming, or originating, as used in contexts where God is described as the Creator or Possessor of heaven and earth, or when Eve declared she had "acquired" a man-child from the Lord.

In legal and redemptive contexts, qanah refers to the lawful acquisition of property or persons through the payment of a redemption price.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

Throughout the Old Testament, qanah is used in rich, descriptive contexts:

• The Creator's Ownership: God is described as the one who qanah (created or possessed) His people. He did not merely make them; He acquired them as His prized possession through the Exodus.

• The Acquisition of Wisdom: In the Wisdom literature, qanah is the active pursuit of truth. Believers are urged to "buy" wisdom, meaning they must spare no effort or cost to make godly insight their own permanent possession.

• The Legal Redemption: In real estate transactions, particularly in Jeremiah and Ruth, qanah involves the formal, public transfer of ownership deeds, witnessed by elders, ensuring that the buyer assumes all legal rights and duties associated with the property.

4. Theological Implications

The theological weight of qanah shines brightest when paired with redemption (gaal). In Ruth 4, buying the land is not merely an investment; it is an act of sacrificial love. Boaz does not buy the land for personal enrichment. By buying (qanah) the estate of Elimelech and taking Ruth as his wife, he ensures that the name of the deceased will not be blotted out.

This directly prefigures the New Testament doctrine of redemption. Believers are not their own; they have been "bought with a price" (1 Corinthians 6:20) by Jesus Christ, our ultimate Kinsman-Redeemer. Christ assumed our debt, bought us out of our spiritual bankruptcy, and acquired us as His cherished bride.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 4:8, the closer relative tells Boaz, "Buy it for yourself." This occurs after Boaz reveals the full terms of the transaction in verse 5: to buy the field from Naomi also requires acquiring (qanah) Ruth the Moabitess to perpetuate the name of the dead. The closer relative realizes this dual obligation will jeopardize his own inheritance. He wants the property, but he cannot afford the personal cost of the person attached to it. When he says, "Buy it for yourself," and draws off his sandal, he formally and publicly yields his right of acquisition. Boaz steps in, willing to bear the full cost of both the land and the bride.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 25:10 - "The field which Abraham purchased from the sons of Heth. There Abraham was buried, and Sarah his wife."

• Exodus 15:16 - "Fear and dread will fall on them; By the greatness of Your arm They will be as still as a stone, Till Your people pass over, O LORD, Till the people pass over whom You have purchased."

• Proverbs 4:7 - "Wisdom is the principal thing; Therefore get wisdom. And in all your getting, get understanding."

• Ruth 4:9 - "And Boaz said to the elders and all the people, 'You are witnesses this day that I have bought all that was Elimelech’s, and all that was Chilion’s and Mahlon’s, from the hand of Naomi.'"

7. Other Relevant Insights

The transaction in Ruth 4:8 is sealed by the removal of the sandal. In ancient Israel, this gesture symbolized the relinquishing of one's right to walk upon and claim the land. By handing the sandal to Boaz, the near kinsman legally declared, "I yield my right to step onto this property and possess it; it is now yours to buy, cultivate, and guard."
