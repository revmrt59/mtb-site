BOUGHT - ( qanah , Strong's H7069 )

Summary:

The Hebrew word qanah means to acquire, purchase, or possess, frequently carrying the deeper theological nuance of a redemption that establishes a new, secure relationship. In Ruth 4:9, Boaz publicly declares that he has "bought" the inheritance of Elimelech's family, securing not just land, but a legacy, a lineage, and a bride. This act of acquisition is a beautiful Old Testament picture of redemption, demonstrating how a redeemer willingly pays the required price to restore what was lost and bring the helpless into a covenant of love and protection.

Full Study:

1. Word Picture / Background

The ancient Near Eastern concept of qanah extends beyond a cold, commercial transaction. It involves the act of bringing something into one's possession, often creating a deep bond of ownership and care between the buyer and the acquired. In agrarian societies, land and family lineage were inextricably linked. To buy or acquire land that belonged to a deceased relative was an act of intense loyalty, preserving the family name and preventing the permanent loss of ancestral inheritance. The word carries the warmth of securing, establishing, and even bringing forth life, reflecting a deep personal investment by the one who makes the purchase.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The Hebrew word qanah is a primary verb meaning to get, acquire, buy, purchase, or possess. Its semantic range includes:

• To acquire or purchase through a financial transaction or legal agreement (such as buying land, cattle, or servants).

• To obtain or possess wisdom, understanding, or knowledge as a prized acquisition.

• To create, form, or bring forth, reflecting ownership by right of creation (as when God is described as the Possessor/Creator of heaven and earth).

• In legal and covenantal contexts, it represents the formal transfer of ownership that establishes legal rights and protective responsibilities over the object or person acquired.

3. Usage and Word Pictures

• The Legal Transaction: qanah is used for purchasing physical property, such as Abraham buying a burial cave or Jeremiah purchasing a field during a time of national crisis. The transaction is open, witnessed, and legally binding.

• The Divine Acquisition: The word is used to describe God "purchasing" His people out of Egypt. Here, the word pictures God's intense, personal claim on Israel, showing that they belong to Him by right of rescue and redemption.

• The Pursuit of Wisdom: In wisdom literature, qanah is used for acquiring wisdom and understanding. It is depicted as a vital purchase worth more than gold, requiring personal sacrifice and valuation.

4. Theological Implications

• The Principle of Redemption: qanah in the book of Ruth is paired with the concept of the goel (kinsman-redeemer). It shows that true redemption is costly. It requires a legal transaction and a public declaration of ownership. Redemption is not a vague sentiment; it is a paid-in-full reality.

• Christological Type: Boaz serves as a type of Jesus Christ. Just as Boaz bought the property and Ruth to restore a dead man's name and rescue Ruth from poverty, Christ bought believers with His own blood to rescue them from spiritual death, bringing them into a glorious covenant relationship.

• Transition from Bondage to Security: When something is qanah (bought) by a benevolent redeemer, its status changes from vulnerable and desolate to secure and treasured. The acquired is no longer unprotected; they are under the wings of the redeemer.

5. Contextual Nuance in the Passage

In Ruth 4:9, the context is the legal gathering at the city gate of Bethlehem. Boaz confronts the nearer kinsman who was willing to buy (qanah) the land but unwilling to acquire (qanah) Ruth the Moabitess because it would jeopardize his own inheritance. Boaz steps forward as the true redeemer. By using qanah, the narrative emphasizes that Boaz's purchase of the land is inseparable from his acquisition of Ruth as his wife. He is not merely purchasing property; he is restoring a broken family, showing that his motivation is covenant faithfulness (chesed) rather than financial gain.

6. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

• Genesis 25:10 - "the field which Abraham purchased from the sons of Heth. There Abraham was buried, and Sarah his wife."

• Exodus 15:16 - "Fear and dread will fall on them; By the greatness of Your arm They will be as still as a stone, Till Your people pass over, O LORD, Till the people pass over whom You have purchased."

• Proverbs 4:7 - "Wisdom is the principal thing; Therefore get wisdom. And in all your getting, get understanding."

• Isaiah 11:11 - "It shall come to pass in that day That the Lord shall set His hand again the second time To recover the remnant of His people who are left..."

• Ruth 4:10 - "Moreover, Ruth the Moabitess, the widow of Mahlon, I have acquired as my wife..."

7. Other Relevant Insights

• The Play on Words: The Hebrew word qanah sounds similar to qana (to be zealous or jealous). While etymologically distinct, this homophonic quality subtly underscores the passionate, zealous love that drives a redeemer to make a costly purchase.

• The Contrast of Motives: The closer relative refused to buy (qanah) because it would ruin his own inheritance. Boaz, however, willingly risks his own estate to buy (qanah) the land and Ruth. Real redemption prioritizes the restoration of others over self-preservation.
