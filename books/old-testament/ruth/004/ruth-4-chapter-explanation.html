Chapter Study

Ruth 4

Verse-by-Verse
Ruth 4:1

NKJV

Ruth 4:1 Now Boaz went up to the gate and sat down there; and behold, the close relative of whom Boaz had spoken came by. So Boaz said, "Come aside, friend, sit down here." So he came aside and sat down.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:1 Boaz went to the town gate and took a seat there. Just then the family redeemer he had mentioned came by, so Boaz called out to him, “Come over here and sit down, friend. I want to talk to you.” So they sat down together. (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz immediately goes to the city gate to settle the matter of redemption, demonstrating his integrity and determination to fulfill his promise.

Verse Explanation

"Now Boaz went up to the gate..."

True to Naomi's expectation (Ruth 3:18), Boaz wastes no time. The city gate served as Bethlehem's public place for legal proceedings, business transactions, and the administration of justice. By going there early, Boaz begins the legal process required to redeem Naomi's property and, if possible, marry Ruth.

"And sat down there..."

Boaz intentionally positions himself where official business is conducted. He is not acting privately but openly and transparently, ensuring that everything is done according to God's law and before witnesses.

"And behold, the close relative of whom Boaz had spoken came by."

The nearer kinsman appears at exactly the right moment. From a human perspective, it seems like a fortunate coincidence. In reality, the reader sees God's quiet providence continuing to direct every detail of the story.

"So Boaz said, 'Come aside, friend, sit down here.' So he came aside and sat down."

Boaz respectfully invites the nearer relative to discuss the matter. He neither pressures nor manipulates him but begins the legal process with fairness and integrity.

Remark(s)

The nearer redeemer appears at precisely the moment Boaz needs him. What seems like an ordinary meeting is another reminder that God is quietly directing events behind the scenes to accomplish His purposes.

Ruth 4:2

NKJV

Ruth 4:2 And he took ten men of the elders of the city, and said, "Sit down here." So they sat down.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:2 Then Boaz called ten leaders from the town and asked them to sit as witnesses. (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz gathers official witnesses to ensure that the matter of redemption is settled openly, legally, and beyond dispute.

Verse Explanation

"And he took ten men of the elders of the city..."

The elders were respected leaders who helped govern the community and served as witnesses in legal matters. By gathering them, Boaz ensures that everything will be conducted properly and publicly.

"And said, 'Sit down here.'"

Boaz invites the elders to observe the proceedings. Their presence gives legal weight to whatever decision is made and protects everyone involved from future disputes.

"So they sat down."

The elders willingly take their places, and the legal hearing is ready to begin. Every step reflects Boaz's commitment to do what is right and according to God's law.

Remark(s)

Boaz does not rely on a private agreement when a public commitment is required. Godly integrity welcomes accountability and seeks to conduct important matters openly and honestly before others.

Ruth 4:3

NKJV

Ruth 4:3 Then he said to the close relative, "Naomi, who has come back from the country of Moab, sold the piece of land which belonged to our brother Elimelech.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:3 And Boaz said to the family redeemer, “You know Naomi, who came back from Moab. She is selling the land that belonged to our relative Elimelech. (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz presents Naomi's situation to the nearer redeemer, giving him the first legal opportunity to redeem the family property.

Verse Explanation

"Then he said to the close relative..."

Boaz addresses the nearer redeemer respectfully, recognizing his legal right to act before anyone else. Although Boaz desires to redeem Ruth himself, he faithfully follows the proper order established by God's law.

"Naomi, who has come back from the country of Moab..."

Boaz begins with Naomi because the issue before them is the redemption of Elimelech's family property. Naomi's return from Moab has created the need for a redeemer to preserve the family's inheritance.

"Sold the piece of land which belonged to our brother Elimelech."

The land had belonged to Elimelech's family and was now available for redemption. Under God's law, a close relative could redeem the property so that it remained within the family. Boaz presents the matter objectively, allowing the nearer redeemer to consider his legal responsibility.

Remark(s)

The first matter Boaz discusses is the land, not Ruth. By presenting the case one step at a time, Boaz ensures that the nearer redeemer makes his decision with a full understanding of both the privilege and the responsibility that redemption requires.

Main Idea

Boaz publicly offers the nearer redeemer the opportunity to redeem the property, making the legal process clear and transparent before the witnesses.

Verse Explanation

"And I thought to inform you..."

Boaz explains that he is bringing the matter before the nearer redeemer because it is the right thing to do. He withholds nothing and ensures that the man has the opportunity to exercise his legal right.

"Saying, 'Buy it back in the presence of the inhabitants and the elders of my people.'"

The transaction is conducted before the elders and other witnesses at the city gate. This public setting guarantees that whatever decision is made will be recognized as legally binding and beyond future dispute.

"If you will redeem it, redeem it..."

Boaz gives the nearer redeemer the first opportunity without pressure or manipulation. Although Boaz himself desires to redeem the property, he willingly places obedience to God's law above his own desires.

"But if you will not redeem it, then tell me, that I may know..."

Boaz simply asks for a clear decision. If the nearer redeemer declines, Boaz is ready to fulfill the responsibility himself.

"For there is no one but you to redeem it, and I am next after you."

Boaz openly acknowledges the legal order. He is fully prepared to accept whatever decision is made, confident that the matter will soon be settled.

"And he said, 'I will redeem it.'"

The nearer redeemer immediately agrees to redeem the property. At this point, the reader may wonder whether Boaz will lose the opportunity to marry Ruth, creating tension that prepares for the next turn in the story.

Ruth 4:5

NKJV

Ruth 4:5 Then Boaz said, "On the day you buy the field from the hand of Naomi, you must also buy it from Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of the dead, to perpetuate the name of the dead through his inheritance."(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:5 Then Boaz told him, “Of course, your purchase of the land from Naomi also requires that you marry Ruth, the Moabite widow. That way she can have children who will carry on her husband’s name and keep the land in the family.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz explains that redeeming the property also requires marrying Ruth in order to preserve the name and inheritance of the deceased.

Verse Explanation

"Then Boaz said..."

Only after the nearer redeemer agrees to purchase the land does Boaz explain the full responsibility that accompanies redemption. The issue is not merely acquiring property but preserving Elimelech's family line according to God's purposes for Israel.

"On the day you buy the field from the hand of Naomi..."

The redemption of the land and the care of the family belong together. Boaz makes it clear that the redeemer cannot separate the benefits from the responsibilities.

"You must also buy it from Ruth the Moabitess..."

Boaz identifies Ruth by both her name and her nationality. Although she is a Moabite by birth, she has become part of God's people through her faith in the LORD, demonstrated in her remarkable loyalty to Naomi. Redeeming the inheritance also means accepting responsibility for Ruth

"The wife of the dead..."

Ruth is identified in relation to her deceased husband because the purpose of redemption is to preserve his family line and inheritance.

"To perpetuate the name of the dead through his inheritance."

The goal is not merely to transfer property but to preserve the family inheritance and the memory of the deceased within Israel. God's laws protected families from permanently losing their inheritance and demonstrated His concern for both people and property.

Remark(s)

Boaz reveals that redemption always carries responsibility as well as privilege. The nearer redeemer was willing to acquire the property, but he had not yet considered the personal cost. Godly obedience embraces both the blessings and the responsibilities that come with His calling.

Ruth 4:6

NKJV

Ruth 4:6 And the close relative said, "I cannot redeem it for myself, lest I ruin my own inheritance. You redeem my right of redemption for yourself, for I cannot redeem it."(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:6 “Then I can’t redeem it,” the family redeemer replied, “because this might endanger my own estate. You redeem the land; I cannot do it.” (NLT)

Main Idea

The nearer redeemer declines to redeem the property because he is unwilling to bear the personal cost, clearing the way for Boaz to fulfill the role of redeemer.

Verse Explanation

"And the close relative said, 'I cannot redeem it for myself...'"

After learning that redemption includes marrying Ruth and preserving the deceased man's family line, the nearer redeemer changes his decision. The responsibility is greater than he was willing to accept.

"Lest I ruin my own inheritance."

The text does not explain exactly how his inheritance might be affected. There are several possibilities:

  1. Financial cost – Redeeming the land and caring for Ruth could reduce his estate or complicate the inheritance for his existing heirs.

  2. Family inheritance – Any son born through Ruth would continue the deceased man's family line rather than his own, affecting how his inheritance would be distributed.

  3. Ruth's Moabite background – The previous verse specifically identifies her as "Ruth the Moabitess." Some believe the nearer redeemer was unwilling to bring a Moabite woman into his family, although the text does not state this explicitly.

Whatever the reason, he concluded that the personal cost was greater than he was willing to bear.

"You redeem my right of redemption for yourself..."

The nearer redeemer voluntarily transfers his legal right to Boaz. He does not oppose Boaz's desire to redeem Ruth but willingly steps aside.

"For I cannot redeem it."

His final decision clears the last legal obstacle. The way is now open for Boaz to become Ruth's redeemer and preserve Elimelech's family.

Remark(s)

The nearer redeemer readily agreed to acquire the land until he learned the full cost of redemption. He changed his mind when Boaz explained that redemption also meant accepting responsibility for Ruth the Moabitess and preserving another man's family line. Whether his concern was Ruth's background, the effect on his inheritance, or something else, he concluded that the cost to his own inheritance was greater than he was willing to bear. Boaz, however, willingly accepted both the responsibility and the sacrifice.

Ruth 4:7

NKJV

Ruth 4:7 Now this was the custom in former times in Israel concerning redeeming and exchanging, to confirm anything: one man took off his sandal and gave it to the other, and this was a confirmation in Israel.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:7 Now in those days it was the custom in Israel for anyone transferring a right of purchase to remove his sandal and hand it to the other party. This publicly validated the transaction. (NLT)

Main Idea

The ancient custom of removing a sandal publicly confirmed the legal transfer of the right of redemption.

Verse Explanation

"Now this was the custom in former times in Israel..."

The Book of Ruth pauses to explain an ancient custom that later readers might not have understood. By the time this book was written, the practice was apparently no longer common, so this explanation helps us understand the legal transaction that follows.

"Concerning redeeming and exchanging..."

The custom applied to legal transactions involving the redemption of property and the transfer of legal rights. It provided an accepted and public way to complete such agreements.

"To confirm anything..."

This ceremony served as legal confirmation that the transaction had been completed. With the elders and other witnesses present, the agreement became publicly recognized and could not easily be disputed.

"One man took off his sandal and gave it to the other..."

Removing and giving a sandal symbolized the transfer of one's legal right to another person. By handing his sandal to Boaz, the nearer redeemer publicly surrendered his right to redeem the property and transferred that right to Boaz.

"And this was a confirmation in Israel."

The matter was now legally settled according to the accepted customs of the day. Everything had been conducted openly, honestly, and before witnesses, leaving no question about the validity of the transaction.

Remark(s)

Scripture does not explain why a sandal was used to confirm the transfer of redemption rights. Since sandals represented a person's ability to walk on and possess the land, many believe giving one's sandal symbolized surrendering the legal right to claim that inheritance. Whatever its original meaning, everyone present understood that giving the sandal publicly transferred the right of redemption.

Ruth 4:9

NKJV

Ruth 4:9 And Boaz said to the elders and all the people, "You are witnesses this day that I have bought all that was Elimelech's, and all that was Chilion's and Mahlon's, from the hand of Naomi.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:9 Then Boaz said to the elders and to the crowd standing around, “You are witnesses that today I have bought from Naomi all the property of Elimelech, Kilion, and Mahlon. (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz publicly declares before the elders and the people that he has redeemed all that belonged to Elimelech's family.

Verse Explanation

"And Boaz said to the elders and all the people..."

With the legal transfer complete, Boaz addresses both the elders and the gathered witnesses. Everything is being done openly and publicly so that there can be no question about the validity of the redemption.

"'You are witnesses this day...'"

Boaz calls the people to serve as official witnesses to the transaction. Their testimony will establish the legality of what has taken place and protect everyone involved from future disputes.

"'That I have bought all that was Elimelech's, and all that was Chilion's and Mahlon's, from the hand of Naomi.'"

Boaz formally redeems the property that belonged to Elimelech and his sons. By doing so, he preserves the family's inheritance within Israel and provides security for Naomi and Ruth. His actions demonstrate both generosity and faithfulness to God's provision for redemption.

Remark(s)

Boaz did not redeem Ruth quietly or reluctantly. He publicly identified himself with Naomi and Ruth before the elders and the people. In the same way, Jesus is not ashamed to identify Himself with those He has redeemed, calling them His brothers and sisters (Hebrews 2:11).

Ruth 4:10

NKJV

Ruth 4:10 Moreover, Ruth the Moabitess, the widow of Mahlon, I have acquired as my wife, to perpetuate the name of the dead through his inheritance, that the name of the dead may not be cut off from among his brethren and from his position at the gate. You are witnesses this day."(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:10 And with the land I have acquired Ruth, the Moabite widow of Mahlon, to be my wife. This way she can have a son to carry on the family name of her dead husband and to inherit the family property here in his hometown. You are all witnesses today.” (NLT)

Main Idea

Boaz publicly declares that he is taking Ruth as his wife in order to preserve the name and inheritance of the deceased within Israel.

Verse Explanation

"Moreover, Ruth the Moabitess..."

Boaz openly identifies Ruth as the woman he is taking as his wife. Once again, Scripture reminds us of her Moabite background, highlighting the remarkable grace of God in bringing a foreign woman into the covenant community and ultimately into the lineage of David.

"The widow of Mahlon..."

Boaz's marriage is not simply a personal decision. It is connected to God's provision for preserving Mahlon's family line and inheritance.

"I have acquired as my wife..."

Boaz publicly commits himself to Ruth before the elders and the people. His redemption is now complete, bringing together both the redemption of the property and the redemption of the family.

"To perpetuate the name of the dead through his inheritance..."

The purpose of the marriage is to preserve the family line and inheritance so that the deceased man's name will continue in Israel. This reflects God's concern that families and their inheritance not disappear from among His people.

"That the name of the dead may not be cut off from among his brethren and from his position at the gate."

Boaz's actions restore what had been in danger of being lost. The family line, inheritance, and standing within the community are all preserved through his faithful obedience.

"You are witnesses this day."

Once again, Boaz emphasizes that everything has been done publicly and lawfully before the community.

Remark(s)

Boaz's public pledge to redeem and receive Ruth as his bride points forward to Jesus Christ, our greater Redeemer. Christ has redeemed His people, claimed them as His own, and now prepares for the day when He will receive His Bride in the marriage supper of the Lamb (Revelation 19:7-9).

Ruth 4:11

NKJV

Ruth 4:11 And all the people who were at the gate, and the elders, said, "We are witnesses. The LORD make the woman who is coming to your house like Rachel and Leah, the two who built the house of Israel; and may you prosper in Ephrathah and be famous in Bethlehem.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:11 Then the elders and all the people standing in the gate replied, “We are witnesses! May the LORD make this woman who is coming into your home like Rachel and Leah, from whom all the nation of Israel descended! May you prosper in Ephrathah and be famous in Bethlehem. (NLT)

Main Idea

The elders and the people joyfully bless Boaz and Ruth, praying that God will make their marriage fruitful, honorable, and influential in Israel.

Verse Explanation

"And all the people who were at the gate, and the elders, said, 'We are witnesses.'"

The witnesses formally affirm the legal transaction and then go beyond their legal role by pronouncing a blessing upon Boaz and Ruth. The community not only recognizes the marriage but celebrates it.

"The LORD make the woman who is coming to your house like Rachel and Leah..."

Rachel and Leah were the mothers of the tribes of Israel. The blessing is a prayer that Ruth, despite being a Moabite by birth, would become a fruitful mother in Israel and that God would use her to build His people.

"The two who built the house of Israel..."

This acknowledges God's work through Rachel and Leah in establishing the nation. The elders pray that God would likewise work through Ruth's family in His continuing purposes.

"And may you prosper in Ephrathah and be famous in Bethlehem."

The blessing is not merely for material prosperity but for a respected name and an honorable legacy within the community. As the reader later discovers, God answers this prayer far beyond what anyone present could have imagined through the birth of David and, ultimately, Jesus Christ.

Remark(s)

The people prayed that Ruth would become like Rachel and Leah, builders of Israel. God answered their prayer beyond anything they could have imagined. Through Ruth came King David and, generations later, Jesus Christ. God's answers often exceed both our prayers and our expectations.

Ruth 4:12

NKJV

Ruth 4:12 May your house be like the house of Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah, because of the offspring which the LORD will give you from this young woman."(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:12 And may the LORD give you descendants by this young woman who will be like those of our ancestor Perez, the son of Tamar and Judah.” (NLT)

Main Idea

The elders bless Boaz's household, praying that God will establish his family through Ruth just as He established the family of Perez.

Verse Explanation

"May your house be like the house of Perez..."

The elders pray that Boaz's family will become great and influential in Israel. Perez became the ancestor of a prominent family in Bethlehem, and Boaz himself was one of his descendants. Their prayer is that God would likewise bless Boaz and Ruth with a lasting family line.

"Whom Tamar bore to Judah..."

This blessing looks back to the account of Tamar in Genesis 38. After Tamar's husband died, Judah was responsible to see that his family fulfilled its obligation to preserve his son's family line. Judah promised Tamar that his younger son, Shelah, would marry her when he was old enough, but he never kept his promise.

Seeing that Judah had failed to keep his promise, Tamar disguised herself as a prostitute and seduced her father-in-law, Judah in order to obtain the offspring that had been wrongly withheld from her. Their incestuous union resulted in the birth of twins, Perez and Zerah. Although the events were marked by sexual sin, deception, and Judah's failure to fulfill his responsibility, God graciously worked even through those deeply broken circumstances and brought blessing through Perez, who became an ancestor of Boaz.

"Because of the offspring which the LORD will give you from this young woman."

The elders recognize that children are a gift from the LORD. They pray that God will bless Boaz and Ruth with descendants who will establish a lasting family line. God answered their prayer beyond anything they could have imagined through the birth of Obed, the grandfather of King David.

Remark(s)

The Book of Ruth begins with famine, death, emptiness, and hopelessness. It ends with a bride, a bridegroom, redemption, new life, and the birth of a son. This is not only Ruth's story—it is ours. Because of sin, humanity's story began with death, separation, and spiritual emptiness. Through Jesus Christ, our Redeemer and Bridegroom, God brings us into His family, gives us new life, and promises the day when we will be united with Him forever.

Ruth 4:13

NKJV

Ruth 4:13 So Boaz took Ruth and she became his wife; and when he went in to her, the LORD gave her conception, and she bore a son.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:13 So Boaz took Ruth into his home, and she became his wife. When he slept with her, the LORD enabled her to become pregnant, and she gave birth to a son. (NLT)

Main Idea

No content added yet.

Verse Explanation

“So Boaz took Ruth and she became his wife...”
The redemption settled at the city gate now becomes personal. Boaz not only redeems the family property but receives Ruth as his wife. The Moabite widow who came to Bethlehem as a foreigner is now fully joined to the family through which God is accomplishing His purposes.

“And when he went in to her, the LORD gave her conception...”
This is one of the clearest statements of God's direct activity in the entire book. Much of Ruth has shown God working quietly through ordinary circumstances, but here Scripture specifically says that the LORD gave her conception. Ruth had previously been married to Mahlon for about ten years without any children (Ruth 1:4–5). The text does not explain why she had been childless, but it deliberately identifies this conception as God's gracious gift.

“And she bore a son.”
The birth of this son changes far more than Ruth and Boaz can presently see. He will bring restoration to Naomi, preserve the family line, and become the father of Jesse and grandfather of David. What appears to be the birth of one child in Bethlehem becomes part of God's much greater redemptive plan.

Remark(s)
For most of Ruth, God's hand is present but largely unseen. Here the writer pulls back the curtain and tells us plainly, “the LORD gave her conception.” The God who had been quietly directing fields, relationships, decisions, and circumstances was also the One working behind the entire story.

There is also a beautiful movement from Ruth 1 to Ruth 4. The book begins with famine, death, widowhood, and childlessness; here it reaches marriage, conception, and new life. God has not merely provided for Ruth and Naomi, He is bringing restoration out of what had seemed hopelessly lost.

Ruth 4:14

NKJV

Ruth 4:14 Then the women said to Naomi, "Blessed be the LORD, who has not left you this day without a close relative; and may his name be famous in Israel!(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:14 Then the women of the town said to Naomi, “Praise the LORD, who has now provided a redeemer for your family! May this child be famous in Israel. (NLT)

Main Idea

The women of Bethlehem praise the LORD for providing Naomi with a redeemer and restoring hope to her family.

Verse Explanation

"Then the women said to Naomi..."

The same community that witnessed Naomi return from Moab in bitterness now witnesses God's complete reversal of her circumstances. Their words focus not on chance or human effort but on the LORD's faithfulness.

"'Blessed be the LORD, who has not left you this day without a close relative...'"

The women recognize that God Himself has provided the redeemer. Although Boaz willingly fulfilled his role, it was the LORD who orchestrated every event leading to this moment. God's providence has supplied exactly what Naomi needed.

The "close relative" here most naturally refers to the newborn son, Obed, who will carry on the family line and preserve the inheritance. Through him, God's work of redemption continues into future generations.

"'And may his name be famous in Israel!'"

The women pray that this child's name will become well known throughout Israel. God answers that prayer in a far greater way than they could have imagined. Obed becomes the grandfather of King David, and through David's family line comes Jesus Christ, whose name is now honored throughout the world.

Remark(s)

Naomi once believed the LORD had gone out against her and had left her empty (Ruth 1:20-21). Now the women praise the LORD because He had not left her without a redeemer. When God seems silent, He is often working in ways we cannot yet see. His purposes are frequently understood best when we look back and see how faithfully He has led us.

The Book of Ruth is one of Scripture's clearest illustrations of Romans 8:28. Naomi could not see what God was doing while she was walking through famine, loss, and bitterness. Looking back, she discovered that God had been working through every circumstance to accomplish a far greater purpose than she could have imagined.

Ruth 4:15

NKJV

Ruth 4:15 And may he be to you a restorer of life and a nourisher of your old age; for your daughter-in-law, who loves you, who is better to you than seven sons, has borne him."(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:15 May he restore your youth and care for you in your old age. For he is the son of your daughter-in-law who loves you and has been better to you than seven sons!” (NLT)

Main Idea

The women declare that Obed will bring renewed life and hope to Naomi, recognizing Ruth's extraordinary love and God's gracious restoration.

Verse Explanation

"'And may he be to you a restorer of life and a nourisher of your old age.'"

The women bless the newborn child, recognizing that through him Naomi's future has been transformed. Obed will preserve the family line, provide security in Naomi's later years, and become the means through which God continues His work of restoration.

"For your daughter-in-law, who loves you..."

The women recognize that this restoration began with Ruth's faithful love. From the day Ruth chose to remain with Naomi, her actions consistently demonstrated sacrificial love, loyalty, and faith in the God of Israel.

"'Who is better to you than seven sons...'"

In Israelite culture, seven sons represented the ideal picture of family blessing and security. To say that Ruth was better than seven sons was an extraordinary expression of honor. Her faithful character and unwavering devotion had proven more valuable than the greatest family blessing Naomi could have expected.

"Has borne him."

The child is God's gift through Ruth, bringing the story full circle. Naomi, who once believed her family line had come to an end, now holds in her arms the child through whom God will continue His redemptive purposes.

Remark(s)

The women do not praise Ruth for what she possessed but for who she was. Her love, faithfulness, and godly character proved more valuable than wealth, status, or even the blessing of seven sons. God measures greatness by character, not by position or possessions.

NKJV

Ruth 4:16 Then Naomi took the child and laid him on her bosom, and became a nurse to him.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:16 Naomi took the baby and cuddled him to her breast. And she cared for him as if he were her own. (NLT)

Main Idea

Naomi receives Obed into her care, vividly demonstrating God's complete restoration of the woman who once believed she had been left empty.

Verse Explanation

"Then Naomi took the child..."

Naomi personally receives Obed into her arms. The woman who returned to Bethlehem grieving the loss of her husband and sons now holds the child through whom her family line will continue.

"And laid him on her bosom..."

This tender expression pictures Naomi embracing the child with deep affection. It is one of the most touching scenes in the book, illustrating the complete reversal of her circumstances.

"And became a nurse to him."

Naomi assumes an important role in caring for and helping to raise Obed. This does not mean she became his mother, but that she lovingly participated in his upbringing as his grandmother. God not only restored Naomi's family but also gave her a meaningful place in its future.

Remark(s)

The woman who once cried, "The LORD has brought me home again empty" (Ruth 1:21), now holds the evidence of God's faithfulness in her arms. God's final answer to Naomi's sorrow was far greater than she could have imagined. What she thought was the end of her story became the beginning of God's greater purpose.

Ruth 4:17

NKJV

Ruth 4:17 Also the neighbor women gave him a name, saying, "There is a son born to Naomi." And they called his name Obed. He is the father of Jesse, the father of David.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:17 The neighbor women said, “Now at last Naomi has a son again!” And they named him Obed. He became the father of Jesse and the grandfather of David. (NLT)

Main Idea

The women of Bethlehem name the child Obed and recognize that through him God has continued Naomi's family line. The book concludes by revealing that this child would become the grandfather of King David.

Verse Explanation

"Also the neighbor women gave him a name..."

The women who had witnessed Naomi's grief now celebrate God's restoration. Their participation in naming the child reflects the community's shared joy over what God has done.

"'There is a son born to Naomi.'"

The child is, of course, Ruth's son, but the women speak this way because Obed restores Naomi's family line and inheritance. The emphasis is on God's restoration of Naomi's household rather than on biological motherhood.

"And they called his name Obed."

The name Obed means "servant" or "one who serves." Although little is recorded about his life, God would use him as an important link in His unfolding plan of redemption.

"He is the father of Jesse, the father of David."

With these words, the Book of Ruth reaches its historical climax. This ordinary family from Bethlehem becomes the family through which Israel's greatest king will come. The reader also recognizes God's greater purpose, for from David's line would eventually come Jesus Christ, the promised Messiah and eternal King.

Remark(s)

The women do not say, “A son has been born to Ruth,” but, “A son has been born to Naomi.” God refuses to let “Mara” be the final chapter. It is a declaration that God has reversed Naomi's bitter circumstances and brought restoration where she had known devastating loss. And “Mara” will not be the final chapter of our lives either. A day is coming when God will wipe away every tear and sorrow will be no more.

Ruth 4:18

NKJV

Ruth 4:18 Now this is the genealogy of Perez: Perez begot Hezron;(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:18 This is the genealogical record of their ancestor Perez: Perez was the father of Hezron. (NLT)

Main Idea

The Book of Ruth concludes by tracing the family line from Perez to David, revealing that God's work in the lives of Boaz and Ruth was part of His much larger plan for Israel.

Verse Explanation

"Now this is the genealogy of Perez..."

The book closes with a genealogy, connecting the events of Ruth to God's unfolding plan throughout Israel's history. What began as the story of one family's suffering is now revealed to be part of the lineage through which God would establish Israel's greatest king.

"Perez begot Hezron..."

Perez was the son of Judah and Tamar (Genesis 38). By beginning with Perez, the genealogy links the story of Ruth with an earlier account in which God brought blessing through deeply broken circumstances. The same God who worked through Judah and Tamar has now worked through Boaz and Ruth to continue His redemptive purposes.

Remark(s)

The genealogy reminds us that God is writing a much larger story than we can usually see. Boaz and Ruth simply lived faithfully where God had placed them, yet their ordinary obedience became part of God's eternal plan to bring David—and ultimately Jesus Christ—into the world.

Ruth 4:19

NKJV

Ruth 4:19 Hezron begot Ram, and Ram begot Amminadab;(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:19 Hezron was the father of Ram.Ram was the father of Amminadab. (NLT)

Main Idea

The genealogy continues from Hezron to Ram and Amminadab, tracing God's faithful preservation of the family line through successive generations.

Verse Explanation

"Hezron begot Ram..."

Hezron was a descendant of Perez and an ancestor in the line that would eventually lead to David. Although Scripture records little about his life, his place in the genealogy reminds us that God faithfully preserved His promises from one generation to the next.

"Ram begot Amminadab."

Amminadab continued the family line that God was quietly preserving through the centuries. Each generation became another link in God's unfolding plan, even though those individuals likely never realized the significance of their place in His redemptive purposes.

Remark(s)

God's work often advances through ordinary people whose names receive little attention in Scripture. Though little is known about Hezron or Amminadab, their faithfulness became part of the family line through which God would ultimately bring Jesus Christ into the world. Faithfulness in one generation can become a blessing to generations yet to come.

Ruth 4:20

NKJV

Ruth 4:20 Amminadab begot Nahshon, and Nahshon begot Salmon;(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:20 Amminadab was the father of Nahshon.Nahshon was the father of Salmon. (NLT)

Main Idea

The genealogy continues through Amminadab, Nahshon, and Salmon, demonstrating God's faithful preservation of the promised family line.

Verse Explanation

"Amminadab begot Nahshon..."

Nahshon was a prominent leader of the tribe of Judah during Israel's wilderness journey after the Exodus (Numbers 1:7; 2:3). He served as chief of Judah and represented the tribe during Israel's early years as a nation.

"Nahshon begot Salmon."

Salmon continued the family line that God was preserving through the tribe of Judah. According to Matthew 1:5, Salmon married Rahab, the woman from Jericho who believed in the God of Israel and was spared when the city fell. Their son was Boaz.

This means Boaz's family history already testified to God's grace. His own mother had once been a Gentile outsider who found refuge among God's people by faith.

Remark(s)

Boaz was the son of Rahab, a former prostitute from Jericho, and Ruth was a Moabite widow who found refuge in the God of Israel. God's grace welcomed both women into His people and, ultimately, into the family line of Jesus Christ. God's redeeming grace is greater than our past and reaches far beyond every human sin.

Ruth 4:21

NKJV

Ruth 4:21 Salmon begot Boaz, and Boaz begot Obed;(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:21 Salmon was the father of Boaz.Boaz was the father of Obed. (NLT)

Main Idea

The genealogy continues through Salmon, Boaz, and Obed, connecting the events of this book directly to God's unfolding plan of redemption.

Verse Explanation

"Salmon begot Boaz..."

Boaz was born into a family that had already experienced God's remarkable grace. His father was Salmon, and according to Matthew 1:5, his mother was Rahab, the woman of Jericho who placed her faith in the God of Israel. The grace Boaz extended to Ruth reflected the grace that had already been shown to his own family.

"Boaz begot Obed..."

The birth of Obed fulfilled the hopes and prayers that had been building throughout the book. Through him, Naomi's family line was preserved, God's promise of redemption was displayed, and the lineage leading to King David continued.

Remark(s)

Boaz's own family line had already been touched by God's remarkable grace through Rahab. Now Boaz becomes a channel of extraordinary grace to Ruth, another foreign woman who has come to trust the God of Israel. Those who have truly experienced God's grace are called to become channels of that grace to others.

Ruth 4:22

NKJV

Ruth 4:22 Obed begot Jesse, and Jesse begot David.(NKJV)

NLT

Ruth 4:22 Obed was the father of Jesse.Jesse was the father of David. (NLT)

Main Idea

The Book of Ruth concludes by identifying Obed as the father of Jesse and Jesse as the father of David, revealing God's ultimate purpose in the story.

Verse Explanation

"Obed begot Jesse..."

Obed became the father of Jesse, continuing the family line that God had faithfully preserved through generations. What seemed like the ordinary birth of a son was actually another step in God's unfolding plan of redemption.

"And Jesse begot David."

The genealogy reaches its climax with David, Israel's greatest king. For the original readers, this explains why the story of Ruth mattered. God used the faithfulness of Boaz and Ruth to establish the royal line through which David would come.

From the perspective of the New Testament, the story reaches even farther. David's greater Son, Jesus Christ, was born from this same family line, fulfilling God's promises to Abraham, Judah, and David.

Remark(s)

Ruth begins in a world marked by the consequences of Adam's sin—death, loss, famine, and hopelessness. It ends by pointing to a coming King. Our Redeemer has come, and one day His kingdom will completely reverse the curse that sin brought into the world. This is the great hope of every believer.
