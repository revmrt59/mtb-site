Ruth Chapter 4

Verse-by-Verse
Section 1

Ruth 4:1–6 - Boaz Redeems at the City Gate

Overview

≡Section Summary

Early the next morning, Boaz goes to the city gate, Bethlehem's legal and civic center, and waits for the nearer redeemer to pass by. He gathers ten elders as official witnesses and presents the opportunity to redeem Naomi's land. Initially the nearer redeemer agrees, but when he learns that redemption also involves marrying Ruth and preserving Elimelech's family line, he declines because it would jeopardize his own inheritance. His refusal legally clears the way for Boaz to redeem Ruth and Naomi's family.

◎Key Observations

  • Boaz acts immediately, fulfilling the promise he made at the threshing floor.• The city gate serves as Bethlehem's courtroom, town hall, and public meeting place where legal matters are settled before witnesses.• Boaz follows God's legal process completely, demonstrating integrity rather than seeking a private arrangement.• The presence of ten elders provides official witnesses and confirms the legality of the proceedings.• The nearer redeemer willingly redeems the land but refuses when he learns redemption includes responsibility toward Ruth and preserving the family line.• The cost of redemption reveals the true heart of each potential redeemer.• The nearer redeemer relinquishes his legal right, clearing the way for Boaz to act.• God's providence works through lawful, ordinary civic proceedings just as surely as through personal acts of kindness.

★Main Idea

True redemption requires both the willingness and the ability to pay the full cost. Boaz's integrity, patience, and obedience to God's order demonstrate that redemption is accomplished righteously, while the nearer redeemer's refusal highlights that only the right redeemer is willing to bear the complete cost.

Section 2

Ruth 4:7–12 - The Redemption Is Confirmed

Overview

≡Section Summary

After the nearer redeemer declines his right, the customary sandal exchange publicly confirms the legal transfer of redemption rights. Boaz declares before the elders and the people that he has redeemed all that belonged to Elimelech, Chilion, and Mahlon, and that he is taking Ruth as his wife to preserve the family name and inheritance. The witnesses affirm the transaction and pronounce blessings upon Boaz and Ruth, praying that Ruth would be like Rachel and Leah, who built the house of Israel, and that Boaz's family would become like the house of Perez through the offspring the Lord would give them.

◎Key Observations

  • The sandal ceremony legally transfers the right of redemption and serves as a public witness to the completed transaction.• Boaz openly declares every aspect of the redemption, demonstrating complete transparency and integrity.• Redemption includes both restoring the inheritance and preserving the family line through marriage to Ruth.• Boaz willingly accepts the full cost and responsibility of redemption that the nearer redeemer refused.• The elders and all the people become official witnesses, affirming both the legality of the redemption and God's blessing upon it.• The blessing invokes Rachel, Leah, and Perez, connecting Ruth's story to God's covenant history and His continuing work among His people.• Ruth, once known primarily as "the Moabitess," is now publicly welcomed into the covenant community and honored before all Bethlehem.• The redemption that began quietly in the fields is now publicly established before the entire community.

★Main Idea

God's redemption is complete, public, and permanent. Boaz willingly bears the full cost to restore both the inheritance and the family, demonstrating that true redemption restores what was lost, secures a future, and brings the redeemed into a place of honor and blessing.

Section 3

Ruth 4:13–17 - Boaz Marries Ruth

Overview

≡Section Summary

Boaz marries Ruth, and the Lord enables her to conceive and bear a son. The women of Bethlehem praise the Lord for providing Naomi with a redeemer and declare that the child will restore and sustain her in her old age. They recognize Ruth's extraordinary love, saying she is better to Naomi than seven sons. Naomi embraces the child, becoming his nurse, and the neighborhood women name him Obed. The chapter closes by revealing that Obed becomes the father of Jesse and the grandfather of David, showing that God's redemption extends far beyond one family into His greater plan for Israel.

◎Key Observations

  • The Lord is specifically credited with giving Ruth conception, emphasizing that the child is God's gift.• The focus shifts from Boaz and Ruth to Naomi's complete restoration.• The women who once questioned, "Is this Naomi?" (1:19), now publicly praise the Lord for what He has done.• Naomi, who said she returned "empty," now holds new life in her arms, demonstrating God's complete reversal of her circumstances.• Ruth is publicly honored as being "better than seven sons," an extraordinary statement in Israel's culture, highlighting her remarkable faithfulness and love.• The child is described as Naomi's restorer and sustainer, emphasizing God's restoration of both family and hope.• Obed's birth connects this personal story of redemption to God's unfolding plan for the nation through David.• God's blessings often exceed the immediate need, accomplishing purposes that extend to future generations.

★Main Idea

God's redemption does more than restore what has been lost—it creates a future filled with hope and purpose. The Lord transforms emptiness into fullness, sorrow into joy, and personal restoration into a vital part of His greater plan of redemption for the world.

Section 4

Ruth 4:18–22 - The Family Line to David

Overview

≡Section Summary

The narrator concludes with a genealogy tracing the family line from Perez to David. Beginning with Perez, born to Judah and Tamar, the genealogy continues through Hezron, Ram, Amminadab, Nahshon, Salmon, Boaz, Obed, Jesse, and finally David. What began as the story of two widows struggling to survive is revealed to be a vital chapter in God's unfolding plan to establish Israel's greatest king. The genealogy reminds the reader that God's providence was never limited to Naomi's restoration or Ruth's marriage—it was preparing the royal line through which the Messiah would ultimately come.

◎Key Observations

  • The genealogy shifts the focus from one family's story to God's redemptive plan for the nation.• Perez connects Ruth's story to the tribe of Judah and God's covenant purposes.• The genealogy spans multiple generations, demonstrating God's faithfulness across centuries.• David's name serves as the climax of the book, revealing the ultimate historical significance of Ruth and Boaz's faithfulness.• Ordinary acts of obedience become part of God's extraordinary plan for history.• Ruth, once a Moabite outsider, becomes an honored ancestor in Israel's royal line.• The genealogy demonstrates that God often works through faithful individuals to accomplish purposes far beyond their own lifetime.• The ending invites the reader to see the entire book through the lens of God's sovereign providence rather than merely human circumstances.

★Main Idea

God's providence reaches far beyond the present generation. Through the faithful obedience of ordinary people, He accomplishes His eternal purposes, weaving individual lives into His greater plan of redemption that ultimately points to the coming Messiah.
