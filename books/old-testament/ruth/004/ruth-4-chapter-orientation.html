Ruth Overview Chapter 4

Chapter 4

Chapter at a Glance

Chapter Theme

Ruth 4 brings God's plan of redemption to its glorious conclusion. Boaz legally redeems Ruth and Naomi's inheritance, restoring what had been lost and demonstrating that God's providence ultimately leads to redemption, restoration, and the fulfillment of His greater purposes.

Purpose & Importance

This chapter completes the redemption story by resolving the legal question introduced in Chapter 3. It reveals the biblical role of the kinsman-redeemer, demonstrates Boaz's integrity and willingness to redeem, restores Naomi's family and inheritance, and traces God's work beyond one family's restoration to the royal line of David and ultimately to Jesus Christ. The chapter shows that God's purposes often extend far beyond what His people can see in the present.

Chapter Flow

Boaz Redeems at the City Gate (4:1–6) → The Redemption Is Confirmed (4:7–12) → Boaz Marries Ruth (4:13–17) → The Family Line to David (4:18–22)

Chapter Summary

Boaz meets the nearer redeemer at the city gate and publicly presents the opportunity to redeem Naomi's land and marry Ruth. When the nearer redeemer declines, Boaz legally redeems both the inheritance and Ruth before the city elders. The people bless the marriage, and the Lord grants Ruth a son named Obed. Naomi's emptiness is transformed into fullness as she receives new hope through her grandson. The book concludes with the genealogy from Perez to David, revealing that God was not only restoring one family but also preparing the royal line through which the Messiah would come.

Study Foundations

Key Truths Taught

  • God's redemption is both righteous and complete.• God restores what sin, loss, and suffering have taken away.• God's plans often reach far beyond our immediate circumstances.• Faithfulness in ordinary life becomes part of God's extraordinary purposes.• God delights in bringing fullness where there was once emptiness.• God's providence accomplishes both personal restoration and His larger plan of redemption.

Pastoral Insights

  • God's delays are never God's denials; He works according to His perfect timing.• Integrity honors God even when no one is watching.• Today's acts of faithfulness may influence generations yet unborn.• God often accomplishes His greatest purposes through ordinary people living ordinary lives in faithful obedience.• The ending of one story often becomes the beginning of a much greater story.• What God redeems, He restores with purpose, not merely with replacement.

Christ in This Chapter

Boaz fully fulfills the role of the kinsman-redeemer by willingly paying the cost to redeem both the inheritance and Ruth. He foreshadows Jesus Christ, who became our Redeemer by taking upon Himself our humanity, paying the full price for our redemption, restoring our lost inheritance, and bringing us into God's family. The genealogy concluding with David reveals that Ruth's story is part of God's greater redemptive plan, ultimately leading to Jesus Christ, the Son of David and the perfect Redeemer. The book begins with famine and emptiness and ends with redemption, fullness, and the promise of the coming King, beautifully illustrating the unfolding story of God's salvation.
