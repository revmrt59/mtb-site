Chapter Overview

At a Glance

Chapter 4
Main Idea:

At the city gate Boaz publicly redeems Elimelech’s land and takes Ruth the Moabitess as his wife, and the LORD gives them a son whose birth restores Naomi’s family and leads, through the closing genealogy, to David.

Setting:

Naomi has said that Boaz will not rest until the matter is settled (3:18). The chapter opens as he goes to the town gate, the place of public and legal business, to meet the nearer relative (3:12–13).

Go Deeper

The Heart of the Chapter

Ruth 4 completes the romance of redemption. Boaz goes to the gate, gathers ten elders as witnesses, and sets the matter before the nearer relative. That man is ready to redeem the land until Boaz names “Ruth the Moabitess” (4:5); then he declines, saying he would ruin his own inheritance. His stated reason concerns his estate, though the timing suggests that prejudice against Ruth also played a part. Boaz willingly takes on what the other man refuses, and before all the people he declares that he has bought Elimelech’s land and acquired Ruth as his wife.

Ruth comes from the country of Moab, and the Moabites had opposed Israel and were barred from the assembly of the LORD (Deuteronomy 23:3–6). Yet she is publicly received into Boaz’s household, a display of God’s redeeming grace, and the witnesses pray that the LORD will make her like Rachel and Leah, the mothers of Israel (4:11). Then “the LORD gave her conception” (4:13), and the women who once asked “Is this Naomi?” (1:19) now bless the LORD for a son who will restore her life. The emptiness of chapter 1 is answered.

The genealogy carries this one household from Perez to David. In the larger story of Scripture, Boaz and Ruth stand in the family of Jesus Christ, the greater Redeemer (Matthew 1:5–6).

Chapter Sections

Ruth 4:1–2 — Boaz Gathers the Close Relative and the Elders at the Gate

Boaz goes up to the city gate, calls aside the close relative he had spoken of, and seats ten of the town’s elders as witnesses. The question of redemption will be settled openly and lawfully.

Ruth 4:3–6 — The Nearer Relative Declines to Redeem

Boaz offers the relative the right to redeem Elimelech’s land, and he agrees, until Boaz explains that the transaction also involves Ruth the Moabitess and the preservation of the dead man’s name. Then the relative declines, saying he would ruin his own inheritance, and passes his right to Boaz.

Ruth 4:7–10 — Boaz Redeems Before Witnesses

The relative confirms the transfer by the old custom of handing over a sandal. Boaz then declares before the elders and all the people that he has bought all that belonged to Elimelech and his sons and has acquired Ruth the Moabitess as his wife, so that the dead man’s name will not be cut off.

Ruth 4:11–12 — The Witnesses Bless Boaz’s House

The people and elders confirm that they are witnesses and pray that the LORD will make Ruth like Rachel and Leah. They ask that Boaz’s house be like the house of Perez through the children the LORD will give him.

Ruth 4:13–17 — The LORD Gives a Son, and Naomi’s Family Is Restored

Boaz marries Ruth, and the LORD gives her a son. The women bless the LORD for not leaving Naomi without a close relative, honor Ruth’s love as better than seven sons, and name the child Obed, the grandfather of David.

Ruth 4:18–22 — The Genealogy from Perez to David

The book closes with the family line from Perez through Boaz and Obed to Jesse and David, placing this household’s story within the history of Israel’s king.

Why This Chapter Matters

Ruth 4 shows that redemption is costly and willing. The nearer relative had the right but would not bear the cost; Boaz had both the right and the willingness, and he acted publicly. That contrast sharpens the picture of Jesus Christ, who redeems His people at far greater cost and receives them as His own.

The chapter also shows how God works through ordinary faithfulness toward purposes far larger than anyone in the story could see. A Moabite widow’s loyalty and a farmer’s integrity become part of the line of David, and through David, of Christ (Matthew 1:5–6). Romans 8:28–29 describes God working all things together for good toward His purpose in Christ, and Ruth 4 shows that purpose at work in one family.

Key Themes to Watch For

Public, lawful redemption. Boaz settles the matter at the gate before elders and witnesses (4:1–2, 9–11).

The willing redeemer. The nearer relative declines to redeem (4:6), but Boaz willingly takes on both the land and Ruth (4:9–10).

Grace to Ruth the Moabitess. Ruth is named as a Moabite at the moment of her redemption (4:5, 10) and blessed beside Rachel and Leah (4:11).

Emptiness filled. The LORD gives a son, and the women bless Him for restoring Naomi’s life (4:13–17), answering her complaint in 1:21.

From one household to David. The genealogy traces the line from Perez to David (4:18–22), and Matthew includes Boaz and Ruth in the family of Jesus Christ (Matthew 1:5–6).

Key Words & Concepts

The gate (4:1). The entrance to the town, where elders sat and public and legal business was settled before witnesses.

Redeem (4:4). To buy back family land or help a relative in need. A close relative had this right and duty under Israel’s law (Leviticus 25:25).

Land and marriage joined (4:5, 10). Israel’s law treats land redemption (Leviticus 25:25) separately from the duty of a surviving brother to marry his deceased brother’s widow when the brothers lived together and the dead man left no son (Deuteronomy 25:5–6). Boaz was not Mahlon’s brother, and the text does not fully explain how law and custom combined in this case. The NKJV says the redeemer must buy the field “from Ruth” (4:5). Many translations instead say he must ‘acquire Ruth,’ meaning that marrying her is part of the purchase; the NLT, for example, says the purchase “also requires that you marry Ruth.” Either way, Boaz’s declaration in 4:9–10 joins the recovery of the land to his marriage to Ruth and the continuation of the dead man’s name.

The sandal (4:7–8). Handing over a sandal confirmed a transfer of rights in earlier times in Israel. Here it is a voluntary exchange, different from the public shaming described in Deuteronomy 25:9–10.

Close relative (4:14). The women praise the LORD for not leaving Naomi without a close relative. Most likely they mean the newborn child, who will carry on her family; some interpreters think they mean Boaz.

Perez (4:12, 18). The son of Judah and Tamar (Genesis 38). At the gate the witnesses pray that Boaz’s household will be like the house of Perez (4:12), and the closing genealogy then traces the family line from Perez through Boaz to David (4:18–22).
