Song Of Solomon Book Outline

Revision 2 • November 3, 2026

Major Movements

I. Called, Favored, and Awakened (Song of Solomon 1:1–4)

“The song of songs” (1:1) opens with the bride’s cry, “Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth,” a longing born of what she has already tasted, for “The king has brought me into his chambers” (1:2–4). The King’s favor comes before her maturity, as it did for Esther, and her desire is the fruit of His welcome rather than its price. She joins those who loved Him first and agrees from her own experience, “Rightly do they love you” (1:4).

II. Dark but Lovely: Off Track, Called Back, and Assured (Song of Solomon 1:5–2:7)

Before the daughters of Jerusalem she confesses, “I am dark, but lovely,” ashamed to be seen and burned out from keeping her brothers’ vineyards, for “my own vineyard I have not kept” (1:5–6). She asks where He feeds His flock, and He brings her back: He speaks her beauty over her, seats her at His table where her spikenard rises, becomes her bundle of myrrh, shades her under the apple tree, and brings her to “the banqueting house,” where “his banner over me was love” (1:7–2:4). As His love is received and returned, she rests in His embrace, and the movement closes with the first charge not to “stir up nor awaken love Until it pleases” (2:5–7).

III. The Call to Follow (Song of Solomon 2:8–3:5)

The Beloved comes “Leaping upon the mountains,” stands near behind “our wall,” declares that “the winter is past,” and calls, “Rise up, my love, my fair one, And come away,” seeking her face and voice “in the secret places of the cliff” (2:8–14). After the first declaration of belonging, “My beloved is mine, and I am his,” she turns Him away “Until the day breaks And the shadows flee away,” not feeling clean enough to follow (2:15–17). Missing Him, she seeks Him by night, finds that the watchmen cannot take His place, holds Him, and brings Him to her mother’s house before the second charge (3:1–5).

IV. The King Revealed and the Garden Offered (Song of Solomon 3:6–5:1)

“Who is this coming out of the wilderness” opens a revelation of the King in His glory, crowned “On the day of his wedding” (3:6–11). He praises her, answers her refusal by going “to the mountain of myrrh,” declares, “there is no spot in you,” and calls again, more specifically, “Come with me,” into the heights of Lebanon and the lions’ dens (4:1–8). His words draw her until she yields, “Awake, O north wind, And come, O south!”, her garden becomes “his garden,” and He comes to receive its fruit (4:9–5:1).

V. Tested After Surrender: Comfort, Absence, Wounding, and Witness (Song of Solomon 5:2–6:3)

He knocks by night, “Open for me, my sister, my love,” but comfort delays her, and when she opens, “my beloved had turned away and was gone” (5:2–6). Struck and wounded by the watchmen yet still lovesick, she answers the daughters of Jerusalem with her fullest praise of Him: “he is altogether lovely” (5:7–16). Her testimony awakens them to seek Him with her, and she declares, “I am my beloved’s, And my beloved is mine” (6:1–3).

VI. Restored Without Reproach: Beautiful in His Eyes, Partner in His Work (Song of Solomon 6:4–8:4)

He returns without reproach, repeats His praise, and names her “the only one,” who “looks forth as the morning” and moves in “the dance of the two camps” (6:4–13). Resting in the third declaration, “I am my beloved’s, And his desire is toward me,” she joins His concern for fruit and invites Him, “Come, my beloved, Let us go forth to the field” (7:1–13). She gives to Him in her mother’s house, and the movement closes with His embrace and the third charge (8:1–4).

VII. Leaning, Sealed, and Longing (Song of Solomon 8:5–14)

“Who is this coming up from the wilderness, Leaning upon her beloved?” presents the bride transformed and in union with Him, brought back to the apple tree where love was awakened (8:5). She asks, “Set me as a seal upon your heart,” for “love is as strong as death,” and the flame of that love cannot be quenched by many waters (8:6–7). Mature love then cares for a little sister, keeps “My own vineyard” before Him while the King receives His full fruit, still lets Him hear her voice, and ends in longing: “Make haste, my beloved” (8:8–14).
