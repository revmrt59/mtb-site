Song Of Solomon 2 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Song of Solomon 2 carries the bride from rest in the Beloved’s love to His call to follow. She is sheltered, filled, and held until the first charge closes the movement at 2:7; then His voice comes over the mountains, He calls her to rise and come away, and she claims Him as hers but sends Him away until the day breaks.

Chapter Sections

1. Song of Solomon 2:1–4 — The Lily, the Apple Tree, and the Banner of Love

She speaks of herself as an ordinary flower, “the rose of Sharon,” and He answers that she is “Like a lily among thorns.” She praises Him as an apple tree whose shade and fruit she enjoys, and tells how He brought her to the banqueting house, where “his banner over me was love.”

2. Song of Solomon 2:5–7 — Lovesick, Embraced, and the First Charge

Overwhelmed by His love, she asks to be sustained, and His left hand is under her head while His right hand embraces her. Then the daughters of Jerusalem are charged not to stir up nor awaken love until it pleases, the first of the Song’s three rests.

3. Song of Solomon 2:8–9 — The Voice of the Beloved at Our Wall

Her Beloved comes leaping on the mountains like a gazelle, free on the heights she has not climbed. Then He comes near and stands behind “our wall,” looking through the windows and gazing through the lattice.

4. Song of Solomon 2:10–14 — The Call to Rise Up and Come Away

He calls her to rise and come with Him, for the winter is past and the land is full of flowers, singing, green figs, and fragrant vines. He seeks the dove hidden “in the secret places of the cliff,” asking to see her face and hear her voice.

5. Song of Solomon 2:15–17 — Belonging Declared, the Beloved Sent Away

A call goes out to catch the little foxes that spoil the vines, for the vines have tender grapes, and she declares the first mark of belonging: “My beloved is mine, and I am his.” Yet she asks Him to turn away like a gazelle “Until the day breaks And the shadows flee away,” because she does not feel clean enough to follow.
