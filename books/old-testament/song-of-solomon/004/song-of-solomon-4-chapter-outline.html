Song Of Solomon 4 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Song of Solomon 4 stands at the center of the movement in which the King is revealed and the garden is offered. The Beloved praises the bride, she resolves to go to the mountain of myrrh, He declares her spotless and calls her to come with Him, and His delight in her love and her enclosed garden draws out her surrender: “Let my beloved come to his garden.”

Chapter Sections

1. Song of Solomon 4:1–5 — He Praises Her Whole Person

He praises her eyes, hair, teeth, lips, temples, neck, and breasts with images drawn from flocks, fruit, and the tower of David. The washed sheep bearing twins and the fawns of a gazelle point to fruitfulness and to the likeness she will come to share with Him.

2. Song of Solomon 4:6–8 — To the Mountain of Myrrh: Spotless and Called to Come

Taking up the daybreak words she once used to send Him away, the bride resolves, “Until the day breaks And the shadows flee away, I will go my way to the mountain of myrrh And to the hill of frankincense,” choosing to share His suffering love and to join Him in intercession. He answers, “there is no spot in you,” and calls her to come with Him from Lebanon, from the lions’ dens and the mountains of the leopards.

3. Song of Solomon 4:9–11 — Her Love Ravishes His Heart

He tells her that one look of her eyes has ravished His heart, and He returns her own first words to her: “How much better than wine is your love.” Her lips, her perfumes, and her garments all carry a sweetness and fragrance He delights in.

4. Song of Solomon 4:12–15 — A Garden Enclosed and a Well of Living Waters

He calls her a garden enclosed, a spring shut up, and a fountain sealed, set apart for Him and rich with pomegranates, spikenard, myrrh, and all the chief spices. The enclosed garden is also “A well of living waters,” with streams flowing from Lebanon.

5. Song of Solomon 4:16 — The Garden Offered

The bride calls the north wind and the south wind to blow on her garden so that its spices may flow out, welcoming hardship and refreshment alike. Within the one verse “my garden” becomes “his garden,” as she invites her Beloved to come and eat its pleasant fruits.
