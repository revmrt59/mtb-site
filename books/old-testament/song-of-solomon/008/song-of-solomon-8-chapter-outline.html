Song Of Solomon 8 Chapter Outline

Chapter Focus

Song of Solomon 8 closes the movement of restoration and brings the Song to its climax and open ending. The bride longs to love the Beloved openly and rests again in His embrace; then she comes up from the wilderness leaning on Him, asks to be set as a seal upon His heart, and turns to care for a little sister, keep her own vineyard, let Him hear her voice, and call Him to make haste.

Chapter Sections

1. Song of Solomon 8:1–4 — Open Love, the Mother’s House, and the Third Charge

She wishes she could kiss Him in the open without being despised, and she longs to bring Him into her mother’s house and give Him spiced wine and the juice of her pomegranate. His embrace returns, and the third charge not to stir up nor awaken love closes the movement that began at 6:4.

2. Song of Solomon 8:5 — Coming Up from the Wilderness, Leaning on Her Beloved

The question “Who is this coming up from the wilderness, Leaning upon her beloved?” presents the bride transformed and in union with Him. Under the apple tree, where love was awakened and new life brought forth, she recognizes the joy her new life brings to the One whose cross won it.

3. Song of Solomon 8:6–7 — Set as a Seal: Love as Strong as Death

She asks, “Set me as a seal upon your heart, As a seal upon your arm,” for love is as strong as death and burns as a most vehement flame. Many waters cannot quench it, and all the wealth of a house cannot buy it.

4. Song of Solomon 8:8–10 — Care for a Little Sister

The speakers consider what they will do for their little sister: if she is a wall, they will build on her; if she is a door, they will enclose her with cedar. The bride says, “I am a wall,” and tells how she became in His eyes “As one who found peace.”

5. Song of Solomon 8:11–12 — Solomon’s Vineyard and My Own Vineyard

Solomon leases his vineyard at Baal Hamon to keepers, each of whom is to bring a thousand silver coins for its fruit. The bride answers, “My own vineyard is before me,” and says that Solomon may have his thousand and those who tend its fruit two hundred.

6. Song of Solomon 8:13–14 — Her Voice for Him and Her Call to Make Haste

The Beloved addresses her in the gardens where the companions listen for her voice and asks, “Let me hear it!” The Song ends with her call, “Make haste, my beloved,” to come like a gazelle on the mountains of spices.
