New Testament History

Acts

Understanding This Portion

Overarching Theme

Through the power of the Holy Spirit, Jesus continues His work through His Church, advancing the gospel from Jerusalem to the ends of the earth and building His Kingdom among all nations.

Overview

The Book of Acts records the birth, growth, and expansion of the early Church after Jesus' resurrection and ascension. It shows how the Holy Spirit empowered ordinary believers to proclaim the gospel, plant churches, make disciples, and endure persecution. Acts is not merely a history of the early Church; it is the continuing account of Christ's work through His people by the power of the Holy Spirit.

Role in the Bible Story

Acts bridges the Gospels and the Epistles. It records the fulfillment of Jesus' promise to send the Holy Spirit, the establishment of the Church, and the spread of the gospel from the Jewish world to the Gentile nations. It explains how the Christian movement grew from a small group of disciples in Jerusalem into a worldwide mission, laying the historical foundation for the New Testament letters.

Historical Setting

Acts covers approximately thirty years, from Jesus' ascension to Paul's imprisonment in Rome (about A.D. 30–62). The events unfold under Roman rule as the gospel spreads throughout Judea, Samaria, Asia Minor, Greece, and ultimately to Rome. The Church experiences rapid growth, intense persecution, doctrinal challenges, missionary expansion, and the increasing inclusion of Gentile believers.

Key Themes

  • The Holy Spirit's power
  • The spread of the gospel
  • The birth and growth of the Church
  • Prayer and dependence on God
  • Missions and disciple-making
  • Persecution and perseverance
  • Unity in the body of Christ
  • God's salvation for all peoples

Reading This Portion of Scripture

Read Acts as both inspired history and a model of how God works through His Church. Distinguish between events that are descriptive and teachings that are prescriptive, while recognizing that Luke intentionally records patterns that reveal God's priorities for His people. Pay particular attention to the central role of the Holy Spirit, prayer, the proclamation of the gospel, and the formation of healthy local churches.

Major Emphases

Acts emphasizes that Jesus continues His ministry through the Holy Spirit working in His Church. The gospel is for every nation, every culture, and every person. God empowers ordinary believers for extraordinary ministry, builds His Church despite opposition, and advances His kingdom through faithful witness, prayer, obedience, and dependence upon the Holy Spirit.

Common Misreadings

Do not read Acts merely as a collection of miraculous events or as a history book disconnected from the Church today. Neither should every narrative automatically be treated as a universal command without considering its context. Acts reveals the continuing mission of Christ through His Church and provides enduring principles for gospel ministry, spiritual dependence, and faithful witness.

Christ in This Portion of Scripture

Although Jesus ascends into heaven in the opening chapter, He remains the central figure throughout Acts. He pours out the Holy Spirit, directs the mission of the Church, saves sinners, calls leaders, performs miracles through His servants, and reigns as the exalted Lord and King. The Book of Acts demonstrates that the ministry of Jesus did not end at His ascension—it continues through His Spirit-empowered people until the gospel reaches the ends of the earth.
