Canonical Context

New Testament Gospels

Matthew–John — Matthew • Mark • Luke • John

Understanding This Portion

Overarching Theme

Jesus Christ, the promised Messiah and Son of God, reveals the Father, proclaims the Kingdom of God, accomplishes our redemption through His death and resurrection, and calls all people to follow Him.

Overview

The Gospels record the life, ministry, death, resurrection, and ascension of Jesus Christ. Although each Gospel presents Jesus from a unique perspective and emphasizes different aspects of His ministry, together they proclaim one unified message: Jesus is the long-awaited Messiah, the Son of God, and the Savior of the world. Everything the Old Testament anticipated finds its fulfillment in Him.

Role in the Bible Story

The Gospels stand at the center of the Bible's story. They reveal the fulfillment of God's promises made throughout the Old Testament, introduce the New Covenant, and establish the foundation for everything that follows in Acts and the Epistles. Through Jesus' life and work, God's plan of redemption reaches its decisive turning point as the promised King inaugurates His kingdom and provides salvation for all who believe.

Historical Setting

The events of the Gospels take place during the first century under Roman rule in Israel. God's people were living with great expectation for the coming Messiah while experiencing political oppression and spiritual decline. Into this setting Jesus comes proclaiming the Kingdom of God, fulfilling Old Testament prophecy, and demonstrating God's power through His teaching, miracles, death, and resurrection.

Key Themes

  • The person and work of Jesus Christ
  • The Kingdom of God
  • Salvation by grace through faith
  • Fulfillment of Old Testament prophecy
  • Discipleship and following Christ
  • The New Covenant
  • The death and resurrection of Jesus
  • Eternal life

Reading This Portion of Scripture

Read each Gospel as a complementary witness to the same historical Jesus. The four writers emphasize different events and themes for different audiences, but they do not contradict one another. Pay attention to Jesus' words, actions, miracles, and interactions with people, allowing each Gospel to contribute its unique perspective while seeing the complete portrait they present together. Read the Gospels with the Old Testament in view, since Jesus continually fulfills its promises, patterns, and prophecies.

Major Emphases

The Gospels emphasize who Jesus is before simply focusing on what He teaches. They reveal Him as the promised Messiah, the Son of God, the perfect Savior, and the King who brings God's kingdom near. They call every person to repentance, faith, discipleship, and a transformed life through a relationship with Christ.

Common Misreadings

Do not read the four Gospels as four different versions of Jesus' life that compete with one another. Neither should they be reduced to collections of moral teachings or miracle stories. Their primary purpose is to reveal the identity of Jesus Christ and call people to believe in Him. Every miracle, parable, and event ultimately points to His person, His mission, and His saving work.

Christ in This Portion of Scripture

The Gospels are the direct revelation of Jesus Christ. Matthew presents Him as the promised King and Messiah. Mark reveals Him as the faithful Servant and Son of God. Luke portrays Him as the perfect Son of Man and Savior for all people. John proclaims Him as the eternal Son of God, through whom all who believe receive eternal life. Together the four Gospels present the fullest picture of Jesus' life, ministry, death, resurrection, and glory, inviting every reader to know Him, trust Him, and follow Him.
