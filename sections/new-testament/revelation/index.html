New Testament Prophecy

Revelation

Understanding This Portion

Overarching Theme

Jesus Christ will return in glory to judge evil, fulfill every promise, establish His eternal kingdom, and dwell forever with His redeemed people.

Overview

Revelation is the climactic conclusion of the Bible. Through visions, symbols, and prophetic imagery, God reveals the ultimate triumph of Jesus Christ over Satan, sin, death, and every earthly kingdom. Though it contains warnings of coming judgment, its primary purpose is to encourage believers to remain faithful by revealing the certainty of Christ's final victory and God's eternal plan.

Role in the Bible Story

Revelation brings God's redemptive story to its glorious completion. The promises begun in Genesis, anticipated throughout the Old Testament, fulfilled in Christ's first coming, and proclaimed by the apostles reach their final fulfillment. God's kingdom is fully established, evil is forever judged, creation is renewed, and God dwells eternally with His people.

Historical Setting

The Book of Revelation was written by the Apostle John while exiled on the island of Patmos during a time of increasing persecution under the Roman Empire, likely near the end of the first century. The churches of Asia Minor faced pressure to compromise, persecution for their faith, and the temptation to lose hope. Revelation assured them—and every generation of believers—that Christ reigns and His victory is certain.

Key Themes

  • The supremacy of Jesus Christ
  • The sovereignty of God
  • The victory of the Lamb
  • Faithful endurance
  • Judgment and justice
  • Spiritual warfare
  • The return of Christ
  • The New Heaven and New Earth

Reading This Portion of Scripture

Read Revelation as prophetic literature rich with symbolism that is deeply rooted in the rest of Scripture, especially the Old Testament. Focus first on what the book clearly reveals about Jesus Christ, God's sovereign rule, and His ultimate victory before attempting to resolve every symbolic detail. Allow Scripture to interpret Scripture, recognizing that Revelation was written to strengthen faith, inspire hope, and encourage perseverance.

Major Emphases

Revelation emphasizes that Jesus Christ is reigning now and will one day return visibly and victoriously. God remains completely sovereign over history, evil will be fully judged, and every promise He has made will be fulfilled. Believers are called to remain faithful, resist compromise, endure suffering with hope, and worship the Lamb who alone is worthy of all honor and glory.

Common Misreadings

Do not read Revelation primarily as a puzzle to decode or a timeline of current events. Neither should its symbols be interpreted apart from the rest of Scripture. The book's central message is not speculation about the future but confidence in the sovereign reign of Jesus Christ and the certainty of His ultimate victory. Its purpose is to strengthen believers to live faithfully until He returns.

Christ in This Portion of Scripture

Like all of Scripture, Revelation centers on Jesus Christ. He is the risen and glorified Son of Man who walks among His churches, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Lamb who was slain, the faithful Witness, the King of kings and Lord of lords, the righteous Judge, and the Alpha and the Omega. Revelation unveils Christ in His glory and power, revealing Him as the victorious King who defeats every enemy, establishes His everlasting kingdom, and dwells forever with His redeemed people in the New Heaven and New Earth.
