Old Testament Law (Torah, Pentateuch)

Genesis–Deuteronomy — Genesis • Exodus • Leviticus • Numbers • Deuteronomy

Understanding This Portion

Overarching Theme

God reveals Himself as the Creator, Redeemer, and Lawgiver, establishing the foundation of His relationship with humanity and preparing the way for His plan of redemption through Jesus Christ.

Overview

The Torah, also called the Law or the Five Books of Moses, forms the foundation of the entire Bible. Here God reveals Himself as Creator, calls a people to Himself, delivers Israel from slavery, gives His Law, establishes worship, and teaches His people how to live in relationship with Him. These books are far more than ancient laws; they reveal God's character, humanity's need, and the beginning of His unfolding plan to redeem the world.

Role in the Bible Story

The Torah lays the foundation for everything that follows in Scripture. It introduces creation, the fall, God's promises to Abraham, the birth of Israel, the Exodus, the giving of the Law, and the establishment of God's dwelling among His people. Every major biblical theme begins here and ultimately finds its fulfillment in Jesus Christ.

Historical Setting

These books span history from the creation of the world to Israel's preparation to enter the Promised Land. They record the lives of the patriarchs, Israel's bondage in Egypt, the Exodus, the wilderness journey, and God's covenant instructions through Moses before Israel entered Canaan.

Key Themes

  • God's holiness and character
  • Creation and humanity
  • Sin and its consequences
  • Redemption and deliverance
  • Faith and obedience
  • Worship and God's presence
  • Sacrifice and atonement
  • God's promises and covenant faithfulness

Reading This Portion of Scripture

Read these books as the foundation upon which the rest of Scripture is built. Pay attention not only to the commands God gives but also to what they reveal about His character and His relationship with His people. The narratives, laws, sacrifices, priesthood, feasts, and tabernacle all contribute to God's unfolding plan of redemption and should ultimately be understood in light of their fulfillment in Christ.

Major Emphases

God reveals His holiness before He gives His commands. He redeems before He instructs. The Torah emphasizes God's desire to dwell among His people, humanity's inability to overcome sin apart from Him, the necessity of sacrifice, and the call to trust and obey Him. Throughout these books God faithfully keeps His promises while preparing the world for the coming Redeemer.

Common Misreadings

Do not read the Torah merely as an ancient legal code or a collection of moral rules. Neither should it be viewed as a failed religious system replaced by grace. The Law reveals God's holy character, exposes humanity's need for salvation, and points forward to Christ. The ceremonial and sacrificial systems were never ends in themselves but shadows anticipating their fulfillment in Him.

Christ in This Portion of Scripture

Like all of Scripture, the Torah ultimately points us to Jesus Christ. Genesis promises the coming Seed who will crush the serpent. The Passover lamb foreshadows the Lamb of God. The sacrifices, priesthood, tabernacle, and feasts all anticipate Christ's perfect work of redemption. Moses points to the greater Prophet, and the covenant established through him prepares the way for the New Covenant fulfilled in Jesus.
