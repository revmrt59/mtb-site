Canonical Context

Old Testament Major Prophets

Isaiah–Daniel — Isaiah • Jeremiah • Lamentations • Ezekiel • Daniel

Understanding This Portion

Overarching Theme

God calls His people to trust Him alone, warning of judgment for persistent rebellion while revealing His sovereign plan to redeem, restore, and ultimately establish His everlasting kingdom through the Messiah.

Overview

The Major Prophets are called "Major" because of the length of their books, not because they are more important than the Minor Prophets. These books contain God's extended messages through His prophets during some of Israel's darkest days. They expose sin, call for repentance, proclaim God's holiness and sovereignty, and offer hope beyond judgment through His promises of restoration and the coming Messiah. Even in the midst of national collapse, God reveals that His purposes cannot be defeated.

Role in the Bible Story

These books stand at the turning point of Israel's history. As God's people reject His ways, the prophets explain why judgment is coming and call the nation back to the Lord. At the same time, they look beyond exile to a future restoration, a New Covenant, the coming Messiah, and God's eternal kingdom. They deepen the expectation that only God can accomplish the salvation His people need.

Historical Setting

These prophets minister before, during, and after the Babylonian exile. They speak during periods of political turmoil, moral decline, idolatry, international conflict, captivity, and restoration. While their messages address immediate historical circumstances, they also reveal God's unfolding plan for the future of Israel and the nations.

Key Themes

  • The holiness of God
  • Repentance and faithfulness
  • God's sovereignty over nations
  • Judgment and restoration
  • The coming Messiah
  • The New Covenant
  • The Kingdom of God
  • Future hope and restoration

Reading This Portion of Scripture

Read these books as both historical prophecy and divine revelation. Many prophecies have an immediate historical fulfillment while also pointing to future events and ultimately to Christ. Pay attention to the historical context, but also recognize the recurring patterns of God's character, His dealings with His people, and His promises that unfold throughout Scripture. Allow Scripture to interpret Scripture when reading prophetic visions and symbolic language.

Major Emphases

God is holy, sovereign, and faithful to His promises. He lovingly confronts sin, disciplines His people for their good, and never abandons His plan of redemption. These books emphasize that God rules over every nation and every generation, keeps His promises despite human failure, and directs history toward the reign of His Messiah and the establishment of His eternal kingdom.

Common Misreadings

Do not read the Major Prophets as collections of obscure predictions or merely as messages of doom. Neither should every prophecy be forced into modern events without regard for its original context. These books primarily reveal God's character, call His people to repentance, and proclaim His sovereign plan to redeem and restore through the coming Messiah.

Christ in This Portion of Scripture

Like all of Scripture, the Major Prophets ultimately point us to Jesus Christ. Isaiah presents the promised Immanuel, the Suffering Servant, and the coming King. Jeremiah foretells the New Covenant written on the heart. Ezekiel promises a new heart, a new spirit, the Good Shepherd, and a restored dwelling place of God among His people. Daniel reveals the Son of Man whose everlasting kingdom will never be destroyed. Together these books provide some of Scripture's clearest and most profound portraits of the person and work of Jesus Christ while revealing God's eternal plan for His kingdom.
