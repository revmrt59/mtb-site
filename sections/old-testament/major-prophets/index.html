Old Testament - Major Prophets

Canonical Context

(Isaiah, Jeremiah, Lamentations, Ezekiel, Daniel)

These books are called “Major” not because they are more important, but because they are longer in length than that of the Minor prophets. All the prophets speak with equal authority from the Lord

The Major Prophets speak when God’s people turn away from the Lord who redeemed them.

These books are not primarily about predicting distant events. They are about God confronting His people when they drift, compromise, and refuse to listen. The prophets rise in moments of spiritual decline. They call the nation back to obedience, justice, and wholehearted worship.

If the Law establishes how God’s people are to live with Him, and the Historical Books show what happened when they obeyed or rebelled, the Prophets speak when that relationship is neglected.

They are the voice of God refusing to let His people remain comfortable in rebellion.

The Holiness of God

The Major Prophets reveal the holiness of God with clarity and force.

  • Isaiah sees the Lord high and lifted up.

  • Jeremiah weeps over a stubborn people.

  • Ezekiel beholds the glory of God departing and later returning.

  • Daniel remains faithful in a foreign land.

God is not casual about sin because He is good and He loves what He has made. Idolatry and injustice damage people and dishonor His name. When His people turn from Him, discipline follows. Not because He rejects His people, but as loving correction meant to bring them back.

Judgment and Mercy

The Prophets announce judgment, but judgment is not their final word. Invasion comes. Cities fall. The temple is destroyed. Exile follows. But even in discipline, God preserves hope.

The Prophets teach us that because God loves us, God confronts sin, keeps His promises, and even when His people fail Him, He does not abandon His purposes.

First the Natural, Then the Spiritual

The Prophets speak into real history and historical crises. We see political instability, foreign invasion, corruption, and exile. But at the heart of it all is rebellion, compromise, idolatry and spiritual decline. The answer is not a new government or national reform. The answer is turning back to God in true repentance.

What the Major Prophets Are Doing in Scripture

These books reveal:

  • The seriousness of turning away from God

  • God is a God of justice and righteousness

  • The danger of empty, exernal religious activity without the heart

  • The holiness of the Lord

  • The promise of a coming Redeemer

  • The hope of renewal

  • The mercy, patience and long-suffering of God

They teach that worship without obedience is not acceptable to God. They show that God cares about how His people treat one another. They remind us that true devotion cannot be separated from righteous living. As Jesus said, if you love me, you will keep my commandments.

The Promise of Renewal

The Prophets do not only warn. They also promise.

  • A new heart.

  • A new spirit.

  • A restored people.

  • A faithful servant.

  • A coming Messiah

  • A kingdom that will not be shaken.

When everything familiar collapses, God is still a God of restoration.

Christ in the Major Prophets

  • Isaiah points to the suffering servant who bears our sin.

  • Jeremiah speaks of a new covenant written on the heart.

  • Ezekiel promises a new spirit and the return of God’s tangile presence.

  • Daniel sees a kingdom that will never be destroyed.

The Prophets prepare the way for Christ.
