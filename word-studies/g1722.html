IN, AMONG, BY, WITH - (en, Strong's G1722)

Summary

The Greek preposition en most often places something inside a setting. That setting can be a town, a crowd, a stretch of time, a condition of heart, or a person. From that basic sense it also names the means by which something is done, which is why the NKJV renders it "in," "among," "at," "on," "by," "with," "while," and more. Context, not the English word, shows which relationship is meant. En matters most when Paul says believers are "in Christ." That phrase describes where a believer now stands before God, and it is the ground of Paul's confident "no condemnation" in Romans 8:1.

Full Study

1. Role & Function

En is one of the most frequent words in the New Testament. As a preposition it connects a noun to the rest of the sentence and tells the reader how the two relate. Its basic picture is location within boundaries: inside a house, among a people, during a season. Greek uses that picture flexibly. A person can be "in" agony, "in" the Spirit, or "in" Christ. In many passages the word marks the tool or agent by which an action happens, and it is then better read as "by" or "with." This instrumental use reflects the way Hebrew uses its own preposition. The English rendering is therefore an interpretation of the relationship, not a fixed equivalent of the word.

2. Primary Ways It Is Used

Location, association, and sphere (in, among, at, on)

Micah's prophecy, as Matthew quotes it, addresses Bethlehem, which is "not the least among the rulers of Judah" (Matthew 2:6). Here en places the town within a group. The same word marks a realm of existence: "There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus" (Romans 8:1). Paul is describing more than physical position. He is describing the sphere in which a person now lives and is judged, which is Christ Himself.

Means or instrument (by, with)

John says, "I indeed baptize you with water… He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire" (Matthew 3:11). Water is the element John uses, and the Spirit is what Jesus will give. The Pharisees accuse Jesus: "He casts out demons by the ruler of the demons" (Matthew 9:34). Here en names the power they claim stands behind His miracles. That charge is the source of Jesus' sharp reply in Matthew 12.

Time and circumstance (in, when, while, during)

Paired with time words or with a verbal construction, en sets the time frame of an event. On the Emmaus road, "it was, while they conversed and reasoned, that Jesus Himself drew near" (Luke 24:15). The risen Lord came to them in the middle of their confused conversation. In Mark 2:19 a related construction underlies "while the bridegroom is with them," which ties the disciples' freedom from fasting to the time of Jesus' presence.

State or condition (in, with)

En can also describe the condition a person is in. In Gethsemane, "being in agony, He prayed more earnestly" (Luke 22:44). Mary "was found with child of the Holy Spirit" (Matthew 1:18). That phrase renders a Greek idiom built on en and means "pregnant."

3. Practical Reading Insight

Paul's phrase "in Christ" is the place where this small word carries the most weight. A reader could take it to mean simply "with Christ's help." Romans 8:1 says more than that. Condemnation is not removed from people who merely draw on Christ's assistance. It is absent for those located in Him. Their standing before God belongs to the One they are in. That is why Paul can say "now."

A second observation is worth keeping. In Luke the same word frames both Christ's agony in Gethsemane and His arrival on the Emmaus road. He entered fully into suffering, and later He entered just as fully into the discouragement of His disciples. En does not create these truths, but it helps readers see where the Lord places Himself.

4. Reading Guidance

Do not build doctrine on English prepositions. Matthew 3:11 reads "with the Holy Spirit," and 1 Corinthians 12:13 reads "by one Spirit," yet both render en. Students still debate whether en in 1 Corinthians 12:13 marks the Spirit as the agent of baptism or as the element into which believers are baptized. The grammar allows either reading. In every passage, let the surrounding context decide whether en means place, means, time, or condition.
