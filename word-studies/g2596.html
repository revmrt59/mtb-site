ACCORDING TO, AGAINST, THROUGHOUT - (katá, Strong's G2596)

Summary

Katá is a Greek preposition that helps the New Testament say two very different things. Most often it names the standard something follows, as when Paul says that "Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures" (1 Corinthians 15:3). It can also mark opposition: "He who is not with Me is against Me" (Matthew 12:30). Which meaning applies depends on the grammatical form of the word that follows it.

Katá also spreads an action across time or place. In those cases English often turns it into words like "daily," "every," "throughout," or "privately." When you know what katá does, you can see the rule that governs a life, the line between those who stand with Christ and those who oppose Him, and the steady, repeated patterns in the early church's life.

Full Study

1. Role & Function

Katá connects an action or state to something that measures it, opposes it, or spreads it out. Greek nouns change their endings to show how they fit into a sentence. These forms are called "cases," and they decide much of what katá means. When the noun after it is in the form grammarians call the accusative, katá usually means "according to," "in keeping with," or "throughout." When the noun is in the genitive form, katá usually means "against," occasionally "down," and in phrases describing an entire region, "throughout." English readers cannot see these endings. That is why one Greek word can appear in our Bibles as "according to," "against," "every," "daily," "by," and many other renderings.

2. Primary Ways It Is Used

Standard or conformity: "according to," "in accordance with," "as"

This is the most frequent and weightiest use. Katá names the rule or pattern an action follows. Paul says believers "do not walk according to the flesh but according to the Spirit" (Romans 8:4). Here katá names what directs a person's walk. In 1 Corinthians 15:3, "Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures," it shows that the cross happened in line with what God had already said. Paul later adds that Christ "rose again the third day according to the Scriptures" (1 Corinthians 15:4, reference only). Titus 3:5 uses the same pattern for salvation itself: "not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to His mercy He saved us."

Opposition: "against"

With the genitive, katá marks the person or thing that hostility is aimed at. Jesus leaves no neutral ground: "He who is not with Me is against Me" (Matthew 12:30). Paul's question in Romans 8:31, "If God is for us, who can be against us?", sets katá beside a different Greek word for "for." The contrast between the two prepositions carries the argument. The same use shows up in courtroom scenes, such as the false testimony sought "against" Jesus (Matthew 26:59; Mark 14:55, reference only).

Distribution and extent: "every," "daily," "throughout," "privately"

Katá can divide time, places, or people into units, or spread an action across an area. Paul and Barnabas appointed elders "in every church" (Acts 14:23, paraphrased reference), and Luke says that "the churches throughout all Judea, Galilee, and Samaria had peace and were edified" (Acts 9:31). Some of these phrases became fixed expressions. The Bereans "searched the Scriptures daily" (Acts 17:11). The English word "daily" there renders a Greek phrase built with katá that means "day by day." Likewise, Jesus led Peter, James, and John "up on a high mountain apart by themselves" (Mark 9:2), using a katá phrase meaning privately or on their own.

Downward movement: "down"

This older spatial sense is rare in the New Testament. When the demons entered the swine, "the herd ran violently down the steep place into the sea" (Mark 5:13).

3. Practical Reading Insight

Romans 8:4–5 shows how much weight katá can carry. "Those who live according to the flesh set their minds on the things of the flesh, but those who live according to the Spirit, the things of the Spirit." Paul is not describing people who sometimes resemble the flesh or the Spirit. Katá names the standard each life follows, and the surrounding verses show that this standard directs the mind and the walk. The question for a reader is not only "What did I do today?" but "What rule am I living by?"

The same word anchors the gospel to God's prior word. Christ's death and resurrection happened "according to the Scriptures," so the cross was no accident of Roman politics. It was the fulfillment of what God had promised. Titus 3:5 points just as firmly to God's mercy, rather than our works, as the measure of how He saved us. Romans 8:31 then completes the picture. Because God is for His people, no one can stand against them with final success.

4. Reading Guidance

Do not assume that "according to" and "against" mean opposite Greek words. They are often the same word, and the grammar around it decides the sense. Also, "according to" in Scripture usually names a governing standard, not a loose resemblance. Finally, many English adverbs such as "daily," "privately," and "individually" hide katá. The English rendering alone cannot tell you where katá stands in the Greek.
