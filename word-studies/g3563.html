MIND, UNDERSTANDING - (noûs, Strong's G3563)

Summary

The Greek word noûs means "mind" or "understanding." It is the part of a person that thinks, judges, weighs, and decides what is true and what matters. In the New Testament the mind is never a neutral calculator. Paul describes the mind of people who turned from God as "debased" and walking "in the futility of their mind," while believers are called to "be transformed by the renewing of your mind." The same word appears when the risen Jesus "opened their understanding, that they might comprehend the Scriptures," and when Paul makes the astonishing claim, "we have the mind of Christ."

This word shows that how we think is a spiritual matter. The mind can be darkened, captive, shaken, or deceived. It can also be opened by Christ, renewed by God, guarded by His peace, and joined with other believers in a shared understanding. God does not bypass the mind when He saves and grows His people. He rescues it and makes it new, so that we can recognize and gladly do His will.

Full Study

1. Word Background & Setting

Noûs was a common Greek word for the mind, intellect, or capacity to understand. Greek philosophers often prized the mind as the highest and most trustworthy part of a person. Scripture treats it differently. The New Testament takes the mind seriously, but it never treats the mind as untouched by sin or able to reach God unaided.

Nearly every use comes from Paul. The exceptions are a single use in Luke and two in Revelation. The word appears in letters dealing with sin and salvation (Romans), church division and worship (1 Corinthians), the new life in Christ (Ephesians), false teaching (Colossians and the Pastoral Letters), and pressure about the last days (2 Thessalonians and Revelation).

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

At its core, noûs is the faculty of thinking and understanding. Context brings out three closely related senses:

1. The capacity to understand or comprehend. When Jesus "opened their understanding" (Luke 24:45), the word refers to the disciples' ability to grasp what they read. In 1 Corinthians 14:14–19 it refers to the conscious, intelligible understanding involved in prayer and speech.

2. A way of thinking, settled judgment, or mindset. Romans 1:28 speaks of "a debased mind." In Romans 14:5 Paul urges each person to be fully convinced in his own mind, meaning settled in his own judgment.

3. The mind of God, His thoughts and counsel. Romans 11:34 asks, "who has known the mind of the LORD?"

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

The word's moral direction depends heavily on what describes it. The minds of the disobedient are described as debased, futile, fleshly, corrupt, and defiled. The believer's mind is described as renewed. The mind is also linked closely with other parts of the inner person. In Titus 1:15 mind and conscience are defiled together. In Ephesians 4:23 Paul speaks of "the spirit of your mind." In 1 Corinthians 14:14–15 he sets the mind beside the human spirit in prayer and song.

Two other familiar uses of the English word "mind" come from a different Greek word: "Let this mind be in you" (Philippians 2:5) and setting the mind on the flesh or the Spirit (Romans 8:5–6). These passages still shed light on the theme. Readers should simply know that they are not uses of noûs.

4. Theological Significance

Scripture refuses to treat human reasoning as morally neutral. Romans 1:28 traces a sobering exchange. Because people "did not like to retain God in their knowledge, God gave them over to a debased mind." Rejecting God was not only an error in thinking. It led to God's judgment on the thinking itself. Paul describes the Gentile world as walking "in the futility of their mind" (Ephesians 4:17), going nowhere however busy its reasoning. He describes the false teacher at Colossae as "vainly puffed up by his fleshly mind" (Colossians 2:18). He speaks of corrupt minds among those who oppose the truth (1 Timothy 6:5; 2 Timothy 3:8). For the unbelieving, "even their mind and conscience are defiled" (Titus 1:15). This means that a corrupted mind cannot simply be educated out of its blindness. God Himself must act on it to open, rescue, and renew it.

That is the hope in this word. The same faculty that sin darkens is the faculty God restores. In Scripture, salvation reaches all the way into how a person thinks.

5. Important Biblical Passages & Distinctions

Romans 7:23, 25: the captive mind. Paul describes a person who sincerely agrees with God's law. He sees "another law in my members, warring against the law of my mind, and bringing me into captivity to the law of sin which is in my members." In verse 25 he sums it up: with the mind he serves God's law, but with the flesh the law of sin. This mind is awake. It sees God's goodness and agrees with it, yet it is still a prisoner. Agreeing with God's commands does not break sin's hold. Paul answers that need in Romans 8:1–4, where God does through His Son and Spirit what the law and the willing mind could not.

Romans 12:2 and Ephesians 4:23: the renewed mind. Paul's call is plain: "do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind." Paul states it as a command, yet he describes a change worked in us. Believers yield to God's renewing work rather than producing it themselves. The world presses the mind into its mold, and renewal reverses that pressure. Its purpose is practical: "that you may prove what is that good and acceptable and perfect will of God." A renewed mind learns to recognize God's will and approve it from the heart. Ephesians 4:23 uses the same language for the believer who has put off the old way of life: "be renewed in the spirit of your mind." The futile mind of Ephesians 4:17 is not left as it was. In Christ it is made new.

Romans 11:34 and 1 Corinthians 2:16: the mind of the Lord and the mind of Christ. Paul quotes Isaiah's question twice. Where English Isaiah 40:13 reads "Who has directed the Spirit of the LORD," Paul's wording asks about "the mind of the LORD." In Romans 11:34 the question ends a hymn of wonder: "For who has known the mind of the LORD? Or who has become His counselor?" The expected answer is no one. God's thoughts are beyond our reach.

In 1 Corinthians 2 Paul asks the same question and then gives an unexpected answer: "But we have the mind of Christ." The context explains how this can be true. "The Spirit searches all things, yes, the deep things of God" (2:10). Believers have received "not the spirit of the world, but the Spirit who is from God" (2:12), while "the natural man does not receive the things of the Spirit of God" (2:14). Believers do not know everything. Through the Spirit, though, the church is given Christ's way of seeing and valuing the cross, wisdom, and the things of God. The unsearchable God has made Himself known.

1 Corinthians 14:14–19: mind and spirit in worship. Paul observes that when he prays in an uninterpreted tongue, his spirit prays but his understanding bears no fruit for others. He does not despise praying in the spirit. He resolves to pray and sing with both the spirit and the understanding. In the gathering, he would rather speak a few words with understanding to teach others than thousands in a tongue (paraphrase of vv. 15, 19). Spiritual gifts in the church are meant to pass through the mind so that others can be built up.

6. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Luke places the word at a turning point in the gospel story. After the resurrection, the disciples had the Scriptures and had walked with Jesus for years, yet they did not understand how His suffering and rising fulfilled what was written (compare Luke 24:25–27, 44). Then "He opened their understanding, that they might comprehend the Scriptures" (Luke 24:45). Knowing the Bible was not enough. The risen Lord had to open their minds before they could see Him in Moses, the Prophets, and the Psalms.

This moment carries forward into the rest of the New Testament. The Christ who opened minds on Easter evening now gives His people His own mind by the Spirit (1 Corinthians 2:16). Romans 1 shows a humanity whose minds fell into futility, and the gospel brings a renewed people whose minds are being restored to know and do God's will. Revelation adds that this renewed understanding will be needed until the end, when God's people must see through powers that disguise themselves as legitimate authority.

7. Spirit & Life

Philippians 4:7 holds a striking paradox. When believers bring their anxieties to God in prayer with thanksgiving, Paul promises that God's peace, which surpasses all understanding, will guard their hearts and minds in Christ Jesus. The mind tends to calculate, rehearse, and try to secure outcomes. God's peace goes beyond what that reasoning can produce. Here noûs is the understanding that peace surpasses; the minds it guards are described by the related word noēmata, meaning thoughts. That same peace keeps the heart and its thoughts in Christ. The believer does not have to think his way out of anxiety. He can bring it to God and find his thoughts kept in Christ.

A renewed mind is also a shared mind. Paul pleads with a divided church to be perfectly joined together in the same mind and judgment (1 Corinthians 1:10). Unity in Christ rests on a common way of seeing things, formed by the cross. Paul allows room for individual conviction on secondary matters (Romans 14:5), but the church's basic understanding must be one.

Finally, a renewed mind stands steady. Paul warned the Thessalonians not to be quickly shaken in mind by reports that the day of Christ had already come (2 Thessalonians 2:2). Panic about the future often spreads through rumor. A mind anchored in what God has actually said is not easily moved.

8. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Revelation 13:18 — Identifying the number of the beast calls for understanding. In Revelation, discernment is part of faithful endurance.

  • Revelation 17:9 — Interpreting the vision of the woman and the beast requires a mind that has wisdom. The mind must see through earthly splendor to the spiritual reality behind it.

  • 1 Timothy 6:5 — Paul describes people of corrupt minds who treat godliness as a means of gain. A corrupted mind can turn even religion into a business.

  • 2 Timothy 3:8 — Those who resist the truth, like Jannes and Jambres resisting Moses, are described as people of corrupt minds. Their opposition grows from how they think.

  • Romans 14:5 — Each person should be fully convinced in his own mind about observing special days. God cares that His people act from settled conviction, not from pressure.
