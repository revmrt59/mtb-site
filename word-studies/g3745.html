AS MANY AS, WHATEVER, AS MUCH AS, AS LONG AS - (hósos, Strong's G3745)

Summary

Hósos is a Greek word of measure. It answers the questions "how many?", "how much?", "how great?", and "how long?" The NKJV renders it in many ways, most often "whatever," "as many as," "all that," "as much as," and "as long as." Whatever the English, the word draws a boundary around a group, an amount, a degree, or a stretch of time, and then says that everything inside that boundary counts. When John writes "as many as received Him," no one who received Christ is left outside the promise. When Paul writes "whatever things are true," the whole category is in view. Reading hósos well means asking what is being measured and how far the measure reaches.

Full Study

1. Role & Function

Hósos is a relative word of quantity. It refers back to, or sets up, a group or amount and defines its full extent. Sometimes it stands alone as "all who" or "all that." Sometimes it pairs with a matching word to build a proportion, so that one thing is as great as another. At other times it joins a preposition to describe duration or degree. In each case it works like a measuring line. It marks out a group, an amount, a degree, or a length of time, and the sentence then says something about everything within that line. Its force is usually inclusive: "as many as" means every one of them, not a random few.

2. Primary Ways It Is Used

All who, all that (inclusive scope) — NKJV: "as many as," "whatever," "all," "whoever"

This is the most frequent use. In John 1:12 it defines who receives the right to become God's children: "But as many as received Him, to them He gave the right to become children of God, to those who believe in His name." The word sets the size of the group exactly as wide as those who receive Christ. In Philippians 4:8 it repeats six times: "whatever things are true, whatever things are noble, whatever things are just..." Each repetition opens a whole category, and Paul wants the believer's mind to range across all of them.

As much as, in the measure that (proportion) — NKJV: "as," "measure," "inasmuch as"

Here hósos ties two amounts together. Hebrews 1:4 says the Son has become "so much better than the angels, as He has by inheritance obtained a more excellent name than they." The greatness of His name sets the measure of His superiority. Revelation 18:7 applies the same logic to judgment: "In the measure that she glorified herself and lived luxuriously, in the same measure give her torment and sorrow." Babylon's self-exaltation becomes the measure of her sorrow.

As long as (duration) — NKJV: "as long as," "while"

Joined to a preposition, the word measures time. Jesus asks, "Can the friends of the bridegroom mourn as long as the bridegroom is with them?" (Matthew 9:15). Fasting does not fit the time of His presence. Peter uses the same phrase of his remaining life: "as long as I am in this tent" (2 Peter 1:13). In both verses the English phrase translates a preposition together with hósos, so no single English word matches it alone.

How much, how great (measuring what happened) — NKJV: "how great," "how many," "what"

Hósos can also introduce a report that measures what God has done. Jesus tells the healed man of the Gadarenes to go home and tell his friends how great the things are that the Lord has done for him (Mark 5:19, paraphrased; compare Luke 8:39). In Acts 9:16 the Lord says He will show Saul how many things he must suffer for His name (paraphrased). The word turns a simple report into a measure of the size of God's mercy, or of the cost of serving Him.

3. Practical Reading Insight

John 1:12 shows how much this word can carry. Verse 11 has just said that "His own did not receive Him," and a reader might expect the door to close there. Instead, hósos opens it: "as many as received Him." The group is defined only by receiving Christ and believing in His name. It is not limited by nation, by background, or by the rejection just described. Every person who receives Him is included, and the right to become a child of God is given "to them." The word does not by itself build a doctrine of salvation, but it shows that John's promise reaches exactly as far as faith reaches.

Proportion matters as well. Hebrews 1:4 and Revelation 18:7 both use hósos to say that outcomes match what is true. The Son's exaltation matches His name, and Babylon's sorrow matches her pride. God's dealings are not arbitrary. He gives and judges according to a true measure.

4. Reading Guidance

Do not read "whatever" or "as many as" as careless or dismissive, as English sometimes uses "whatever." Hósos usually stresses completeness within a defined group. Context also decides whether it measures number, amount, degree, or time. In phrases such as Romans 11:13, "inasmuch as I am an apostle to the Gentiles," readers sometimes ask whether Paul means "to the extent that" or "as long as." The NKJV's reading of degree fits the context well. The word itself allows some flexibility, so let the surrounding argument settle the question.
