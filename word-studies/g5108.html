SUCH, SUCH A ONE, OF THIS KIND - (toioûtos, Strong's G5108)

Summary

Toioûtos points to the kind or character of something rather than simply to the thing itself. Where "this" says which one, toioûtos says what sort. In the New Testament it describes things "of this kind," gathers a list into a wider category ("such things"), and lets a writer speak of a known person without naming him ("such a one"). The NKJV usually renders it "such," though it sometimes appears as "such a man," "such a one," or "such things." Recognizing it helps the reader look back at the description that came before. When Paul warns that "those who practice such things will not inherit the kingdom of God," he means everything that shares the character of the sins he has named, not only the items on his list.

Full Study

1. Role & Function

Toioûtos works by looking back. A writer describes someone or something, and then toioûtos carries that description forward into the next sentence. The word asks the reader to remember what has just been said and apply it. This differs from the ordinary Greek word for "this" (houtos), which identifies a particular person or thing. Toioûtos identifies a type. It can sit beside a noun ("such a High Priest"), or it can stand alone as a noun ("such things," "such a one"). In both cases its meaning depends on the surrounding passage. "Such" means nothing until you know what it is pointing back to.

2. Primary Ways It Is Used

Describing a noun as "of this kind" (NKJV: "such")

Mark 4:33 follows a series of Jesus' parables: "And with many such parables He spoke the word to them." The word ties the many other parables to the ones Mark has just recorded. They were stories of the same kind, told the same way.

Hebrews 8:1 shows the word gathering up a whole argument: "We have such a High Priest, who is seated at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens." The "such" reaches back through chapter 7, where the writer has described Christ's priesthood at length. Hebrews 7:26, in paraphrase, calls Him a High Priest who is holy, blameless, separate from sinners, and exalted above the heavens. The writer does not repeat all that in 8:1. One word summons it.

Standing for a whole class of people or things (NKJV: "such," "such things")

In Matthew 19:14 Jesus says, "for of such is the kingdom of heaven" (see also Mark 10:14 and Luke 18:16). He does not say the kingdom belongs only to these children. He says it belongs to people of this kind. The children in front of Him become the pattern for those who receive the kingdom.

Galatians 5:21 closes Paul's list of the works of the flesh with a warning that "those who practice such things will not inherit the kingdom of God." The word turns a specific list into a category.

Referring to a known person without naming him (NKJV: "such a man," "such a one")

With the article, toioûtos can point to a definite individual whose identity the readers already know. In 2 Corinthians 2:6 Paul writes that the church's discipline "is sufficient for such a man." The Corinthians knew exactly who he was, and Paul chose not to name him. In 2 Corinthians 12:2 Paul uses the same device about himself: "such a one was caught up to the third heaven." He keeps his own name out of an account of extraordinary revelation.

3. Practical Reading Insight

The word changes how we read Paul's lists. In Galatians 5:21 toioûtos falls on "such things." The phrase "and the like," which comes just before it, translates a different Greek expression. Paul is not handing the Galatians a complete legal code that a clever person could work around by finding a sin he forgot to mention. He names the works of the flesh and then says, in effect, "everything of this character." Two verses later the same word appears again. After listing love, joy, peace, and the rest of the fruit of the Spirit, Paul says (paraphrased) that against such things there is no law (Galatians 5:23). Both lists describe a kind of life rather than a checklist. The flesh produces a recognizable family of behaviors, and the Spirit produces another.

Second Corinthians 12 shows the word serving humility. Paul speaks of "such a one" who saw Paradise, and then draws a line: "Of such a one I will boast; yet of myself I will not boast, except in my infirmities" (2 Corinthians 12:5). Scholars debate exactly why Paul distances himself this way. The effect, however, is plain. He will not build his authority on the vision. He builds it on his weakness, where Christ's power rests.

4. Reading Guidance

Do not read "such" as a simple synonym for "these," and do not read it as a mere intensifier, the way English uses "such a great day." Always look back for the description it carries forward. Keep in mind too that "such" in English translations can render other Greek words, and in Galatians 5:21 "and the like" is not toioûtos. The category "such things" is defined by the listed items. It is not a blank space to be filled with whatever a reader dislikes.
