SOCKET, BASE, FOUNDATION - (ʼeden, Strong's H0134)

Summary

The Hebrew word ʼeden means a socket or base. It is the heavy block that holds an upright board or pillar firmly in place. Nearly every use comes from the building of the wilderness tabernacle, the tent where God chose to dwell among Israel. Under its gold-covered boards and its pillars sat rows of metal sockets that kept the structure steady on desert ground. Twice the word moves beyond the tabernacle into poetry. In Job 38:6, God asks to what the earth's "foundations" were fastened. In the Song of Solomon, the Beloved's legs are pillars "set on bases of fine gold."

One detail gives this ordinary building word its weight. The hundred silver sockets that carried the sanctuary's walls and the veil were cast from the half-shekel "atonement money" paid by every numbered man in Israel. The rich paid no more than the poor, and the poor paid no less. God's house literally stood on the ransom price of His people. Peter later reminds believers that they "were not redeemed with corruptible things, like silver or gold... but with the precious blood of Christ" (1 Peter 1:18–19). The silver under the tabernacle points to a costlier foundation.

Full Study

1. Word Background & Setting

ʼeden is a Hebrew building term. The word occurs 57 times in 39 verses of the Hebrew Bible. Of these, 51 are in Exodus, in God's instructions for the tabernacle (chapters 26–27) and in the account of how Israel built it (chapters 35–40). Four more are in Numbers 3–4, where the Levites are assigned to carry the tabernacle's parts. The remaining two are poetic, in Job 38:6 and Song of Solomon 5:15.
Count source — Leningrad Hebrew text (Qere readings).

The tabernacle was a portable sanctuary. It had no stone footings. Its upright boards ended in two projecting pegs, called tenons, which slid into two sockets at the base (Exodus 26:19). The sockets gave each board a broad, heavy footing on loose ground. Notably, the word never appears in the accounts of Solomon's temple. ʼeden belongs to the years when God's house moved with a pilgrim people.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

1. A socket or base: a heavy block, usually cast metal, that receives and steadies a board or pillar. This is the sense throughout Exodus and Numbers, as in "forty sockets of silver under the twenty boards" (Exodus 26:19).

2. A footing or foundation in a figurative or poetic picture: the base on which something stands firm. Job 38:6 uses it for the earth's foundations, and Song of Solomon 5:15 uses it for the golden bases beneath marble pillars.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

The tabernacle texts care a great deal about the metal of each socket. The boards of the sanctuary and the four pillars holding the inner veil stood on silver (Exodus 26:19–25, 32). The five pillars at the tent's entrance, and every pillar around the outer court, stood on bronze (Exodus 26:37; 27:10–18). Even in the courtyard, the pillars carried hooks and bands of silver above their bronze sockets (Exodus 27:10–11, 17). The numbers are exact. Forty-eight boards with two sockets each, plus four for the veil pillars, make the hundred silver sockets of Exodus 38:27.

In Song of Solomon 5:15, the bride borrows this heavy building language to describe the man she loves: "His legs are pillars of marble set on bases of fine gold." The picture is of strength, worth, and steadiness. He stands like something built to last. This golden image serves a different purpose from the silver sockets of the sanctuary and should not be loaded with their meaning of ransom.

4. Theological Significance

The sockets show that God attends to foundations. His instructions do not stop at the gold, the colored curtains, and the ark. They reach down to the blocks at ground level, and He specifies their metal, number, and weight. The God who dwells with His people decides what His dwelling rests on.

That foundation is redemption. The bronze for the outer sockets came from the people's offering (Exodus 38:29–31). The silver for the sanctuary came from a different source: the ransom required of every counted man "that there may be no plague among them" (Exodus 30:12). As a worshiper moved from the court toward the holy place, the footing beneath the structure changed from bronze to silver. The inner dwelling stood on the price of atonement. Some readers also link bronze with the altar and judgment. That connection is suggestive rather than stated, but the move toward silver at the center is plain in the text.

Job 38 extends the picture. The God who directed the building of the tabernacle also built the world, and Scripture describes both with the same kind of language.

5. Important Biblical Passages & Distinctions

Exodus 30:11–16 and 38:25–27 — sockets cast from ransom money. At a census, God required each man twenty years and older to "give a ransom for himself to the LORD" (Exodus 30:12). The amount was fixed: "The rich shall not give more and the poor shall not give less than half a shekel... to make atonement for yourselves" (30:15). Moses was to "appoint it for the service of the tabernacle of meeting, that it may be a memorial for the children of Israel before the LORD" (30:16).

Exodus 38 reports where that money went. The silver of 603,550 men, "a bekah for each man" (38:26), came to a hundred talents. "And from the hundred talents of silver were cast the sockets of the sanctuary and the bases of the veil: one hundred sockets from the hundred talents, one talent for each socket" (38:27). A talent weighed roughly 75 pounds, so each socket was a massive block of silver. The small remainder of silver became hooks for the pillars, overlay for their capitals, and bands (38:28). Nearly all of the ransom money went into the ground-level foundation.

As a result, every board of the holy place and every pillar of the veil stood in atonement silver. The gold-covered walls never rested directly on the desert floor. The memorial before the LORD that Exodus 30:16 describes was built into the base of His house. Every time the tabernacle went up, the ransom of Israel went down first. Genesis 3:17 says "cursed is the ground," and the image of holy boards set in silver above that ground is a fitting reflection. Exodus does not draw that link itself.

Job 38:4–6 — the earth's sockets. God answers Job's suffering with questions: "Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth?... Who determined its measurements?... Or who stretched the line upon it? To what were its foundations fastened? Or who laid its cornerstone" (38:4–6). Measuring, line-stretching, setting footings, and laying a cornerstone are a builder's tasks. In verse 6, "foundations" is the same word used for the tabernacle sockets; the "foundations" of verse 4 translate a different Hebrew word. The effect is to present the world as a building carefully planned and set in place, and some readers rightly sense a sanctuary in the picture. Job cannot name what holds the earth steady. God can, because He set it there. The man who feels his world collapsing is reminded who placed its footings.

Numbers 3:36–37; 4:31–32 — the sons of Merari. When Israel moved, the Levite clan of Merari was assigned "the boards of the tabernacle, its bars, its pillars, its sockets" (Numbers 4:31). That included "the pillars around the court with their sockets, pegs, and cords" (Numbers 4:32). The silver sockets alone weighed several tons. Merari carried the heaviest and least glamorous load, the parts no one would admire once the tent was up. God supplied what the work needed: Merari received four carts and eight oxen, twice what the Gershonites received, "according to their service" (Numbers 7:8). The command closes with a personal note: "you shall assign to each man by name the items he must carry" (Numbers 4:32).

6. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

The silver sockets anticipate what the New Testament says openly. Peter writes, "you were not redeemed with corruptible things, like silver or gold... but with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot" (1 Peter 1:18–19). Israel's ransom was literally silver, and that silver literally held up God's house. Peter names the greater price that silver could only point toward.

The New Testament uses a different Greek word for "foundation," but it continues the same building theme. Paul says, "For no other foundation can anyone lay than that which is laid, which is Jesus Christ" (1 Corinthians 3:11), and he immediately calls the church "the temple of God" in whom "the Spirit of God dwells" (3:16). Believers are "being built together for a dwelling place of God in the Spirit," with "Jesus Christ Himself being the chief cornerstone" (Ephesians 2:20–22). The temporary tent on silver sockets gives way to a living house resting on Christ. Beyond that is the hope Abraham held: "the city which has foundations, whose builder and maker is God" (Hebrews 11:10). The builder of Job 38 finishes what He began.

7. Spirit & Life

No one in Israel could point to a socket and say, "My money holds up more of this than yours." Every socket was made of identical half-shekels. The ground beneath the church is level in the same way. Christ's blood is the same price for the respected and the forgotten, the long-faithful and the newly forgiven. No believer contributes more of the foundation than another, and none stands on a thinner part of it.

The sockets were also the least visible part of the tabernacle. The eye went to the gold and the woven colors, yet without seven thousand pounds of silver at ground level the walls would lean and fall in the first wind. Much of what holds a believer steady works the same way. It is not a feeling or a visible success. It is the finished ransom beneath everything, carrying the weight whether anyone notices it or not.

Merari's assignment speaks to those whose service is heavy and unseen. They hauled the foundations, not the ark. God named each man and his load, and He gave the clan carts in proportion to the weight. He does not hand out hidden burdens carelessly, and He does not leave His servants to carry them without help.

8. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Exodus 26:19 — "two sockets under each of the boards for its two tenons." Each board was anchored in two places, so no wall section depended on a single footing.

  • Exodus 26:32 — The four pillars holding the veil stood on silver sockets. The curtain before the Most Holy Place rested on the same ransom silver as the walls.

  • Exodus 26:37 — The entrance pillars received bronze sockets. This marks the shift in metal at the threshold of the tent.

  • Exodus 27:17 — The court pillars had silver bands and hooks but bronze sockets, mixing the two metals in the outer area.

  • Exodus 40:18 — When Moses raised up the tabernacle, the sockets were set in place first, before the boards and pillars. The foundation came first in the order of building.

  • Numbers 3:36–37 — The sockets are listed among Merari's permanent duties, making their care an ongoing calling rather than a one-time job.
