GATAM - (Gaʻtâm, Strong's H1609)

Summary

Gatam was a grandson of Esau, the fourth-named son of Eliphaz, Esau's firstborn by his wife Adah. He appears only in the genealogies of Edom (Genesis 36:11, 16; 1 Chronicles 1:36), where he is listed among the chiefs who headed clans in the land of Edom. The meaning of his name is uncertain, and the name tells us nothing reliable about the man himself.

Gatam's importance lies in where Scripture places him. Genesis records Esau's family organizing into chiefdoms while Jacob's family held only small pieces of land in Canaan, such as the family burial field with its cave and a parcel of land near Shechem (Genesis 23:17–20; 33:19). Gatam's brief entry shows God keeping faith with His word that Abraham would father nations, and it shows that the Lord kept careful account of peoples outside the line of promise.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Gatam is one person, a son of Eliphaz and grandson of Esau. His brothers were Teman, Omar, Zepho (spelled Zephi in 1 Chronicles 1:36), and Kenaz. Amalek, born to Eliphaz's concubine Timna, was his half-brother (Genesis 36:12). Gatam's family belonged to Mount Seir, the region south and southeast of the Dead Sea that became Edom.

The name is Semitic, and its meaning and derivation are uncertain. Scripture makes no wordplay on the name.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Genesis first names Gatam among "the sons of Eliphaz" (Genesis 36:11). It then lists him again with a title: "Chief Korah, Chief Gatam, and Chief Amalek. These were the chiefs of Eliphaz in the land of Edom" (Genesis 36:16). The Hebrew word for chief (alluph, "duke" in older English versions) points to the head of a clan or tribal division. The chronicler repeats the same family record centuries later (1 Chronicles 1:36). After these lists, Gatam's clan never appears again. Its history merges into the story of Edom as a whole.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states plainly that Esau's descendants had chiefs, and kings "before any king reigned over the children of Israel" (Genesis 36:31). God Himself gave Mount Seir to Esau (Deuteronomy 2:5). From these facts we can draw a fair conclusion. Esau's line gained land and political structure earlier than Israel, while Jacob's family waited through Egypt and the wilderness for what God had promised. Gatam is one of the names in that earlier Edomite record.

Gatam's brothers also took different paths. Amalek's people became bitter enemies of Israel (Exodus 17:16). Teman later became a place associated with wisdom (Jeremiah 49:7). Gatam's clan left no further record.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No direct connection to Christ is established through Gatam. His place in the larger story belongs to Edom's history, including the prophets' later judgments against Edom's pride (Obadiah; Jeremiah 49).

5. Spirit & Life

Gatam held real standing in his world. He was a named chief over a clan. Yet that rank is nearly all Scripture says of him. This does not prove his life meant nothing. It does show that early prosperity and visible power were not the measure of God's purposes. The family that waited longest carried the promise. God also remembered Gatam by name, and that care for every people is part of His character.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:11 – Names Gatam among the sons of Eliphaz.

  • Genesis 36:16 – Identifies him as a chief of Eliphaz in Edom.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:36 – Repeats his lineage in Israel's later record.

  • Genesis 36:31; Deuteronomy 2:5 – Show Edom's early rise and God's gift of Seir to Esau.
