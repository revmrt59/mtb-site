GETHER - (Gether, Strong's H1666)

Summary

Gether is a son of Aram, grandson of Shem, and great-grandson of Noah. He appears in only two genealogical lists, the Table of Nations in Genesis 10 and the Chronicler's opening genealogy in 1 Chronicles 1. The meaning of his name is unknown. Standard Hebrew lexicons give no established derivation, and Scripture makes no wordplay on it. As a son of Aram, Gether stands at the head of one branch of the Aramean peoples who spread across the region later known as Syria. His brother Uz bears the same name as Job's homeland, and some connect the two, though Scripture does not say so and names more than one Uz (Job 1:1; Genesis 36:28; Lamentations 4:21). Gether's people, in any case, disappear from the biblical record.

His name still carries weight. He belongs to the generation that fulfilled God's command after the flood to fill the earth, and to the family of nations out of which God called Abram. God promised Abram that all the families of the earth would be blessed through him (Genesis 12:3), and that promise reaches lines like Gether's. A people that history forgot was not forgotten by God, who preserved Gether's name in His Word.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Gether is a single individual, the third of four sons of Aram listed in Genesis 10:23: "The sons of Aram were Uz, Hul, Gether, and Mash." His father Aram was a son of Shem (Genesis 10:22), which places Gether in the Semitic line in the generations after the flood. Scripture names no other person or place called Gether.

The meaning of the name is unknown. Major lexicons, including Brown-Driver-Briggs, give no secure derivation. Some proposals based on Arabic roots have been offered, but none has gained agreement, and none should be treated as the meaning of the name. Scripture does not comment on it or play on its sound. Gether's people probably lived among the wider Aramean population north and northeast of Canaan, but their specific territory is not known.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Gether has no recorded deeds. Genesis 10 presents him as one ancestor in the spread of Noah's descendants after God told them to be fruitful, multiply, and fill the earth (Genesis 9:1). Centuries later, the Chronicler repeats the list when he grounds Israel's history in the whole human family. His version is shorter: "The sons of Shem were Elam, Asshur, Arphaxad, Lud, Aram, Uz, Hul, Gether, and Meshech" (1 Chronicles 1:17). Here Aram's sons follow directly after Aram under the heading "sons of Shem." The Chronicler compresses the generations in this list, and Genesis 10:23 makes clear that Gether was Aram's son. The fourth brother is called Mash in Genesis and Meshech in Chronicles. He should not be confused with Meshech the son of Japheth (Genesis 10:2; 1 Chronicles 1:5).

3. Significance in God's Purposes

The text states plainly that Gether was a son of Aram and one of the nations descended from Noah. A further point follows from where his name appears. Genesis 10 describes the nations, Genesis 11 tells of their scattering at Babel, and Genesis 12 narrows the story to one man, Abram. From there the story's focus narrows from the nations to Abram's family, and Gether's line drops out of view. It was never left out of God's plan, because the promise to Abram was meant for all the families of the earth (Genesis 12:3). Moses later says that God set the boundaries of the peoples (Deuteronomy 32:8), and Paul tells the Athenians that God made every nation from one blood (Acts 17:26). Gether's small entry stands within that larger claim.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

The New Testament never mentions Gether, and he is not presented as a type of Christ. The connection is a broad canonical one. The nations scattered in Genesis 10–11 reappear, gathered, in John's vision of "a great multitude which no one could number, of all nations, tribes, peoples, and tongues, standing before the throne and before the Lamb" (Revelation 7:9). The Lamb's people are drawn from the same family tree that Genesis records, including its forgotten branches.

5. Spirit & Life

Many peoples who ever lived left no lasting mark, and Gether's tribe seems to be among them. Its land and its end are unknown. Even so, its founder's name is written twice in Scripture. God keeps track of beginnings that human history loses, and He does not measure the worth of a people by how long it is remembered. The Lord who recorded Gether also knows those whom no one else will remember. That is why the promise to Abram and the multitude before the Lamb are good news for the nations and not only for Israel.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 10:22–23 — Establishes Gether as a son of Aram, grandson of Shem.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:17 — Repeats the record in compressed form, listing Gether after Aram under the sons of Shem, with Mash spelled Meshech.

  • Genesis 9:1; 12:3 — Provide the setting: God's command to fill the earth and His promise of blessing for all families.

  • Deuteronomy 32:8; Acts 17:26 — Affirm God's sovereignty over the origins and boundaries of the nations.

  • Revelation 7:9 — Shows the nations of the earth gathered before the Lamb.
