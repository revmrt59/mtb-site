DIKLAH - (Diqlâh, Strong's H1853)

Summary

Diklah is one of the thirteen sons of Joktan named in the Table of Nations (Genesis 10:27). The Chronicler repeats his name in Israel's genealogical record (1 Chronicles 1:21). Through Joktan he descends from Shem by way of Arphaxad, Salah, and Eber. His name probably means "palm grove," a meaning drawn from a related Aramaic word for the date palm. Scripture records no deeds or words of his. Like his brothers, he stands at the head of a people who settled in the region Genesis describes as stretching "from Mesha as you go toward Sephar, the mountain of the east" (Genesis 10:30), which is generally placed in Arabia. Diklah belongs to the branch of Eber's family that did not lead to Abraham. Even so, Genesis names him, and the Chronicler names him again centuries later. God's record of the nations does not skip over the peoples who lived far from Israel's story.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Diklah is the seventh of Joktan's thirteen sons in Genesis 10:26–29. His line runs Noah, Shem, Arphaxad, Salah, Eber, Joktan, Diklah. His uncle Peleg's line leads on to Abraham (Genesis 11:16–26). Joktan's sons appear to function as founders of tribal groups, so Diklah is best understood as an individual ancestor whose name also came to stand for his descendants. The name is generally understood as "palm grove," connected with the Aramaic diqla, "date palm." That meaning is probable, not certain. The palm-grove meaning has led some to link the tribe with an Arabian oasis, but no specific location can be identified.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Diklah has no narrative. He appears only in the list of Joktan's sons in Genesis 10:27, set within the spread of Shem's descendants after the flood. Genesis 10:25 records that in the days of Peleg "the earth was divided." The list appears again in 1 Chronicles 1:21, where the Chronicler opens Israel's history with the whole human family.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture says only that Diklah was Joktan's son and that Joktan's descendants dwelt between Mesha and Sephar. From that we can draw a careful inference. God chose Peleg's line to carry the promise to Abraham, yet the Bible still names the brothers who went another way. Paul later declares that God "has determined their preappointed times and the boundaries of their dwellings" for every nation (Acts 17:26). Diklah's people were among those whose homes God appointed.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No direct connection to Christ is established for Diklah. At a broader level, the nations listed in Genesis 10 form the backdrop for God's promise that all the families of the earth would be blessed in Abraham (Genesis 12:3). At Pentecost, "Cretans and Arabs" heard "the wonderful works of God" in their own tongues (Acts 2:11). Scripture does not trace those hearers to Diklah. Still, the gospel reached the region where Joktan's sons had settled. The great multitude "of all nations, tribes, peoples, and tongues" before the Lamb (Revelation 7:9) shows how far God's purpose extends.

5. Spirit & Life

A name with no story can seem easy to skip. Yet God recorded Diklah twice, once at the beginning of the nations and once at the restoring of Israel's records. No people group is too remote for the Lord of the whole earth to know where it lives.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 10:25–30: Diklah's place among Joktan's sons, his family setting beside Peleg, and the region where Joktan's descendants settled.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:21: the Chronicler preserves Diklah's name in Israel's genealogical prologue.

  • Acts 17:26: God appoints the times and boundaries of every nation.

  • Acts 2:11; Revelation 7:9: the gospel reaches Arabs, and people of every nation worship before the Lamb.
