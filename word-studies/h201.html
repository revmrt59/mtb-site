OMAR - (ʼÔwmâr, Strong's H0201)

Summary

Omar was a grandson of Esau, listed second among the sons of Eliphaz, Esau's firstborn, and he became one of the clan chiefs of Edom (Genesis 36:11, 15; 1 Chronicles 1:36). The meaning of his name is probable but not certain. It most likely comes from the Hebrew verb ʼamar, "to say" or "to speak," and is often explained as "speaker" or "eloquent." Scripture tells us nothing about his words, deeds, or faith. He appears only in family lists, and the Bible gives him no other story.

Omar's significance lies in where his name appears. Genesis 36 records the rise of Esau's family into an organized people with chiefs and, in time, kings who reigned before Israel had any king. Jacob's descendants, by contrast, would wait through slavery in Egypt and long years beyond before they had a land and a king of their own. Omar's title, "Chief Omar," shows that God kept His word to Esau. It also reminds readers that God's deepest promises to Jacob's line often unfolded slowly and out of public view.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Omar is one individual, and no other person in Scripture bears this name. He was the son of Eliphaz and the grandson of Esau through Adah, the daughter of Elon the Hittite (Genesis 36:2, 4, 10–11). His full brothers were Teman, Zepho (spelled Zephi in 1 Chronicles 1:36), Gatam, and Kenaz. Amalek was his half-brother, born to Eliphaz's concubine Timna (Genesis 36:12).

The name probably derives from the common Hebrew verb ʼamar, "to say, speak," which gives the sense "speaker" or "eloquent." That connection is a reasonable proposal from the Hebrew root. The biblical text, however, never comments on the name or plays on its meaning. Nothing in Scripture suggests that Omar was known for his speech, and his name should not be read as a summary of his life.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Omar's whole biblical record consists of three verses. Genesis 36:11 lists him among "the sons of Eliphaz." Genesis 36:15 then names him again with a title: "The sons of Eliphaz, the firstborn son of Esau, were Chief Teman, Chief Omar, Chief Zepho, Chief Kenaz." The Hebrew word translated "chief" (KJV "duke") is ʼalluph. It is commonly connected with the word for "thousand," which points to the leader of a clan. Omar therefore stood at the head of one branch of the Edomite people in the land of Seir, south and southeast of the Dead Sea. Centuries later, the Chronicler copied the same family line into Israel's records (1 Chronicles 1:36).

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Before the twins were born, the LORD told Rebekah, "Two nations are in your womb" (Genesis 25:23). Genesis 36 shows that word coming true: Esau's family grew into a nation. Isaac's later word, "By your sword you shall live, And you shall serve your brother" (Genesis 27:40), was fulfilled over Edom's longer history rather than in this genealogy itself. Moses later records that the LORD Himself gave Mount Seir to Esau as a possession (Deuteronomy 2:5). Those are the plain facts of Scripture.

A careful reading also suggests why Genesis includes this chapter. The text deliberately lists Edom's chiefs and kings "before any king reigned over the children of Israel" (Genesis 36:31). Then it turns back to Jacob's family with the words "This is the history of Jacob" (Genesis 37:2). Genesis honors the history of Israel's neighbor, closes Esau's account, and returns to the line through which God's promises would continue.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Omar himself is never mentioned again, and no connection between him and Christ is established. The nation he helped found does carry weight in later Scripture. Edom's hostility toward Israel draws the prophets' judgment (Obadiah; Malachi 1:2–3). Paul cites "Jacob I have loved, but Esau I have hated" to show that God's saving purpose stands "not of works but of Him who calls" (Romans 9:11, 13). That teaching concerns God's choosing of Jacob's line over Esau's. It is not a verdict on Omar personally.

5. Spirit & Life

Chief Omar led a clan of Edom, and Edom had chiefs and kings before Israel had any king (Genesis 36:31). Esau's family received titles early. Israel waited centuries for land and a king. Scripture does not condemn Edom's progress. It does show, though, that early visibility is not the measure of God's purposes. The promises given to Abraham moved forward through a family that looked far less impressive. Believers who wait on God's timing can take that seriously. The same God who kept His lesser word to Esau will certainly keep His greater word in Christ.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:11 – Records Omar's birth as a son of Eliphaz.

  • Genesis 36:15 – Names him "Chief Omar" among Edom's clan leaders.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:36 – Shows that Israel's later records preserved his lineage.

  • Genesis 27:39–40 – Gives Isaac's word to Esau, which Edom's later history fulfills.

  • Genesis 36:31; 37:2 – Mark the contrast between Edom's early kings and the return of the narrative to Jacob's line.

  • Malachi 1:2–3; Romans 9:11–13 – Present the later prophetic and apostolic interpretation of Jacob and Esau.
