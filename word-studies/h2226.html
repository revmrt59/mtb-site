ZERAH - (Zerach, Strong's H2226)

Summary

Zerah is a Hebrew name shared by several different men in the Old Testament. It comes from a verb meaning "to rise," "to shine," or "to appear," the word used for the sun coming up, so the name means something like "rising" or "dawning." The best known is Zerah the son of Judah and Tamar. He was the twin whose hand came out first at birth and received the midwife's scarlet thread, only for his brother Perez to break out ahead of him. Zerah's family became a major clan in Judah. It produced Achan, whose theft at Jericho brought defeat on all Israel, yet it was still serving God's people after the exile. Matthew names Zerah beside Perez in the genealogy of Jesus Christ (Matthew 1:3). A second Zerah, "the Ethiopian," marched against King Asa with "an army of a million men" (2 Chronicles 14:9). He became the occasion for one of the clearest prayers of dependence in the Old Testament. The name also belongs to an Edomite chief descended from Esau, a son of Simeon, and a Levite ancestor of Asaph the singer.

Taken together, the Zerahs of Scripture show God working in ways people do not expect. A child marked as firstborn by human hands did not become the line of the kings. A family stained by Achan's sin was not wiped out but preserved. An army no one in Judah could have defeated "fled" when a king confessed, "LORD, it is nothing for You to help" (2 Chronicles 14:11). The name itself carries no hidden spiritual message. What Scripture reveals through these men is the steady faithfulness of a God who keeps His promises through messy family histories and impossible odds.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

The name. Zerah comes from the Hebrew verb zarach (Strong's H2224), meaning "to rise," "to shine," "to appear," or "to break forth." It is commonly used of the sun rising (as in Ecclesiastes 1:5). The name therefore means "rising," "dawning," or "shining." This is the accepted derivation, and it fits the name's form well.

Genesis 38 is worth reading closely here. The text explains Perez's name directly: the midwife cries, "How did you break through? This breach be upon you!" and "therefore his name was called Perez" (Genesis 38:29). Zerah's name receives no such explanation. Scripture says only that the twin "who had the scarlet thread on his hand" came out "and his name was called Zerah" (38:30). Many readers have seen a fitting connection between a name meaning "appearing" or "shining" and the hand that appeared first with its bright scarlet thread. That connection is reasonable, but it remains an inference. Genesis does not spell it out as it does for Perez. Nor should the meaning "rising" be read as a description of any Zerah's life or character.

People sharing the name. One Strong's entry covers several separate people. They should not be merged.

1. Zerah son of Judah and Tamar. He was the twin brother of Perez (Genesis 38:30; 46:12; 1 Chronicles 2:4) and the father of the Zarhite clan of Judah (Numbers 26:20). His descendants include Achan (Joshua 7:1, 18, 24; 22:20), Jeuel and his kinsmen in post-exilic Jerusalem (1 Chronicles 9:6), and Pethahiah (Nehemiah 11:24). Matthew 1:3 names him in Greek as Zara (G2196), a separately indexed form of the same person's name.

2. Zerah the Ethiopian (Cushite). He was a military leader who invaded Judah during the reign of Asa (2 Chronicles 14:9). Scripture gives no lineage and no royal title. Historians have proposed identifying him with the Egyptian pharaoh Osorkon I, or with a Nubian or Arabian Cushite leader. The question is unresolved, and the biblical text does not depend on it.

3. Zerah the Edomite. He was a son of Reuel and a grandson of Esau and Basemath, and he was listed as a chief of Edom (Genesis 36:13, 17; 1 Chronicles 1:37). The list of Edom's early kings includes "Jobab the son of Zerah of Bozrah" (Genesis 36:33; 1 Chronicles 1:44). The text does not state whether this Zerah is the same man as Reuel's son, so the two are best kept distinct unless further evidence settles it.

4. Zerah son of Simeon. He was the father of a Zarhite family in Simeon (Numbers 26:13; 1 Chronicles 4:24). In Genesis 46:10 and Exodus 6:15 the same son appears as "Zohar," a separately indexed name (H6714).

5. Zerah the Levite(s). A Gershonite Levite named Zerah appears in 1 Chronicles 6:21, son of Iddo and father of Jeatherai, and a Zerah appears in Asaph's ancestry in 1 Chronicles 6:41, son of Adaiah and father of Ethni. Some interpreters treat these as the same man under variant names in parallel genealogies; others, and the standard lexicon's separate listing, allow two distinct Levites. Either way, the Zerah of 6:41 is an ancestor of Asaph, David's appointed singer.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Zerah, Son of Judah

A disputed birth. Zerah entered the world in one of the most uncomfortable chapters in Genesis. Judah had failed to give his widowed daughter-in-law Tamar to his son Shelah. Tamar then disguised herself and conceived by Judah himself (Genesis 38:6–26). "At the time for giving birth," the text says, "behold, twins were in her womb" (38:27). One child "put out his hand; and the midwife took a scarlet thread and bound it on his hand, saying, 'This one came out first'" (38:28). The midwife did exactly what she should have done, because in that world being firstborn mattered for inheritance and family standing. But the hand drew back, "his brother came out unexpectedly," and Perez was born first (38:29). Only "afterward" did Zerah follow, still wearing the thread that declared him first (38:30).

A clan in Judah. Zerah went down to Egypt with Jacob's household (Genesis 46:12). By the wilderness census his descendants formed "the family of the Zarhites" in Judah (Numbers 26:20). First Chronicles lists his sons as Zimri, Ethan, Heman, Calcol, and Dara (1 Chronicles 2:6). Similar names appear in 1 Kings 4:31 among famous wise men whom Solomon surpassed, and "Ethan the Ezrahite" and "Heman the Ezrahite" are named in the titles of Psalms 89 and 88. Some interpreters connect these figures with Zerah's family, since "Ezrahite" may relate to the Zarhite name. Others identify the psalmists with the Levitical musicians of 1 Chronicles 6 and 15. Scripture does not settle the matter.

Achan the Zarhite. Zerah's clan comes to the center of Israel's story at Jericho. Joshua 7:1 traces the sin with unusual precision to "Achan the son of Carmi, the son of Zabdi, the son of Zerah, of the tribe of Judah." Achan took from the devoted things God had claimed. Israel then fell at Ai, and the lot narrowed from tribe to clan to household until it landed on him (Joshua 7:16–18). Here "son of Zerah" means descendant, since several generations lie between them. Achan and his household were judged in the Valley of Achor (Joshua 7:24–26). Years later the tribes still remembered: "Did not Achan the son of Zerah commit a trespass in the accursed thing, and wrath fell on all the congregation of Israel? And that man did not perish alone in his iniquity" (Joshua 22:20). The Chronicler calls him Achar, "the troubler of Israel" (1 Chronicles 2:7), a play on the Hebrew word behind Achor.

The repeated naming of Zerah in Achan's story places the sin inside one particular branch of Judah. Meanwhile the other branch, through Perez, was producing men like Nahshon and Salmon, ancestors of David (Ruth 4:18–22). Scripture does not draw that contrast in words, but the genealogies set the two branches side by side.

After the exile. Zerah's line did not end in the Valley of Achor. When the exiles returned, "the sons of Zerah" were among those living in Jerusalem, with Jeuel and "their brethren—six hundred and ninety" (1 Chronicles 9:6). In Nehemiah's day, "Pethahiah the son of Meshezabel, of the children of Zerah the son of Judah, was the king's deputy in all matters concerning the people" (Nehemiah 11:24). The king in view is the Persian ruler. A descendant of the clan that once troubled Israel now stood as a trusted representative for the restored community.

Zerah the Ethiopian

Asa began his reign by tearing down foreign altars and calling Judah to seek the LORD (2 Chronicles 14:2–5). He fortified cities and raised a large army (14:6–8). Then "Zerah the Ethiopian came out against them with an army of a million men and three hundred chariots, and he came to Mareshah" (14:9), a town in the low hill country of western Judah. Asa set his troops "in battle array in the Valley of Zephathah at Mareshah" (14:10). Then he prayed:

"LORD, it is nothing for You to help, whether with many or with those who have no power; help us, O LORD our God, for we rest on You, and in Your name we go against this multitude. O LORD, You are our God; do not let man prevail against You!" (14:11)

The answer came at once: "the LORD struck the Ethiopians before Asa and Judah, and the Ethiopians fled" (14:12). They were pursued to Gerar and "broken before the LORD and His army" (14:13). "The fear of the LORD" fell on the surrounding cities, and Judah returned to Jerusalem with great spoil (14:14–15).

The story continues past the battle. The prophet Azariah met Asa on his return and told him that the LORD is with His people while they are with Him (2 Chronicles 15:1–2), and Asa led a renewed reform. Years later, however, Asa hired the king of Syria against Israel rather than trusting God. The seer Hanani rebuked him by recalling this very victory: the Ethiopians and Lubim had been a huge army, yet God delivered them into Asa's hand because he relied on the LORD (2 Chronicles 16:7–9, paraphrased). The defeat of Zerah became the measure of what Asa later forgot.

The Brief Figures

Zerah the Edomite was a grandson of Esau and one of the "chiefs" of Edom (Genesis 36:13, 17; 1 Chronicles 1:37). He shows how carefully Scripture recorded the family of Jacob's brother. A Zerah of Bozrah is also named as father of King Jobab (Genesis 36:33; 1 Chronicles 1:44).

Zerah of Simeon headed a Zarhite family in that tribe (Numbers 26:13; 1 Chronicles 4:24). He is called Zohar in the earlier lists (Genesis 46:10; Exodus 6:15).

Zerah the Levite appears in the Gershonite line (1 Chronicles 6:21) and in the ancestry of Asaph, whom David set over the singing in the house of the LORD (1 Chronicles 6:39–43). Whether these two references name one man or two Levites of the same name is uncertain, but the Zerah of 6:41 is certainly in Asaph's line.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

What Scripture states. Judah's line runs through Perez, not Zerah, toward David (Ruth 4:18–22; 1 Chronicles 2:4–15) and toward Christ (Matthew 1:3). Scripture also states that Zerah's family remained a recognized clan in Judah (Numbers 26:20). It records that Achan's sin brought wrath on the whole congregation (Joshua 7:1; 22:20) and that Zerah's descendants were part of the restored people (1 Chronicles 9:6; Nehemiah 11:24).

What follows from it. The scarlet thread was a sincere human judgment about who was first, and events overturned it. Genesis does not comment on the reversal, but it fits a pattern the book repeats again and again. Abel's offering was accepted over Cain's, Isaac was chosen over Ishmael, Jacob over Esau, Joseph over his older brothers, and Ephraim over Manasseh. God's purposes are not bound by birth order or by the markers people tie on. The line through which God would bring the kings, and finally the Messiah, came by an unexpected "breach."

That choice did not mean Zerah was rejected. His family stayed within Judah, kept its place in Israel's records, and outlasted the exile. The line of the promise was narrow, but the people God claimed were broad.

The Cushite Zerah serves God's purposes differently. He is an outsider and an enemy, and his army is so large that the Chronicler's account turns entirely on whom Judah will trust. Chronicles repeatedly teaches that seeking the LORD brings help and that leaning on human strength brings trouble. Asa's victory over Zerah and his later failure (2 Chronicles 16:7–9) form one of the clearest paired examples of that teaching in the book.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Explicit New Testament use. Matthew opens the story of Jesus with these words: "Judah begot Perez and Zerah by Tamar, Perez begot Hezron, and Hezron begot Ram" (Matthew 1:3). Matthew needed only Perez to trace the line. Luke's genealogy, running in the opposite direction, names Perez alone (Luke 3:33). Matthew does mention unnamed brothers elsewhere ("Judah and his brothers," 1:2), but here he names a particular brother who is not in the line. He also names the mother, Tamar, which ancient genealogies rarely did.

Together those two choices point any reader who knows Genesis straight back to chapter 38: Judah's failure, Tamar's desperate act, and the twins with the scarlet thread. Matthew did not tidy up the family of the Messiah. He placed its most awkward birth story in his opening lines.

Canonical development. The reversal at Zerah's birth belongs to the larger biblical pattern in which God chooses the unexpected one so that His grace, not human standing, accounts for the outcome. Paul draws on the same pattern when he writes of Jacob and Esau that God's purpose stands "not of works but of Him who calls" (Romans 9:11–12, paraphrased). Zerah's story is not cited there, but it moves in the same current.

Thematic resonance. Achan's story finds an echo in later Scripture. Hosea promises that the Valley of Achor, where Achan the Zarhite was judged, will become "a door of hope" (Hosea 2:15; compare Isaiah 65:10). Hosea is speaking of the place and of Israel's restoration, not of Zerah's family as such. Still, the same God who turned Achor into a door of hope also preserved Zerah's descendants and set one of them in a position of trust (Nehemiah 11:24). Neither the scarlet thread nor any Zerah is presented in Scripture as a type of Christ, and nothing more should be claimed than these patterns support.

5. Spirit & Life

God is not bound by our markers. The midwife was not wrong to tie the thread. She acted on what she saw. Yet the thread did not determine what God would do. Believers still tie threads: on the gifted, the respectable, the obvious candidates, sometimes on themselves. Genesis 38 teaches that God's purposes run deeper than our labels. That is a comfort to anyone who has been passed over, and a warning to anyone who trusts in being first.

Sin is never private, and judgment is not the last word. "That man did not perish alone in his iniquity" (Joshua 22:20). Achan's hidden theft cost lives at Ai and brought grief on his household. Scripture takes that seriously. Yet the Zerahites kept their place in Israel, and generations later "the children of Zerah the son of Judah" were serving in Jerusalem (Nehemiah 11:24). God judged Achan's sin without erasing Achan's people. A family's past failure does not define what God may do with its future.

"It is nothing for You to help." Asa's prayer before Zerah the Ethiopian is worth learning by heart. He did not pretend the army was small. He named it a "multitude." Then he set it beside God and confessed that God is no more strained by many than by few. He rested his case on relationship ("O LORD our God... we rest on You") and on God's honor ("do not let man prevail against You!") (2 Chronicles 14:11). The LORD answered by breaking the army "before the LORD and His army" (14:13).

Asa's later story keeps this from becoming a slogan. The same king who prayed this way later reached for a foreign alliance, and Hanani reminded him of the day he had trusted God against the Ethiopians (2 Chronicles 16:7–9). Yesterday's deliverance does not secure today's faith. The God who scattered Zerah's army calls His people to keep resting on Him when the next threat comes, whether it is smaller or larger.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 38:27–30 — The birth of Perez and Zerah; the scarlet thread and the unexpected reversal.

  • Numbers 26:13, 20 — The Zarhite families of Simeon and of Judah in the wilderness census.

  • Joshua 7:1, 18, 24 — Achan's sin, traced to his descent from Zerah, and his judgment.

  • Joshua 22:20 — Israel's later memory of Achan's trespass and its effect on the whole congregation.

  • 1 Chronicles 2:4–7 — Zerah's sons and Achar, "the troubler of Israel."

  • 1 Chronicles 9:6; Nehemiah 11:24 — Zerah's descendants in post-exilic Jerusalem; Pethahiah as the king's deputy.

  • 2 Chronicles 14:9–15 — Zerah the Ethiopian's invasion, Asa's prayer, and the LORD's deliverance.

  • 2 Chronicles 16:7–9 — Hanani recalls the victory over the Ethiopians in rebuking Asa's later lack of trust.

  • Matthew 1:3 — Zerah (Zara, G2196) named with Perez and Tamar in the genealogy of Jesus Christ.

  • Genesis 36:13, 17, 33; 1 Chronicles 1:37, 44 — Zerah the Edomite chief, and Zerah of Bozrah, father of King Jobab.

  • 1 Chronicles 4:24 (with Genesis 46:10; Exodus 6:15) — Zerah of Simeon, also called Zohar.

  • 1 Chronicles 6:21, 41 — Zerah the Gershonite Levite (6:21) and Zerah in Asaph's ancestry (6:41), possibly the same man.
