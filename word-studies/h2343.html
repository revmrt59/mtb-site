HUL - (Chûwl, Strong's H2343)

Summary

Hul was a son of Aram and a grandson of Shem. He belongs to the generations after the Flood, listed in the Table of Nations in Genesis 10. Scripture names him only twice, in Genesis 10:23 and again in the Chronicler's genealogy at 1 Chronicles 1:17. No story is told about him. The meaning of his name is uncertain. It has been tentatively linked to the idea of a "circle" or to the Hebrew word for "sand," but neither suggestion is secure. As one of Aram's sons, Hul stands at the head of an Aramean family whose descendants would later become Israel's northern neighbors.

Hul matters because of where Scripture places him. Before the Bible narrows its focus to Abraham, it records the wider human family, including Shemite lines like Aram's that would not carry the promise. God knew Hul's household by name. Paul later declared that this same God "has made from one blood every nation of men" (Acts 17:26), and the same God is praised for redeeming people "out of every tribe and tongue and people and nation" (Revelation 5:9).

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Hul is an individual ancestor. Genesis 10:23 names him second among Aram's four sons: "The sons of Aram were Uz, Hul, Gether, and Mash." He was therefore Noah's great-grandson through Shem. His line is separate from Arphaxad's, the branch that leads to Eber and Abraham.

The meaning of the name is uncertain. Strong's proposes "circle." The name is spelled the same as a common Hebrew verb meaning to twist, whirl, dance, or writhe (H2342). That verb is a separate word, and Scripture draws no wordplay between it and the man Hul.

Where his descendants lived is also unknown. Older geographers connected the name with the Hula Valley and Lake Huleh north of the Sea of Galilee, a lake often linked with "the waters of Merom" (Joshua 11:5). Josephus associated Hul with Armenia, and others look to the Syrian desert. Each of these is a proposal, not a biblical statement.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Hul appears only in genealogy. In Genesis 10 he is one of the families that spread out after the Flood. Aram's sons fill in the Shemite world beyond the line that leads to Abraham.

The Chronicler repeats the list centuries later for a people returning from exile. In 1 Chronicles 1:17 the NKJV reads, "The sons of Shem were Elam, Asshur, Arphaxad, Lud, Aram, Uz, Hul, Gether, and Meshech." This verse places Hul directly in the list of Shem's sons, and it has Meshech where Genesis has Mash. Genesis 10:22–23 makes the relationship clear. Hul is Aram's son, and the Chronicler compresses the generations. Biblical genealogies often use "sons" broadly to mean descendants.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states plainly that Hul descended from Shem through Aram. Two further points are drawn from that fact rather than stated in the text.

First, the Bible records families that fall outside the line of promise before it turns to Abraham. God's purpose through Abraham was meant for a world He already knew in detail.

Second, Aram's line became the Aramean world in which Abraham's relatives later settled. Abraham himself came from Arphaxad, not Aram. Hul shows that sharing Shem's blessing in a general sense (Genesis 9:26) was not the same as receiving God's specific promise. That promise came by God's choice.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No New Testament text mentions Hul. The connection is thematic. Paul tells the Athenians that God "has determined their preappointed times and the boundaries of their dwellings" (Acts 17:26). Revelation 5:9 sings of the Lamb who was slain and "redeemed us to God by Your blood out of every tribe and tongue and people and nation." The scattered families of Genesis 10 find their hope in Christ, who gathers worshipers from all of them.

5. Spirit & Life

Most readers pass over Hul's name. Yet it was written down in Genesis and copied again in Chronicles for a people rebuilding after exile. God does not lose track of families that history forgets. The returning exiles needed that reassurance as they wondered about their own place, and it still holds for believers today.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 10:22–23: establishes Hul as Aram's son in Shem's line.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:17: preserves his name in a compressed post-exilic genealogy.

  • Acts 17:26: God's rule over the origins and boundaries of every nation.

  • Revelation 5:9: the redeemed gathered from every tribe and nation.
