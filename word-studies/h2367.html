HUSHAM - (Chûwshâm, Strong's H2367)

Summary

Husham was one of the early kings of Edom, the nation descended from Esau. Scripture says only that he came "of the land of the Temanites," that he followed Jobab of Bozrah on the throne, and that Hadad the son of Bedad succeeded him when he died (Genesis 36:34–35; 1 Chronicles 1:45–46). His name is traditionally understood to mean "haste" or "hasty," from a Hebrew root meaning to hurry, though the Bible never comments on the name or connects it to his reign.

What makes Husham worth noticing is where Moses places him. He belongs to a list of "the kings who reigned in the land of Edom before any king reigned over the children of Israel" (Genesis 36:31). Esau's family had thrones and capitals while Jacob's family, the people who carried God's promise, waited generations for a king. Each Edomite reign in the list closes the same way: the king died, and another took his place. Husham's single line quietly sets the passing crowns of this world against the King God would one day give His people.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Husham is a single individual, the third king named in the Edomite king list (after Bela and Jobab). His origin is given as "the land of the Temanites." Teman was a region of Edom that took its name from Teman, Esau's grandson through Eliphaz (Genesis 36:11). Husham should not be confused with Hushim (H2366), a separate name borne by a son of Dan (Genesis 46:23) and by a wife of Shaharaim (1 Chronicles 8:8, 11).

Strong's and BDB connect the name with the Hebrew root chush, "to hasten" or "make haste," which yields a meaning such as "haste," "hasting," or "impetuous." This is a probable lexical derivation and not a meaning the biblical text states or uses in wordplay. Nothing in Scripture suggests Husham was hasty in character.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

The record is brief: "When Jobab died, Husham of the land of the Temanites reigned in his place" (Genesis 36:34), and when Husham died, Hadad the son of Bedad, "who attacked Midian in the field of Moab," reigned in his place from his city Avith (Genesis 36:35). The Chronicler repeats the same list after the exile (1 Chronicles 1:45–46). No length of reign, deeds, or dates are recorded.

The kings in the list come from different places: Dinhabah, Bozrah, the land of the Temanites, Avith. None is said to be the son of the king before him. This suggests that early Edomite kingship was not passed from father to son and that power moved among regional clans, though the text does not explain how kings were chosen.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states that these kings ruled Edom before Israel had any king (Genesis 36:31). God had promised that kings would come from Abraham and from Jacob (Genesis 17:6; 35:11). Edom reached settled monarchy first, while Israel was still waiting for the kings God had promised. Genesis does not explain the contrast, but placing the list there invites the reader to notice it. The line of promise moves on God's timetable and not according to the pace of its neighbors.

Husham's homeland also has a later history in Scripture. The prophets single out Teman for judgment. Jeremiah asks, "Is wisdom no more in Teman?" (Jeremiah 49:7). Obadiah warns, "Your mighty men, O Teman, shall be dismayed" (Obadiah 1:9). Amos announces, "I will send a fire upon Teman, Which shall devour the palaces of Bozrah" (Amos 1:12). Teman's renowned wisdom and strength did not protect Edom when God judged its pride.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No later Scripture mentions Husham, and he is not a type of Christ. The repeated refrain of the king list ("and he died… and reigned in his place") does stand in clear thematic contrast with the promise that David's Son would reign forever, a promise announced of Jesus in Luke 1:32–33. Edom's rulers came and went. The King God promised to Israel has a kingdom without end.

5. Spirit & Life

Genesis 36 is easy to skim, but it gives an honest picture of how things can look to God's people. Others often seem to get their crowns first. Esau's descendants had kings while Jacob's descendants had only a promise. Yet the lists repeatedly mark each king's death, and the prophets later address Teman's proud wisdom in judgment. God's delays are not His neglect. He was preparing a kingdom that would not pass to the next man when its king died.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:31 – Frames the list as Edom's kings before Israel had a king.

  • Genesis 36:34–35 – Husham's accession from the land of the Temanites and his death.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:45–46 – The Chronicler's repetition of the same record.

  • Genesis 36:11 – Teman, Esau's grandson, the namesake of Husham's region.

  • Jeremiah 49:7; Obadiah 1:8–9; Amos 1:12 – Later judgment pronounced on Teman.
