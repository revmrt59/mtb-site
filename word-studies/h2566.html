HAMRAN - (Chamrân, Strong's H2566)

Summary

Hamran is a Horite, a member of the people who lived in the hill country of Seir before Esau's descendants took it over. He appears only once under this spelling, in 1 Chronicles 1:41, where he is the first-named son of Dishon. The parallel list in Genesis 36:26 calls the same man Hemdan, a separately indexed name (H2533). The meaning of Hamran is probable rather than certain. It likely comes from a Hebrew root connected with "red" or "fermenting," the family of words that includes chemer, "wine." The Genesis form Hemdan probably means "desire" or "pleasant." The two spellings differ by only one letter, and the difference is probably an early copying variation.

Scripture records nothing Hamran said or did, and it offers no account of his relationship with the LORD. His importance lies in the people he belonged to. Moses points to the Horites as a nation that once held Seir and lost it when "the descendants of Esau dispossessed them" (Deuteronomy 2:12). The Chronicler later includes Hamran's family in the genealogies that open his history, before narrowing to the line of David. God rules the histories and boundaries of every nation, not only Israel's, and He keeps a record that even includes the clans of Seir.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Hamran is the son of Dishon, a Horite of Seir. In 1 Chronicles 1:41 his name heads the list: "The sons of Dishon were Hamran, Eshban, Ithran, and Cheran." Genesis 36:26 gives the same four brothers in the same order, except that the first is named Hemdan. Because the family, brothers, and sequence match, Hamran and Hemdan are one person recorded under two spellings. Hemdan has its own Strong's number (H2533), so this entry treats it as a related, separately indexed form rather than a second occurrence of H2566.

The two spellings differ by one letter. In Hamran the third letter is resh, while in Hemdan it is dalet. These Hebrew letters look very much alike, and confusion between them is a well-known feature of textual transmission. The variation does not change the person's identity or the reliability of the record. The surviving forms do not tell us exactly when or how the difference arose, but the matching family details leave the identity clear.

Each spelling has its own probable meaning. Hamran likely comes from a root associated with "red" or "fermenting," related to chemer, "wine." Hemdan likely comes from chamad, "to desire," and carries the sense "desire" or "pleasant." Both meanings are linguistic probabilities, and Scripture draws no lesson from either one.

Hamran's exact line needs a little care. Two men named Dishon appear in these lists. One is a son of Seir (Genesis 36:21; 1 Chronicles 1:38), and the other is a son of Anah (Genesis 36:25; 1 Chronicles 1:41). The Chronicler places "the son of Anah was Dishon" directly before "the sons of Dishon," which suggests Anah's son. The Genesis list, however, follows the order of Seir's sons, which points toward Seir's son. The spellings add to the difficulty: in Genesis 36:26 the Hebrew has the form Dishan (H1789), though the NKJV renders it Dishon, while 1 Chronicles 1:41 reads Dishon (H1787). Either way, Hamran belonged to a leading Horite family of Seir.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Hamran lived in the patriarchal period, around the time of Esau and his early descendants. Genesis 36:20 introduces his people as "the sons of Seir the Horite who inhabited the land," and verses 29–30 list their chiefs "in the land of Seir." This family was already tied to Esau's household. Esau's wife Aholibamah was a daughter of Anah (Genesis 36:2, 25).

The Horites did not keep their land. Deuteronomy 2:12 records that they "formerly dwelt in Seir, but the descendants of Esau dispossessed them and destroyed them from before them, and dwelt in their place." Deuteronomy 2:22 credits this outcome to the LORD, who acted on behalf of Esau's descendants. Centuries later, the Chronicler still preserved the family names in his opening genealogies (1 Chronicles 1:38–42).

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states directly that God gave Seir to Esau's line and removed the Horites before them (Deuteronomy 2:5, 22). Moses sets this beside Israel's own experience of receiving "the land of their possession which the LORD gave them" (Deuteronomy 2:12). The LORD who settled Edom in Seir was the same LORD who was giving Israel its inheritance.

The Chronicler's purpose can also be reasonably inferred. He begins with Adam and works through the nations, Edom and the Horites included, before he focuses on Judah and David. That order places Israel's story inside a world that belongs entirely to God.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Scripture establishes no direct connection between Hamran and Christ. At most, Paul's statement echoes the broader pattern: God "has determined their preappointed times and the boundaries of their dwellings" (Acts 17:26).

5. Spirit & Life

Hamran's name gives no personal example to imitate. What his place in the record does show is that God sees the whole of history, including families and peoples who vanished long before Israel had a king. Their rise and loss of land fell under His rule, just as Israel's history did.

6. Key Scriptures

  • 1 Chronicles 1:41 – The only occurrence of Hamran, naming him as Dishon's son.

  • Genesis 36:26 – The parallel list, where the same man is called Hemdan (H2533).

  • Genesis 36:20–30; 1 Chronicles 1:38–42 – The Horite genealogy of Seir.

  • Deuteronomy 2:12, 22 – The Horites' removal from Seir by the LORD's action for Esau's descendants.

  • Acts 17:26 – God's rule over the times and boundaries of nations.
