HAZARMAVETH - (Chătsarmâveth, Strong's H2700)

Summary

Hazarmaveth is a son of Joktan and a great-great-great-grandson of Shem. He appears only twice in Scripture, both times in the list of Noah's descendants known as the Table of Nations (Genesis 10:26; 1 Chronicles 1:20). His name joins two Hebrew words, chatsar, "court," "village," or "settlement," and maveth, "death," so it means "Court of Death" or "Village of Death." Scripture never explains why anyone would carry such a name. Many scholars link it with Hadhramaut, a region of southern Arabia in what is now eastern Yemen. The Bible itself does not make that identification, but the sound correspondence is widely recognized.

Hazarmaveth matters for where he stands in the family tree. Eber had two sons, Peleg and Joktan (Genesis 10:25). Through Peleg came Abraham and, in time, Jesus Christ (Genesis 11:16–26; Luke 3:35). Hazarmaveth belongs to Joktan's branch, the peoples who settled toward the east and south. His name is a small piece of evidence that God keeps track of the nations outside the chosen line and records them by name.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Hazarmaveth is an individual descendant of Shem through Arphaxad, Salah, Eber, and Joktan. He is listed third among Joktan's thirteen sons, after Almodad and Sheleph (Genesis 10:26–29). Like many names in Genesis 10, his seems to refer both to an ancestor and to the people or territory descended from him.

The name combines chatsar (H2691), "court," "enclosure," or "village," and maveth (H4194), "death." That gives the meaning "Court of Death" or "Village of Death." That is the standard Hebrew analysis of the name; it does not establish the historical origin of the native South Arabian name. Scripture offers no wordplay on the name and gives no reason for it. Suggestions that it reflects the region's harsh climate or the dangers of desert trade routes are guesses.

Most scholars identify Hazarmaveth with Hadhramaut in southern Arabia (the ancient region's exact boundaries are uncertain and need not match the modern one), which was known in ancient times for frankincense. That identification is a well-supported inference from the related Arabic name. Scripture does not state it.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Scripture records no actions by Hazarmaveth. Genesis 10:26 names him among Joktan's sons: "Joktan begot Almodad, Sheleph, Hazarmaveth, Jerah." Their territory stretched "from Mesha as you go toward Sephar, the mountain of the east" (Genesis 10:30). The exact location of those boundary points is uncertain.

The Chronicler repeats the line word for word in 1 Chronicles 1:20. His genealogies begin with Adam and pass through all the nations before narrowing to Israel. Hazarmaveth's place in that list sets Israel's story inside the larger history of humanity.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Genesis 10 shows Noah's family spreading across the earth after the Flood, as God had commanded (Genesis 9:1). Eber's two sons divide the story. Peleg's line leads straight to Abram (Genesis 11:16–26), while Joktan's sons, Hazarmaveth among them, settle their own lands.

The text says plainly that God chose to work through one family. It does not say God forgot the others. Their names are written into Scripture alongside the chosen line, and the call of Abram follows directly with the promise that "all the families of the earth" would be blessed through him (Genesis 12:3). Hazarmaveth's descendants belong to those families. Scripture leaves their later history untold, but they fall within the scope of that promise.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No direct link to Christ is established for Hazarmaveth himself. The Table of Nations does supply the setting for the gospel. Luke traces Jesus' ancestry back through Peleg and Eber to Adam (Luke 3:35–38), and Hazarmaveth stands on the branch that divided from that line. The Savior who came through Peleg's descendants came for the whole family of nations, including peoples like Joktan's sons who were never part of Israel.

5. Spirit & Life

For a name that appears only in a genealogy, the lesson should stay modest. Hazarmaveth is a name that would mean little to most readers, yet God preserved it twice in His Word. The God who chose Abraham also counted the nations that never received Abraham's promise directly, and His purpose of blessing reaches them too. A name as bleak as "Court of Death" does not put anyone beyond the scope of that blessing.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 10:25–30 – Records Eber's two sons and names Hazarmaveth among Joktan's descendants, with their territory.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:17–23 – Repeats the Joktanite line in the Chronicler's genealogy, which surveys all humanity before narrowing to Israel.

  • Genesis 11:16–26 – Follows Peleg's line to Abram, the branch from which Joktan's line divided.

  • Genesis 12:3 – Promises blessing to all the families of the earth through Abram.

  • Luke 3:35–38 – Traces Jesus' ancestry through Peleg and Eber, placing Christ within the same family tree.
