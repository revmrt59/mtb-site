AHUZZAM - (ʼĂchuzzâm, Strong's H0275)

Summary

Ahuzzam was a man of the tribe of Judah, named only once in Scripture, at 1 Chronicles 4:6. He was a son of Ashhur, "the father of Tekoa," and Ashhur's wife Naarah, and his name heads the list of her four sons. His name comes from the Hebrew verb meaning "to grasp" or "to take possession," and it probably means "possessor" or "their possession." The exact sense is uncertain. Scripture tells us nothing about his deeds. Still, his place in the long family records of Judah shows that God remembered the ordinary households who settled the land, the same tribe from which David's royal line and finally the Messiah would come.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Ahuzzam belongs to the family of Hezron, Judah's grandson. Hezron's widow Abijah bore Ashhur after Hezron's death (1 Chronicles 2:24). Ashhur had two wives, Helah and Naarah (1 Chronicles 4:5). Naarah's sons were Ahuzzam, Hepher, Temeni, and Haahashtari (4:6). Helah's sons, Zereth, Zohar, and Ethnan, were Ahuzzam's half-brothers (4:7).

The name is built on the Hebrew root ʼachaz, "to grasp, take hold, possess." It likely means "possessor" or "their possession." Whether the ending is a pronoun ("their") or simply part of how the name is formed is debated, so the meaning is probable rather than certain. The text offers no wordplay on the name. Ahuzzath (H0276), the Philistine adviser of Abimelech in Genesis 26:26, is a separate person with a separately indexed name, though the two names appear to share the same root.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Scripture records no events from Ahuzzam's life. His name stands in the Chronicler's record of Judah's clans connected with Tekoa. In this record "father of Tekoa" most naturally means Ashhur was the founder or leading figure of that town. Tekoa, in Judah's hill country south of Bethlehem, later became known as the home of the wise woman Joab brought to David (2 Samuel 14:2) and of the prophet Amos (Amos 1:1).

3. Significance in God's Purposes

The text itself states only Ahuzzam's parentage and his place among his brothers. Two observations can be drawn from the setting. First, Judah's genealogies fill three chapters (1 Chronicles 2–4), more space than any other tribe receives, because Judah carried the royal line of David. Second, Ahuzzam was not in David's direct ancestry, which runs through Hezron's son Ram (2:9–15). He belonged instead to the wider community of Judah that surrounded and supported that line. For the Chronicler's first readers, who were returning from exile, names like his confirmed that their families and inheritance had not been forgotten.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No direct connection to Christ is established for Ahuzzam himself. His significance belongs to the tribe as a whole. That tribe produced "the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David" (Revelation 5:5).

5. Spirit & Life

We know nothing Ahuzzam said or did. God nonetheless kept his name in the record of His people. Scripture does not let us read heroic qualities into him. It does show a God who does not lose track of the quiet families through whom He keeps His promises.

6. Key Scriptures

  • 1 Chronicles 4:5–6: The only mention of Ahuzzam. These verses give his father Ashhur, his mother Naarah, and his full brothers.

  • 1 Chronicles 4:7: Names his half-brothers through Helah.

  • 1 Chronicles 2:24: Records the birth of his father Ashhur to Hezron's widow.

  • 1 Chronicles 2:9–15: Traces David's line through Ram, which distinguishes it from Ahuzzam's branch.

  • Revelation 5:5: Identifies Christ as the Lion of the tribe of Judah.
