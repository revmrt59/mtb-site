ABIDA - (ʼĂbîydâʻ, Strong's H0028)

Summary

Abida was a grandson of Abraham through Midian, one of the six sons Abraham had with Keturah. He appears only twice in Scripture, both times in a list: "the sons of Midian were Ephah, Epher, Hanoch, Abidah, and Eldaah" (Genesis 25:4; 1 Chronicles 1:33). The NKJV spells his name "Abidah" in Genesis and "Abida" in Chronicles, but the Hebrew name is the same. It is built from the words for "father" and "to know," and is usually rendered "my father knows" or "father of knowledge." Scripture does not explain the name or say which father is in view. Abida was one of the founders of the Midianite clans, the people who settled in "the country of the east" after Abraham sent Keturah's sons away from Isaac (Genesis 25:6).

Abida's importance lies in where Genesis places him. He belongs to Abraham's family, yet he stands outside the line through which God would keep His promise. Abraham "gave all that he had to Isaac," gave gifts to his other sons, and sent them away eastward (Genesis 25:5–6). God's choice of Isaac was firm, but Abraham did not disown the rest of his household. Scripture remembered Abida's name, and the gospel later opens Abraham's family to people from every nation through faith in Christ (Galatians 3:29).

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Abida is one person, the fourth of Midian's five sons and a great-grandson of Terah through Abraham (Genesis 25:1–4). His uncles were Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Ishbak, and Shuah. No one else in Scripture bears this name. Genesis 25:1 calls Keturah Abraham's wife, while 1 Chronicles 1:32 calls her his concubine. Genesis 25:6 appears to include her sons among "the sons of the concubines."

The name ʼĂbîydâʻ combines ʼab, "father," with a form of yādaʻ, "to know." Standard lexicons give "my father knows" or "father of knowledge." The parts of the name are clear, but its exact intended sense is not. "Father" could refer to God, as it does in many Hebrew names, or to a human father. The text itself attaches no wordplay or explanation to the name.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Abida has no recorded deeds. Genesis lists him among Keturah's descendants just before it reports that Abraham settled his estate. Isaac received the inheritance. The sons of the concubines received gifts and were sent "eastward, away from Isaac his son, to the country of the east" (Genesis 25:5–6). Abida was a grandson, so the text does not say he received gifts himself. His family line, however, went east with Midian.

First Chronicles 1:33 repeats the list word for word. The Chronicler places Abraham's wider family there before narrowing his attention to Israel. Abida's clan cannot be confidently matched to any known ancient Arabian tribe.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture plainly states that the promise would pass through Isaac and not through Abraham's other sons (Genesis 17:19–21; 25:5). Abida's line shows the other side of that choice. It shows real family, real descent from Abraham, and still a different place in God's plan. Genesis commonly records the lines outside the promise briefly and then moves on. Ishmael's line, for example, is listed in Genesis 25:12–18 before Isaac's story begins in 25:19.

It is a reasonable inference that sending these sons away protected Isaac's inheritance in Canaan. Genesis also notes that some who stand apart from the promised line move east. Cain went east of Eden (Genesis 4:16), and Lot journeyed east toward Sodom (Genesis 13:11). That pattern is worth noticing, but the text does not pass judgment on Abida himself.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No passage connects Abida directly to Christ. His wider Midianite family reappears often in the Old Testament. Moses found refuge with a priest of Midian (Exodus 2:15–21). Midian later led Israel into sin (Numbers 25) and oppressed Israel in the days of Gideon (Judges 6–8). Isaiah pictures Midian and Ephah bringing gifts to Zion (Isaiah 60:6). The New Testament declares that those who belong to Christ are Abraham's seed (Galatians 3:29). The families once sent east are not shut out of the blessing God promised through Abraham.

5. Spirit & Life

Abida's name is not a key to his life, and it would be unwise to build a lesson on it. His place in the record still says something true. God chose one line, Isaac's, to carry His promise, and He meant that choice. Yet Abraham gave gifts to the sons who did not inherit, and God kept their names in His Word. God's choosing does not mean He has forgotten the others.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 25:1–4 – Abida's parentage and his place in Keturah's line.

  • Genesis 25:5–6 – Isaac's inheritance and the sending of the other sons east.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:32–33 – The Chronicler's record of Abida and of Keturah as a concubine.

  • Genesis 17:19–21 – God's promise established with Isaac.

  • Isaiah 60:6; Galatians 3:29 – Midian's later hope and the widening of Abraham's family in Christ.
