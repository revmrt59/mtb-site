JAALAM - (Yaʻlâm, Strong's H3281)

Summary

Jaalam was a son of Esau by his wife Aholibamah. He was born in the land of Canaan and later became a clan chief among Esau's descendants in Edom (Genesis 36:5, 14, 18; 1 Chronicles 1:35). His name probably means "concealed" or "hidden." It comes from a Hebrew root meaning "to hide," though the derivation is not absolutely certain. Scripture says nothing of his deeds, words, or faith. What it preserves is his place in the family that grew into the nation of Edom.

That small record still carries weight. Genesis notes that Jaalam was born in Canaan, yet Esau's household settled in Mount Seir, separate from Jacob's. In time Esau's sons ruled as chiefs, while Jacob's family long remained a household of shepherds. Jaalam belongs to a line that prospered early and visibly, and that history is part of the larger story in which God keeps His promise through the slower, weaker line of Jacob.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Jaalam (spelled "Jalam" in some English versions) is one individual. He is the second listed of the three sons Aholibamah bore to Esau, after Jeush and before Korah (Genesis 36:5, 14). Genesis identifies Aholibamah as the daughter of Anah and granddaughter of Zibeon the Hivite (Genesis 36:2). This sets her sons apart from the sons of Esau's other wives, Adah and Basemath. No other biblical person bears this name.

The Hebrew name Yaʻlâm is traced by Strong's traditional derivation (H5956) to the root ʿalam, "to conceal" or "to hide." On that basis the name means "concealed" or "hidden." The Brown-Driver-Briggs entry identifies the man and his passages but does not itself give this derivation, so the meaning remains uncertain, and the Bible makes no wordplay on it. Nothing in Scripture suggests the name describes Jaalam's character or life.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Jaalam lived in the patriarchal era. Genesis 36:5 records his birth: "And Aholibamah bore Jeush, Jaalam, and Korah. These were the sons of Esau who were born to him in the land of Canaan." Genesis 36:6–8 then reports that Esau took his wives, children, and livestock to Mount Seir, because the land could not support both his herds and Jacob's. Esau is already linked with Seir in Genesis 32:3 and 33:16, so this passage summarizes the separation of the two households rather than fixing one new move after all those events.

In Edom, Jaalam became one of the chiefs of Esau's sons (Genesis 36:18), the head of a clan that carried his name. Centuries later, the Chronicler listed him again among Esau's sons (1 Chronicles 1:35), within a record of the nations surrounding Israel.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states three facts about Jaalam: he was born in Canaan, he left with Esau's household, and he became a chief in Edom. The meaning of those facts comes from their setting. Genesis presents Esau's move as a practical result of large herds, yet it also made room between Esau's household and Jacob's in the land God had promised to Jacob's descendants. Genesis 36:31 adds that Edom had kings "before any king reigned over the children of Israel."

From this it is fair to draw an implication. Esau's line gained early political order and territory, while Jacob's family would later go down into Egypt and wait generations for its inheritance. Genesis does not date each chief's rule. Jaalam stands among the early, visible successes of a family that lived outside the line of promise. That prosperity matches Isaac's words to Esau in Genesis 27:39–40.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No direct connection between Jaalam and Christ is established. The clans he helped found belong to Edom, however, and Edom's role in Scripture develops over time. At the Red Sea, Moses sang that "the chiefs of Edom will be dismayed" (Exodus 15:15). Later prophets spoke of Edom's pride and judgment (Obadiah). Isaiah 63:1–6 pictures the Lord coming from Edom having trodden the winepress, an image Revelation 19:15 echoes for the returning Christ. Those passages concern the nation, not Jaalam himself, but his genealogy marks part of where that story began.

5. Spirit & Life

Jaalam's line received its portion early, in chiefs and kings, and Scripture records nothing further of his clan. Jacob's line waited, suffered, and inherited by promise. God does not measure His purposes by how fast a family rises. He keeps His word on His own timetable, and the slower line in Genesis was the one through which blessing came to the nations.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:2, 5 – Jaalam's mother and his birth in Canaan.

  • Genesis 36:6–8 – Esau's household moves to Mount Seir.

  • Genesis 36:14, 18 – Jaalam among Aholibamah's sons and listed as a chief.

  • Genesis 36:31 – Edom's kings before Israel's.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:35 – Jaalam in the Chronicler's genealogy.

  • Exodus 15:15 – The chiefs of Edom dismayed at God's deliverance.
