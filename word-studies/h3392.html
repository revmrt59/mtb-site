JERAH - (Yerach, Strong's H3392)

Summary

Jerah was a son of Joktan in the line of Shem. His name appears only twice in Scripture, both times in a list of Joktan's thirteen sons (Genesis 10:26; 1 Chronicles 1:20). The name means "moon" or "month." In form it is the same as the Hebrew word for "month," and it is closely related to the word for "moon." Jerah is best understood as both a man and the ancestor of a people. Genesis places Joktan's descendants in the lands "from Mesha as you go toward Sephar, the mountain of the east" (Genesis 10:30), a region usually located in southern Arabia.

Scripture records no deeds, words, or encounters with God for Jerah. His place in the Table of Nations still matters. Genesis traces the far-flung peoples of Arabia back to Noah's family and keeps them inside God's account of the human race. That is the world God set out to bless through Abraham in the very next movement of the story.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Jerah is the fourth listed son of Joktan, who descended from Shem through Arphaxad, Salah, and Eber (Genesis 10:22–26). His brothers included Sheba, Ophir, and Havilah (Genesis 10:28–29). Like many names in this table, Jerah probably refers both to an individual ancestor and to the tribe that came from him.

The Hebrew name yerach means "moon" or "month." It matches the common Hebrew word for "month" and shares a root with the word for "moon." The text offers no explanation of why he received this name and builds no wordplay on it.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Jerah has no narrative in Scripture. Genesis 10 lists him as part of the spread of Shem's descendants. The Chronicler repeated the list word for word centuries later (1 Chronicles 1:20). The Joktanite peoples lived between Mesha and Sephar (Genesis 10:30). Most interpreters place this territory in southern Arabia, although its exact boundaries are uncertain.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Genesis states that Jerah belonged to Joktan's family, which was a branch of Eber's line separate from Peleg's line, the line that leads to Abraham (Genesis 10:25; 11:18–26). From this placement we can draw a careful conclusion. Even a people remembered by a single name was counted in God's record of the nations. Paul later declared that God "has made from one blood every nation of men to dwell on all the face of the earth, and has determined their preappointed times and the boundaries of their dwellings" (Acts 17:26). The Chronicler also kept Jerah's name in his list, and he opened Israel's history with the whole human family.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No later Scripture mentions Jerah. However, the nations listed in Genesis 10 form the backdrop for God's promise that all the families of the earth would be blessed in Abraham (Genesis 12:3). That promise reaches its fulfillment in Christ. Jerah's descendants belong among those families.

5. Spirit & Life

Genesis 1:16 presents the moon as the "lesser light," a created thing that God made. Moses later warned Israel against worshiping the sun, moon, or stars (Deuteronomy 4:19). These passages give the wider biblical setting for a name meaning "moon"; Genesis 10 does not use Jerah's name to teach a lesson against idolatry. Its contribution is quieter: God keeps account of peoples whom history barely remembers.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 10:26–30 – Lists Jerah among Joktan's sons and describes where their descendants settled.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:20 – Preserves the same genealogy for the post-exilic community.

  • Genesis 1:16 – Presents the moon as a created light.

  • Genesis 12:3 – Promises blessing to all the families of the earth.

  • Acts 17:26 – Teaches that God made all nations from one blood and set their boundaries.
