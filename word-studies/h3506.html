ITHRAN - (Yithrân, Strong's H3506)

Summary

Ithran is the name of two different men in the Old Testament, and they should not be confused. The first is a Horite, a son of Dishon (Genesis 36:26; 1 Chronicles 1:41) among "the sons of Seir the Horite who inhabited the land" (Genesis 36:20). The Horites lived in the mountains of Seir before the descendants of Esau took the region, which came to be called Edom (Deuteronomy 2:12). The second is an Israelite of the tribe of Asher, a son of Zophah. The NKJV spells his name "Jithran" (1 Chronicles 7:37). The name comes from the Hebrew root yathar, "to remain over" or "to excel," and is generally understood to mean "excellent" or "abundance."

Neither man does anything that Scripture records. Both appear only in family lists. Still, those lists carry weight. The Horite Ithran belongs to a people whose loss of their land Moses later pointed to when he spoke of the land the LORD gave Israel (Deuteronomy 2:12). The Asherite Ithran stands in a line the Chronicler honors as "choice men, mighty men of valor, chief leaders" (1 Chronicles 7:40). The God who rules history keeps account of the nations He moves and the families He preserves, down to names no one else remembers.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Two separate men bear this name, and the biblical text gives no reason to connect them:

  • Ithran the Horite is a son of Dishon. Genesis 36:26 lists his brothers as Hemdan, Eshban, and Cheran. The parallel in 1 Chronicles 1:41 has "Hamran" where Genesis has "Hemdan." Ithran lived among the Horite clans of Seir, the mountainous country south and east of the Dead Sea. His father's exact place in the family tree is not fully clear. Seir has a son named Dishon (Genesis 36:21), and Anah also has a child named Dishon (Genesis 36:25; 1 Chronicles 1:41). The lists do not settle which Dishon was Ithran's father, or whether the two are the same man.

  • Ithran (Jithran) the Asherite is one of the sons of Zophah, a descendant of Asher through Beriah and Heber (1 Chronicles 7:30–37).

The name comes from the Hebrew root yathar, which means "to remain over, to leave, to excel." With its -an ending, it is generally rendered "excellent" or "abundance, advantage." That is the meaning of the name itself. Scripture never comments on it, and the name should not be read as a description of either man's life.

In 1 Chronicles, the verse right after Jithran's lists "the sons of Jether" (1 Chronicles 7:38). Jether (indexed separately as H3500) comes from the same root. Many interpreters think Jithran and Jether are two forms of one man's name, with verse 38 giving his sons, Jephunneh, Pispah, and Ara. The placement of the two verses makes this a reasonable reading, but the text does not state it outright.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

The Horite. Ithran appears within the record of "the chiefs of the Horites, the sons of Seir, in the land of Edom" (Genesis 36:21). Genesis 36 sets these older Horite clans beside the descendants of Esau, showing who held Seir before Edom took it over. Moses describes how the Horites lost their land: "The Horites formerly dwelt in Seir, but the descendants of Esau dispossessed them and destroyed them from before them, and dwelt in their place" (Deuteronomy 2:12). Later in the same chapter he says the LORD Himself did this for Esau's descendants (Deuteronomy 2:22).

The Asherite. Jithran belongs to a large branch of Asher's family. Asher's tribal land lay along Canaan's northwestern coast. The Chronicler closes his list with high praise. These men were "heads of their fathers' houses, choice men, mighty men of valor, chief leaders," counted "among the army fit for battle; their number was twenty-six thousand" (1 Chronicles 7:40).

3. Significance in God's Purposes

The two men stand on opposite sides of Israel's story. The Horite Ithran belongs to a people God removed so that He could give their land to someone else. Scripture states plainly that Esau's descendants took Seir, "just as Israel did to the land of their possession which the LORD gave them" (Deuteronomy 2:12). This tells Israel that the LORD distributes lands among the nations and that what He did for Esau He would also do for Jacob's children. The Horite names in Genesis 36 are a record of real people who once held that land.

The Asherite Ithran belongs to the people God kept. Chronicles was written after the exile. By then the northern tribes, Asher among them, had long been scattered by Assyria. Even so, the Chronicler records Asher's families and warriors by name. The likely purpose is to show that every tribe still belonged to Israel and was not forgotten. Paul told the Athenians that God "has determined their preappointed times and the boundaries of their dwellings" (Acts 17:26), and these lists show that truth at work in particular families.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Scripture makes no connection between Ithran and Christ. One wider thread is worth noting, though it concerns Asher's tribe rather than Ithran himself. Asher did not disappear from God's purposes. When the infant Jesus was brought to the temple, the prophetess Anna, "of the tribe of Asher," gave thanks and spoke of Him to all who were waiting for redemption (Luke 2:36–38). Asher also appears among the tribes in Revelation 7:6.

5. Spirit & Life

All we know of either Ithran is a name in a list. Yet God chose to keep both names in His Word. One man came from a people whose time in the land ended. The other came from a tribe that history nearly erased. The Chronicler called Ithran's Asherite relatives "mighty men of valor" without telling a single story about them. Their deeds went unrecorded, but God still knew them by name. Faithfulness that no one writes down is not lost on Him.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:20–26 – Places Ithran the Horite among the sons of Seir in the land of Edom.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:41 – The Chronicler's parallel list ("Hamran" for "Hemdan").

  • Deuteronomy 2:12, 22 – The Horites lose Seir to Esau's descendants, compared with Israel's receiving of its land.

  • 1 Chronicles 7:37–40 – Jithran the Asherite, the possibly related Jether, and the description of Asher's leaders.

  • Acts 17:26 – God sets the times and boundaries of the nations.
