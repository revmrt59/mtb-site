JETHETH - (Yᵉthêth, Strong's H3509)

Summary

Jetheth was one of the chiefs of Edom, the nation descended from Esau. He appears only twice in Scripture, in the list of Edomite chiefs in Genesis 36:40 and in its repetition in 1 Chronicles 1:51. Genesis presents these chiefs "according to their families and their places," so the name Jetheth seems to stand for both a clan leader and the clan or district that carried his name somewhere in the region of Mount Seir. The meaning of the name is uncertain. Scripture gives no explanation of it, and no derivation is settled.

Scripture records nothing Jetheth said or did. His name still matters because of where it stands. He belongs to a record showing that Edom had organized leadership and settled territory "before any king reigned over the children of Israel" (Genesis 36:31). Israel would wait far longer for a king of its own; Genesis does not say exactly when Jetheth lived or how this list relates in time to Jacob's family. The Bible does not erase Esau's descendants from its pages. God gave Mount Seir to Esau (Deuteronomy 2:5), and the patient record of his chiefs shows that the Lord governs the history of every nation, not Israel's alone.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Jetheth was an Edomite chief, or alluph (Strong's H441), the title the NKJV renders "chief." He is listed third among eleven chiefs of Esau (Genesis 36:40–43). The phrase "according to their families and their places" suggests that these names identify clans and their territories as well as individual leaders. No other person or place in Scripture bears this name. The location of Jetheth's district within Edom, south and southeast of the Dead Sea, is unknown.

The meaning of the name is uncertain. Standard lexicons list the name without a settled derivation. Scripture offers no wordplay on the name, so no spiritual lesson should be drawn from any proposed meaning.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Genesis 36 moves from Esau's sons and grandsons, to the kings who reigned in Edom (36:31–39), and then to a closing list of chiefs "according to their dwelling places in the land of their possession" (36:43). Jetheth belongs to that closing list. Interpreters debate whether these chiefs succeeded the kings or governed districts alongside them. First Chronicles 1:51 places the list right after "Hadad died also," which some read as succession, but the text does not settle the question. The Chronicler's opening genealogies reach from Adam through the surrounding nations, and Edom's chiefs are still remembered there.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states that the Lord gave Mount Seir to Esau as a possession and forbade Israel to take "so much as one footstep" of it (Deuteronomy 2:4–5). Jetheth's chiefdom is part of that God-given inheritance. Genesis 36:31 also notes that Edom had kings before Israel did. From these verses we can draw a careful implication. Esau's line had organized rule well before Israel's monarchy, while the line of promise waited a long time, including its years in Egypt, for a king of its own. Genesis does not date Jetheth's own chiefdom within that span. In God's ways, being the heir of His promise does not mean arriving first.

The same title connects Edom's chiefs to Israel's redemption. At the Red Sea Moses sang, "Then the chiefs of Edom will be dismayed" (Exodus 15:15). The settled power of these leaders could not stand against the God who rescued a nation of slaves.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Scripture establishes no direct connection between Jetheth and Christ. A broad thematic resonance, not a claim that the two were contemporaries, appears in the contrast between Edom's chiefs, recorded "according to their families and their places" (Genesis 36:40), and the patriarchs, who lived in tents "with Isaac and Jacob, the heirs with him of the same promise," because Abraham "waited for the city which has foundations, whose builder and maker is God" (Hebrews 11:9–10). The prophets later announced judgment on Edom's pride and violence (Obadiah; Jeremiah 49; Ezekiel 25).

5. Spirit & Life

A name nearly lost in a list still testifies that God sees the nations, gives them their lands, and holds them accountable. Edom's earlier organized rule did not overturn His purpose for Jacob, and visible advantage today does not overturn His promises to those who wait for Him.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:40–43 — Jetheth among the chiefs of Esau, named by families and places.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:51 — The list preserved in Israel's later record.

  • Genesis 36:31 — Edom organized before Israel had a king.

  • Deuteronomy 2:4–5 — God gave Mount Seir to Esau.

  • Exodus 15:15 — Edom's chiefs dismayed at the Exodus.

  • Hebrews 11:9–10 — The patriarchs waited for God's city.
