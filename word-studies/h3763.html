CHERAN - (Kᵉrân, Strong's H3763)

Summary

Cheran was a Horite, one of the original inhabitants of Mount Seir, the rugged highland south of the Dead Sea that later became the land of Edom. He appears only in two parallel genealogies, Genesis 36:26 and 1 Chronicles 1:41. Both name him last among the four sons of Dishon within the Horite clan genealogy; this Dishon is most naturally read as the chief who was a son of Seir. The meaning of his name is uncertain. The Horites may have spoken a non-Semitic language, possibly Hurrian, and attempts to connect the name to Hebrew words for "lyre" or "lamb" are guesses rather than established meanings.

Scripture records no words or deeds of Cheran. His family's story still matters. Moses notes that "the descendants of Esau dispossessed" the Horites and "dwelt in their place" (Deuteronomy 2:12), and he credits that change to the LORD (Deuteronomy 2:22). Cheran's name marks a real clan in a real land that God gave to Esau, and its later disappearance from the record shows that God rules over the boundaries of every nation, not only Israel's.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Cheran is a single individual, a son of Dishon among "the sons of Seir the Horite who inhabited the land" (Genesis 36:20). His brothers were Hemdan, Eshban, and Ithran (Genesis 36:26). In 1 Chronicles 1:41 the first brother is spelled Hamran, and the other names match. Cheran is listed fourth. The text gives the order of the names but does not state the order of birth. No other person in Scripture bears this name.

The meaning of Kᵉrân is uncertain. Many scholars connect the biblical Horites with the Hurrians known from ancient sources outside the Bible. If that connection holds, the name could have a non-Semitic origin, which would explain why Hebrew roots do not settle its meaning. Older reference works proposed links to words meaning "lyre" (Smith's Bible Dictionary) or "lamb" (Dillmann, as cited in the International Standard Bible Encyclopedia), but these are proposals rather than verified meanings. Genesis makes no wordplay on the name.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Genesis 36:20–30 lists the Horite chiefs of Seir. These were the people already living in the land when Esau's family settled there. A Dishon is named among Seir's sons and is called "Chief Dishon" (Genesis 36:21, 30). Genesis 36:25 also names a Dishon as a son of Anah, and 1 Chronicles 1:41 places that Anah notice immediately before Cheran's sibling list. The order of the Genesis clan list favors reading Cheran's father as the chief, the son of Seir, but the text leaves some ambiguity. Either way, Cheran belonged to the Horite ruling family. The same Horite family intermarried with Esau's line. Aholibamah, daughter of Anah, became Esau's wife (Genesis 36:2, 25).

Over time the descendants of Esau took the land from the Horites (Deuteronomy 2:12, 22). Scripture does not say when Cheran lived in relation to that change. The Chronicler later copied the Horite list again (1 Chronicles 1:38–42) as part of his survey of the nations surrounding Israel, which he places before he turns to the line of David.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states plainly that the LORD gave Mount Seir to Esau as a possession (Deuteronomy 2:5) and that He destroyed the Horites from before Esau's descendants (Deuteronomy 2:22). Moses sets this beside Israel's own experience: Esau's people did to the Horites "just as Israel did to the land of their possession which the LORD gave them" (Deuteronomy 2:12). Israel was being taught to see that God governs land and history among the nations as surely as He fulfills His promises to Israel.

The next point is an implication rather than a direct statement of the text. Cheran's clan held power in formidable mountain country, yet the Bible remembers them only as names in a list. That fact sets human dynasties in sober perspective beside the purposes of God.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Scripture does not connect Cheran to Christ or to any prophecy. His one canonical role beyond Genesis is his place in the Chronicler's record of the nations, which forms the backdrop to the line of David.

5. Spirit & Life

Cheran's name shows that God's Word is rooted in real history, with real clans, chiefs, and mountains. The LORD who gave Seir to Esau is the same God who keeps His word to His people. When Israel faced Canaan, Moses pointed to what God had already done in Seir. Believers today can also look back on God's faithful work in history as solid ground for trusting Him with what lies ahead.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:20–30: Lists the Horite chiefs of Seir and names Cheran among Dishon's sons (v. 26).

  • 1 Chronicles 1:41: Repeats the record in Israel's later survey of the nations.

  • Deuteronomy 2:5, 12, 22: State that the LORD gave Seir to Esau and that the Horites were dispossessed.

  • Genesis 36:2, 25: Record the marriage that linked Esau to this Horite family.
