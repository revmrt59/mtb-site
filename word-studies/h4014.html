MIBZAR - (Mibtsâr, Strong's H4014)

Summary

Mibzar was one of the chiefs of Edom, the nation descended from Esau. He is named only twice, in the closing list of Edomite chiefs in Genesis 36:42 and again in the Chronicler's copy of that list in 1 Chronicles 1:53. The name means "fortress" or "fortified place." It is the same word Hebrew uses for a stronghold or walled city. Genesis says these chiefs were listed "according to their families and their places" (Genesis 36:40), so Mibzar probably names both a clan leader and the territory or stronghold his family held in the rugged country of Seir.

Scripture tells us nothing about Mibzar's deeds. His place in the record still matters. Genesis sets Edom's chiefs and kings beside Jacob's family, still a household of sojourners (Genesis 37:1). Joshua later summarizes the contrast: God gave Esau Mount Seir, "but Jacob and his children went down to Egypt" (Joshua 24:4). Esau's line came to have territory, rulers, and fortified places, though Genesis does not date Mibzar's own rule. Later prophets warned Edom that its high, rocky refuges had made it proud and could not save it (Obadiah 1:3; Jeremiah 49:16). A chief called "Fortress" fits that picture well. The rest of Scripture points God's people to a different refuge: "The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer" (Psalm 18:2).

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Mibzar is an Edomite chief (Hebrew 'alluph, rendered "Chief" in the NKJV and "duke" in the KJV) in the line of Esau. He appears ninth in the eleven-name list of Genesis 36:40–43, between Chief Teman and Chief Magdiel. First Chronicles 1:53 repeats that list unchanged. He is the only figure under this Strong's entry.

The name is spelled the same as the common Hebrew noun mibtsar (H4013), meaning "fortress," "fortification," or "fortified city." That noun comes from the verb batsar (H1219), which carries the idea of cutting off, enclosing, or making inaccessible. A mibtsar is a place made hard to reach and hard to take. The meaning "fortress" is therefore well supported.

We cannot say whether the name first belonged to a man and later passed to a stronghold, or whether a clan took its name from the fortified place it held. Genesis frames the list "according to their families and their places" (36:40) and "according to their dwelling places in the land of their possession" (36:43). Several other names in the list, such as Teman and Timnah, are also known as places. So Mibzar most likely identifies a family and its territory together. Proposals that link Mibzar with a known Edomite site such as Bozrah or Sela (itself only debatably identified with Petra) remain unproven, and a chief named "Fortress" does not by itself prove a particular fortification existed under his rule.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Mibzar belongs to the record of Esau's descendants in Mount Seir, the mountainous region south and east of the Dead Sea. Scripture says the children of Esau drove out the Horites who had lived there (Deuteronomy 2:12, 22). It also says God Himself gave Seir to Esau as his possession (Deuteronomy 2:5; Joshua 24:4). Genesis 36 traces Esau's sons and grandsons, the Horite clans, a line of kings who reigned in Edom "before any king reigned over the children of Israel" (36:31), and finally the chiefs listed by their places (36:40–43). Mibzar is named there with no story attached.

Centuries later the Chronicler repeated the same list, with minor spelling variants such as Alvah/Aliah, in his opening genealogies (1 Chronicles 1:53). The people returning from exile were reading the history of the nations around them, and that history included Edom, the brother nation that had often been a bitter rival.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states plainly that Esau's line became a settled people with territory, chiefs, and kings (Genesis 36:31, 40–43), and that God gave them their land (Deuteronomy 2:5). God kept His word to Esau as surely as He kept it to Jacob.

The placement of the list also invites a comparison, and this part is an inference from the arrangement of Genesis. Right after Edom's chiefs are listed by their dwelling places, Genesis turns to Jacob, who lived in the land where his father had been a stranger (Genesis 37:1). Edom looked established, with a chief named "Fortress" among its leaders. The family that carried God's promise owned almost nothing and would later go down into Egypt (Joshua 24:4). The arrangement is a literary comparison; it does not show that Mibzar ruled during Jacob's lifetime or how quickly Edom became secure. Visible security belonged to Esau's line. God's purposes for Jacob's line moved through weakness, delay, and deliverance.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No New Testament or prophetic passage mentions Mibzar, and no type of Christ is found in him. His name does connect with a theme the prophets develop. Edom trusted in its mountain strongholds. Obadiah charges, "The pride of your heart has deceived you, You who dwell in the clefts of the rock, Whose habitation is high" (Obadiah 1:3). Through Jeremiah the LORD answers that same confidence: "Though you make your nest as high as the eagle, I will bring you down from there" (Jeremiah 49:16). These verses do not use the word mibtsar. They describe the same kind of rocky, high refuge that the name calls to mind.

Against that background the psalmist calls the LORD Himself "my fortress" (Psalm 18:2). Hebrews makes a related contrast, though not with Edom. Abraham lived in tents in the land of promise and waited for a city whose builder and maker is God (Hebrews 11:9–10). The patriarchs gave up visible security for a promise. In Christ, believers are heirs of that same hope.

5. Spirit & Life

Genesis 36 can read like a record of the world getting ahead. Esau's family is listed with chiefs and kings and fortified places, while Jacob's family is still waiting. God's people have often felt that tension. Scripture does not hide Edom's success, and it does not treat that success as the last word. The prophets show where Edom's confidence led: its pride deceived it, and the LORD brought it down.

The name Mibzar is not a moral lesson about the man, of whom we know nothing. It does mark a contrast that runs through the whole Bible. A fortress built in the rock can feel unreachable, but God can reach it. The safe place is the one David names: "The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer; My God, my strength, in whom I will trust" (Psalm 18:2).

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:40–43 — Lists Mibzar among Esau's chiefs, arranged by families, places, and dwelling places. This is the basis for treating the name as both a clan and a territory.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:53 — The Chronicler's repetition of the list, kept for the post-exilic community.

  • Genesis 36:31; Deuteronomy 2:5, 12, 22 — Edom's early kings, God's gift of Seir to Esau, and the displacement of the Horites.

  • Genesis 37:1 — Jacob still a sojourner, set right after Edom's settled chiefs.

  • Obadiah 1:3; Jeremiah 49:16 — Prophetic judgment on Edom's pride in its high, rocky refuges.

  • Psalm 18:2 — The LORD as His people's true fortress and stronghold.
