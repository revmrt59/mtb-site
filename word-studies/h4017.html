MIBSAM - (Mibsâm, Strong's H4017)

Summary

Mibsam is the name of two different men in the Old Testament genealogies. The first, and better known, is the fourth-listed son of Ishmael, Abraham's son by Hagar. He is listed among the twelve sons who became tribal chiefs in the Arabian wilderness (Genesis 25:13; 1 Chronicles 1:29). The second is a descendant of Simeon, the son of Shallum and father of Mishma, whose family lived on Israel's southern frontier (1 Chronicles 4:25). The name is generally understood to mean "sweet odor" or "fragrance." It is related to the Hebrew noun for spice or balsam, which suits a people later known in Genesis for carrying spices by caravan.

Mibsam has no recorded words or deeds, but his place in Ishmael's line is not trivial. Years earlier God had told Abraham that Ishmael "shall beget twelve princes" (Genesis 17:20), and Genesis 25:16 closes Ishmael's family list by counting exactly "twelve princes according to their nations." Mibsam is one of the names that make that count. Even for the son through whom Isaac's specific covenant line would not pass, God did precisely what He said. The Simeonite Mibsam adds a quieter discovery. The same two names, Mibsam and Mishma, appear in both lists: as consecutive brothers among Ishmael's sons, and as father and son in Simeon's line. One possible explanation is that Israel's desert border was a place of real contact between Simeon's families and the tribes of Ishmael.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Two separate individuals bear this name, and they should not be merged. Mibsam of Ishmael is the fourth-named son of Ishmael, after Nebajoth, Kedar, and Adbeel (Genesis 25:13; 1 Chronicles 1:29). He belongs to the patriarchal period. Both he and the tribal group descended from him stood among Ishmael's twelve princes. Mibsam of Simeon appears in the Simeonite genealogy as "Shallum his son, Mibsam his son, and Mishma his son" (1 Chronicles 4:25). The line seems to run from Shaul, one of Simeon's sons (1 Chronicles 4:24), through Shallum. Like many Chronicles genealogies, this list may compress generations, and the text gives no date for his life.

The name shares its root with the Hebrew noun besem or bosem (H1314), the ordinary word for spice, balsam, or sweet fragrance, so "sweet odor" or "fragrance" is a fair rendering. The Bible offers no wordplay on the name and no explanation of why either man received it. For Ishmael's son, the fit with the later Ishmaelite spice trade is suggestive (see Genesis 37:25). It remains an observation about the setting, not a stated reason for the name.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Mibsam son of Ishmael. Genesis 25:12 introduces "the genealogy of Ishmael, Abraham's son, whom Hagar the Egyptian, Sarah's maidservant, bore to Abraham." Mibsam stands fourth in the list, followed by his brother Mishma (25:14). Genesis 25:16 describes the twelve sons "by their towns and their settlements," so each name marks a man and also the clan that grew from him. Ishmael's descendants lived in the desert country between Havilah and Shur, toward Egypt (Genesis 25:18). Later in Genesis, a caravan of Ishmaelites passes through Canaan from Gilead toward Egypt carrying spices, balm, and myrrh, and it is to these traders that Joseph is sold (Genesis 37:25-28). The Chronicler repeats Ishmael's sons with Mibsam in the same place (1 Chronicles 1:29). Israel kept this record of Abraham's other descendants long after the patriarchal age.

Mibsam of Simeon. In 1 Chronicles 4, Mibsam is a link in the line of Simeon. Through his son Mishma came Shimei, who had many children, though the chapter notes that Simeon as a whole did not grow as large as Judah (1 Chronicles 4:26-27). Simeon's inheritance lay inside Judah's territory in the far south, around Beersheba, Hormah, and Ziklag (Joshua 19:1-9). That put them next to the wilderness regions associated with Ishmael's tribes. The chapter later tells how some Simeonites moved out to new pastureland and to Mount Seir, where they struck down the remaining Amalekites (1 Chronicles 4:39-43).

The pairing of names is striking. Mibsam and Mishma appear back to back among Ishmael's sons (Genesis 25:13-14), and again as father and son in Simeon's line (1 Chronicles 4:25). Scripture does not explain the overlap. Given where Simeon lived, one possible explanation is that frontier Israelites and neighboring desert peoples lived close together and shared names through contact or intermarriage. The names may also simply come from a common stock of names. Either way, this is an inference rather than a stated fact.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states plainly that God promised twelve princes from Ishmael (Genesis 17:20) and that Ishmael's sons numbered "twelve princes according to their nations" (Genesis 25:16). Mibsam is part of the evidence that God kept that word. In the same conversation, God made clear that His covenant would be established with Isaac (Genesis 17:21). Ishmael's blessing was real, but it was a different blessing. The genealogy shows that God's choice of Isaac did not make Him careless toward Ishmael. He blessed the son outside the chosen line exactly as He said He would.

For the Simeonite Mibsam, the significance is more modest. His name helps preserve the identity of a smaller tribe in the record of all Israel. The likely echo of Ishmaelite names also shows that Israel's history unfolded in a real world of neighbors and borders, not in isolation.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Neither Mibsam appears in later prophecy or the New Testament, and no direct connection to Christ is established. The name's meaning, "fragrance," should not be turned into a picture of Christ. The value lies in the larger story: the God who kept His smaller promise about Ishmael is the same God who carried His promise through Isaac to its fulfillment in Christ.

5. Spirit & Life

Genesis could have dismissed Ishmael in a sentence. Instead it names his sons one by one and counts them, so the reader can see that God's word to a grieving father had come true. God had "heard" Abraham concerning Ishmael (Genesis 17:20), and the list of names is the answer to that prayer. If God was this exact about the temporal flourishing of a line outside the promise, His people can trust Him fully with the promises that concern their salvation. Nothing God says is left to fall to the ground.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 17:20 – God promises that Ishmael will father twelve princes.

  • Genesis 25:12-16 – Ishmael's genealogy names Mibsam fourth and confirms the twelve princes.

  • Genesis 37:25-28 – An Ishmaelite caravan carries spices to Egypt, which shows the trading setting.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:29 – The Chronicler repeats Mibsam among Ishmael's sons.

  • 1 Chronicles 4:24-27 – The Simeonite Mibsam, son of Shallum and father of Mishma.

  • Joshua 19:1-9 – Simeon's southern inheritance within Judah, bordering the desert.
