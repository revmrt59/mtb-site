MAGDIEL - (Magdîyʼêl, Strong's H4025)

Summary

Magdiel was one of the chiefs of Edom, the nation descended from Esau, Jacob's twin brother. His name appears only twice in Scripture: in the closing roster of Edomite chiefs in Genesis 36:43 and in the Chronicler's retelling of that list in 1 Chronicles 1:54. The name is a compound of a Hebrew word for "excellence" or "choice thing" and El, "God," so it most likely means "choice gift of God" or "preciousness of God." Scripture tells us nothing of his personal life. He appears simply as "Chief Magdiel," the tenth of eleven chiefs listed "according to their families and their places."

Even so, the list he belongs to carries real weight. Genesis records that Esau's line came to have settled territory, chiefs, and even kings before Israel had a king, and it sets that record beside Jacob's family, still a household of sojourners waiting on a promise (Genesis 37:1). Genesis does not date Magdiel's own rule. Centuries later, after Israel's exile, the Chronicler copied Magdiel's name again. God keeps an accurate record of the nations outside the chosen line. He gave Esau his land, and He has not forgotten the men who ruled it.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Magdiel is the only person in Scripture with this name. He was an 'alluph, a chief or clan leader of Edom, in the territory around Mount Seir south of the Dead Sea. Genesis presents the final chiefs "according to their families and their places, by their names" (Genesis 36:40). That wording leaves open whether "Magdiel" names an individual leader, a clan, or the district that clan held. The names may serve all three purposes at once.

The name joins meged, a word for excellence or choice things that the Old Testament often uses for the rich gifts of land and harvest, with El, the common Semitic word for God. The resulting meaning, "choice gift of God" or "preciousness of God," is supported by Strong's and the NAS lexicon; Brown-Driver-Briggs lists the name without that derivation. The text itself makes no wordplay on the name.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Magdiel has no narrative of his own. He stands near the end of Genesis 36, which follows Esau's family from his sons, through the Horite clans of Seir and a line of kings who reigned in Edom "before any king reigned over the children of Israel" (Genesis 36:31, paraphrased), to a closing list of chiefs. That list ends: "Chief Magdiel, and Chief Iram. These were the chiefs of Edom, according to their dwelling places in the land of their possession. Esau was the father of the Edomites" (Genesis 36:43).

The Chronicler reproduces the list after the Edomite kings: "Chief Magdiel, and Chief Iram. These were the chiefs of Edom" (1 Chronicles 1:54). He places Israel within the wider family of nations before he narrows his focus to Judah and David.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states that Edom held "the land of their possession" (Genesis 36:43). Deuteronomy explains that this was God's own doing. He gave Mount Seir to Esau and forbade Israel to take it (Deuteronomy 2:4–5). Isaac had foretold an earthly portion for Esau apart from Jacob's blessing (Genesis 27:39–40), and chiefs like Magdiel show that word coming true.

The contrast with Jacob's line is drawn by the arrangement of Genesis itself. Edom's settled chiefs are listed just before Genesis returns to Jacob, still a sojourner, whose family would later be enslaved in Egypt. The narrative order invites the comparison, though it does not show that Magdiel ruled during Jacob's lifetime. It is a fair inference that Genesis wants readers to notice the contrast. Early worldly establishment is not the same as being the bearer of God's promise. Romans 9:10–13 confirms that the line of promise came through Jacob by God's choice, "not of works but of Him who calls."

The name includes El, a common Semitic word for God, as does the name of Esau's son Reuel (Genesis 36:4). That shows religious naming in the family, but El by itself does not identify which deity was meant or prove that the family kept the memory of the LORD. It does not tell us anything about Magdiel's personal faith.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No direct connection to Christ is made through Magdiel himself. The nation he helped lead, however, has a long later story. The prophets announce judgment on Edom's pride (Obadiah; Isaiah 34). Amos also foresees "the remnant of Edom" coming under the restored house of David (Amos 9:11–12), and James applies that passage to Gentiles turning to God through Christ (Acts 15:16–17). The descendants of Esau's chiefs are not beyond the reach of David's greater Son.

5. Spirit & Life

Genesis 36 can look like a list to skim. Yet God took the trouble to preserve these names twice, across centuries. Paul tells the Athenians that God "has determined their preappointed times and the boundaries of their dwellings" (Acts 17:26). Magdiel's "dwelling places" were part of that ordering. The chapter also steadies believers who watch the world settle comfortably while they still wait on God's promises. Edom had chiefs long before Israel had a king, but the promise ran through Jacob.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:40–43 – The chiefs of Esau listed by families and places. Magdiel appears in verse 43.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:54 – The Chronicler's post-exilic preservation of the list.

  • Genesis 36:31 – Edom had kings before Israel did.

  • Genesis 27:39–40; Deuteronomy 2:4–5 – Esau's portion foretold and Seir given by God.

  • Romans 9:10–13 – God's choice of Jacob over Esau.

  • Acts 17:26 – God appoints the times and boundaries of nations.

  • Amos 9:11–12; Acts 15:16–17 – Edom's remnant within the hope of David's restored kingdom.
