MAHALALEL - (Mahălalʼêl, Strong's H4111)

Summary

Mahalalel is the name of two men in the Old Testament. The better known is the patriarch of Genesis 5. He was the son of Cainan, the father of Jared, and the fifth generation from Adam through Seth. The name means "Praise of God" or "God's praise." It joins the Hebrew word for praise with El, God. Scripture records only his birth, his fatherhood, his 895 years, and his death. Yet his place in the line running from Adam to Noah matters. Luke's genealogy names him among the ancestors of Jesus (Luke 3:37), so his quiet life becomes one link in the chain God kept unbroken until His Son came into the world.

The second Mahalalel appears in Nehemiah 11:4. He was a man of Judah, from the family of Perez, and an ancestor of Athaiah, who lived in Jerusalem after the return from exile. Together the two men show something steady about God. In the generations before the Flood, and again among the families rebuilding the holy city, He preserved a people who called on His name. The patriarch's record also ends with the same words as almost every life in Genesis 5: "and he died." Long life and a worshipful name could not undo the curse. Only the descendant Luke traces back through Mahalalel could do that.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

The Hebrew name Mahălalʼêl belongs to two separate individuals. The two should not be merged.

Mahalalel the patriarch (Genesis 5:12–17; 1 Chronicles 1:2) was born to Cainan when Cainan was seventy. He was the great-grandson of Seth and the grandfather of Enoch, who walked with God (Genesis 5:21–24). He lived before the Flood. Luke 3:37 names him in the ancestry of Jesus. The Greek form there is indexed separately (G3121), and the NKJV renders it "Mahalalel."

Mahalalel of Judah (Nehemiah 11:4) was a descendant of Perez, the son of Judah. Nehemiah lists him five generations above Athaiah, who was living in Jerusalem in Nehemiah's day. The text does not say when this Mahalalel himself lived. It only places him in Athaiah's family line.

The name means "Praise of God" or "God's praise." It is a compound of mahalal, "praise," from the root halal, meaning to praise or to boast, and El, "God." This meaning is well supported. The text itself makes no wordplay on the name, and it does not explain why Cainan chose it.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

The patriarch. Genesis 5 follows a fixed pattern for each man. It gives his age when his named son was born, the years he lived afterward with "sons and daughters," his total years, and then the phrase "and he died." Mahalalel fits that pattern exactly. He "lived sixty-five years, and begot Jared," then "lived eight hundred and thirty years, and had sons and daughters," and "all the days of Mahalalel were eight hundred and ninety-five years; and he died" (Genesis 5:15–17).

His generation stands between two important moments. In the days of his grandfather Enosh, "men began to call on the name of the LORD" (Genesis 4:26). Two generations after Mahalalel came his grandson Enoch, who walked with God and was taken (Genesis 5:24). If Genesis 5's Hebrew figures are added up, Mahalalel's life would also have overlapped Adam's by several centuries. First Chronicles 1:2 later repeats his name in a bare list, "Cainan, Mahalalel, Jared." That list ties Israel's returned exiles to the very beginning of the human story.

The Judahite. Nehemiah 11 records the families who lived in Jerusalem as the city was repopulated after the exile. Among "the children of Judah" is "Athaiah the son of Uzziah, the son of Zechariah, the son of Amariah, the son of Shephatiah, the son of Mahalalel, of the children of Perez" (Nehemiah 11:4). Beyond his place in that line, nothing more is said of him.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states plainly that Mahalalel belonged to the line of Seth, the son given in place of Abel (Genesis 4:25). That line runs through Enoch to Noah and is the one Luke follows to Christ. From this we can draw a careful conclusion. God was preserving a family line through a world that grew more violent and corrupt, so that the promise of Genesis 3:15 would not fail.

The name itself invites a second observation, though it is an inference and not a statement of the text. Genesis 4 and 5 set two families side by side. In Cain's line, Lamech boasts to his wives about the man he killed (Genesis 4:23–24). In Seth's line, people call on the LORD's name, and a son is named "Praise of God." The name tells us nothing directly about Mahalalel's character. It does suggest that his family remembered whom they belonged to.

Nehemiah's Mahalalel shows the same faithfulness at another point in history. Long after the Flood, and after Jerusalem's fall, a family of Judah still traced itself through a man bearing that ancient name. That family took its place in the restored city of worship.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Luke 3 makes the connection explicit: "the son of Jared, the son of Mahalalel, the son of Cainan" (Luke 3:37). Luke continues past Abraham and David, back through "the son of Enosh, the son of Seth, the son of Adam, the son of God" (Luke 3:38). Matthew traces Jesus from Abraham. Luke carries His ancestry all the way to the first man. Jesus belongs to the whole human family, and that includes the generations before the Flood.

Read in light of Paul's teaching on Adam and Christ (Romans 5:12–21; 1 Corinthians 15:21–22), the connection gains force. Mahalalel inherited Adam's death, and Genesis says so without softening it. The descendant who came through his line inherited his humanity. He then broke death's hold by rising from the dead.

5. Spirit & Life

Genesis 5 can read like a list to be skimmed, but it repeats a sober truth on purpose. Mahalalel lived nearly nine centuries. He raised sons and daughters and carried a name full of praise. Still the record closes, "and he died." Neither a long life nor a godly heritage could save him from the curse. Death reached the worshiping family too. The gospel answers this with a Person rather than a longer life, the Son of God who entered Mahalalel's line and conquered the grave.

The chapter also shows how God works. Apart from God's taking of Enoch, Genesis 5 records mostly births, years and deaths, and Mahalalel's own notice records nothing more. Yet His promise was moving forward the whole time, carried by ordinary people whose names He did not forget. Families who honor the LORD today are doing the same quiet work. By how they live, and even by what they name their children, they pass on the confession that God is worthy of praise. God keeps those records, and He brings them to their goal in Christ.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 4:25–26 – Seth's line begins, and people begin to call on the LORD's name. This is the setting for Mahalalel's family.

  • Genesis 5:12–17 – The primary record of the patriarch: his birth, his son Jared, his 895 years, and his death.

  • Genesis 5:21–24 – His grandson Enoch walks with God, showing how the line develops.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:2 – The post-exilic genealogy keeps Mahalalel in Israel's record of the human story from Adam.

  • Nehemiah 11:4 – The separate Judahite Mahalalel, ancestor of Athaiah, who lived in restored Jerusalem.

  • Luke 3:37–38 – Mahalalel is named in the ancestry of Jesus, which Luke traces back to Adam and to God.
