MIZZAH - (Mizzâh, Strong's H4199)

Summary

Mizzah was a grandson of Esau, the fourth-listed son of Reuel and Basemath's grandson, and he became one of the chiefs of Edom. His name appears only three times, all in family records (Genesis 36:13, 17; 1 Chronicles 1:37). The meaning of the name is uncertain. Strong's tentatively connects it to an unused root meaning to faint with fear, but the evidence is too thin to build anything on, and it may simply have been an old Edomite clan or place name.

Mizzah's place in Scripture is small but telling. Through his grandmother Basemath, whom Genesis 36:3 calls "Ishmael's daughter," his family joins the lines of Esau and Ishmael, two branches of Abraham's family (Ishmael a son, Esau a grandson) that stood outside the chosen line of Isaac and Jacob. Mizzah's clan came to hold a recognized title and territory in Edom, and Genesis records Edom's kings before Israel crowned a king, though it does not date Mizzah's own chiefdom. His name is quiet evidence that God kept His promises to Esau, and a reminder that early visible success does not reveal where God's saving purposes are moving.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Mizzah is a single individual. He is the son of Reuel, Esau's son by Basemath, and he gave his name to an Edomite clan. His lineage runs Abraham, Isaac, Esau, Reuel, Mizzah. On his grandmother's side it runs Abraham, Ishmael, Basemath, Reuel, Mizzah. No other person in Scripture bears the name.

The meaning of the Hebrew name is uncertain. Strong's tentatively derives it from an unused root meaning to faint with fear. Older Bible dictionaries offered different glosses, such as "fear" (Smith), "despair" (Easton), and "strong" or "firm" (ISBE), and it may simply be an old Edomite clan or place name whose meaning has been lost. The Bible offers no wordplay on the name, and its meaning should not be read as a comment on Mizzah's life.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Genesis 36 lists Mizzah twice. The first list is a family record: "These were the sons of Reuel: Nahath, Zerah, Shammah, and Mizzah" (Genesis 36:13). The second list adds a title: "Chief Mizzah," one of "the chiefs of Reuel in the land of Edom" (Genesis 36:17). The change from son to chief marks the movement from a household to a nation. Esau's family had settled in the region of Mount Seir, southeast of the Dead Sea, and organized itself into clans with recognized leaders. Whether "Chief Mizzah" refers to the man himself holding office or to the clan descended from him is not stated, and ancient genealogies often used a name in both ways. The exact location of Mizzah's territory within Edom is unknown.

After the exile, the Chronicler repeats the family list in his survey of the nations that came before David's line (1 Chronicles 1:37). Israel kept careful records of its relatives, even those outside the chosen line.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states that Mizzah descended from Esau and Basemath and that he became a chief in Edom. From this it can fairly be drawn that God had fulfilled His word. He told Rebekah that two nations were in her womb (Genesis 25:23). He promised that Ishmael would become a great nation (Genesis 17:20), and Isaac's blessing gave Esau a life of his own apart from Jacob (Genesis 27:39-40). Mizzah's clan is part of that fulfillment.

The chapter also sets up a tension Genesis does not explain away: "Now these were the kings who reigned in the land of Edom before any king reigned over the children of Israel" (Genesis 36:31). Genesis sets Edom's titled chiefs beside Jacob's family, still sojourners who held only small plots such as the field at Shechem (Genesis 33:19) and would later go down to Egypt and slavery. The juxtaposition is literary; Genesis does not date Mizzah's chiefdom against Jacob's life. The promised line did not look like the winning line. Later prophets show Edom becoming Israel's persistent enemy and coming under judgment (Obadiah; Malachi 1:2-3), but that history belongs to the nation as a whole, not to Mizzah personally.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No connection between Mizzah and Christ is established. His importance to the larger story is that Genesis records Esau's line, settles it in Seir, and then turns to "the history of Jacob" (Genesis 37:2), the line through which the promised Seed would come.

5. Spirit & Life

God's attention reaches the families outside Israel's story. He named them, kept His word to them, and recorded their leaders. Yet Edom's titles and territory came before Israel had a king, while the line of promise waited and suffered. Prosperity did not prove where God's saving purpose lay. Believers who see others flourish while they wait on God can remember that God's timing in Genesis was not measured by who held land first.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:13 names Mizzah as Reuel's son and Basemath's descendant.

  • Genesis 36:3 identifies Basemath as Ishmael's daughter and links the two lines.

  • Genesis 36:17 gives him the title of chief in Edom.

  • Genesis 36:31 shows Edom's rulers established before Israel had kings.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:37 shows that Israel preserved the record after the exile.
