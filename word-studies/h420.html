ELDAAH - (ʼEldâʻâh, Strong's H0420)

Summary

Eldaah was a grandson of Abraham through Keturah. He was the last of the five sons of Midian listed in Genesis 25:4 and again in 1 Chronicles 1:33. His name joins El, "God," with a second element whose root is debated. It probably means "God has known," though some scholars read it as "God has called" or "God of knowledge." No other person in Scripture bears this name, and he does nothing in the biblical record beyond appearing in these two family lists.

Eldaah's place in those lists still carries meaning. He belongs to the sons whom Abraham provided for with gifts and then "sent... eastward, away from Isaac his son" (Genesis 25:6). His life shows that God truly multiplied Abraham into many peoples, and it also shows that the promised inheritance passed through Isaac alone. Later prophecy pictures Midian, Eldaah's family line, coming to "proclaim the praises of the LORD" (Isaiah 60:6). The sons who were sent away were not forgotten.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Eldaah is an individual person, the fifth-listed son of Midian. Midian was the fourth-listed son of Abraham by Keturah (Genesis 25:1–2). Eldaah's brothers were Ephah, Epher, Hanoch, and Abidah (spelled Abida in Chronicles). He lived in the patriarchal period. As a son of Midian, he is counted among the ancestors of the Midianite clans.

The name is a compound built on El, the common Semitic word for God. The second element is uncertain. Many connect it to the Hebrew root for "know," which gives "God has known" or "God of knowledge." Others point to an Arabic cognate meaning "to call," which gives "God has called." "God has known" is the more commonly cited meaning. Scripture itself offers no wordplay or explanation of the name.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Eldaah appears only in genealogy. His story is therefore the shared story of Keturah's descendants. Genesis 25 records that "Abraham gave all that he had to Isaac" (25:5). To the sons of his concubines he gave gifts, and while he was still living he sent them "to the country of the east" (25:6). Eldaah's family thus settled east of Canaan, in the region later associated with Midian. The Chronicler later preserves the same list (1 Chronicles 1:33) as part of the record of Abraham's descendants. Eldaah's clan never appears again by name. It was likely absorbed under the broader name of Midian.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states plainly that Keturah's sons were Abraham's offspring, so Eldaah is part of God's promise that Abraham would become the father of many nations (Genesis 17:4). Scripture also states that the inheritance went to Isaac while the other sons received gifts. From this we can draw a careful implication: being descended from Abraham did not by itself make someone an heir of the promise. Paul develops this same principle in Romans 9:6–9 and Galatians 4:21–31, though he names Isaac and Ishmael rather than Keturah's sons.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Isaiah 60:6 envisions "the dromedaries of Midian and Ephah" coming to Zion to "proclaim the praises of the LORD." Eldaah himself is not named there, but his father and his brother Ephah are. The prophecy reaches the very family line that was once sent east, away from Isaac. This fits the wider biblical hope that God's blessing through Abraham's promised Seed would reach the nations. The gospel fulfills that hope, because in Christ Gentiles are counted as Abraham's children by faith (Galatians 3:7–9, 29). Isaiah 60 is not presented here as a prophecy about Eldaah's clan in particular. It is a hope that embraces Midian as a whole.

5. Spirit & Life

Eldaah's name is uncertain in meaning, and we should not build too much on it. The narrative around him is clear, though. Abraham's generosity to the sons he sent away matches God's own way with the nations: He gives real gifts to peoples outside the line of promise. Yet gifts are not the inheritance. Isaiah's vision reminds us that God did not lose track of the peoples of the east, and He never intended to leave them out of His praise.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 25:1–4: Eldaah's parentage and place among Midian's sons.

  • Genesis 25:5–6: The distinction between Isaac's inheritance and the other sons' gifts, and the move eastward.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:33: The Chronicler's preservation of the same lineage.

  • Isaiah 60:6: Midian and Ephah pictured praising the LORD.

  • Romans 9:6–9; Galatians 3:7–9, 29: Paul's teaching on the children of promise and the blessing of the nations.
