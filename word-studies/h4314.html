MEZAHAB - (Mêy Zâhâb, Strong's H4314)

Summary

Mezahab is named only in the royal record of Edom, as the ancestor of Mehetabel, wife of Hadar (called Hadad in Chronicles), the eighth king listed in Edom (Genesis 36:39; 1 Chronicles 1:50). His name means "waters of gold," a Hebrew compound of the words for "waters" and "gold." Scripture never explains why he bore the name, and it records nothing he did. He appears in Edom's king-list, which Genesis introduces as the kings "who reigned in the land of Edom before any king reigned over the children of Israel" (Genesis 36:31).

That framing gives this small name its setting. Esau's descendants had kings, cities, and court families while Jacob's family was still waiting for the kings God had promised them (Genesis 35:11). God kept Edom's record accurately and in order, yet His saving purpose moved forward through the slower line of Jacob.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Mezahab is a single individual, and no one else in Scripture bears the name. He is linked to the household of Hadar/Hadad, king of Edom, through his descendant Mehetabel. The most natural reading of "Mehetabel, the daughter of Matred, the daughter of Mezahab" is that Matred was Mezahab's daughter and Mehetabel's mother, which would make Mezahab Mehetabel's maternal grandfather. Hebrew genealogies sometimes use "daughter" for a more distant descendant, however, so the exact link should not be pressed. Scripture does not say whether he was a native Edomite or came from a family absorbed into Edom.

The name joins mêy ("waters of") and zâhâb ("gold"), giving the meaning "waters of gold." The text does not explain it. The International Standard Bible Encyclopedia reports a proposal that it began as a place name, and later interpreters cited in McClintock and Strong's Cyclopaedia took it to describe a trade such as gold refining. These are conjectures. The biblical wording treats Mezahab as a personal ancestor, and his name tells us nothing certain about his wealth or character.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Genesis 36:31–39 lists eight kings of Edom. The list runs from Bela of Dinhabah to Hadar of Pau, and no succession in it is expressly described as passing from father to son, though not every king's father is named. The record closes with a rare personal detail: the name of Hadar's wife and her ancestry, which ends with Mezahab. First Chronicles 1:50 repeats the entry with slight differences in spelling and detail ("Hadad" for "Hadar," "Pai" for "Pau," and no mention of Achbor in that verse, though he appears in 1:49). The Chronicler kept this Edomite record in the opening genealogies that lead toward Israel and David.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture plainly states that Edom had kings before Israel did (Genesis 36:31), and it states elsewhere that God gave Mount Seir to Esau: "I have given Mount Seir to Esau as a possession" (Deuteronomy 2:5). From these two statements a reasonable conclusion follows. God oversaw the rise of nations outside the chosen line and recorded them truthfully, even to a queen's grandfather. Edom's head start in kingship did not cancel His promise that kings would come from Jacob (Genesis 35:11).

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Scripture makes no direct connection between Mezahab and Christ. The broader setting does connect thematically. The kingship Jacob's line waited for came through Judah (Genesis 49:10) and David, and it is fulfilled in Jesus, the Son of David (Matthew 1:1). Edom's early kings form part of the background against which that promised kingship unfolds.

5. Spirit & Life

Edom had settled kings while Israel had a promise and a long wait. God's people often see others prosper sooner and more visibly. Genesis 36 does not hide that, and it does not treat Edom's success as the end of the story. God keeps an exact record of every nation, and He keeps His word to His people on His own schedule.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:39 – Names Mezahab as Mehetabel's ancestor in the household of King Hadar.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:50 – Repeats the entry in the Chronicler's genealogy, with minor variations.

  • Genesis 36:31 – Places the Edomite kings "before any king reigned over the children of Israel."

  • Genesis 35:11 – God's promise to Jacob that kings would come from him.

  • Deuteronomy 2:5 – God's gift of Mount Seir to Esau.
