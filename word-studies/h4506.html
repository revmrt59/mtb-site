MANAHATH - (Mânachath, Strong's H4506)

Summary

Manahath is a name the Bible uses in two ways. It belongs to a Horite man, a son of Shobal, who lived among the original inhabitants of Mount Seir before Esau's descendants took that land (Genesis 36:23; 1 Chronicles 1:40). It is also the name of a town or clan district in Israel. In Judah's genealogy, the "Manahethites" are counted among the families descended from Shobal and Salma, grandsons of Caleb son of Hezron (1 Chronicles 2:52, 54). In Benjamin's genealogy, certain families from Geba "forced them to move to Manahath" (1 Chronicles 8:6). Standard Hebrew lexicons give the name's meaning as "rest" or "resting place," from the Hebrew root for rest.

Manahath never takes center stage, but its few appearances still teach something. A Horite name and a Judahite clan name sit beside the same name, Shobal. This raises a fair question, though not a settled one: did some families with roots outside Israel become part of the tribe that would produce David and the Messiah? The Benjaminite record offers a different lesson. Families of God's people were uprooted and resettled, apparently within the land and probably before the exile, though the text gives neither date nor Manahath's exact location. The name means "rest," yet people were made to move there under pressure. That contrast points beyond any place on a map to the rest God Himself gives, which Scripture finally locates in Christ.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Manahath is the name of a Horite man and of a place or clan district in Israel. The two should be distinguished.

The first is a person. Manahath was the second-listed son of Shobal, a Horite chief and son of Seir (Genesis 36:20, 23; 1 Chronicles 1:38, 40). His brothers were Alvan (Alian in Chronicles), Ebal, Shepho (Shephi), and Onam. He belongs to the patriarchal era and to the land of Seir, which later became Edom.

The second is a place or clan district in Israel's own territory. First Chronicles 2:52 lists "half of the families of Manuhoth" among the descendants of Shobal, "the father of Kirjath Jearim." Verse 54 lists "half of the Manahethites" among the descendants of Salma, "the father of Bethlehem." These two Judahite men were sons of Hur, and Hur was the son of Caleb son of Hezron (1 Chronicles 2:19, 50–51). This Caleb should not automatically be identified with Caleb son of Jephunneh, Joshua's companion. First Chronicles 8:6 then names Manahath as the destination of families moved out of Geba in Benjamin. Many interpreters take the Manahath of 8:6 to be the home district of the Manahethites of chapter 2, since both point to the region around Judah and Benjamin. That link is reasonable, but the text does not state it. A location near modern Malha, southwest of Jerusalem, is a common scholarly proposal and is no more than that.

Two related forms appear in 1 Chronicles 2. "Manuhoth" in verse 52 appears to be a plural form, and "Manahethites" in verse 54 is a clan name; traditional Strong's numbering lists them separately (H2679, H2680). They are best treated as closely related forms of the place or clan name rather than as another person.

As for meaning, standard lexicons connect Manahath with the Hebrew root nuach, "to rest," and give the meaning "rest" or "resting place." The name itself is reasonably clear. What it meant to those who first used it, whether a quiet settlement, a camping place, or something else, is not known. Nothing in the text treats the name as a sign of a person's character or a town's destiny.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

The Horite son of Shobal. Genesis 36 records the line of Seir the Horite alongside the descendants of Esau, because the two peoples came to share the same mountain country. Manahath appears only in this list and in its parallel in 1 Chronicles 1:40. No deeds are recorded. The Horites were later dispossessed by Esau's descendants, as Deuteronomy 2:12 explains, so Manahath's name survives as part of the record of a people who once held Seir.

The Manahethites of Judah. In 1 Chronicles 2:50–55 the Chronicler maps the clans descended from Hur. These clans settled the hill country around Kirjath Jearim and Bethlehem. Shobal's line included Haroeh "and half of the families of Manuhoth" (2:52), along with the clans of Kirjath Jearim from whom "came the Zorathites and the Eshtaolites" (2:53). Salma's line included "Bethlehem, the Netophathites, Atroth Beth Joab, half of the Manahethites, and the Zorites" (2:54). The Manahethites were therefore one people group divided between two branches of the same Judahite family. They lived among the towns and clans of Judah's northern hill country, the neighborhood of David's own Bethlehem.

The repeated pairing of "Shobal" and "Manahath" is striking. It occurs in Genesis 36 among the Horites and in 1 Chronicles 2 among the Judahites. Some scholars propose that clans with Horite or Edomite roots were absorbed into Judah. Others think that Judahites took up older local names. Shared names alone cannot settle the question, but the pattern is real and worth noticing.

The move to Manahath from Geba. First Chronicles 8:6–7 is a compressed and difficult passage: "These are the sons of Ehud, who were the heads of the fathers' houses of the inhabitants of Geba, and who forced them to move to Manahath: Naaman, Ahijah, and Gera who forced them to move." Geba was a Benjaminite town, later assigned to the Levites (Joshua 21:17). The Hebrew is hard to follow, and it is not clear who was moved, by whom, or when. Scripture does not say whether this was a quarrel between clans, outside military pressure, or something else. It also does not say whether this Ehud is the judge of Judges 3. What the text does make plain is that Benjaminite families were uprooted from one town and resettled in another.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states only a few things about Manahath. A Horite man bore the name. Judahite clans called Manahethites descended from Hur's family. Families from Geba were moved there under compulsion. Everything beyond this is careful inference.

Chronicles was written for God's people after the exile, as they rebuilt their identity around the temple and the line of David. Its long genealogies told returning families where they belonged. Within that purpose, even minor clans like the Manahethites are worth recording. God did not lose track of the small families of Judah's hill country.

Two further points are implications rather than direct statements. First, if Horite-related families did become part of Judah, they would join a pattern Scripture states elsewhere. Caleb son of Jephunneh is called a Kenizzite (Numbers 32:12), yet he served as Judah's representative (Numbers 13:6) and received an inheritance in Judah because he "wholly followed the LORD God of Israel" (Joshua 14:14). Many take his family to be of outside origin, though the text does not say so. Rahab and Ruth are clear cases of outsiders brought into the line of Judah. Belonging to God's people was never a matter of bloodline alone. Second, the record in 1 Chronicles 8:6 refuses to tidy up Israel's past. The land God gave was meant to be a place of rest (Deuteronomy 12:9–10), yet His people knew displacement, apparently even inside the land.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No New Testament passage mentions Manahath, and no prophetic fulfillment is attached to it. Its connection to Christ is indirect. The Manahethites are recorded among the families surrounding Bethlehem, in the same tribal record that Chronicles preserves to keep David's line in view. That is the line from which Jesus came (Matthew 1; Luke 3).

There is also a thematic resonance in the name, though not a type. Israel lived in a land meant for rest, yet families were forced to move to a place called "Rest." Hebrews makes the larger point explicit: if Joshua had given Israel final rest, God would not have spoken afterward of another day, and so a rest still remains for God's people (Hebrews 4:8–9, paraphrased). Jesus Himself invites the weary to come to Him and find rest for their souls (Matthew 11:28–29). Manahath does not predict this. It simply shows how much the human story needed a rest no town could provide.

5. Spirit & Life

The rest that matters most has never depended on an address. The families from Geba arrived at a town whose name meant rest, but they came because they were made to leave home. Many believers know something like this: a move they did not choose, a job lost, a family scattered. A place, even a well-named one, cannot give the heart what only God gives. The rest that remains for God's people is entered by trusting Him (Hebrews 4:3, 9).

Chronicles also shows that God keeps records of people history overlooks. Half of a clan in one branch of Hur's family and half in another would be forgettable to almost anyone, yet both halves are written down. Whatever the Manahethites' origins, they were counted among the families of Judah. A God who keeps track of the Manahethites does not forget anyone who belongs to Him.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:20–23 – Identifies Manahath as a son of Shobal in the line of Seir the Horite.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:38–40 – Repeats the Horite genealogy, confirming Manahath among Shobal's sons.

  • Deuteronomy 2:12 – Explains that Esau's descendants dispossessed the Horites of Seir.

  • 1 Chronicles 2:50–54 – Places the Manahethites, divided between Shobal's and Salma's descendants, among the Judahite clans of Hur near Kirjath Jearim and Bethlehem.

  • 1 Chronicles 8:6–7 – Records Benjaminite families from Geba forced to move to Manahath.

  • Joshua 14:14; Numbers 32:12 – Show Caleb, called a Kenizzite yet Judah's representative (Numbers 13:6), receiving an inheritance in Judah. Many explain this as a family of outside origin incorporated into Judah; the verses themselves do not say so, so this is a possible parallel.

  • Hebrews 4:8–9; Matthew 11:28–29 – Point to the rest God provides beyond any earthly place.
