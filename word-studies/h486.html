ALMODAD - (ʼAlmôwdâd, Strong's H0486)

Summary

Almodad is the first-named son of Joktan in the Table of Nations. Joktan was a son of Eber and a descendant of Shem through Arphaxad and Salah. Almodad appears only twice in Scripture, both times in the same list: "Joktan begot Almodad, Sheleph, Hazarmaveth, Jerah" (Genesis 10:26; 1 Chronicles 1:20). The meaning of his name is uncertain. Some interpret it in Hebrew as something like "God is a friend" or "measure of God." Many scholars instead see the opening Al- as the Arabic definite article and connect the name to Mudad, a name found in later Arabian genealogies. Neither view is settled.

Almodad's importance comes from where he stands in the family tree. His uncle Peleg carried the line that leads to Abram (Genesis 11:16–26). Almodad belongs to the brother's line, the Joktanite peoples who settled "from Mesha as you go toward Sephar, the mountain of the east" (Genesis 10:30), a region usually associated with southern Arabia. Scripture tells no story about him. Even so, God's Word takes the trouble to name him, twice. The God who chose Abraham's family was still keeping record of the families He did not choose for that role.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Almodad is one individual, the first of Joktan's thirteen sons (Genesis 10:26–29). His father Joktan was the brother of Peleg, and both were sons of Eber (Genesis 10:25). That makes Almodad Peleg's nephew and a member of the generations after the flood. Because the Table of Nations traces peoples as well as persons, his name probably also stands for the clan descended from him. No later passage identifies that clan by name, however.

The meaning of the name is uncertain. One proposal reads it as Hebrew, combining El ("God") with a form related to measuring or loving, which gives "God is a friend" or "measure of God." Many scholars prefer a different explanation. They take Al- as the Arabic article "the" and link the name to Mudad or al-Mudad, a figure remembered in later Arabian tribal tradition. If that is correct, it would be a rare Arabic form preserved in the Hebrew text, fitting for a family that settled in Arabia. Both explanations remain proposals. Scripture itself makes no wordplay on the name.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Almodad's whole biblical record is genealogical. Genesis 10:25 notes that in Peleg's days "the earth was divided," and then the text turns to Joktan's sons, with Almodad listed first. Their territory ran from Mesha toward Sephar (Genesis 10:30). The exact locations of Mesha and Sephar are debated. Several of Almodad's brothers, including Sheba, Hazarmaveth, and Ophir, bear names later associated with southern Arabia, which supports a general placement there.

The Chronicler repeats the list word for word in 1 Chronicles 1:20, inside a genealogy that runs from Adam to Israel. For the community returning from exile, this account located Israel within the whole human family God had made.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

The text plainly states that Almodad descends from Eber through Joktan and not through Peleg. It is also plain that Genesis 11 follows Peleg's line to Abram. From this we can draw an implication. Genesis often records a side branch first and then sets it aside to follow the line of promise. It does this with Cain before Seth, Ishmael before Isaac, and Esau before Jacob. Almodad's family fits that pattern. They are acknowledged and placed on the map, and then the story moves on. Being set aside in the narrative does not mean being forgotten. Moses later says that the Most High "divided their inheritance" among the nations and set their boundaries (Deuteronomy 32:8). Almodad's people received their place from the same God.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No passage in the New Testament or the prophets mentions Almodad. The connection to Christ is thematic and runs through the Table of Nations as a whole. God promised Abram that "all the families of the earth" would be blessed through him (Genesis 12:3). Paul told the Athenians that God "has made from one blood every nation of men" and "determined... the boundaries of their dwellings" (Acts 17:26). Revelation pictures the Lamb redeeming people "out of every tribe and tongue and people and nation" (Revelation 5:9). The families listed in Genesis 10, Almodad's among them, are the world that Abraham's Seed came to bless.

5. Spirit & Life

Readers often skim genealogies to find the names that lead to Jesus. Almodad is not one of them, yet God included him anyway. The Lord who narrowed His saving purpose to one family never stopped seeing the others. When the gospel later reached Arabia and beyond, it was going to people God had named and placed long before.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 10:25–30: Almodad's place in Eber's family and the territory of Joktan's sons.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:20: The Chronicler's exact repetition of the list for post-exilic Israel.

  • Genesis 11:16–26: Peleg's line to Abram, the branch Almodad does not belong to.

  • Deuteronomy 32:8; Acts 17:26: God sets the boundaries of the nations.

  • Revelation 5:9: The redeemed come from every tribe and nation.
