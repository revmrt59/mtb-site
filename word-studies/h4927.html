MISHMA - (Mishmâʻ, Strong's H4927)

Summary

Mishma is the name of two different men in the Bible's family records. The first is the fifth-listed son of Ishmael, a brother of Mibsam, listed among the twelve princes who descended from Abraham through Hagar (Genesis 25:14; 1 Chronicles 1:30). The second is a man from the tribe of Simeon, the son (not the brother) of Mibsam and father of Hamuel (1 Chronicles 4:25–26). The name comes from the Hebrew verb shama, "to hear," and means "hearing," "report," or "that which is heard."

Neither man has a recorded story, but both names carry weight in context. Mishma the Ishmaelite stands in the list that shows God keeping His word to Abraham about Ishmael. His name also echoes his father's name, Ishmael, "God hears," which recalls the God who heard Hagar's distress in the wilderness. Mishma the Simeonite appears beside a Mibsam, just as the Ishmaelite Mishma does. This hints at close contact between Israel's southern tribe and Abraham's other descendants in the Negev.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

The Strong's entry H4927 covers two separate men. They should not be merged.

  • Mishma son of Ishmael was Abraham's grandson through Hagar. He was the fifth-listed of Ishmael's twelve sons and the head of an Arabian tribal group (Genesis 25:13–14; 1 Chronicles 1:30).

  • Mishma of Simeon belongs to a later period. His line runs from Shaul son of Simeon through Shallum and Mibsam to Mishma, and on to Hamuel, Zacchur, and Shimei (1 Chronicles 4:24–26).

The name is a noun formed from shama, "to hear," and means "hearing," "report," or "that which is heard." This meaning is well supported. Scripture never explains why either man received the name. Its link to Ishmael's name (H3458), "God will hear," is a real connection of sound and root, but it is our observation rather than a wordplay the text spells out.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Mishma the Ishmaelite. Genesis introduces "the genealogy of Ishmael, Abraham's son, whom Hagar the Egyptian, Sarah's maidservant, bore to Abraham" (Genesis 25:12). Mishma is named after Mibsam and before Dumah and Massa (25:13–14). The list closes by saying, "These were the sons of Ishmael and these were their names, by their towns and their settlements, twelve princes according to their nations" (25:16). Chronicles repeats the list (1 Chronicles 1:29–31). These tribes lived in the desert regions south and east of Canaan.

Mishma the Simeonite. Chronicles traces Simeon's descendants: "Shallum his son, Mibsam his son, and Mishma his son. And the sons of Mishma were Hamuel his son, Zacchur his son, and Shimei his son" (1 Chronicles 4:25–26). Simeon's land lay in the far south, for "their inheritance was within the inheritance of the children of Judah" (Joshua 19:1). That placed the tribe beside Ishmaelite territory.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states that God promised Abraham He would bless Ishmael and make him the father of twelve princes (Genesis 17:20). Genesis 25:16 records that this came true, and Mishma's name is part of that record. Ishmael was not the son through whom the promised line ran, yet God kept His word to him in detail.

The Simeonite list raises a further point. Mibsam and Mishma appear together in both the Ishmaelite and Simeonite genealogies. The text does not explain why. Given Simeon's location on the southern frontier, one possible explanation is intermarriage or the absorption of an Ishmaelite clan into Simeon. Another is that the Simeonites simply used names common in the region. Shared names alone do not prove either. The firmer connection to Jacob's word that Simeon would be scattered within Israel (Genesis 49:7) is Simeon's allotment inside Judah's territory (Joshua 19:1–9), which matches the picture of a small tribe living in the margins of Judah's land.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No later Scripture mentions either Mishma, and no direct link to Christ is established. More broadly, Isaiah foresees the flocks of Kedar and Nebaioth, two of Mishma's brothers, being gathered to the Lord's house (Isaiah 60:7). That vision shows that Abraham's other descendants were not outside God's purpose to bless the nations through Abraham (Genesis 12:3).

5. Spirit & Life

Ishmael's name was a standing testimony that the LORD had heard Hagar's affliction (Genesis 16:11). God later heard the boy's voice when he was dying of thirst in the wilderness (Genesis 21:17). Hagar's grandson, Ishmael's son named "Hearing," keeps that word sounding in the next generation, whatever his parents intended by it. The God who heard a runaway servant and her son still hears those who cry to Him from the edges.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 17:20 – God's promise of twelve princes from Ishmael.

  • Genesis 25:12–16 – Mishma among Ishmael's sons, and the promise fulfilled.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:29–31 – The Ishmaelite list repeated.

  • 1 Chronicles 4:24–26 – Mishma in Simeon's line, paired with Mibsam.

  • Joshua 19:1 – Simeon's inheritance within Judah, near Ishmaelite lands.
