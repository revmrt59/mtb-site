MASREKAH - (Masrêqâh, Strong's H4957)

Summary

Masrekah was a town in ancient Edom, the land settled by Esau's descendants in the mountains of Seir south and east of the Dead Sea. Scripture names it twice, in one narrative notice preserved in two parallel records: "When Hadad died, Samlah of Masrekah reigned in his place" (Genesis 36:36; 1 Chronicles 1:47). The name probably means "vineyard" or "place of choice vines." It appears to come from the same Hebrew root as a word for a choice vine, although this derivation is likely rather than certain. Its exact location is unknown.

Masrekah matters because of the list in which it appears. Genesis 36 records "the kings who reigned in the land of Edom before any king reigned over the children of Israel" (Genesis 36:31). In that list one king follows another, several tied to different towns, and no successor is called the son of his predecessor. Genesis sets this record beside God's promise of kings from Jacob; Edom had kings before Israel did, though the list does not date Samlah more precisely. Masrekah is one small marker in that record. It shows that God kept His word to Esau, and it shows how long Israel had to wait before God gave His people the king He had promised.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Masrekah is a place, and Scripture records only one location by this name. It was the hometown of Samlah, the fifth king in Edom's list of early rulers (Genesis 36:36; 1 Chronicles 1:47). The town lay somewhere in Edomite territory, but no site has been securely identified.

The name is generally understood to mean "vineyard" or "place of choice vines." It appears to share a Hebrew root with the word for a choice vine used in Isaiah 5:2, where God's vineyard is "planted . . . with the choicest vine," and in Jeremiah 2:21, where the Lord says, "I had planted you a noble vine." The link is lexically sound, but the meaning remains probable rather than proven. If the name does mean "vineyard," it may point to grape-growing land within Edom's rugged hills. Scripture makes no wordplay on the name, and it does not tell us that the meaning says anything about the town's spiritual character.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Masrekah's whole biblical record is one line in the king list of Edom (Genesis 36:31–39), which the Chronicler repeats in 1 Chronicles 1:43–50. After Hadad, who "attacked Midian in the field of Moab" and whose city was Avith, died, "Samlah of Masrekah reigned in his place" (Genesis 36:35–36). Nothing else is said about Samlah or his town.

The list itself is unusual. Bela's city was Dinhabah, Jobab came from Bozrah, Husham came from the land of the Temanites, and Hadad's city was Avith (Genesis 36:32–35). After Samlah, Saul of Rehoboth reigned (Genesis 36:37). Several kings are tied to different places (Baal-Hanan has no city listed, and not every father is named), and none of them is called the son of the king before him. It is a reasonable inference, though not stated, that kingship in early Edom did not pass from father to son. Scripture does not explain how each ruler came to power. The phrase "of Masrekah" names Samlah's place of origin. It may or may not mean that Masrekah served as his royal seat.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

The text states two things directly. First, Edom had kings "before any king reigned over the children of Israel" (Genesis 36:31). Second, God Himself gave Esau his land: "I have given Mount Seir to Esau as a possession" (Deuteronomy 2:5). Masrekah belongs to a nation that God allowed to settle and organize early, and God later told Israel to respect its borders.

From these facts we can draw a careful conclusion. Genesis records Edom's early kings just after God promised Jacob that "kings shall come from your body" (Genesis 35:11). The narrative sets Edom's earlier monarchy beside that promise, while Israel would wait through Egypt and many generations for its own kings. The parade of separate towns, Masrekah among them, also stands in quiet contrast to the ideal God later promised Israel: a royal line in David, ruling from a chosen city.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No later passage mentions Masrekah, and Scripture does not treat it as a type. The wider connection runs through the king list. Genesis 36:31 looks ahead to the day when Israel would have a king. That hope was fulfilled in David's house and ultimately in Christ, the Son of David, of whose kingdom "there will be no end" (Luke 1:33). Edom's kings came and went one after another. The King God promised came later, and He came to stay.

5. Spirit & Life

The Bible names Masrekah and then moves on. It was a hometown, perhaps a place of vineyards, that had its moment when one of its men ruled Edom, and then the record turns to the next king and the next town. That is how human power usually looks in Scripture: real and sometimes early, but passing. God gave Esau his mountain and let his kingdoms rise. Yet the story of Scripture was moving toward the king who would come from Jacob. When others seem to get established faster than God's people, Genesis 36 reminds us that God's timing has its own purpose. He gives the world its season, and He gives His people a kingdom that cannot be shaken (Hebrews 12:28).

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:36 – The primary record: Samlah of Masrekah succeeds Hadad as king of Edom.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:47 – The Chronicler's identical record, preserving Edom's king list within the family of nations around Israel.

  • Genesis 36:31–39 – The full king list. It shows Edom's early kingship, with no succession described as father to son, "before any king reigned over the children of Israel."

  • Genesis 35:11 – God's promise of kings from Jacob, the backdrop for Genesis 36:31.

  • Deuteronomy 2:5 – God's gift of Mount Seir to Esau.

  • Isaiah 5:2; Jeremiah 2:21 – Uses of the related word for a choice or noble vine, which supports the probable meaning of the name.
