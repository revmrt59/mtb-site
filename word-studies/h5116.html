HABITATION, DWELLING, PASTURE, FOLD - (nâveh, Strong's H5116)

Summary

The Hebrew word nâveh means a settled place to live. It is often a shepherd's word for the pasture or fold where a flock is brought to rest, and it also describes the homes of people, the dens of wild animals, and the holy dwelling of God. It is the word behind the "green pastures" of Psalm 23, the "good fold" of Ezekiel 34, and the "holy habitation" toward which God led Israel after the Red Sea (Exodus 15:13). It also names the sheepfold from which God took David to make him king (2 Samuel 7:8).

The word carries a shepherd's picture of security. In blessing, God settles His people where they are fed and safe and blesses "the home of the just" (Proverbs 3:33). In judgment, proud cities become grazing land for camels and dens for jackals. Jeremiah calls the LORD Himself "the habitation of justice" (Jeremiah 50:7). The deepest home Scripture offers is the Shepherd Himself, more than any piece of ground.

Full Study

1. Word Background & Setting

Nâveh comes from the life of a herding people. It often names the place where flocks lie down: open pastureland, a sheltered fold, the settled spot to which a shepherd returns his animals. That background stays alive even when the word moves into city life. It differs from the common word for a built house (bayit). A nâveh is valued less for its walls than for the rest and settledness it gives.

The word appears 39 times, mostly in poetry and prophecy. Jeremiah uses it most often, followed by Isaiah, the Psalms, and Job. These are books full of exile and return, ruin and restoration, so a word about being at home or losing home fits them naturally.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The core meaning is a dwelling place where one is settled and at rest. Context decides who lives there:

1. A pasture or fold for flocks, the place where sheep graze and lie down (Psalm 23:2; Ezekiel 34:14; 2 Samuel 7:8).

2. A human home or residence, especially as a place of security (Proverbs 3:33; Proverbs 24:15).

3. The dwelling of God, the holy place where He meets His people (Exodus 15:13; 2 Samuel 15:25).

4. The lair of wild animals in desolate land (Isaiah 34:13; 35:7), or figuratively, the dark haunts of violent people (Psalm 74:20).

Jeremiah 6:2 translates a related form as "lovely": "I have likened the daughter of Zion To a lovely and delicate woman." The idea of being at home and content may lie behind that sense. Verse 3 goes on to describe shepherds pitching their tents around her with their flocks, which keeps the pasture picture close by.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

When God is the subject, nâveh describes His provision. He leads, settles, feeds, and restores His flock to its folds (Jeremiah 23:3). When the righteous are in view, the word speaks of a home under God's blessing and protection. Proverbs 24:15 warns the wicked man, "Do not lie in wait… against the dwelling of the righteous; Do not plunder his resting place."

In judgment oracles the same word turns grim. A city's nâveh is laid waste (Jeremiah 10:25; Psalm 79:7), or a fortified place becomes a pasture again, this time for camels or jackals. Even God's own people are not exempt: Jeremiah 25:30 pictures the LORD roaring "mightily against His fold." The word's warmth is what makes these warnings sting. The reader knows what a home is supposed to be, and so feels its loss.

4. Theological Significance

Across its uses, nâveh shows that God wants His people settled with Him. Rescue alone does not finish His work. He intends to bring them home. The first use of the word makes this plain. In the Song of Moses, sung on the far shore of the Red Sea, Israel praises God for guiding the people He redeemed by His strength to His holy habitation (Exodus 15:13). No temple or settled land existed yet. Israel sang of the destination before they had seen it, because the God who broke Pharaoh's grip had brought them out in order to bring them in to Himself.

The word also ties security to right standing with God. Proverbs 3:33 sets the two kinds of dwelling side by side: "The curse of the LORD is on the house of the wicked, But He blesses the home of the just." Bildad, wrong as he was about Job, still spoke from this conviction: "If you were pure and upright, Surely now He would awake for you, And prosper your rightful dwelling place" (Job 8:6). In these books a home is safe only when the God who blesses it is near.

The prophets press the same truth from the other side. God brings down whatever people build against Him. Ammon's royal city Rabbah would become "a stable for camels" (Ezekiel 25:5). The Philistine coast would become pastures for shepherds (Zephaniah 2:6). Edom's and Babylon's strong dwelling places would be made desolate (Jeremiah 49:19–20; 50:44–45). God takes cities that trusted in their walls and returns them to grazing ground. The word that names the safety He gives also names the ruin that comes when people trust their own strength.

5. Important Biblical Passages & Distinctions

Psalm 23:2 and Ezekiel 34:14: the Shepherd who settles His flock. David says the LORD makes him lie down in green pastures. The picture is of sheep settled in good pasture under the Shepherd's care, a flock with every need met. The Shepherd takes the initiative; He makes His sheep lie down. Rest here means being fully provided for, and it involves more than stopping work. Ezekiel 34 widens the picture from one man to a scattered nation. After condemning the shepherds who fed themselves and abandoned the sheep, God promises to search out His flock personally: "I will feed them in good pasture… There they shall lie down in a good fold and feed in rich pasture on the mountains of Israel." Ezekiel's "good fold" is David's green pasture given to a whole people.

2 Samuel 7:8 and 15:25: from the sheepfold to God's dwelling. When God makes His promise to David, He reminds him where it began: He took David from the sheepfold, from following the sheep, to rule His people Israel (repeated in 1 Chronicles 17:7). God passed over palaces and chose a boy who smelled of the flock. Later in David's life the word returns. Fleeing Jerusalem during Absalom's revolt, David sends the ark back and says that if he finds favor with the LORD, God will bring him back to see it and His dwelling place (2 Samuel 15:25). The man God took from a fold now trusts God to bring him home to God's own fold. He does not grasp for it by force.

Jeremiah 50:7: the LORD, the habitation of justice. This is the word's richest use. Babylon's soldiers excuse their cruelty to Israel: "We have not offended, Because they have sinned against the LORD, the habitation of justice, The LORD, the hope of their fathers." Here the nâveh is a Person. Some interpreters read the phrase as describing Israel's rightful pasture, but the sentence most naturally applies the title to the LORD. It gains force beside Jeremiah 31:23, where the restored city is blessed as a "home of justice." Jerusalem could be called that only because the LORD is that home first. The enemy's own words admit what Israel had forgotten: to sin against the LORD was to wander out of the only fold where they were truly safe. Israel's security had never rested in a city wall or a temple building. It rested in God.

6. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

The word follows a path through Scripture. God redeems His people in order to bring them to His habitation (Exodus 15:13). He chooses a king from a sheepfold (2 Samuel 7:8). He promises, through Ezekiel, to shepherd His scattered flock Himself and to set "My servant David" over them (Ezekiel 34:23). He reveals that He Himself is the true home of His people (Jeremiah 50:7).

Jesus gathers these threads. He is the Son of David, laid in a manger at His birth (Luke 2:7), the Shepherd-King who comes from humble surroundings, as David did. In John 10 He claims both the shepherd's role and the fold's entrance: "I am the door. If anyone enters by Me, he will be saved, and will go in and out and find pasture," and "I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd gives His life for the sheep." John writes in Greek, so nâveh is not the word there, yet the picture is the one Psalm 23 and Ezekiel 34 painted. The pasture is found by entering through Christ. Scripture closes with the habitation theme completed: God's dwelling is with His people, and He lives among them (Revelation 21:3).

7. Spirit & Life

Isaiah shows where lasting peace comes from. He promises that God's people will dwell in a peaceful habitation, in secure dwellings and quiet resting places (Isaiah 32:18). That promise follows the Spirit being poured out from on high, the wilderness becoming fruitful, and righteousness producing peace (Isaiah 32:15–17). A quiet home here is the fruit of God's Spirit at work. It does not come from human planning. Isaiah 33:20 adds that Zion will be a quiet home, a tent whose stakes will never be pulled up. Every earthly home can be lost, as the judgment oracles show. The home God gives will not be taken down.

Isaiah 35:7 holds a small but striking reversal. The "habitation of jackals," the very image of ruin in Isaiah 34:13, becomes ground for grass, reeds, and rushes. God does more than avoid the desolate places. He makes them fit to live in again.

This frees a believer from two errors. One is the anxious effort to build a safe place apart from God, the same effort the prophets watched God return to camel pasture. The other is treating rest as idleness. Rest in the Shepherd's fold means being fed, guarded, and settled by Someone who knows the way. A believer can lie down without fear because the Shepherd is awake. The LORD who is "the habitation of justice" offers Himself as the place His people live.

8. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Job 5:24 – Eliphaz describes a man God protects: he knows his dwelling is safe and finds nothing missing when he inspects it.

  • Psalm 65:12 – God's goodness overflows even onto the wilderness pastures, which drip with blessing.

  • Psalm 68:12 – After God scatters kings, the woman who stayed home shares in the plunder. The victory belongs to God, and its benefits reach those who never went to battle.

  • Isaiah 65:10 – In the coming restoration, Sharon becomes a fold for flocks for the people who have sought the LORD.

  • Jeremiah 33:12 – A land Babylon left empty will again have a dwelling for shepherds resting their flocks.

  • Hosea 9:13 – Ephraim was once planted in a pleasant place, yet its sin turns that place toward slaughter.

9. Related Words & Canonical Pairings

  • H1004 (bayit, house) – Canonical pairing. Proverbs 3:33 sets the "house" of the wicked under the LORD's curse beside the "home" (nâveh) of the just under His blessing.
