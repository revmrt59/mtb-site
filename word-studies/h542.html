WORKMAN, ARTISAN - (ʼâmân, Strong's H0542)

Summary

The Hebrew word ʼâmân means a master craftsman: a skilled artisan whose trained hands produce fine, carefully finished work. It occurs once in the Hebrew Bible, in Song of Solomon 7:1 (7:2 in Hebrew numbering); STEPBible counts 1 occurrence in 1 verse in the Leningrad text with its Qere readings. There a husband praises his bride's beauty by comparing the curves of her body to jewels, "the work of the hands of a skillful workman." The NKJV's word "skillful" brings out what the Hebrew word already implies. He is not picturing an ordinary laborer. He is picturing an expert jeweler at his bench.

This single use carries real weight. In a poem celebrating married love, the lover says the body he cherishes looks like something deliberately designed and shaped by a master's hand. The rest of Scripture shows who that Master is. Every person is "fearfully and wonderfully made" (Psalm 139:14). God personally fashioned the first woman (Genesis 2:22). In Christ believers are "His workmanship" (Ephesians 2:10). The rare word teaches us to look at the human body, and especially at a husband or wife, with the respect owed to God's handiwork.

Full Study

1. Word Background & Setting

ʼâmân is a Hebrew noun found only in Song of Solomon 7:1 (7:2 in Hebrew Bibles). A word used only once is called a hapax legomenon. Hebrew dictionaries list it with the word family built on the letters ʼ-m-n, which includes the verb ʼâman (H0539), "to be firm, faithful, trustworthy," and the familiar word "amen." Many scholars also connect ʼâmân with the Akkadian ummânu, a term from the wider ancient Near East for a master craftsman or expert specialist. Either way, the background points in one direction: an ʼâmân is a recognized expert, not a casual worker.

The setting is poetry. Song of Solomon 7:1–9 is a song of praise in which the husband describes his bride from her feet upward. He calls her "O prince's daughter," a title that honors her dignity and nobility.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

ʼâmân has one sense: a skilled craftsman or master artisan. The word highlights expertise and the quality of finished work. Old Testament Hebrew more often uses chârâsh (H2796) for a craftsman or metalworker. Because ʼâmân is the rarer term, it adds an elevated, poetic note to the single verse where it appears.

The connection to the "firm, faithful" word family should not be pressed too far. Shared letters do not mean that every idea in the family is packed into this noun. In use, ʼâmân simply names an expert maker whose work can be trusted to be excellent.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

The only use is a simile. The husband says, "The curves of your thighs are like jewels, the work of the hands of a skillful workman" (Song of Solomon 7:1). The bride's body is compared to ornaments, and the ornaments are praised by pointing to the craftsman who made them. The phrase "the work of the hands" places the emphasis on how the jewels came to be. Their beauty is no accident of nature. It is the result of skill, attention, and patient effort.

That gives the word a particular emphasis within the comparison. The lover praises more than how his bride looks. He praises her as someone whose form shows the care of a master's design.

4. Theological Significance

These passages do not treat the human body as cheap or shameful in itself. The Song of Solomon dwells on physical beauty within married love without embarrassment, and it uses the language of fine craftsmanship to do so. That fits the whole biblical teaching on creation. God formed the man from the dust, fashioned the woman, and called His work "very good" (Genesis 1:31).

The craftsman image also says something about the Maker. A master artisan works with purpose. He chooses his materials, shapes them with care, and takes pleasure in what he produces. When the Bible elsewhere speaks of God as the One who forms, builds, and knits together human life, it presents Him as this kind of Maker: deliberate, attentive, and delighted in His work. The lover in the Song admires a human jeweler's craft, but behind every human body stands the Creator whose craftsmanship that jeweler can only imitate.

5. Important Biblical Passages & Distinctions

Song of Solomon 7:1 — the bride as a master's handiwork. The husband's praise begins with her feet in sandals and moves upward with growing admiration. When he reaches her thighs, he does not reach for a crude comparison. He reaches for the jeweler's workshop. In this poem the body of the woman he loves deserves the same careful attention that people give to precious objects shaped by expert hands. The bride uses similar imagery earlier when she describes her beloved in terms of gold, ivory, and precious stones (Song of Solomon 5:10–16). Both spouses look at each other and see something crafted and valuable. Married love in this book admires and honors. It does not use the other person.

Psalm 139:14 — "fearfully and wonderfully made." David turns the same wonder toward God. He praises God because his own body was formed by God's hand: "I will praise You, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made; marvelous are Your works, and that my soul knows very well." The surrounding verses describe God covering him and weaving him together before birth (Psalm 139:13–16). This psalm does not use ʼâmân. It supplies the truth that the Song's simile points toward. The lover admires a craftsman's work in his bride. David names the Craftsman.

Genesis 2:22 — the woman fashioned by God. "Then the rib which the LORD God had taken from man He made into a woman, and He brought her to the man." The Hebrew verb behind "made" is bânâh (H1129), the ordinary word for building. The woman did not appear as a random byproduct of creation. God shaped her personally and then brought her to the man as a gift. When the husband in the Song marvels at his bride as a master's work, he stands where Adam stood, receiving a person God designed and gave.

6. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Proverbs 8:30 offers a striking parallel. Wisdom, speaking of the creation of the world, says, "Then I was beside Him as a master craftsman; and I was daily His delight." The Hebrew word there is ʼâmôn (H0525), a close relative of ʼâmân, though some interpreters read it as "darling child" instead of "craftsman." Read as the NKJV renders it, the verse pictures creation as skilled work carried out in shared joy.

The New Testament says plainly that all things were made through the Son (John 1:3; Colossians 1:16), and Paul calls Christ "the wisdom of God" (1 Corinthians 1:24). Proverbs 8 is not a direct prophecy of Christ. Still, the connection carries real weight: the One through whom the world was crafted is the One who came to redeem it.

That redemption brings the craftsman image to its fullest point. Paul writes, "For we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand that we should walk in them" (Ephesians 2:10). God did not stop crafting at Eden. In Christ He makes new people and prepares the good works they will do. The Song also gives a picture of the Bible's larger story. A husband delights in a bride whose beauty he praises, and Paul later describes Christ loving the church and giving Himself for her so that He might present her to Himself glorious and without blemish (Ephesians 5:25–27). The Song's tender admiration echoes the love of the Bridegroom who makes His bride beautiful.

7. Spirit & Life

The lover's simile corrects two common errors. One treats the body with contempt, as merely animal or as something spirituality must escape. The other worships the body as though appearance were everything. The Song does neither. It honors the body as handiwork, and handiwork points beyond itself to the one who made it. A husband who looks at his wife this way sees more than attraction. He sees someone God shaped, gave, and values, and his delight in her becomes a form of honoring her Maker.

The same view applies outside marriage. Every person you meet is the work of God's hands. David's response in Psalm 139 is the right one: praise. Many believers find it easier to praise God's work in a sunrise than in their own bodies, especially when age, illness, or disability makes the body feel like a failure. Scripture does not deny the effects of a fallen world. It does insist that the One who knit you together knew you before anyone else did (Psalm 139:15–16), and in Christ He is still at work making you His workmanship. The Craftsman has not set down His tools.

8. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

ʼâmân occurs only in Song of Solomon 7:1, which is discussed above. The supporting passages in Sections 5 and 6 use different Hebrew and Greek words and are cited for their teaching, not as further occurrences of this word.

9. Related Words & Canonical Pairings

  • **H0539 (ʼâman)** — the "firm, faithful, trustworthy" word family to which Hebrew dictionaries assign ʼâmân. This is a lexical relative.

  • **H0525 (ʼâmôn)** — "master craftsman" in Proverbs 8:30. This is a closely related noun.

  • **H2796 (chârâsh)** — the more common Hebrew word for a craftsman or artisan. This is a distinct word with a similar meaning.

  • **H1129 (bânâh)** — "to build," used for God fashioning the woman in Genesis 2:22. This is a thematic connection.

  • **G4161 (poiēma)** — "workmanship" in Ephesians 2:10. This is a thematic connection across the Testaments.
