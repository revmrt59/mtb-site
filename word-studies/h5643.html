SECRET PLACE, HIDING PLACE, COVER - (çêther, Strong's H5643)

Summary

The Hebrew word çêther means a cover, a hiding place, or something kept secret. Most often it simply describes something covered or out of sight: a covert in the hills, a private message, a conversation held "secretly." Scripture uses the word in two opposite directions. People use a çêther to hide sin, such as idols set up "in secret," adultery under a disguise, or slander whispered behind a neighbor's back. God, by contrast, offers Himself as a çêther for His people: "the secret place of the Most High" (Psalm 91:1).

The contrast at the center of this word is between hiding from God and hiding in God. David hid his sin "secretly" until God exposed it before all Israel, and Psalm 32, a psalm attributed to David, says to the Lord, "You are my hiding place" (Psalm 32:7). Isaiah looks ahead to a righteous King whose just rule will provide "a cover from the tempest" (Isaiah 32:1–2), while every refuge built on lies will be swept away. In Christ the believer's life is "hidden with Christ in God" (Colossians 3:3). The secret place is not a refuge from God's eyes. It is a refuge in His presence.

Full Study

1. Word Background & Setting

Çêther is a Hebrew noun built on the verb meaning to hide or to conceal. It appears across the Law, the historical books, the poetry, and the prophets. In Deuteronomy, Job, and Proverbs it usually describes wrongdoing done out of sight. In the Psalms it becomes one of Scripture's most treasured words for God's protection. In Jeremiah it describes the whispered meetings of Jerusalem's last days. Isaiah uses it both for God's open speech and for the shelter a coming King will provide.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word itself carries no moral charge. It names something covered or hidden from view, and context decides whether that covering is good or evil.

1. A literal covering or hiding place. Abigail rides down "under cover of the hill" (1 Samuel 25:20), and Behemoth lies "in a covert of reeds and marsh" (Job 40:21).

2. Secrecy, something done privately. In this sense the word usually appears as "in secret" or "secretly," as when Zedekiah questions Jeremiah "secretly in his house" (Jeremiah 37:17).

3. A shelter or place of protection. This sense describes being kept safe, often in God's presence: "He who dwells in the secret place of the Most High" (Psalm 91:1).

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

The subject of the hiding changes everything. When people make the secret, it is usually an attempt to get away with something: the man who makes an idol and "sets it up in secret" (Deuteronomy 27:15), the adulterer who says, "No eye will see me," and "disguises his face" (Job 24:15), or the man who "secretly slanders his neighbor" (Psalm 101:5). When God provides the hiding place, the word describes safety, closeness, and rest.

A third use belongs to God alone. He sometimes surrounds Himself with darkness or storm as His "secret place" (Psalm 18:11). Here the covering expresses His majesty. It is not concealment born of guilt.

4. Theological Significance

Scripture is plain that no human secret escapes God. Deuteronomy 27 pronounces curses on sins committed "in secret," and the whole people answer, "Amen!" Proverbs exposes the folly of the one who boasts that "bread eaten in secret is pleasant" (Proverbs 9:17). Behind each of these lies the conviction stated in Hebrews: "there is no creature hidden from His sight, but all things are naked and open to the eyes of Him to whom we must give account" (Hebrews 4:13).

God also refuses to deal in secrets about Himself. Against the hidden rites of idolatry He declares, "I have not spoken in secret, In a dark place of the earth; I did not say to the seed of Jacob, 'Seek Me in vain'" (Isaiah 45:19). He repeats the point: "I have not spoken in secret from the beginning" (Isaiah 48:16). The God who sees every hidden thing also makes Himself known openly. His truth is not a puzzle reserved for a few, and those who seek Him do not seek in vain.

God's care reaches into hidden places as well. In Psalm 139:15 David says his frame was not hidden from God when he was made in secret, formed in the womb. Before any eye could see him, God was at work. The same word that describes sinners hiding from God describes the place where God, unseen, forms a human life.

5. Important Biblical Passages & Distinctions

The secret place of His presence (Psalms 27, 31, 61, 91). David knew literal hiding places in caves and wilderness strongholds. In the Psalms the shelter becomes God Himself. "You shall hide them in the secret place of Your presence From the plots of man" (Psalm 31:20). The location is specific. It is "the secret place of His tabernacle" (Psalm 27:5), and "the shelter of Your wings" alongside the vow to "abide in Your tabernacle forever" (Psalm 61:4). This language may echo the sanctuary, where the cherubim spread their wings over the ark (Exodus 25:20), though the psalms do not say so. Psalm 91 joins the two images: the one who "dwells in the secret place of the Most High" will "abide under the shadow of the Almighty" (91:1), and "under His wings you shall take refuge" (91:4). The verbs dwells and abide matter. This is a place to live in, not a bunker for emergencies.

David's two hiding places (2 Samuel 12:12; Psalm 32:7). After David took Bathsheba and arranged Uriah's death, God told him through Nathan, "For you did it secretly, but I will do this thing before all Israel, before the sun" (2 Samuel 12:12). David's çêther gave him no cover at all. Psalm 32, also attributed to David, describes a man who stopped hiding. The psalm does not name its sin or tie itself to the Bathsheba story, but set beside 2 Samuel 12 it makes a fitting contrast. He tells how he acknowledged his sin and no longer kept it hidden (32:5), and then he says, "You are my hiding place; You shall preserve me from trouble" (32:7). Together the two passages show the same man, and the same word, on both sides: hiding sin from God and hiding in God. Confession opened the way into the shelter.

The secret place of thunder (Psalms 18:11; 81:7). When God came down to rescue David, "He made darkness His secret place; His canopy around Him was dark waters And thick clouds of the skies" (Psalm 18:11). In Psalm 81:7 God reminds Israel that He answered them from the secret place of thunder, recalling Sinai. The shelter God gives His people comes from a God whose glory is too great to look on directly. The secret place is safe because the One who dwells there is holy and mighty, and this keeps closeness with God from becoming casual.

Two shelters in Isaiah (Isaiah 28:17; 32:2). Jerusalem's rulers trusted political deals to survive invasion, boasting that they had hidden themselves under falsehood. God answers: "The hail will sweep away the refuge of lies, And the waters will overflow the hiding place" (Isaiah 28:17). Four chapters later, Isaiah announces that "a king will reign in righteousness, and princes will rule with justice" (32:1). Verse 2 describes the shelter that just rule provides: "A man will be as a hiding place from the wind, And a cover from the tempest, As rivers of water in a dry place, As the shadow of a great rock in a weary land" (32:2). One hiding place is flooded by judgment. The other stands in the storm, and it is personal: righteous rulers who protect their people.

6. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Since Eden, when Adam and Eve hid among the trees from the Lord, people have tried to cover themselves. Scripture's answer is never better hiding. God provides a covering Himself. The sanctuary pointed to this answer, with God's presence behind the veil, meeting His people from between the cherubim (Exodus 25:22). When Christ died, the temple veil was torn, and the way into that presence was opened.

Isaiah 32:2 is not quoted in the New Testament, and in Isaiah the shelter belongs to the just rule of the king and his princes. Still, the picture of a righteous King whose reign becomes a shelter fits Jesus closely. He is the shelter where the storm of judgment breaks and cannot reach those inside. Paul states the result: "For you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God" (Colossians 3:3). The hiding place of the Psalms becomes a union with Christ.

The alternative is grim. At the end, people will cry to the mountains and rocks, "Fall on us and hide us from the face of Him who sits on the throne and from the wrath of the Lamb!" (Revelation 6:16). They will seek a refuge of stone and find none. Jesus also matched the Father's openness: "I spoke openly to the world... and in secret I have said nothing" (John 18:20). The God of Isaiah 45 does not whisper the way of salvation in dark places, and neither did His Son.

7. Spirit & Life

Every believer chooses between two hiding places, and this word names both. One is the cover we build for ourselves: the private sin, the quiet slander, the hidden habit. It feels safe for a while, as David's did. The other is God's presence, entered by confession, as Psalm 32 shows. We cannot hide in God while hiding from Him.

The Song of Solomon adds a tender picture. The beloved is a dove "in the secret places of the cliff," and the lover's request is "Let me see your face, Let me hear your voice" (Song of Songs 2:14). The secret place is where we are safe enough to be seen. Psalm 91 calls it dwelling: a settled life with Him, not occasional visits in trouble.

Jerusalem's last days show the cost of refusing this shelter. King Zedekiah questioned Jeremiah "secretly in his house" and asked, "Is there any word from the LORD?" (Jeremiah 37:17). The answer was plain: he would be delivered to the king of Babylon. Later he admitted what held him back: "I am afraid of the Jews who have defected to the Chaldeans" (Jeremiah 38:19). Secrecy itself was not his failure. Ruled by fear of men, he sought God's word in private and refused to obey it.

8. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Judges 3:19 — Ehud tells King Eglon, "I have a secret message for you, O king." The tyrant clears his private room, and the "secret" becomes the means of his judgment and Israel's deliverance.

  • 1 Samuel 19:2 — Jonathan warns David to stay in a secret place and hide from Saul. This is a literal hiding place for a man God was protecting.

  • Deuteronomy 32:38 — God mocks the idols Israel trusted and challenges them to rise up and be a refuge. False gods can offer no cover.

  • Proverbs 21:14 — A gift given in secret calms anger. The word records how quietly bribery works.

  • Proverbs 25:23 — A "backbiting tongue" provokes an angry face. Secret speech damages the relationships it pretends to protect.

  • Isaiah 16:4 — Moab is urged to be a shelter for outcasts fleeing destruction. God expects His people to provide shelter for others as well.
