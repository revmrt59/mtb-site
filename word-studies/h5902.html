IRAM - (ʻÎyrâm, Strong's H5902)

Summary

Iram was one of the chiefs of Edom, the nation descended from Esau, Jacob's twin brother. His name appears only twice in Scripture, as the last entry in the list of Edom's chiefs (Genesis 36:43; 1 Chronicles 1:54). The meaning of the name is probable rather than certain. It seems to come from the Hebrew word for "city" or "town," and lexicons suggest a sense such as "belonging to a city" or "citizen." Genesis lists these chiefs "according to their families and their places," so "Iram" may name both a leader and the clan or district connected with him.

Iram has no recorded words or deeds. His importance lies in what the list shows. God had promised Esau a life of his own, and He gave him Mount Seir as a possession (Deuteronomy 2:5). Iram's name stands in Scripture as part of the evidence that God kept that word. The Lord who chose Jacob did not forget Esau's descendants in His providence and gift of land. He also governs the times and boundaries of every nation (Acts 17:26).

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Iram was an Edomite alluph, a "chief," in the line of Esau. Scripture identifies no other person by this name. The Genesis list names the chiefs "according to their families and their places, by their names" (Genesis 36:40). For that reason, Iram may be the founder of a clan, a clan or territory that carried his name, or both. The text does not settle the question.

The name probably derives from the Hebrew word for "city" or "town," which would give a meaning like "belonging to a city" or "citizen." This city-related meaning is supported by Strong's and by Smith's and Easton's Bible dictionaries. Scripture does not comment on the name or use it as wordplay, so the etymology should be treated as probable, not as a key to Iram's character.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Genesis 36 traces Esau's family after he moved to the mountains of Seir, southeast of the Dead Sea. The chapter closes with eleven chiefs, ending: "Chief Magdiel, and Chief Iram. These were the chiefs of Edom, according to their dwelling places in the land of their possession. Esau was the father of the Edomites" (Genesis 36:43). Centuries later, the Chronicler copied the same list into his opening genealogies, where it ends simply: "These were the chiefs of Edom" (1 Chronicles 1:54). Nothing more is recorded about Iram himself.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states that Edom held its land as "the land of their possession" and that God Himself had given Seir to Esau (Deuteronomy 2:5). Isaac had foretold a separate future for Esau (Genesis 27:39–40). The list of chiefs shows that future taking shape. Genesis also notes that Edom had kings "before any king reigned over the children of Israel" (Genesis 36:31). A careful observation follows from this. Genesis arranges Esau's settled, organized line beside Jacob's family, still sojourning and later to go down to Egypt. The arrangement is literary; Genesis does not date Iram's chiefdom to Jacob's lifetime, only Edom's kings to a time before Israel's. Being established early on the earth is not the same as carrying God's promise. Genesis turns from Esau's chiefs straight back to Jacob's story (Genesis 37:1).

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No connection to Christ is established for Iram himself. Edom as a nation does take on a significant prophetic role later in Scripture, as in Obadiah and Isaiah 34. That development belongs to a study of Edom, not to this one name.

5. Spirit & Life

The Bible records the name of an Edomite chief whose worship is not recorded, and it does so twice. The God of Israel did not see the nations around His people as background noise. He gave them their lands, marked their boundaries, and remembered their leaders (Acts 17:26). The same God who carefully kept His word to Esau can be trusted to keep His far greater promises to those who belong to Him.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:40–43 — Iram's place in the list of Edom's chiefs, organized by families, places, and "dwelling places."

  • 1 Chronicles 1:54 — The Chronicler preserves Iram's name centuries later.

  • Genesis 27:39–40 — Isaac's word about Esau's future.

  • Deuteronomy 2:5 — God gave Mount Seir to Esau as a possession.

  • Acts 17:26 — God determines the times and boundaries of the nations.
