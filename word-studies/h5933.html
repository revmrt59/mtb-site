ALVAH - (ʻAlvâh, Strong's H5933)

Summary

Alvah was one of the chiefs of Edom, the nation descended from Esau, Jacob's twin brother. He appears only in the list of Edomite clan leaders in Genesis 36:40 and in the Chronicler's repetition of that list, where the name is spelled "Aliah" (1 Chronicles 1:51). The meaning of the name is uncertain. Some connect it with a root that suggests height or loftiness (as Hitchcock's dictionary does), while Strong's and Smith's connect it with a Hebrew word for iniquity. Neither proposal is settled. Scripture records nothing Alvah did. His name stands in a record of Esau's settled power in Mount Seir. That record shows God keeping His word that Esau, too, would become a nation, and it shows that the Lord knows the peoples and territories outside Israel by name.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Alvah is listed second among the eleven "chiefs of Esau," after Timnah and before Jetheth (Genesis 36:40–43). The Hebrew title translated "chief" refers to a clan leader or tribal head. Scripture gives no other person by this name. In 1 Chronicles 1:51 the same chief is called Aliah. The two spellings differ by a single Hebrew letter, and the two letters were easily confused in copying, so the variant is most likely a scribal one.

The meaning of the name is not settled. One proposal draws on a root meaning "to go up" or on related words suggesting height or loftiness. Another, found in Strong's and Smith's, links the name to a Hebrew word meaning "iniquity." Both are proposals, and the safest conclusion is that the meaning is uncertain. Scripture offers no wordplay on the name.

Genesis says these chiefs are named "according to their families and their places" (Genesis 36:40). Several names on the list also belong to Esau's family or to known places. Aholibamah was the name of Esau's wife (Genesis 36:2), and Teman and Kenaz were his grandsons (Genesis 36:11). This pattern suggests that "Alvah" may name a clan and its home region in Mount Seir as much as a single man.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

The list closes Genesis 36, the chapter that traces Esau's descendants after he moved to Mount Seir. It ends with the summary: "These were the chiefs of Edom, according to their dwelling places in the land of their possession. Esau was the father of the Edomites" (Genesis 36:43). Centuries later, the Chronicler placed the same names in his survey of the nations surrounding Israel. He introduced them right after the line "Hadad died also" (1 Chronicles 1:51), which follows his list of Edom's early kings. Scripture records no further events connected with Alvah.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Before the twins were born, the Lord told Rebekah that two nations were in her womb (Genesis 25:23). Isaac later spoke of Esau's future living by the sword (Genesis 27:39–40). The organized clans of Genesis 36, Alvah's among them, show that word coming true. Genesis also notes that Edom had kings "before any king reigned over the children of Israel" (Genesis 36:31). Esau's line had organized rule before Israel's monarchy, while Jacob's family went down to Egypt and waited generations for the land promised to them; Genesis does not date each chief's rule. This contrast arises from the text's own arrangement. Reading Alvah as a symbol of "the flesh" goes beyond what Scripture says about him personally.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No connection to Christ is established through Alvah himself. His nation, Edom, later becomes a subject of prophetic judgment (Obadiah; Malachi 1:2–4), and Paul cites God's choice of Jacob over Esau to show that God's saving purposes rest on His mercy, not human standing (Romans 9:10–16). Scripture never singles out Alvah in these passages.

5. Spirit & Life

The early prominence of Edom is a sober counterpoint to Israel's long wait. Edom's earlier organized rule, set beside Israel's wait in the arrangement of Genesis, did not make Esau's line the people through whom God would bless the nations. Even so, God did not leave the Edomite chiefs unnamed. Paul told the Athenians that God "has determined their preappointed times and the boundaries of their dwellings" (Acts 17:26). A brief line like Genesis 36:40 shows that this is true of real clans in real places, even those outside Israel.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:40–43 – Names Alvah among Esau's chiefs and ties the list to families and places.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:51 – Repeats the list with the spelling "Aliah."

  • Genesis 25:23; 27:39–40 – Foretell Esau's descendants becoming a nation.

  • Genesis 36:31 – Contrasts Edom's early kings with Israel's later monarchy.

  • Acts 17:26 – God appoints the times and boundaries of the nations.
