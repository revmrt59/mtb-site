PINON - (pîynôn, Strong's H6373)

Summary

Pinon is one of the chiefs of Edom, the nation descended from Esau, Jacob's twin brother. The name appears only twice, in the closing list of Edomite chiefs in Genesis 36:41 and in the parallel record in 1 Chronicles 1:52. The meaning of the name is uncertain. It is widely thought to share a root with Punon, a place in the Arabah where Israel camped during the wilderness years, but no meaning has been firmly established. Genesis says these chiefs were named "according to their families and their places," so Pinon likely identifies a clan and its territory as well as a leader.

Pinon receives no story, yet the name still carries weight. God had promised Esau a dwelling and a future of his own (Genesis 27:39–40). The organized clans of Edom, Pinon among them, show that promise kept. Long before Israel had a king, Esau's descendants held land, leaders, and settled places. The God who chose Jacob did not forget Esau's line, and Scripture names these clans one by one.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Pinon is listed among "the chiefs of Esau" in Genesis 36:40–43 and again in 1 Chronicles 1:51–54. The Hebrew title translated "chief" designates a clan leader. Genesis 36:40 introduces the list as names given "according to their families and their places, by their names," and verse 43 adds "according to their dwelling places in the land of their possession." Pinon therefore functions as a clan and territorial name, not only as the name of one man. Other names on the same list support this reading. Timnah and Aholibamah appear earlier in the chapter as names of women connected to Esau's household (Genesis 36:12, 14), and here they serve as names of chiefdoms.

The meaning of Pinon is uncertain. Hebrew lexicons commonly connect it with Punon (Strong's H6325, pûnôn), a separately indexed place name in the Arabah, and treat the two as forms of the same name. McClintock and Strong's Cyclopaedia reports older proposals of "darkness" (attributed to Gesenius) and "ore-pit" (attributed to Fürst), but neither has been confirmed, and the Bible offers no wordplay on the name. The text does not record when Pinon lived. It places the chiefs in Edom's early history, alongside the kings who reigned "before any king reigned over the children of Israel" (Genesis 36:31, paraphrased).

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Genesis 36 traces the generations of Esau after he separated from Jacob and settled in Mount Seir (Genesis 36:6–8). The chapter closes with a summary roster of chiefs: "Chief Aholibamah, Chief Elah, Chief Pinon" (Genesis 36:41). First Chronicles 1:52 repeats the list centuries later, showing that Israel kept careful knowledge of Edom's clans and territories. Scripture records nothing Pinon did.

If Pinon is connected with Punon, as is traditionally and probably the case, the clan's territory lay in the Arabah south of the Dead Sea. Israel passed that way near the end of its wandering: "They departed from Zalmonah and camped at Punon. They departed from Punon and camped at Oboth" (Numbers 33:42–43). The site is commonly identified with Wadi Feynan, a region known for extensive ancient copper working. That would make Pinon's territory valuable land, though the Bible itself says nothing about Edom's mines.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states that God gave Mount Seir to Esau as a possession. When Israel approached Edom, God told His people not to provoke them, "because I have given Mount Seir to Esau as a possession" (Deuteronomy 2:4–5, paraphrased). Lists like Genesis 36 show that this gift was real. Edom had settled clans, named leaders, and recognized territories. Pinon is one small piece of that evidence.

A broader pattern can also be drawn from these lists, though Scripture does not state it as a lesson. Edom had kings before Israel did (Genesis 36:31). Esau's line had organized rule before Israel's monarchy, while Jacob's line went down into Egypt and waited on God's timing; Genesis does not date each chief. The same pattern appears elsewhere: Cain's descendants build a city and develop crafts (Genesis 4:17–22), and Ishmael's sons become princes (Genesis 25:16). Early worldly success is never the measure of God's purpose. The promise ran through the line that waited.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Pinon is never mentioned in prophecy or in the New Testament. A connection to Christ comes only through geography, and it should be held loosely. Numbers 21 tells of the fiery serpents and the bronze serpent Moses lifted up. Then Israel "moved on and camped in Oboth" (Numbers 21:10, paraphrased). Numbers 33 lists Punon as the stop just before Oboth. Because of this order, and because the area is rich in copper, many have suggested the bronze serpent was raised at or near Punon. The Bible does not say so directly.

The event itself is firmly tied to Christ, whatever its exact location. Jesus said, "And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of Man be lifted up, that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have eternal life" (John 3:14–15). Whether or not that scene took place in the land of Pinon, Israel was then journeying around the land of Edom (Numbers 21:4), having been refused passage through it (Numbers 20:14–21), and on that wilderness road God provided healing to everyone who looked.

5. Spirit & Life

Genesis 36 is easy to skim, but it tells us something about God. He knows the nations by name, down to their families and places. Edom was not Israel, and Edom's later pride brought severe judgment (Obadiah; Isaiah 34). Even so, God kept His word to Esau, gave his descendants their land, and recorded their chiefs in His Book. The Lord's choice of Jacob never made Him careless toward anyone else. A name like Pinon is a quiet sign that no people and no place lie outside God's attention or His rule.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:40–43 — The full list of Edom's chiefs, named by families, places, and dwelling places; the primary occurrence of Pinon (v. 41).

  • 1 Chronicles 1:51–54 — The chronicler's repetition of the list (Pinon in v. 52).

  • Genesis 36:31 — Edom's kings reigned before Israel had a king.

  • Genesis 27:39–40; Deuteronomy 2:4–5 — God's promise to Esau and His gift of Mount Seir.

  • Numbers 33:42–43 — Punon (H6325), the related place name, as a wilderness camp.

  • Numbers 21:4–10; John 3:14–15 — The bronze serpent and Jesus' interpretation of it; the link to Punon is traditional, not stated.
