PAU - (Pâʻûw, Strong's H6464)

Summary

Pau was a royal city in Edom, the land of Esau's descendants south and southeast of the Dead Sea. Scripture names it only as the city of Hadar, the eighth and last-listed of the Edomite kings in Genesis 36. First Chronicles 1:50 repeats the record, calling the king Hadad and the city Pai. No one knows exactly what the name means. It is often linked to a Hebrew root meaning "to cry out" or "to bleat," but that remains a proposal. The city's location has never been identified.

Pau matters because of the list it belongs to. Genesis introduces these rulers as "the kings who reigned in the land of Edom before any king reigned over the children of Israel" (Genesis 36:31). Esau's family had kings and royal cities before Israel had a king of its own. Jacob's family had households and flocks, but it still awaited the national kingship God had promised. Pau stands at the end of that list as a reminder that God kept His word to Esau, and that early success is not the same as lasting blessing. The fulfillment of God's promise of kings to Jacob came later, and it led to the throne of David and to Christ.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Pau is a city, not a person. It is named twice, both times as the city of Edom's eighth listed king (Genesis 36:39; 1 Chronicles 1:50). Genesis spells the name Pau and the king's name Hadar. Chronicles has Pai and Hadad. The differences are plausibly explained as copying variations, since the Hebrew letters involved closely resemble each other, though the cause is not established. Both forms clearly refer to the same king and city. No other place or person in Scripture bears this name.

The meaning of the name is uncertain. The most common proposal connects it to a Hebrew root meaning "to cry out" or "to bleat." That could point to a pastoral area known for flocks, but this is a guess, not a fact. Scripture makes no wordplay on the name, and no spiritual lesson should be built on it. The city lay somewhere in Edom, but its exact location is unknown.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Genesis 36 records Esau's descendants, and verses 31–39 list eight kings who reigned one after another. None is said to be the son of the king before him. Scripture links several of them to different places. Bela ruled from Dinhabah, Hadad son of Bedad from Avith, and Hadar from Pau. Others are described by their home region: Jobab "of Bozrah," Husham "of the land of the Temanites," Samlah "of Masrekah," and Saul "of Rehoboth-by-the-River."

Pau enters the record at the end of the list: "And when Baal-Hanan the son of Achbor died, Hadar reigned in his place; and the name of his city was Pau" (Genesis 36:39). This verse also names the king's wife, Mehetabel, and two generations of her family: "the daughter of Matred, the daughter of Mezahab." No other king in the list has his wife named. Mezahab is a separately indexed name, often understood to mean "waters of gold." Some have suggested this detail points to a time of wealth or an important marriage alliance at Pau, but the text does not explain why she is mentioned.

Genesis does not record Hadar's death, though 1 Chronicles 1:51 adds that Hadad died. The Chronicler included the whole list in Israel's genealogies after the exile, keeping Pau in the memory of God's people.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture plainly shows that Edom had kings before Israel did (Genesis 36:31). That fits what God had said. He told Rebekah that two nations were in her womb (Genesis 25:23), and He later gave Mount Seir to Esau as his possession (Deuteronomy 2:5). Pau is one small piece of evidence that God did what He said for Esau's line.

The same verse also points forward. God had promised that kings would come from Jacob (Genesis 35:11), yet when Moses wrote Genesis, Israel had no king. The record does not say why Edom's kings came from different places. Many commentators think Edomite kingship passed by choice or strength rather than by family line. That is a reasonable reading but not a stated fact. The different places named with these rulers at least show that Edom's kings were associated with several towns. By contrast, God later fixed Israel's throne in one family and one city, Jerusalem.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Pau itself is never mentioned again, and Scripture does not treat it as a picture of Christ. The list it belongs to does connect to a larger storyline, though. Balaam foresaw "a Star" and "a Scepter" rising from Israel, with Edom becoming a possession (Numbers 24:17–18). David later placed garrisons throughout Edom (2 Samuel 8:14), fulfilling the word that the older would serve the younger (Genesis 25:23). The prophets carried this hope further. Amos spoke of the rebuilt tabernacle of David possessing "the remnant of Edom" (Amos 9:11–12), and in Acts 15:16–17 James applies that passage to Gentiles coming to the Messiah. Edom's early kings, Hadar of Pau among them, came first. The lasting kingdom belongs to David's Son.

5. Spirit & Life

Anyone in Israel reading Genesis 36 had good reason to wonder about it. Esau, who sold his birthright, had kings and royal cities before Israel had any king of its own. God did not hide that fact from His people. He recorded it, and then He kept His slower promise. Pau now cannot even be found on a map, and Scripture says nothing more of it after Hadar. The kingdom God promised through Jacob was delayed, but it was never in doubt. When God's promises seem slow and others appear to have gotten ahead, this short verse is a quiet witness that God is still keeping His word.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:31 – Introduces Edom's kings "before any king reigned over the children of Israel."

  • Genesis 36:32–38 – The list of kings and their places, including the royal cities Dinhabah and Avith.

  • Genesis 36:39 – Names Pau as Hadar's city, along with his wife Mehetabel.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:50–51 – Repeats the record with the forms Hadad and Pai.

  • Genesis 25:23; 35:11 – God's words about two nations and about kings from Jacob.

  • Numbers 24:17–18; 2 Samuel 8:14 – Israel's future kingship over Edom.

  • Amos 9:11–12; Acts 15:16–17 – Edom included in the hope of David's restored kingdom.
