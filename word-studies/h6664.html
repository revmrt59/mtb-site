RIGHTEOUSNESS, JUSTICE, HONEST - (tsedeq, Strong's H6664)

Summary

Tsedeq is the Hebrew word for what is right, meaning what measures up to a true standard. Its roots in Scripture are concrete. The law uses it for honest scales and full weights in the marketplace and for fair verdicts in court. From there the word rises to describe God Himself: His throne rests on righteousness, His Word is righteous through and through, and His judgments never tilt toward the powerful or the bribe.

The prophets show that God's righteousness also moves to rescue. In Isaiah it stands beside His salvation as He acts to deliver His people. Psalm 85 pictures righteousness and peace meeting in a kiss, and Jeremiah announces a coming King from David's line whose name will be "THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS." The standard God requires and His people cannot meet, He supplies in His Messiah. The New Testament brings this to its fullness in Jesus Christ, who "became for us... righteousness" (1 Corinthians 1:30).

Full Study

1. Word Background & Setting

Tsedeq is a masculine Hebrew noun. It belongs to the same family as tsedaqah (H6666), a closely related feminine noun, and the two often appear side by side with overlapping meaning. Tsedeq appears in the law, the Psalms, the wisdom books, and the prophets. The Psalms and Isaiah use it most often.

The NKJV translates it most often as "righteousness." It is also rendered "honest," "just," "justice," "righteously," and "right," and occasionally "cause." Those English choices show the range of settings: a merchant's balance, a judge's bench, a king's rule, a worshiper's prayer, and God's own throne.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

At its heart, tsedeq means rightness: what is true to the standard, accurate, fair, and as it ought to be. Context determines whether the word describes a measurement, a verdict, a way of life, or God's own action.

1. Honest and accurate by a true standard. In Leviticus 19:36 the word appears four times in a single command, requiring honest scales, honest weights, an honest ephah, and an honest hin. A scale with tsedeq gives true weight.

2. Just judgment and right conduct. Judges are to judge their neighbors in righteousness (Leviticus 19:15; Deuteronomy 1:16), and the person who may dwell on God's holy hill is one who works righteousness (Psalm 15:2).

3. A right cause, the claim of someone in the right. David asks God to hear "a just cause" (Psalm 17:1) and speaks of those who "favor my righteous cause" (Psalm 35:27). In Psalm 9:4, the word instead describes God "judging in righteousness."

4. God's righteousness as His faithful, saving action. In Isaiah, God's righteousness draws near alongside His salvation, so the word describes God setting things right for His people (Isaiah 51:5).

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

In the law, tsedeq lives in ordinary places. The command for honest weights in Leviticus 19:36 ends with God reminding Israel that He is the LORD their God who brought them out of Egypt. Their Redeemer cares about the weights in their bags. Deuteronomy 25:15 ties honest weights to long life in the land, and Ezekiel 45:10 repeats the demand for a restored people. God's holiness reaches into commerce.

One grammatical pattern carries real force. In Deuteronomy 16:20 the word is doubled, which the NKJV renders "altogether just." It reads literally as "righteousness, righteousness" you shall pursue. Hebrew repeats a word to intensify it, so Israel was charged to chase justice wholeheartedly and refuse half measures.

In the Psalms the subject shifts more often from human conduct to God. He judges the world in righteousness (Psalm 9:8; 96:13; 98:9), and the heavens declare it: "Let the heavens declare His righteousness, For God Himself is Judge" (Psalm 50:6). In the wisdom books, Proverbs makes tsedeq the foundation of a stable throne (Proverbs 25:5), while Ecclesiastes 3:16 laments finding wickedness in the very place where righteousness should have been.

4. Theological Significance

The Psalms twice declare, "Righteousness and justice are the foundation of Your throne" (Psalm 89:14; see 97:2). God's rule is not arbitrary power. Its foundation is unwavering rightness, so every decree from that throne is trustworthy. Psalm 97:2 adds that "clouds and darkness surround Him." His ways may be hidden from us, but what holds up His throne is not hidden. When believers cannot see what God is doing, they still know what His rule is built on.

That same righteousness fills His Word. Psalm 119 uses tsedeq repeatedly for God's judgments and testimonies: "Your righteousness is an everlasting righteousness, And Your law is truth" (Psalm 119:142), and the psalmist praises God "when I learn Your righteous judgments" (119:7). Because God is right, what He speaks is right. Scripture is the place where His character can be read.

Isaiah then shows that God's righteousness is active. God calls His Servant in righteousness to be a light to the nations and to open blind eyes (Isaiah 42:6–7). He tells His people that His righteousness is near and His salvation has gone forth (Isaiah 51:5). In these passages, God's righteousness is His faithfulness to His promises, moving Him to put wrongs right and deliver those who trust Him.

5. Important Biblical Passages & Distinctions

Psalm 85:10–13 — Righteousness and peace kiss. This psalm prays for a people God has already brought back yet who still need reviving. The answer comes in a striking image: "Mercy and truth have met together; Righteousness and peace have kissed" (85:10). The tension is real. A righteousness that gives true weight must reckon with sin, and that reckoning seems to rule out peace for guilty people. The psalm does not explain how the two meet. It simply announces that they do. Then it adds, "Truth shall spring out of the earth, And righteousness shall look down from heaven" (85:11), with truth rising from below and righteousness bending down from above. The psalm closes with movement: "Righteousness will go before Him, And shall make His footsteps our pathway" (85:13). God's righteousness clears a road His people can walk.

Jeremiah 23:6 and 33:16 — The LORD our righteousness. In Jeremiah 23:5–6, God promises to raise a righteous Branch for David who will reign wisely, and He gives this King a name: "THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS." The rightness Israel's kings failed to practice and the people failed to keep will be found in the King Himself, and it will be called "ours." Jeremiah 33:16 then makes an astonishing move. The same name is given to the city: "And this is the name by which she will be called: THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS." The King's name becomes the name of His people.

Isaiah 11:4–5 — The Branch girded with righteousness. The Spirit-anointed shoot from Jesse will not judge by what His eyes see or His ears hear (11:3). "But with righteousness He shall judge the poor, And decide with equity for the meek of the earth" (11:4). Then: "Righteousness shall be the belt of His loins, And faithfulness the belt of His waist" (11:5). A belt gathered loose garments so a person could act or run. This King's readiness for action is bound up with righteousness, and the poor, who rarely won a hearing in human courts, finally receive one.

Hosea 2:19 — Betrothed in righteousness. After exposing Israel's unfaithfulness, God promises to betroth her to Himself forever, in righteousness and justice, in lovingkindness and mercy. Here tsedeq sits beside tender words in a marriage pledge. God's rightness is the integrity with which He keeps His vow, and it outlasts the bride's failures.

6. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

The story of tsedeq begins with honest scales and ends with a gift. The law demands true weight in every exchange. The Psalms reveal a throne built on that same rightness, and an honest reader of either soon realizes no one measures up. The prophets answer with promise. Isaiah 11 describes a King girded with righteousness, and Daniel 9:24 foresees a time "to make reconciliation for iniquity, To bring in everlasting righteousness." Jeremiah names the King "THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS" and gives that name to His people.

The New Testament declares where these lines meet. At the cross God shows "His righteousness, that He might be just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus" (Romans 3:26). There Psalm 85's kiss finds its deepest fulfillment. The psalm does not predict the cross by name, but the cross is where God's righteousness and peace for sinners stand together without either being compromised. "He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us, that we might become the righteousness of God in Him" (2 Corinthians 5:21). Jeremiah's pattern, in which the King's name becomes the people's name, comes to its fullness in Christ, "who became for us wisdom from God—and righteousness and sanctification and redemption" (1 Corinthians 1:30). Hebrews 1:8–9 applies Psalm 45's words about the king who loves righteousness to the Son. In the gospel, "the righteousness of God is revealed from faith to faith" (Romans 1:17).

7. Spirit & Life

The God who saves sinners is the same God who cares about honest weights. Righteousness received in Christ does not leave daily life untouched. It reaches the invoice, the timesheet, the testimony, and the way we weigh other people's words. Deuteronomy's doubled command still fits those who belong to the righteous King: pursue what is right, and pursue it wholeheartedly.

Psalm 85 also gives believers a way to read their own lives. Righteousness does not stand at a distance waiting to condemn. It "will go before Him, And shall make His footsteps our pathway." The Christian walks in tracks God's righteousness has already laid down. When conscience accuses, the answer is not to lower the standard. The answer is to look at the King whose name is "THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS" and remember that Jeremiah gave His people that same name.

Hosea adds tenderness to the picture. The God of the true balance is also the Bridegroom who pledges Himself forever. His righteousness is the reason His promises can be trusted, even by people whose record says they should be set aside.

8. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Psalm 23:3 — The Shepherd leads His sheep in paths of righteousness for His name's sake. He guides them in the right way because His own reputation is bound up with it.

  • Psalm 17:15 — David expects to behold God's face in righteousness and to be satisfied when he awakes in His likeness, a hope that reaches beyond his present troubles.

  • Isaiah 41:10 — God promises to uphold His fearful people with His righteous right hand. The hand that judges rightly is the hand that holds them up.

  • Zephaniah 2:3 — The humble are urged to seek the LORD, to seek righteousness and humility, in view of the coming day of His anger.

  • Hosea 10:12 — Israel is called to sow in righteousness and to break up fallow ground, because it is time to seek the LORD until He comes and rains righteousness on them.

  • Psalm 4:5 — Worshipers are told to offer sacrifices of righteousness and put their trust in the LORD. Worship and right living belong together.

9. Related Words & Canonical Pairings

  • (a) Lexical relative: H6666 (tsedaqah). This feminine noun from the same word family overlaps heavily in meaning with tsedeq and often appears beside it in the same contexts.

  • (b) Canonical pairing: righteousness and justice (mishpat, H4941). Psalm 89:14 and 97:2 pair tsedeq with justice as the twin foundation of God's throne. Hosea 2:19 joins the same pair to lovingkindness and mercy.

  • (b) Canonical pairing: righteousness and peace (shalom, H7965). Psalm 85:10 sets these two side by side in the image of a kiss.

  • (c) Thematic connection: righteousness from heaven. Psalm 85:11 pictures righteousness looking down from heaven, and Isaiah 45:8 calls on the skies to pour down righteousness. In both, righteousness comes down from God to His people.
