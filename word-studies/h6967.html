HEIGHT, STATURE - (qôwmâh, Strong's H6967)

Summary

The Hebrew word qôwmâh means height. It can describe how tall a building or object is, how tall a person stands, or how high a tree grows. Most of its uses are plain measurements: the height of Noah's ark, the ark of the testimony, the altars, Solomon's temple, and its bronze pillars. In many of these passages God is the one who sets the measurements. People do not design their own way into His presence. He gives the pattern, and He comes to meet His people in it.

The same ordinary word also exposes a human weakness. People are impressed by height, and they read size as a sign of worth, strength, and God's favor. Samuel made that mistake when he looked at Eliab, and the Lord answered, "Do not look at his appearance or at his physical stature... the LORD looks at the heart" (1 Samuel 16:7). Saul had been the tallest man in Israel, and he ended by falling "full length on the ground" in terror. The prophets picture proud empires as towering cedars whose hearts were "lifted up" in their height, and God cuts those trees down. The word's story finds its answer in Jesus. He did not come with an impressive appearance. He humbled Himself to the cross, and God exalted Him. He now builds up His whole church together toward "the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ" (Ephesians 4:13).

Full Study

1. Word Background & Setting

Qôwmâh is a Hebrew noun for vertical measurement. It appears in technical building instructions, in narratives about people, in one love poem, and in prophetic oracles. In ancient Israel, as in many cultures, height suggested strength and capability. A tall warrior or a towering structure looked secure. That common assumption gives this measuring word its moral force when Scripture turns it toward kings and nations.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word means height or stature. Context decides what is being measured:

1. The height of a structure or object, usually given in cubits: "a cubit and a half its height" (Exodus 25:10).

2. A person's bodily stature, such as Eliab's impressive build (1 Samuel 16:7) or Saul's whole length as he collapsed (1 Samuel 28:20).

3. The height of a tree or vine. The prophets use these plants as pictures of kingdoms, as when Assyria is a cedar "of high stature" (Ezekiel 31:3).

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

The NKJV usually translates the word "height." It also uses "stature," "high," "tall," and once "length." The building texts in Exodus and 1 Kings simply list dimensions. The narrative uses describe the human body. In the prophets, height stops being a neutral fact. It becomes the thing a nation trusts and boasts in.

One phrase is worth noting. In 1 Samuel 28:20 the Hebrew pictures Saul falling the whole of his height to the ground. Translators render this as "full length." The tall king now lies stretched out on the dirt.

4. Theological Significance

Two truths run through this word. The first is that God measures the place where He meets His people. Moses was told, "See that you make all things according to the pattern shown you on the mountain" (Hebrews 8:5). The height of the ark, the table, the incense altar, and the bronze altar all came from God (Exodus 25:10, 23; 27:1; 30:2). The ark of the testimony stood only a cubit and a half high, and the altar of burnt offering stood three cubits high (Exodus 27:1). Genesis 11 tells of people who tried to build a tower "whose top is in the heavens." God's sanctuary worked the other way. He set modest, ordered dimensions and came down to dwell among His people. That contrast with Babel comes from comparing the two narratives rather than from a direct statement in the text. It still fits Scripture's consistent picture: access to God is His gift, never a human achievement.

The second truth is that God refuses height as a measure of worth. He judges any height that exalts itself against Him. Ezekiel 17:24 states the principle: "I, the LORD, have brought down the high tree and exalted the low tree."

5. Important Biblical Passages & Distinctions

1 Samuel 16:7 — The Lord looks at the heart. Israel had asked for a king "like all the nations" (1 Samuel 8:5). The people received Saul, of whom it was said, "From his shoulders upward he was taller than any of the people" (1 Samuel 9:2). When Samuel arrived at Jesse's house, he reached for the same standard. Eliab looked like a king. The Lord replied, "Do not look at his appearance or at his physical stature, because I have refused him. For the LORD does not see as man sees; for man looks at the outward appearance, but the LORD looks at the heart." God chose the youngest son instead, a boy out with the sheep (1 Samuel 16:11). In the next chapter, that boy faced the giant Goliath, before whom Saul and all Israel "were dismayed and greatly afraid" (1 Samuel 17:11). By refusing Eliab's stature, God separated His anointing from the world's standard of impressiveness. His kingdom rests on what He sees in a heart.

1 Samuel 28:20 and 2 Kings 25:17 — Height that falls. After the Spirit of the Lord had departed from Saul (1 Samuel 16:14), the once-towering king sought out a medium before his last battle. When he heard Samuel's words, "Saul fell full length on the ground, and was dreadfully afraid... And there was no strength in him." Here "full length" is this word: Saul fell the whole of his height. First Samuel 9:2 used a different Hebrew word to call him taller than all the people, yet the contrast is plain: the man who stood above Israel lay stretched on the ground. Later the Chaldeans broke Solomon's bronze pillars in pieces and carried their bronze to Babylon (2 Kings 25:13), and the accounts of Jerusalem's fall in Second Kings 25:17 and Jeremiah 52:21–22 include the pillars' height, recalling how tall they had stood before they were broken up and taken away. A tall king and a tall temple both failed once God's presence was withdrawn because of disobedience.

Ezekiel 31 — The cedar whose heart was lifted up. Ezekiel warns Pharaoh by pointing to Assyria: "Assyria was a cedar in Lebanon... And of high stature" (Ezekiel 31:3). The prophet then gives the diagnosis: "Because you have increased in height, and it set its top among the thick boughs, and its heart was lifted up in its height" (Ezekiel 31:10). Height led to pride. God delivered the tree to "the mighty one of the nations," and its branches fell "in all the valleys" (Ezekiel 31:11–12). God states His purpose: "so that no trees by the waters may ever again exalt themselves for their height" (Ezekiel 31:14).

The irony is sharp. Assyria's king had once boasted that he would cut down Lebanon's "tall cedars" (2 Kings 19:23; Isaiah 37:24, using this same word). In an earlier oracle about Assyria, Isaiah announced that "those of high stature will be hewn down, and the haughty will be humbled" (Isaiah 10:33). The empire whose king boasted that he would cut down tall trees is itself pictured in Ezekiel 31 as the tall tree brought down.

6. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

The pattern of the high tree brought down and the low tree raised up comes to its fullest expression in Jesus. In Ezekiel 17, God takes a tender twig from the top of the cedar and plants it Himself on a high mountain, where it becomes a sheltering tree (Ezekiel 17:22–23). God Himself, not human ambition, plants and raises this tree. Read in the light of the whole Bible, it points to the King God raises up.

Jesus grew normally: He "increased in wisdom and stature" (Luke 2:52). Yet Isaiah said the Servant would have no outward form or majesty to draw people to Him (Isaiah 53:2). Jesus was chosen as David was, by the Father who sees the heart. His exaltation did not come from rising above others. "He humbled Himself and became obedient to the point of death, even the death of the cross. Therefore God also has highly exalted Him" (Philippians 2:8–9). Even His being "lifted up" (John 3:14) was a lifting up in shame on the cross. Christ is also the true meeting place between God and His people, the reality that the measured tabernacle only shadowed (Hebrews 8:5). The New Testament then gives believers a new measure of height. Christ equips His church "for the edifying of the body of Christ, till we all come to the unity of the faith... to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ" (Ephesians 4:12–13), and the whole body grows up together into Him (Ephesians 4:15–16).

7. Spirit & Life

Height still impresses us. We admire tall leaders, large ministries, and visible success. We assume these things show God's blessing, and we try to look taller ourselves. Samuel was a prophet, and he still made this mistake in Jesse's house. That should make us humble about how quickly we judge by appearance, whether we are judging others or ourselves. The Lord is looking at the heart He is forming, not at the image we project.

Ezekiel 31:10 explains why height is dangerous. The cedar's growth was a gift; God says, "I made it beautiful" (Ezekiel 31:9). But its heart rose along with its branches. Any gift and any success can carry the same temptation. Saul's collapse shows where impressiveness ends without God: "there was no strength in him."

The good news is that God raises the low tree. The Song of Solomon gives a gentler picture of height: "This stature of yours is like a palm tree" (Song of Solomon 7:7). Elsewhere, "The righteous shall flourish like a palm tree" (Psalm 92:12). There is an upright height that pleases God. Exaltation does not produce it; it grows from being planted by Him. Christian maturity is growth into Christ's stature. Christ reached His exaltation by stooping, and so we grow upward by bowing low.

8. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 6:15 — The ark was "thirty cubits" in height. God's first measured provision lifted Noah's family safely above the flood.

  • 1 Kings 6:2 — First Kings gives the height of Solomon's house for the LORD as "thirty cubits." Separately, First Chronicles 28:11–19 records David giving Solomon the plans for the temple, plans David said the LORD made him understand in writing.

  • 1 Kings 6:23 — Inside the inner sanctuary Solomon made two cherubim, each ten cubits high, and their outstretched wings reached from wall to wall across the room (1 Kings 6:23–27).

  • 2 Chronicles 6:13 — Solomon stood on a bronze platform three cubits high, then knelt there to pray. The king raised up before Israel humbled himself before God.

  • Ezekiel 17:6 — Judah is pictured as "a spreading vine of low stature." The vine grew while it stayed low, until it stretched toward another eagle, a picture of Judah turning to Egypt for help (Ezekiel 17:7, 15).

  • Ezekiel 40:5 — In Ezekiel's vision, a man whose appearance was like bronze measures the height of the temple wall. God measures the restored house as well.

9. Related Words & Canonical Pairings

  • G2244 (hēlikia) — This is a distinct Greek word for stature, not a lexical relative of qôwmâh. It is a conceptual counterpart in the New Testament. Jesus "increased in wisdom and stature" (Luke 2:52), and believers grow toward "the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ" (Ephesians 4:13).
