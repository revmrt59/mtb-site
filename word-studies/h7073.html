KENAZ - (Qᵉnaz, Strong's H7073)

Summary

Kenaz is the name of several men in the Old Testament. The first is an Edomite chief, a grandson of Esau through his son Eliphaz (Genesis 36:11, 15). The most significant is a man of Judah remembered as the father of Othniel, the first judge of Israel (Joshua 15:17; Judges 3:9). A third is a later descendant of Caleb, the son of Elah (1 Chronicles 4:15). The meaning of the name is uncertain. Strong's suggests a probable connection with an unused root meaning to hunt, while the NAS lexicon marks the derivation uncertain. Scripture builds nothing on it.

The name matters because of the family it marks inside Israel. Othniel "the son of Kenaz" belonged to the household of Caleb, who is called "the Kenizzite" and is honored because he and Joshua "wholly followed the LORD" (Numbers 32:12). When Israel turned to idolatry after Joshua's death, God answered the people's cry by raising up Othniel. The book of Judges first mentions the Spirit of the LORD in his story: "The Spirit of the LORD came upon him, and he judged Israel" (Judges 3:10). The name Kenaz appears both among Esau's chiefs and in the family of Judah's most faithful warriors. That pattern points ahead to the Spirit-anointed Deliverer God would one day send and to His welcome of people who come to Him from the margins.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

The Hebrew name קְנַז (Qᵉnaz) is held by at least three separate men. They should not be blended into one person.

  • Kenaz the Edomite chief. He was the son of Eliphaz and grandson of Esau (Genesis 36:11; 1 Chronicles 1:36), and he is listed among the chiefs of Esau's sons (Genesis 36:15). The name appears again in the later list of Edomite chiefs arranged "according to their families and their places" (Genesis 36:40–43, with Kenaz at verse 42; compare 1 Chronicles 1:53). That second list may name the clan or district that carried his name rather than the original man himself.

  • Kenaz of Judah, the father of Othniel. He is named in Joshua 15:17, Judges 1:13, 3:9, 3:11, and 1 Chronicles 4:13, where his sons are Othniel and Seraiah. He belongs to the family of Caleb, though the exact relationship is debated (see Section 2).

  • Kenaz the grandson of Caleb. He was the son of Elah, Caleb's son (1 Chronicles 4:15).

The meaning of the name is uncertain. Strong's suggests that Kenaz probably comes from an unused root meaning to hunt, while the NAS Exhaustive Concordance lexicon marks the derivation uncertain. Because the root is not used in the Hebrew Bible itself, "hunter" should be treated as a possible meaning only. No biblical wordplay on the name appears in any passage, so the spiritual weight of Kenaz comes from the people who bore it, not from its etymology.

A related term needs care. Caleb is repeatedly called "the son of Jephunneh, the Kenizzite" (Numbers 32:12; Joshua 14:6). Kenizzite is a separately indexed word (H7074) and is not counted among the eleven occurrences of Kenaz. The Kenezzites also appear in God's promise to Abraham, in the list of peoples whose land his descendants would receive (Genesis 15:19). Many readers connect the Kenizzite family of Caleb with an earlier non-Israelite people, and some link that people to Kenaz of Edom. Scripture does not state either connection directly. The Edomite chief and Othniel's father share a name, but the text never says they share a family tree.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Kenaz among the chiefs of Edom. Genesis 36 traces the descendants of Esau, the brother who sold his birthright and whose line became the nation of Edom in the region of Mount Seir. Kenaz appears there as one of Eliphaz's sons and as a chief (Genesis 36:11, 15, 42). Chronicles repeats the record centuries later (1 Chronicles 1:36, 53). The record gives no story about him. He belongs to the history of a people that grew up alongside Israel and was often hostile to it.

Kenaz of Judah and the household of Caleb. In the time of the conquest the name appears again, now inside Israel. Caleb had been one of the twelve spies and one of the two faithful spies who trusted God's promise at Kadesh Barnea (Numbers 14:24; 32:12). At eighty-five he asked Joshua for the hill country of Hebron, and he received it as his inheritance (Joshua 14:6–14). When he offered his daughter Achsah in marriage to whoever captured Kirjath Sepher (Debir), "Othniel the son of Kenaz, Caleb's younger brother, took it; so he gave him his daughter Achsah as wife" (Judges 1:13; compare Joshua 15:15–17). Achsah then asked her father for springs of water to go with her dry southern land, and he gave them (Joshua 15:18–19; Judges 1:14–15).

The Hebrew phrasing leaves open whether "Caleb's younger brother" describes Othniel or Kenaz. Many interpreters understand Kenaz to be Caleb's younger brother, which would make Othniel Caleb's nephew. Others read Othniel as Caleb's younger brother or half-brother. Either way, Kenaz's son is closely associated in the record with Caleb, and Othniel's own courage at Kirjath Sepher and his deliverance of Israel show the same faith in action. Scripture says nothing directly about his upbringing.

The father of the first judge. After Joshua's generation died, Israel "did evil in the sight of the LORD," served the Baals and Asherahs, and fell under Cushan-Rishathaim, king of Mesopotamia, for eight years (Judges 3:7–8). When the people cried out, the LORD raised up "Othniel the son of Kenaz" as a deliverer (Judges 3:9). The Spirit of the LORD came upon him, he went to war, and the LORD gave the oppressor into his hand (Judges 3:10). The land then had rest forty years until "Othniel the son of Kenaz" died (Judges 3:11). This pattern of sin, oppression, crying out, and deliverance becomes the template for the rest of the book.

Kenaz in the Chronicler's records. Chronicles preserves both uses of the name. Kenaz of Edom stands in the record of the nations (1 Chronicles 1:36, 53). Kenaz of Judah appears in the tribe's genealogy as the father of Othniel and Seraiah (1 Chronicles 4:13). Two verses later, a grandson of Caleb bears the name again: "The son of Elah was Kenaz" (1 Chronicles 4:15). Repeating a family name in a later generation suggests the household valued it, though the text gives no reason for the choice.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

The plain facts are these. Kenaz was a name among Esau's chiefs. It was also the name of the father of Israel's first judge, and it belonged to the family of Caleb, whom Scripture repeatedly calls a Kenizzite and praises for following the LORD wholly. God chose Othniel, the son of this Kenaz, to begin the era of the judges, and the first explicit mention of the Spirit of the LORD in Judges falls on him.

Beyond that, the conclusions are drawn implications. The Kenizzite label suggests that Caleb's household had roots outside the main line of Jacob's sons and was fully joined to Judah, sharing its inheritance and leading in its battles. If that reading is right, the people who trusted God most fully at Kadesh and fought hardest for the land God had promised included a family whose name pointed back to the peoples of Genesis 15:19. God's promise to Abraham always aimed at blessing for "all the families of the earth" (Genesis 12:3), and Caleb's household is an early, quiet sign of that intent.

Othniel's deliverance also shows God's character in a dark period. Israel had forgotten Him, yet He heard their cry and acted through a man He empowered. The deliverance did not come from Israel's strength. It came because the LORD raised up a judge, sent His Spirit, and gave the victory.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Kenaz is never mentioned in the New Testament, but his son's story belongs to a line of development the Bible carries forward. Othniel is the first judge on whom the Spirit of the LORD comes for deliverance. The prophets later describe a coming Servant on whom the Spirit would rest (Isaiah 11:2; 61:1). Jesus read Isaiah 61 in Nazareth and declared it fulfilled in Himself (Luke 4:18–21). Othniel is never called a type of Christ. Still, the pattern Judges begins with him is completed in Jesus: God's people in bondage, a cry for help, and a Spirit-anointed deliverer sent by God. The difference is that Othniel gave forty years of rest and then died (Judges 3:11), while Christ gives a rest that does not end (Hebrews 4:9–10).

There is also a thematic resonance with the inclusion of outsiders. Matthew names Rahab and Ruth in the genealogy of Jesus (Matthew 1:5), and Paul speaks of Gentiles grafted into Israel's olive tree by faith (Romans 11:17–24). If the Kenizzite family of Caleb and Kenaz came from outside Israel, it fits that larger pattern: people welcomed into God's people, not by birth, but by trusting and following Him.

5. Spirit & Life

The household connected to Kenaz is remembered for taking God at His word. Caleb believed the promise when most of Israel shrank back, and he still asked for the hill country at eighty-five. Othniel risked his life at Kirjath Sepher and later led Israel against a foreign king. Neither man treated God's promises as something to admire from a distance. Whatever their family background, they acted as heirs who expected God to keep His word.

The first judge's story also reveals how God rescues. Israel did not earn Othniel. They had served other gods, and God handed them over to oppression. When they cried out, He answered. Judges 3:10 places the credit carefully: the Spirit came upon Othniel, and the LORD delivered the enemy into his hand. That is the shape of the gospel. We are rescued, not by our own strength, but by the Deliverer God sends and empowers. The fitting response is the one Caleb's family modeled: to follow the LORD wholly.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:11, 15, 42; 1 Chronicles 1:36, 53. Kenaz as a grandson of Esau and a chief of Edom.

  • Genesis 15:19. The Kenezzites listed among the peoples of the land promised to Abraham (related term, separately indexed).

  • Numbers 32:12; Joshua 14:6–14. Caleb "the Kenizzite," who wholly followed the LORD and received Hebron (related term, H7074).

  • Joshua 15:15–19; Judges 1:12–15. Othniel son of Kenaz takes Kirjath Sepher and marries Achsah.

  • Judges 3:7–11. Othniel son of Kenaz, the first judge, empowered by the Spirit of the LORD to deliver Israel.

  • 1 Chronicles 4:13, 15. Kenaz as the father of Othniel and Seraiah, and a later Kenaz, the son of Elah and grandson of Caleb.

  • Isaiah 61:1; Luke 4:18–21. The Spirit-anointed Deliverer fulfilled in Christ, completing the pattern Judges begins with Othniel.
