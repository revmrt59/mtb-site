RIPHATH - (Rîyphath, Strong's H7384)

Summary

Riphath was the second-listed son of Gomer, a grandson of Japheth, and a great-grandson of Noah. His name appears only in the family lists of the nations that spread across the earth after the flood (Genesis 10:3; 1 Chronicles 1:6). The meaning of the name is uncertain. Riphath is an ancient tribal name, and no explanation of it has been established. Scripture records no deeds, speeches, or later history for him. He stands in the record as the ancestor of one of the peoples descended from Japheth, traditionally placed in the north.

His two genealogical notices, with no later narrative or prophetic mention, still carry weight. Genesis lists him among the nations just before God calls Abraham and promises that all the families of the earth will be blessed through him (Genesis 12:3). Riphath's descendants belong to that wide Gentile world. Unlike his brothers Ashkenaz and Togarmah, Riphath never reappears in the prophets, yet God still preserved his name in Scripture. The God who "determined their preappointed times and the boundaries of their dwellings" (Acts 17:26) knows peoples that history itself has lost track of.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Riphath is one person, the second-listed of Gomer's three sons. Ashkenaz is named first and Togarmah third (Genesis 10:3). Gomer is the first-listed son of Japheth (Genesis 10:2); the lists do not state birth order. Riphath's line therefore belongs to the Japhethite peoples, who are generally associated with regions north and west of the ancient Near East. Like many names in Genesis 10, Riphath names both an ancestor and the people descended from him. No other biblical person shares the name.

The meaning of the name is unknown. Some have proposed Hebrew roots behind it, but as an old tribal name its etymology remains speculative, and the text offers no wordplay on it. Josephus, the first-century Jewish historian, connected Riphath's descendants with the Paphlagonians of northern Asia Minor along the Black Sea. Others have linked the name to the "Rhipaean Mountains" of classical geography, though ancient writers placed that range in different locations. These are later proposals. Scripture itself does not locate Riphath's people.

The two biblical occurrences differ in spelling. Genesis 10:3 reads "Riphath." The NKJV of 1 Chronicles 1:6 reads, "The sons of Gomer were Ashkenaz, Diphath, and Togarmah." The Hebrew letters for r (resh) and d (dalet) look very much alike, and a scribe could easily confuse them. The Genesis parallel and the Septuagint support "Riphath," and some English versions use that spelling in Chronicles. The person is clearly the same in both lists.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Riphath has no narrative. Genesis 10 opens, "Now this is the genealogy of the sons of Noah: Shem, Ham, and Japheth. And sons were born to them after the flood" (Genesis 10:1). The chapter traces how Noah's family spread out to fill the earth, as God had commanded (Genesis 9:1), and Riphath appears in that account. Centuries later, the Chronicler repeated the list for Israel after the return from exile (1 Chronicles 1:6). Those genealogies begin with Adam and set Israel's story within the history of the whole human race.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states only Riphath's place in the family of nations. Its larger setting supplies the meaning. Genesis 10 lists the scattered peoples, and Genesis 11 explains their scattering at Babel. Genesis 12 then turns to Abraham, through whom God promises to bless "all the families of the earth" (Genesis 12:3). Riphath's people are among those families. The connection is an inference from the order of Genesis, but it is a sound one. The nations are named first so that the reader knows who the promise is for.

His brothers' names return in the prophets. Ashkenaz is summoned against Babylon (Jeremiah 51:27), and "the house of Togarmah" trades horses with Tyre (Ezekiel 27:14; see also 38:6). Riphath never reappears. Scripture gives no reason for his absence. His descendants may have merged into other peoples or moved beyond Israel's horizon, but this cannot be known.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

The New Testament never names Riphath. The connection here is thematic rather than explicit. The promise to Abraham reaches its goal in Christ, whose gospel goes to all nations (Matthew 28:19). John saw "a great multitude which no one could number, of all nations, tribes, peoples, and tongues, standing before the throne and before the Lamb" (Revelation 7:9). The peoples scattered in Genesis 10 are gathered in that vision. The vision does not list specific tribes, but its hope reaches the distant lines whose names, like Riphath's, the Bible mentions only in genealogies.

5. Spirit & Life

Scripture records no personal deeds or speech of Riphath, and his people are not mentioned again in the biblical record. Still, God preserved his name in both Genesis and Chronicles. Paul told the Athenians that God "has made from one blood every nation of men" and fixed the times and boundaries of their dwellings (Acts 17:26). No people is outside God's knowledge or His saving purpose, including those history has forgotten. Christ's gospel goes to them as well.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 10:1–3 – Places Riphath as Gomer's son within Japheth's line after the flood.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:6 – The Chronicler's repetition of the list, where the NKJV spells the name "Diphath."

  • Genesis 12:3 – The promise of blessing for all the families of the earth, which follows the Table of Nations.

  • Jeremiah 51:27; Ezekiel 27:14; 38:6 – Show his brothers reappearing in the prophets while Riphath does not.

  • Acts 17:26 – God's authority over every nation's times and boundaries.

  • Revelation 7:9 – The gathering of all nations before the Lamb.
