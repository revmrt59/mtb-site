SHOBAL - (Shôwbâl, Strong's H7732)

Summary

Shobal is the name of at least two different men in the Old Testament, and they should not be confused. The first is a Horite chief, a son of Seir, whose people lived in the hill country of Seir before Esau's descendants took it over (Genesis 36:20–29; 1 Chronicles 1:38–40). The second is a man of the tribe of Judah, a son of Hur and a descendant of Hezron's son Caleb. Chronicles calls him "Shobal the father of Kirjath Jearim" (1 Chronicles 2:50, 52; 4:1–2). The meaning of the name is uncertain. Proposed meanings include "flowing" or "overflowing" and "traveler" or "pilgrim," but none is certain, and Scripture makes no wordplay on the name.

Scripture tells us nothing about either man's deeds or character. Their importance comes from where God's record places them. The Horite Shobal shows that God tracked the peoples around Israel as carefully as Israel itself. The Judahite Shobal is tied to Kiriath-jearim, the town where the ark of God rested for many years after the Philistines sent it back and before David brought it to Jerusalem (1 Samuel 7:1–2; 1 Chronicles 13:5–6). His brother Salma is called "the father of Bethlehem" (1 Chronicles 2:51). So one household in Judah's records is linked both to the town that sheltered the ark and to the town of David, from which Christ would come.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

At least two separate men bear this name. The Shobal of 1 Chronicles 4:1–2 is commonly identified with the Judahite founder of 1 Chronicles 2, though older lexicons and dictionaries allow that he may be a separate Judahite.

Shobal the Horite is the second-named son of "Seir the Horite who inhabited the land" (Genesis 36:20). Genesis lists him among "the chiefs of the Horites, the sons of Seir, in the land of Edom" (Genesis 36:21, 29). His five sons were "Alvan, Manahath, Ebal, Shepho, and Onam" (Genesis 36:23). The parallel list in 1 Chronicles 1:38–40 gives slightly different spellings for two of these names. He belongs to the patriarchal era, among the original inhabitants of Seir.

Shobal the Judahite is a son of Hur, "the firstborn of Ephrathah," and Hur descends from Caleb (1 Chronicles 2:50). This Caleb is Caleb the son of Hezron, who married Ephrath and fathered Hur (1 Chronicles 2:18–19). Chronicles also names Hur as the grandfather of Bezalel, the craftsman of the tabernacle (1 Chronicles 2:20; Exodus 31:2). If the lists are read together, Shobal belongs to the same household as Bezalel's father, Uri. Shobal's brothers are Salma, "the father of Bethlehem," and Hareph, "the father of Beth Gader" (1 Chronicles 2:51). In these lists, "father of" a town usually means its founding ancestor or clan head, not a biological parent.

The meaning of the name is unknown. The proposals mentioned above ("flowing" or "overflowing," "traveler" or "pilgrim," found in Strong's, the NAS lexicon, Smith, and Easton) come from comparing related words, and none can be confirmed. Because the meaning is this uncertain, no spiritual lesson should be built on it.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

The Horite chief. Genesis 36 records Esau's family and then the earlier people of the land. Deuteronomy explains that the Horites once lived in Seir, but the descendants of Esau drove them out and settled in their place, and that the LORD did this for Esau (Deuteronomy 2:12, 22). Shobal's family was not simply erased, though. His brother Zibeon's line produced Aholibamah, one of Esau's wives (Genesis 36:2, 24–25). Timna, Lotan's sister, became the concubine of Esau's son Eliphaz and the mother of Amalek (Genesis 36:12, 22). Shobal's clan therefore helps explain how Edom and Amalek came to be.

The founder of Kiriath-jearim. Kiriath-jearim first appears as one of the Gibeonite cities that deceived Joshua into a treaty (Joshua 9:17). It later marks Judah's border with Benjamin and is also called Kirjath Baal (Joshua 15:9, 60; 18:14). Chronicles names Shobal as its "father," which suggests that his Judahite clan settled the town and led it at some point after the conquest. Scripture does not explain how this happened.

Chronicles traces Shobal's descendants: "Haroeh, and half of the families of Manuhoth" (1 Chronicles 2:52). It then names the families of Kirjath Jearim, the Ithrites, Puthites, Shumathites, and Mishraites, from whom came the people of Zorah and Eshtaol (1 Chronicles 2:53). In 1 Chronicles 4:1, the Chronicler lists "Perez, Hezron, Carmi, Hur, and Shobal" among the sons of Judah. This compressed list of descendants or clan heads clearly places Shobal within Judah, though Carmi's place in it and its relation to the Caleb line of chapter 2 are debated. The Chronicler names Shobal because his family mattered in Judah's memory. The next verse continues through Shobal's son Reaiah to the families of the Zorathites (1 Chronicles 4:2).

The town that held the ark. Shobal's town gained its greatest importance long after his lifetime. When the Philistines returned the ark, it came to the house of Abinadab "on the hill" in Kiriath-jearim. There his son Eleazar was consecrated to keep it (1 Samuel 7:1). Twenty years passed while "all the house of Israel lamented after the LORD" (1 Samuel 7:2, paraphrased). The ark stayed there until David gathered Israel to bring it up (1 Chronicles 13:5–6; 2 Samuel 6:2).

The town's later history is also recorded. The prophet Urijah of Kiriath-jearim preached the same warning as Jeremiah and was killed by King Jehoiakim (Jeremiah 26:20–23). After the exile, men of the town returned to the land (Ezra 2:25; Nehemiah 7:29). These were the people for whom Chronicles was written.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

The main identifying facts Scripture states about these men are these: the Horite Shobal was a chief in Seir, and the Judahite Shobal was a son of Hur and the father of Kiriath-jearim. Everything else is a careful conclusion drawn from where they appear.

The Horite record shows that God oversees the history of nations other than Israel. Moses says the LORD gave Seir to Esau (Deuteronomy 2:5, 12). In the same way, He gave Canaan to Israel. The peoples who came before Esau were recorded by name, not forgotten.

The Judahite record matters because of what it meant to returning exiles. Chronicles was written for a small community rebuilding in a land that seemed emptied of its past. When they read "Shobal the father of Kirjath Jearim," they learned that the town where the ark had once waited was part of their own family inheritance. The record told them they belonged to a story God had not abandoned. It should be stated plainly that Shobal himself was long dead before the ark arrived. Scripture never credits him with guarding it. The connection is to his town and to the families who descended from him.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Shobal is not named in the New Testament and is not in Jesus' direct ancestry. Matthew and Luke trace Jesus through Hezron's son Ram, not through Caleb and Hur (Matthew 1:3–4; Luke 3:33). The connection to Christ comes through the places linked to Shobal's family, not through Shobal himself.

Psalm 132 recalls David's longing to find a dwelling place for the LORD. It describes hearing of the ark "in Ephrathah" and finding it "in the fields of the woods," most likely a reference to Kiriath-jearim, whose name suggests "town of forests" (Psalm 132:6, paraphrased). The psalm then joins this search with God's sworn promise of a son of David on the throne (Psalm 132:11–12, 17). In Chronicles, Ephrathah is the mother of Hur, whose sons are named as the "father" (clan founder or head) of Kiriath-jearim and of Bethlehem (1 Chronicles 2:50–51). Micah later names "Bethlehem Ephrathah" as the birthplace of the ruler "whose goings forth are from of old" (Micah 5:2), and Matthew applies that prophecy to Jesus (Matthew 2:5–6).

This is a real canonical link between places, not a claim that Shobal is a type of Christ. The household linked as ancestral clan founders with the ark's waiting place was also linked with the town where God's promised King would be born. The God who once let His ark rest in a forest town on Judah's border later sent His Son to be born in a small town of the same family region.

5. Spirit & Life

The Horite list is a quiet rebuke to any assumption that God cares only about the people at the center of the story. He knew the chiefs of Seir by name, along with their sons and their marriages, and He governed what happened to their land.

Kiriath-jearim teaches something about God's dealings with His people when worship has gone wrong. After Shiloh's priests had despised the LORD and the ark had been captured, the ark did not return to a grand sanctuary. It went to an ordinary household on a hill, where one consecrated man kept it for years while Israel mourned and waited (1 Samuel 4; 7:1–2). God did not need an impressive setting. He needed people willing to keep what was holy and a nation brought to lament after Him.

Scripture says nothing about Shobal's personal faith, so we should not invent virtues for him. What we can say is that the families and towns recorded in these lists became the settings where God later acted. For returning exiles, and for us, that is reason enough to read genealogies with patience. God remembers where His people came from, and He keeps His promises across generations that never saw their fulfillment.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:20–23, 29 – Shobal the Horite, a son of Seir and chief in Edom, with his five sons.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:38, 40 – The Chronicler's parallel record of the Horite Shobal.

  • Deuteronomy 2:12, 22 – The Horites of Seir displaced by Esau's descendants under God's direction.

  • 1 Chronicles 2:18–20, 50–53 – Shobal the son of Hur, "the father of Kirjath Jearim," with his descendants and the families of the town.

  • 1 Chronicles 4:1–2 – Shobal named in a compressed list of Judah's descendants, continuing through Reaiah.

  • Joshua 9:17; 15:9, 60 – Kiriath-jearim as a Gibeonite city and later a border town of Judah.

  • 1 Samuel 7:1–2; 1 Chronicles 13:5–6 – The ark's stay at Kiriath-jearim and David's move to bring it up.

  • Psalm 132:6, 11–12 – The ark found "in the fields of the woods," joined with God's oath to David.

  • Micah 5:2; Matthew 2:5–6 – Bethlehem Ephrathah as the birthplace of the promised Ruler.
