SHELAH - (Shelach, Strong's H7974)

Summary

Shelah was a son of Arphaxad and grandson of Shem. He lived in the generations after the Flood and was the father of Eber. His name, Hebrew Shelach (שֶׁלַח), comes from a root meaning "to send." Its exact meaning as a personal name is uncertain. The noun can mean "a missile" or "weapon," something sent out, and it can also mean "a shoot" or "sprout," something a plant sends forth. Genesis records nothing he did beyond living, fathering children, and dying at 433. Yet his name stands in the line Genesis traces from Shem to Abram, and Luke names him again among the ancestors of Jesus.

This Shelah should not be confused with Judah's son Shelah, who has a different Hebrew name. The only real difficulty attached to the patriarch concerns his father. The Hebrew text of Genesis and 1 Chronicles makes Shelah the son of Arphaxad, while Luke places a man named Cainan between them. Shelah's life is a quiet one. God carried His promise forward through it toward Abraham and, in the end, toward Christ.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Shelah is the post-Flood patriarch listed as Arphaxad's son and Eber's father (Genesis 10:24; 11:12–15; 1 Chronicles 1:18, 24). All seven verses indexed under H7974 refer to this one man. The NKJV spells his name "Salah" in Genesis and "Shelah" in 1 Chronicles, but the person is the same. In Luke's Greek genealogy he appears in the list of Jesus' ancestors.

The name comes from the Hebrew root sh-l-ch, "to send." As a noun, shelach can mean a "missile," "weapon," or "dart." It can also mean a "shoot" or "sprout," the growth a plant sends out. Either meaning could lie behind the name, and the text does not tell us which one his parents intended. Genesis also makes no wordplay on his name, so neither meaning should be read as a summary of his life.

Two other names are easily confused with this one, the Shelah son of Arphaxad:

  • Shelah, son of Judah (Genesis 38) is a different man, and his Hebrew name is spelled differently (H7956).

  • The Pool of Shelah in Jerusalem (Nehemiah 3:15) shares the same root but is a place, indexed separately from this entry. It is generally identified with the Pool of Siloam, which John explains as meaning "Sent" (John 9:7).

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Genesis gives Shelah's life in the same plain formula it uses for each generation from Shem to Abram. "Arphaxad lived thirty-five years, and begot Salah" (Genesis 11:12). Since Arphaxad was born two years after the Flood (Genesis 11:10), Shelah was born within a few decades of it, in a world being repopulated by Noah's descendants. Then "Salah lived thirty years, and begot Eber. After he begot Eber, Salah lived four hundred and three years, and begot sons and daughters" (Genesis 11:14–15). That gives a lifespan of 433 years. This is long by our standards, but well below the near-thousand-year lives before the Flood. Across Genesis 11 the lifespans show an overall decline, though not in every generation: Eber's 464 years exceed Shelah's 433, Peleg and Reu both reach 239, and Terah's 205 exceed Nahor's 148 (by the Masoretic figures).

His grandson was Peleg, "for in his days the earth was divided" (Genesis 10:25). Genesis places Shelah's family in the era of the scattering of nations, though the text does not say whether Peleg's "division" is the same event as Babel.

Centuries later, the Chronicler repeated Shelah's name in the genealogy that opens 1 Chronicles (1:18, 24). He wrote for a people returning from exile, and the list reminded them that their history reached back through Abraham and Shem to the beginning.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Genesis 10 spreads out the families of all nations, but Genesis 11:10–26 narrows to one line from Shem to Abram. Noah had spoken blessing over Shem (Genesis 9:26), and this genealogy shows God keeping that line through generation after generation until He calls Abram in Genesis 12. Shelah is one link in that chain.

His son Eber heads the family Genesis calls "all the children of Eber" (Genesis 10:21), from which Abram came. Many connect Eber's name with the word "Hebrew," though Scripture does not state the link. What Scripture states plainly is Shelah's place in the line. That God was quietly preserving His purpose through such names is a fair inference from where Genesis places this genealogy, between the scattering at Babel and the call of Abram.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Luke traces Jesus' ancestry back through "Eber, the son of Shelah" (Luke 3:35) and continues: "the son of Cainan, the son of Arphaxad, the son of Shem, the son of Noah, the son of Lamech" (Luke 3:36). Shelah is therefore an ancestor of the Messiah by explicit New Testament testimony. The same God who kept this family line intact through the generations after the Flood brought His Son into the world through it.

Luke's inclusion of Cainan differs from Genesis 11:12 and 1 Chronicles 1:24 in the Hebrew text, which have no generation between Arphaxad and Shelah. Ancient Greek copies of Genesis include Cainan, and Luke's list matches them. Interpreters have explained the difference in several ways:

  • Luke may have followed the familiar Greek Old Testament.

  • Biblical genealogies sometimes include or omit intermediate ancestors, since their purpose is to show descent rather than to give a complete record of every generation.

  • Some point to questions about how the name was handed down in copies of Luke's text.

Readers can hold the difference honestly without doubting either Genesis or Luke on the main point, which both affirm: Shelah stands in the line that leads to Christ.

Some have suggested that the meaning "shoot" links Shelah to the prophetic "Branch" or to the "Rod from the stem of Jesse" (Isaiah 11:1). However, Isaiah uses different Hebrew words, and no biblical text makes that connection. Shelah's name also shares a root with the Pool of Siloam, "Sent," where Jesus healed the man born blind. That is a shared root, not a link Scripture draws. The real connection to Christ is the genealogical one Luke states.

5. Spirit & Life

Scripture records no speech, journey, or act of faith for Shelah. Genesis preserves only brief genealogical notices of him, and the account of his own years occupies just two verses (Genesis 11:14–15). Yet the Holy Spirit kept his name in Genesis, repeated it in Chronicles, and placed it in the family tree of Jesus. God's purposes moved forward through years in which nothing seemed to happen beyond births and deaths. The promise to bless the world did not wait for a dramatic hero. It passed through ordinary households until Abram heard God's call. God knows the names of the people He has used to carry His purposes forward, even those history otherwise forgets.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 10:24 places Shelah in the Table of Nations, between Arphaxad and Eber.

  • Genesis 11:12–15 gives his birth, his fathering of Eber at thirty, his remaining 403 years, and his other children.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:18, 24 repeats his place in Israel's family history for the post-exilic community.

  • Luke 3:35–36 names him among Jesus' ancestors and includes Cainan between him and Arphaxad.

  • Genesis 9:26; 11:10–26 sets out the blessing on Shem and the narrowing line toward Abram.
