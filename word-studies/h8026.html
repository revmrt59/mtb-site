SHELEPH - (sheleph, Strong's H8026)

Summary

Sheleph is one of the thirteen sons of Joktan named in the Table of Nations (Genesis 10:26) and again in the Chronicler's opening genealogy (1 Chronicles 1:20). He belongs to the family of Shem through Eber, and his descendants were among the Joktanite peoples whose homeland Genesis describes as stretching "from Mesha as you go toward Sephar, the mountain of the east" (Genesis 10:30). His name is commonly connected with a Hebrew root meaning "to draw out," so it is often rendered "drawn out." Because the name most likely belongs to an Arabian tribe, that meaning should be treated as probable rather than certain.

Scripture tells no story about Sheleph. Yet his name is preserved twice, once in the record of the nations after the Flood and again centuries later, after Israel's exile. The God who chose Abraham through Eber's son Peleg also kept a record of Peleg's nephews, whose peoples are usually placed in Arabia, far to the south. Sheleph stands among the peoples whom God made, placed, and did not forget, the same nations His promise to Abraham would one day reach.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Sheleph is an individual listed second among the sons of Joktan. His line runs Shem, Arphaxad, Shelah, Eber, Joktan (Genesis 10:22–26; 1 Chronicles 1:17–20). His uncle was Peleg, through whom the line continues to Abraham (Genesis 11:16–26). Like his brothers, Sheleph is best understood as the ancestor of a people as well as a person. The Table of Nations often uses personal names for tribes descended from them.

The name is usually linked to the Hebrew verb shalaph (H8025), "to draw out" or "pull off." That word is used of drawing a sword or pulling off a sandal, and the link yields the sense "drawn out." This is a probable derivation, not a settled one. Many of Joktan's sons carry names tied to South Arabian peoples and places, so Sheleph may be a tribal name whose original meaning lies outside Hebrew. The text offers no wordplay on the name, and nothing in Scripture attaches spiritual meaning to it.

Scholars have proposed connections with a South Arabian tribe called the Salapeni, mentioned by the geographer Ptolemy, and with a later Yemeni district or tribe called Sulaf or Salif. These identifications are plausible but not proven.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Sheleph appears only in two genealogies. In Genesis 10, the family of Eber divides: "the name of one was Peleg, for in his days the earth was divided; and his brother's name was Joktan" (Genesis 10:25). Genesis follows Peleg toward Abraham but first names all of Joktan's sons and records where they lived (Genesis 10:26–30). First Chronicles 1:20 repeats Sheleph in the same position, and Chronicles repeats the genealogy (with small spelling differences elsewhere, such as Obal and Ebal) as part of Israel's account of its place among the nations after the return from exile.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states that Sheleph was a son of Joktan and that the Joktanites settled in the east. It is a fair inference that Genesis names such peoples before narrowing to Abraham in order to set God's choice of one family within His care for the whole human race. The same pattern appears with Ishmael and Esau, whose lines are recorded before the story returns to Isaac and Jacob (Genesis 25:12–18; 36:1–43).

The Chronicler's repetition of the name carries weight. A people just returned from exile, small and struggling, began their history with Adam and the nations. Israel's God was never a merely local god. Paul said the same at Athens: God "has made from one blood every nation of men to dwell on all the face of the earth, and has determined their preappointed times and the boundaries of their dwellings" (Acts 17:26).

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No direct connection to Christ is established for Sheleph. The broader setting still matters. Peleg's line leads to Abraham, to whom God promised that "in you all the families of the earth shall be blessed" (Genesis 12:3). The families listed beside that line, Sheleph's among them, are the kind of peoples the promise was meant to reach through Abraham's Seed.

5. Spirit & Life

If the proposed Arabian identifications are right, Sheleph's descendants lived far from Jerusalem and from the main events of biblical history; Genesis alone cannot fix their exact boundaries. Even so, God had their names written down twice. That record does not make Sheleph a hero or a lesson. It shows a God whose knowledge of the world reaches beyond the center of the story to its edges, and who sets the boundaries of every people with purpose.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 10:25–30: Sheleph's place among Joktan's sons, his relation to Peleg, and the Joktanites' territory.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:17–20: the post-exilic repetition of his lineage.

  • Genesis 11:16–26: Peleg's line, which leads to Abraham.

  • Genesis 12:3: the promise that all the families of the earth will be blessed in Abraham.

  • Acts 17:26: God's sovereign ordering of the nations and their dwellings.
