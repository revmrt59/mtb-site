SAMLAH - (Samlâh, Strong's H8072)

Summary

Samlah was an early king of Edom, the nation descended from Esau. He is the fifth ruler named in the Edomite king list of Genesis 36, and the same list is repeated in 1 Chronicles 1. His name most likely means "garment" or "mantle." It matches the ordinary Hebrew word for a cloak or wrapper, though Scripture never comments on why he bore it. The Bible tells us only three things about him: he came from a place called Masrekah, he succeeded Hadad the son of Bedad, and Saul of Rehoboth-by-the-River followed him. We know nothing of his character, his deeds, or his worship.

Even so, his place in the record matters. Moses introduces these rulers as "the kings who reigned in the land of Edom before any king reigned over the children of Israel" (Genesis 36:31). Samlah sat on a throne while Jacob's family was still waiting for the king God had promised them. The list quietly reminds the reader that early political success did not reveal where God's chosen line ran. The kingdom God was building through Israel took longer to appear, and it led in the end to David and to Christ.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Samlah is a single individual, a king of Edom, and all four occurrences refer to him (Genesis 36:36–37; 1 Chronicles 1:47–48). He is called "Samlah of Masrekah," which connects him with a town whose location is now unknown.

His name most likely means "garment" or "mantle." It shares its form with simlah (H8071), the common Hebrew word for an outer garment or wrapper. That connection is a reasonable lexical identification rather than an explanation the Bible gives. Scripture offers no wordplay on the name and draws no lesson from it, so it should not be read as a key to his life.

The name of his hometown, Masrekah (H4957), is probably related to a Hebrew word for a choice vine. If so, it may point to a vine-growing area within Edom. That derivation is probable, not certain.

This Saul, Samlah's successor, is an Edomite ruler and should not be confused with Saul, the first king of Israel.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Scripture records only Samlah's succession: "When Hadad died, Samlah of Masrekah reigned in his place" (Genesis 36:36), and "when Samlah died, Saul of Rehoboth-by-the-River reigned in his place" (Genesis 36:37). First Chronicles 1:47–48 repeats the account word for word. That repetition preserved Edom's early history for Israel's readers long after the events.

The wider list is unusual. Several of the first six kings in Genesis 36:32–37 are linked with a city or region: Bela with Dinhabah, Jobab with Bozrah, Husham with the land of the Temanites, Hadad with Avith, Samlah with Masrekah, and Saul with Rehoboth. The list of eight continues to Baal-Hanan, identified only by his father, and to Hadar of Pau (36:38–39), so it is not a complete roster of different capitals or birthplaces. No king is said to be the son of the king before him. This may suggest that early Edom did not pass its throne from father to son. Whether its kings were chosen, rose by strength, or led a loose alliance of towns, the text does not say.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Genesis 36:31 is the key to reading this list. Scripture states plainly that Edom had kings before Israel did. God had told Abraham that kings would come from him (Genesis 17:6). Through Esau, that promise saw an early and partial fulfillment. God's separate promise to Jacob that "kings shall come from your body" (Genesis 35:11, paraphrased) was still waiting while Samlah reigned.

It is a fair inference that Moses wanted Israel to notice the contrast. The brother who had been set aside reached royal power first. Israel's kingship came later, by God's own timing. When it came, God promised David a lasting house (2 Samuel 7:16), which looks very different from Edom's shifting line of rulers.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Samlah is not a type of Christ, and the New Testament never mentions him. His list does belong to a larger biblical thread, however. "Before any king reigned over the children of Israel" (Genesis 36:31) looks ahead to the king God would give Israel. Genesis 49:10 promises that the scepter will not depart from Judah, and Balaam foresees a Star from Jacob, with Edom becoming a possession (Numbers 24:17–18).

Obadiah, prophesying against Edom, closes with these words: "the kingdom shall be the LORD's" (Obadiah 21, paraphrased). Samlah's brief biblical notice stands among the early kingdoms that came and went while God was preparing the throne of David's Son.

5. Spirit & Life

The record of Samlah does not teach a moral lesson from his life, because Scripture gives us no account of his life. What it does show is God's way of working. Edom had kings, towns, and an orderly succession while Israel's promised kingship had not yet appeared. Yet the promise proved more substantial than Edom's throne. Visible power and early establishment are not proof of God's favor. The people of God are often called to wait while others seem to arrive first, trusting that what God has spoken will come in its time.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:31 – Frames the list: Edom's kings reigned before Israel had a king.

  • Genesis 36:32–37 – The first six kings of the succession, several linked with a city or region.

  • Genesis 36:36–37 – Samlah of Masrekah succeeds Hadad, and Saul succeeds him.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:47–48 – The parallel record preserved for later Israel.

  • Genesis 17:6; 35:11 – God's promises of kings to Abraham and to Jacob.
