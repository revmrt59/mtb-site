SHEPHO / SHEPHI - (Shᵉphôw, Strong's H8195)

Summary

Shepho was a Horite of Mount Seir, the rugged highland south of the Dead Sea that later became Edom. He was the son of the chief Shobal and a grandson of Seir the Horite. Genesis does not list Shepho himself among the chiefs. Genesis spells his name Shepho (Genesis 36:23), and 1 Chronicles spells it Shephi (1 Chronicles 1:40). The meaning of the name is uncertain. Lexicons link it to a Hebrew root meaning "to sweep bare" or "be smooth," which suggests a meaning such as "bareness" or "smoothness." Some scholars, however, connect the Horites with the non-Semitic Hurrians, and if they are right, the Hebrew derivation may not apply.

Scripture records no deeds of Shepho, yet his people have a notable place in the Bible's story. The Horites held Seir until the descendants of Esau dispossessed them (Deuteronomy 2:12, 22). God had given those mountains to Esau (Joshua 24:4). Moses later pointed to this as a working example of what the LORD would do for Israel in Canaan. Shepho's name, kept in two genealogies, shows that God's record of the nations includes real families, even those that passed out of history.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Shepho is the fourth of five sons of Shobal named in the lists: "Alvan, Manahath, Ebal, Shepho, and Onam" (Genesis 36:23). Shobal was a son of Seir the Horite and is listed among the "chiefs of the Horites" (Genesis 36:20, 29). No one else in Scripture bears this name. Genesis has Shepho and Chronicles has Shephi, and the two forms differ only in their final letter. Spelling variations of this kind are common between Genesis and Chronicles. The parallel list shows a similar shift in the name of Shepho's brother, Alvan in Genesis and Alian in Chronicles.

The name probably comes from a Hebrew root meaning "to sweep bare" or "be smooth." That would give a meaning like "bareness," and the name may have described a bare hill or open landscape. This derivation is not certain. It assumes the name is Semitic, and the Horites' ethnic identity is still debated.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Shepho has no narrative of his own. He appears in the section of Esau's family records that lists the original inhabitants of Seir (Genesis 36:20–30). The Chronicler repeats this list in his survey of the nations that surrounded Israel, before he narrows his focus to Jacob's line (1 Chronicles 1:38–42). Deuteronomy tells what became of these clans: "The Horites formerly dwelt in Seir, but the descendants of Esau dispossessed them and destroyed them from before them, and dwelt in their place" (Deuteronomy 2:12; compare 2:22).

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states plainly that God gave Seir to Esau (Joshua 24:4) and that the LORD drove out the Horites before Esau's descendants (Deuteronomy 2:22). Moses tells this history while Israel stands on the edge of its own inheritance, and he draws the parallel himself: Esau's people did "just as Israel did to the land of their possession which the LORD gave them" (Deuteronomy 2:12). Shepho belongs to the people who lost that land. His people were established and organized under chiefs, yet they were displaced when God assigned Seir to another people. One reasonable conclusion follows from this. No ruling family's hold on a land is permanent apart from the will of the God who "set the boundaries of the peoples" (Deuteronomy 32:8).

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Later Scripture does not use Shepho prophetically, and no connection to Christ is established.

5. Spirit & Life

A clan that vanished from history could have been recorded simply as "the Horites." Instead, Scripture names Shobal's sons one by one, including Shepho. The God who governs the rise and fall of nations does not deal with peoples as faceless masses. He knows the families within them. That should make us humble about how secure any earthly position really is, and it should also assure us that no one escapes God's notice.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:20–23, 29–30 – Identifies Shepho as the son of Shobal, who is listed among the Horite chiefs of Seir.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:40 – Gives the parallel listing with the spelling Shephi.

  • Deuteronomy 2:12, 22 – Records the dispossession of the Horites by Esau's descendants.

  • Joshua 24:4 – States that God gave the mountains of Seir to Esau.

  • Deuteronomy 32:8 – Affirms God's sovereign allotment of the nations.
