SIXTY - (shishshîym, Strong's H8346)

Summary

The Hebrew word shishshîym is the ordinary number "sixty." It has no hidden meaning of its own. It counts years, soldiers, cities, offerings, and cubits, and it appears inside larger numbers such as sixty-five or three hundred sixty. Its interest for Bible readers comes from where Scripture places it. A number in the Bible is part of a record kept by a God who pays attention to exact years, real people, and actual walls.

Several of these sixties carry real spiritual weight. Isaac was sixty when Jacob and Esau were born, twenty years after he married Rebekah, and Genesis tells us the birth came in answer to his prayer for his barren wife. In Leviticus, sixty is the age at which the valuation schedule for a person vowed to the Lord drops to a lower figure. The "sixty cities" of Bashan, walled and barred, became a lasting reminder that God gave His people what they could never have taken alone. Through Isaiah, God told a frightened king that "within sixty-five years" his enemy would be broken. In the Song of Solomon, sixty valiant men guard Solomon's couch (Song of Solomon 3:7), and in a separate scene the king's one beloved is praised above sixty queens (6:8–9). Taken together, these passages show a God who keeps exact time, measures human strength with mercy, and overcomes what His people fear.

Full Study

1. Word Background & Setting

Shishshîym is the Hebrew word for "sixty." In Hebrew the tens from thirty to ninety are formed from the plural of the matching single number, so "sixty" is built from shêsh, "six" (H8337). The word appears in genealogies, census lists, sacrificial records, conquest accounts, royal administration, poetry, prophecy, and Ezekiel's vision of the temple. The NKJV translates it "sixty" every time.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The word has one meaning. Shishshîym is the number sixty, whether it stands alone, as in the sixty rams of Numbers 7:88, or forms part of a larger figure, such as Methuselah's nine hundred sixty-nine years in Genesis 5:27. Any significance beyond the number itself comes from the passage, never from the word.

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

Most uses are records. Genesis 5 counts the ages of the patriarchs. Numbers counts tribes, such as Dan's 62,700 (Numbers 1:39). Ezra 2:64 totals the returning exiles at 42,360. These figures show that Scripture's history is concrete and accountable. God's dealings with His people happened in years that could be numbered and among people who could be counted.

Sometimes the number marks a limit set by law, as in Leviticus 27. Sometimes it is a prophetic deadline, as in Isaiah 7:8. In the Song of Solomon it counts the valiant men who guard Solomon's couch (Song of Solomon 3:7) and, in a separate scene, the queens who praise the king's one beloved (6:8–9). Ezekiel 40:14 uses it as a measurement in the visionary temple, where the gateposts are sixty cubits. The setting decides what the number contributes.

4. Theological Significance

These passages consistently show God's exact rule over time, human capacity, and obstacles that look impossible to overcome. He counts the years of a barren couple's waiting and the years left to a hostile kingdom. He sets a lower valuation for those over sixty and makes room for the poor who cannot pay. He brings down walls that made His people feel small. The same God who lowered the valuation for a person over sixty overthrew sixty fortified cities. He is gentle with the weakness of those who come to Him and unstoppable against what opposes Him. Nothing in these records is vague, because the Lord's faithfulness works through specific days, specific people, and specific places.

5. Important Biblical Passages & Distinctions

Isaac at sixty (Genesis 25:20–26). Genesis gives two ages that are easy to read past. "Isaac was forty years old when he took Rebekah as wife" (25:20), and "Isaac was sixty years old when she bore them" (25:26). Twenty years lie between those two verses. The line of promise had already passed through Sarah's barrenness, and now it stalled again. Genesis does not say when Isaac began to pray or how long he prayed, but it tells us what he did: "Isaac pleaded with the LORD for his wife, because she was barren; and the LORD granted his plea" (25:21). The promise did not continue by natural course. It continued because God answered prayer. The number sixty, set beside the forty of verse 20, shows how long Isaac and Rebekah waited for children.

The valuation at sixty (Leviticus 27:3, 7). When someone consecrated a person to the Lord by a vow, the vow was fulfilled by paying that person's set value to the sanctuary. Leviticus 27 gives a schedule of these values by age and sex. For a man "from twenty years old up to sixty years old," the valuation was "fifty shekels of silver" (27:3). From sixty years and older, it dropped to fifteen shekels for a man and ten for a woman (27:7). The schedule appears to follow a person's working strength rather than personal worth. It is a fixed table, not a price adjusted to what each worshiper could afford. That separate mercy comes in verse 8: anyone "too poor to pay your valuation" could come before the priest, who would set a value "according to the ability of him who vowed." Between the fixed schedule and the provision for the poor, no one was shut out of making a vow. A similar sense of measure appears when Levites retired from heavy tabernacle work at fifty (Numbers 8:25).

The sixty cities of Argob (Deuteronomy 3:4–5; Joshua 13:30; 1 Kings 4:13). Moses recalls the defeat of Og of Bashan: "there was not a city which we did not take from them: sixty cities, all the region of Argob" (Deuteronomy 3:4). He adds why the number mattered: "All these cities were fortified with high walls, gates, and bars" (3:5). A generation earlier, the report of walled cities had sent Israel back into the wilderness (Numbers 13:28). Now sixty such cities fell. Joshua 13:30 assigns them as an inheritance. Centuries later, 1 Kings 4:13 still describes them as "sixty large cities with walls and bronze gate-bars," now administered by Solomon's officer. The walls that once frightened Israel came to protect Israel.

Sixty-five years (Isaiah 7:1–9). When Syria and Ephraim marched on Jerusalem, Ahaz's heart shook "as the trees of the woods are moved with the wind" (7:2). God sent Isaiah to tell him, "do not fear," and to call the two kings "two stubs of smoking firebrands" (7:4). Then God gave an exact deadline: "Within sixty-five years Ephraim will be broken, So that it will not be a people" (7:8). The deadline was given to steady the king, and it came with a warning: "If you will not believe, Surely you shall not be established" (7:9). Isaiah does not say which event marks the end of the sixty-five years. Samaria fell to Assyria in the days of Ahaz's son Hezekiah (2 Kings 18:9–10). Many interpreters propose that the full span reaches the later resettlement of the land by foreign peoples (2 Kings 17:24; compare Ezra 4:2), after which Ephraim no longer existed as a people, but Scripture does not draw that connection itself. Ahaz, however, turned to Assyria for help instead (2 Kings 16:7).

6. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

Isaac's sixty years belong to the long line of births that only God could give. That line runs from Sarah to Rebekah to Rachel and on to Elizabeth, and it reaches its fullness in the virgin birth of Jesus. The promised Seed never came through human strength alone.

The sixty-five years of Isaiah 7:8 stand in the same chapter as the sign Ahaz refused: "the virgin shall conceive and bear a Son" (Isaiah 7:14), which Matthew applies directly to Jesus (Matthew 1:23). In that chapter, God's exact timetable for judgment and His promise of Immanuel belong together. The God who numbered Ephraim's years also sent His Son "when the fullness of the time had come" (Galatians 4:4).

The king of the Song who looks past sixty queens to his one dove offers a fitting picture of Christ's devotion to His church (Ephesians 5:25–32). This is a resonance rather than a stated fulfillment, but it fits the way Scripture describes the Bridegroom's love.

7. Spirit & Life

Genesis 25 speaks to anyone whose prayers are still unanswered. The text does not say how long Isaac prayed, but it shows twenty years between his marriage and the birth of his sons, and it records that "the LORD granted his plea." God does not hide that waiting from the record, and He does not describe Isaac's prayer as wasted. Waiting on a promise is part of how God's people come to depend on Him.

Leviticus 27 shows that God receives the devotion of the aging. The valuation schedule has a place for those sixty and older, and verse 8 opens a way for anyone too poor to pay. No one is refused a vow because of age or poverty. Older saints can bring their years to Him without shame.

Bashan's walls answer the believer's fears. What looks fortified "with high walls, gates, and bars" is not beyond the Lord, and what He conquers He can turn into a blessing for His people.

In the Song, sixty queens cannot compete with the one the king loves. In Christ, no believer is lost in a crowd.

8. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Genesis 5:23 – Enoch's days total three hundred sixty-five years. In a chapter that repeats "and he died," Enoch's short life is marked instead by walking with God.

  • Numbers 3:50 – Moses collects one thousand three hundred sixty-five shekels to redeem Israel's firstborn. The count shows that redemption was paid in full.

  • Numbers 7:88 – The leaders bring sixty rams, sixty male goats, and sixty lambs for the altar's dedication. Every tribe gave in equal measure.

  • Ezra 2:64 – The whole returning assembly numbered 42,360. After the exile, God's people were again counted as one company.

  • Ezekiel 40:14 – The gateposts of the visionary temple measure sixty cubits, which conveys the grandeur of the place God will restore.

9. Related Words & Canonical Pairings

  • Lexical relative: H8337 (shêsh), six. "Sixty" is formed from this word. Compare the combined numbers in Numbers 7:88.

10. Other Relevant Insights

The figure six hundred sixty-six appears with this word in 2 Chronicles 9:13 (Solomon's yearly gold) and Ezra 2:13 (the descendants of Adonikam). These are plain historical counts. They should not be read as coded references to Revelation 13:18.
