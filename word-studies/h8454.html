SOUND WISDOM, SUCCESS, PRUDENCE - (tûwshîyâh, Strong's H8454)

Summary

Tûwshîyâh is a Hebrew word for wisdom that works. The NKJV translates it as "sound wisdom," "success," "prudence," "plans," "judgment," and "guidance." The range of translations makes sense once we see the idea behind them. This word describes good sense and the ability to carry it through to a good result. It is wisdom with substance, the kind that holds up when life puts weight on it.

The word is rare, and it appears only in Job, Proverbs, Isaiah, and Micah. Scripture places it first with God: "With Him are strength and prudence" (Job 12:16). He "stores up sound wisdom for the upright" (Proverbs 2:7). He also frustrates the crafty, "so that their hands cannot carry out their plans" (Job 5:12). In Job, a righteous man feels his "success" driven from him. In Micah, this wisdom is the insight that recognizes God's name while His rod of discipline falls. Taken together, these passages teach that lasting success is a gift held by God. It is not something people can manufacture apart from Him. They point toward Christ, "the power of God and the wisdom of God" (1 Corinthians 1:24).

Full Study

1. Word Background & Setting

Tûwshîyâh belongs to the vocabulary of Israel's wisdom writers and prophets. The broad Hebrew word for wisdom is chokmah, which covers skill, insight, and godly understanding of every kind. Tûwshîyâh is narrower and more practical. It carries the idea of sound counsel that actually accomplishes something, so that the same word can mean "wisdom" in one verse and "success" in the next.

Job uses it more than any other book, and that matters. The word lands in the middle of a long argument about why a righteous man is suffering and whether success reveals God's favor.

2. Meaning / Lexical Definition

The core idea is effective wisdom: practical understanding joined to the capacity to bring about a sound outcome. Context determines which side of that idea is in view.

1. Sound wisdom or practical judgment. This is the reliable good sense that guides life well, as in "He stores up sound wisdom for the upright" (Proverbs 2:7) and the call to keep sound wisdom and discretion (Proverbs 3:21).

2. Success or an effective outcome. Here the word means the resources or results that sound planning produces. Job asks, "And is success driven from me?" (Job 6:13), and the crafty find that "their hands cannot carry out their plans" (Job 5:12).

3. Biblical Usage & Nuance

The meaning shifts according to who holds the wisdom. When the word describes God, it is complete and secure. In Job 12:16 He holds "strength and prudence," and in Isaiah 28:29 He is "excellent in guidance." In Proverbs 8:14, personified Wisdom claims it as her own possession. When the word describes people, it is either received or lost. The upright receive it as God's stored-up gift. The crafty see their plans collapse. The person who cuts himself off from others "rages against all wise judgment" (Proverbs 18:1). In each human case, the word measures a relationship to the God who possesses it.

4. Theological Significance

The most basic truth this word teaches is that effective wisdom has its home in God. Proverbs 2:7 pictures Him storing up sound wisdom the way a household stores grain against a hard winter, and the same verse calls Him "a shield to those who walk uprightly." What He keeps in reserve, He gives to people who walk with Him. Sound wisdom flows out of His own character.

A second truth follows: in God, wisdom and power are never separated. "With Him are strength and prudence" (Job 12:16). Wisdom speaks in the same way: "Counsel is mine, and sound wisdom; I am understanding, I have strength" (Proverbs 8:14). Isaiah 28:29 joins "wonderful in counsel" with "excellent in guidance." Human beings often know what ought to be done and cannot do it, or they can act but do not know how to act well. God's counsel always carries the strength to accomplish it.

The third truth is the reverse of the second. Success that people build apart from God does not last. God "frustrates the devices of the crafty" (Job 5:12). Job 12:16 goes further: "The deceived and the deceiver are His." No scheme stands outside His rule, and no cleverness can keep its results once He withdraws His hand.

5. Important Biblical Passages & Distinctions

Job's dialogues (Job 5:12; 6:13; 11:6; 12:16; 30:22). Job's friends use tûwshîyâh as a moral scoreboard. Eliphaz speaks of God frustrating the crafty so they cannot carry out their plans (Job 5:12), and he implies that Job's collapse belongs in that category. Zophar wishes God would show Job "the secrets of wisdom," which "would double your prudence" and expose that "God exacts from you less than your iniquity deserves" (Job 11:6). For both men, success and failure plainly reveal a person's standing with God.

Job's experience will not fit their formula. He asks, "Is my help not within me? And is success driven from me?" (Job 6:13). His inner resources are gone. Yet he also confesses, "With Him are strength and prudence" (Job 12:16). He places sound wisdom where it truly lives, with God, while still unable to explain his pain. Much later he cries, "You lift me up to the wind and cause me to ride on it; You spoil my success" (Job 30:22). (The Hebrew text of that verse has a marginal reading that some translations render "storm," but the NKJV follows "success.")

Job does not move neatly from loss to insight. He holds both at once: wisdom and power belong to God, and God has allowed his own success to be taken away. The book does not resolve that tension by restoring Job's fortune first. It resolves it by bringing Job face to face with God (Job 38–42). In the end the LORD says the friends had not spoken rightly (Job 42:7). Their theology of success was too small for the God who actually holds it.

Micah 6:9. "The LORD's voice cries to the city— Wisdom shall see Your name: 'Hear the rod! Who has appointed it?'" Israel is under judgment. Only two verses earlier, Micah has summed up what God requires: to do justly, love mercy, and walk humbly with Him (Micah 6:8). Now God's voice calls out to a city that has not listened. The wise person, Micah says, will see God's name in what is happening. In Scripture, God's name is His revealed character, proclaimed to Moses as "merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abounding in goodness and truth," yet "by no means clearing the guilty" (Exodus 34:6–7). The question "Who has appointed it?" pushes past the rod to the One who holds it.

The fool sees only bad events. The wise see God, holy enough to discipline and merciful enough to warn before the blow falls. (Some translations read this line as "it is sound wisdom to fear Your name"; either way, wisdom responds rightly to God's name in the hour of discipline.) This is tûwshîyâh at its deepest. It is spiritual perception that hears God's voice inside hard circumstances and answers Him.

Isaiah 28:23–29. Isaiah describes a farmer who plows, sows dill and cumin, wheat and barley, each in its place, and threshes each crop with the right tool. He never crushes the grain endlessly. "This also comes from the LORD of hosts, Who is wonderful in counsel and excellent in guidance" (Isaiah 28:29). The parable concerns God's judgments: He matches His dealings to their purpose and does not thresh forever. His guidance is measured, timely, and aimed at a harvest.

6. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

The union of counsel and strength that tûwshîyâh describes reaches its fullness in Jesus Christ. Paul calls Him "Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God" (1 Corinthians 1:24). This statement concerns the cross, which the world counted as both foolish and weak. There God's wisdom accomplished salvation. It is the clearest case in Scripture of wisdom that works.

Isaiah's description of the LORD as "wonderful in counsel" (Isaiah 28:29) echoes the names he gives the coming Child: Wonderful, Counselor, Mighty God (Isaiah 9:6). The God whose guidance is excellent promises a Son who embodies that counsel. Proverbs 2:7 speaks of sound wisdom stored up for the upright. Paul says all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden in Christ (Colossians 2:3). The storehouse now has a name.

Paul also draws from Eliphaz's speech. In 1 Corinthians 3:19 he cites Job 5:13, the line that follows our word in Job 5:12, to show that God catches the wise in their own craftiness. Eliphaz misapplied the truth to Job, but the truth itself stands. At the cross, God overturned the plans of rulers who thought they were in control.

7. Spirit & Life

Proverbs and Job need each other. Proverbs gives the normal pattern: God grants sound wisdom to the upright and shields them. Job keeps that pattern from turning into a guarantee of visible prosperity. Because "with Him are strength and prudence," God may allow a faithful person's success to be spoiled without that loss meaning He has turned away. When outward success disappears, sound wisdom is still available, because it was always kept with Him rather than in us. Job's question "Is my help not within me?" receives its answer there.

Micah teaches believers what to look for when discipline comes. Hebrews says, "whom the LORD loves He chastens" (Hebrews 12:6). In practice, wisdom asks "Who has appointed it?" before it asks "How do I escape it?" It looks for God's name in the trial and finds there both His holiness and His mercy.

Proverbs 18:1 adds a warning that is easy to miss: "A man who isolates himself seeks his own desire; He rages against all wise judgment." The man who withdraws to pursue his own desire cuts himself off from counsel, correction, and the shared wisdom of God's people. He ends up fighting against the very sound judgment he needs. God's practical wisdom often reaches us through others.

8. Key Uses in Other Verses (NKJV)

  • Proverbs 3:21 – The father urges his son to keep sound wisdom and discretion in view. A gift God stores up must also be guarded.

  • Proverbs 8:14 – "Counsel is mine, and sound wisdom; I am understanding, I have strength." Wisdom speaks as one who possesses what she offers, the background for the call to seek her throughout Proverbs 8.
