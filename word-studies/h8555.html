TIMNA - (Timnâʻ, Strong's H8555)

Summary

Timna is a name from the family records of Edom in Genesis 36 and 1 Chronicles 1. It most clearly belongs to a Horite woman, the sister of Lotan and daughter of Seir, the original people of the mountain country south of the Dead Sea. She became the concubine of Eliphaz, Esau's firstborn son, and bore him Amalek. The same Hebrew name later appears among the "chiefs" of Edom, where the NKJV spells it "Timnah." The meaning of the name is uncertain. It is often connected with a Hebrew root meaning "to withhold" or "to restrain," but because Timna came from a Horite family, that link should be held loosely. Timna should not be confused with Timnah (H8553), the place name used for a hill-country town associated with Judah and for a border town associated with Samson. The two names look alike in English but are spelled differently in Hebrew.

Timna never speaks or acts in Scripture. Her importance comes from the son she bore. Amalek's descendants became the first people to attack Israel after the Exodus. They struck the weary stragglers at the rear of the line, and God promised to war against Amalek "from generation to generation." Genesis gives that later conflict a real beginning in a named woman, a named father, and a family joining between Esau's house and the people of Seir. The record also shows God at work beyond the chosen line. He gave Mount Seir to Esau, recorded Esau's clans, and then turned the story back to Jacob, through whom His promise of blessing for all nations would come.

Full Study

1. IDENTITY & NAME

The Hebrew name (תִּמְנָע) ends with the letter ayin. This sets it apart from Timnah (H8553, ending in he). Brown-Driver-Briggs distinguishes two places by that name: a town in the hill country of Judah, where Judah went to shear sheep (Genesis 38; Joshua 15:57), and a town on the Judah–Dan border, where Samson sought a Philistine wife (Joshua 15:10; 19:43; Judges 14). English Bibles sometimes spell the two names almost the same way, but they are different names for different people or places.

The six occurrences of H8555 point in two directions:

  • Timna the woman. She is the sister of Lotan among the sons of Seir the Horite (Genesis 36:22; 1 Chronicles 1:39), and she is the concubine of Eliphaz and mother of Amalek (Genesis 36:12). Scripture does not explicitly say these are the same woman. Still, the same chapter names both, and both belong to the same intermarried families of Seir and Edom, so most readers reasonably take them as one person.

  • Chief Timnah. This name, spelled "Timnah" in the NKJV but belonging here to H8555, appears in a list of Edomite chiefs, which the text arranges "according to their families and their places, by their names" (Genesis 36:40; 1 Chronicles 1:51). The list may be naming clans or districts rather than individuals. A clan or territory may have carried Timna's name, but Scripture does not explain how the chief relates to the woman. The connection is possible, not certain.

The meaning of the name is uncertain. It is commonly linked to the Hebrew verb mana', "to withhold" or "to hold back," which would suggest something like "restraint." The name belongs to a Horite family, however, and the Horites' language and naming customs are not clearly known, so a Hebrew derivation is only probable. Scripture makes no wordplay on the name and never draws a lesson from it.

2. BIBLICAL STORY & SETTING

Genesis 36 is the record of "Esau, who is Edom." Esau had settled in Mount Seir, the rugged highlands southeast of the Dead Sea. That land was home to the Horites (Genesis 14:6). Deuteronomy later explains that Esau's descendants dispossessed the Horites and settled in their place (Deuteronomy 2:12, 22). Genesis 36 shows the other side of that process: the two peoples also married into each other's families. Timna is one of the clearest examples.

Her concubine-and-mother notice is told in a single sentence: "Now Timna was the concubine of Eliphaz, Esau's son, and she bore Amalek to Eliphaz. These were the sons of Adah, Esau's wife" (Genesis 36:12). Eliphaz was Esau's firstborn by Adah (Genesis 36:4, 15). As a concubine, Timna held a lower position than a full wife. Even so, Genesis counts her son within Adah's line of grandsons. Later in the chapter, the Horite list adds that "Lotan's sister was Timna" (Genesis 36:22), which ties her to one of the leading families of Seir. Genesis gives no comment on the arrangement. It simply records it.

First Chronicles 1 repeats these genealogies for Israel after the exile. In verse 36, the Chronicler lists the sons of Eliphaz as "Teman, Omar, Zephi, Gatam, and Kenaz; and by Timna, Amalek." In the Hebrew text, the line reads more compactly, with Timna appearing beside the sons. This may be a shorthand that assumes readers know Genesis 36:12, or it may reflect how the Timna-Amalek branch came to be counted within Eliphaz's house. Some commentators, following Brown-Driver-Briggs, read the Hebrew as naming a separate male Timna among Eliphaz's sons. Genesis clearly identifies Timna as Amalek's mother, but it does not by itself settle which person each use of the name in Chronicles identifies. The Chronicler also repeats Timna's place as Lotan's sister (1 Chronicles 1:39) and the name "Chief Timnah" among Edom's chiefs (1 Chronicles 1:51).

3. SIGNIFICANCE IN GOD'S PURPOSES

Scripture states several things plainly. Timna belonged to the Horites of Seir. She bore Amalek to Esau's son. The same name later appears among Edom's chiefs, though Scripture does not explain how that chief or clan relates to her. Scripture also states that God Himself gave Mount Seir to Esau and told Israel not to take any of it (Deuteronomy 2:5). Esau's growth into a nation of chiefs and kings fulfilled what Isaac had spoken over him (Genesis 27:39–40). These were not accidents of history. God was keeping His word to the son who did not carry the line of promise.

Genesis follows a familiar pattern here. It briefly records the line that branches off, then returns to the chosen line, just as it records Ishmael's sons before returning to Isaac (Genesis 25:12–19). Edom had kings "before any king reigned over the children of Israel" (Genesis 36:31). In the arrangement of Genesis, the record of Esau's organized house is set beside Jacob's family of shepherds as Genesis 37 turns to Jacob and Joseph. Genesis does not date every Edomite reign to Jacob's lifetime. In God's plan, strength and early success did not settle who would carry the promise.

Beyond what Scripture states, the reader can draw a careful conclusion. Timna's place in the genealogy gives historical roots to Amalek, whose name runs through the rest of the Old Testament. Amalek attacked Israel at Rephidim. Joshua fought, Moses held up his hands, and the LORD declared He would have war with Amalek "from generation to generation" (Exodus 17:8–16). Moses later reminded Israel that Amalek had cut down the weak and weary at the rear of the march and "did not fear God" (Deuteronomy 25:17–19). Balaam called Amalek "first among the nations," with a final end ahead (Numbers 24:20). Saul's failure to carry out God's judgment on Amalek cost him the kingdom (1 Samuel 15). Amalekites later raided Ziklag (1 Samuel 30), and a remnant was struck down, probably in Hezekiah's time (1 Chronicles 4:41–43). Haman is called "the Agagite" (Esther 3:1), a name widely understood to link him with Agag, the Amalekite king of 1 Samuel 15.

Scripture never blames Timna for any of this, and the genealogy gives no reasons for Amalek's later character. Her significance lies in being the documented beginning point. The enemy Israel met in the wilderness had a known family history.

4. CHRIST & THE LARGER BIBLICAL STORY

No New Testament passage mentions Timna, and no type of Christ is established in her. The larger story she belongs to still matters. God's long conflict with Amalek shows that He does not leave His people defenseless against those who prey on them. At Rephidim the victory came as Moses held up his hands, and the altar Moses built was named "The-LORD-Is-My-Banner" (Exodus 17:15). The battle belonged to the LORD.

The house of Edom, which Timna married into, is also not beyond the reach of God's purposes. Amos foresaw a day when the restored house of David would possess "the remnant of Edom" and all the nations called by God's name (Amos 9:11–12). At the Jerusalem council, James quoted that prophecy in its Greek form as fulfilled in Gentiles turning to God through Christ (Acts 15:13–18). The family lists of Genesis 36 describe peoples outside the chosen line, but they do not mark those peoples as permanently shut out of God's mercy.

5. SPIRIT & LIFE

Timna's brief notices show a God who keeps careful records. He knew the name of a concubine in the hill country of Seir, the name of her brother, and the son she bore. Long before Israel had kings, God had recorded where the nations around His people came from and what land He had given them (Deuteronomy 2:5, 9, 19). Nothing in Israel's later history caught Him unprepared.

The story that grows out of her line also shows God's heart for the weak. Amalek's particular evil was attacking the faint and weary stragglers at the back of the march (Deuteronomy 25:18). God did not forget those who were struck down, and He did not treat their suffering as a small matter. That tells us something true about Him: He notices who is trailing behind, and He holds accountable those who prey on them.

6. KEY SCRIPTURES

  • Genesis 36:12 – Timna as concubine of Eliphaz and mother of Amalek; the anchor text.

  • Genesis 36:20–22; 1 Chronicles 1:38–39 – Timna as Lotan's sister among the Horite sons of Seir.

  • Genesis 36:40; 1 Chronicles 1:51 – "Chief Timnah" (H8555) among the clan leaders of Edom.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:36 – The Chronicler's compressed listing of Timna with Eliphaz's line.

  • Deuteronomy 2:5, 12, 22 – God gives Seir to Esau, and Esau's descendants displace the Horites.

  • Exodus 17:8–16; Deuteronomy 25:17–19 – Amalek's attack on Israel and God's lasting judgment.

  • 1 Samuel 15; Esther 3:1 – Later chapters of the Amalekite conflict.

  • Amos 9:11–12; Acts 15:13–18 – Edom's remnant within God's purpose for the nations.
