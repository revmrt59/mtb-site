BEDAD - (Bᵉdad, Strong's H0911)

Summary

Bedad was an Edomite, a descendant of Esau's people, and Scripture remembers him only as the father of Hadad, one of the early kings of Edom. His name appears twice, in parallel records: "Hadad the son of Bedad, who attacked Midian in the field of Moab, reigned in his place" (Genesis 36:35; 1 Chronicles 1:46). The name is derived from a Hebrew root meaning "to be separated" or "to be alone," so it likely carries the sense of "separation" or "solitary." The Bible attaches no wordplay or lesson to that meaning.

Bedad's place in Scripture is small, but it sits inside a list with real weight. Genesis introduces these rulers as "the kings who reigned in the land of Edom before any king reigned over the children of Israel" (Genesis 36:31). Esau's family had thrones and capitals while Jacob's family was still waiting for the kings God had promised Abraham. Bedad himself is never called a king, and that fact, together with the changing names in the list, suggests how Edom's crown passed from one man to another.

Full Study

1. Identity & Name

Bedad is the only person in Scripture with this name. He is identified solely as the father of Hadad, who succeeded Husham as king of Edom. He should not be confused with his son, who is indexed separately (Hadad, H1908). Other Edomites named Hadad appear later in Scripture, but none of them is called a son of Bedad.

The name comes from the Hebrew verbal root badad (H0909), which means to be separated, isolated, or alone; a related noun and adverb (H0910) carries the sense of isolation or being alone. "Separation" or "solitary" is the likely sense of the name. The text never comments on it, and nothing about Bedad's life is drawn from it.

2. Biblical Story & Setting

Bedad has no story of his own. He serves as his son's identifier in Edom's king list (Genesis 36:31–39), which the chronicler copies almost word for word (1 Chronicles 1:43–50). In that list, Bela son of Beor reigned from Dinhabah. Jobab son of Zerah of Bozrah followed him, and then came Husham "of the land of the Temanites" (Genesis 36:32–34). When Husham died, Bedad's son Hadad took the throne, ruled from the city of Avith, and was remembered for attacking Midian in the field of Moab.

Where the list names fathers, each is different (Beor, Zerah, Bedad, Achbor), for several kings no father is named, and the kings often come from different cities. Bedad is not himself listed as a king. Together these details suggest that Edom's early kingship did not pass from father to son. Hadad's military victory may have counted for more than his ancestry. The battle also places Esau's descendants in conflict with Midian, who came from Abraham through Keturah, and on land tied to Moab, who came from Lot. These were Abraham's own relatives, fighting one another in lands that also figure in Israel's own story. The text does not say when this battle took place in relation to Israel's later journey through the region.

3. Significance in God's Purposes

Scripture states plainly that Edom had kings before Israel did (Genesis 36:31). God had promised Abraham that "kings" would come from him (Genesis 17:6), yet Edom, descended from Abraham through Esau, had kings before Israel did. This fits what Isaac foretold, that Esau would live by the sword (Genesis 27:39–40). Bedad's mention as the father of a warrior-king, while he himself is not listed among the kings, illustrates how Edom's power changed hands. The contrast with the lasting throne God later promised David (2 Samuel 7:12–16) is a carefully drawn implication, not something the king list itself says.

4. Christ & the Larger Biblical Story

No New Testament or prophetic passage mentions Bedad. The larger story he belongs to does point forward, because God had said "the older shall serve the younger" (Genesis 25:23). The promised kingdom would come through Jacob and finally through David's Son, even though Edom's rulers arrived first.

5. Spirit & Life

Genesis lets Edom's kings stand on the page while Israel has none. God's promises were not late, though they were slower than Esau's success. Readers who watch others build quickly can take this record seriously: God keeps careful account of the nations, down to a king's father, and He brings His own kingdom in His own time.

6. Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 36:35 gives the first record of "Hadad the son of Bedad," his victory over Midian, and his city of Avith.

  • 1 Chronicles 1:46 repeats the record for Israel after the exile.

  • Genesis 36:31–34 frames these kings as reigning before Israel had a king.

  • Genesis 27:39–40; 25:23 contain the words spoken over Esau that this history fits.

  • Genesis 17:6 records God's promise of kings from Abraham.
